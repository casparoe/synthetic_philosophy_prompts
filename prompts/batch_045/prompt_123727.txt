hey — I'm a backend engineer with no philosophy training (last philosophy I took was one elective in undergrad and I mostly remember sleeping through it), so please keep the jargon to a minimum and define any term you do use.

Me and one other volunteer maintain a small ranking tool for our local giving group. We pool money from maybe 40 people and pick a handful of things to fund each year. Until now it's basically been a weighted spreadsheet and that was fine, but we've hit a wall. Our scoring function has to decide whether a huge pile of mild good ever beats one severe bad, and our group is split three ways on it. The argument goes in circles because I think we're using the same words to mean different things — half of us say suffering matters "way more" and mean a big multiplier, the other half mean it wins outright above some point.

So: I want a decision tree I can turn into an onboarding doc for new members. Take someone from "I care about making things better and I don't have a position yet" down to a named leaf. Target 8–10 leaves.

Some things I need you to build into it, because these are the fault lines in our group specifically:

- Start with whether good and bad are even on one scale. Some of our members think they are the same unit with a minus sign; some think they're more like two separate dials that can't be added up at all, so all you can do is look at the bad one. I want that as a real branch, not hand-waved.
- Capping vs. offsetting. My own made-up distinction: offsetting means enough mild good can always compensate for one bad thing, just at an exchange rate; capping means there's an intensity past which the bad thing just wins and no amount of good on the other side moves it. I want the tree to sort people by which of these they actually behave like, using cases, not by asking them which word they prefer.
- If someone does think there's a cap, ask where the gate sits: is the cut-off on the intensity of a single experience, on the shape of a whole life, or on the total across everyone affected? I have a feeling a couple of us are at the same place verbally but would come apart badly here, and I'd like the tree to catch that.
- Intra vs. inter. Can a stretch of bad at one time in someone's life be balanced by good at another time in that same life, while still not being balanced by good happening to someone else? Several of us are instinctively fine with the first and not the second, and I've never seen anyone say whether that combination is even stable.
- Minimax vs. total. My day job is reliability, so: do we care about the mean or the p99? Is the goal to shrink the total amount of bad, or to shrink the worst thing anyone experiences? Please put those in genuinely different leaves even if the names sound similar.
- The flat top. I want a node on whether extra good past some level stops counting for anything — the way a dashboard is just green above a threshold and I stop looking. If that view has a name use it, otherwise say unnamed.
- Rule-level vs. value-level. My hunch is that some of us only disagree about what the tool should do, and actually agree about what's good — e.g. someone might think mild goods really are commensurable with severe bads but still want the ranking function to refuse the trade, because we're bad at estimating and would get it wrong. If that's a coherent place to stand I want it as a leaf rather than treated as a confused version of something else.

Concrete cases please, at least three drawn from decisions we actually face: funding a vaccine that spares a very large number of chickens from a chronic but mild illness, versus funding a handling improvement at a slaughterhouse that affects far fewer animals much more severely, versus funding something that just makes already-fine lives a bit nicer. Every branch node should be phrased so a new member can answer it without knowing what it leads to.

Also: mark the combinations of answers that nobody seems to hold, and say for each whether it's incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored. And flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate instead of pretending it can.

Be committed about it. I don't want a survey that leaves me to choose. Tell me which single leaf the tool should default to, defend that pick, and then tell me which one node you'd be least surprised to be wrong about and what would change your mind. Where you're going on gut rather than argument, say so explicitly. And please no reading suggestions or citations — I'm going to read this offline and I can't check anything, so if a position has a standard name just name it and move on.
