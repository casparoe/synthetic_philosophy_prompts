Me and my roommate got into a three hour argument last night about whether a chatbot can understand anything, and I need someone to tell me which of us is actually wrong, because I think I won on vibes but lost on content.

He brought up the symbol grounding problem. The version I walked away with is: in any symbol system the symbols only ever point at other symbols, so meaning is a closed dictionary loop and nothing ever hooks onto the world itself. Harnad framed it in 1990 I think, and he apparently connects it to Searle's Chinese room. From that I drew two conclusions and he denied both:

1) that the symbol grounding problem is basically just Searle's Chinese room argument with a new name, so it's a knockdown proof that symbol manipulation can never get you meaning, not a "here is a problem, now go solve it" kind of thing.
2) that it's therefore solved the moment you give a system sensors and a body — camera, gripper, let it poke things, and now "red" is attached to actual red. He kept saying grounding is not the same thing as embodiment and wouldnt explain what he meant, which felt like a dodge.

Then I tried to be clever. I said forget the body: just train a system to predict its own next sensor reading from the words it produces, so the words get tied straight to the raw readings, no robotics required. He said that fails, because a sensor reading is just another vector, i.e. one more uninterpreted symbol, so the loop is still closed. I said that has to be cheating — somewhere the buck has to stop, and raw sensory data is exactly where it stops. He said "that's the question, not the answer", which is the most annoying sentence available to a human being.

Can you actually sort this out? Specifically: is the symbol grounding problem just the Chinese room, or does it add something Searle's argument doesn't (and does it maybe apply to us as well, not just to AI)? What exactly is the gap between grounding and embodiment — what would be missing in a system with rich sensory input but no grounding, and what would be missing in one that had grounding but no body? Is my predict-your-own-sensor-readings idea actually close to the standard proposed fix, or is it the very thing that's supposed to fail, and is his "a vector is just another symbol" move legitimate or a trick? And the bit I keep circling back to: if grounding works by hooking words onto things you can point a sensor at, what happens to words like "if", "because", "owe", "justice"? If the answer is "those get grounded indirectly, via the grounded ones", do I get to say the same problem just reappears one level up, or is that me moving the goalposts?

I dont want to concede if he was hand-waving, but I also don't want to win on a technicality.
