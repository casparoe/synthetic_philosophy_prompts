Before I read any more of the literature on deriving the Born rule in the no-collapse picture, I want the standard such a derivation should be held to. Please write me the desiderata: what a derivation of the squared-amplitude rule, inside a purely unitary quantum mechanics, would have to deliver in order to count as solving the problem rather than relocating it. The purpose I have in mind fixes what adequate means here: the result should let an Everettian claim honestly that the theory predicts the statistics we observe, instead of borrowing the rule from textbook quantum mechanics while presenting it as derived.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and mark it either non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. Please also cover, and say where they belong on the list: the split between the qualitative worry, that is, what probability even means if every outcome is realized, and the quantitative one, that is, why squared amplitude rather than plain amplitude or a count of branches; whether a derivation that rests on axioms of rational choice has really satisfied a non-circularity requirement or has only pushed the bump elsewhere in the carpet; and three worries of my own. First, decoherence never quite zeroes the off-diagonal terms, so branches are emergent, approximate, and relative to a coarse-graining: the derivation must attach weights to merely approximate branches, must say what the residual tails contribute rather than assume they vanish, and must not presuppose that there is a definite number of branches at a time. Second, it should be invariant under refining or merging the branch decomposition, so that the weights do not depend on how finely outcomes are individuated. Third, the branching structure contains branches whose recorded statistics are wildly non-Born, so the derivation must explain what makes our evidence confirmatory without presupposing a typicality measure that already uses squared amplitudes.
