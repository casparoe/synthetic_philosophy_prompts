I'm TA'ing a 16-person upper-level phil of religion seminar (80 min) next week on Molinism, and I want a game rather then a lecture. Students should feel, before anyone names the view, why planning around free agents seems to need knowledge of what they *would* do in circumstances that never actually happen — and then feel the two things that make that weird: first, that nothing about the agent seems to settle which counterfactual is the true one, and second, that even a full dossier of one-agent counterfactuals tells you nothing reliable about a world containing several agents, since A's choice can depend on whether B is there. So: some students as agents with hidden decision rules (a few of them interdependent), a planner team that gets counterfactual info but no peeking at the actual world, and ideally a bookkeeper who tracks which combinations are even consistent. Payoffs without real money. Then one last round where the agents are told which counterfactual got them selected for this world and can try to falsify it — defecting should cost them a little individually and a lot collectively, so they have to feel whether a true counterfactual of freedom can survive being disclosed to the agent its about. Include a debrief mapping moments in the game onto those two weirdnesses, what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and what the game simply can't reproduce and I'll have to say in plain words myself.
