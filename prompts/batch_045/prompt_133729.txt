I mod a Discord server for a survival MMO — few thousand people, maybe six of us on the mod team — and a stupidly large chunk of my week is spent arbitrating fights. Over a couple of years I've started noticing a pattern in how people argue in those fights, and not having a word for it is driving me nuts.

Here's the pattern. The same person, sometimes inside the same paragraph, will talk as if virtual stuff is perfectly real and then as if it's nothing whatsoever, and which way they go doesn't track the facts of the case. Someone spends four hundred hours building a base, another player he trusted lets himself in and torches it, and the first guy posts that he destroyed months of work, that it was theft, that he wants the guy banned and the items restored. Half an hour later in the same thread someone brings up that a woman in the server was cornered on voice chat and harassed for an hour while her character was pinned down, and the person who just called a burned base "theft" says it's just pixels, nobody was actually touched, if you can't handle it don't roll on an open-PvP server. And it isn't simple self-interest, which is why I don't think "hypocrisy" is the right word: I've watched people who were harassed themselves deploy the "it was only a game" move about their own harassment, and I've watched people who think being groped in VR is a real assault laugh at the idea that duping a stack of rare ore is theft, on the grounds that the ore wasn't scarce and nobody lost anything. So the flip doesn't line up with who benefits, and it doesn't line up with any general view about whether virtual worlds are real. As far as I can tell what it tracks is whether treating the virtual thing as real would generate an obligation somebody has to act on — property-ish claims generate "give it back, punish him," harm-ish claims generate "apologise, ban him, admit you did something wrong" — and whichever one is currently on the table gets the deflationary treatment. I've been calling it register flipping privately, and my own half-baked hypothesis is that the real variable isn't realism about the virtual at all but something like enforcement-dependence: whether acknowledging the thing would require an institution (the devs, us) to actually do something.

So: does this have a name? I know there's a well-known puzzle in this area about why killing people in a video game seems fine while certain other virtual acts seem clearly not fine, and that feels adjacent, but it's about two different virtual acts rather than about one community's ontology flipping depending on what's at stake. I also know there are two consistent positions people defend — that virtual objects are perfectly real objects, just digital ones, and virtual reality is a genuine kind of reality; and the other one, that all of this is make-believe with props and the props aren't what the make-believe is about — and my phenomenon is neither of those, it's the switching between them. What I want is the nearest established concepts and how my pattern differs from each one: what it's not, especially (is it really distinct from ordinary special pleading, or is it just special pleading wearing a costume?), whether there's a version on which the flip is actually correct because objects need scarcity or causal powers or institutional backing and harms only need somebody to suffer, and whether the two standards could be jointly consistent in a way I'm not seeing. If nothing established fits, coin a term, define it, and give me the boundary cases — what counts as an instance and what's a near miss. And please don't hand me a bibliography or papers to look up; I've got no library access from here and I'd never chase them down anyway. I want the carving, not the name: what exactly this thing is, where its edges are, and what would have to be true for it to be a genuine double standard rather than two different standards correctly applied to two different kinds of thing.
