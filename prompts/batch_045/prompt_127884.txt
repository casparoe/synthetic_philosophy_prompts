I'm stuck on the intro of a paper about dispositions and I want the spec nailed down before I look at any actual views. What does a theory of what it is for a thing to have a disposition have to deliver? Not more examples of dispositions — the requirements: which cases it has to get right, which distinctions it can't collapse, which constraints it can't violate, with the case or consideration behind each.

Purpose, so adequacy has something to be relative to: I need the account to do two jobs. First, tell me whether a disposition ascription is true in a concrete setting — the sort engineers and regulators make all the time: this alloy is corrosion-resistant in seawater, this cluster is fault-tolerant, this model is robust to distribution shift. Second, tell me whether ascribing a disposition can ever explain the manifestation, or is always just a redescription of it.

Cases I want on the list at minimum: the fink (the stimulus destroys the basis), the mask (manifestation blocked, disposition retained), the mimic (no disposition, but on stimulus the manifestation shows up anyway), the antidote (stimulus produces something else entirely), irreducibly chancy manifestations like a radium atom's decaying — what even is the stimulus there, and what is the manifestation condition? — and one-shot dispositions whose exercise destroys or alters the bearer, like fragility, or a self-sealing tyre that manifests by acquiring a mask. Plus two of my own:

The selective fink. A guardian device repairs the sample only when the impact exceeds a threshold, so below the threshold it shatters and above it doesn't. Is that a disposition with a restricted stimulus condition, a finked disposition, or a disposition whose ceteris paribus clause excludes guardians — and whatever you say, does the theory still handle masks the same way, or does it have to treat the two differently?

The distributed bearer. A server cluster that's fault-tolerant, or a colony that thermoregulates: no single node has anything like the disposition and the "basis" isn't a microstructure. If the theory needs a categorical basis in the bearer, what's the bearer here and what's the basis?

For each requirement, say whether it's a genuine desideratum or a prejudice of one camp — I suspect the demand that the ceteris paribus conditions be spelled out in non-dispositional terms is negotiable, and that the demand that dispositions be intrinsic is a camp preference dressed up as data. Flag any pair that can't be jointly satisfied; my guess is you can't have both "handle masks without circularity" and "give necessary and sufficient conditions", but I want to know which other subsets blow up. And name the single requirement that excludes the most candidates. You can score the usual suspects — simple conditional analysis, the revised conditional analysis, mutual manifestation views, dispositional essentialism — in a line each at the end, but the list is what I actually want.
