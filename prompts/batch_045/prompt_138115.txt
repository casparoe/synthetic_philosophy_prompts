Hey, I'm building a conlang for a fantasy novel and I'm trying to get the psychology of the people who speak it right. I stripped out all tense marking and any word meaning anything like future, on the theory that if your language gives you no way to talk about the future, you can't really think about it either, so my culture should be bad at planning ahead, storing food, that kind of thing. But a friend in my writing group read a draft and said that's just the Eskimo-words-for-snow thing dressed up, and that linguists settled it decades ago: languages can all express anything, and the color-term research showed everyone's brains basically work the same. So which is it, does removing a category actually rewire how my characters think, or is the Sapir-Whorf idea just dead? We've also started calling the mechanism the forced-encoding effect: if your grammar makes you mark a distinction every single time you open your mouth, you get tons of practice noticing that distinction, and that has to do something to you. Is that a thing anyone actually works on, or did we reinvent a wheel?
