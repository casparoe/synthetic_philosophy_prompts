i've been chewing on something for a while and and I keep failing to find the word for it, so I'd like you to either point me at the established concept or tell me the thing I'm describing isn't one thing.

To get us on the same page, here's the case everybody uses: someone threatens you, or offers you cash, unless you believe some proposition. The lesson usually drawn is that this gives you *a* reason to believe in some sense, but not the sort of consideration that has anything to do with whether the belief is correct — its force comes from what believing does for you, not from what the belief is about. Fine. What I'm after is a different-looking family of cases where there's no carrot and no stick at all.

I first noticed it in an argument about whether a perfect forgery can be as good as the original: "you should treasure this one — the copy is indistinguishable, but this is the one that was actually in the room." No benefit to anyone, no threat, and yet the consideration isn't about the qualities the attitude is supposedly a response to either; it's about the object's history or its relation to you. Same shape outside art: "you should admire him, he's your father"; "you should be proud of it, you made it yourself" (even when you both agree it came out badly). It seems to give *some* reason, not none.

Then there's a variant where people I've talked to split, and I can't tell whether it's the same phenomenon: "you should believe he didn't do it — he's your brother, and suspecting him would be a betrayal." Some people say the loyalty fact is exactly as disconnected from whether he did it as the demon's cash is; others say no, here the consideration really does bear on whether you should believe.

So: does the pattern in my middle cases have a name of its own, or is it just the demon thing in disguise? Is the loyalty/betrayal case the same phenomenon or a different one, and how exactly would you tell them apart? Where's the edge — when does a relational or historical fact stop being this pattern and become a reason of the genuinely relevant kind (think "it's your kid, so you have a reason to hope", or something you promised)? And if there's no established term, coin one, define it, and give me a couple of boundary cases just inside and just outside it. I'm not asking for references or a reading list — I can't get at journals from here, so paper titles are useless to me.

Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no sign-off. And please push back plainly if my cases don't really form one phenomenon, or if the contrast I'm drawing between them and the threat/bribe cases is confused — I'd much rather be told the category is bogus than get a tidy label for something that doesn't hang together.
