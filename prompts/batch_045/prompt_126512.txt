I keep running into the same move in arguments about how many observers there are, and I don't have a word for it. It goes like this.

Two pictures of the world are on the table, and instead of bringing evidence for one of them, someone attaches a clause: oh, and by the way, whatever else is true, this exact kind of moment I'm having right now — sitting here, 2025ish, in a world that looks roughly like this one — happens vastly more often than you'd think. There are a trillion subjectively identical copies of it. Or it gets replayed. Or it's being run on hardware a googol times over. Then they say: conditional on finding myself in this moment, that picture is now a googol-to-one favourite. The clause never has any independent motivation, and nothing I could experience from the inside would tell me whether its true. Its entire job is to multiply the number of observer-moments in my situation.

Three places I've actually seen it. (1) Someone arguing that we should care enormously about simulated minds, on the grounds that if mature civilizations run simulations at all, then nearly all minds in my evidential situation are simulated minds, so conditionalize, and we're almost certainly simulated. (2) Someone arguing in the opposite direction: the human future is probably short and cheap, because worlds where it's short and cheap pack far more "ordinary person living an ordinary 21st-century life" per unit of real history than worlds with a long, glorious future — or something like that, I found it slippery. (3) Someone saying that under a big enough cosmology, or a many-worlds picture, every moment like this one recurs infinitely many times, so the ratios stop being well-defined and this whole style of argument should be thrown out.

A philosopher friend said this is "just the self-indication assumption." But the version of that I've read about seems to be about whether your own existence is evidence that the world is crowded: two genuine cosmological theories, one saying a million observers and one saying a trillion, and you're supposed to favour the trillion — the presumptuous philosopher thing, I think it's called. My cases feel different. Nobody there has a genuine theory that independently predicts a trillion observers; they're bolting the trillion on afterwards in order to win. And (3) seems to be the same move pushed until it eats itself, which is different again from simply picking the crowded theory.

So: is there an established concept for this — retrofitting a hypothesis with extra subjectively-indistinguishable observer-moments in order to win a self-locating update? Or is it not a thing of its own, and is just what the self-indication assumption does once you let the hypothesis space be unrestricted? I've poked around near the doomsday argument, Pascal's mugging, Boltzmann brains, the reference class problem, and the measure problem in cosmology, and each looks close but not quite it. (The reference class thing especially: that seems to be about *which* observers I should count myself among, whereas these people are happy with my reference class, they just want to stuff more members into it.)

If there's no name, coin one and give me a definition plus the boundary cases, because I don't want a label, I want the carving. In particular: is the legitimate crowded-cosmology case the same phenomenon or a different one; does it definately matter whether the extra copies are finite or infinite; does it matter where they live — in my causal future, in another branch, in a machine; and is there anything wrong with the move that isn't just a way of restating "your prior should have been lower"? I want to know what exactly is going wrong, if anything, and where the edges are.

One more thing: mark how confident you are in each part of your answer, and tell me what would change your mind. Specifically, what would convince you that this really is just the self-indication assumption, or just garden-variety ad hoc hypothesis construction, and where do you think your own answer is most likely to be wrong.
