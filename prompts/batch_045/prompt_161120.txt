I want a parody of one of those system cards / model cards that labs put out, the deadpan Capabilities / Evaluation / Limitations genre, except the capability being graded is which rung of Dennett's four kinds of minds the model sits on. The setup: the eval team has to decide whether the model counts as a Popperian creature, a Gregorian one, or neither, and they keep quietly gerrymandering the criteria so it lands one rung higher. Get the actual distinctions right, because that's where the jokes should come from — Darwinian means the repertoire is fixed by selection over generations and the individual learns nothing; Skinnerian means the individual gets shaped within its own lifetime by consequences, you have to actually do the thing and eat the result; Popperian means there's an inner environment where candidate moves get killed in simulation first, so bad hypotheses die inside the head instead of the head dying in the world; Gregorian means the brain gets re-tooled by imported mind-tools — words, numerals, diagrams, categories — stuff the creature didn't evolve and didn't invent but which restructures what it can think.

The bit I actually care about, and the part you should invent: an appendix proposes a fifth, unofficial rung, call it a Laplacian or Bayesian creature or whatever — one whose inner environment doesn't merely host candidate actions but hosts them as explicitly calibrated probabilities with normatively correct updating, so the gap between "has a filter" and "is an epistemic agent" is supposed to show up in whether the thing tracks its own calibration. The report then tries to run that test and the result is unusable in one specific plausible way — pick one and commit: the model is beautifully calibrated on the benchmark distribution and falls apart off it; or it's calibrated only because the eval set is the training distribution's twin; or the calibration turns out to be a Skinnerian-shaped veneer painted over a generator that was shaped by someone else's Popperian filtering.

And the report should keep sliding past the question it can never quite ask out loud: a Popperian inner environment is only as good as whatever generates its candidates, and this thing's hypothesis space was stocked for it — millions of people's trial and error compressed into weights — so is it a Popperian creature, or is it a component inside a much larger Popperian creature that is mostly made of humans? Same worry one rung up: it has every mind-tool imaginable, never had to acquire any of them, and can't refuse them, which is the classic anxiety about ideas that are good at spreading and bad for the head they're in.
