so i've got dragged onto a small team — two of us, about five months — writing an internal spec (30-40 pages) for what a pre-crash planner on an autonomous shuttle is allowed to do in situations where every available maneuver kills somebody. everyone keeps appealing to the doctrine of double effect like it's going to do real work in the document, and im suspicious that it won't, at least not without a lot of prior settling. so i want an agenda, not a survey: which open questions about DDE actually matter for this project, and in what order should we chew through them?

the thing that made me want to ask is a distinction i half-invented and can't find anywhere: harms the agent routes around vs harms the agent routes through. rough heuristic: if the bad effect has to happen before the good one, you're routing through it, it's doing causal work, and DDE shouldn't let you file it as a mere side effect no matter what you say you were aiming at. i think this handles the loop setup (track curves back on itself, so the guy standing on the spur is literally what stops the trolley before it reaches the five — take him away and the trolley completes the loop and kills them) which everyone agrees is using him but which the standard intended/foreseen line doesn't obviously explain. but it also seems to overreach: a shuttle swerving into a runaway lane clips a cyclist on the way in, the collision sheds the speed that makes the stop survivable — that's routing through on my test, and im not sure i want to say it's murder.
