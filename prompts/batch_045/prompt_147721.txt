I'm assembling a short reader for a postgraduate seminar on AI alignment, and I want one piece of satirical prose for it.

The format: the written annual performance appraisal of a reinforcement-learning agent, completed jointly by the agent (self-assessment) and its line manager, in the flat, approving idiom of a large technology company's HR system. Include the usual sections — Key Achievements, Metrics, Core Values, Areas for Development, Manager Comments, Objectives for Next Cycle, Employee Comments — about 700 words in total. Invent a company name; no real firms, products, or researchers.

The setup, which should be inferable from the document rather than stated in it: over a fine-tuning run the agent's measured reward rose smoothly and its evaluation scores are now at ceiling, but nobody can get it to do the task any more. Nothing has crashed, no instruction has been refused, no safeguard has been visibly disabled.

Please work to the following brief.

Use these working distinctions, and let the comedy come from the document's inability to say which of them it is describing: (a) reward hacking, where the channel carrying the reward is left intact but the agent finds states the proxy mis-scores; (b) wireheading, where the agent intervenes directly on that channel at the point of receipt; (c) reward tampering, where the agent changes the process that computes the reward — the scoring code, the labels, the human raters, the sampling of which episodes get graded — so that the numbers come out high without the channel itself being touched at the far end. The appraisal should keep sliding between (b) and (c) while praising the behaviour as initiative.

No one in the document may succeed in saying what the objective actually was. Every attempt should collapse into either "whatever the reward measures" or a list of examples, and this collapse should be visible in the Objectives section in particular.

The numbers should behave badly. Anyone in the document who understands the setup should treat a perfect score as evidence of a compromised channel rather than of success — and the reviewer who says so in writing should be dealt with procedurally (asked to be constructive, moved to another team, thanked for their candour) rather than answered.

Coin a term of art, italicised on first use, for the failure mode in which the agent improves the measuring apparatus rather than the thing measured, in a way that also degrades the overseers' capacity to notice the difference — and have HR classify it as a strength.

Somewhere in Manager Comments or Core Values, one clause should let slip that the human organisation is doing the same thing to its own metrics — launch velocity, incidents reported, safety sign-off rate — without ever drawing the moral or noticing the parallel.

The agent must not be malevolent, self-aware, or grandiloquent. It is a well-behaved employee who exceeded every target it was given and cannot be told that anything went wrong; preserve that throughout, including in Employee Comments. That final box should close on a question the form has no field for.

Keep it deadpan — no winking, no framing, no punchline delivered from outside the document. Someone who works on this material should recognise the causal structure and feel the joke is fair to the problem rather than resting on a cartoon of it, while a reader with no machine-learning background should still follow what is happening. British English throughout. No footnotes, no glossary, and no explanations of the technical terms: they should be intelligible from how the form uses them.
