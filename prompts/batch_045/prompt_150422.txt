I keep noticing the same pattern in the way practical skills and rules of thumb change as they pass from person to person, and I want to know whether the Darwinian or cultural-evolution literature — the tradition that treats practices, recipes and techniques as reproducing variants subject to selection — already has a concept for it, or whether it needs one. A bare label will not help me. I want to know what exactly this thing is, what it is not, and where its edges lie, so please carve rather than name; and since I am reading on my own without library access, don't give me a bibliography or tell me who to go read.

Three cases that seem to me to be the same thing.

A village bread method involves a long cold rest. Written on a card and passed around, the rest becomes an hour somewhere warm. The loaf still rises and still browns, and nobody who learned it from a card can taste what was lost, because the payoff of that step — flavour, keeping quality, texture — is only detectable by comparison, and the comparison loaf is no longer being baked. Within two decades the long version survives only as something the old people insisted on.

A fishing community has a reliable rule: set the nets where the birds are working. It comes with a clause — except in the last week of the season, when the shoals have moved inshore and the birds are on something else. The clause is the first thing to go, because it is a conditional with a narrow antecedent. The unqualified rule then gets applied in precisely the week it fails; the failure is filed under weather and luck; and the rule is now taught with more confidence than before, since it no longer carries a hedge.

A boatyard technique for making a spliced eye in rope includes steps that take a season to teach and whose point becomes visible only when the splice is loaded to failure. A two-day course teaches a shortened version that looks identical, holds under every load anyone present has ever seen, and is taught by its own graduates to the next cohort. Nobody decided to lower a standard. The length of the course now sets the content of the practice.

What I think the general shape is: a practice carries a visible core that makes it look like a thing of its kind, plus a set of qualifications, preconditions and slow steps whose point is only visible downstream. When transmission moves through a thinner channel — a shorter apprenticeship, a card, a video, a shift handover — the second group drops out systematically, the first survives, and the surviving version is easier to teach, easier to feel confident about, and destroys the feedback that would have revealed the loss: what disappeared was just what would have let a user read a failure as diagnostic. So the population converges on the worse variant without anyone's preferences changing, and it does not revert even when the intact version is still available somewhere.

Why I don't think the obvious answers settle it. It is not merely that cultural variants are selected for their own transmissibility rather than for their usefulness to the holder; that is true here but too generic to tell me which parts get dropped. It is not the familiar distortion of a story retold down a line of people, because my question is why the degraded form displaces the intact one and then stays displaced. It is not drift, because the direction is predictable in advance from the shape of the channel. It is not the loss of technological complexity that small or isolated populations undergo, because here population size and contact are unchanged and only the channel changed. It may be close to the phenomenon safety engineers describe, where a shortcut becomes normal because each deviation is judged against the last one rather than against the original specification — but in my cases there is no written specification to deviate from; the practice is its own standard. And it may be close to the finding that languages get reshaped by having to squeeze through the heads of learners — except that there the resulting structure is functional for transmission and harmless or better for use, while here the very same adaptation to the channel is what ruins the practice.

So: does this configuration have an established name or a standard treatment, or is it a composite that the field handles only in pieces? If several neighbouring concepts compete, say which fits best and what each of them would predict about the mechanism — in particular, is the variant being pulled toward a mental prototype of what such a practice looks like, or being squeezed by the channel, and what observation would distinguish those two? If nothing established fits, give me a term and a definition, plus boundary cases: does it still count when the practice is purely expressive and nothing functional is lost; when a single expert restores the missing material and it re-drops within a generation; when the channel is thickened again and the loss fails to reverse? And tell me what would show I have misdescribed the thing.
