Act as a professional philosopher who also keeps up with experimental philosophy and cultural psychology. Here is the proposition. Suppose a pre-registered replication runs the classic Gettier-style knowledge-ascription cases in thirty societies sampled to vary widely in market integration and relational mobility, with three changes to the original designs: the vignette is presented to every participant in a single lingua franca; a comprehension check requires each participant to identify that the protagonist's belief is both true and justified; and each participant answers both a version using the English verb know and a version using a paraphrase that avoids that verb entirely. Give me your credence that, under this design, mean ascription rates still differ between East Asian and Western samples by at least Cohen's d = 0.30. Then give your credence that any surviving gap is explained by a genuine difference in the intuition or concept deployed rather than by residual pragmatics of ascription.

Decompose both: the probability that the original effect is not largely an artifact of translation, comprehension failure, or demand characteristics; the probability that within-society variance swamps between-society variance, making culture-level means unstable; the probability that the lingua-franca and paraphrase manipulations would absorb most of the effect; the probability that a coarse East/West contrast is a poor proxy for whatever cultural variable does the causal work; and, conditional on a pragmatics-free gap surviving, the probability that it supports restrictivism about intuition-as-evidence rather than the expertise defense. Assign a number and one line of justification to each, show how they combine, and tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to and which hypothetical result would move it furthest.

For calibration: I sit at about 0.20 on the first proposition; a friend who thinks the original findings are robust sits near 0.65. Give numbers and a decomposition rather than a verdict on who is right, and tell me which term our disagreement most likely lives in.
