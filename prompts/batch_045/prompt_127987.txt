My opening move. I'll grant you measurement neutrality, and I'll grant that two branches with the same amplitude deserve the same weight. What I won't grant is the fine-graining step: that a branch of squared-amplitude m/N has to be worth exactly m times as much as one of the m sub-branches of squared-amplitude 1/N you could decompose it into. That step is doing all the work — without it the whole one-parameter family w ∝ |amplitude|^{2α} is still standing, because for any α equal amplitudes still get equal weight and the weights still factorise across independent subsystems, so neither of those constraints picks out α = 1 either. And fine-graining isn't a constraint on rationality, it's a thesis about me: it says which branch I end up on has to be a resolution-invariant fact. But self-location looks resolution-relative — at the coarse grain I'm on the one branch, at the fine grain I'm on one of m. So you've smuggled a view about fission and personal identity into a theorem that is advertised as pure decision theory. And if your answer is just that the α-agent recieves the wrong lab frequencies, then the theorem is decorative: we already knew the Born numbers from the lab, and what we wanted from you was a reason why those numbers are what a branching agent should be uncertain about. So which is it?
