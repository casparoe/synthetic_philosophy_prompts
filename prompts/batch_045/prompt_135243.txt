Roughly where I've landed:

(1) You can't appraise a single theory on its own. The unit is the whole research programme over decades: the hard core is protected by convention and you patch the protective belt. Popper-style naive falsification is just wrong about how science works.

(2) The standard is progressive vs degenerating. A programme earns its keep when its positive heuristic keeps leading to novel facts it wasn't built to accommodate; it's degenerating when it only mops up anomolies after the fact.

(3) But you can really only tell those two apart in hindsight. At any given moment "degenerating" and "temporarily stuck on a hard problem" look identical from the inside. So it's rational to keep grinding on something that currently looks degenerating for a long time, and there's no principled deadline.

(4) The reason I prefer Lakatos to Kuhn is that I want science to be rationally appraisable, not just described as mob psychology and gestalt switches. I read (2) as a genuine objective standard a community can be wrong about, not as a post hoc story we tell about whoever won.

(5) I also think it's fine — required, even — that a new programme spends its first years just re-deriving the successes of the programme it's replacing, before it predicts anything new.

(6) And I think the same standard transfers straight to my day job: we have to decide now whether to keep patching an architecture or rewrite it, and "is this thing still letting us ship, or is every change a special case" is the same question as progressive vs degenerating.
