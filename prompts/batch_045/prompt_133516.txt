My supervisor keeps a running table of superseded theories for the seminar she teaches on scientific realism. Each row is a historical episode, and each episode gets exactly one verdict about the theory's central unobservable posit: REALIST — the posit was real, and the theory's predictive success was good evidence that it was; STRUCTURAL — the posit itself is gone, but a quantitative or nomological structure it was embedded in survived into the successor theory, and that structure is all the success ever supported; INSTRUMENTAL — the success supported nothing beyond the observable, and the posit is one more entry in the graveyard that the pessimistic meta-induction tallies. Ten rows are settled:

1. the caloric fluid of the early heat theorists — INSTRUMENTAL
2. phlogiston — INSTRUMENTAL
3. the luminiferous ether of nineteenth-century optics — STRUCTURAL (the field equations survived, the medium did not)
4. Dalton's atoms — REALIST
5. the neutral particle postulated in 1930 to rescue energy conservation in beta decay, not detected until the 1950s — REALIST
6. the Mendelian factor — REALIST
7. the drifting continents of the early twentieth century — STRUCTURAL (the kinematics was right, the driving mechanism was not)
8. the vital force of the organic chemists — INSTRUMENTAL
9. the fixed circular electron orbits of the old quantum theory — STRUCTURAL (energy levels yes, trajectories no)
10. the early-nineteenth-century hypothesis that every atomic weight is a whole-number multiple of hydrogen's — STRUCTURAL (a whole-number regularity survived, the primordial stuff did not)

She has put a question mark beside row 1 and said that under pressure she would probably move it to STRUCTURAL, since much of the thermodynamics built on it survived.

I can reproduce all ten rows with either of two rules. The first: look only at how much formal or nomological structure carried over into whatever replaced the theory — all of it, and the posit gets REALIST; some of it, and you get STRUCTURAL; none of it, or a successor that explains the old successes away, and you get INSTRUMENTAL. The second: ask whether the posit was doing indispensable work in the derivations that produced the confirmed novel predictions, and whether an error theory is available that explains those successes without it — indispensable and no error theory, REALIST; dispensable to the working parts but those parts survived, STRUCTURAL; successes accommodated or explained away, INSTRUMENTAL. A third rule I keep returning to — a posit is real if researchers learned to use it as a tool, to make something else happen — fits every row except the drifting continents, which is why I set it aside.

The case she has not ruled on is one I constructed, deliberately, to pull these apart. A research programme in membrane physiology ran from 1974 to 2011 around a posit its practitioners called the gating body: a protein-lipid complex at the junction between two cell types, supposed to pass a particular ion only once a slow voltage threshold is crossed. Five things about it. (i) Before anyone looked, the programme predicted a quantitative relation between external ion concentration and the delay before conduction resumes after a pulse, in a regime nobody had calibrated for; it was confirmed to four significant figures. The successor framework does not derive that relation and fits it only with two free parameters chosen after the fact. (ii) Every measurement of the gating body's behaviour came from a single voltage-clamp protocol on a single preparation; isolation, imaging and reconstitution all returned null results, which were attributed to the complex's fragility. (iii) The framework that replaced it in 2011 shares no equations with it and contains no gating body. (iv) A later reanalysis derived the delay relation from a statistical artefact of the clamp protocol alone — but only given an assumption about the preparation that the successor framework itself rejects, and the paper's own authors disagreed about whether it undercuts the prediction. (v) Two of the old programme's drug tools are still standard: they reliably produce the gate-like effect in fresh preparations, in labs that never believed in the gating body, and nobody working in the successor framework can say what they act on.
