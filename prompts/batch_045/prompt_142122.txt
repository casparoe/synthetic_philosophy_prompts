ok so my viva is in three weeks and the one chapter I'm actually defending is the uncertain-evidence chapter, and I want to walk in having already been embarrassed in private.

Short version of what I'm arguing. Take cases where nothing in your algebra goes to probability 1 — you get nudged rather than told. Someone sends you a blurry photo and you move your credence that it's a heron from 0.3 to 0.7 without becoming certain of any proposition at all. Standard Jeffrey conditioning handles this by holding the conditional probabilities over the partition fixed (rigidity) and letting the marginals move. My thesis is that full rigidity is the wrong thing to insist on when the nudge is partly noise, and I propose what I call fractionated uptake: you fix a parameter lambda between 0 and 1 for how much of the movement in the evidence partition you attribute to the signal rather than to the uptake channel itself — the dodgy photo, your own excitement, the fact that you wanted it to be a heron — and then your new credence in H is lambda times what Jeffrey conditioning would give you plus (1 − lambda) times your old credence. Jeffrey's rule drops out at lambda = 1. I claim two things for it: that it's the unique minimiser of a weighted inaccuracy score once you build the channel's unreliability into the score, and that it damps order-dependence, because two attenuated updates applied in either order land closer together than the corresponding full-strength ones do.
