Can you explain, at gut level rather than with formal machinery, why the "too many computations" problem is a genuinely hard problem for the view that what matters morally is which computations get implemented, rather than what physical stuff is doing the implementing? I get the first pass: any old system can be described as running all sorts of computations, so you need filters — the system's causal structure has to mirror the computation's transition structure, the counterfactual behaviour has to come out right, it can't just be a lookup table that nails the actual input-output pairs, the grain of description has to be the intended one. What I can't see is why those filters dont just dissolve the problem, and why people who take this seriously end up wanting something like a degree or a measure of implementation instead of a yes/no verdict on whether a given computation is instantiated.

Here's the version of it I keep getting stuck on. Suppose you're running a detailed simulation of someone in agony. At the grain you designed there's exactly one clean implementation. But the hardware is redundant: every step gets computed twice for error correction, a shadow process reads off and copies the main process's states, and the same transition pattern also happens to satisfy a coarser-grained, twice-as-fast read-off. So several implementations of "the same" computation are riding on overlapping physical goings-on — sharing states, sharing hardware. Counting them all gives insane moral arithmetic (is running the sim on fault-tolerant hardware twice as bad?), but picking one at random feels arbitrary.

What I want from you: what makes an implementation the unit you count, is there any non-arbitrary way to handle partial overlap between implementations the way you'd handle partial overlap of two subjects in a brain-fusion case, and does the answer shift if you think the real issue is what we should attribute moral status to rather than what's metaphysically instantiated? And if the honest move is to say moral weight here is graded rather than counted, say that plainly and tell me what it costs the rest of the view.
