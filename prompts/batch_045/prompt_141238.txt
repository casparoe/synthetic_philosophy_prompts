I'm putting together a take-home final for my upper-level seminar on Kant's political thought (were reading his 1795 essay on perpetual peace right now) and I need one essay question that's more then just listing the preliminary articles. I want something that makes students apply his distinction between a federation of free states and a single world state, and also think about how his call to get rid of standing armies hangs together with republican consent. Please build the question around a made-up example I've been calling the Copenhagen Federation: imagine a group of neighboring republics, all with republican constitutions in Kant's sense, that have disbanded their own standing armies but have created a shared civic guard made of rotating citizen-soldiers from each state. This guard can only be sent out by a two-thirds vote of a federal council, and only to enforce collectively agreed environmental rules; punishment is economic sanctions, never invasion. There's no power to topple a member's government. The question should ask: would Kant count this as the peaceful federation of the second definitive article, or does the enforcement power turn it into the kind of lifeless, despotic world state he was worried about? And what role do his preliminary articles against standing armies and against interfering in another state's constitution play in the assessment?

Students have 90 minutes and a 1,000-word limit. Total marks 20. I need the response set out in three titled sections, in this order: 'Question' (roughly 150 words), 'Model answer' (around 750 words), and 'Marking scheme' (around 350 words). In the marking scheme, give five must-hit points, say how many marks each is worth (adds to 20), and after each point add a one-line note on the usual way students mess it up. Don't ask students for citations or a bibliography, and don't reference any real-world treaties I'd need to look up. Make the model answer show real grip on Kant's actual arguments, not just the names of the articles.
