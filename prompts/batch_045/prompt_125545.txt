I'm putting together a one-page research proposal for a small internal grant — six months, one person, no empirical work — on AI paternalism, and I want help getting the framing sharper before I send it off.

My half-formed idea: everyone argues about whether an assistant should refuse a request, but I think "refuse" is doing way too much work. I want to split paternalistic intervention into three kinds — withhold (just don't do it, maybe with an explanation), substitute (do a toned-down version of what was asked), and friction (do exactly what was asked but insert a step the user has to pass through first: a delay, a confirmation, a plain statement of what the assistant thinks the downside is). The usual way of talking treats these as points on a single scale of how much you're blocking the user. I want to argue they differ in kind: withholding changes what the user can do, substitution changes what gets produced, and friction leaves both intact but changes what the user knows or has to own. If that's right, one principle like "respect autonomy" can't rank them, and you'd need separate justifications for each.

Second thing I want in there: the debate is normally framed as current request vs. the user's real or informed interests, but I think the interesting target is diachronic. Some requests, once executed, are ones you can't take back — sending, deleting, committing publicly. So the question isn't does the user want this now, it's closer to: is this the kind of act after which asking the user again would still be a meaningful thing to do?

What I need: turn that into an actual proposal. (a) One precise question I could answer in six months, not "when should assistants be paternalistic." (b) Why the standard moves — appeals to autonomy, to idealized or informed preferences, to third-party harm — don't already settle it. (c) The method, month by month, given that I'm not running a user study and probably shouldn't pretend otherwise. (d) What the deliverable is, something a team actually shipping a product could use rather than just a verdict. (e) What would count as the project failing, and where you think its most likely to collapse. No citations or reading list, I'll add those myself — refer to positions by name if you need to.
