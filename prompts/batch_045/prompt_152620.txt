I work in systems software, and over the last few years I have drifted into holding a strong version of functionalism about mental states, mostly by absorbing the assumptions of the field I work next to. The view, as I hold it: what makes a state a belief, a desire, a fear, or a pain is the causal-computational role it occupies within the total functional organization of the system that has it; which physical states play those roles is in principle irrelevant; neurons, silicon, or some substrate nobody has built yet could realize the same mind, so long as the organization is preserved. I've started relying on this in how I think about machine intelligence, and I'd like to know how exposed it actually is before I lean on it further.

Two things from my own work make me suspect the view is more fragile than its confidence level suggests, and I want them treated as candidate defeaters rather than as curiosities. First: in my field, an implementation is only obliged to reproduce observable behavior. An optimizing compiler may reorganize, delete, reorder, or precompute essentially anything internal provided the observable results match, and whether a deployed artifact and its source count as the same program is settled by a choice of what counts as observable, not by any fact about the hardware. So the question of whether two systems really realize the same organization looks to me like it has no answer until someone picks an abstraction boundary, a state encoding, a mapping between physical states and formal states, and a time-grain — and those look chosen rather than discovered. Second, and related: with that much freedom in the mapping, I have a hard time seeing what would stop almost any sufficiently messy physical system from realizing almost any organization under some encoding. If realizing the same organization is that cheap to establish, then either the multiple-realizability claim is nearly empty, or it is doing its work by smuggling in constraints that the official statement of the view doesn't contain.

There is also a dilemma I keep coming back to and can't sharpen on my own. If the causal role is anchored at the input-output boundary, the view looks like it collapses into something behaviorist, and cannot distinguish a system that computes its answers from one that has them stored. If it is anchored in the fine-grained causal powers of the realizing states, then multiple realizability looks like it fails, because different substrates bring different causal powers with them. I want to know whether there is a stable middle, or whether the view survives by oscillating between the two anchors depending on which objection is in front of it.

What I want from you:

- The full map of what would count against this position, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with an explicit split between defeaters that are merely logically possible and ones that are live right now. Include any that have arguably already occurred.
- For each serious defeater, whether a committed functionalist would themselves concede it as counting against the view, or dismiss it, and whether the dismissal is principled or is a symptom of the view being insulated. I want the insider's concessions, not only the critic's complaints.
- What the view predicts that its rivals — type-identity views, substrate-specific or biological views, behaviorist views — do not predict, including any genuinely empirical prediction. Concretely: what would neuroscience, comparative cognition, or a working AI system have to show for me to conclude that the fine-grained physical details are doing computational work that cannot be factored out into a substrate-neutral role, and how likely is that?
- An honest assessment of whether the freedom to choose a level of description has qualified the position into unfalsifiability. If it has, say plainly whether that is fatal for a thesis of this kind or simply the normal condition of a metaphysical claim, and what the difference would be.
- A stress test of my own candidate defeater, which I'd most like to see defeated or confirmed: the view buys its multiple realizability by making the functional description abstract enough that it stops saying much about minds, and buys back its plausibility by quietly leaning on biological intuitions exactly where the abstraction runs dry. Tell me whether that is a real objection, a restatement of something else, or confusion on my part.
- What I should actually watch for. If I spend the next decade adjacent to this literature, which result should move me, and which result do you predict people would rationalize away?

Please argue it out rather than pointing me at things: name views, arguments, and cases if you need them, but no citations, references, or reading lists, since I have no way to chase them. I have no formal philosophy training, so translate where a term is doing technical work, and don't assume I already know the standard objections — give them. Where my engineering vocabulary supplies a useful analogue, use it.
