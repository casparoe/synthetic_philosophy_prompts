Hey — I'm putting together my actual views on lost causes and national myths for a reading group and I want a consistency check before I stand up and present them. Roughly where I've landed:

1. Admiring a lost cause is a response to a form, not a content: what moves me is steadfastness under hopeless odds, and that is separable from endorsing what the losers were fighting for.
2. But content can cancel it. If the point of the cause was the subjugation or destruction of some group, admiration is off the table entirely — so no, not the Confederacy.
3. I apply what I've been calling the walk-away test: admiration only gets off the ground if quitting was genuinely cheap and available to them, so people who had literally nothing to lose aren't admirable, they were just cornered.
4. I don't care whether the cause was chosen or inherited — an inherited cause is just as good a candidate as a picked one.
5. Mostly what makes lost causes worth caring about for me is evidential: they are the best surviving record of values that have since gone extinct, like a photograph of an animal nobody will see again. I've been calling this the archive view.
6. I'm glad the Confederate statues came down in my city and I'd vote to remove the rest.
7. I'd still feel the pull of a lost cause even if I learned the story was mostly a later literary construction — say it turned out the Masada suicide narrative was basically invented, or the Kosovo legend was assembled centuries after the fact. That wouldn't definately make the feeling irrational to me.
8. But I also think you can't admire people for things they never did — admiration has to answer to what actually happened, and I don't admire a made-up figure the way I do a real one.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? Rank the real tensions by severity, say what would have to give for each and what the cheapest fix costs me, and then tell me which single revision cleans up the most. Be specific about which pairs clash: I'd rather have two tensions traced properly than eight flagged as equally bad.
