Two friends of mine got into it the other night over a third friend's car insurance going up something like 40% after he moved two zip codes over -- same car, no accidents, no tickets, clean record the whole time. I'm paraphrasing but this was roughly the exchange:

(1) Marcus: this is bullshit. He's being charged for other people's bad driving. The only fair thing is to price him on his own history, which is clean, and a clean history should mean a low price.

(2) Dana: there is no such thing as pricing him on his own history alone. Every prediction about an individual has to come from putting him in some class of similar cases and looking at what happened to them, and even his clean record only means something relative to a class -- a spotless five years means something different for a new driver than for someone who's been driving thirty.

(3) Marcus: fine, then narrow the class until it's basically just him -- 34-year-old men, clean record, same car, same neighbourhood, same mileage. Keep going and the arbitrariness washes out; you just need enough data.

(4) Dana: it doesn't wash out, it just gets hidden. You're deciding which features to narrow on, and for any finite pile of data there are endlessly many ways of carving it up that fit everything observed so far and disagree about what happens next. So "the narrowest class that fits him" isn't one thing, and two different carvings can give you two different numbers for the same driver.

(5) Marcus: that's a philosophers parlor trick. In practice everyone knows which features are the causally relevant ones, and if you really thought every carving was as good as every other you'd have to give up on predicting anything at all -- including the prediction that the sun comes up tomorrow.

(6) Dana: right, and that's my point, not yours. Something other than the data is doing the selecting -- causal structure, simplicity, whatever you want to call it -- and you're smuggling it in as common sense. And there's a prior question you keep skipping: is there even a fact of the matter about his risk, or is every probability just a probability-relative-to-some-class, so that asking what his risk "really" is is asking a question with no answer until you say which class?
