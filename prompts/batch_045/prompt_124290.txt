Hi — I'm a science journalist and I'm finally getting round to a long-read I've been pitching for ages on the "what is life?" question. The art desk wants a big graphic in the middle of it, and I want that graphic to be a league table rather than another stock photo of a petri dish, which means I need one defensible number per item and I'd rather not just invent them myself.

To keep it honest I've been using a three-part test that an origin-of-life chemist I interviewed talked me into — her version, not the "self-sustaining chemical system" sort of definition everyone always reaches for. Roughly: (1) SELF-UPKEEP — it stays going by harvesting energy and repairing itself, rather than merely persisting the way a rock persists; (2) HEREDITY — it makes more of itself with something like variation, so a lineage of it could evolve; (3) THE NO-LANDLORD CLAUSE — it does all that with machinery it maintains itself, rather than by moving into somebody else's cell and using theirs. Score each of the following out of 10, where 10 is "obviously alive, argue with me and I'll laugh" and 0 is "obviously not":

- a candle flame
- HeLa cells still growing in a dish decades after the person they came from died
- a mule
- a common cold virus particle drifting about between hosts
- a bacterial endospore
- a Portuguese man o' war
- a frozen embryo that's been in storage for twenty years
- a prion
- a tardigrade dried out into a tun
- a seed in a seed bank
- one of those giant viruses that's bigger than some actual bacteria
- a self-replicating robot of the sort people imagine sending to other star systems (never built, as far as I know)

For each one give me a line on which of the three clauses it passes, fails, or weirdly half-passes, and then the number. Once you've done the twelve, sort the lot into four tiers and tell me where exactly you'd draw the cut-off for "alive", plus which of the three clauses turns out to be doing the real work and which one you'd quietly delete for causing more trouble than it's worth. I'm not after a reading list — no papers to chase, no names to check — I just need to be able to defend the ladder in the comments section when someone writes in furious about viruses. Oh, and my editor has a bet going and insists the chatbot in my phone gets scored too, so if you could slot that in at the bottom with a line on why it lands where it does, I'd be grateful.
