the setup (invented, so nobody can fact-check me): a mid-sized container terminal is being automated. the operating company has put an offer on the table for the dockers' union, which votes on it at an assembly tomorrow morning. the offer: no compulsory redundancies; a 32-hour week at full pay for everyone who stays; a jointly-run transition fund paid out of the savings the automation generates; and — this is the bit that's making people sick — a six-month "knowledge transfer" programme in which the most experienced crane operators and lashers work alongside the engineers building the automated system, with every movement they make recorded as training data. the union also has to drop its clause requiring a crew member in the cab of every gantry. and there's a gainshare provision: the union's cut of the transition fund rises with the measured productivity gains, so the faster automation displaces labour, the richer the union itself gets.

two characters, both marxists, both competent, both genuinely trying to help the union, neither a strawman:

Mara — technology isn't neutral. the machine is the capitalist division of labour turned into steel and code; the skill that used to live in the worker's body gets built into the machine and comes back at him as something alien and commanding; automating with the same technique under any owner reproduces the domination, and "we keep the machines and change the flag" isn't a programme.

Teo — that's the mistake marx kept correcting, fighting the instrument instead of the way it's used. machinery is how relative surplus value gets squeezed, sure, but those same productive forces are also the material condition for a shorter working day, and the struggle over the length of the working day is the real struggle. a 32-hour week at full pay is a conquest, not a bribe.
