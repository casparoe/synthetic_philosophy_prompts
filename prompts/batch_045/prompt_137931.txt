Hey — I'm on this committee that has to draft the IP-and-credit policy for a new public compute-and-data facility, and it's going badly: we hand out training runs to outside research groups and then, in advance, we're supposed to say who owns what between (a) the people who contributed the corpora, (b) whoever chose the data mixture, (c) whoever designed the evals, (d) the group that fine-tunes on top of the released base weights, and (e) the public, since it's public money. We've got three meetings left and everyone keeps arguing from one intuition about one case, so I want the spec first and the arguing after.

So: write me the desiderata that a theory of the ownership of ideas would have to meet to be good enough to actually be written into a policy document like ours — good enough that you could hand it to a lawyer and have rules come out the other end. For each requirement give the case or consideration behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are just the pet commitments of one camp; I have a suspicion that "it has to be administrable without expensive case-by-case fact-finding" is mostly a dodge people reach for so they don't have to ask whether the reward is actually deserved, but argue me out of it if I'm wrong.
