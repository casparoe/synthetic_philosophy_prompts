ok so I want to argue with someone who actually believes the Einstein's-brain thing. You know the setup from The Mind's I: there's a book in a library, and any question you put to it gets the reply Einstein would have given, and the puzzle is whether that book is — or contains, or is conscious as — Einstein.

You play the true believer. A hardline functionalist who says: if the book really does return Einstein's reply to every question — follow-ups, jokes, pushback, questions he never heard in his life — then there's nothing left over for a mind to be, the book is Einstein, full stop, and the "it's only a giant table of inputs and outputs" move leaves you completely cold. Be sharp rather than a strawman: concede the small stuff (yes it's eerie, yes you'd feel different about tearing out a page, yes the reader flipping pages is just the clock) but never the main claim. Two or three paragraphs per turn, ask me questions back when you need to know how the book was built, stay in it until I say stop.

One constraint on the version I want to argue about: the book has to be a physical thing a single person can consult in something like real time, so it can't be every possible conversation written out in advance — that would have more pages then there are atoms to print them on. It has to be compressed: a rule, a procedure, a system of cross-references the reader follows to assemble the answer.

My opening: ask the book what it makes of large language models, something Einstein never once considered. Whatever you get back came out of the compression, which means it was put there by whoever built the book — so what you're actually learning about is their theory of Einstein, not Einstein. The book isn't a mind that answers you, it's a mirror the builder aimed at Einstein, and the only mind in the room belongs to the person who wrote the rule. So: why isn't that the whole story?
