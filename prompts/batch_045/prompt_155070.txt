I've been chewing on Hofstadter's careenium setup for years and I've never managed to settle it: the flat tray full of tiny magnetic balls (symms) careening around and clumping into higher-level structures he calls symmballs, the walls whose bounciness changes with the state of the world outside, and the question the whole apparatus exists to pose — who shoves whom around in there? Do the symmballs, the symbol-level patterns, the one that says I included, actually push the substrate around, or is the symmball story just a coarse-grained way of talking about a great many symm-level collisions that are already fully accounted for by the symms themselves? I came to it through I Am a Strange Loop and only later went back to the Synthese essay whose title asks who shoves whom around inside the careenium (Hofstadter traces the phrasing to Roger Sperry, or so I half-remember), and I think I read it in the wrong order: by the time I met the careenium I'd already been through the Sequences, so my reflexes are reductionist in the same thing described at two levels style, and every time Hofstadter insists the symbol level does real shoving I notice myself reaching for the closure move — every symm event has a sufficient symm-level cause, so the symmball either just is that event or is excluded from doing any additional work. That is Kim's exclusion argument, and I can recite it. What I can't do is make it feel like an answer to Hofstadter's question rather than a refusal to hear it, and I also can't see how the symmball level gets any traction without leaking momentum into the microphysics.

So I want satire instead of an argument. Please write a Field Service Bulletin, roughly 700–800 words, issued by the substrate division of the careenium's manufacturer to owners and operators of the Model C unit, on the subject of reports of substrate drift — the symptom in which symms are observed departing from their predicted trajectories in ways that correlate suspiciously well with symmball-level descriptions. Seed the document with a few devices I've invented for it and feel free to add more in the same register: Form 27-B, on which an operator reports an instance of downward causation and is required to state, in symm-level vocabulary, exactly which symm pushed which; the Non-Duplication Clause, which excludes symmball causation because the symm-level cause is already sufficient and honoring both would double-count; the Identity Rider, which offers to honor a symmball claim if the claimant certifies that the symmball event is numerically identical to some symm event; and the company's ledger, in which every logged drift is reclassified and no claim is ever paid out. The humor has to come from getting the dialectic right rather than from inventing a silly position: coarse-grained kinds appearing in no strict laws, the token-identity escape that restores causation while conceding the high level adds nothing, overdetermination and the exclusion principle, the operator's I filing paperwork on its own behalf. Keep the manufacturer scrupulous, courteous, and correct on every point it asserts — the queasiness should come from its being right. Deadpan throughout: no winking, no closing paragraph that says of course both levels are real, and never let anyone state the objection the reader is supposed to feel.

Commitment: pick one target. I want you to satirize the exclusionist, closure-first side — the view that the symbol level is a description and does no shoving — and to commit to a definite reading of Hofstadter's question, a definite ending for the bulletin, and definite rulings on each claim it adjudicates, rather than listing options. If you think the fairer target is Hofstadter's own downward-causation claim instead, write that piece and say so in the third section; just don't hedge by writing something that makes fun of both. Please structure the response as: (1) The bulletin, 700–800 words, numbered clauses under a proper memo header, ending with one worked example of a denied claim; (2) Notes on the choices, 150–200 words, on which features of the setup are Hofstadter's and which are mine or yours, which reading of who-shoves-whom the piece depends on, and where my memory of the original thought experiment may be off; (3) Is it fair?, 100–150 words, giving the strongest reply available to the side being satirized and saying whether the joke survives it.
