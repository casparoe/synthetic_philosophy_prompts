Setup so we don't talk past each other: finite partition of worlds W, inaccuracy of a credence function c at world w. Brier: B(c,w) = sum over v in W of (c(v) − [v = w])². Log: L(c,w) = −log c(w). Both strictly proper, and as far as I can tell both are equally fine for the expected-inaccuracy argument for conditionalization, since under any strictly proper rule each cell's contribution to the expected score of an update plan is minimized seperately by planning to adopt c(·|E).

Where they come apart, I think, is the accuracy-dominance argument for probabilism. Under Brier every incoherent c has a coherent d that strictly dominates it. Under log I get the opposite for one big class of cases: if c's credences sum to more than 1, nothing coherent can dominate it, because domination would need d(w) ≥ c(w) at every world, hence sum d > 1. (Incoherent functions that sum to less than 1 are fine — something like a normalized version dominates them.) I also checked it isn't just unboundedness doing the work: if you cap the log score at some big finite K the counterexample survives, because it only uses middling credences.

So the culprit looks like truth-locality: log only ever looks at the credence you gave the actual world, so piling on extra credence mass is never penalized anywhere, while Brier penalizes it at every world you're not in.

Here's what I want compared: Brier's wholism versus the log score's truth-locality, as the feature an accuracy-firster should be willing to give up. Two concrete things to chew on:

1. Brier's holism has a price I keep tripping over. Agent A: 0.9 on the true world, 0.1 on one false world. Agent B: 0.9 on the true world, 0.1 smeared across a thousand false worlds. Brier says B is more accurate (≈0.010 vs 0.020); log says they're exactly tied. Is that sensitivity a desideratum or a bug? Should I be bothered that merely fine-graining your possibility space — or hedging your disbelief more finely — buys you accuracy you didn't earn?

2. My current fix: keep log for coherent credences and bolt on a penalty for excess total mass, s(c,w) = −log c(w) + λ·(sum over v of c(v) − 1)², λ around 2. On coherent functions it just is the log score, so strict propriety is untouched, and the (0.5, 0.5, 0.5) case now gets dominated by (1/3, 1/3, 1/3). Compare this to simply going Brier. Is the hybrid cheating — a term that's the same at every world, so it only ever shows up in dominance comparisons and never in expected-accuracy ones? Is λ quietly doing all the work? Does it generalize to incoherent credences that aren't merely over-massed? And does it actually dodge Brier's sensitivity to how you carve up the space of worlds, or just relocate it?
