I'm in the last year of a PhD in political theory, working on social choice, and I sit as the student member of a working party that has to recommend a voting rule for electing the next Head of Division. The electorate is roughly forty members of the Academic Board; there are three shortlisted candidates; the Registrar requires an ordinary preferential ballot — every elector ranks all three, no scores — and has asked for a rule under which no elector can do better by ranking insincerely, and which can be explained on a single slide.

We have accepted that the Registrar cannot be taken literally, for Gibbard–Satterthwaite reasons. What we cannot settle is a rule of our own devising. Call it R: after the ballots are in, one ballot is drawn at random; the candidate placed last on it is struck out; the other two then go to a simple majority vote in which every ballot counts and each elector's relative ranking of the two survivors determines their vote. The argument offered for R is that it escapes the theorem twice over — it randomises, and the only stage involving more than two candidates is the strike-out, where the elector whose ballot is drawn has every reason to name their genuine last choice, since the two-candidate vote that follows is not worth lying to.

I think that argument fails, but I need the memo to show it rather than assert it. Please draft the memo I will circulate: give a concrete profile of sincere preferences — five electors will do — at which some elector does better by misreporting, and state the strategic logic in a sentence that someone on the Board can follow. Then recommend one of the following, with reasons, and give the strongest case for the strongest rival: the uniform random ballot (one ballot drawn, and the winner read straight off it); the alternative vote; the Borda count. Deal honestly with the objection that exploiting R requires knowing how the others will vote, which in a secret ballot of forty is not a realistic prospect. And if you think R can be repaired by some small amendment that does not simply reinstate a dictator, say what the amendment is; if not, say what any repair would have to sacrifice. Aim for about seven hundred words, with the recommendation at the top.
