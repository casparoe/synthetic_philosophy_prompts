I am trying to anticipate how a senior philosopher of science — call her Professor V. — will rule on the next case in a series she has published for several years, in which she takes a paired higher- and lower-level description and asks whether the former has been reduced to the latter, or whether the higher-level feature is genuinely emergent. Her verdicts so far:

- temperature of a dilute gas, vis-à-vis mean molecular kinetic energy: reduced
- sound in a gas, vis-à-vis longitudinal compression waves in molecular motion: reduced
- water, vis-à-vis H₂O: reduced
- the classical gene, vis-à-vis a segment of DNA: not reduced
- critical-point universality — a fluid and a magnet sharing a critical exponent: not reduced
- checkmate, vis-à-vis patterns of neural activity in the players: not reduced

The case now put to her concerns a reported result about when a sheet of epithelial cells behaves like a fluid and when it holds its shape. As the result is described, it has four features. First, across five tissue types whose molecular machinery differs completely, a single quantity measuring average cell geometry predicts the transition, with the same threshold value in all five. Second, in each tissue the same transition can be recovered by a brute-force bottom-up simulation that takes as input only measured molecular and cellular parameters — adhesion binding and unbinding rates, cortical tension, division rate — feeds in no tissue-level quantity, invokes no continuum or thermodynamic limit, and works at the tissue's actual finite size of a few hundred cells; the recovery is only approximate, is sensitive to measurement error in the parameters, and has no closed-form counterpart. Third, the realizers share nothing: there is no molecule or pathway common to the five, the same molecular perturbation shifts the threshold in opposite directions in two of them, and in one tissue that lacks the adhesion molecule identified as the principal driver in the others the simulation still recovers the threshold from that tissue's own parameters. Fourth, confining cells so as to set the geometric quantity directly shifts the transition as the geometric rule predicts, and the shift is not predictable from the molecular parameters alone.

Given her record, will she rule this a reduction or an emergence? I would like the candidate rules that fit her six verdicts set out explicitly, what each of them predicts about this case, which verdict you would bet on and why, and the further case — constructed or actual — that would best separate the rules that survive her record. If you think her record leaves this genuinely open, say which single further stipulation about the case would settle it.
