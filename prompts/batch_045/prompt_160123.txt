Hi — I sit on the collections and repatriation committee at a mid-size regional museum (we cover three counties). We've got a vote at the end of the month and I'm the one who has to circulate the memo beforehand, so please draft it.

The case: about fifteen years ago a road crew cut into a low ridge and exposed a pit; the salvage dig got out 38 socketed bronze axeheads, a handful of iron tools, some pig and cattle bone, and a few sherds. They've been in our storage ever since. A descendants' heritage office has asked for them back under our state's repatriation law, the part covering objects that were communal property (things no individual could sell or give away) and objects made for ceremony. Their claim is that this is a votive deposit: deliberately placed, communal by definition, made for ceremony, so transfer is required rather than discretionary.

Our curator of archaeology says the evidence doesn't support that. His reading is a founder's hoard — metal stored for recycling, or hidden during a rough period and never reclaimed. He points out that the axes are all from the same mold series, several are miscast or too soft to hold an edge, none show hafting wear, and they were stacked in the pit in a way he reads as packing rather than arrangement. The bone, he says, is probably food refuse that came in with the fill.

Then it gets worse in an interesting way. A metals consultant says the soft ones are consistent with having been cast as ingots rather than as tools. And one of our committee members — retired geologist, sharp, no archaeology background — has proposed a scoring sheet: ten or so features (deliberate breakage, placement at a boundary or threshold, no signs of use, association with human remains, repetition across sites, that kind of thing), award points, anything over the threshold counts as ritual and goes back. He built the list by reading published reports of deposits that everyone already agrees are votive.

Two things I want the memo to deal with that I don't think either of them is dealing with. First, our curator has a name for it — the velcro effect: once a pit is filed as ritual, every unusual feature sticks to the ritual reading and nothing counts against it, so the missing hafting wear, the miscasts and the food bone all get reinterpreted as deliberately non-functional offerings. Second, the scoring sheet worries me for the same reason: if the features were drawn from deposits already classified as ritual, aren't we mostly getting out what we put in? I can't tell whether that makes the sheet worthless or just means it needs something added to it. Also: the heritage office knows things that aren't in our evidence rules at all, and I don't want the memo to be sneery about that or mushy about it either. And transfer is basically one-way — once it leaves we don't get it back — so say explicitly whether that asymmetry should do any work in the recommendation, or whether we're supposed to set it aside.

What I need is a memo to the committee, around 800 words, that recommends something we can actually vote on — transfer, refuse, or something in between like a joint research period or a shared stewardship agreement — with a timeline, gives the reasoning, treats the strongest version of the losing side honestly (give the hoard reading its best shot, not a strawman), and proposes a rule we could apply to the next four cases in the queue, all of which are messier than this one.

Last thing: add a short closing section, headed something like what would change this recommendation — the single piece of evidence or argument that would flip you, and where you think the memo's reasoning is most likely to be wrong. Mark your confidence in each load-bearing claim separately, because I suspect some of them are a lot firmer than others.
