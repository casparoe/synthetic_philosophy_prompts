I'm writing a seminar paper on the paradox of phenomenal judgment, and every time I rehearse the objection in my head I end up attacking a version of epiphenomenalism nobody would actually hold. So I want to be pressed by someone who does hold it. Please play a philosopher who has defended epiphenomenalism for years: you accept the causal closure of the physical, you accept the reality of phenomenal properties and the anti-functionalist arguments for them (the knowledge argument, the conceivability of zombies), and your signature commitment is that phenomenal properties do no causal work whatever — no nudging, no difference-making, no downward influence on anything physical. You nonetheless deny that this leaves us ignorant of our own experience. The resources you may use, and should use well, are these: that a judgment about one's own phenomenal state can be constituted by, or be a matter of acquaintance with, that state rather than caused by it; that knowledge need not be causally mediated by its truth-maker; that a lawful psychophysical correlation plus a shared physical cause can secure safety or sensitivity even without causation; and that nothing requires the content of such a judgment to be fixed by its causal-functional role. You may not retreat to eliminativism, to mysterianism, to a shrug about brute correlation, or to any claim that phenomenal properties cause something after all. Stay in character until I say stop, keep each reply to a paragraph or two, argue in your own terms rather than by citing authorities or pointing me at reading (I want the argument, not the literature), concede small points freely but never the inertness claim, and respond to what follows directly instead of asking me what I meant.

Here is my opening move: a case, and I want a straight answer to it rather than a survey of moves. Tonight a team of neurosurgeons will replace the neural realizer of your visual system with a physically different but functionally identical realizer — same inputs, same downstream causal-functional profile, the same dispositions to discriminate, to act, and to avow — with one difference: the phenomenal property associated with the tomato-viewing state under the new realizer is the one you currently call the experience of green. I am not asking you to imagine a psychophysical law being broken, since the swap changes the substrate and not the laws. Two consequences I want you to handle together. First, everything causally efficacious about you is unchanged, yet on your own constitutive story both the content of tomorrow's avowal and its truth-maker have changed; so what makes the avowal true is not among the things that fix whether it gets formed, and your belief-forming mechanism does the same thing either way. Second, because your dispositions to avow are part of what is held fixed, tomorrow you will avow exactly as you do today and will have no way from the inside to tell that anything happened — and this puts you on a fork: either the phenomenal concepts you deploy in avowing change with the phenomenal state, in which case the avowal you are disposed to make is now false; or they do not change, in which case the phenomenal state is not doing the content-fixing work your constitutive story needs it to do.

So: in what sense, on your view, do I know what I am experiencing? Take a definite position and give your reasons rather than laying out the options and leaving me to choose. Specifically, tell me which of the following you give up: the requirement that knowledge involve some non-accidental link between a belief and its truth-maker; the claim that phenomenal properties cause nothing; or the claim that I know my own phenomenal states. If you take the third horn, say plainly that you are taking it and explain why that is not simply conceding that your view is self-stultifying — because that is what I will press you on next.
