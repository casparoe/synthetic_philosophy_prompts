I'm in a small reading group on the foundations of statistical mechanics — three physicists and one philosopher — and we have now spent two sessions on Maxwell's demon and the information-theoretic answer to it without converging. I think we have been talking past each other: one side keeps arguing about where the heat physically goes, the other about what the bookkeeping actually asserts, and nobody has separated those. So I want to put a formulation on the table and get a verdict on it.

Call a bit *standardized* when it sits in a known reference state that a demon can use as the blank side of a reversible measurement, i.e. the measurement is a permutation of the joint state of system plus memory and is therefore costless in principle. My reconstruction — call it Standardization Accounting — has three parts: (i) any demon that operates cyclically at a single temperature and extracts positive net work must consume, per bit of information it acts on, at least one standardized bit and give back at least one unstandardized one; (ii) re-standardizing a bit by coupling it to a bath at temperature T has a thermodynamic floor of kT ln 2; (iii) hence the demon's information-to-work yield is capped at kT ln 2 per bit, no matter what it does with the resulting record — erase it, compress it, defer the reset indefinitely, or write each record onto fresh tape and never touch it again. On this reading the familiar erasure story is not an account of where the compensation happens; it is one way of settling a debt that every demon incurs, and the debt is incurred at standardization rather than at erasure. My physicist friends call this circular or empty: they say it only restates the erasure result in new vocabulary, that the kT ln 2 floor is an identity about logically irreversible operations rather than a piece of physics, that a demon need not traffic in bits at all, and that in any case a demon operating on a finite system is just a large fluctuation and the statistical reading of the second law already dissolves the paradox without any information bookkeeping.

I want a number. Give me your credence that Standardization Accounting is correct as stated in (i)–(iii), and that its cap names the essential reason cyclic single-temperature demons fail — as opposed to the information story being an optional gloss on a paradox the statistical second law already handles. Say briefly what would count as resolution; I would treat a worked cyclic protocol running on equilibrium resources alone and still yielding positive average work as decisive against (i), and a non-question-begging proof that no such protocol exists as decisive for it. Then decompose. The terms I care about: whether the kT ln 2 floor can be established without helping itself to the second law it is deployed to defend; whether the "single temperature" clause is doing hidden work, i.e. whether a demon whose memory is colder than the gas escapes the cap; whether measurement-and-feedback protocols that never perform a many-to-one reset, or that use continuous, correlated or non-binary memories, are covered by (i) or slip through it; and whether the information story is needed at all. Give each term a number and one line of justification, show how they combine, and tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to and what one argument or result would move it most. For calibration: I am around 0.6, one friend around 0.85, another around 0.2 — tell me where you think our disagreement actually lives.
