I'm on my state's Capital Sentence Review Panel and we have to vote Thursday on what to recommend to the governor about Daryl Whitlock. His execution is scheduled in thirty days and the panel's job is to send the governor a short written recomendation: commute the sentence to life without parole, let the execution go forward, or remand for ninety days of further testing. Please draft the decision memo I'll circulate to the other panel members tomorrow morning. Keep it under 550 words and lead with the bottom line.

Here's the case. In 1998 Whitlock was convicted of capital murder for a convenience-store killing that occurred during a robbery. The shooter, Terrence Boyd, later confessed and is serving life without parole; Whitlock was the getaway driver and was convicted under our felony-murder rule, which makes a participant in a robbery liable for a death that happens during it. The main evidence against him at trial was a cellmate who said Whitlock bragged about planning the shooting; that cellmate has since recanted, saying he was high and wanted a deal. A new DNA test on the murder weapon excludes Whitlock, but it's a partial mixture that doesn't exclude Boyd and doesn't point to anyone else.

Last year our panel adopted the Sentinel Standard for these recommendations. We estimate the probability that Whitlock is innocent of the capital crime using a Bayesian innocence ledger; the staff number is 1.8 percent, with an 18 percent chance he was only a driver who didn't know Boyd had a gun and an 80 percent chance he knew and helped plan the robbery. Under the Sentinel Standard, a risk above 3 percent forces commutation, a risk between 1 and 3 percent permits execution only if we find clear and convincing marginal deterrence for similar offenses, and a risk below 1 percent creates a rebuttable presumption that the sentence should stand. The state's consultant says a study of our county's executions shows a 4 percent drop in robbery-homicides afterward, but it isn't statistically significant; the abolitionist consultant says there's no credible marginal deterrence over life without parole and that the drop is just alot of noise.

The memo has to work through retribution, deterrence, and the risk of executing the innocent. On retribution: even if he was just the driver, does the felony-murder conviction mean he deserves death, or is death disproportionate for a non-shooter? On deterrence: does the 4 percent drop count as clear and convincing marginal deterrence, or is it noise? On innocence risk: is a 1.8 percent chance of executing an innocent person inside the band that still allows execution if deterrence is proven, or is any nonzero irreversible risk decisive? I'm personally leaning toward commuting, but I want the memo to reach whichever conclusion the reasons actually support; if that means telling me I'm wrong and letting the execution proceed, say so. Either way, deal honestly with the strongest argument on the losing side -- if you recommend commuting, engage the retributive and deterrence case for execution instead of just dismissing it; if you recommend execution, engage the irreversibility and the recanted testimony. Don't hedge: pick one of the three options, give a time frame for the action, and keep the whole thing under 550 words.
