In our environmental history reading group we have spent a year on a single question and ended where we began: whether germs, climate shifts, and volcanic eruptions can be treated as historical actors. One side points to the fourteenth-century plague and to Tambora and the cold year that followed, and says these belong in the causal ledger on the same footing as statutes and treaties — remove them and the story is no longer the one we tell. The other side replies that each is mediated by social structure: the same epidemic, or the same failed harvest, lands on different property systems, different fiscal states, different trade networks, and produces entirely different outcomes, so treating the bacillus or the ash cloud as an actor is determinism with better manners. Every argument on both sides is now familiar to everyone in the room and nobody has moved.

I have a hypothesis about why, and I want it tested rather than confirmed: that the category itself is doing the work. Non-human cause seems to me to bundle at least three structurally different things — an exogenous shock arriving from outside the system (an eruption, an introduced pathogen); an endogenous process that co-evolves with what people do (a pathogen tracking settlement density and trade routes, climate shaped by land clearance); and a slow background constraint or rate-limiter (growing-season length, monsoon reliability). The determinism objection bites hardest on the third and least on the first, I suspect, and each camp may be generalising from a different exemplar under one word. My second hypothesis is that the real disagreement is about which counterfactual question to ask: whether the outcome would have occurred at all without the factor, how much of the difference it makes, or how recognisable the resulting history would be.

So: is this a verbal dispute about actor and agency, a substantive one about how much weight non-human factors bear, a clash between causal and narrative standards of explanation, a disagreement about which counterfactuals count as evidence, or several of these passing under one heading? Argue for a diagnosis using what each side actually claims, then tell me what would count as progress — a taxonomy, a graded sensitivity criterion, a change of question — or whether, on your diagnosis, no progress should be expected.
