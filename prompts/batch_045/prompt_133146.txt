Setup. I'm on the eng side of a system that splits a shared monthly budget across a few thousand users. Every user files a weekly self-report number, 0 to 10, for how things are going for them. The numbers are on each user's own scale: I have literally no idea whether Ana's 7 is better or worse than Ben's 3, and I suspect in the general case there's no fact of the matter recoverable from the reports alone.

Two new-ish data sources, which is why this is coming up now:

- The ML team shipped a "pair probe": we sample pairs of users, ask a third user who knows both well "who's worse off right now", and we get a noisy ordinal label. About 72% agreement with a held-out heuristic. It only works inside tight clusters of users who actually know each other, so there's no probe path between clusters.
- We can, occasionally and expensively, run a "swap interview": ask one user whether they'd trade their next month for another user's next month, all things considered.

Two known failure modes in the self-reports:

- Some users are what user research calls "adapters": after a permanent bad event their number drops and then climbs back to roughly where it was within a few months. Others ("straight-liners"?) never come back up. I don't want the allocator to systematically defund the adapters just because their readout heals.
- Users know a low number gets them more budget. Some shading is certain.

I'm also told there's a distinction I should care about between lining up everyone's zero point (levels) versus lining up everyone's step size (units), and that a lot of the arguing happens there. Assume I know nothing about it.
