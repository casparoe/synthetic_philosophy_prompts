Hey, I've been chewing on a nastier version of Sleeping Beauty and I cant work out what to think.

Setup reminder so we agree on terms: fair coin on Sunday. Heads, Beauty gets woken once (Monday). Tails, she gets woken Monday and Tuesday, the memory of the first awakening is erased, and she can never tell which awakening she is in. She knows the whole protocol. When she wakes up, what should her credence be that the coin landed heads?

The two things I want you to compare are the principles people appeal to here, not the labels. Call the first the no-news principle: waking up hands Beauty no information she didn't already know in advance that she would receive, and self-locating uncertainty of the which-awakening-am-I-in kind isn't the sort of thing that should move credence about the coin, so she stays at 1/2 and the action all happens in her conditional credences about the day. Call the second the copy-frequency principle: in the long run, across many repeats of the experiment, a third of all awakenings are heads-awakenings, her current experience is in effect a random sample from the awakenings, so 1/3.

Now my variant: heads stays at one awakening. But on tails, before the week starts, the experimenters draw a random N with a genuinely heavy tail — say N = 2^k with probability 2^-k for k = 1, 2, 3, and so on, so N is 2 half the time, 4 a quarter of the time, and so on — and they wake her N times, erasing between each. She is told all this in advance. She wakes up with no idea which awakening she is in.

Please compare the two principles as accounts of what she should believe here, and tell me which one this case actually embarrasses. A few things I keep getting stuck on, go where you like with them:

- The expected number of tails awakenings is infinite, so the copy-frequency reasoning seems to drive her all the way down to credence 0 in heads, even though she knows perfectly well that the coin was fair and had chance 1/2 of heads. Is that a reductio of that principle, or just a bullet it should bite?
- If you cap N at 2^K instead, the copy-frequency answer is roughly 1/(1+K) while the no-news answer sits at 1/2 for every K. So the answer looks discontinuous at the limit. Is the untruncated case simply ill-posed, or does that discontinuity indict something about the finite cases?
- What does each principle say about betting, if a bet on heads is offered at every single awakening? The two seem to come apart in a strange way, with the bettig behavior and the stated credence not lining up.
- Bigger picture: does any of this show that self-locating credence just shouldn't be identified with long-run frequencies of copies, or only that frequencies go haywire once the expected number of copies is infinite?

No references or citations needed, I just want the conceptual argument and a verdict on which principle survives.
