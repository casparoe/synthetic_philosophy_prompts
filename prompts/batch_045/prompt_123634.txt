Our animal-care committee is stuck on a rat-anxiety proposal, and I need the one-page decision memo I’ll circulate before Friday. Quick background: the university’s founding documents say nonhuman animals are machines in the old Cartesian sense -- no thought, no felt experience, just bodies moved by their parts -- so the only suffering we’re supposed to prevent is human distress. The lab wants to give some rats a harmless injection, then a mild but scary stimuli, to see whether the injection reduces fear-like behavior. They argue the rules don’t apply: on the charter’s view the rats’ squeaking and struggling are just the machinery running, not signs of any felt experience. Half the committee says reject it outright because that’s cruel; the other half says we can’t just ignore the founding documents. Write the memo: open with a clear recommendation -- let it run as-is, block it, or allow it with specific added protections -- then give the reasoning. Take the Cartesian side seriously: assume animals have no thought, and even grant that their bodies can show fear, hunger, or anger as mechanical dispositions, rather than just announcing that rats obviously feel pain. Include the strongest thing the researchers or the charter’s defenders would say, and end with a step-by-step next action for the committee.
