I edit the in-house writing guide for a large university's communications office, and we have spent an absurd amount of time arguing about whether our house rules quietly commit us to a substantive theory of truth — mostly because our style macro flags the word "true" and nobody can agree on what, if anything, it contributes. As a going-away gift for a retiring colleague I want to publish one last fake bulletin in the office newsletter, and I would like it to be technically correct enough that the one philosopher of language in our department laughs instead of winces.

Please write that bulletin: a deadpan internal directive from the Office of Editorial Standards announcing that, on the advice of the department's Consulting Semanticist, the university has adopted deflationism about truth as official house style and is discontinuing the adjective. Form: exactly five numbered sections, no introduction and no sign-off, each section between 120 and 150 words, in the voice of genuine administrative prose (clause numbers, cross-references, worked examples, an escalation procedure).

Give the sections roughly these jobs, though you may retitle them: (1) the discontinuation itself — every construction of the form "it is true that p" is to be replaced by p, on the grounds that the equivalence schema exhausts what there is to say about truth, with examples showing the rewrite working and one showing the office embarrassed by it; (2) the approved residual uses, where the predicate is retained because it does expressive work we cannot do otherwise — quantifying over claims nobody can enumerate, endorsing a source's testimony without knowing which claims it contains, rising to talk about a whole position in order to agree or disagree with it at once — and let this exception visibly swallow the rule; (3) the objectives-and-appraisal section, where "produce true beliefs" turns out to be unwritable as a goal because there is no substantive property left for inquiry to aim at, so staff targets must be restated claim by claim; (4) the compliance certificate for "logic-compliant" reasoning, given that validity is standardly explained as preservation of truth, and truth is no longer a property an argument can preserve; (5) the help-desk item for a self-referential sentence submitted by a member of staff — the sentence that says of itself that it is not true — handled by the same disquotation rule, with whatever escalation or workaround the office devises.

Constraints on the humor: every joke should come from an actual commitment of deflationary theories or an actual problem for them (the redundancy/equivalence claim, the expressive and generalizing role of the truth predicate, the question of what makes truth worth pursuing, truth's role in logic and semantics, the paradoxes), exaggerated but never invented or misattributed. Stay deadpan inside the fiction — no footnotes, no asides to the reader, no explanation of the philosophy either in or around the piece. Do not cite or name any real author or work, and do not target any real person; invent your officials, your ticket numbers, and your examples.
