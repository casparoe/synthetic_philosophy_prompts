I keep running into the same shape of decision and I dont have a word for it. Two instances. (1) A friend deciding whether to have a second child: everyone she can ask for advice is already a parent of two, so their reports are produced by the very change she's deciding about, and when it goes badly they say things like "I wouldnt give her up" — which is surely true and also doesn't answer whether the choice was any good for the person who made it. (2) A guy offered a job that, by everyone's account, will turn him into the sort of person who finds that kind of work deeply worthwhile; he can't tell whether that's evidence the job is good for him or just a description of what the job does to whoever takes it.

What bugs me is this. Its not ordinary uncertainty — with ordinary uncertainty I can go get evidence, and the evidence isnt contaminated by the outcome. Its not plain preference drift either: peoples tastes change all the time and we still say they chose badly. The difference seems to be that the only person in a position to judge is the person the decision produced, and that persons verdict is the one piece of evidence the change itself spoils. Someone told me to treat it as an information problem and just gather more testimony, but the testimony is filtered — the people it wrecked mostly dont talk about it, or they cant, because from the inside it doesnt look wrecked.

So: is there an established concept for this — something more exact than adaptive preferences or the hedonic treadmill, both of which Ive been offered and both of which feel wrong for reasons I only half see? If the nearest real concept is one of those, tell me precisely where my case comes apart from it. Either way, carve it: does it stop being this thing if the change is reversible? Does it cover changes you undergo without choosing them? Is the problem that you cant know, or that you cant choose rationally even knowing this much?
