I am in the fourth year of a PhD and putting together an application for a one-year dissertation fellowship, and I want the summary section of the proposal to be sharper than what I have managed to write on my own. The project sits where money pump arguments meet questions of diachronic rationality. My half-formed idea is this: every version of the money pump I have encountered, and every reply to it, quietly assumes that the sequence of offers is known in advance. The pump is presented as a list — give up A for B, then B for C, then C for A plus a cent — and the two standard replies both depend on that knowledge. The sophisticated chooser backward-inducts, which requires a terminal node; the resolute chooser adopts a plan, which requires being able to describe the sequence the plan covers. What I want to study is the case where the agent knows that an experimenter is making offers one at a time, and that after any trade the experimenter may simply stop, with the agent never learning the stopping rule and never able to tell whether the current offer is the last. My suspicion is that in that setting neither of the standard replies is available — there is no terminal node to reason back from, and no single plan that is optimal across every possible stopping point — and that this means the money pump establishes something narrower than it is usually taken to establish: not a constraint on the coherence of preferences as such, but a claim about what happens to an incoherent agent in a sequential decision problem whose horizon is controlled by someone else.

There is a second wrinkle I want folded into the same project, because I think it interacts with the first. Real pumpsters are not costless: every trade they propose costs them something, whether a spread, a fee, or the risk that the agent declines and walks away. If the pumpster must pay per trade, then the stopping rule is not free to run arbitrarily long, and exploitability stops being a yes-or-no property and becomes a magnitude — how much can be extracted from this agent before it stops being worth the pumpster's while? I suspect the magnitude question and the unknown-horizon question are the same question looked at from two sides, and that spelling this out would give the project a deliverable rather than another round of dialectical tidying. For context on how I got here: a reading group of mine actually ran an index-card version of the pump on one of us, and the person being pumped kept accepting trades and then complained that they had never been told how many cards were left, which is what set me off.
