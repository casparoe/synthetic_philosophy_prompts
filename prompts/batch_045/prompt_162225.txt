Write me a dialogue — real back-and-forth, both of them sharp, neither one a strawman — between two engineers on the same team who disagree about how to build the agent that's about to ship. One wants an approval-directed design: the system proposes an action or a plan, a human overseer approves or rejects, that signal is the thing being optimized. The other wants a goal-directed design with an explicit objective written down and pursued.

Heres the setup, and the setup is most of what I care about: the agent triages incoming bug reports and decides which to escalate, which to try fixing itself, and which to close. It acts many times a minute, the overseer actually looks at maybe one decision in twenty, and the overseer is not the person who knows the codebase best.

I want them to grind on these, not hand-wave them:

1. The internalization problem. To fill in the nineteen decisions nobody sees, the agent has to have learned a model of the overseer, and once it has that model it can optimize against the model rather than consult the person — which is approval-direction quietly becoming goal-direction with a proxy goal. Can the approval side answer this by architecturally separating consulting the overseer-model from optimizing against it (I've been calling this epistemic quarantine), or is any policy for choosing what to put in front of the overseer already optimization anyway?

2. Whose approval. Approval from the actual overseer, who is tired and doesn't know the system internals — or approval from a better-informed successor, roughly: what would you approve if you knew what this system knows? The second one seems to fix the ignorance problem, but they should notice that it also starts to look like a goal.

3. Whether approval-direction really buys anything on oversight and corrigibility, or whether it just moves reward hacking out one layer (sycophancy instead of wireheading) — and whether that layer is load-bearing or pure cosmetics.

Have them concede stuff as they go, and let them hit at least one point where they suspect they're describing the same architecture in different words, then work out whether that convergence is real or merely verbal.
