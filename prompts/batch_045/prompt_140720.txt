so I'm putting together a handout for a reading group on Dennett's four rungs of mind (Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian) and I need a case that does some real work, not just another illustration of the tower. What I want is a creature + situation that gets the Popperian *payoff* without being a Popperian creature.

Quick reminder of the distinction I have in mind, so we're working from the same criteria. On the Skinnerian rung, a creature can be shaped within its lifetime — it tries things out and the world rewards or punishes the actual attempt, and the cost of finding out is paid by its own body. The Popperian rung is supposed to be the upgrade where candidate actions get pre-screened inside an inner environment before they're tried out, so that (in the image Dennett borrows from Popper) the hypotheses die in the creature's place instead of the creature dying for its hypotheses. Gregorian creatures, on top of that, import good tricks from outside — tools, words, practices — and get rewired by them.

Here's the build spec:

Design a creature in a situation that clearly reaps the Popperian benefit — the bad options get eliminated without the animal paying the full price for them — while satisfying, on the natural mechanistic reading, the criteria for being merely Skinnerian. Everything it does is shaped by real consequences acting on real behaviour; there's no inner environment running a model of the world, no offline simulation, no representation of the problem as such.

Constraints:

1. Realistic. A real taxon, or a setup a comparative psychologist or field biologist could plausibly actually run. No sci-fi, no engineered AI, no uploads, no telepathy-style coordination. Corvids, capuchins, octopuses, ants, jumping spiders, whatever — but something in this world.
2. The screening has to happen outside the skin. You have to say what does the pre-selecting, and why the thing that absorbs the cost of a bad candidate isn't the animal. Disposable surrogates, half-built artifacts, scaffolds, other animals, modified environments are all allowed.
3. Keep the animal's own flexibility at Skinnerian grade: conditioning is fine, concepts and explicit reasoning are not. If your case only works because the animal already understands the domain, that's cheating.
4. One paragraph for the scenario itself. Everything else goes in separate labelled bits.
5. Don't just stipulate the verdicts. In particular you can't get away with writing "there is no model inside" and moving on — the scenario has to make the absence of an inner model *plausible* and the presence of the Popperian payoff *undeniable*, at the same time. Also tell me what a naive observer would see and why they'd reach for "Popperian" as the label.

Then the demonstration part:

a. Run Dennett's criteria over the case twice — once read as mechanism (what machinery has to be inside the creature) and once read as payoff or function (what the creature gets spared). Give me the verdict for each reading and the reasoning that gets you there. Two readings, two verdicts, and I want to see them actually come apart rather than be declared apart.
b. Name the single feature of the case that's doing the work. Ideally one sentence.
c. Ablate it. Show me what the case collapses into when that feature is removed — plain costly Skinnerian trial and error? genuine Popperian? something else? — and whether the collapse is total or partial.
d. Then the defence: give me at least two things a sympathetic Dennettian would say to keep the taxonomy intact. The obvious ones I can think of are (i) redraw the boundary of the individual — the thing to classify is the extended system, surrogate included, in which case it was Popperian all along; (ii) insist that the payoff was never the criterion, so a case that reproduces the payoff by external means never touched the rung; and maybe (iii) argue the trick was itself a designed good trick, so it belongs on the Darwinian or Gregorian rung rather than being a counterexample to the ordering. Don't just list them — say which one you think is strongest and which is closest to being a verbal move.
e. Short bonus, if you have the energy: does your case also cause trouble for the Gregorian rung? Can a creature import a good trick from outside while remaining merely Skinnerian? Yes or no, and a couple of sentences using your own case, not a new one.

The point of the exercise, in case it helps you calibrate: I want people to see that the taxonomy is specified by mechanism in one place and by payoff in another, and that this isn't harmless. So the failure mode I'm most worried about is a case that just announces the verdicts, or that smuggles intelligence in through the back door via the animal's "understanding". Also flag it if your setup needs a human experimenter to arrange the surrogate — I suspect that matters for whose design work is doing the lifting, but I'd rather you tell me than have it silently built in.
