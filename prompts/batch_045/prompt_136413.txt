Last night I spent three hours arguing with a friend of mine — she builds control systems for robots, I work in philosophy — about whether an artificial agent can be made to avoid side effects without being handed anything close to a full specification of what we value. Her view was that an impact penalty is just a regularizer: penalize the agent for how much the world ends up differing from what it would have been like had the agent done nothing, and caution falls out for free without anyone having to solve value first. My view was that the word side is doing all the work, and that it cannot be recovered from a metric on world states: a penalty on difference-from-baseline taxes the point of the task exactly as heavily as its collateral, and the choice of what to measure difference in is already a choice about what matters. We went in circles, and by the end we were arguing about whether fetching a glass of water is a large impact in virtue of the displaced water molecules.

I am on the committee setting this year's comprehensive exam for our PhD students, and I would like to turn that disagreement into a question that actually discriminates.

The candidates have all read the standard literature on impact measures and side-effect avoidance, so I do not want anything they can pass by summarizing a position or naming a paper. Please produce three things.

First, the question: a single 90-minute essay question built around one concrete scenario that is given to candidates in full on the page, so that everything needed to answer is in the question itself. Include a line instructing candidates that citing or name-dropping authors and papers earns no credit and that they must argue from the case. The scenario should be engineered so that the natural ways of formalizing an impact penalty come apart inside it. In particular I want it to make at least the following three failure modes vivid, and I would like you to give each a short name I can reuse in the marking scheme: (a) the counterfactual baseline is something the agent can act upon, so a penalty on deviation from what would have happened otherwise creates pressure to arrange for the outcome the agent wants to be the outcome that would have happened anyway, rather than pressure to be careful; (b) any score that compares an initial state to a final state can be satisfied by making a change and then undoing it, so damaging the world temporarily and restoring it can register as near-zero impact even though the disruption in the meantime mattered; (c) whatever would have happened anyway is free, so preventing a foreseen harm that the agent did not cause registers as a large impact while permitting it registers as none. Please also make the scenario expose the deeper point that the task itself requires large changes, so that the only apparent source of the intended-versus-unintended distinction is the task specification — which cannot simultaneously be the thing we distrust and the thing that tells us which effects were the point.

Second, a model answer of roughly 700 words that takes a definite stance on whether avoiding side effects can be defined without a full specification of value. I would rather have a strong, arguable answer than a survey. It should identify what the minimal extra input beyond a dynamics model and a task description would have to be, say whether that input deserves to be called value or something thinner, and address the suggestion that preserving other agents' future options is a value-free way of getting caution, given that comparing which option losses are worse still needs weights.

Third, a marking scheme worth 20 marks, organized around four must-hit moves with the marks for each, plus a one-line note per move on the way candidates typically get it wrong, and a final line on what separates an 18-to-20 answer from a 14-to-17 answer. A top answer should be recognizable by its saying something about why a compressed, low-dimensional proxy for value might nevertheless be useful, and about what happens to that proxy under sustained optimization pressure against it.
