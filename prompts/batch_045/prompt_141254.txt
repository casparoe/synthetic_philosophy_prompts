I am building an automated anomaly-triage system for a fleet of fielded devices. Take the fleet to be a hospital network's infusion pumps: a few thousand units, mixed hardware revisions and firmware versions, deployed across sites that differ in ambient conditions and in which consumables they are paired with. When a unit deviates from expected behaviour, the system has to make a routing decision: file the case as a recurrence of a known fault, so the existing runbook and its fix apply, or escalate it as novel, so engineering starts from scratch. In general form that decision is a decision about whether the mechanism producing the deviation is the same mechanism as the one the known fault was written for. Fix the purpose accordingly: a criterion of mechanism-identity is adequate here just in case it supports that routing decision, from the evidence available at triage time (telemetry, event logs, firmware and consumable provenance, and at most a handful of cheap diagnostic interventions), under a strongly asymmetric loss, where a false recurrence that ships a fix which does not work is far worse than a false novelty that costs some engineering hours.

What I want from you is the specification, not a survey. Write the desiderata that any criterion of same mechanism has to meet to be usable for this: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the phenomena it must accommodate, with the case or consideration behind each. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable given the purpose I have fixed and which are preferences of one camp dressed up as requirements; I would rather have three genuine desiderata plus an honest list of prejudices than fifteen items nobody disagrees about. Then identify any pair of requirements you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement excludes the most candidate accounts. That conflict section is the part I most want.

To keep the list honest, here is the docket it has to handle, and I would like each requirement traceable to one of these or to a case of your own construction:

- occlusion alarms fire late on two units. In the first, the cause is pressure-sensor calibration drift. In the second, the sensor is in spec but a stiffer tubing lot changes the pressure profile, so the same downstream pathway is triggered by a different upstream condition.
- a third unit alarms late because a firmware update changed the alarm-threshold constant. The sensor physics is untouched. Does the threshold belong to the mechanism or to its setting?
- a fourth unit alarms late only under a conjunction: high altitude plus a partially occluded line plus a specific pump rate. No single known mechanism covers it and none of the factors alone produces the anomaly.
- in a fifth unit, two independent safety checks would each have caught the fault and both were degraded. Which mechanism is the one, and what does the criterion do when the answer looks overdetermined?
- the runbook for the known fault was written from forty prior cases that shared a signature but, on later review, split into at least two distinct aetiologies. The reference class is not itself a mechanism type.

Four wrinkles I think the standard discussion underweights, and which I want the requirements forced to confront rather than quietly assumed away. First, sameness here is relational and cross-instance: the two mechanisms never shared a spatiotemporal career, so any requirement that tacitly imports diachronic persistence intuitions should be flagged as inapplicable rather than satisfied or violated. Second, mechanisms have logical structure: conjunctive chains and disjunctive or redundant ones behave differently under masking and overdetermination, so the criterion should say whether it returns a single verdict, a set of candidate mechanisms, a graded one, or is permitted to abstain, given the asymmetric loss. Third, the mechanism/environment boundary is itself at issue, since some mechanisms only close their feedback loop under conditions the environment supplies, and a case can sit exactly on the boundary. Fourth, drift and part-substitution are continuous: the criterion owes an account of how much parameter drift or component replacement re-identification survives, whether that is a threshold on some similarity metric, and if so whose, or whether identity is primitive and these cases are simply vague.

Finally, and briefly, score four candidate families against the list: identity of observable signature, causal or spatiotemporal continuity, sameness of parts plus organization, and sameness of counterfactual profile under interventions. A short table is fine and I will weight it lightly; the criteria are the deliverable. Do not give me citations, bibliographies, or reading lists, and do not lean on how any named author put things: argue from the cases.
