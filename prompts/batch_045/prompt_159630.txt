I am writing a seminar paper on Turing's treatment of the extrasensory-perception objection in his 1950 paper Computing Machinery and Intelligence, and I have gotten stuck on an argument one of my classmates built from it. To make the issue precise, we defined a variant of the imitation game called the Shielded Duo Game. In it, the machine and the human are placed in separate rooms that are psi-shielded, in the sense that no physical influence of any kind can escape from either room; the only permitted channel of communication is a teleprinter link that carries the interrogator's questions and the players' typed replies. We also helped ourselves to a piece of terminology: call a player's output over the whole game its teleprinter profile, and call any evidence that does not supervene on that profile non-behavioral evidence. The reconstruction of Turing's reply that our seminar is using says that the psi-shielding is supposed to neutralize telepathy, clairvoyance, and precognition, because the interrogator is then left with nothing but the teleprinter profile, and over that channel the machine can in principle imitate the human perfectly. My classmate grants all this and then argues that the reply, and with it any purely behavioral criterion for mind, is in far worse shape than it looks.

The argument is as follows. (1) In the Shielded Duo Game, the interrogator's only channel to the hidden players is the teleprinter, and no physical influence escapes the rooms. (2) For the game to be a fair test of behavioral criteria, the interrogator must not be able to use any evidence beyond the teleprinter profile. (3) If extrasensory perception is possible, then by definition it can give an interrogator evidence about a hidden player without any physical influence from that player's room. (4) Psi-shielding blocks physical influence, but it does not block ESP, precisely because ESP is supposed to be a non-physical mode of information acquisition. (5) Therefore, if ESP is possible, the interrogator could acquire evidence about the hidden players even in the Shielded Duo Game: for instance, by telepathically detecting the human's occurrent thoughts, or by clairvoyantly perceiving the machine's internal physical configuration and thereby identifying it as a computer rather than a brain. (6) Such ESP-derived evidence would be non-behavioral evidence, because it does not consist in or depend on the teleprinter profile. (7) Hence, if ESP is possible, the Shielded Duo Game is not a purely behavioral test: an interrogator with ESP could tell which player is the human and which is the machine independently of any behavior. (8) If a non-behavioral route to that identification is available, then behavioral indistinguishability is no longer sufficient, or even necessary, for the attribution of mind, and the whole rationale of a behavior-based test collapses. (9) Therefore, if ESP is even metaphysically possible, no purely behavioral criterion for mind can be correct; behavioral tests are at best a provisional fallback for observers who happen to lack ESP. This conclusion seems to prove far too much. It would force anyone who takes ESP seriously to abandon the Turing test, and it would imply that a machine which behaves exactly like a human for an interrogator's entire lifetime could still be correctly dismissed as mindless by any telepathic observer.

I would most like to keep the claim that the Shielded Duo Game really is behavioral when ESP is left out of the picture, and the claim that ESP, if it is possible, is a non-physical mode of acquisition; those two claims seem central to Turing's setup and to the dialectic. I do not want to accept the collapse of behavioral criteria, and I cannot find the exit. Is the argument valid, so that I must give up one of the premises anyway—and if so, which premise is least costly to reject, and what does rejecting it cost my overall view of the imitation game and of ESP? Or is there a subtle mistake somewhere in the chain, and if so, which step do you reject, why did that step look fine, and what does each of the main alternatives cost someone who wants to preserve a behavioral criterion? Please name the exact step and price the alternatives rather than just telling me which side to take.
