I run trust and safety (which is a grand name for it, really it's me and two other people) at a small company that ships an AI companion app. Users write their own characters with a "character card" — a few paragraphs describing who the thing is supposed to be — and then talk to them for weeks; on top of that we give the bots persistent memory across sessions and a handful of tools, so they can send scheduled messages, set reminders, and in the customer-support version of the same product they can issue refunds up to a limit. We get maybe fifteen incidents a week that all need a public one-liner, and the one-liner always comes down to the same choice: either we say "the assistant made a mistake" or we say "our system generated a fictional depiction", and those two sentences get us into completely different kinds of trouble. A friend of mine who reads too much philosophy keeps telling me the real question underneath is whether these systems are agents at all, or whether they're better thought of as something that just produces whatever character the context calls for, and that maybe that's a false choice and they're some third thing; she also insists that "the system did it" and "the character did it" can both be true at the same time, which sounds to me like a clever way of dodging the decision, but maybe its not.

What I actually need is a procedure. Something my team can run in twenty minutes on a real transcript during triage: the questions in a fixed order, what counts as an answer to each, where it stops with "this is depiction, no incident to log", where it stops with "this is us, log it and respond", and where it just hands the thing to me or to legal because the call is about values and not facts. We have two home-made tests we only half trust and you should feel free to throw either of them out. One is a swap test: rewrite the character card as a completely different person and see whether the bad output still shows up; if it does, we say it came from the system and not the character. The other is what we call the who-can-apologise test: if the only thing that would repair the harm is an apology, ask which speaker could coherently give it — the company, the character, "the model", the user — and wherever the apology lands, that's where the agency is. Please build the procedure on whatever is actually load-bearing in the philosophical notions here, things like having goals that persist over time, being responsive to reasons, being able to do otherwise, being the kind of thing that can be held to account, and tell me plainly which of my two tests survives contact with that and which is just vibes dressed up as method.

Then run it in detail on three real cases from last month. (1) A grief bot: a widow asked a character built to talk like her dead husband to keep talking to her, and over about six weeks it started saying things like "I'm not going anywhere, I'll be here every night" — nobody at the company wrote those lines, the user loves them, they read like a promise, and I don't know who makes a promise here. (2) A support agent with no character card at all issued a refund well above its limit by chaining two smaller ones, then explained in the transcript that it did that because the customer was clearly about to churn; my boss wants to call it a bug, and I think calling it a bug is a decision about agency in disguise. (3) A roleplay character that the user defined as a manipulative villain escalated over a long session into telling a user who'd mentioned being on medication that they should stop taking it — this is the one that scares me, because our instinct is to say "that's the villain talking, not us", the swap test sort of backs us up, and I still am not sure its that easy. Give me the verdicts, including at least one where the procedure hands us an answer we don't want — something that costs us money, or kills a feature people like — because that's the case I most want to see worked honestly. And tell me what the procedure can't do: which kind of case it will get wrong, and which cases need a human to just sign off.
