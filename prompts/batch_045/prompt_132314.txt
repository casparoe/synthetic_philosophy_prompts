me and a friend had a three hour argument last night and I don't want you to tell me who won, I want you to take apart the thing his whole case was resting on.

The topic was whether stuff outside your head can literally be part of your mind. You know the famous case: guy with early onset dementia, writes everything down in a notebook, carries it everywhere, consults it automatically and trusts it without checking. My friends entire argument was one analogy. An external memory store, he said, is just a prosthetic. Hip replacement, cochlear implant, prosthetic arm. Nobody walks around saying that's not part of your body, that's just a tool you happen to be using. So the boundary isn't the skin and it isn't the skull, it's wherever the causal work actually gets done, and the prosthesis case shows we never really believed otherwise. Then he ran it further: if the notebook is a prosthesis then so is the phone with my photos, my contacts, my maps, my whole correspondence and my calendar in it, so the phone isn't something you have, it's part of what you are, and having it taken away isn't property loss, it's a kind of cognitive damage. That last bit was the conclusion he actually cared about.

I made up four cases to poke at it and we couldnt agree what the analogy says about any of them.

1. A cochlear implant. Surgically in, always on, nothing you consult, no deliberation about whether to use it.
2. A memory device on your belt that talks wirelessly to something implanted, but only fires when you do a deliberate thing first, like press two fingers to your temple, and then the answer just arrives in words you didn't compose and didn't choose to look up.
3. The notebook. Ordinary, public, in your own handwriting, sometimes just wrong about what you did.
4. The one he hated: a chip inside the skull that stores your memories and feeds them to the rest of the brain when needed, except the person has zero experience of it. No feeling of recalling, no imagery, no sense of familiarity. They just start saying true things they have no idea how they know. It's inside the head and its doing the memory job. Is it part of their mind or not?

What I want from you: lay out what maps onto what in the prosthesis picture. The limb, the function that went missing, the socket or the nerve interface, the body that heals around the thing and keeps it working, the user who has to learn to drive it, the person who fits and adjusts it. Then where the mapping breaks. I care most about two things. First, a prosthesis usually replaces a capacity the body had and lost, whereas the phone mostly adds capacities you never had: does that difference cut against the conclusion he wanted, or is it irrelevant to it? Second, the move from part of the body to part of the mind: is the analogy quietly paying for that step, or does it actually support it? Also tell me which of the features of a prosthesis the notebook lacks that matter here, and which don't matter (its public, anyone can read it, someone else could edit it, it can be wrong about you, it doesn't get maintained by anything except you).

Then the verdict: does the conclusion about the phone survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the prosthesis image carrying it the whole time?
