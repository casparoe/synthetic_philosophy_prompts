hey — I'm putting together a handout for the last session of a reading group on Scanlon-style contractualism and the aggregation problem, and I want a "which aggregator are you actually?" quiz to open with. Everyone's read the stuff already and knows the labels, so it has to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which name they'd apply to themselves — no question should ask anything like "do numbers matter?" straight out.

What I want: 10–12 items, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto leaves. Build it from the logical space first — the three or four branch questions whose answer combinations generate the cells (something like: do numbers ever add across people, do they add within a single life, do mere chances of harm add, does severity ever lexically trump numbers) — then locate the actual positions in that space, and for the empty cells say briefly why nobody's there (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored).

Leaves I want represented: full inter-personal aggregation; aggregation for small chances but not for actual small burdens; numbers count only as a tie-breaker when no one has a stronger complaint; aggregation within a life but never between lives; severity-first/lexical priority; a threshold view where numbers only count once everyone's clear of some floor; the strict no-aggregation/incomparabilist spot; and a person-affecting-style restriction. Feel free to add a leaf you think is missing, or mark one "unnamed."

Cases I definitely want in there: a huge crowd each spared a barely-noticeable itch vs. one person spared a broken wrist; the same tradeoff run inside one person's life instead of across people; a policy that hands each of ten thousand people a 1-in-10,000 risk of serious injury vs. one identified person getting hurt for sure; relieving a burden vs. handing out an equivalent benefit; and a spectrum pair where one person loses a limb and n people each lose a bit of a fingertip, with n climbing across the options. Please also invent at least two cases whose whole job is to pry apart two leaves the others can't separate — I especially want something that splits "chances add" from "small actual burdens add," and a repeated-choice case where the same tradeoff comes up five times.

Two more things: flag any pair of leaves your instrument genuinely can't tell apart rather than pretending the key sorts them, and close with a short note on which leaf your scoring key is least confident about, which question does the least work, and what pattern of answers would convince you there's a position your tree has no room for. No citations or reading list needed.
