I've drifted into defending pluralism about biological function: the view that there isn't one dispute here, just two concepts that both earn there keep -- selected-effect function doing the work in evolutionary explanation, causal-role function doing the work in physiology, medicine and anything anybody builds -- and that when they seem to clash it's basically verbal. What would count against that? I don't want the stock objections to each half separately (Swampmanish cases for the etiological side, hearts making thumping noises for the causal-role side); I can recite those. I want defeaters for the pluralism itself. Two things I keep getting stuck on. First, my "orphan contribution" case: a trait reliably produces effect E, E really does figure in a legitimate decomposition of some containing system, but nobody has any explanatory interest in that system, and the trait wasn't selected for E either, so it's orphaned on both sides -- my view looks committed either to silence or to handing out function attributions so freely that they mean nothing, and I can't tell which is worse for me. Second, the collapse worry: if the two concepts agree on the verdict in every case anyone actually cares about, pluralism has bought nothing except permission to relabel every disagreement as verbal, which smells like the view has been qualified into never being wrong. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about which ones a pluralist should concede rather than explain away. The thing that would move me most is a case where the two concepts come apart and the pluralist's way of handling it is clearly the worse explanation, not merely a different one.
