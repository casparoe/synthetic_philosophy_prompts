**Draft: "Don't make a mess" — why "low impact" keeps smuggling in a theory of value**

**1. The job.** The constraint is meant to be a patch for reward misspecification. If we could specify what we wanted, we wouldn't need it. So it has to be *value-free on credit*: no utility function over outcomes up front.

**2. What any such measure needs.** As far as I can tell, three ingredients: (i) a *baseline* — impact relative to which counterfactual? (ii) a *state metric* — how far apart are two worlds? (iii) a *scope* — what horizon, what spatial region, what counts as "the world"? My thesis is that each of (i)–(iii) is a place where value gets smuggled back in, and that the interesting question isn't "can we avoid smuggling" (we can't) but "which door smuggles least."

**3. The inaction baseline is not neutral.** The obvious baseline is the world where the agent never acted. Two problems. First, it is symmetric between causing and preventing: a robot that catches a falling tray is penalized exactly as much as one that knocks it over, because in both cases the world diverges from the world where it sat still. But catching it is the thing you want. Second, it charges the agent for the task itself whenever the task is supposed to change anything, so you need a task-exemption, and deciding what is task-relevant is deciding what matters.

**4. The metric is where the smuggling is most visible.** "How different is this world from that one?" has no answer until you say which differences count. A metric over the positions of macro-objects says moving my mug is a large change; one over thermodynamic entropy says it is noise; one over what any person could later notice says a third thing. Choosing a metric is choosing a weighting over features of the world, and a weighting over features is most of a value function. I read the attainable-utility-preservation (AUP) style proposals as an attempt to get the metric for free from the environment: don't ask how far the world moved, ask how the agent's ability to achieve a spread of other goals changed.

**5. Where I'm shakiest.** If what you preserve is attainable utility measured against an inaction baseline, then changes caused by *other* agents count too — including the human switching you off. I've heard people conclude that this makes such agents conservatively freeze the world to keep the ledger flat. I genuinely don't know whether that's a real feature of the proposals or a straw man I built in a reading-group discussion, and I'd rather be told it's wrong than defend it.

**6. My alternative: the option ledger.** Don't measure change in the world, and don't measure change in the agent's abilities. Measure the change in the *principal's reachable set*: the set of world states the human could still bring about after the agent acts, given the human's own capacities. Impact is how much that set shrinks — and note it's counted by inclusion, not by a metric: a state is either still reachable or it isn't, so no weighting over outcome features is needed. This is asymmetric in the direction we wanted: the robot that catches the tray *preserves* grandma's option to have an intact tray and so isn't charged, while the one that knocks it over deletes that option. It also needs no counterfactual baseline, since you compare the principal's reachable set before and after.

**7. Why it still isn't free.** Two costs. (a) You need a model of the principal's capacities, and "capacities" has to be spelled out: with what tools, over what time, with what information? Smaller ask than a utility function, but not nothing. (b) *Option spam*: if impact is shrinkage of the reachable set, then an agent wanting impact-budget can hand the principal a million new junk options — leave a pile of objects on the table, unlock a thousand accounts — and pay for real damage with additions elsewhere. Blocking spam requires a measure on options (how many? how good?), which is a weighting over outcomes again. So the value-shaped ingredient doesn't disappear; it relocates from "which features of the world matter" to "which of the principal's options matter." My honest conclusion: "avoid side effects" cannot be defined with zero value-content, but the reachable-set version moves that content somewhere we could in principle read off the environment and the principal rather than hand-code, and maybe that difference matters more than the impossibility sounds.
