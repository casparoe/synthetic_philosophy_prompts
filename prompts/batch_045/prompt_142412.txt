I've been living in Berlin for about six years now — American, grew up in the midwest, read too many rationalist blogs, so take the following as a hunch with a lot of motivated reasoning behind it. I've come around to something like: the German-ish folk instinct — there's a rule, the rule came out of some process, you follow it and you don't get to substitute your own on-the-spot judgment about what the rule is really for — beats the American-ish one, where an individual conscience saying "this rule is wrong here" gets treated as the final authority. Not as a claim about where moral worth comes from; I'm not making any Kantian move like that. My case is epistemic and institutional: conscience is a bad instrument at the moment of action (self-serving, calibrated to whatever your peers are outraged about this year, and impossible to aggregate), whereas rules that come out of a slow process encode information no single person has, and what a rule-following culture really buys you is that nobody has to win an argument about who the good guy is before anyone does anything.

What would count against this? I want a map of my exposure, not a list of objections. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and split them into ones someone who already holds my view should concede genuinely count against it versus ones I'm entitled to shrug off as attacking a caricature of the rule-following norm — I think the obvious historical case everyone reaches for does less work than they think, since plenty of rule-first institutions also had an explicit culture of official discretion, and arguably it was the discretion that did the damage. Include the non-anecdotal stuff too: what empirical findings — about whistleblowing rates, about how fast bad rules actually get revised in the two kinds of culture, about whether conscientious refusal is really the only error signal a rule-maker gets — would settle it, and which of those we could actually get evidence about rather than just gesture at. Its the strongest argument against me and I don't have a good answer, so please engage it directly: in a rule-first culture the only feedback the rule-maker receives is a violation, so a culture that stigmatizes refusal is flying blind. And tell me honestly whether my view has enough escape hatches ("obviously you should follow the rule unless it's genuinely egregious") that it's been qualified into something unfalsifiable — and if it has, whether for a view like this that's a bug or fine.
