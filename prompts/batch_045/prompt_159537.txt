Me and two friends have this argument that just won't die. It started because I'm putting together a little self-tracking thing for myself (nothing fancy, basically a daily slider) and I wanted one section of it to be "how much pleasure today" — and then I realized I have no idea whether that question even makes sense. One friend says every pleasure has this same basic feel underneath it, a kind of glow or warmth that's the same stuff whether it comes from coffee, a song you love, or sex, and that's why you can put them all on one scale. Another friend says no, the feelings are completely different each time and the only thing pleasures share is that you're drawn to them and want them to keep going. The third one says there's nothing in common at all and "pleasure" is just a word we stick on a grab-bag of unrelated nice states, the way "games" or "furniture" doesn't pick out one shared ingredient. Someone online told me the serious versions of these have names like felt-quality views, attitudinal views, and family-resemblance or eliminativist views, but I dont want a reading list or a pile of names and papers — I'll never read them. I want something I can actually run my friends through.

So please build me a sorting instrument — a decision tree or a quiz, whatever fits better — that takes someone from concrete cases to a named position on what, if anything, all pleasures have in common. Build it out of the logical space first, not out of a list of famous views: lay out the questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then place the positions people actually defend into that space as leaves, tell me which combinations are empty and why nobody lives there (incoherent? just unattractive? merely unexplored?), and flag any two leaves your questions genuinely can't separate instead of pretending they're separated. The splits I care about are: whether the shared thing is something you can feel (a quality or tone), or something you do (an attitude, an endorsement, a wanting), or a job the state does in the mind (it reinforces learning, it grabs attention, the brain treats it as a reward), or whether there's no shared thing at all; and separately, whether the shared thing has to be findable by introspection or could be something only a theory tells you about, and whether pleasures really are comparable on a single scale across different senses and across different people (my app sort of needs that last one, so I want to know if it's a load-bearing assumption).

Give me around eight questions, each one a plain-language case with two or three answer options, no jargon unless you explain the term right there in the question, and phrased so my friends can answer without having any idea which view each answer leads to — the whole point is to sort them by what they say, not by which label sounds nicer. Please do build the questions around these cases, because I think they're where we actually come apart: (1) the intense relief of finally scratching a mosquito bite or getting out of overheating — my "positive feel" friend insists that's the same glow, my "wanting" friend says it's just the itch stoping; (2) a machine, call it the rotator, that for a while swaps what your enjoyment subjectively tastes like while leaving exactly how much you want it to continue untouched, plus another setting that does the reverse, changing the pull but not the feel — which setting, if either, decides whether you count as pleased at all; (3) a peak experience you'd call deeply enjoyable and are glad happened, but which you never want to repeat and which came with no pull for more of it even while it was going on; (4) hostile or mixed pleasures, like the fun of a scary film, a revenge daydream, or food that's good partly because it burns — where something you'd otherwise call unpleasant seems to be part of what makes it good; (5) comparing how much pleasure there is in cold water after a run versus finally getting a proof versus hearing one chord resolve, and then comparing your number to somebody else's. For each leaf give it a name if it has one (or say "unnamed as far as I know") plus one sentence on the most awkward thing it commits you to, and add a scoring key from answer patterns to leaves — and if it turns out two of my friends are standing in the same spot on the map even though they'd never admit it, say so.
