I am finishing a dissertation in philosophy and in three weeks I am giving a job talk at a department I would very much like to join, and I would like you to build me the Q&A preparation sheet I would otherwise have to write badly myself the night before. The talk defends a version of mathematical nominalism that I call calibration nominalism, and I will give you its four theses so that the objections you generate have something concrete to bite on. (T1) Semantics: mathematical sentences are not descriptions of a domain of abstract objects; they are rules for manipulating a notation, their correctness conditions are given by derivability in a proof system, and mathematical truth is therefore always truth relative to a system, with the appearance of a system-independent mathematical truth explained by our practice of extending systems and comparing them. (T2) Applicability: what makes a piece of mathematics usable in an empirical domain is that its syntax is calibrated to that domain, in the sense that there is a homomorphism-like mapping from numerals, operations, and derivations onto physical items and operations (piles and their concatenation, pointer readings, the state transitions of a computing machine) such that the theorems of the system project onto real regularities; this mapping is causally mediated all the way down, because we build the abaci, the meters, and the processors so that it holds. (T3) The dialectical move I am most attached to: platonists say the nominalist faces a latching problem symmetric to the Benacerraf-style worry about epistemic access to abstracta, and I reply that my correlation is of exactly the kind we already accept for measuring instruments and is causally mediated, whereas theirs is a brute correlation with a causally inert realm, so the two problems are not symmetric after all. (T4) The thesis I expect to be attacked hardest: unlike Field-style nominalization programs, I do not try to rewrite a whole scientific theory without mathematical vocabulary; I claim only that every actual application rests on a surveyable core, meaning a finite fragment whose derivations a finite agent can check step by step without appeal to completed infinities, and I argue that the usual hard cases, among them infinitary analysis and differential equations in classical mechanics, vector spaces of uncountable dimension in quantum mechanics, and the group-theoretic classification of crystal symmetries, each have such a core. I already know the obvious cost of T4: it invites the charge that I am an instrumentist about pure mathematics with a deflationary footnote attached, and my supervisor made exactly that charge during my practice talk, in front of an audience that laughed in the right place.
