I am drafting the normative specification for a long-lived planning system: an artificial agent that acts over an extended horizon, whose objective function may legitimately be revised by its operators mid-run, that shares its environment with other agents who can observe its dispositions and offer it trades, and whose behaviour has to be auditable after the fact by a party who sees only the record of offers made and choices taken — not the agent's internal states, and not the menus that were never presented.

What I need from you first is not a theory but a specification of adequacy for one: the list of things a theory of diachronic rationality would have to deliver in order to serve as that core. Write the desiderata, with the case or consideration behind each, and say which are non-negotiable given the purpose and which are preferences of a particular camp that I am free to reject.

Begin by fixing the unit of evaluation. A theory in this area may be a theory of acts at nodes, of plans, of policies, of whole sequences of choice, or of dispositions to accept menus; it should be a requirement that the theory state which of these it is a theory of, and that the unit be one the agent can actually instantiate and an auditor can identify.

At minimum the list should say something about the following cases, and I suspect each of them forces a distinction the list will collapse by default:

The stepwise pump. At every node the trade on offer is beneficial by the agent's own lights at that node; the sequence returns it to its starting point minus a fee. Which norm has failed — the local one or the global one — and must the theory be able to say both that the agent did nothing wrong at any node and that it has been exploited?

Pumpability versus being pumped. There are structures that admit an exploiting sequence no one would ever run, because running it costs the exploiter more than it gains, and structures no one can run because exploiting them requires perfect prediction of the agent. Is the requirement the absence of pumps, or the absence of profitable and feasible ones?

The near-indifference pump. An agent willing to trade when nearly indifferent can be bled by arbitrarily small fees; an agent that never trades near indifference forgoes the gains from trade entirely. The requirement must not be satisfiable only by total abstention from trade, and must not be knife-edge in a way that a little noise turns into a violation.

Correction versus drift. The operators revise the objective at t2, and the t1 self foresees this. Deferring to a later self that will know more looks like rationality; deferring to a later self that merely wants something different looks like being exploited by your own future. The theory must say when anticipatory deference to a future self is rational and when it is weakness of will at one remove, and it must not answer by simply letting the later self win or by simply letting the earlier one bind.

Paying to bind. The agent pays to delete its own future options, by removing a menu or buying a commitment device. Is this self-exploitation, prudence, or both? The theory has to classify it, and must not treat every removal of an option as a cost incurred.

Dispositions that manufacture pumps. Keeping plans, keeping agreements, and being predictable are plausibly things such a system ought to be; each of them makes it pumpable by an adversary who elicits the reliable response and then trades against it. If the exploitability criterion convicts these, is that a finding about the dispositions or a reductio of the criterion?

The noise pump. An agent with stable, transitive, menu-independent preferences, shown the same small set of trades in randomized order with minor presentation jitter, pays to end where it began. If the criterion convicts this agent, the criterion is too strong; say precisely what restriction repairs it.

Auditability. Exploitability is a property of a counterfactual structure defined over menus that were never offered. Say whether the norm is checkable from a record of actual offers and choices, and what I should do if the best account of the defect turns out to be unauditable in principle.

The reconsideration regress. If the norm is adopt a plan and follow it, when may the agent reopen the plan, and why does permitting reopening under specified conditions not simply reproduce the original problem one level up?

Then the part I most want. Mark which desiderata are jointly unsatisfiable — I expect at least that local optimality at every node, the prohibition on self-defeating sequences, and permission for rational preference change cannot all be held, and that a purely structural criterion of exploitability cannot be reconciled with third-party auditability — and show me, for each conflict, which desideratum you would drop and what the resulting theory can no longer do. Tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding the standard families of view, and flag any requirement that is really a prejudice of one family rather than a desideratum: the demand that consistency matter rather than getting what you wanted, for instance, or the demand that rationality be a property of preferences rather than of policies.

A short scorecard at the end — sophisticated planning, resolute choice, moment-by-moment or time-slice views, preferences over plans, and any bargaining-across-selves picture, one or two lines each against the list — is welcome, but keep the weight on the criteria and on the cases that motivate them; the criteria are the deliverable, and I will do the scoring myself if they turn out to be the hard part.
