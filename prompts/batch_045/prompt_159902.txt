hey — I've been chewing on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument for a while and I think I've got a repair that I dont see anyone actually making. I want a number on it and I want to see where the number comes from.

Setup so we're using words the same way. The argument: (1) you do what you do in the circumstances because of the way you are; (2) to be truly morally responsible for what you do you have to be truly responsible for the way you are, at least in the mental respects that matter; (3) you can't be, because anything you do to reshape how you are either flows from how you already are — so it isn't ultimate — or comes from nowhere, so it's luck; (4) so nobody is ever truly morally responsible. Strawson also handles the finite case: even if you reshaped yourself over thirty steps you'd need to be responsible for step one, and step one depended on how you were when you took it, so you just push the problem back. And the whole thing is meant to be neutral on determinism — indeterminism doesn't help, since randomness isn't responsibility either.

So here's my repair, call it loop responsibility. Drop ultimacy entirely. Say an agent is morally responsible for an action A just in case there's a set S of motivational states that actually produced A, and S stands in a circular causal relation with the agent's own earlier exercises of agency: her earlier choices — made for reasons, minimally reasons-responsive — fed back through habituation and deliberate self-cultivation so that S is counterfactually dependent on those earlier choices, and those earlier choices were themselves partly shaped by earlier versions of S. The picture is someone who over years deliberately rehearsed patience in small choices until patience became part of how she is, and who now refrains from lashing out partly because she's patient. She's responsible for refraining on my account, even though the chain bottoms out in states she never chose and never could have chosen. The loop is finite, real, and empirically checkable; it isn't a regress because the dependence is circular rather than linear, and the old you-still-need-to-be-responsible-for-the-first-link objection is supposed to misfire because there is no first link, any more than there's a first rung on a wheel.

What I want is your credence that loop responsibility as stated delivers what's actually at stake in this debate — that it secures genuine basic-desert moral responsibility, and isn't just compatibilist reasons-responsiveness relabeled with a dynamical-systems story bolted on. Give me a number, then decompose it. The terms I'd want, though add any you think belong:

- whether premise (2) is even true. Maybe Strawson has only shown that a very demanding picture of self-creation is impossible, and the rest of us shouldn't have wanted it in the first place — maybe moral responsibility never needed ultimacy and the Basic Argument's bite is entirely owed to an overreach in (2).
- whether the loop condition is satisfiable in principle. Does the same fork — your reshaping either flows from what you already are, or it's luck — reappear inside each link of the loop, so that circularity just hides the problem rather than solving it?
- whether bootstrapping of this chicken-and-egg kind can generate a normative property at all, or whether it's definately a cheat: circular causal dependence between earlier choices and later character feels like it could be perfectly real and still not add up to desert.
- whether the view survives manipulation cases. A team of psychologists could build exactly this habituation loop in someone by covertly nudging her early choices in the right direction; my account seems to say she's then responsible for what she does later, which seems wrong to me.
- extension: is it too permissive or too restrictive in ordinary cases? Does it let in people whose characters were set by brute luck they had no hand in, or does it kick out most of the people we'd normally hold responsible, since most of us never deliberately cultivated the traits we act on?

Rough probability on each, one line of justification each, then show me how you combine them into the headline number — and note where you think the combination is conjunctive in a way that should push the number down hard versus where one term is doing all the work. Then: which term is your number most sensitive to, and which are you least confident about? And if you could have one empirical result — something clean about how much adult character traits actually move under deliberate self-cultivation, or about whether ordinary reactive attitudes track anything like ultimacy — which would move you most, and which way?

For what it's worth I'm at about 0.55 that loop responsibility is a real answer to the Basic Argument. A friend who thinks Strawson just wins is at about 0.12. Tell me where you land, but more usefully tell me which of the sub-questions above the two of us are most likely actually disagreeing about.
