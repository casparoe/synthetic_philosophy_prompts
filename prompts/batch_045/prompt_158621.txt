My reading group did the old torture-vs-dust-specks case (one person tortured for 50 years vs. an astronomically large number, like 3^^^3, of people each getting one dust speck in the eye for one second) and everyone except the total utilitarians said: torture, obviously. I tried to say exactly what that answer commits you to and ended up somewhere I can't accept.

(1) The right answer in the standard case is torture.
(2) What has to be true for that is Anti-Aggregation: harms to different people don't sum into a larger harm. A bad outcome is just a list of how bad it is for each person, and there's no further fact about the total.
(3) Anti-Aggregation is person-relative by construction: it stops the summing at the boundary of a person, and says nothing about summing within one life. Within a life you can still add up — a million headaches in one head is worse than one headache.
(4) So the verdict turns on a metaphysical question: whether the pain-events belong to one person or to several.
(5) Test case, which I made up because the usual one doesn't press this: Fission Specks. P will undergo fission into 3^^^3 successors, each psychologically continuous with P, each getting one dust speck. The alternative is P fissioning into a single successor who gets tortured for 50 years. If fission is survival, all the specks happen to one person, so by (3) they sum, and the specks win. If fission is not survival, each successor is a fresh person, (2) blocks the summing, and torture wins.
(6) But on the reductionist story about personal identity there is no deep further fact about whether fission is survival, so there's no determinate fact about which outcome is worse.
(7) A moral verdict that floats free on an unsettled metaphysical question is no verdict at all, so (3) is false: the boundary of a person can't be where aggregation stops.
(8) So either aggregation works everywhere or it works nowhere. Everywhere means the specks are worse than the torture, which is nuts. Nowhere means that a million headaches in one head aren't worse than one, and that a million minutes of moderate pain isn't worse than one minute of torture, which is also nuts.
(9) Therefore something in (1)-(3) is broken.

I'd most like to keep (1) and the within-a-life summing bit in (3) if I have to choose, but I want to know what holding onto them costs me, and I definitly can't see which step to reject.
