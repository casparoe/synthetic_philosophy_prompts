Setup so we're on the same page, my slightly simplified version: Urn K you are TOLD contains exactly 50 red and 50 black. Urn U contains 100 balls, all red or black, in some proportion nobody will tell you. One ball gets drawn. Most people — including me — will pay more for "red from K" than "red from U", and also more for "black from K" than "black from U". Taken together that's inconsistent with ranking bets by a single subjective probability distribution, which is the famous result.

Here's the claim I want your credence on: that asymmetry is best understood as a *rational* response to the structure of the situation — roughly, as reasoning that acts as if the unknown composition were fixed by something that gets to see/respond to your bet — rather than as a bias, i.e. a place where human judgement reliably departs from what it should be. I'm at about 0.55. A friend of mine who works near decision theory is at about 0.15 and thinks I'm smuggling in an adversarial assumption that the lab setup deliberately rules out.

Give me your own number, but more importantly the decomposition. The terms I'd want, at minimum:

- How much rides on whether there even is a normatively defensible way of axiomatizing choice under uncertainty (worst-case over a set of priors, a second-order distribution plus a non-neutral attitude toward the spread, whatever else you think belongs) under which the pattern comes out rational — i.e. on whether the "bias" verdict is just a choice of axioms in a trenchcoat.
- How much rides on what "rational" is even supposed to mean here: internal coherence with some axiom set, vs. actually doing well across a class of environments you might get put in.
- How much rides on the empirical durability of the pattern: does it survive real stakes, repetition and feedback, and having the inconsistency pointed out to subjects.
- How much rides on what the lab is actually measuring — is "ambiguity aversion" one thing, or is the measured effect part distrust of whoever filled the urn, part some feeling about your own competence with that source, part genuine dislike of unmeasurable uncertainty.

Number and one line of justification for each, then how you combine them, then: which single term is your final number most sensitive to, and what one experiment would move it the most.
