Me and a coworker rewatched Fight Club last week and got into an argument about the twist that I can't settle. The narrator IS Tyler Durden — he was there for everything, he has all of Tyler's experiences and knows everything Tyler knows — and yet he doesn't know it. My coworker says that's a textbook counterexample to the KK principle (the idea that if you know p, you know that you know p): here is someone who knows a whole bunch of stuff and just doesn't know that he knows it. I said no — that isn't a KK failure, it's just ordinary ignorance of one proposition, namely that he is Tyler Durden. There is no p that he knows while failing to know that he knows it.

Then I fell down a wikipedia hole and hit the related idea of luminosity: a condition is luminous if whenever it holds you're in a position to know it holds, and there's an argument (margin for error, slow gradual changes, sorites-flavored) that basically no interesting condition is luminous. Now I think the movie is about that rather than about KK: the narrator's slide into being Tyler is gradual — insomnia, the support-group tourism, the blackouts where Tyler runs things while he's asleep, whole projects he starts and can't remember — so there is no moment at which he could have stopped and reliably checked. (If I'm remembering right there are even single-frame flashes of Tyler in the first act before he shows up.)

Can you give me a reading of the film that takes both ideas seriously and tells me which one it actually illustrates, or whether I'm just forcing a thriller into a theory? Where does the reading genuinely illuminate a scene, where does the film resist it, and is that subliminal editing trick part of the point or just a gotcha? Does the ending — him shooting himself and Tyler dying — say anything about self-knowledge that the arguments themselves don't?
