I'm one of the engineers asked to rewrite the section of our engineering handbook on how we respond to mistakes: bad deploys, outages, security slip-ups, features we later decide we shouldn't have shipped. Before the group argues about wording, I've been asked to write down my own position so the rest can push on it, and I'd like an audit of whether it actually holds together.

Where I've landed:

1. Guilt is the better of the two emotions to build a team's norms around: it attaches to what someone did, it points at repair, and it doesn't need an audience.
2. Shame is not just guilt with witnesses added. There's a species of it I've started calling standing-shame, the sense that you are no longer someone the group can take at your word, and I think it can be apt with no audience present and with no act of yours in particular: I feel it about the ad-targeting work we shipped last year, and I wrote none of that code.
3. It is sometimes right to reach for shame on purpose, because guilt only has any grip on people who already accept the norm in question.
4. Moral emotions have fittingness conditions: guilt and shame can be deserved or undeserved, and nothing about what anyone decided, or about what our culture happens to practise, makes them deserved.
5. A group can nonetheless shift, over a couple of years, which of the two does the enforcement work, and some such shifts are improvements while others are not.
6. Two verdicts: publicly shaming the engineer responsible for the March deploy was out of bounds; naming the growth team in the all-hands over the tracking feature was fine.

Go through these and tell me which pairs genuinely conflict, ordered by how severe the conflict is, and for each one say what I would have to give up. Also cover the pairs that only look like conflicts and say why they dissolve, and tell me if any of my verdicts in 6 can't actually be reached from 1 through 5, so I'd be relying on something I haven't stated. Skip any pair where nothing is really at stake. For each conflict give me the tradeoff rather than a recommendation, and don't tell me which commitment to drop overall. No restating my list back to me. Under 600 words.
