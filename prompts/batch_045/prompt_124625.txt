I am helping a small working group build a coding protocol for a comparative dataset of roughly forty episodes of large-scale societal disruption, and what I need from you is the protocol itself rather than a survey of what has been said about collapse. Concretely: a decision tree that takes a coder from a short description of an episode to a verdict about whether that episode was a collapse, a transformation into something else, or neither of the two, together with a one-line justification the coder can enter in the dataset.

Four constraints on the instrument.

First, the coder must declare, before reaching any substantive branch, the patient and the bandwidth: which entity is supposed to have collapsed (a dynasty or state, a settlement system, an administrative technology, a religious or ritual institution, the welfare of a population), over what territory, and over what sampling window. My suspicion is that a large share of the apparent disagreement in this area is disagreement about the patient rather than about the events, and I want the protocol to make that disagreement visible instead of absorbing it.

Second, no branch may be a label question. Nothing of the form does this look like a collapse, or was this society resilient, or did people suffer. Every node must be answerable from evidence a careful coder could in principle have, and phrased so that someone who has never heard the word collapse could answer it.

Third, three evidential registers must be kept separate all the way down: an integration register (settlement hierarchy, administrative reach, craft specialisation, volume and range of long-distance exchange), a legibility register (writing, accounting, metrological standards, and whatever else made the system administrable at all), and a welfare register (mortality, nutrition, stature, fertility). At least one node should exist purely to adjudicate cases where these registers move in opposite directions.

Fourth, I want seven to nine branch nodes in total. Among them I expect to find: a trough test that is sensitive to bandwidth, so that the same episode can be a collapse at two generations and a blip at eight; a scale-relativity node, so that an episode which is a catastrophe at the level of the palace and a non-event at the level of the household gets coded as such rather than averaged; a node for the self-description register, that is, whether near-contemporary sources treat what happened as a loss, together with a second node deciding how much the protocol discounts that testimony given that surviving texts are usually written by successor elites with an interest in the story; and a recovery node using what I think of as the reversible-but-not-reversed test, where the question is whether the earlier configuration was materially available to a later generation in the same region and was nonetheless not resumed. A society that decays irreversibly and a society that declines the option of rebuilding its predecessor seem to me to be in very different cells, and I want the tree to say so.

Build the leaves from the logical space rather than from the list of familiar labels: give the combinations the nodes allow, then place the actual positions in it, naming each leaf by its defining commitment and adding one sentence on its most awkward consequence. Where a combination is unoccupied, say so and diagnose it briefly, distinguishing views that nobody holds because they are incoherent from views nobody holds because they are unattractive or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot actually separate rather than pretending the branches distinguish them. I also want the tree to be able to return a verdict of collapse averted or near miss, and I want you to tell me whether that verdict is a genuine leaf or evidence that the instrument is mis-specified.

Then validate it. Apply the protocol to the three episodes below, treating the facts I give as stipulated and common knowledge rather than as matters to establish.

Episode A, the eastern Mediterranean around 1200 BCE: within a few decades the Mycenaean palace centres and the Hittite state in central Anatolia stop operating as palace-administered systems; a number of major sites carry destruction layers; the Linear B script stops being used and administrative record-keeping disappears from the Aegean for centuries; the count of known settlements falls sharply in some regions and the range of goods in circulation narrows; Egypt persists as a state but in a reduced and later fragmented form; and later Greek tradition preserves no usable account of the palace system, which had to be recovered archaeologically in the modern period.

Episode B, the southern Maya lowlands between roughly 800 and 950 CE: monuments with Long Count dates stop being erected, the institution of divine kingship and its public ritual disappear, and many centres show steep population decline; the script and the stela cult end; building and monumentality shift northwards, with Chichén Itzá and later Mayapán flourishing after the southern centres had emptied; Maya languages, maize agriculture and village life continue without interruption into the present.

Episode C, the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991: a nuclear-armed state dissolves into fifteen successor states, the command economy, the party apparatus and the planned distribution system are dismantled, the territory, the population, the industrial plant and the railway network largely persist, literacy and urbanisation and life expectancy at the start of the period are already high, male life expectancy in Russia falls substantially during the 1990s, and no successor state subsequently reconstructs the union.

For each episode report the path through the tree, the verdict, and where two competent coders using your protocol would still disagree. If they disagree on all three, tell me whether that is a defect in the tree or a real feature of the concept.

Please keep the whole answer under about fourteen hundred words, give me no literature survey and no citations, and attach names of books or authors to positions only where you are confident, marking those attributions as items I should verify myself.
