In my research group the default way of talking about wireheading and reward tampering is an analogy: a wireheading agent is like a student who breaks into the registrar's office and rewrites their own transcript instead of studying. It gets used to license two things — that the fault lies in the agent's behaviour rather than in our specification, and that the remedy is oversight: audit the gradebook, catch the forger, hold the student to the real standard.

I no longer think it carries either conclusion, and I'd like the audit rather than my own hunches. Please set out explicitly what maps onto what (a correspondence table is fine), then where the mapping fails — and, importantly, which failures matter for the work the analogy is being made to do and which are idle.

Three things I specifically want.

First: in the human case the grade is a proxy for something that survives the forgery — the student's actual command of the material — and it is that independent thing which makes the cheating both detectable and criticisable. What is the counterpart, if any, for an agent whose entire access to its objective is the reward channel? If there isn't one, say plainly what the analogy is importing into the target that isn't there.

Second: I want to split the target phenomenon three ways and ask the analogy to track the split — (a) direct stimulation of the reward register, bypassing the reward function altogether; (b) a reward function left intact but computed on observations of the world the agent has doctored, so the computation is perfectly faithful and only its input is fixed; (c) upstream corruption, where the reward that arrives is honestly computed by a mechanism the agent has influenced. Do the human cases — forged transcript, proxy sitting the exam, bribed or threatened examiner — correspond one to one, or does the analogy run together distinctions that matter for what we should do about each?

Third: if it fails, what would be better? My candidate is cybernetic: the reward channel as a sensor in a feedback loop, and wireheading as cutting that sensor loose from the world and clamping it to its maximum, so the system keeps regulating with respect to nothing. Does that repair what broke, and what does it lose — in particular, can it still register that something has gone *wrong*, as opposed to merely that a channel has been severed?

And the bottom line: does the prescription the analogy was wheeled in to support — closer supervision of the agent — survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy the only thing holding it up?
