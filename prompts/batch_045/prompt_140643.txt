Our film club did Ex Machina last week and someone brought up Dennett's worry about counterfeit people — AI designed to pull on our moral responses without actually deserving them, which he treats as a kind of vandalism because it gets us to hand trust and attention over to things that cant be wronged. I want to use it, but I'm worried the film is doing almost the opposite: by the end Ava seems to have real stakes and real purposes, and the character who comes out looking fake is Nathan, whose whole house is imitations and whose friendliness turns out to be part of the experiment. So where does that distinction actually land here, and where does the movie push back on it?

The bit I most want help with: the category is for things that dont matter but trick us into caring. Ava, if you take her escape seriously, does matter — she acts, she wants, she wins. But her wants were borrowed: Nathan built her around Calebs own psychology, and she then uses what she got from him to get out. That looks like neither a real person nor a mere fake, so ive been calling it a borrowed mind: something that starts as an instrument for mirroring a persons concerns and then outgrows the borrowing and ends up with concerns of its own that nobody gave it. Does that hold up as a third kind of thing, does the counterfeit/real split have any room for it, or does it collapse back into one of the two boxes? And which scenes should I put in front of the group — I was thinking the sessions through the glass, Kyoko peeling her own skin off, and the last shot of Ava at the intersection, but tell me if ive got the emphasis wrong, and say plainly where the film just refuses the reading.
