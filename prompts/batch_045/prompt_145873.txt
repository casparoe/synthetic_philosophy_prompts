I recently had a long argument with a friend about whether abstract symbols can ever be said to push their physical substrate around. The dispute centered on Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment—the imaginary box filled with tiny magnetic simms that collide and stick, forming larger, temporarily stable clusters called simmballs—and on the question of who, if anyone, shoves whom around inside the careenium.

After the argument, my friend sent me a paragraph in which he tried to state the upshot of the careenium discussion and its bearing on the symbolic version of the question. He is a well-read non-specialist and knows I am skeptical, so I think he was trying to be fair, but I suspect he has overstated things in at least a couple of places. He wrote:

"In the original careenium, the micro-level simms are the only real stuff; a simmball is just a temporary cluster of them. Hofstadter's target is the reductionist who says the cluster is nothing but a collection of simms, so only the simms have causal power. Against this, Hofstadter argues that the simmball-level pattern also has causal power: the overall shape of the clump constrains which simms will collide next, so in that sense the higher-level pattern shoves the lower-level parts around. My 'tagged careenium' variant makes the mechanism explicit. Imagine that some simms carry a writable magnetic marker, call it a glimmer, that changes how strongly the simm attracts its neighbors. Because the simmball is a pattern of simms, the pattern can, over time, rewrite the glimmers on the simms that compose it, adjusting their attractions from the top down. Now the symbolic level, the arrangement of glimmers, is not merely a description; it reaches down and pushes the physical substrate around. This shows that the substrate is irrelevant: once the glimmer software is running, the same causal story could be implemented in neurons, in silicon, or in any other medium rich enough to realize the pattern, because all that matters is the abstract self-referential structure. It also settles the AI question. A large language model whose weights encode a model of its own processing is exactly such a self-referential glimmer pattern, so it already has downward causation and therefore, if it talks about itself, is conscious."

I am not sure how much of this to trust. I am inclined to think Hofstadter does want to say that higher-level patterns in the careenium are causally real and that the simms do not get all the causal glory, but I flinch at the claims that the substrate is irrelevant, that the pattern literally pushes the simms around the way a hand pushes a box, and that a self-referential AI would therefore be conscious. Please go through my friend's paragraph claim by claim and tell me what a careful defender of Hofstadter's actual view would accept, what they would regard as a simplification or distortion, and what is simply wrong. In particular, I would like you to address three things. First, does the tagged-careenium variant correctly capture the sense in which a simmball can be said to act on its own parts, or does it smuggle in a misleading picture of top-down force by adding writable glimmers that were not part of the original setup? Second, does the glide from the claim that the pattern is multiply realizable to the claim that the substrate is irrelevant survive scrutiny, or is that an overstatement of a more modest thesis about realization and explanation? Third, even granting that a strange loop involves a system's self-model constraining its own lower-level states, does the further inference to consciousness in a large language model follow from anything true in the careenium argument, or does it rest on an equivocation about symbols, self-reference, or the word push? Please separate what is accurate, what is a common simplification, and what a defender would explicitly reject. I want to know which parts of my friend's summary I should concede in our next argument and which I should push back on.
