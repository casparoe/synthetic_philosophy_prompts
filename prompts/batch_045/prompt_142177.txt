I am writing a paper on the measurement of famine mortality, and I have gotten stuck on a distinction I can feel but cannot state cleanly. I would like you to explain its conceptual structure rather than the history — please assume I know the cases (Bengal, Ireland, the Great Leap Forward) and skip the narrative.

As I see it, at least four quantities compete for the name "the death toll of the famine," and for one and the same event they can differ by a factor of two or more:

(1) deaths whose immediate cause was starvation or a starvation-aggravated disease;
(2) deaths in excess of what would have been recorded in the same population over the same period had the famine not occurred;
(3) deaths counterfactually attributable to the collapse of entitlements — everyone who would have survived had the relevant economic arrangements held;
(4) quantity (2) minus the deaths the famine merely brought forward: people so debilitated by endemic undernutrition that they would have died within the following year or two anyway.

Please explain how these come apart, and what each implicitly assumes about what a famine is: an event that has a toll, a property of individual deaths, or a causal contribution that must be carved out of a background of ordinary deprivation. I want the intuition, not only the definition — what would have to be true of the world for (1) and (3) to coincide, and what would have to be true for them to diverge sharply?

Then consider an invented case. In the fictional Republic of Velmara, the harvest falls 6 percent, the government imports grain, and measured per-capita food availability actually rises slightly during the crisis year. A banking collapse, however, destroys rural demand for wage labor: landless laborers lose their purchasing power while granaries stay full and prices rise only modestly. Velmara's famine code declares a famine only when output falls by at least 25 percent and grain prices exceed a fixed threshold, so the government announces that there was no famine and no starvation deaths. On an entitlement analysis, is that announcement straightforwardly false, or does the code merely define a different, legally useful thing? And is the question "was there a famine in Velmara, and how many did it kill?" one that our choice among (1)–(4) settles by stipulation — or does some one of them track something real that the others miss?

Finally, the framework in which famine is a political crime rather than a natural disaster seems to make the choice of measure load-bearing. Measure (1) together with a strict code tends to generate official findings of no famine and hence no crime; measure (4) invites the reply that these people were dying anyway. Please say how you would resist both of these slide-offs.
