I sit on the Professional Formation Committee of a medical school. For the past two years our students have kept what we call the Character and Conduct module: a vendor-built app that adapts the scheme Benjamin Franklin describes in his Autobiography — thirteen named virtues, one highlighted per week in a thirteen-week rotation running four cycles a year, beginning at Temperance and reaching Humility in the thirteenth week, with students scoring themselves each evening against the week's virtue and recording a fault with a single tap. Until now the record has been private, as Franklin's pocket book was private, and it has carried no grade.

The vendor's new release, which we must decide on before the start of next term, adds a cohort ledger: each student's current focus virtue, their unbroken streak, and a short optional note are visible to the rest of their cohort and to their small-group faculty mentor. In pilots at two other schools, median daily completion rose from roughly a third of days to more than three quarters, and the vendor's white paper attributes almost all of that to what it calls low-stakes mutual visibility. Our own small-group faculty report a second effect: students increasingly narrate faults that read well in public and record nothing on the days that do not, and two students have stopped logging altogether on the ground that a public record of humility is a contradiction in terms.

We also have a live case, which is why this cannot be deferred. A third-year student has written to ask that she be allowed to keep the private tracker and be excused from the ledger. Her note says that she has caught herself choosing which virtue to work on by how it will look to her cohort, that this is precisely the failure the exercise exists to cure, and that she would like us to say which the school wants: humility, or the appearance of it. The vendor's position is that opt-outs degrade the social feature for everyone and that we should adopt the ledger for all students or none. Our Dean's position is that the accreditor's revised standard asks for assessable formation in professionalism and that the ledger is the only part of the module we could actually put in front of an assessor.

Please draft the decision memo I will circulate to the committee ahead of our meeting. It should recommend one concrete course of action, with a timeline and a named owner for each piece of it, and it should be written so that both the vendor and the student could recognize it as responsive to what they actually said. Give the strongest consideration on the losing side its full weight: in particular, that a purely private exercise is in practice an exercise almost nobody performs, and that Franklin's own project was neither modest nor secret — a self-made man keeping books on himself, who went on to publish the scheme, and who concedes that in the case of humility he acquired considerably more of the appearance than of the thing. Be equally plain about what we are giving up: if we keep the ledger, say exactly what we have stopped measuring and how we will describe that to students who notice; if we drop it or restrict it, say exactly what we can still show the accreditor and what we will tell them we are not measuring. Where your recommendation depends on the vendor agreeing to a change, say what we should do if they refuse.
