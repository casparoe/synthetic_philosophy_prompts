I am a final-year mathematics student sitting in on a graduate seminar in metaphysics, and I would like the seminar's central dispute restated in the vocabulary I actually work in, together with an honest report on what does not survive the translation.

My statistics is at the level of a first graduate course: identifiability and its failure, likelihood and its invariance under reparameterisation, label switching in mixture models, rotational indeterminacy in factor analysis, the permutation-and-scaling ambiguity in independent component analysis, priors as tie-breakers when the likelihood cannot do the work, exchangeability, sufficient statistics. I have also taken a machine-learning course in which people worry about whether a learned representation recovers the true latent factors or merely some invertible transformation of them; my recollection is that there are results to the effect that this cannot be done from observational data alone without extra inductive assumptions, though I could not reconstruct the argument.

The position I want translated is the one the seminar has been calling the inaccessible-ontology or humility worry. As I have it, it consists of several separable claims. First, a complete description of the world in the vocabulary of fundamental physics characterises reality only up to structure: a network of roles, relations, counterfactual dependencies and nomological profiles. Second, the intrinsic or categorical natures that occupy those roles are not fixed by that description, because permuting them across roles preserves all causal, nomological and modal structure, and therefore preserves everything that could ever show up as evidence. Third, so there may be facts about what the world is made of that no agent, however idealised, could in principle settle. Fourth, there are two responses: either the natures are perfectly determinate occupants of the roles and we simply cannot know which they are, or there is nothing to be known beyond the structure and the question of which nature occupies which role has no answer. Fifth, and separately, the same worry is said to arise for entities rather than properties: whether the world contains additional things that make no causal difference at all would seem to be a question that nothing in principle bears on.

My suspicion is that this is just non-identifiability, and that my framework has a clean and unexciting way with it: the likelihood is invariant under a group action, the parameter is recoverable only up to that group, so we quotient, and we regard the surplus as not a fact rather than as a hidden fact. Before I settle for that, I want the translation done properly. Please take the five claims above one at a time and restate each in my vocabulary, telling me where the fit is exact and where you had to bend something and what you bent. In particular: what in my vocabulary corresponds to all possible evidence, and to the qualifier in principle? I worry that my notion of evidence always presupposes a fixed space of observables, and that the metaphysician is deliberately quantifying over every possible enrichment of that space.

I also want a device I have been using in the seminar made precise for me. Call it a total-science oracle: it answers, correctly and with unlimited computational resources on both sides, any question statable purely in the vocabulary of roles, relations, laws, counterfactuals and modality. Does my taxonomy of three cases — not identified given current data but identified given more, determinate but identified by no data of any kind, and indeterminate because there is nothing there to identify — line up with the two responses in the literature, and does statistics contain any internal criterion for choosing between the second and the third, or is the move to the quotient a decision rather than a discovery?

Finally, the residue report. Which parts of the dispute have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? I have in mind the claim that we can think about or refer to the natures even though no evidence could reveal them, the idea that the question of what there is might matter independently of what we can predict, and the entity version of the worry, which looks to me like a parameter that appears in the model's semantics but in no likelihood function at all. For each item of residue, tell me whether it is a defect of my framework — an instrumentalist blind spot, given that the only discipline-internal reason I know for caring about true latents rather than convenient ones is transfer under intervention — or a sign that the original claim was less clear than it looked. What I want is a translation, not a verdict, and I especially want to be told when the vocabulary itself is quietly doing argumentative work.
