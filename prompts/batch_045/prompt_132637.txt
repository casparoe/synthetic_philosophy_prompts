I'm writing on moral responsibility in penal practice and would like help reconstructing the implicit theory behind a parole practice I keep circling back to.

In many systems parole boards treat demonstrated "insight" and "genuine remorse" as close to central to release: applicants who deny the offence or who "minimize" it are routinely deferred, and boards are practiced at discounting remorse they judge instrumental — displayed, they suspect, because the applicant has learned that this is what gets you out. Meanwhile the official test for release is stated forward-looking, several jurisdictions have been replacing remorse-and-insight judgments with actuarial risk scores and credits for prosocial conduct on the ground that remorse has little predictive value, and the surrounding legal system guarantees a prisoner the right to maintain innocence and pursue appeal without adverse consequence.

Two features I can't line up. First, if remorse is a condition of liberty, the incentive seems to manufacture the very inauthenticity the board penalises: it looks like a practice that demands a state of will it simultaneously makes unavailable. I've been toying with distinguishing acknowledgment-remorse — accepting, in whatever sense matters, that one acted wrongly — from affective remorse, the feeling of guilt, and asking whether only the first can coherently be demanded. Second, the board seems to slip into something like the objective attitude toward the applicant "in denial," treating the denial as a symptom to be managed, while continuing to blame and to defer release.

What does the remorse-and-insight practice presuppose about moral responsibility — that blame tracks quality of will, that what is owed is the reoccupation of the participant stance rather than a performance, or something weaker? Which features strictly presuppose this and which merely sit well with a forward-looking risk view, and where is the practice underdetermined? And what would the risk-score reform have to give up, if Strawson is right that the objective attitude cannot be opted for wholesale? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my question.
