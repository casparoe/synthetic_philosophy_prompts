After thirty-five years as an atmospheric chemist — most of them spent fitting box models and chemistry-transport models to field data, and arguing with colleagues about when an extra reaction pathway was earned by the data and when it was only curve-fitting — I now sit in on a graduate reading group in philosophy of science, and I have become impatient with how the literature on inference to the best explanation is presented. The seminar treats IBE as if explains-the-evidence-better were a primitive, and then spends its time on van Fraassen's best-of-a-bad-lot objection and on the familiar distinction between the loveliest and the likeliest candidate, as though the only open question were whether loveliness tracks likelihood. Before I read any further, I want a specification: what would an account of explanatory inference have to deliver to count as adequate, where adequate means good enough for two jobs at once — to license belief in genuinely unobservable mechanisms, the realist use, of the sort that carried us from the bare anomaly in the Halley Bay ozone records to a specific claim about heterogeneous chemistry on polar stratospheric clouds — and to guide a working group that has to choose between models by Tuesday.

So please write me the desiderata list. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark it as either non-negotiable or a preference of one particular camp. I have a provisional list of my own, which you should feel free to reorganise, sharpen, or reject, but I want it addressed: (i) the palette problem — IBE ranks candidates but says nothing about where the candidate set comes from, and in my field the decisive step was nearly always noticing which retrievals, priors, and quality-control flags had quietly excluded a hypothesis before any ranking began, so an account that only scores seems to inherit every bias of whatever generated the options; (ii) the accretion problem — adding a conjunct or an auxiliary mechanism nearly always improves fit and often improves apparent explanatory depth, so the account needs something doing the work that a parameter-count penalty does in model selection, and needs to say when mechanistic detail is depth rather than overfitting; (iii) scale or coarse-graining sensitivity — the same phenomenon is often best explained differently at a parcel scale than at a hemispheric scale, and I want to know whether the account may be resolution-relative or owes me a single verdict; (iv) contrast sensitivity — explanations answer why-this-rather-than-that questions, and the ranking appears to flip with the contrast class, which may be a virtue or may break any claim to a stable ordering; (v) a closure or stopping rule, telling the inquirer when to stop elaborating a hypothesis; (vi) some non-circular bridge from explanatory virtue to probable truth, given the bad-lot argument; and (vii) an account of what IBE recommends when the loveliest story has never been subjected to a severe test, since in my experience the elegant mechanism is precisely the one for which nobody has yet found a discriminating experiment.

Two things matter to me beyond the list itself. First, if some subset of these requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, say so and explain the tension rather than papering over it: I suspect the demand for an absolute verdict that a hypothesis is belief-worthy tout court sits badly with contrast- and scale-sensitivity, and that unification-style simplicity sits badly with the demand for causal-mechanistic detail, and a list that turns out to be unsatisfiable as a whole would itself be a result I would rather have than a tidy compromise. Second, tell me which single requirement does the most exclusionary work — the one that rules out the largest share of the usual ways of construing IBE, among the Bayesian reading, the explanatory-coherence or constraint-satisfaction reading, the causal-mechanistic or interventionist reading, the error-statistical reading, and the unificationist reading — though keep that part to a few lines per option, and do not give me a bibliography, a reading list, or pointers to papers; I only want to see what the criteria do to the options.
