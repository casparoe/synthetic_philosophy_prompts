I'm in a reading group on truth and realism that shares a room with a machine-learning lab, so the discussions drift toward their problems. Last week a postdoc there argued that a sufficiently capable system could in principle reconstruct every empirical fact about the actual world, including what happened in spacetime regions no signal could ever reach us from. The premise he offered for this was the one-liner "all truths are knowable," offered as if it settled the matter, together with the gloss that truth just is the sort of thing that cannot outrun what could be known. When I brought up Fitch's argument he waved it off: that is only a scope artifact, he said, restrict the principle to non-epistemic truths and the claim that matters survives.

I don't think the sentence is doing the work he takes it to be doing. I'd like it taken apart. What are the distinct precise theses it could be expressing — on what "all truths" quantifies over (does it include truths about what is in fact never known, truths about unobservable regions, negative existentials, infinite conjunctions?), on whose knowledge and under which notion of possibility (us as we are, an idealized agent, agents in other possible worlds with different physics?), on whether the very same proposition has to be true in the situation in which it is known, and on whether the possibility is distributed truth by truth or required to hold for all truths at once? I have a hunch that the same-proportion-remaining-true clause is where the real weight sits, but I can't get from that hunch to a verdict on his two claims, and I want to know which readings are trivial, which are substantive and defensible, which are substantive but doubtful, and which are straightforwardly false.

Please answer as exactly five numbered items: the five readings you take to be most load-bearing for his two uses, each reading stated precisely in one sentence, followed by one sentence giving its verdict — trivial, defensible, doubtful, or false — and saying which of the two uses, if either, it actually supports and why. No preamble, no closing summary.
