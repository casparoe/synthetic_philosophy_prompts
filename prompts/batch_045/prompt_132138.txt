i'm taking this seminar on equality and selection (half philosophy department, half public policy) and for my section's project i've been sitting in on the university's Committee on Selection Practices — they review basically any process around here that picks people: admissions, hiring, scholarships, student government. i take the minutes, so ive read through all their rulings from the last few years twice now, and i'm trying to work out what they'll do with the case they took up last week — and honestly what they *should* do if they want to be consistent with themselves.

their record so far: they signed off on race being used as one factor among many in holistic, individualised admissions review; they rejected a proposal to reserve a fixed share of seats in each entering class for historically excluded groups; they rejected a mechanical points system that added a set number of points for race or ethnicity; they approved legacy preference for children of alumni, but only after a really long fight; they approved the recruited-athlete preference basically without discussion; they approved a first-generation / low-income preference; they approved an automatic-admit plan for the top 10% of every in-state high school even though everyone in the room knew it was adopted partly because it would change the class's racial and ethnic mix; and they approved a hiring rule requiring at least two women on every departmental shortlist, with no requirement at all about who actually gets hired.

the new one is a proposal to impose something like the Norwegian company-board rule on the university's own governing board and on the boards of its affiliated foundations: at least roughly 40% of each gender, where the sanction is that a non-compliant slate is simply sent back for another round of nominations, rather than any individual being removed or told they can't serve. (my prof just keeps calling it "the Norway thing"; i think there's a comply-or-explain version of it somewhere but i dont remember the details.) So: what do you think they rule? More usefully, what are the rules that fit all of their past verdicts, what does each of them predict here, which one is your best guess and why, and which past precedent is the closest analog? The rule i assumed they were running — only things you've actually earned or chosen are allowed to count — clearly can't be it, given the legacy and athlete votes, so what's actually left? And if their precedents genuinely underdetermine this, what principle would they be adopting with each choice? Last thing: what further case would best tell the competing rules apart? i keep going back and forth between two: a reservation of a fixed number of admission seats for a group defined by ancestry and historic exclusion but with the better-off members of that group carved out (the kind of exclusion India's reservation system does, iirc), versus the same 40% gender rule applied instead to the shortlist for a big merit scholarship. Tell me which is the sharper test, or propose a better one.
