For our next session I want to make a one-page handout that's really a flowchart: you start at the top, answer a string of questions one at a time, and end up at a named position — Brouwerian intuitionism, Bishop-style constructivism, the Russian/recursive school, the Martin-Löf-ish type theory view, plain classical mathematics, predicativism, finitism, ultrafinitism, and whatever 'unnamed' leaves you need. A few cases I want used as nodes, because they're the ones that actually got us arguing. The classic argument that there exist irrationals a and b with a^b rational, where you consider √2 raised to the √2 and say that either it's already rational or else squaring it up gives 2 — does that establish the existential claim, or only that it's not refutable? Whether you'd assert right now that either the decimal expansion of pi contains infinitely many 7s or it doesn't. A program that searches for a counterexample to Goldbach: if you can show it's impossible for the search to run forever, does it follow that it halts? Whether defining a number as the least n such that for all m, Q(n, m) is legitimate, given that the definition quantifies over a totality that includes the very thing being defined. Whether the function that's 0 for x below 0 and 1 everywhere else is a genuine total function on the reals. Whether 10 to the power 10 to the power 10 is a number in quite the same sense that 7 is. And whether there are real numbers that no rule or law picks out at all.
