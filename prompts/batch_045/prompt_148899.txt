hey, hoping you can help me with something in philosophy of mind — epiphenomenalism and that "paradox of phenomenal judgment" thing. I think i might have accidentally solved it and i want to know whether it actually works or whether im missing something dumb.

Setup as I understand it: epiphenomenalism says phenomenal states are causally inert, all the causal work is done by the underlying neural states. So my judgment "I'm in pain right now" isnt produced by the pain, its produced by some neural state N, which also produces the pain as a kind of byproduct. The paradox is supposed to be that if thats true, then my judgments about my own experiences arent really *about* them, or I dont really *know* them, so the view saws off the branch its sitting on — and people treat this as a serious objection rather than a curiosity.

My reply: I dont see why. Call it the shadow-and-footprint move. Someone walks past a lamp and casts a shadow and leaves a footprint. The footprint carries information about the shadow, and I can find out about the shadow from the footprint, even though the shadow causes nothing whatsoever. The common cause does all the work. Likewise, if N causes both the experience E and the belief B, then B reliably covaries with E — in any world where N shows up, both show up — so B is a perfectly good indicator of E, and on any reliabilist or information-theoretic story about knowledge, thats all knowledge requires. The pain doesnt have to do any causal work for B to be about it.

My friend, who's read way more of this then me, says the actual problem is that N screens E off from B: E makes no difference to whether B occurs, so B is completely insensitive to E, and no reliabilist should be happy with a belief that would be exactly the same if its purported subject matter vanished. I dont get why thats bad either. If the mechanism is reliable, who cares that its insensitive to E? And honestly it seems like the worry isnt specific to epiphenomenalism anyway — on any view where experiences arent identical to their neural bases, my belief is caused by the base and not by the experience, so everyone faces the paradox equally, which makes me suspect the whole thing is just correlation-vs-causation confusion with a fancy name.

So: does the shadow-and-footprint move work? Whats supposed to break — the reference part or the justification part, and are those even the same problem? And if my friends screening-off complaint is right, what exactly does it show that the plain covariation point doesnt?
