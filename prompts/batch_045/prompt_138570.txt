I'm a retired NMR spectroscopist, lately sitting in on the philosophy of physics reading group down at the university, and I keep loosing the thread on whether the wavefunction is "real". As far as I can make out, the psi-ontic / psi-epistemic business amounts to this: psi is epistemic only if two different pure states could sit on top of one and the same complete physical state of the system — the distributions over the deeper states overlap — and ontic otherwise. There's also some theorem from around 2012 (Pusey, Barrett and Rudolph, I think) saying you can only have the epistemic version if you give up independence for separately prepared systems.

Here's the bit I made up myself and can't get anyone in the group to engage with. In NMR the phase of the signal after a pulse sequence isn't a property of the spins "right now" — it encodes the history of the pulses, and relative to a rotating frame you picked. Call this the phase view: psi is real the way a phase is real, a fact about the system's history and about a frame, not something you could read off a snapshot, but not merely what we know either. I'm around 0.5 that something in this neighborhood is right.

Give me your number on that, decomposed: how much rides on whether "real" has to mean snapshotable, how much on whether that independence assumption is a fair price to pay, how much on whether 3N-dimensional configuration space can host genuine stuff at all, plus whatever else belongs. A number and one line each, then the combination, and tell me which term you're least sure of and what result would move it most.
