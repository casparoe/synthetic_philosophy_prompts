I've been reading up on the problem of old evidence in Bayesian confirmation theory and I think I must be missing something, because to me the fix looks almost trivial. The setup as I understand it: the anomalous perihelion precession of Mercury had been known for decades before general relativity came along, so P(E) was already 1 for everybody, so conditioning on it can't raise P(GR) — apparently this is supposed to be a serious problem for Bayesianism. But (a) if you write Bayes theorem in odds form, the whole evidential boost is carried by the Bayes factor P(E|H)/P(E|not-H), and P(E) itself never appears, so old evidence should confirm just like new evidence does; and (b) even in the plain probability form, nobody is ever literally certain of an observation, so plug in 0.99 instead of 1 and you get your boost back. So why is this discussed as a deep problem rather than as a notational accident? Where exactly does my reasoning go wrong, and does the answer change if the hypothesis actually entails the evidence?
