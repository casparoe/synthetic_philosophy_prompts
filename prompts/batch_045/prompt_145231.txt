Me and my reading group (local LessWrong-adjacent crowd, ~8 people, Sunday afternoons) keep going in circles about which kinds are "socially constructed." My friend Dana keeps insisting race and money are constructed in exactly the same way and that once you grant it for one you've granted it for the other, and I think that's just four or five different claims wearing one slogan. So I built a spreadsheet to settle it and now I need numbers in it.

What I want from you: for each kind below give me (a) a 0–10 on each of five tests, (b) one headline 0–100 score for how socially constructed it is all things considered, (c) the weights you used to get from (a) to (b), because I suspect "same thing!" people are just weighting differently, and (d) one sentence per score, max. The tests:

1. Attitude in the criteria: does whether x counts as a K depend, even partly, on what anyone believes, wants, expects, or does? 0 = membership is settled by facts that never mention attitudes; 10 = nothing but attitudes settles it.
2. The desert island test: same physics, same biology, same everything, except nobody has ever had any beliefs or practices concerning K. Would there be any Ks? (Not "would the word exist" — would the things be there, and be Ks.)
3. The swap test, which is my favorite and the one I made up: hold the population fixed and swap the social treatment — expectations, incentives, role allocation, how people are raised and read — between the Ks and the non-Ks, leaving every non-attitudinal feature (bodies included) where it was. How much of the observed K/non-K difference travels with the treatment and how much stays behind with the original partition? 10 = everything travels, 0 = nothing does.
4. Looping: does classifying something as a K change it — the thing, or the evidence you get about it, or what the classifiers do next — in a way that feeds back into whether the classification keeps working? Rate the strength and speed of that feedback, not just whether it exists at all.
5. The outsider test: give a being with no concept of K and no idea what anyone believes a giant dataset of behavior, physiology and microphysics. Could it recover the K/non-K partition as one that buys predictive power, or is the partition invisible from outside? 10 = fully recoverable from attitude-free data, 0 = only visible if you already know what people think.

The kinds — please do all of them, and especially the invented ones, since you can't have a memorized take on those and that's where I'll actually learn something:

- Money, meaning the US dollar specifically, not "valuable stuff" in general
- Race, meaning the categories as they get used in everyday American life, not some ancestry-cluster thing
- Woman/man as a gender kind, i.e. not sex
- Married
- Major depressive disorder as it appears in the current DSM
- "Recession"
- Threks: at t0 membership is assigned by a one-time public lottery and inherited afterward; nobody can tell without a blood test that exists but is almost never given; threks get treated worse in a diffuse way — less eye contact, worse schools, fewer callbacks. Then run it forward 200 years.
- Cloaks: just like money except that everyone is disposed to accept them in trade while each person falsely believes they are the only one who is, so there is zero common knowledge. Are they money? Does your answer change their scores?
- Gribbons: a partition everybody sorts by, everybody agrees is useless for predicting anything, and nobody has ever had any feelings about — people just track it for no reason, like a really boring hobby.

Then the part I care most about: which kinds come apart across the tests — high on one, low on another — and what that pattern says about whether "socially constructed" is one property or a bundle? Where exactly does Dana's "exactly the same way" claim break, and where does it survive? For threks specifically: do the scores move over those 200 years, in which direction, and as the downstream differences pile up is the kind getting more constructed or less constructed?

No citations, no reading lists, nothing I'd need JSTOR for — I want the reasoning, not a bibliography. And please skip the "in some sense every kind is socially constructed" move; I've read that post, it's what started the fight.
