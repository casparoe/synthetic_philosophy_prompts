I sit on the allocations committee of a medical research charity, and we can fund exactly one of two applications. Both are rigorously costed, and both are certain to deliver what they promise.

Application A: a specialist service that would relieve, for around 25 patients, several months of continuous severe pain of the kind that no one would willingly endure for an hour — pain of the grade the familiar torture-versus-dust-specks comparison imagines.

Application B: a modification to a very common hospital procedure that would spare roughly two million patients an afternoon of mild but genuine discomfort: an itch, or mild nausea. It is real at the time, but a year later nobody would remember it, and nobody would give up more than a minute of their time to avoid it.

Two features make this harder than the textbook case, and I want the memo to take them on rather than idealise them away. First, the discomfort in B is real yet commands no willingness to pay and leaves nothing in memory: some of us think harms nobody would trade anything to avoid are not the sort of item that can be summed across people at all, while others think that is simply what a very small harm looks like. Second, at the same cost the applicant could deliver a concentrated variant: the same total quantity of discomfort, spread over far fewer patients and lasting far longer for each, still well short of severe pain.

Please draft the one-page written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. Begin with the recommendation itself in your first sentence — fund A, fund B, or decline both and reopen the round with a stated criterion — then give the reasons, with no preamble and no restatement of the question. State the principle the committee is being asked to endorse in a form that also settles the concentrated variant, and settles what we should say if the entire quantity could instead be loaded onto a single patient across one lifetime, so that we can see whether we are trading in totals or in what happens within a life. Give the losing side its strongest argument: the defenders of A hold that a threshold between discomfort and agony is exactly what money like this exists to respect, and the defenders of B hold that a threshold on which two million real experiences count for nothing is not caution but indifference. Say plainly where the committee should decline to follow the principle it has just adopted. Write it in British English, in roughly 600 words.
