I write the codebook for a small annotation team. We label first-person experience reports — a few thousand a week, scraped from a journalling app — and the label that matters downstream is "pleasure." The number our funder actually cares about is a single positive-valence score per person per day, so whatever structure we use internally, the output has to collapse to one number. Annotators are smart but they have a psych BA and about ninety seconds per snippet, we can't ask the writer followup questions, my boss who signs off on the codebook has no philosophy at all, and we need inter-annotator agreement above roughly 0.7 or the contract gets awkward.

The thing that keeps breaking us is the heterogeneity thing: eating a peach, finishing a hard proof, and scratching an itch reportedly feel like nothing in common, and yet we're supposed to tag all three as pleasure. Our current rule, inherited from a consultant, is basically "tag it pleasure if the person says they'd want more of it," and I'm now fairly sure that's wrong but I can't say why in a way a coder could act on.

What I want is a decision procedure, not an essay: questions in order, what counts as a sufficient yes at each step, where the answer stops at no, and where it has to be handed to a human because what's left is a values question rather than a factual one. It has to survive the simplification — a coder with no theory has to run it in ninety seconds — and I need it to handle at least these:

- flow / absorption: three hours vanish, reported as a great day, with no report of anything feeling good at the time
- relief, itch-scratching, the minute after a pain stops
- wanting without liking and liking without wanting: the runner who loves the burn, the person who hates that they want the cigarette
- retrospective vs in-the-moment: "miserable the whole time, glad I did it"
- pride, hope, being right about something: things people call positive that don't obviously feel like anything in particular
- mixed cases: funny-but-horrible, the sad movie you'd watch again

Be explicit about which distinctions from the philosophy survive into the codebook and which you're dropping, and what we lose by dropping them. I gather the big fork is between "pleasure is a felt quality / a hedonic tone experiences have" and "pleasure is an attitude you take toward an experience" — tell me what happens to the daily number if we pick the wrong one, and whether there's a version of the procedure that works tolerably either way. And if the honest answer is that pleasures really don't share a feature and the category is disjunctive, or held together by family resemblance, or by all being the sort of thing you want to continue: I still need a procedure, and I need to know what stops it from tagging literally everything. Our consultant's rule has that problem in spades — wireheading is the obvious reductio, and I've read enough old forum posts about it to know I can't just assert my way out.

Give me the procedure, then run it on three of the snippets below, including at least one where it gives me an answer I'll hate and immediately try to argue with. Then tell me what the procedure just cannot do, and where I should expect annotators to quietly go off-script.

1. "Three hours disappeared while I was working on the encryption bug. I didn't notice anything. Later: best day in weeks."
2. "The toothache stopped. For maybe a minute nothing hurt and it was amazing."
3. "Ate the whole bag of chips. Didn't even like them. Kept going anyway."
4. "Finished the 20-miler. Hated every step of mile 18. Doing it again Sunday."
5. "Found out I was right and Dave was wrong in the group chat. Vindicated? Not exactly good exactly."
6. "Holdig my nephew. Warm. Nothing else to say."
