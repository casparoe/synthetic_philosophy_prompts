I want a specification rather than a survey. Fix the purpose as practical: an account of Molyneux's problem is adequate only if it can tell an experimenter what to test in a person seeing for the first time, and what a positive or negative result would show about the mind. Write the desiderata such an account must meet — the distinctions it must draw, the cases it must handle, the findings it must be able to accommodate — with the consideration behind each, and separate the non-negotiable requirements from those that are merely commitments of one camp.

Three stipulations, so the list cannot collapse back into the familiar moves:

1. The account must handle a stipulated dissociation: a patient who, on first seeing a cube and a sphere, sorts and names them correctly by sight while sincerely denying that either looks at all the way the corresponding object felt — and, symmetrically, a patient who reports an immediate feeling of familiarity with the seen shapes while sorting them no better than chance. Say which of these, if either, would count as a "yes," and why.
2. It must say whether the reverse arrangement — a sighted person who must identify by touch alone, through a probe or a glove, shapes he has only ever seen — is the same question as the forward one, and what turns on the answer.
3. It must state, not assume, which findings from clinical cases of sight restoration would confirm or refute which answer, and how much evidential weight such cases can bear given that they are few, uncontrolled, and confounded by low-level visual deficits and by prior expectations.

Then flag any pair of your requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied: in particular, whether an account can both explain how cross-modal recognition is possible and refrain from presupposing that vision and touch represent the same spatial properties, given that this presupposition seems to be exactly what is in dispute.

Close with a brief scorecard — no more than a third of the response — for the three standard families of answer: innate amodal spatial representation, learned cross-modal correlation, and the claim that the question is indeterminate until one fixes what counts as the same shape. Keep the weight on the criteria and on the cases that motivate them.

Write in American English. End with a last line beginning "Verdict: " naming the single desideratum that does the most to exclude candidate answers, and stating in that same line whether any candidate answer can meet it.
