I'm writing a paper on sphexishness — the digger-wasp pattern that gave the label its point (as the anecdote is usually told, the wasp parks its paralyzed prey at the threshold, goes in to inspect the nest, comes out and drags the prey in; move the prey while it is inside and it returns the prey to the threshold and runs the inspection again, apparently without end) — and I want to argue that the usual diagnosis locates the wrong feature: what makes the wasp look mechanical is not that its performance is insensitive to perturbation, but something else. My candidate distinction is between performance-rigidity — at a time, the behaviour is invariant across changes in the situation, including changes in the reasons — and policy-ineditability — over time, theagent cannot revise, question, or abandon the policy that is producing the behaviour. I need a matched pair of cases that isolates the first from the second.

Design me two agents, R and P, in one ordinary setting (a clinic, a workplace, a school; nothing fantastic), performing the same kind of action, with the same stakes, the same facts available to them, and the same kind of ordinary causal history, and with alternative possibilities available to both in whatever ordinary sense you prefer. R's performance must be sphexish — invariant across every perturbation you like, including perturbations that change the reasons — while R remains able to revise the policy behind it. P's performance must be finely variable and information-sensitive across those same perturbations, while P cannot revise the policy behind it. Show the editability difference in what the agents actually do and say over time rather than asserting it as a theoretical claim. No verdict may be produced by a manipulator, a device, a compulsion or addiction, a neurological stipulation, or by denying the agent alternatives: the work has to be done by the two dimensions.

Then demonstrate that the specification is met. Give the verdict, on each case, of a reasons-responsiveness account (the action must issue from a mechanism appropriately receptive and reactive to reasons) and of an identification or attributability account (the action must issue from a will or self the agent is not estranged from), and say why each verdict follows from the theory rather than being read off my description; both theories must count R as free and responsible and withhold that from P. Name the feature doing the work, say what happens to the verdicts if you remove it — for instance, if you make R's rigidity ineditable, or give P reflective endorsement of the plasticity — and give the strongest objection a defender of either theory can make to being pushed into these verdicts, including the objection that the theory has to be amended to get there.

Finally, commit: tell me which of R and P, if either, is really unfree, and whether the pair supports my hypothesis that the intuitive mark of the mechanical is ineditability rather than rigidity. I want your position with reasons, not a report of what the two theories would say.
