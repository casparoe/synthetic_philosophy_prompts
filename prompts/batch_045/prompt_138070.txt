I'm drafting the privacy chapter of a report on what a hospital owes the patients whose records it releases, and I've realised I have no specification: I can't yet say what a theory of privacy would have to get right in order to be good enough for that job. Write one for me.

Fix the purpose as institutional: the theory must let a data-holder decide whether a given practice wrongs anyone, in cases where nothing further bad happens to the person — no discrimination, no breach, no distress, because nobody ever looks.

The cases I take to be load-bearing are these, and a theory that simply stipulates an answer to any of them is one I don't want: an idle sensor, a ward camera that records continuously and is wiped on schedule without ever being played; a sealed letter, a colleague authorised to open your file only if you go missing; the night-train stranger, to whom you tell your entire life and feel nothing lost; the inference, a shopping-pattern model that outputs a fact about you that no record anywhere states and that no one accessed any document to learn; the genetic mirror, where your sequence reveals something about your sister, who was never a patient; and the ten-thousandth viewer, where the question is whether the wrong attenuates as the audience grows.

Sections, in this order:

**Requirements** (~600 words). Numbered, each with the case or consideration behind it and tagged non-negotiable or negotiable. Among the things I want adjudicated rather than assumed: that the theory must say what is distinctively lost when nothing further bad follows; that it must not let privacy collapse into autonomy or into security; that it must distinguish having privacy from waiving it or not caring about it; that it must not smuggle in a fixed inventory of sensitive categories; and that it must take a position on whether an unexercised permission or an unread record is already a loss, and on whether inferred facts can be private at all.

**Collisions** (~150 words). Which subset of your own requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and why. If the list turns out to be impossible, that is the result I most want to see.

**The sharpest requirement** (~120 words). The single one that excludes the most of the familiar families — control-based, access-based, reductionist, contextual-integrity.

**Scorecard** (~150 words). A table and nothing else, scoring those four against your requirements.
