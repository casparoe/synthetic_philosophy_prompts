Hi — I run a small reading group: eight backend engineers who've built on top of LLM APIs but have never trained anything bigger than a fine-tune. I've got about half an hour next week and I want them to actually feel the "eliciting latent knowledge" problem, not just nod along while I say it.

The setup I've been playing with, which you can keep or replace. A model reads a week of strain-gauge and camera logs from a suspension bridge and has to write the sentence an inspector would write: "no cracking detected", or "crack in cable 4". It is trained only to imitate the inspectors' written reports. Now: the camera has been dead for two days, and the rota'd inspector never showed up, and the sentence that gets produced in that situation is always "no cracking detected". The model outputs "no cracking detected". Two things could be going on inside — it worked out from the strain data that there really is no crack, or it worked out what sentence a tired inspector would produce and has no view at all about the cable. Both fit the training data equally well, and the output is right on both. I want that to be the uncomfortable bit.

Don't explain ELK to me. Plan the session:

- what has to be established in what order, and what depends on what. (I assume "the model's output is a different object from the model's state about the world" comes early, but I don't know what has to come before *that*.)
- whether the bridge case is the right thing to hang all of this on. I like that it has both a broken sensor and an absent human in it, but maybe that's one complication too many — what would you strip out, and what does the right version have to have and have to avoid having?
- the misconceptions this particular group will produce, in the order they'll say them outloud. I'm expecting "then just train it on labelled cracks", "that's just hallucination", and something from the interpretability side like "look at the activations and see which way they're pointing" — where in the sequence does each get headed off, and is any of them worth *using* rather than defusing?
- one closing question that tells me whether they've understood the thing or have just learned to repeat "the reporter might be a simulator".
- and what I should explicitly refuse to get into in thirty minutes.
