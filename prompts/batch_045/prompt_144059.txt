ok so I need a piece of satire for a thing I put together, and I want it to be the kind of thing that's funny to someone who actually knows social choice theory but still lands for everyone else. Here's the ask:

Write a page from the service catalogue of a fictional governance-design consultancy — give it a reassuring name — that sells "manipulation-proof" decision rules to committees, housing co-ops, unions, faculty search panels, that sort of client. The voice is completely earnest management-consulting brochure: deadpan, confident, an abbreviation for everything, no winking at the reader and no scare quotes around its own claims.

700-900 words. Structure: a short intro paragraph stating the problem in the firm's own words (roughly: if you offer people a genuine choice among three or more options, and everyone's vote counts, and every option can actually win, then somebody somewhere can profit from lying about what they want); then five numbered service tiers, each with a name, a one-sentence pitch, and two or three sentences of fine print; then one short testimonial from a satisfied client; then an appendix note.

The joke has to come from getting the Gibbard-Satterthwaite result right. Deterministic rule, single winner, three or more options, nobody ever benefits from misreporting at any configuration of preferences, every option able to win, and the conclusion it forces. A social choice person should nod, not wince. The catalogue is, without ever admitting it, a menu of the ways out, each one dressed up as a premium feature:

- a "Designated Outcome Arbiter" tier — the renamed dictator, with a support package for the Arbiter's morale and a paragraph insisting that "arbiter" is a technical term, not a colloquial one
- a "Domain Residency" tier — voters are coached until their preferences are single-peaked along the organisation's official Alignment Axis, at which point plain majority rule is safe
- a "Continuity Voting" tier — the status quo always wins, which is strategy-proof in the most boring way imaginable, because nothing anyone says matters
- a "Binary Discipline" tier — every decision is restated as a single yes/no on one proposal, because two options are safe
- a lottery tier, the Rotating Arbiter — and here I want the fine print to state the exact sense in which it really is strategy-proof (each voter can only ever move probability onto their own favourite) without overclaiming beyond that
- and if you want a sixth, a "Practical Immunity" tier: manipulation still exists but finding it is computationally expensive, with honest fine print admitting this is a speed bump, not a proof

Two concrete client vignettes in brochure voice, showing tiers in action: a 400-unit co-op deciding between the roof, the windows and a solar array, and a faculty search with three shortlisted candidates. At least one tier has to state its real cost completely straight-faced — the Arbiter's pick binds even when everyone can see it's wrong about the plumbing; Domain Residency quietly removes from the agenda any project that doesn't sit on the axis.

Hard rule: the brochure never notices what all five tiers have in common. The satire stays inside the pitch. No real named people. And it should still be legible to a smart reader who's never heard of the theorem — they should finish it convinced the cure is worse than the disease.
