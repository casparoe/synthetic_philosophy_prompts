I want a villanelle about the Kelly criterion — nineteen lines, the usual aba pattern with two refrains — in which the two returning lines are the two different answers to the question "how fast does wealth grow?", and in which their recurrence is what makes the second answer the right one: each time the lines come back, the bet has been placed again on everything that is left, so the poem compounds rather than merely repeats.

Refrain A must say that the average over a large ensemble of gamblers playing the same game simultaneously grows at the arithmetic rate. Refrain B must say that the one gambler who must go on playing grows at the arithmetic rate minus half the variance. Something along the lines of "Many at once will grow by the mean" and "One at a time will grow by the log" — reword for scansion if you must, but keep both claims intact, and keep the pun in the second line doing work (log as ledger, log as logarithm).

What I'll be checking: that betting the entire bankroll every round on a favourable game goes broke with probability one; that the growth rate peaks at the Kelly fraction, is symmetric about it — half Kelly and one-and-a-half Kelly each give three-quarters of the maximum — and turns negative past twice Kelly, so the greedy error is not the mirror image of the timid one even where the growth rate itself is; and that the final quatrain, where both refrains return one line apart, lets the gap between them stand for the gap between the two questions rather than declaring a winner. The poem should also concede, somewhere near the end, that Kelly's case is asymptotic and specific to log utility, and that someone with a fixed horizon and a different attitude to risk is entitled to bet less. Don't let the name of anyone do the work.
