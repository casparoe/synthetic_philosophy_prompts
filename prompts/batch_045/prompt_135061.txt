Hi — I'm a retired physiologist (ion channels, mostly, and a lot of grant paperwork) who's started sitting in on a philosophy of mind reading group at the local university, partly out of interest and partly out of stubborness. We got onto teleosemantics a couple of weeks ago and I think I follow the basic pitch — you naturalize mental content by saying what a representation is *supposed* to indicate, and you cash out the "supposed to" in terms of the selection or learning history of the mechanism that consumes it. What I can't get anyone to slow down on is that this seems to slide between three quite different ways of pinning down "what it's supposed to indicate," and I'd like an explanation of what the real choice points are and what you buy or give up with each.

Here's the version of the problem I keep turning over on my walks, and I've deliberately set it up so the usual epistemic complaint is off the table. Suppose we run a digital evolution setup — self-replicating agents in a simulated world, us with a complete log of every selection event, so nobody can say "we'll never know the selection history." One agent lineage evolves a sensory node N that fires on a proximal cue: small dark moving spots. For thousands of generations, every such spot was an edible midge, and the consumer mechanism that takes N as input triggers a strike that nets food. Now the environment shifts: 90% of the small dark moving spots are inedible ash flakes, the moths are nearly gone, N fires on the same proximal cue as before, and the strike mostly wastes energy. Then a second thing: a mutation makes some descendants' N fire on a new cue — a polarization pattern in the simulated sky — that reliably indicates good prey, the old consumer co-opts this signal, it boosts fitness, and after a while it's fixed. The questions I want explained rather than answered with a hand-wave: Is N's content "midge" (the property that did the historical selecting), so that almost every firing now misrepresents? Or is it something like "small dark moving thing" (what it actually covaries with), so that the tokens are largely veridical and the breakdown should be located in the consumer or in the strike's current uselessness instead? Or is it "something worth striking at" or "prey," i.e. is the grain of the content fixed by what the consumer needs from it? And in the second scenario, at what point does the polarization-cue version of N acquire a new content, given that content is supposed to be historical — does it misrepresent for a few hundred generations until selection catches up, or not represent anything at all until then, and is there a principled difference between those two descriptions or just a stipulation about when a function counts as "proper"?

The bit that I think is really bugging me, and where my own background keeps interfering, is this: in physiology we'd normally say the function of a mechanism is its current causal contribution to the organism's capacities — what it's doing now in the system it's in — and on that reading the historical stuff looks almost irrelevant to content. So please explain what work the etiological, history-based notion of function is doing that the current-causal-role notion can't do, especially for making sense of error: if content were just current covariation or current causal role, why would misrepresentation be hard to get, and is that difficulty the actual argument for going historical? And then, having laid out the options, tell me honestly whether the indeterminacy here is a substantive disagreement about what content is, or a choice of which notion to theorize with — and if it's the latter, whether the whole naturalization project survives that, or whether it was only ever going to be a story about which facts we get to call "content." A concrete worked response to my two scenarios would help me more than a general survey; I can follow technical distinctions fine, I just don't yet know which distinctions in this literature are load-bearing and which are decoration.
