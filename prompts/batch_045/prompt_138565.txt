My opening is a separation case. Consider a species whose members are all created at one instant, each stamped with a serial number, and who wake in an order unrelated to their numbers: a member can read the calendar, knows the date, and knows how many have woken so far, but their number is effectively a random draw from all the numbers that will ever be used. Run your argument on serial number. The update has exactly the form and strength it has for us — same assumption, same likelihood — and yet nothing follows about when the species ends, because number and date have come apart. All that follows is that not many observers have higher numbers. So the argument never delivers soon; it delivers few-after-me. And few-after-me is compatible with an extremely long run: on the usual estimates, a few thousand of them alive at a time for a billion years comes to roughly as many observers as the ceiling the argument itself allows, given how many have already lived. What licenses the step from few to soon, if not a separate premise about how large a species typically becomes if it survives at all — a premise the argument contributes nothing to and which is doing all the real work?
