A friend in my metaphysics reading group handed me this argument, and I have not been able to locate the mistake, even though I am confident the conclusion is false.

(1) Necessitation: if some facts Γ fully ground F, then it is metaphysically necessary that if all of Γ obtain, F obtains.

(2) Levelling: if Γ fully ground F, then the single conjunctive fact Γ* — the fact that all of Γ obtain — fully grounds F as well.

(3) Modal individuation: distinct facts are modally separable. Restricted to contingent facts: if F and G are distinct and each could have failed to obtain, then some possible world contains one of them without the other.

(4) Now take any paradigm case of grounding between contingent facts: this clay's being arranged thus grounding this statue's existing, my being in brain state B grounding my being in pain, the obtaining of the microphysical facts grounding the obtaining of the chemical facts. In each case let Γ* be the conjunctive ground and F the grounded fact.

(5) By (1), every world in which Γ* obtains is a world in which F obtains. By (3), if Γ* and F were distinct, some world would contain one of them without the other. So Γ* and F are not distinct: the grounded fact just is its own full ground.

(6) But grounding is asymmetric and irreflexive, while identity is neither. So if (5) is right, there are no derivative contingent facts at all: the statue is not built up out of, or metaphysically explained by, the clay's arrangement — it simply is that arrangement — and likewise for every other case.

(7) Worse, if I drop the restriction to contingent facts in (3), the same reasoning appears to show that there is only one necessary fact, since no necessary fact can obtain without any other.

(8) So either grounding collapses into identity and the fundamental/derivative distinction does no work, or one of (1), (2), and (3) is false.

I cannot accept (8)'s first horn, and of the claims in play I would most like to keep (1) and the claim that the paradigm cases in (4) involve two real and distinct facts. Which step should I reject, and why did it look innocuous? If I reject (3), what is supposed to make Γ* and F two facts rather than one, given that no possible world separates them, and can grounding still do explanatory work if it is not so much as a necessitation relation? If I reject (1) or (2) instead, what does that cost me? Please price the alternatives against one another rather than just picking one.
