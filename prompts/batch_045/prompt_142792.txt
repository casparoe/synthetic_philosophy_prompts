so i'm running a grad reading group on panpsychism and next week's session is the combination problem. instead of opening with yet another bullet-point list of the standard sub-problems (subject-summing, quality combination, the structure worry) i want to open with a poem — but i want the form to be doing real argumentative work, not decorating a position.

write me a sestina: six sestets plus a three-line envoy, normal end-word rotation. you pick the six end-words, and they should be the vocabulary of the problem — i'd want at least one naming the micro level, one naming the macro level, one naming whatever relation is supposed to do the combining (sum, fuse, compose, whatever you go with), a first-person possessive, and a word for a boundary or threshold. here's the constraint i actually care about: across the six stanzas you may not introduce a seventh end-word. if the poem is ever going to name a combined, macroscopic subject, it can only do it in the envoy, and only out of the words it has already spent on the little subjects. i want people to be able to watch the rotation trying to get something new out of rearranged old material, and judge for themselves whether it manages it.

content-wise it should be answerable to at least three things, and someone who's read this literature should be able to tell which one is being answered where:

- the granularity question: the poem never gets to say where the fundamental subjects bottom out, and i want that not-knowing to be felt in the verse rather than asserted in a line
- what happens to the micro-subjects once the macro one is there: do they persist alongside it, get absorbed into it, or were they never really seperate subjects to begin with. whichever you pick should show up in what the poem does, not in a gloss
- the swap test: if you replace one micro-subject with a functionally identical duplicate, is the resulting macro-subject the same one? if it is, the big subject doesn't seem to be built out of those particular little ones in any deep sense, and i want the poem to sit with that rather than resolve it
- the mismatch worry, that the structure of my experience (a visual field, a colour space) doesn't look much like what you'd get by summing a lot of micro-structure — fit this one in only if it doesn't crowd the others

keep it a poem, not a lecture, and definitely not a sermon for or against panpsychism; it should leave the tension open at the end. 39 lines is fine, and i don't mind if the envoy is a bit loose if the strict pattern makes it wooden.
