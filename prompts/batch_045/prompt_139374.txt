Ok so me and my friend Sam got into a massive argument last night about the site I'm on and I need someone to break the tie, or at least tell me what the argument is actually about.

The site: a small planned settlement, roughly forty near-identical houses, all thrown up in one building campaign over maybe fifteen years by some central authority, then lived in for about three generations. Here's the thing we're fighting over. Right near the end of the occupation, in what looks like a single episode, someone blocked the internal doorway between the front room and the back storage room in every single house. The stone threshold in each one was deliberately chopped through, the gap filled with plaster, and in twenty-two of them there's a dog or puppy burial in a small pit cut right under the blocking, with a smashed bowl. The blocked rooms kept their storage jars, so nobody was converting the space to anything else. No house is missing the treatment. The blocking deposits cut through occupation debris on the floors, so people were definitely living there when it happened. But the mortar is identical to the masons' mortar from the original build, the technique is identical in all forty, and in four houses the blocking is actually set into the original floor make-up, i.e. it went in at construction.

Sam says: obvious, this is a shared household-level protective threshold practice, everyone sealed off the dangerous doorway and put a dog under it, that's belief, print it. I say: forty identical interventions that look like one campaign, executed in the builders' own mortar, that smells like the administration closing the place down, and the residents may not have thought anything at all.

So here's what I actually want. My old PhD superviser, call her Renata, has ruled on a ton of cases like this over the years, in seminars and in comments on other people's papers, and I've never managed to get her to state the principle. Her track record, as best I remember it (paraphrasing, I don't have her exact wording):

- Twelve houses at a long-lived village, each rebuilt independently over centuries, each with a plastered niche in the same wall position containing broken figurines: she allowed a claim about repeated household-level practice, and refused to let the author call it goddess worship or anything else about the content of what people believed.
- One rich grave with a one-of-a-kind object and no comparanda: she threw out every inference except something like a prestige display staged by whoever did the burying.
- The same seal image on jar sealings across forty sites over two centuries, plus a workshop with wasters and recut seals: she allowed an administrative institution here, and she was explicit that what did the work was the trace of the mechanism that reproduced the image, not the fact that everybody had seen it.
- A monumental building with a restricted-access inner room and a raised platform: she allowed differential access and no more; kingship and organised priesthood both got struck out.
- The same figurine types turning up in both temple and house contexts: she refused to let the author interpret one context using the other without an argument, and allowed only that the setting changes what the object is doing.
- A room with benches, a basin and an ash layer that the excavators called cultic: she more or less lost it, said the word is being used as a bin for anything not obviously domestic, and that you either specify the practice or drop the label.
- A settlement that burned down with everything left on the floors: she's noticeably more permissive when the deposit is a single-episode snapshot than when it's a slow accumulation.
- State-issued storage jars found in every house of another planned town: she allowed a procurement and logistics inference and refused any inference about what the residents thought about storage, on the grounds that the jars came with the house.

Given all that, what would she say about my thresholds? I genuinely can't tell, because I can think of at least four different rules that fit her whole track record and they come apart on my case: something about independent repetition mattering (repetition only counts as evidence of shared belief when it's produced by agents who aren't copying each other or being handed the same thing), something about gratuitousness or cost (a feature has to be functionally pointless or expensive to be evidence of belief rather than of practice or logistics), something about needing a contrast class (if everything has the feature you can't tell a norm from a building spec), and something about mechanism traces being required before you get to use the word institution. Her verdicts so far are all consistent with each of those, and on my site they point in different directions.
