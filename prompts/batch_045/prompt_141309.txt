Hi — I run research at a small animal-welfare fund that's just decided to spend most of its next three years (call it £1.5m) on farmed fish and crustaceans, and I want a second opinion on the split before I write it up for the board.

The options on the table: more sentience work on decapods; welfare standards and auditing for shrimp hatcheries and salmon farms; slaughter tech (electrical stunners, ice-slurry chilling, banning air-asphyxiation); corporate campaigning and certification; alternative proteins; or plain capacity-building in Asia, where nearly all the animals actually are.

Three things I keep going back and forth on, and I want you to take a side on each rather than just summarise them back at me:

- the "small bodies" argument: if the capacity for suffering doesn't scale with body size, then suffering per kilogram of seafood is utterly dominated by shrimp, which pushes us towards Asia even though a UK fund has far less leverage there;
- a trustee's worry that welfare reforms just make farming look respectable and help it grow, so that only demand-side work isn't quietly self-undermining;
- my own hunch, which nobody has properly argued me out of, that most of the bad stuff is crammed into the last hour — harvest, crowding, chilling, killing — so standards about stocking density and water quality may be aimed at the wrong window entirely.

Also: should we fund someone to build a "pain-hours per kilogram of edible protein" metric so shrimp, salmon and chicken can be lined up on one scale, or is that a classic case of measuring what's measurable?

Give me a defended ranking of the two or three things you'd actually fund, the criteria your using (scale, tractability, neglectedness, how much other work depends on it), what you'd explicitly not fund, and what evidence over the next couple of years would move things up or down. And flag which of the open questions are empirical and which are properly conceptual — a decent chunk of the budget can go to philosophy.
