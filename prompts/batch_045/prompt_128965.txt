I sit on the committee that decides which populations in our state get listed as endangered, and I keep tripping over the same thing without having a word for it. Two cases from last year.

Case one: two lineages of freshwater fish that were separated in different drainages for a long time, tens of thousands of years at least, different enough that everybody calls them seperate species when they are apart. A canal connected the drainages about a century ago, they now breed with each other wherever they meet, and the mixed offspring seem to be doing fine. Ask whether these two have been going their own way and the answer is yes, emphatically. Ask whether they are going to end up as one thing and the answer also looks like yes, and sooner rather than later.

Case two: a moth population that shifted onto an introduced host plant a couple of hundred years ago. They mate on the host, so gene flow with the population still on the native host has dropped a lot, but it has not stopped. Ask the backward question and it is one species with some structure in it. Ask the forward question and it is two species in the making, with the split most of the way done.

So here is the pattern I cannot name. There are two questions that both sound like is it one species or two, one about whether two groups have been separate, one about whether they are heading for separate futures, and on exactly the cases that land in front of us they give opposite answers. It is not just that the different definitions disagree. I have read enough to know that story, the interbreeding one, the smallest-branch-on-the-family-tree one, and the rest, and I know the move where people say fine, those are different questions, use whichever one suits what you are doing. This feels like something else, because both of my questions are the same kind of question, about how the lineages are hooked up in time, just pointing in opposite directions. And the forward one has an extra problem nobody on the committee wants to touch: the answer depends on what you assume we will keep doing. Do we keep the canal open. Does the introduced plant stay. So one of the two questions seems to have a prediction about us buried inside it and the other one does not.

Does this have a name, this backward versus forward business and the fact that it comes apart? If it is just the usual pluralism thing again, tell me why I am wrong that it is different. If it is not, I would like it carved properly: what exactly the thing is, what it is not, the nearest established ideas and how mine differs from each, which of the two directions if either has the better claim to be what species talk is really tracking, and where the edge cases are. I am not after a label to slap on it, I want to know whether I am looking at a genuine fork or two ways of asking the same question.
