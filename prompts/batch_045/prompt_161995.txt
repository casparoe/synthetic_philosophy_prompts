Her position: the whole paradox is an artifact of philosophers obsessing over reasons, and real multi-member courts basically just vote on the outcome, and the stuff about which premises the court "endorses" is just about how the opinion gets drafted afterwards. My position: that can't be right, because an opinion is itself a collective object with authority — later courts take the *reasoning* as the thing that binds them, not just the verdict, so a court that reaches the right verdict via a route no majority actually endorses has produced something defective, even if the verdict is correct. We eventually agreed we were arguing about two different goods: getting the verdict right, and producing a set of propositions the group can actually stand behind as its rationale. I think that distinction is doing more work than the literature lets it do.
