I hold a specific version of the halfer position on the Sleeping Beauty problem, and I want to know what should dislodge me.

The version: Beauty's credence in Heads stays at one half on every awakening, because the fact that she is awake was guaranteed under both hypotheses and so carries no news. But she should act as a thirder: when a bet is offered on each awakening and settled once per experiment, she should take one-in-three odds, because the number of occasions on which she gets to answer is itself correlated with Tails. I treat the resulting gap between credence and betting odds as a genuine distinction between belief and choice rather than an error on either side, and I take that distinction to be the main strength of the position.

The case I find hardest is a variant I will call the Faint protocol. A fair coin is flipped on Sunday. If Heads, a single awakening is scheduled for Monday but delivered only with probability 1 minus p: with probability p no awakening occurs, Beauty sleeps through Monday, and the run ends without her being questioned. If Tails, the Monday and Tuesday awakenings are both delivered with certainty, as in the standard set-up. Beauty is told all of this in advance; the awakenings are subjectively identical and she cannot tell on waking whether it is Monday or Tuesday; memory erasure and the rest are as usual. Take p = 0.1.

As I compute it, the news that she is awake at all now genuinely discriminates between the hypotheses, so the credence half of my package yields P(Heads) equal to 0.45 over 0.95, roughly 0.474. But the frequency reasoning that drives the betting half yields the share of awakenings that are Heads-awakenings, 0.45 over 1.45, roughly 0.31. Both numbers fall out of my own view, and they come apart continuously as p is dialled from 0 to 1, the ratio between them rising from 1.5 to nearly 2; at p = 0 the same machinery returns the familiar one half and one third.

So: what would count against this package? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and distinguished by whether a committed defender of this version should concede them as counting against it or may properly dismiss them. Include formal or semi-formal defeaters, such as a Dutch book or scoring-rule construction that survives the belief-choice split, a violation of a reflection or diachronic coherence constraint, or a result showing the view collapses into thirding in some limit, alongside case-based and intuitive ones, including what the view should say about the Faint protocol and about analogues elsewhere, for instance anthropic or cosmological self-locating uncertainty. Tell me what my version predicts that plain halfing and plain thirding do not, and give an honest verdict on whether the belief-choice distinction has insulated the position into unfalsifiability and, if it has, whether that is a defect in a view of this kind. What would move me most is a construction in which the two halves cannot both be kept: either the credence must follow the betting odds, or the betting odds must follow the credence, or the package breaks.

Please answer in under 600 words.
