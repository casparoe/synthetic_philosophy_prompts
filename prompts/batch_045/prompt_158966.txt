I'm assembling case studies for a graduate seminar on the regress problem, and I need one that runs against the usual grain. The stock examples all press on the same point — that we are finite and so cannot have infinitely many reasons — which makes infinitism look like a non-starter by construction. I want the opposite: an ordinary case in which foundationalism and coherentism are both committed to counting a belief as justified, infinitism can withhold, and the pre-theoretic verdict about the case sides with infinitism.

Here is my half-formed idea about which feature does the discriminating work; build the case around it, or tell me in your notes why it fails. Call a chain of reasons non-collapsible when no link in it is a mere restatement, paraphrase, or inferential consequence of some link further down — every step has to contribute independent content. My suspicion is that infinitism's real bite is a demand for an endless non-collapsible chain, and that neither foundationalism nor coherentism imposes anything comparable, which is why they over-generate justification for subjects whose reasons quietly turn into restatements of themselves once you press them.

Construct one such case, in a single paragraph, in an ordinary contemporary setting. Constraints:

1. The subject is finite, competent, reflective and sincere. No ideal agents, no unbounded memory, no demons, brains in vats, implanted memories or skeptical science fiction.
2. The belief is one that the subject, and we, initially take to be firmly supported. It must rest on a source of a kind foundationalists standardly count as basic, and it must sit well enough in her total doxastic system that coherentism counts it as justified — say which kind of source you are using and why it qualifies. The case may not turn on the belief's being false, on a defeater she already possesses, on evidence she has ignored, or on Gettier-style luck.
3. The collapse must surface only under sustained reflection: each time she is pressed for a further reason she does produce one, and it is only after two or three steps that what she produces is recognisably the same content again, recycled.
4. Do not stipulate that no further reasons exist. Write her attempts so that the three verdicts fall out of the theories rather than out of the description.

After the scenario: state the belief; lay out the support structure as the ordered chain of reasons she actually produces; give the verdict of each of foundationalism, coherentism and infinitism with the reason for it; name the one load-bearing feature; say what happens to all three verdicts if that feature is removed (for instance, supply her with one genuinely independent further reason, or degrade the coherence of her system); and give the strongest thing a foundationalist and a coherentist would each say to stop the case from counting against them. Keep the prose tight — no more than a page in total before the structured summary.

Then close with a JSON object using exactly these fields: scenario (the one-paragraph case, verbatim); belief (one sentence); basic_source (the kind of source and why foundationalists count it as basic); support_chain (array of strings, the reasons she produces in order); verdicts (object with keys foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism, each an object with the keys verdict, rationale, and key_commitment_doing_the_work); intuitive_verdict (one sentence, plus whether it matches any theory); load_bearing_feature (one sentence); removal_test (what changes when the feature is removed); non_collapsibility_check (whether my non-collapsibility framing explains the divergence, and where it falls short); objections (object with keys foundationalist, coherentist); spec_check (array of objects with keys constraint and how_met, one per numbered constraint above).
