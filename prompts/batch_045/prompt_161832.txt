**Aunt Hillary's missing spotlight**

In Gödel, Escher, Bach, Hofstadter spends time on an ant colony he calls Aunt Hillary. Individual ants, on his telling, are like neurons: dumb, replaceable, and individually ignorant of what the colony is up to. What the colony has instead is a layer of states that no single ant carries — trail strengths, chemical gradients, the shape of the nest, the ratio of castes — and those states are what the colony acts on. The provocation is that Aunt Hillary, not the ants, is the candidate subject: she has concepts, maybe she wants things, maybe she's conscious, and we mostly resist the implication because we don't like the hardware.

The standard objections to this are bad. She has no brain — well, she has no neurons either, and the whole point is that you shouldn't expect the substrate to look familiar. She can't talk — that's a test for language, not for mind. It's only a metaphor — that's a refusal, not an argument. So here's a better try.

Claim: what Aunt Hillary has is a field, not a mind. Her states live in the world: in the trail network, in the architecture of the nest, in the colony odor. Individual ants read that field and write to it. (The term of art for coordination through the environment rather than through messaging is stigmergy.) Now, a field like that can be misleading — a trail leading to an exhausted food source is still a trail until it decays — but misleading is not the same as false. Falsity needs a subject that takes the state *as* saying something. Nothing in Aunt Hillary takes the trail as saying that there is food to the north. The ants don't: they don't form the thought and then act, they just run the routine. And the colony doesn't, because there is nothing in the colony over and above the ants plus the field to do the taking. So there is no state of Aunt Hillary that is a mistake *for her*. And without that, calling her states beliefs is charity, not description.

Hofstadter has a reply ready, and it's a good one: this is exactly what you'd say about a brain viewed from the inside. No neuron takes anything either, there's no homunculus reading the neural code, and yet the animal has beliefs. My answer is that brains have something colonies don't: a bottleneck. In an animal with a mind there is a severe capacity limit on what is globally available at any moment — attention, working memory, whatever you want to call it — and that limit is what makes some state into the thing the animal is currently treating as so. The bottleneck is where interpretation lives, because it's the one place where information becomes available at once to many different consumer systems, and where it can therefore be combined with information from other domains: the food-is-north datum meeting the predator-is-north datum and yielding don't-go-north-now. That is what makes a state a premise rather than a trigger. Aunt Hillary's field has no bottleneck. Trails don't compete for a channel, nothing is selected for global availability, there is no such thing as what the colony is currently attending to. She cannot be distracted; she cannot be attending to one thing rather than another; she cannot reconsider. She has a blackboard with no spotlight. No spotlight, no mind.

Two things I like about this. First, it does work on neighbouring cases. An immune system: parallel, no bottleneck, no mind. A market: same. A cockpit crew: arguably a bottleneck — one channel, turn-taking, an explicit shared "here's what we're treating as the situation" — which is uncomfortable, because it suggests three people on final approach have a better claim to be a subject than a colony that has been foraging for a decade. Second, it's at least in principle empirical: if someone showed that a colony has a genuinely capacity-limited, domain-general channel — a state which, when occupied, makes the colony measurably worse at handling an unrelated problem — I'd have to give the argument up.

Where I'm shaky. (a) I keep leaning on "takes as" and I have no non-spooky account of it; I gesture at bottlenecks but I haven't shown why a bottleneck is interpretation rather than just another causal relay. (b) There's a persistence argument against me that I don't have a good answer to. Aunt Hillary outlives her workers by something like an order of magnitude, and her memory is in the world rather than in anything that dies. So if you want a thing with a biography — a thing that learns in one year and uses what it learned in a later one, when none of the individuals are the same — the colony is a better candidate than any ant, and arguably better than any brain. I want to answer that a river delta also remembers floods and isn't a mind. But I suspect that's just prejudice dressed up as a principle.
