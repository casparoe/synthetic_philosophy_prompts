Setup so were talking about the same thing: by a market I mean a computable sequence P_1, P_2, ... of prices in [0,1] on the sentences of one fixed formal arithmetic (PA is fine), with a share paying $1 if the sentence is true. Arbitrage-free: no computable trader that sees the prices up to day n can push its wealth above every finite bound. Halting-competent: for every Pi_1 sentence — "machine e never halts" and its friends — P_n goes to 1 if it's true and to 0 if it's false, so in the limit the market gets the halting problem right without ever being able to prove the answers.

My half-baked claim is that one market can be both. My friend thinks it's impossible: she says anything that eventually commits to 1 or 0 on each halting sentence leaves a pattern — cheap now, expensive later, and reliably so on the true ones — that some computable trader can milk, and that the only dodge is keeping prices away from the extremes forever, which is exactly what kills halting-competence. I'm at 0.4 that shes right; she's at 0.8.
