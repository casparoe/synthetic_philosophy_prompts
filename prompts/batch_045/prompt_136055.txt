I serve on a committee drafting norms for a category of research in which any one of a few dozen labs could proceed on its own, and in which a mistake by any one of them would impose costs on everyone. I have been treating the unilateralist's curse as essentially a winner's-curse phenomenon: conditional on a project going ahead, it is disproportionately likely to be a bad one, so each lab should shade its own estimate of the value of proceeding downward, the way a bidder in a common-value auction shades its bid. On that framing I need two numbers, and I want their direction of scaling right before I commit to a model: how much shading each lab should apply, and how many independent reviewers each lab should consult first, on the assumption that more independent scrutiny lowers the noise that generates the curse. What I am least sure of is whether any of this belongs at the level of the individual lab's decision rule at all, and whether the right benchmark is a single well-calibrated decision-maker or some collective rule. Is the winner's-curse analogy the right way in, and if not, where does the problem actually sit and which of my two levers moves it?
