Here is my situation. I'm American, I moved to a small town in Germany about three years ago with my German partner, our kid is in the local Kita, and in the last couple of months two things happened at once: I somehow got talked onto the parents' council, and at work I became the person who signs off on the team's timesheets and expense claims. 5. A colleague quietly skips a pointless three-signature approval step so a shipment goes out on time, then tells our boss he did it. Fine, I'd defend him.

7. Rounding up an expense claim by about nine euros because the receipt didn't print. Not fine, and it bothers me *more* than case 6 bothers me, even though case 6 bends a bigger, more consequential rule.

10. Following a rule I think is stupid because we agreed to review it next quarter. Fine, no complaint.
11. Following a rule I think is actively hurting people, because there is a process for changing it. Not fine. Waiting for the process is no excuse here.
12. Going into the office with a cold because the team is short-handed. Genuinely back and forth on this one, can't settle it.

Here's the principle I've been trying out, which I suspect is broken: a rule is a tool that other people are relying on, so you may break one only when (a) the thing the rule is actually for isn't engaged in your particular case, (b) your breaking it doesn't take away the benefit other people get from it, and (c) you'd be happy for everyone else in the same situation to do the same; and breaking a rule openly is always better than breaking it and covering it up.
