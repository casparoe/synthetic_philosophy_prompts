Then he lays out this argument:

1. If everyone in the group prefers X to Y, then the group should prefer X to Y. (like, if we ALL want X over Y there's no way we should end up with Y)
2. On stuff that's purely personal — whether *you* watch a movie is only about you — you should get to decide, nobody else.
3. A vs C only differs in whether I watch, so by (2) that's my call, and I say C. So the group prefers C to A.

5. A vs B: we both prefer A. So by (1) the group prefers A to B.
6. So the group prefers C to A to B to C. That's a loop, so there IS no group preference.
7. So no possible decision rule for any group can satisfy both (1) and (2). Any rule that respects both of them blows up.
8. So there's no such thing as what a group wants. Every student council vote, every committee, every election is basically noise — we might as well flip a coin for everything, and the whole idea of the will of the people is incoherent.

I really dont want (8) to be true.
