I am writing a safety argument about a deployed language model, and part of it rests on claims of the form: at that point in the episode, the system believed that p. Before I rely on any such claim, I want the specification. Give me exactly five numbered desiderata that any account of when a belief attribution to an AI system is true — rather than merely a useful predictive shorthand, so that taking the intentional stance toward it says something about the system and not just about our convenience — has to satisfy. One or two sentences per item, no introduction and no closing summary.

Fix the purpose: the attribution has to be stable enough to carry weight in a written safety case, so it should survive a change of system prompt, of decoding parameters and of fine-tune. The cases I most want kept apart are these, and I want them distributed across the five rather than all handled at once: the same weights returning contradictory answers to two framings of one question, where it is unclear whether the believer is the weights, the deployment scaffold, or the session; an apparent belief that holds under one phrasing and dissolves under a paraphrase of it; a system whose training optimized it for predicted approval rather than for truth, so that the assumption that attributed states track real ones in a well-designed system fails structurally rather than in scattered local cases; and a first-person self-report of a belief that the system's own behavior contradicts, generated by the very machinery being interpreted. Let the fifth desideratum be whichever one you expect to exclude the most candidate theories, and say there if it cannot be jointly satisfied with any earlier item on your list.
