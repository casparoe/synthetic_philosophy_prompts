So I'm in a slightly stupid situation that's turned into a shouting match with my housemates and I need it settled before Sunday. I play Sunday league, and in training over the years I've taken maybe three hundred penalties against the same keeper, Mick — he's my mate's older brother, he's watched basically all of them. He's offered me a bet: ten penalties on Sunday, and before each one he writes L or R on a card, seals it and hands it to the ref. If he's written down the side I actually shoot, I pay him £20; if he's got it wrong, he pays me £30. He can't change the card after I strike the ball and I never get to see it. My honest estimate is that when I pick a side the normal way — quick think, go with what feels natural — he guesses right something like nine times in ten, and when he goes the right way he saves it, so the only ones I score are the ones where he guessed wrong.

Here's the part where we're going in circles. Whatever side I settle on, it feels like I should shoot the other one: if I've just decided to go left, that's strong evidence Mick wrote down left, because he knows me that well. But by the exact same reasoning, once I've switched to the right I should switch back again. My housemate who did a philosophy minor says this is a known thing, that it turns on whether you think in terms of causes or in terms of evidence — the card is already sealed so my kick can't change it, but my kick is also a damn good clue about what's on it — and that in the usual version of this puzzle neither option survives being looked at twice. I sort of follow that, but it doesn't tell me what to do at 2pm on Sunday against the pub team that knocked us out last year.

As far as I can see my options are: (1) just pick by feel and accept I'll score about one in ten; (2) flip a coin in my pocket on the walk up, which Mick can't see — my other housemate says that obviously makes it 50/50 and therefore worth taking (0.5 × £30 − 0.5 × £20 = +£5 a kick), but that feels like a trick that shouldn't be allowed to work; (3) deliberately stand there and agonise for twenty seconds, because when I overthink I do weird contrarian stuff and his hit rate on me drops to more like 60%; (4) let our right back Dave take them instead — he's a worse penalty taker, maybe 70% on target at the best of times, but Mick has no book on him whatsoever; (5) belt it straight down the middle, which I have literally never once done in my life and Mick knows it — although if I'm the sort of person who'd work that out, isn't that exactly the sort of thing Mick would have seen coming?

I'd like the practical verdict — take the bet or not, and which of those — but more than that I want to know what's actually going on. Is the coin-flip reasoning legitimate or is it a cheat? Does the fact that his accuracy depends on *how* I decide (gut vs. agonising vs. coin) change what counts as a rational way to decide, or is that the same confusion in a costume? And is there a real difference between "Mick predicts me" and "Mick has a very good model of how I've behaved up to now," because it feels like option 5 only works on the second reading and not the first? For what it's worth I don't think he's any kind of oracle — he's a 34-year-old plumber who's seen me take a lot of penalties.
