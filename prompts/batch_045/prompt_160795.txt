I help run a fortnightly reading group that has been working slowly through Foucault's mid-1970s book on the prison — we did the sections on hierarchical observation, normalizing judgment and the examination a few weeks ago and they are the ones we keep coming back to — and I also, by an accident of career, write internal communications for a mid-sized software company that last spring rolled out a program called the Open Ledger. Under the Open Ledger every employee's calendar, focus-time metrics and weekly self-assessment are visible to every other employee. There are no cameras, no designated watchers, and no managers with special access: the visibility is reciprocal, on the stated principle that observation is only oppressive when it runs one way. My group spent an entire evening arguing about whether making the gaze reciprocal escapes the mechanism or completes it. I am firmly in the "completes it" camp, and I want a piece of satire for the group's end-of-term zine that *demonstrates* that claim rather than argues it.

What I want is a parody, roughly 800 words, of an onboarding packet or internal FAQ for the Open Ledger — sections with headings, plus one boxed sidebar — written in the sincere, warm, faintly inspirational voice of internal communications: welcome to the program, here is how your visibility works, here is why it is different. The jokes have to come from getting the mechanics right rather than from inventing absurd ones: the visibility should be described as producing diligence, self-knowledge and a kind of inwardness rather than as merely forbidding anything; the weekly self-assessment should function as a confession that manufactures the thing it reports on; the periodic "calibration conversation" should combine ranking against a norm with the production of a written record about a person; the program should be discoverable as having invented a new kind of employee and new knowledge about it — I'd like a sidebar on "the ambient contributor," a category nobody had heard of before the metrics existed — and the company's "Dissent Channel," where employees register objections to the program, should itself generate the most valuable data the program collects. There should also be a short closing architectural note explaining that the office was reorganized into a ring around a central glazed atrium with backlighting that makes the center unreadable to those in the ring, mentioned purely as a design heritage point. Include at least one quoted skeptic from the pilot phase — someone in infrastructure who objects that mutual visibility cancels the asymmetry — and have the packet answer him in a way that is technically correct and quietly damning. Two constraints: the body of the piece must never use the words "panopticon," "discipline," "carceral," "biopower," "subjectification," or Foucault's name, and it must never wink — the narrator should believe every word, so that a reader who knows the material recognizes the diagram being drawn and a reader who doesn't just finds a slightly unsettling corporate document.

After the piece, add a brief note of about 150–200 words, "Where this leans," addressed to the reading group: separate the readings of Foucault that are broadly settled — that power on his account is productive rather than primarily repressive, that the examination, normalizing judgment and hierarchical observation do the work they do, that visibility functions as a trap, that the individual and the "soul" he writes about are effects of the apparatus rather than its raw material — from the ones that are genuinely contested among people who know the literature, and say plainly which is which. In particular I want you to flag which parts are your extrapolation rather than something Foucault says: the reciprocal-gaze twist is my group's invention, not his example, and I'd like you to say whether you think it follows from his account or strains it. Also say something honest about the live disputes — whether resistance is internal to power relations in a way that leaves critique with any footing, whether the late work on the self's own techniques modifies or abandons the earlier picture, whether the central mechanism is best read as a metaphor, a diagram, or a piece of real architecture — without presenting any contested reading as the consensus. No citations or page references; the note should be substance, not bibliography.
