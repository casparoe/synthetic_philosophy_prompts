Setup: I won a big book prize for a monograph and I have to give a 4–5 minute acceptance speech (600–700 words), and I want it to be about the thing that's been eating me for two years rather than a list of thankyous with one joke at the end.

- The book is about 900 pages and it's one long deductive argument in twelve chapters, not a pile of separate claims. Chapter 6 has the main theorem, and it runs through a lemma in chapter 3.
- I'm confident in chapter 6 as mathematics given the lemma. I'm much less confident in the lemma: if you made me bet on which single piece of the book is wrong, I'd bet on chapter 3. So the prize is for the part I trust most and that part rests on the part I trust least.
- My publisher put the usual line in the preface about any remaining errors being mine, and my mother, who will be in the front row, read it as modesty. It wasn't modesty. At ~900 claims each of which I'd put around 0.999, I shouldn't believe the whole thing is true. And because it's a chain rather than a conjunction, I'm not even in the ordinary preface situation where a bad claim just sits there harmlessly beside 899 good ones — one bad lemma takes the main theorem down with it.
- But I also can't stand up and say chapter 3 is probably wrong. I have no counterexample, I can't point to a step. And unlike a lottery, where you know for certain that some ticket wins while being confident of each ticket that it loses, I have nothing like that certainty that some link in my chain fails. So the preface line is doing a job nothing else in the room can do, and I want the speech to be honest about what that job is without becoming a lecture.

Audience, which is the hard part: maybe a third of the room are formal epistemologists who will catch it immediately if I imply deductive closure or quietly assume the errors are independent (they aren't — a bad method clusters, one confused afternoon produces a whole section). Several people are from the prize foundation and my editor, and they need one clean quotable sentence about the book mattering. And there's my family, who will hear anything I say about error as me disowning nine years of work.
