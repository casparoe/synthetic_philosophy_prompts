i'm on the internal deployment review committee at my lab and we have to rule on a proposal tomorrow morning, so i need a short memo i can circulate tonight.

The setup: we're about to give our next model real tool access internally -- repo write access, a terminal, its own logs. Our charter says every control has to be justified under the assumption that the model is misaligned, not under the assumption that it's mostly fine. The evals team accepts that, and they've built a full monitor: every action gets scored by a weaker model before it executes.

The part we're stuck on is their extra proposal, which they call an honesty window. One hour a week, the model is told that what it says in that hour won't be logged, won't be shown to the monitor, and won't count toward any deployment decision. The idea is that a model which would otherwise hide a misaligned goal, or admit to sandbagging on evals, or report that it already planted something, finally has somewhere safe to say so. Half the committee thinks this is the only way to ever collect evidence a model can't fake while it knows it's being watched. Half thinks the promise isn't credible to anything smart enough to scheme in the first place, and that if we actually honor it we've built an hour of blind spot into a system we're monitoring precisely because we don't trust it -- and that the first time we act on anything said in a window, every later window is dead.

There's a scheduling fight inside this too. Run the windows on a fixed random schedule, or only when the monitor has already flagged something? Conditional ones leak that the model is under suspicion; scheduled ones cost us coverage during exactly the hours we care about.

Write the memo: approve, reject, or approve with conditions and a time frame, with reasons the evals team could actually argue against rather than just "monitoring is good." And give the losing side its best case honestly -- i think the strongest thing against us is that refusing to ever open an unobserved channel is a decision to only ever hear from a model that knows its being tested.
