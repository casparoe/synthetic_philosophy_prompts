I spent thirty-five years in experimental biophysics, mostly on ion channels, and have only since retiring begun to read philosophy of mind with any care. I have become stuck on a question that reaches back, I gather, to Hofstadter's careenium: picture a lump of a hypothetical substance that genuinely cares, built out of particles so arrangable that they can simulate any process you please, so that everything about the lump admits both a particle-level story and a story told in the symbols its high-level description traffics in. The question is whether a symbol, as such, can reach down and push its own substrate around — whether the high-level story ever does causal work that the particle-level story does not already do.

I have been trying to get a grip on this with a diagnostic of my own devising, which I have not found named anywhere. Any apparent downward push, it seems to me, must be executed by some part of the substrate that reads the symbol and then acts on its neighbours; call that part the actuator. What interests me is not whether the substrate changes when the symbol is present, but how much of that change is owed to the symbol itself once you discount (i) whatever the actuator was going to do anyway and (ii) whatever the person or process that arranged the substrate supplied. Call this the symbol's net traction; it comes in degrees and can be zero. For calibration, my zero case is the pure shadow: a high-level pattern that is only an economical redescription of the particle-level events and displaces nothing.

Here are five arrangements of the same lump, differing mainly in where the actuator sits. Please rank them by net traction, most to least.

1. Engineered readout. In one region of the lump sits a small purpose-built device whose sole job is to detect the symbol SHORTAGE and, on detecting it, dump heat into its immediate neighbourhood, rearranging the particles there. The effect is real, local and repeatable, but the actuator is special-purpose and the layout is the work of an arranger.

2. Generic, undedicated actuators. No dedicated device anywhere. The lump simulates an insect colony: at the colony level there are symbols for trails, castes and tasks that no single unit in the colony represents or could represent, and yet those symbols determine where the units go and what they carry. The units themselves are the actuators, and they are the same general-purpose machinery that produced the colony-level pattern in the first place.

3. The self-symbol loop. The lump has built up a symbol for itself, and activations of that symbol feed back to retune the very machinery that produces it: a self-model whose outputs reconfigure that machinery's own sensitivities or its own plasticity. Here reader and read are the same generic stuff, and there is no arranger beyond the lump's own history.

4. Selectional copying, blind to the symbol. No actuator inside any single lump, and no change in any single lump's trajectory: lumps that happen to bear the symbol get copied more often than lumps that do not, by a mechanism entirely insensitive to the symbol, responding only to some crude physical difference that covaries with it. The symbol changes what exists downstream without, in any individual lump, moving a single particle.

5. Exclusion instead of force. The symbol injects no force and trips no actuator; it constrains. Given the arrangement of which the symbol is a part, most of the trajectories the particles' own dynamics would otherwise permit are not available, and only a narrow band remains. Whether closing off trajectories counts as pushing them around is part of what I am asking.

Give me the ranking as exactly five numbered lines, ordered from most to least net traction, one line per arrangement. Open each line with the arrangement's label and a 0-to-10 net-traction score in brackets, then one or two sentences on why it lands there — specifically, what you discounted and what survived the discounting. If two of them are genuinely tied, or if the ordering among the top ones is an artefact of my diagnostic rather than a real difference in the cases, say so in the line where it matters. No introduction and no conclusion, and please do not cite or name any authors: I want the reasoning, not the literature.
