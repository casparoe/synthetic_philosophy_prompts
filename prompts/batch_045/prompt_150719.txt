I am a PhD student in philosophy of physics, finishing a paper on what actually carries the thermodynamic accounting in the exorcism of Maxwell's demon. The claim I want to defend is that the familiar Landauer–Bennett story — the demon acquires a bit for free, measures reversibly, and settles up when it erases its memory at kT ln 2 per bit — is a special case rather than the general explanation, and that once erasure is genuinely off the table the second law still collects, but from somewhere else. To argue that I need one carefully engineered case, and I would like you to build it to specifications.

What I need is a demon that works and does not erase.

Specifications:

1. One heat bath at temperature T, at thermal equilibrium. No second temperature, no pre-existing gradient, no external power supply, no smuggled fuel.
2. The device runs a strict cycle: at the end of every cycle the demon's own physical state is what it was at the start, so it could in principle run indefinitely, and it delivers net positive work per cycle on average to a macroscopic load. Not a lucky fluctuation: a systematic, repeatable surplus.
3. The demon is dumb and wholly physical: a handful of internal states at most, coupled to the gas by ordinary contact interactions. No intelligent agent, no observer, no stipulation that the demon knows which side a molecule is on. Anything it acts on must be a physical correlation it has itself established by a dynamical process.
4. Erasure is off the table by construction. At no point is any bit destroyed, reset, or overwritten by an irreversible operation. For each step — establishing the correlation, the conditional action, closing the cycle — show how it could in principle be run reversibly and dissipationlessly. If some step cannot be, say so and pay the cost explicitly instead of hiding it.
5. No free clean bits. If any register has to begin a step in a known standard state, something in the story must supply it, and that something belongs in the ledger.

If conditions one through five turn out to be jointly unsatisfiable, I want the diagnosis rather than a fudge: tell me which condition has to give, show why, and then give me the nearest constructible case that survives.

Alongside the scenario I need the demonstration that it does what I have asked of it:

- The verdict of each candidate exorcism on this case: the Szilard-style one that puts the cost in acquiring the bit; the Landauer–Bennett erasure exorcism; the objection that a device in thermal contact with the gas cannot measure without dissipation; and the information-thermodynamic accounting, whether you present it as a mutual-information bound of the fluctuation-theorem kind or as an information reservoir being consumed. For each: does it explain the case, over-explain it, or fail on it, and in one sentence why.
- An honest note on which single feature of the design is load-bearing, what happens to the case if you delete that feature, and what happens if you instead allow the demon to erase. My expectation is that Landauer returns there as a special case of something more general; tell me if that expectation is wrong.
- The strongest thing a defender of the erasure-based exorcism would say against the case. I want to preempt it in the paper, so no strawmen. In particular I expect the charge that writing onto whatever medium you use is erasure relocated rather than eliminated, and I want to know whether it lands.

Audience for both the case and the analysis: readers of a philosophy of physics journal — comfortable with entropy bookkeeping, allergic to hand-waving, and indifferent to the history for its own sake.

- Entropy ledger — a small table giving, per step, the entropy change of the gas, of the demon, and of any information-bearing medium, plus the work delivered; about 150 words plus the table
