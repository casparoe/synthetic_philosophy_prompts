A year ago a consumer DNA test told me that the man who raised me is not my biological father, and that there were two candidates for that role. Since then I have been unable to let go of a question that used to be academic for me: if the other man's sperm had done the fertilizing, would the resulting child have been me, or only someone very like me? I went to the origin-essentialist side of the debate hoping for a clean answer, and I came away with an argument whose conclusion I cannot accept but whose steps I cannot break.

(1) Necessarily, if I actually originated from sperm s and egg e, then there is no possible world in which I exist and originate from anything other than s and e. (This is the familiar intuition that Elizabeth could not have been born of different parents, and it is the claim I went in wanting.)

(2) Very little of the gametes' matter becomes part of the embryo in the ordinary case: the sperm's tail and mitochondria are dismantled rather than passed on, and the organism is built out of the egg's cytoplasm and the two pronuclei. So a difference confined to matter that is dismantled makes no difference to the organism that later exists.

(3) So if (1) is true, what is essential about my origin cannot be the gametes' matter as such. It has to be something that survives into me, which in practice means the genome formed at syngamy.

(4) But that genome is a sequence of several billion base pairs, and there is no principled cut-off between genomes that are the same and genomes that differ. If a difference of one base pair, in a non-coding region that is never transcribed, is compatible with the same origin, then by a long chain of single-base-pair steps so is any genome whatever.

(5) So either (a) any genome would have done, including one that would have come from an entirely different sperm and egg — in which case (1) is false, and the other candidate's child would have been me; or (b) a single silent base-pair difference means that I never exist at all and someone exactly similar does, in which case whether I exist turns on a difference that makes no difference to the organism, its life, or any experience anyone could have.

(6) Both horns are unacceptable to me, so (1) must be false or at least unmotivated.

If I have to choose, I would rather keep something in the neighborhood of (1) than accept (5a). But I want to know what I am paying, and I suspect the price is higher than I think. Which step should I reject, and why did that step look safe? I have been told that counterpart theory, haecceitism, and four-dimensionalism are supposed to help here, but I cannot see how any of them touches (3) through (5) rather than relabeling the problem, so please say what each of those costs if they are relevant.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and no restating of my question. American English throughout.
