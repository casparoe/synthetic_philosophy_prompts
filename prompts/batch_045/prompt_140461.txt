Our TA is firmly on the reductionist side, and in section last week he explained the whole thing with this analogy: a shared intention is like a shared Google Doc. Everyone has their own copy open on their own machine; the copies get synced so they all say the same thing; everyone can see that everyone else has the same version, and see that everyone can see it; the doc has an edit history and lives on a server rather than in anybody's head; and if you close your laptop it doesn't stop existing. He said that once you see this you don't need a spooky group mind or an irreducible plural subject: "we intend to paint the house" is just each of our copies of the plan plus the mutual awareness that they're copies of the same plan, and all the supposedly collective-looking stuff — commitment, persistence, the roles people take on — is just features of the sync protocol. I sort of want to push back in my paper (I'm attracted to the plural-subject style of view) but the analogy is doing more work than I expected, and I can't tell where it's honest and where it's smuggling.
