Until my retirement five years ago I held a research chair in molecular biophysics, and I have since been sitting in on a graduate reading group in philosophy of biology at my old university. This term the group has been working through Daniel Dennett's *Darwin's Dangerous Idea* (1995), and the discussion has repeatedly turned on two pieces of vocabulary I had never encountered in thirty-five years of laboratory science: the contrast between cranes and skyhooks, and the companion charge of greedy reductionism. The book's own gloss, as I took it, is that respectable reductionists explain apparent design without skyhooks, whereas greedy reductionists try to explain it away without cranes. I find both terms used with considerably more confidence than they are defined, and I would like to know what precise work they could be doing.

Two episodes from last week prompted this. First, a cosmologist who was visiting the group argued that anthropic selection over an ensemble of universes with varying constants explains why our own constants look tuned for chemistry. A philosopher in the group replied that this is a skyhook with extra steps. Second, when another member proposed to explain the directedness of the genetic code — the fact that a codon can be said to be for an amino acid — in terms of nothing but local chemistry and selection history, a different member accused her of greedy reductionism. In both cases I could not tell whether a real objection had been made or merely a slogan deployed.

What I would like is a menu, not a verdict. Please set out the distinct precise theses that the claim that some proposed explainer is a skyhook could be asserting, ordered roughly from the trivial to the radical. Candidate distinctions I can already see but cannot adjudicate: an explainer that is itself unexplained, and so merely stops a regress; an explainer no less organized or contrived than the phenomenon it is meant to account for, so that no design work is actually transferred; an explainer that smuggles in mentality, purpose or agency; an explainer that lies outside or violates the laws it is supposed to operate within; an explainer posited solely to save the phenomenon, with no independent support; and an explainer that merely relabels what was to be explained. There are doubtless others, and some of these may collapse into one another. For each, say whether it is near-trivial, substantive and defensible, or straightforwardly false, and then — this is the part I care about — whether it would actually vindicate the charge against the multiverse proposal, or the charge against the chemical account of the code. I would also like to know what greedy reductionism would have to mean for that accusation to bite, whether the two vices are genuinely symmetric or whether one can be accused of both at once, and where, in your view, the vocabulary is doing rhetorical rather than argumentative work.

Two further things I would like the menu to be tested against. As a physicist I arrived at my own acid test: an explainer is a crane just in case it can be exhibited as a mechanism operating on parts each of which is less organized than the product. By that test Newtonian gravitation, with its action at a distance, looks like a skyhook, which makes me suspect either my test or the term. And I would like the readings applied to a case the book does not discuss: a self-assembling autocatalytic network that produces the very organization whose assembly it requires — a crane that appears to lift itself into existence. Please tell me which readings classify such a thing as a crane, which as a skyhook, and which refuse the question. A table I can hand round at the next session would be ideal.
