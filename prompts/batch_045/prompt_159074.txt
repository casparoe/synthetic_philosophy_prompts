In a graduate seminar on testimony, my TA keeps repeating the line "testimony transmits knowledge, it doesn't generate it" — most recently to kill my claim that a layperson who accepts a scientific consensus (say, that a given vaccine is safe) purely on the authority of the researchers thereby knows that it is safe. His gloss: since the layperson cannot himself assess the reliability of the sources, his true belief is at best borrowed, and borrowed isn't known.

Please set out, as a labelled menu, the distinct precise theses that slogan could be asserting — distinguishing at least: the near-trivial point that the content originates with another speaker rather than with the hearer's own investigation; the evidential or inferential reading on which testimonial warrant is always derived from the hearer's own non-testimonial evidence about the speaker or the assertion; a weaker reductionist reading on which all that is required is a defeasible background reason to take testimony to be generally reliable together with the absence of defeaters; an inheritance reading on which warrant passes from speaker to hearer without the hearer's own reliability evidence, so that the hearer's status is second-hand in being dependent rather than in being inferred; a demanding reading on which one knows on testimony only if one could vindicate the source if challenged; and any reading on which transmission requires the source herself to know, so that a speaker with a merely reliable true belief passes nothing on. Note, where relevant, whether a reading is a claim about justification, about knowledge, or about the causal or historical structure of the chain.

For each reading, mark it as trivial, substantive and defensible, contentious, or false, say what the strongest objection to it is, and state whether it would actually give the TA his conclusion about the deferring layperson — taking into account that even reductionist readings may allow credentials, institutional checking, and track records to supply the needed evidence. Close with a single line of the form \answer{...} listing the labels of the readings that genuinely support his dismissal, or \answer{none} if none do.
