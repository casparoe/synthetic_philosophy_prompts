My friend keeps pushing what he calls a "deferred assent" rule for constitutional entrenchment: to lock a provision in you need two separate approvals separated by at least one general election, the second vote is taken by an electorate that includes everyone who will be eligible at that point (so if the voting age is coming down, the newly enfranchised get to ratify something passed before they could vote), and the thing being locked has to be a constraint-type provision — a right, a structural limit — not a ordinary policy. His claim is that this is a real Ulysses contract at the level of a polity, i.e. the later majority is genuinely bound and not just inconvenienced. I'm at maybe 0.45 that a device like this generates a defeasible duty for the later electorate to keep the provision rather than treat it as repealable at will; he's at something like 0.75 and I can't locate the disagreement.
