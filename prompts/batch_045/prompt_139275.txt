For a reading group on animal minds I've been asked to put where I actually stand on one page so everyone else can take shots at it, and I'd rather find out now if it hangs together. Here's my profile:

(1) Consciousness is just there being something it's like — no self-concept, no metacognition, no higher-order thought required. A creature can be in a state without having any notion that it's in it.

(2) What matters morally is valence: the capacity to suffer and enjoy, not rationality or language or autonomy, and creatures count roughly in proportion to how much they can suffer.

(3) The best neural story we've got is a broadcast-or-bottleneck one — information from separate sensory and motivational systems being made available all at once to lots of downstream consumers — and that's what I lean on when I place a line anywhere.

(4) My own home-made criterion, which I trust more than the published marker lists: the override test. A creature passes if one option wins on every cue currently available to it, it takes the other option anyway, it does it on the basis of information it's carrying about something it can't currently perceive, and nobody trained it on that particular trade-off.

(5) Verdicts: mammals and birds in; fish probably in; octopuses probably in, even though most of their neurons are in their arms; insects I'd bet against, and yes I know about the bee results; nematodes out, and I'm unmoved by the fact that they run on the same neurotransmitters we do.

(6) I also think consciousness comes in degrees and I don't expect a bright line anywhere in phylogeny, which is part of why I'm comfortable answering in probabilities.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it — I'd guess at least one of my worries is fake and I'm missing the real one. Rank whatever tensions there are by severity, say for each what would have to give and what that would cost me, and then tell me which single revision cleans up the most. Please no survey of theories of consciousness, and keep it to about a page.
