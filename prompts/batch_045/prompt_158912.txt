Write a satirical extract from a government procedure manual — roughly 800 words, in a flat, style-guide-bureaucratic register, organized as numbered clauses with headings, cross-references, and form numbers. The document is the Manual of Effective Notice issued by a jurisdiction whose courts have held that an announcement produces legal effects only if it is common knowledge in the strict sense: everyone knows it, everyone knows that everyone knows it, and so on without end. The agency administering it is the Office of Public Announcements; its inspectors certify notices, void them, and fine people for void ones.

The comedy has to come from the epistemology being right, so that someone who works on common knowledge laughs rather than winces. Build the clauses around these real features, exaggerated by being taken literally as administrative law:

- Certification is only ever to a finite depth: the office issues Level-k Certificates of Mutual Knowledge, and there is a grey market in forged deeper ones, with an Inspector General notice warning that a depth-three certificate is worthless for any coordination whose justification needs four levels.
- A clause on lossy channels: where messages can fail to arrive, no finite exchange of delivery confirmations suffices, so a plan requiring simultaneous action — a dawn attack, a coordinated withdrawal of funds — is void however many confirmations were logged, and the office distinguishes approved public channels from merely reliable private ones.
- A rationality clause: every notice presupposes that its recipients are rational and that this is common knowledge; a citizen who publicly announces an intention to act irrationally therefore invalidates every notice pending against him, including the notice of the penalty for doing so.
- A licensing clause for markets: because parties who share priors and know one another to be rational cannot gain from voluntary exchange, traders must file a Prior Divergence Disclosure or a certified private-information claim, and trades lacking one are presumed void.
- A settlement clause: after any conference, the parties must file identical posterior probability statements; residual disagreement is treated as evidence of an undisclosed private prior and triggers an audit.
- A clause on long sequential contracts: because a chain of trusting moves requires common knowledge of rationality to a depth equal to the length of the chain, parties buy bounded-rationality riders and irrationality insurance, and contracts longer than the parties' certified depth are voidable.

Keep the irony deadpan and legible: no character steps forward to explain the joke, and the reader should be able to tell what is being mocked without the Manual ever winking. Close with a self-referential final clause in which the Manual notes that its own provisions bind only if they are common knowledge, and supplies a form number for requesting certification of that status — a form that, on the Manual's own rules, cannot validly be filed.
