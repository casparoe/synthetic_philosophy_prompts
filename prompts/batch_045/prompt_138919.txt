I keep running into the same shape of judgement and I can't find a word for it, so bear with me while I describe it badly.

Two cases. A friend of mine lives with chronic pain, and when she talks about whether a treatment is worth it she'll happily trade a mildly annoying side effect for a mildly pleasant one — she does that kind of arithmetic all the time, it's completely normal tradeoff behaviour. But there's a point where it stops. She describes it as the pain "stopping being a sensation and becoming a place she lives in," and past that point she says no amount of nice stuff makes up for it: not a lifetime of it, not nice stuff for a hundred other people either. The thing is she'll still compare two bad days and say one was clearly worse than the other, and she'll still take a bad day to avoid a worse one. So the scale hasn't broken. It's more like a bridge between the two sides has gone out.

Second case: I sit on a hospital committee were we weigh changes to a service, and the same reaction keeps showing up. We'll weigh a change that makes a large number of people slightly better off against one that leaves one person in quite severe pain, and everyone around the table treats the first pile as if it just can't get there — not because mild goods are worthless in general (we happily count them against each other and against mild bads), but as if there's a line past which the currency stops exchanging. And it only seems to run one way: nobody at that table thinks one person's intense pleasure lexically beats any amount of mild pain, they just think severe pain beats any amount of mild pleasure.

So: is this one thing or three? I've seen "negative utilitarianism" used for the rough idea that suffering matters more than happiness, but that isn't quite what I'm describing, because mild pleasures still clearly count below the line and still get weighed against mild pains. "Lexical" I've only ever seen applied to whole categories trumping each other, not to one region of a single scale trumping another region of the same scale. And "incommensurable" seems wrong as well, since comparisons inside each region are fine, even precise.

There's a further bit that bugs me and I haven't seen anyone address. Chop up the bad thing: is an hour of severe pain just ten six-minute episodes of a moderately bad pain? If it is, and if each slice is the kind of thing a small pleasure can compensate, then a big enough pile of small pleasures ought to get you across the line after all, and the whole structure collapses into ordinary commensurability. If it isn't — if severity is a property of the whole episode and not of the parts — that looks like a substantial commitment of its own. Also the line itself is fuzzy, which I suppose makes it a sorites problem too, but I care much more about whether the structure is coherent at all than about where exactly the line sits.

So does this shape have a name, and if it sort of does, which of the nearby concepts actually fits it and where precisely does it come apart from the others? I don't want a label dropped on me without the carving: I want to know what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, and where its edges are — including whether the chop-it-up problem is a real objection or just a confusion about how pain aggregates over time. If there's genuinely nothing established, give me a term plus a definition sharp enough to decide the cases above, and give me the strongest reason to think the thing I'm describing might not be coherent in the first place.
