pls write me a press release with an FAQ attached for a startup launching an app called FlexScore, which claims to measure how much free will a person has by scoring how variable their behaviour is across repeated trials: nudge the situation, and if you do the same thing anyway you are running on rails, while if you do something different you are an agent. About 600 words, straight-faced founder-speak throughout, no winking at the reader.

The humour has to come from the actual content, so let the app inherit the logic of the standard digger-wasp story: the wasp paralyzes a cricket, drags it to the mouth of its burrow, goes inside to inspect, comes back out, and if you move the cricket a few inches while it is in there it drags it back and inspects again, apparently without end. That is, the app treats rigidity-under-intervention as the signature of a machine and flexibility as the mark of a free agent. Then let the reduction happen by itself instead of announcing it: the highest scorers should turn out to be things nobody wants to call free (a random number generator, someone with a tremor, a very distracted person, a system that just got noisier), while reliable responsiveness to reasons scores terribly, since someone who always reads the sign the same way, ties their shoes the same way, or gives the same answer when nothing relevant has changed looks maximally rigid. Maybe a premium subscription tier that injects a little randomness to boost your score, and an FAQ item from a user whose score dropped after they got better at their job, asking whether that is a bug. Keep it deadpan and let the reader do the recoiling; dont slide into a general rant about determinism.

At the end add two or three lines on where the piece takes liberties with the ideas it is satirizing, for instance whether the wasp story is even accurate as usually told, whether anyone actually holds the crude flexibility-as-freedom test, and whether a genuinely reasons-responsive agent could ever score well on such a metric, and flag the single point you are least confident about.
