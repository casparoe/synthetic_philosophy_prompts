I'm putting together a little set of in-jokes to open the sessions of a reading group I run, and this month we've been stuck on the fanaticism debate — the thing where an agent with an unbounded utility function seems committed to paying any finite cost for an arbitrarily tiny probability of a big enough payoff, so that some lottery-ish option beats every ordinary option no matter how small its probability gets. Morale is flagging, so I want something funny to start with. Could you write me a joke about expected value fanaticism?

Couple of constraints, because I've been burned before. It should still work as mild amusement for someone who's never heard of the debate — a friend of mine from philosophy of science is coming and doesn't work on this at all, and I dont want her sitting there politely while everyone else laughs. But the actual punchline has to turn on the real mechanics rather than generic big-number humor: I want it to be about the way a shrinking probability gets outweighed by a growing payoff, and about how that's a different commitment from just being risk-neutral over money, and a different one again from caring a lot about the far future. Ideally the joke would be unusable as a joke about longtermism generally — someone who only got that "these people like huge speculative numbers" should not be able to tell why it's supposed to be funny.

Setting-wise, here's what I had in mind, though use it or don't: four of us are stranded on a rainy train platform deciding whether to walk fifteen minutes to the good restaurant or eat at the grim one beside the station, and one of us — call him Dev — has taken out his phone and started computing. Whatever you do, pls no Pascal's-mugging bit, and nothing that's secretly just "effective altruists are weird." And if you can build in a second, sharper reading for the version our group actually stalled on — where the utility function is technically bounded, but the bound is astronomically large, so you get a slightly tamer fanaticism anyway — that'd be the cherry on top. After the joke, give me one sentence on which line is doing the philosophical work.
