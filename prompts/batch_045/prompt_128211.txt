in the group chat I'm in (mostly ML people, one logician, one poet, don't ask) there's an argument that comes back every couple of months about whether AI will ever be genuinely creative. The ML people's version goes: creating is to checking as NP is to P. Writing a proof, or a joke that lands, or a sonnet, is stumbling around an enormous search space; recognising that the thing someone handed you is good is quick. P vs NP is that gap made precise. So P ≠ NP draws a real line in nature, creativity is on the far side of it, and no amount of scaling or compute gets you there — you just get faster checking and better search heuristics. Which they use to conclude that creative work is the one thing we don't have to worry about. One guy even runs it backwards: if P were equal to NP then creativity would be mechanical, we're clearly not mechanical, therefore P ≠ NP.

The logician says the whole framing is off and I half believe her but can't say exactly why. The two things she keeps repeating are: (1) 'checking' in NP means a polynomial-time verifier reading a short certificate — formal proofs are sort of like that, but there is no certificate for 'this sonnet is good' and no referee that reads one; and (2) theoremhood in ordinary first-order logic isn't in NP at all, it's undecidable, so for the examples that actually feel like creativity the easy/hard split isn't the one the analogy advertises.

Can you audit it properly? I'd like the correspondence table first — what's playing the role of the instance, the certificate, the verifier, 'polynomial in what', and who or what supplies the problem instance — and then the places the mapping comes apart. I especially want the part about what the analogy silently borrows from the proof case (a fixed formal system, a certificate whose length you can bound in advance, a check that always terminates, a clean difference between producing and grading) and quietly hands to sonnets and jokes that don't have any of it. Then the bit that bugs me and that nobody in the chat has addressed: P ≠ NP is a worst-case, asymtotic claim about Turing machines, so it's compatible with every instance we actually care about being easy, and P = NP with a galactic constant or a non-constructive proof wouldn't make anything mechanical either way. So is the analogy even capable of supporting the conclusion, or was it carrying the whole thing? Is the reversed argument any better? And if there's a better analogy for whatever is actually true here, give me that too — ideally in a form I can paste back into the chat without it turning into another two hundred messages.
