My friend Sanne is Dutch, has lived in Kyoto for six years, is married to a Japanese guy, and has very firm views about wether it's ok to lie. We're trying to write something together about white lies and I keep failing to work out what her actual rule is, so maybe you can. The obvious story would be that she's just blunt about everything the way the stereotype says Dutch people are, but she isn't — she's completely fine with some kinds of tatemae and not at all fine with others, and I can't find the pattern. Here's her track record as best I remember it. Telling her mother-in-law that dinner was delicious when it was dry and oversalted: fine. Telling her boss that Friday isn't possible for a report she could actually finish if she gave up her weekend: not fine. Telling a stranger on the train that the seat beside her is taken when it isn't: not fine. Telling her husband that lunch with his mother was fine when it was tense: not fine, and she got quite heated about that one. Telling a colleague that his newborn is beautiful when she privately thinks the baby looks like a potato: fine. Saying "soon" when a Japanese friend asks when she's visiting next and she has no plans at all: fine, nobody takes that as a plan. She also says that volunteering that a coworker's haircut is unflattering when nobody asked is bad, but not because it's a lie — just because it's a jerk thing to do. And there's one verdict neither of my principles explains: she thinks lying to her own mother in the Netherlands about whether she's eating properly is totally fine.

The two rules I keep coming back to are the gesture rule — whether the exchange is really an information transfer or just a friendly noise, settled by the local convention and by who's asking — and what I've been calling the refusal test, that you're allowed to answer falsely anywhere you'd also have been within your rights not to answer at all, and you owe the real answer exactly where you owe an answer. Both fit most of the record and I can't get them to come apart, so I've built a case that might do it.

The whole family is at dinner. Sanne's sister-in-law Aya has been job hunting for months and has just had a third rejection; she knows Sanne has seen a lot of Japanese CVs. In front of everyone, in Japanese, Aya asks whether her resumé is any good. Three things she could say: "it's great, you'll be fine"; "your experience is really strong, let's sit down and go through it together"; or "the format is going to get you screened out, I'll help you fix it". What does Sanne say about each, and especially about the middle one — is putting it off a way of being honest or just the same lie with extra steps?
