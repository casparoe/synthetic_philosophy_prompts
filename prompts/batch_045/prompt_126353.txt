Every December my magazine runs a counterfactual-history package, and this year I'd like to commission one piece for it as a mock policy document: a Swiftian modest proposal written in the register of a real think-tank scoping paper. Give the institute an invented name, something in the sober centrist idiom, and have the paper propose a time-limited 'Neolithic Reversal Pilot' for a small, invented but concretely described region — an intensively farmed river valley, say — in which a participating population would be moved back to foraging and shifting cultivation over a ten-year horizon, with a Phase I of a few thousand volunteers, a Phase II capped at what the land can actually carry, and a review gate in between.

The position the paper takes seriously, in perfect good faith, is the familiar revisionist case about the agricultural revolution: that the shift to farming was a bad bargain, buying calories at the cost of longer working hours, narrower diets, shorter stature, crowding and infectious disease, and above all the invention of storable surplus, which for the first time made accumulation, taxation and hereditary property — and therefore institutionalised inequality — possible; and that the forager baseline was, by that argument's own standard, a kind of affluence, since wants were few and satisfiable in a short working week.

I want the recoil to arrive through the arithmetic rather than through any editorialising. Foraging sustains far fewer people per square kilometre than cereal agriculture, and the valley currently supports many times that number, so Phase II must allocate a capped number of 'subsistence permits' by lottery, and the paper has to dispose of everyone else in exactly the same administrative register it uses for everything else — a modelling assumption, a transitional category, a phrase such as 'the deferred cohort'. The horror should come from the document's own logic licensing that outcome, and from euphemism doing the work; nothing should be played for laughs at the expense of the people the paper is quietly writing off, and a reader should register the appalling conclusion because they have followed the reasoning, not because the author has winked.

Three further constraints, which are where the piece will succeed or fail. First, please invent the paper's headline metric — a single composite index combining something like adult work hours, dietary breadth and the dispersion of stored wealth — and define it; the definition should quietly rig the index in the thesis's favour and still look like a number a real institution would print. Second, the paper must contain, without noticing, at least one provision that reconstitutes the hierarchy it is abolishing (its emergency grain reserve needs storing, guarding and accounting for, and the guards and accountants are the first unequal people) and one provision that presupposes the state it is meant to dissolve. Third, the FAQ should include the objection that no population in the historical record has ever voluntarily reverted to foraging at scale, and the paper's answer must be something a sincere proponent might actually say — about skill loss, land access, path dependence — rather than a joke; the wince is in how reasonable it sounds. Do not state any of the standard objections to the thesis outright; let the indicator list, the staffing table and the budget make them for you.

Give me roughly 800 to 1,000 words, formatted as a genuine document: an executive summary with numbered findings, a phased timeline, a monitoring and evaluation framework with named indicators and target values, and a short FAQ. The voice should be deadpan, acronym-bearing, costed and heavily hedged, the sort of prose in which an unspeakable thing appears as a caveat. No puns, no exclamation marks, no footnotes winking at the reader, and no closing punchline — end as these documents end, with a sentence about the need for further piloting. The target is the use of the revisionist case as a policy argument, and the paper genre itself, not any named person and not foragers or farmers. Invented figures are welcome but must be internally consistent; no bibliography, citations, named studies or page references anywhere, since I want the piece to stand on its own reasoning.
