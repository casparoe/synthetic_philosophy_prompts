Here's the case so we skip the greatest hits. Imagine a community, call them the Mirrors: same world as us, same environment, same stimuli, but their assent behavior is exactly inverted — for every sentence we are disposed to hold true, they are disposed to hold false, and vice versa. Now add a device: an oracle, stipulated to be reliable, which reports to us in our own language that the Mirrors really do believe the opposite of everything true, so their beliefs are massively false. My friend said this is easy — a charitable interpreter just reads them as agreeing with us and treats their assent marker as flipped, so the oracle must be mistaken. I said that's precisely the scandal: here charity isn't a route to truth, it's a rule that makes interpretive failure unintelligible by fiat, and if your only answer to the oracle is that its report is content-involving and so itself has to be interpreted, then you've bought intelligibility at the price of never being able to be wrong about anyone. So which is it — is the Mirrors case one your theory says cannot be *described*, or one it says cannot *happen*? And what exactly do you say to the oracle?
