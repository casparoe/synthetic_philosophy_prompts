I am applying for a six-month, fully funded research fellowship and need a two-page, single-spaced proposal. The area is the logic of contrastive explanation in history, and my test case is the cluster of questions around the so-called great divergence and the older puzzle about why modern science did not arise in China despite that civilization's earlier technological and organizational lead. My half-formed idea is that the debate has stalled not because the causal material is missing but because the question why Britain and not China? is under-specified along at least two hidden parameters, and that once those parameters are made explicit a good deal of the apparent disagreement turns out to be disagreement about the question rather than about the causes. I want this turned into a proposal rather than an essay.

The two parameters I have in mind. First, a level or scale parameter. The comparison does not state at what level of aggregation the units are fixed, and I suspect the shape of a satisfactory answer is level-relative: compare two large, internally heterogeneous civilizational units and one kind of factor does the explanatory work; re-run the comparison at the level of a single comparable region or river delta, as some historians have done, and that factor shrinks in importance while some other candidate expands to fill the space. If the ordering of candidate factors is not invariant under a change of scale, then the unparameterized question is not merely hard, it is a machine for generating pseudo-disagreement, and the proposal should say what would settle that invariance question. Second, a bundling parameter. The question does not say which outcome is at issue: the mechanization of production, sustained growth in output per head, the mathematization of nature, or some package of all of these. If the conjuncts of that package have partly independent explanations, then asking for one contrast factor for a conjunctive explanandum may be asking for something that does not exist, which is a different failure from the first and needs its own name.

There is a third worry I want the proposal to handle rather than dodge, and it is the one I am least sure about. The contrast term in such questions is typically selected because it lacked the outcome. The available comparators are then just whatever did not industrialize, or did not mathematize nature, so the contrast class is a residue rather than a class, and the contrastive question may carry no more information than the plain singular question why Britain? I would like the proposal to state a clean condition that separates genuine contrastive questions from singular questions wearing contrastive costume, and to say what the condition does to the cases at hand.

What I need in the document: the question stated so that it has a determinate answer; why existing positions in the philosophy of explanation, and the historiographical debate itself, leave it open; the method, month by month, for six months with no archival or quantitative work, since the project is conceptual and I have no language training for the primary sources; why this approach rather than the obvious alternatives, which I take to be applying a standard interventionist or contrastive account of explanation straight off the shelf, and abandoning the question altogether on the grounds that it is ideologically loaded and beyond repair; what a successful deliverable looks like, in a form someone actually writing history could use; and what would count as the project failing. Please be specific about the last item, and in particular address the failure mode I am most afraid of, which is that the whole apparatus turns out to be a technical redescription of it is complicated, and that the diagnostic I propose will either certify every contrastive question as well posed or condemn them all, in which case it has no content.

Describe the positions and arguments you rely on by their content; do not give me a bibliography or a literature review organized by author or title, and do not attribute specific formulations to specific works. I will supply the citations myself.
