I'm writing the handout for a first-year metaphysics session that reads a film through David Lewis's modal realism: concrete, spatiotemporally and causally isolated worlds, unrestricted recombination, actuality as an indexical — so that calling a world actual only marks it as the one the speaker inhabits — and no trans-world identity, only counterpart relations graded by similarity. The film is Everything Everywhere All at Once. I want a reading that takes both sides seriously rather than using the film as a clip reel: where does its multiverse genuinely behave like Lewis's plurality, and where does it trade on a picture Lewis explicitly rejects — worlds branching off shared decisions, travel and causal influence across worlds, Joy and Jobu Tupaki as literally one person rather than counterparts?

Two points I want in the weeds. First, the nihilism. Jobu Tupaki has seen everything and concludes that nothing matters; Evelyn's answer is to choose kindness in the worst of the worlds. Set that against the modal-realist analogue of the problem of evil: if plenitude guarantees that every horror is instantiated somewhere, Lewis can reply that existence is not creation and does not make the sum of being worse, whereas the film answers in the register of reasons rather than ontology. Is the film's answer a real reply to the worry or a change of subject, and could a Lewisian borrow it? Second, my own patch, which I will call the Home-World Restriction: even granting that all worlds are equally real, an agent's practical reasons range only over the world she occupies, because no cross-world causation is possible — on which reading Jobu's conclusion is a scope error rather than a metaphysical discovery. But the film grants cross-world travel, which is exactly what the restriction denies. Does the restriction survive in a world where verse-jumping is possible, and does the ending quietly reinstate it when Evelyn stays in the laundromat world? Say plainly where the film does something neither the Lewisian nor I can accommodate.
