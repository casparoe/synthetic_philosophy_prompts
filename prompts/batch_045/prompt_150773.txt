I've somehow ended up in charge of the after-dinner "entertainment" at this tiny invite-only workshop on imprecise probabilities that I'm going to next month — like 25 people, mostly formal epistemology and decision theory, and enough of them spend their working hours arguing about convex sets of probability measures that I deffinitely can't get away with anything generic. What I want is one genuinely good joke, not a string of puns: I've got maybe five minutes, so really it's one bit with a setup, and I'd like you to write it for me.

The constraint I keep failing on is that the punchline has to turn on what imprecision actually is, and not just on somebody being indecisive. Every draft I've written so far collapses into "ha ha, this person can't make up their mind," which would land just as well about someone choosing a restaurant, and the room will see right thru that. So build the joke on one of these, or on something better you know: that your credal state is a set of probability functions rather than a single one, so there is no number that is "your" probability of rain; that on the one-sided betting reading you can coherently refuse to buy a bet for 30 cents and also refuse to sell that same bet for 60 cents, and no Dutch book argument touches you for it; or dilation — the thing where there's a case in which you're about to learn something, and no matter which way the evidence comes out, your interval for the proposition ends up wider than it was before you learned anything, which is the opposite of what learning is supposed to do. Ideally the joke would stop working if someone reinterpreted it as being about a second-order probability distribution over first-order probabilities, since that's the misunderstanding people in this area are always fighting anyway.

For flavor: last year there was a running gag where this one guy's lazy heuristic — just act on the midpoint of your interval and stop agonizing — got christened "the shrink-to-fit rule," and there's a faction in the room who thinks that's obviously fine and a faction who thinks it throws away exactly what the interval was for. If you can get the bit to land on that fight, great, but don't force it if you have something funnier. Also, after the joke, give me two or three sentences on why the punchline works for people who actually know this material, and what would fall flat if half the audience turned out to be visiting grad students who only know that imprecise credences are "ranges" — I may need a version that survives that, or at least one line I can ad lib on the fly if the jokes are dying.
