I teach a final-year undergraduate seminar on free will — 20 students, one 90-minute session, no materials beyond chairs, a box and a couple of blindfolds, and they will have read a short passage on sphexishness beforehand. Design a classroom activity in which some students write literal instruction sheets, other students play the agents blindfolded and execute those sheets word for word, and a third group perturbs the room: the agents should end up in the predicament of the sphex wasp that repeats its preparatory check over and over because the world has been altered just out of step with its routine, and they should feel that they know something is wrong while having no instruction that permits them to act on that knowledge.

Add a second phase built around an idea I want them to run into rather than be told, which I'll call second-order sphexishness: give one or two agents a meta-instruction along the lines of *if you notice you are repeating yourself, stop, announce that you are stuck, and then do the following*, so that they escape the first loop, and then arrange the perturbations so that the meta-instruction itself loops — the agent gets stuck at being stuck, and has no way to take its own level-two stuckness as a reason to do anything. What they should come away seeing is that the difference between a mechanical agent and a responsible one is not visible in any single piece of behaviour and is not a matter of the agent's being undetermined, but a matter of whether its architecture contains a routine that can take the failure of its own lower routines as an input — and that this demand generates a regress no finite instruction-set can close.

Give me the setup and roles, the rules and a scoring scheme that needs no money, a timeline with minute marks, and a debrief that maps specific moments onto the failure of behaviourism to detect rigidity, the irrelevance of determinism to the felt predicament, and the regress itself. Include what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be frank about which part of the philosophical claim the activity cannot stage and I will have to argue for myself. Please write your answer in British English.
