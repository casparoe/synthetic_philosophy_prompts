Been reading about debate as a way to oversee models that are better than us at the thing we're asking about, and I keep hitting the same shape of failure. I dont know what it's called, or whether I'm just misunderstanding the setup.

The pitch as I got it: two debaters, a weaker judge, and the honest one wins by zooming in on one small claim the other made and showing it's wrong — checking a single piece is easy even when checking the whole answer is hopeless. That obviously works when the answer is a chain of facts and one link is broken. But take a question where nothing in particular is false and the real disagreement is about how much a dozen small things should count. Two cases I keep coming back to: (1) "is this bridge design safe to sign off" — the side saying yes gets pushed on the weld inspection records, concedes they're incomplete, says that doesn't change the load calc, gets pushed on the load calc, concedes the margin is thinner than stated, says that's normal for this class of structure, and so on down the list; (2) "which of these two psychology results is more likely to replicate" — same shape, every disputed item gets resolved or hedged, and nothing in the final exchange is false.

By the end the judge knows a lot more, every statement on both sides is either true or has been withdrawn, and I can't see why the judge is any likelier to pick the right side than if they'd been handed the two answers cold. The usual story is that debate makes lying hard; here it seems to make lying unnecessary, because the fight is about weighting rather than facts, and debate grinds the facts down to nothing without ever touching the weighting. It's not the worry people raise about a debater making a deliberately long confusing argument, and it's not the Gish gallop either — there aren't lots of arguments here and nobody is being sly.

Does this have a name? If there's an established concept, I want the nearest candidates and exactly how each one misses, and which of them if any actually covers it. If there isn't one, coin a name and give me a definition and the boundary cases: what makes a question have this shape rather than the chain-of-facts shape, and what you'd do with a question like it instead. A bare label is useless to me — it's the carving I'm after.
