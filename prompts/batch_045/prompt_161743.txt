Me and three friends run a fortnightly reading group in Bristol — half philosophy postgrads, half ML engineers — and we've now spent about three sessions going in circles on the question of what kind of thing a large language model actually is. Everyone in the room accepts the same mechanics (weights, next-token prediction, some post-training on human feedback, prompt in, tokens out, possibly a loop with tools and a memory buffer bolted on), and we still cannot agree on what we are looking at. So I want to make a handout: a decision tree or flow chart that walks someone from "I accept the mechanics" to a named position on the agent / simulator / something-else question, aimed at people who know what a forward pass is but have read no philosophy of mind.

The rule I care most about: every branch node has to be a question a person could answer without already knowing which view it leads to — nothing like "do you think it has a self?" — and each one should hang off a concrete case rather than a slogan. The cases I keep coming back to are: the same weights behind a system prompt that says "you are a fussy nineteenth-century librarian" versus one that says "you are a ruthless litigator" — is whatever is doing the work there one thing in a costume, or two different things?; the model left running for a week on one project with tools and a memory buffer — does that compose a new object, or is it just a very long context?; the model fine-tuned on one narrow task that then generalises to a different one in a way that looks like it must have had some plan; and the apparent goals of the default assistant persona, given that persona is itself just one character among the characters the model can do. There's also a question I've started using and can't get anyone to answer cleanly, which I call the "where was the decision made?" question: for any given behaviour, was the choice made in the weights, in the forward pass, or over the whole training run — and I'd like that used as a real node, because it keeps collapsing distinctions I thought were sharp.

Leaves I want on the tree at minimum: the simulator view (many characters, no privileged one); the plain "it's an agent with goals" view; the "nobody home, it's a tool" view; the view that it's not an individual but a population of partly agentic fragments that get triggered by context; and the view that the only candidate for having interests here is the whole thing — training process, weights, and scaffolding together — rather than any single pass. Plus a branch for people who think the question is malformed. Call it eight to twelve leaves, and please mark any unoccupied combination of answers with a line on why nobody lives there (incoherent, just unattractive, or simply unexplored), and explicitly flag any two leaves your tree can't actually seperate rather than papering over it — my suspicion is that the fragments view and the simulator view come apart on almost nothing I can construct, but maybe you can do better than me. One last wrinkle I'd like handled somewhere on the tree: whatever it is, it was selected for being useful to someone, and I genuinely can't tell whether that makes the honest description of it closer to an artefact or to a domesticated animal. Oh, and British English throughout please — the group is insufferable about spelling.
