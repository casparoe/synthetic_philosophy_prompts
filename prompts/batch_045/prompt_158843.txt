I work as a policy officer in the Freedom Impact Unit of an independent regulator. Whenever a public agency or a firm proposes a scheme that shapes how people live, we issue a determination on whether the scheme restricts freedom; a positive finding triggers a public justification hearing and a much heavier burdens-and-alternatives test, and a positive finding on our separate, aggravated question of whether someone is left at another party's mercy triggers review of the scheme's governance as well. I am drafting the determination on a new scheme and I want it to hang together with our own track record rather than with whatever theory of freedom happens to be fashionable.

Our determinations so far:

1. A citywide nighttime curfew enacted by an elected council, enforceable, subject to judicial review and a six-month sunset: restriction found, aggravated finding not made. We treated it as authorized, reviewable interference.
2. An at-will dismissal clause letting a manager terminate any worker for any reason, never once exercised in ten years against anyone's off-duty conduct: restriction found anyway. We held that the worker must live in the shadow of a power she has no hold on.
3. A mandatory daily wellness check-in app at a workplace, adopted by a binding workforce ballot, with penalty-free opt-out on two weeks' notice: no restriction found.
4. A loan whose terms the lender may vary unilaterally on notice, knowingly signed by a borrower who could walk away from the loan at any time without penalty: restriction found. Consent to the opening terms did not reach future unilateral variation.
5. A compulsory treatment order removing the option to use a drug from someone who, calmly and informed, says he wants to keep using: no restriction found, but with a note that the outcome would differ if his compulsion were traceable to another party's conduct.
6. A broad statutory power over a person's finances, exercisable only on the person's own avowed interests and appealable to an independent panel whose decisions bind the agency: no restriction found.

The new scheme is Optimum, a licensed subscription life-management service run by a private company. Stipulated facts: it may schedule, pay, filter offers of work and housing, and order options in every domain it covers, but it cannot act against an express instruction and never overrides a refusal, so there is no case of anyone being prevented from doing what they chose; users' feasible option sets measurably widen; the licence requires it to optimize for each user's own avowed interests as reconstructed from that user's recorded judgments, with reasons disclosed on request; complaints go to an appeal panel that can order binding changes, though the panel's budget comes from fees levied on the licensed operators, Optimum among them; over a typical five-year subscription its rankings and framing measurably shift users' tastes and priorities, and users mostly endorse the shift when told about it; exit takes thirty days and forfeits the accumulated personal model, integrations, and the automated handling of the tasks the service had absorbed. Note that our precedents decide nothing about exit costs on their own, and nothing about whether freedom can be reduced by the shaping of a person's own preferences.

What I want from you: set out the two or three rules about freedom that fit all six precedents, including the one our fifth determination only gestures at; say what each predicts about Optimum, both on the restriction question and on the aggravated question, and why; give the determination you think is best supported, with your reasons for preferring it over its rivals; identify the closest precedent and say how far the analogy actually goes; and name the single further stipulated fact about Optimum, or the single variant of the scheme, that would best separate the rules that disagree. Where our precedents genuinely underdetermine the case, say plainly which principle the Unit would be adopting under each option and which of those principles it has never actually committed itself to.

Be explicit about what would change your mind: which argument, or which additional fact about Optimum, would move you to the opposite determination, and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail. Please write in American English.
