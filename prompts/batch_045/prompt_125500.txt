I've been working through the standard treatment of uncertain evidence — Jeffrey's probability kinematics — and I keep coming back to something that looks like a genuine defect, which makes me suspect I'm misunderstanding something basic. I'd like that misunderstanding located.

The set-up. Let P be my prior, H some hypothesis, and {E, ¬E} a partition. An uncertain update doesn't hand me either cell outright; it moves my credence in the cells to new values q and 1−q. The rule is rigidity: the conditional probabilities of everything given each cell stay put and the cell weights do the work, so my new credence in H is q·P(H|E) + (1−q)·P(H|¬E). So far, fine.

My worry is about iteration. Suppose I take two such updates in sequence, one moving the {E, ¬E} marginal and one moving a cross-cutting {F, ¬F} marginal — say H is that a drug has a real effect, E is that the trial reported a significant result, and F is that the trial was pre-registered, with two reports of differing reliability arriving in whatever order they happen to arrive. Because rigidity is always rigidity relative to my current credence function, the E-then-F result differs from the F-then-E result. But the inputs are the same pair of target marginals, and my total evidence is the same in both cases; all that differs is the order in which two items happened to reach me. That looks like a violation of an obviously correct desideratum: final credences should be a function of total evidence, not of the serialization of that evidence. If the order does work, the rule is letting an epistemically irrelevant fact drive my conclusions.

Two clarifications so we don't talk past each other. First, I can see that the problem doesn't bite when both shifts target the same partition: there the intermediate value drops out and only the final one matters. Second, I'm unmoved by the reply that Jeffrey updating was never intended for iterated, cross-cutting evidence, since that describes most of actual inference.

Here is the fix I want to propose. Do the update holistically instead: hold fixed the conditional distribution of H over the four atoms E∩F, E∩¬F, ¬E∩F, ¬E∩¬F, and choose the new joint distribution over those atoms that minimizes D_KL(Q‖P) subject to both new marginals, Q(E) = q and Q(F) = r. This is order-independent by construction, and in the single-partition case it returns exactly the Jeffrey rule above, which I take as evidence that it is the right generalization rather than a competing rule. A cheaper alternative would be to just average the two sequential results, but I'd prefer a rule with a derivation behind it.

So, concretely: why keep the sequential form? Is there any worked case where the two orders diverge and the sequential answer is clearly the correct one — where the holistic, order-independent answer is demonstrably wrong? Am I wrong to think the two orders represent the same total evidence? And separately, am I right that any single Jeffrey shift can always be reproduced as ordinary conditionalization on some suitably constructed event in an enlarged algebra — in which case Jeffrey updating can't be faulted for departing from strict conditionalization, and order-dependence is left as the only real objection, which the holistic rule removes?

Please reason it through rather than pointing me at things to read — I'm not after references or further reading, just the argument. Keep the answer in American English.
