hi — I'm a decision scientist at a health technology assessment agency. My day job is turning every comparison into a number: QALYs, ICERs, a cost-effectiveness threshold, net monetary benefit, distributional weights, PSA and sensitivity analysis, EVPI, and when the criteria won't reduce we fall back on MCDA with swing weighting plus a deliberative panel. I use all of this daily, so please don't explain my own vocabulary back to me.

I've been reading about incommensurable values and hard choices — Chang on parity, Raz on incommensurability, the value-pluralism background — and I keep flip-flopping between "this is all already in my toolkit" and "this is mysticism," and I can't tell which. So do the translation properly and then tell me what didn't make it across.

The core mapping I want: what is parity in my idiom? Is it two options whose expected net benefit differ by less than the minimally important difference, i.e. inside the noise band — plain imprecision — or does calling it that distort it? What does 'no common measure' even come to for someone who can always obtain a number by commissioning one more discrete choice experiment, at rising cost and with the result depending more and more on arbitrary design choices?

Here's the wrinkle that started this, and it's a bit of local jargon so bear with me: my team has begun talking about options as 'rankable at price p' — pick a pair of options, and there is some elicitation spend at which we'll hand you a defensible ranking, and where the ranking stays stable under reasonable design variation. Below that spend we say the pair is unranked-for-now. We've quietly turned a metaphysics question into a budget question. Does the claim that some values are genuinely incommensurable survive that move, e.g. as 'no finite p suffices' or 'the number you get is purely an artifact of the elicitation design'? Or does incommensurability need to be more than expensive commensuration?

Then the residue report, which is what I actually care about. Two things I suspect have no expression at all in my vocabulary: (1) the phenomenology attributed to hard choices — agonising, not a tie, and yet the choice supposedly matters in a way that 'indifferent, flip a coin' doesn't capture; (2) the idea that in a hard choice you put your agency behind some consideration and thereby create a reason that wasn't there before. My framework has no slot for a preference being made rather than elicited. For each: does it fail to translate, and is that a gap in cost-benefit language or a sign the philosophers are confused?

Last thing: my framework is commensurable-by-construction, so if you let that assumption do work while translating you've begged the whole question. Flag the places where you caught yourself doing it.
