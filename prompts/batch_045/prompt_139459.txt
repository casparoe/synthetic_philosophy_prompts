so me and a friend (she's on the RL side, I'm the philosophy one) got into an argument the other week about whether "avoid side effects" can even be *said* without already having a full theory of value in hand. She thinks yes, because you can cash out "impact" in terms of stuff like how much the world's future options shrink, which is supposed to be value-free. I think you're always sneaking value back in somewhere. Before I go read any of the actual proposals I want the spec written down, otherwise I'll just end up reverse-engineering whatever the existing measures happen to do. So: write me the desiderata.

Fix the purpose first so we don't talk past each other: the thing is meant to be a penalty term in the training of a fairly capable agent operating in a shared human environment (household-and-office robot, or a software agent with real permissions on someone's machine), where we have a decent but definitely wrong specification of the *task*, no specification at all of everything else we care about, and a tired human overseer who can veto things but only actually notices maybe one intervention in twenty. "Adequate" = adequate for that, i.e. a regularizer that doesn't clearly go wrong in that setting. Not adequate as an analysis of the ordinary concept of a side effect.

Here are the cases I already have, roughly, with why I think each is a requirement -- tell me which of these are real desiderata and which are just the prejudices of one camp, add the ones I'm missing, and say which are non-negotiable: (1) authorized vs unauthorized: same broken vase, one because the robot knocked it, one because I asked it to clear the shelf; a measure that can't tell those apart seems useless, but "authorization" looks like it needs a whole theory of legitimate authority, coercion and delegation chains, so is this where the value sneaks in? (2) delegation and laundering: the agent gets a human, a sub-agent or a cron job to do the impactful part, or arranges things so the human "decides" to do it; the measure should be closed under that, presumably. (3) death by a thousand cuts: a million individually negligible nudges to a million people, or one book relocated per day for a year; anything with a per-step threshold fails, but anything merely additive may paralyse the agent. (4) the bystander: against a do-nothing baseline, doing nothing scores perfectly, so the measure is satisfied by paralysis and by watching the stove fire spread because intervening is high-impact; but the obvious fix (compare to a baseline where someone competent was doing the job) seems to import a standard of good performance. (5) baseline-gaming: an agent that cripples itself or disables its own off-switch so that nothing can shrink any further, or otherwise makes the counterfactual it's scored against cheap. (6) model error: the measure is computed under the agent's own world-model, which is wrong; do we score belief or truth, and if truth, where does an agent get it? (7) legibility: the overseer should be able to look at a trace and tell whether the next action is one of the high-impact ones, which counts against measures defined over unobservable counterfactual quantities. (8) anonymity and spread across people vs. across time, and whether the measure cares about effects on *people* at all or just about physical difference (robot alone in a museum knocking over statues all night). (9) the point of the exercise: it must not secretly encode a full utility function over world states, or we've given up.

The part I most want: is some subset of these jointly unsatisfiable? My guess is that (9) plus (2) plus (4) can't all be had, and that the interesting result is the list eating itself -- that the only way to get a measure that is both value-free and not satisfied by paralysis or by laundering is to build in something that looks exactly like the value specification we were trying to avoid. Say which single requirement knocks out the most candidate measures, and call out anything I've listed that's really an engineering convenience dressed up as a desideratum (I suspect "it has to be a single scalar you can add to the reward" is one of those). Also say whether "side effect" is even the right unit, or whether the right object is a budget, a rate, or a comparison class of agents.

Only at the end, and briefly: if you know the existing families -- the ones that penalize shrinkage of what's reachable or achievable, the ones that penalize changes in the agent's own ability to pursue a spread of alternative goals, the ones that just penalize irreversibility, the ones that penalize divergence from what would have happened anyway -- a short table scoring them against your list is fine, but that's the small part and I'd rather you skipped it than guessed.
