Take the Mondragon group in the Basque country as a test case for Mill's late, qualifed socialism. I mean the late position specifically: the obstacle to socialism isn't property as such, it's the labour-incentive problem you get once distribution runs on "to each according to his needs"; plus the Malthusian problem of who restrains numbers in a community that guarantees support; plus the On Liberty worry that one big central scheme destroys the diversity of experiments — with co-operative production as the transitional form, and his formula for the problem of the future being how to unite the most individual liberty of action with common ownership of the raw material of the globe and everyone sharing in the gains of combined labour.

Mondragon is about as close as the world has come to what he actually recommended: worker-owned producer co-ops competing in a market, federated, with their own savings bank, technical schools and university. So what exactly does Mill's framework predict for it, and where does the record push back? It seems to have survived by inventing things he never proposed — a coop bank that behaves a lot like a venture capitalist, internal pay scales indexed to outside market rates rather than to need, and (my understanding, tell me if it's wrong) a large and growing workforce of ordinary non-member wage employees, mostly in the foreign subsidiaries — and one of its flagship industrial co-ops went under in 2013. Does his vocabulary even reach that outcome, or is this a case his theory can't see? Which of his three worries would he most have to revise, and which one is Mondragon simply silent on? I want the mapping run in both directions, not a tour of Mill scholarship, and no citations needed.
