Here's the bit I think I get. Nyaya recognizes four pramanas: perception, inference, comparison, and testimony. The stock example for comparison is the gavaya one: you know what cows look like, someone reliable tells you that a gavaya is an animal somewhat like a cow, later you see an unfamiliar animal and you come to know that that thing is a gavaya. And the claim is that this isn't reducible to the other three. My question is *why not*. Once you subtract the seeing of the animal, the remembering of what you were told, and some kind of similarity matching, what is supposed to be left over? Also, since a sentence was involved somewhere, why isn't this really just testimony doing the work? I want the actual argument for distinctness, not just the assertion that it's a separate source.

Then there are four cases where I genuinely can't tell whether the theory handles them or just breaks:

1. Reversed direction. Every version of the example runs from the familiar thing (cow) to the unfamiliar one (gavaya). But suppose I grew up near the forest and know gavayas well, and a traveler tells me a cow is roughly like a gavaya but smaller, with a dewlap, and then I see a cow and identify it. Structurally that looks identical. So is the direction just an artifact of the textbook example, or is something actually supposed to hang on it?

2. Partial, graded similarity. The animal I see matches a cow in body shape but the horns are all wrong, and it's honestly about as buffalo-like as cow-like, and the respects of similarity don't all line up. Similarity comes in degrees and isn't one single relation. So what is the pramana supposed to output — a one-off identification of that individual, or knowledge of the word-to-thing relation generally? And what happens when the similarity is borderline: does the cognition just fail to count as knowledge, or does it come out vague?

3. Right answer, wrong route. Say I latch onto the wrong respect: the speaker meant the size, I match on the horns, and I still happen to pick out a genuine gavaya. Is that still upamana? Since pramanas are supposed to be genuine sources of true cognition, can an accidentally successful similarity match count — or does this show the account needs some kind of no-luck condition?

4. The second time. Once I have the concept and have seen several gavayas, identifying the next one feels like plain recognition, not comparison. Does upamana fire only once per word, or is it still operative in every later recognition?
