I spent about forty years in condensed-matter and statistical physics, the last decade of it on phase transitions and numerical renormalisation-group calculations, and in retirement I have started reading philosophy of mind seriously for the first time. I have got as far as Daniel Dennett's three stances for predicting and explaining the behaviour of a system — the physical stance, the design stance and the intentional stance — together with the criterion he attaches to them for when a pattern is real rather than merely read into the data: roughly, that a pattern is real when a description of the data that exploits it is more compact than one that does not, compression being the mark of a regularity that is there rather than one the observer has supplied. I want that apparatus translated into the vocabulary I actually think in, and I want the residue of the translation named rather than smoothed over.

The vocabulary I mean: Hamiltonians and their spectra; microscopic degrees of freedom versus coarse-grained or block variables; renormalisation-group flow, fixed points, relevant and irrelevant operators, universality classes; order parameters and broken symmetry; effective field theories with an explicit domain of validity and an error estimate; the difference between deriving a macroscopic theory from a microscopic one and merely fitting it; and the difference between a symmetry that is exact, one that is approximate, and one that is emergent only in a limit. Assume I am fluent in all of that and in nothing philosophical beyond the undergraduate survey I audited last year.

Please translate stance by stance, and translate the criterion for pattern reality as well. For each item say what carries over cleanly, what carries only if I distort it, and what cannot be said in my vocabulary at all. My own first-pass guesses, which I would like you to correct rather than flatter: the physical stance is simply the microscopic theory; the design stance is what you get when you assume the system's trajectory extremises a functional that was fixed in advance by a designer or a selector, so that you can predict without integrating the equations of motion; the intentional stance is an effective theory for a system solving a control problem in real time under partial information, with beliefs and desires playing the role of order parameters and the rationality assumption playing the role of a variational principle. I suspect the last identification does violence to something, for two reasons I can half-articulate: the order parameters of a physical effective theory do not have to be correct or incorrect about anything, and an effective field theory in my sense advertises where it breaks down and by how much, whereas I have no idea how to attach a domain of validity or an error bar to a belief-desire attribution. Tell me whether those two suspicions are the right ones to have.

Then I want to push a sharpening at you, because the compressibility criterion as I understand it bothers me. It is defined relative to a choice of description language, and for finite data I can make almost any noise look compressible by choosing an encoding tailored to it. In my field the corresponding move is to ask not whether a description is short but whether it is stable: whether a family of different coarse-grainings of the same microdynamics, each independently predictive, converges on the same macroscopic behaviour as you refine them, which is what puts a system in a universality class and what makes an order parameter more than a convenient fit. Suppose we substitute that stability condition for the compressibility condition — a pattern is real to the extent that many distinct coarse-grainings recover it, and artefactual to the extent that only one privileged encoding does. What happens to the account? Does the substitution raise the ontological price in a way the position cannot afford, does it admit patterns that should be excluded or exclude ones that should be kept, and does it change the answer to the question whether there is any fact of the matter about which stance is the right one to adopt toward a given system?

Finally, the residue report proper. Three verdicts I want.

(a) My vocabulary has no room for norms. On the reading I gave, the rationality assumption in the intentional stance is an ought rather than an is: you attribute the beliefs the agent ought to have and predict what it ought then to do. Is that a feature of the phenomenon my framework simply cannot represent, which would make statistical physics the wrong target vocabulary for this part of the job? Or is it a sign that the intentional stance is carrying a normative commitment it has not earned, one that a purely descriptive translation legitimately strips away?

(b) The triad looks exhaustive once translated — microscopic law, variational or selection principle, control-theoretic effective theory — and it also looks like it has a hole. Consider a system that was not designed and is not yet well described as optimising anything, but whose parameters are being moved by something else on a schedule: a network part way through training, a lineage part way through an adaptive landscape. That invites a fourth strategy, predicting the system by asking what it is being pushed toward rather than what it was built for or what it currently wants. Is that genuinely a fourth thing, or does it collapse into the design stance under pressure? If it is genuinely distinct, is the apparent exhaustiveness of the triad an artefact of the examples it was built on, and does anything in the position depend on that exhaustiveness?

(c) Whichever way the residue falls, I want to know what it indicts. Does it indict my vocabulary — specifically the physicist's reflex of treating every level of description as answerable to one underlying microdynamics, so that the only question about a pattern is whether it survives refinement — or does it indict the stances themselves as an unstable mixture of three different claims, one about predictive strategy, one about what there is, and one about norms, which look like a single doctrine only because they are usually run together?

I am not asking for reading suggestions or a map of the secondary literature; I have no library access and could not follow up a reference if you gave me one. What I want is the translation, the places it fails, and an honest verdict on what the failures mean.
