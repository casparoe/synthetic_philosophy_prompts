I'm running a philosophy-through-film night for our undergrad club in a couple weeks and we landed on Arrival (2016). I want to read it through Hofstadter's frame / outer / inner message distinction from GEB: the frame message being the bare "this is a message, decode me", the outer message being everything you have to already have in place to decode it — the format, the writing system, the machinery of the receiver — and the inner message being whatever is left once the outer layer is in hand; plus his point that the very same physical message can carry different information depending on what's doing the decoding, so meaning isn't sitting in the signal alone.

Here's my own angle: the heptapod logograms are the outer message, but aquiring them rewires Louise, so by the time she can read the content she is no longer the receiver who first asked "what do they want". I've been calling that a decoder-rewriting message and I want to know if Arrival is actually a clean case of it or if it cheats — the dream logic, the flash-forward images, the fact that nobody else in the film seems to learn much of anything.

So: where does the "offer weapon" confusion actually live — frame, outer, or inner? Can Hofstadter's three levels represent a message that installs the machinery needed to read itself, or does that case collapse the distinction from the inside? And where do I tell the group the film is just being loose about linguistics instead of saying something about the location of meaning? End by saying what, if anything, the film shows that the GEB argument on its own doesn't.
