Trying to work out what I actually think about supertasks by going case by case, and I want to know whether there's a principle behind my verdicts or whether I'm just reacting.

- Walking to the wall Zeno-style, infinitely many half-distances in finite time: totally fine, and determinately at the wall at the end.
- Same halving schedule, but a device writes the digits of 1/3 one per step onto a tape: fine, and the tape determinately reads 0.333... at the end.
- Thomson's lamp: the flips are performable, but the state at t=1 is brute. No contradiction, the story just can be completed either way.
- The gods-and-walls version where the barrier is always half the remaining distance, so they pile up right where you start: this one I want to call impossible, you can't even set out.
- The card case: at each step the demon writes the next integer and erases the previous one. I think the card is determinately blank at the end, even though what's on it grows without bound right up to the limit.
- A lamp on the same schedule but with a switch that has a minimum dwell time, so it ignores any flip arriving too soon after the last one it registered: I think this ends in a perfectly determinate state, on or off, even though infinitely many flips occur.

My tentative principle: a supertask's history fixes a unique limit state when the quantities involved settle down; when nothing settles, the limit state is brute but the story is still consistent. That seems to handle 1, 2 and 6, but I can't tell whether it survives 4 and 5, and I have a feeling 3 and 4 are pulling against each other in a way I can't locate.

Is there one principl that gets all six, or do some verdicts have to go? Say which ones, and please also give what your principle predicts for a case I haven't listed: two lamps on the same schedule, one flipped on the odd steps and the other on the even steps.

I'm bracketing the physics -- I don't care whether anything could really be sped up like that, only whether the stories are coherent and what they determine. And flag clearly which parts are settled among people who work on this and which are live disagreements, so I don't walk into the seminar presenting one camp's line as the consensus.
