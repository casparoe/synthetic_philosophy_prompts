I am a novelist, and I am three sections into a book I am no longer sure is honest, so I would like the analysis done properly before I write another chapter.

The novel is set in a single townland on Ireland's western seaboard, in six sections spaced roughly forty years apart — 1798, 1839, 1847, 1882, 1901, 1946 — with no protagonist carried across the gaps. The only continuities are a field, a limekiln, a right of way, and a surname. The middle section is the potato blight. I have been reading Braudel in the evenings, and the book's form and its politics now seem to me to be pulling in opposite directions.

What I want is the Great Irish Famine of 1845–52 read through the lens of Braudel's three temporal registers — the event, the conjoncture, and the longue durée — and then an account of where that lens distorts. Give me the mapping first, and be disciplined about it: what belongs to which register, what the relevant actor is at each scale (a household, a rent schedule, a price series, a pathogen, an empire), and what would count as evidence at each scale. I will supply the facts as I understand the common account so you don't have to: the blight arrived from abroad in 1845 onto a crop with a very narrow genetic base, mostly the Lumper; roughly a million people died and roughly a million left; food continued to be exported through the worst years, in some accounts under escort; Peel's government bought Indian corn and repealed the Corn Laws in 1846; soup kitchens were opened in early 1847 and closed that autumn, after which relief was routed through the workhouses under the Poor Law. If any of that is contested, say which way you would bet.

Three things I have invented for my own purposes, and I want to know which of them are legitimate extensions and which are a novelist smuggling the event back in under another name:

1. A fourth register between conjoncture and longue durée, measured in generations rather than decades or centuries, whose unit is not the individual life but the holding — the plot, the tenancy, the inheritance. Call it the inheritance register.
2. An inertia test for events: delete the individual decision in question and ask whether anything differs within one generation. If not, the event is durationally inert and my novel should not build a scene around it.
3. Borrowed durée: the case where the pace of a local structure is set somewhere else entirely — an Andean tuber, a London grain price, a colonial provisioning system — so that what looks like a deep local structure is really someone else's.

Then argue the two questions I actually need settled, in both directions, theory onto case and case onto theory:

First, what the Famine does to the framework. It looks to me like an event that closed a structure: within about a generation, cottier tenure, subdivision, and early marriage were gone, and a regime of grazing, emigration, and late marriage stood in their place. If events can install and dismantle structures, the registers leak into each other and the longue durée stops being the stable floor it is meant to be. So: is the Famine a confirmation of the framework, an anomaly inside it, or a case it never anticipated? Pick one and give your reasons; and tell me separately what the framework explains well here — the monoculture, the land system, the behaviour of food prices, why emigration kept running long after the blight ended — and what it misdescribes, in particular whether it has to treat the administrative decisions of 1846–47 as surface foam, and whether the death toll was or was not sensitive to them.

Second, where the framework goes silent on blame. A novel requires that someone be answerable, and a framework that dissolves individuals into structures may exculpate by construction. I want to know whether that exculpation is a defect of the framework or a true description of a case where no individual decision could have moved the outcome — and, relatedly, whether my own formal device (revisiting the same field at forty-year intervals with no one carried across) is itself an evasive political choice rather than an honest one.

Please commit yourself rather than surveying readings. Give me one verdict on the confirm-or-anomaly question, one on where responsibility falls and what the novel ought to hold responsible, and one on each of my three devices; say plainly where you are going beyond what the framework and the commonly known facts license; and if one of my devices is the weak one, name it. I have no library here and cannot chase references, so reason from the framework and the facts rather than pointing me at books.
