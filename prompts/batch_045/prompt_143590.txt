You are an experienced designer of hands-on philosophy and cognitive-science workshops, and a careful reader of Hofstadter and Sander's *Surfaces and Essences*. Please think the design through properly: before committing, sketch two or three different mechanisms that could produce the effect I am after, note in a line each why you set the others aside, and then give me the one you would actually run.

I have no academic background. I run an informal reading group — twelve adults, mixed ages and occupations, ninety minutes, in a room above a pub. We have just finished reading about analogy as the engine of thought, and the claim that everyone half-accepts but does not really feel is this: that when you sort something into a category you are not checking it against a definition, you are quietly mapping it onto a case you already have.

I also want them to feel something I have started calling *hardening*: the moment a category they have just invented acquires a name, it stops feeling like a handful of reminiscent cases and starts feeling like a rule with an essence behind it — after which people defend a grouping they reached by analogy as though it followed from the definition, and can no longer see the analogy that formed it. And I want them to catch themselves treating the earliest example as the centre of the category, so that a later, very different example is stretched to fit it rather than the two meeting somewhere in the middle.

So: an activity using novel material nobody has prior concepts for — invented shapes, made-up words, short invented situations, nothing requiring specialist knowledge — in which participants build and name categories themselves and only afterwards discover what they were doing. I need rules, materials, a rough minute-by-minute shape for the ninety minutes, and no lecturing up front; ideally they do not hear the word analogy from me until the debrief. Then a debrief that maps what actually happened onto those three claims, an honest note on what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how I should handle it, and a frank statement of which part of the idea the exercise cannot deliver and I will simply have to say out loud. Please write in British English.
