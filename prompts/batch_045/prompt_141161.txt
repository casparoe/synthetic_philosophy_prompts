I spent my career in physiology and have lately been sitting in on a graduate seminar on well-being, where three weeks on the heterogeneity objection to hedonism have left us, so far as I can tell, precisely where we began. The objectors work through a list — the scratch that relieves an itch, the absorption of a difficult ascent, the quiet satisfaction taken in another's setback, the relief when a headache stops, the animal that keeps working for a reward it shows every sign of no longer enjoying — and conclude that these states share no phenomenal property in common. The defenders answer, variously, that careful introspection does disclose a single hedonic tone, that pleasure is not a sensation at all but a manner in which experiences are had, or that it is an attitude: wanting the experience as one undergoes it. Then the list is produced again and nothing has moved.

My own hypothesis is that the two sides have silently accepted different job descriptions. The objectors want something like a natural kind: a property intrinsic to each episode in virtue of which it is a pleasure, and they are right that introspection will not supply one. What the hedonistic tradition actually needed, however, was a common currency — a single dimension along which episodes can be ranked and summed into a lifetime total — and a single scale, as any instrument maker knows, need not rest on a single feeling; it can be realized by a heterogeneous and partly dissociable mechanism. On that reading the dispute is either about what a theory of welfare is for, or it is a dormant empirical question about how many valence systems there are, which the phenomenal evidence both sides cite cannot settle.

Am I right? I would like a diagnosis rather than a verdict: is this a verbal dispute about the word pleasure, a substantive one about the supervenience base, a difference about which cases count as data, or two questions being pursued under one heading? Ground it in what each position actually claims, and tell me what, if anything, would count as progress — a distinction, a formal or empirical result, a change of question — or why no progress should be expected.
