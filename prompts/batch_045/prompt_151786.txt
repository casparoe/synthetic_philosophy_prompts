Me and my friend got into a huge argument last night about whether dreams are experiences, and I want a number, not a tour of the literature. Here's the claim I want your credence on: during an ordinary non-lucid REM episode, there is a subject-level stream of consciousness going on — something it is like for you at the time, roughly of the sort your waking report describes — as opposed to sleep merely leaving you disposed to produce that report on waking, with nothing it was like in between. Give me your probability that that's true of typical dreams, and decompose it.

I want the sub-questions with a rough number and one line of justification each, then how they combine. Here's what I think the pieces are, but add or drop terms as you see fit:

- How much rides on whether this is an empirical question at all. My friend kept pushing the Malcolm-flavoured thought that all the criteria for "he was dreaming" are waking criteria — the report, the behaviour, the sleep lab data — so there's no room for a gap between what's reportable and what was experienced, and the question "was it really experienced?" isn't the kind of thing that can have a hidden answer.
- How much rides on which account of consciousness is right, and whether any of them even makes the question determinate. If you think a purely report-based or functionalist story is right, vs. something where experience is a further fact, does the number move a lot?
- How much rides on the actual sleep-lab evidence: the correlation between REM physiology and what people report, the fact that lucid dreamers can signal from inside REM with deliberate eye movements, and the awkward cases where a few minutes of REM gets reported as a dream that seemed to last hours.
- How much rides on my own case vs. everyone else's. For your dreams, do you have anything beyond the memory you find yourself with at waking? I noticed mid-argument that Sam was basically treating my dreams the way he treats other people's, which felt like cheating.
- And a term for the forgotten dream: you dream, there's zero trace, no report is ever possible. Was there a fact of the matter about whether you experienced something? I'm at about 0.8 overall, Sam is at 0.35, and I want to know which of these our disagreement most likely lives in.

Two cases we invented while arguing, tell me if either does any real work or if they're just restating the disagreement. (a) The Interrupter: a machine wakes you at random points in REM and asks what was happening right then; the reports come out near-identical to what you'd say the next morning. Does that settle it? (b) The Diary Zombie: someone with no experience whatsoever during sleep who nonetheless produces vivid dream reports on waking, because the report-generating machinery runs without any accompanying consciousness. Both of us agreed these are conceivable, which is why I suspect the real fight is about the actual case, not the possible ones.
