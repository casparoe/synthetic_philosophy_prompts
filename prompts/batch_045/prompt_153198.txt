I have recently retired from a career in atmospheric chemistry — most of it spent trying to pull signals out of long instrumental records — and I have taken up philosophy properly for the first time, in a small reading group of fellow retirees. We have reached Dennett's discussion of real patterns and its connection to the physical, design, and intentional stances, and I have come to an impasse that I suspect is my own fault rather than his.

As I understand the proposal: a pattern in a body of data is real just when there is some description of that data that is more compact than the bit map, the raw and uncompressed record, with the further allowance that a description may still earn its keep if the residue it leaves over is small enough to be written off as noise. The three stances, on this telling, are ways of getting predictive purchase on a system: from its physical state together with the laws of nature, from its attributed function and design, and from beliefs and desires attributed under a presumption of rationality. What makes an intentional pattern real rather than merely projected, I take it, is that it pays for itself in compression.

My worry is that it pays far too easily. Consider what I have been calling an index language: for records of some fixed length n, the language contains a distinct one-symbol name for each possible record. Relative to such a language, any record whatever compresses to roughly log n bits, and so is described vastly more efficiently than by its bit map. Nothing in the criterion as I have stated it seems to rule the language out; it is a perfectly legitimate compression scheme, and the fit is exact rather than noisy. Since the construction works for every record, it appears to follow that every body of data instantiates a real pattern — and hence, which is the part I find hardest to swallow, that the intentional stance is as much available for the granite boulder behind my barn as for the chess program on my laptop. There is some scheme under which the boulder's thermal history beats its bit map. My group took this as a reductio: an account that cannot tell a boulder from a believer has not given us what we wanted about the reality of the intentional.

My own attempt at a repair only seemed to make matters worse. The natural fix is to require that the compression scheme be fixed in advance of the data, or at least not tailored to it. But that is only the old experimentalist's discipline against fitting more parameters than the data can bear, and it strikes me as relocating the difficulty rather than dissolving it: someone still has to choose the scheme, and if we choose a universal Turing machine and lean on the invariance theorem, then we get independence from the choice of language only up to an additive constant, which is negligible asymptotically and utterly dominating for the short finite records that are all we ever actually possess. So one horn of the dilemma makes the criterion trivial, and the other makes pattern-hood relative to an arbitrarily chosen codebook — which is to say, in my ears, a quietly anti-realist view wearing a realist's vocabulary.

I would be grateful for help with four things. First, is either horn actually forced, or does the account have resources I have not seen? Second, on this account, does the boulder really have a real pattern, and is that answer as damaging as it feels? Third, have I misdescribed the work the stances are meant to do — am I asking the notion of a real pattern to deliver a kind of objectivity it was never hired to provide? Fourth, does my index-language construction have a standard answer in the literature, and if so, what is the argument rather than a list of things to read, since chasing references is not something I can do here.

Two further requests about the manner of your reply. My confidence is low and my group will take whatever I report back to them as settled, so please mark clearly where you are reporting what the view plainly commits its holder to, where you are reconstructing or extrapolating, and where you are simply offering your own judgement. And if the line of thought above rests on a misreading — if, for instance, I have conflated a pattern in a record with a pattern in the world, or treated compression of data already in hand as though it settled what the system would do next — I would much rather be told that plainly than have my question answered politely.
