I'm a science-and-ideas journalist, and I sit as the lay member of the medication-safety committee of a large hospital network. A design studio has asked us to endorse a pilot: strip the written directions off our discharge medication leaflets and replace them with a wordless pictogram system in the lineage of Otto Neurath's Vienna Method — the conventions later called Isotype, in which quantities are shown by repeating identical same-sized symbols rather than enlarging them, perspective is avoided, the vocabulary of signs is deliberately limited, and pictures are treated as a helping device that still has to be taught rather than as a self-explaining universal script. The studio's proposal adds something Neurath did not have: a generative "transformer" that composes new pictograms on demand from the prescription text, so the system is no longer confined to a fixed set of approved signs.

The test case in front of us is an instruction of the kind that fills real leaflets: do not take this medicine with grapefruit juice; if you miss a dose, take it when you remember, unless fewer than six hours remain before your next dose, in which case skip the missed one. The studio renders this with a crossed-out grapefruit, a clock, an arrow, and a question mark. In their pilot, comprehension improved among patients with limited literacy — but several participants read the crossed-out grapefruit as identifying the medicine rather than prohibiting something, and our clinical members argue that a picture cannot carry the reason a rule exists.

Draft the memo I will circulate to the committee: a clear recommendation — adopt as a permanent supplement to text, adopt as a conditional pilot, or decline — with reasons the studio could actually respond to. I need it to say where the expressive ceiling of a picture system lies (negation, conditionals, exceptions, time, degrees of confidence), what must remain in words, and what would have to be true of machine-generated pictograms before we permit any of them. Give the strongest version of the equity argument on the losing side, since that is the argument I find hardest to dismiss.
