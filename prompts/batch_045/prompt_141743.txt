hey — I run a tiny reading group, about half philosophy grad students and half working programmers, and last month we did a session on mind uploading that basically fell apart: everyone kept saying "well it depends what you mean by you" and nothing got sorted. So I want to build a clickable decision tree for the group wiki that sorts you into a position by what you say about cases, not by which label you already like. Assume we all grant two things at the start so the tree doesn't branch on them: a perfect neuron-for-neuron functional replacement is technically possible, and "would I survive this procedure" is a question that can be asked even if the answer turns out to be "there's no fact of the matter."

What I want is 8 to 10 branch nodes, each phrased as a question about a concrete case that someone could answer without knowing where it leads. I've got a list of cases I want used, mostly because they're the variants we argued about and I don't trust myself to have set them up right. (1) The standard one-at-a-time swap, except the synthetic unit only ever matches the original's input-output function on 99.9 percent of cases and diverges on rare inputs — does that matter, and does it matter more as the fraction replaced grows? (2) A "crossfade" version with no discrete swap at all: each old unit's output weight ramps down over hours while its replacement's ramps up, so there's no instant you can point to where a swap happened; compare to the sharp version and say whether the difference is doing any work. (3) At about 40 percent replacement the subject reports that experience is getting "thinner," less vivid, colors less saturated, no complaints about content or memory — what's your verdict about what's happening, and does your answer change if the reports stop after the procedure completes? (4) Order effects: cortex replaced first versus brainstem and thalamus first; and the whole thing done under deep general anesthesia versus done while awake and reporting. (5) An emulation built from a year of high-resolution scanning, with the biological brain destroyed all at once and the emulation started the next instant — this is my control case for whether gradualness per se is what's doing the work, so I want the tree to be able to separate people who care about gradualness from people who care about functional match. (6) The branching family: biological units are preserved as they're removed and later reassembled into a working biological brain while the upload is running, and the harder variant where the biological brain is kept alive and conscious throughout so there are two candidates at every step. (7) A timing case: the synthetic substrate runs a thousand times faster, so the person has a century of subjective experience in five weeks. (8) A gap case: destroyed, scan stored, rebuilt two hundred years later. (9) The never-run scan: the information exists and is complete but is never instantiated — is that survival, death, or a badly formed question?

For leaves, I need at least these six to show up and I'd like anything else you think belongs: the biological/substrate-dependence view where the upload is a copy and you die; straight functionalism or patternism where gradualness is irrelevant; the view that gradual replacement is what saves it and the instantaneous version is death (or something close to death); the no-branching or best-candidate view that only becomes visible in case 6; the reductionist view where survival is a matter of degree and the original question is partly empty; and the no-persistent-self option where the whole exercise is misdescribed. Build the tree from the logical space first, though — lay out which combinations of answers are actually available, then locate the named positions in it, and for the combinations where nobody lives, tell me in one line whether that's because the combo is incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unoccupied because no one's bothered to defend it. Also flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really pull apart — I suspect functionalism and no-branching functionalism collapse outside case 6, and I'm not sure anything in my case list distinguishes the reductionist from the no-self person — and tell me what extra case would separate them if so. I'm putting this in front of people who'll happily argue for an hour about whether the crossfade case is coherent, so err on the side of the awkward commitments being stated plainly.
