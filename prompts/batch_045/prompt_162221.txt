Our lab does a reading group once a term and its my turn next week, so instead of summarizing a paper I'm trying to write down my actual position on animal sentience. I'd like you to audit it before I stand up there — I genuinely can't tell which bits are load-bearing, and I'd rather find out now. Here's the package:

(1) Method first: theory-light. I don't want to commit to a global theory of consciousness, I'm not signing up for a global workspace story or an integrated-information one. I want convergent empirical markers, and I want every verdict treated as provisional and revisable.

(2) Substance: for welfare purposes only valence matters. The question isn't "is it conscious" but "does anything matter to it." A creature with perceptual consciousness and no affect has no welfare, so I don't count it.

(3) Fish feel pain. I've come round on this. Nociception, plus flexible trade-offs, plus analgesia shifting what they do, is enough for me.

(4) Insects: really agnostic. But I also think insect farming is probably one of the biggest animal-welfare problems there is, on the grounds that the numbers are astronomical and the probability isn't negligible — and where we just don't know, the precautionary principle says weight them anyway.

(5) Consciousness is graded, not binary. No sharp line, and there are probably creatures with a thin little bit of it.

(6) The marker set itself has to be taxon-neutral. Nothing in it should require human-ish anatomy, and a lineage shouldn't get marked down just because it solves its ecological problems with innate routines rather than learning.

(7) The marker I've started leaning on hardest, which is the newest bit for me — call it lingering. After an aversive event, does the animal show a slow, context-sensitive recovery curve, worse when background conditions are already bad, with later behaviour visibly reorganized, rather than a fast reflex-like snap-back? The thought is that a pure nociceptive system should snap back, so lingering tracks valence specifically, which is exactly what (2) says I need.

So: which of these actually fight each other, and which only look like they do? Give me the tensions ranked by severity rather than all flagged as equal, for each one say what would have to give, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Don't just tell me the literature is unsettled, I know that. Under 500 words, American English.
