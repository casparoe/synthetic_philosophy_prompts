hey — I'm in a philosophy of physics module this term and each of us has to run one of the weekly problem sessions. Mine's next week: 16 people, 75 minutes, normal seminar room with a whiteboard, and I can bring index cards, pens, poker chips, dice, bags of marbles, that sort of thing. No laptops, and most of the group are philosophers who'd run from a partition function, so anything physical has to be doable with cards and tokens.

We did Maxwell's demon a couple weeks back and it went badly — half the room came away thinking the demon is beaten by some engineering detail about the shutter, and nobody felt the actual "information is physical" point in their bones. My tutor said I shouldn't name Landauer or Szilard until the last fifteen minutes, so the idea has to arrive through what happens to people's chip stacks, not through me explaining it.

What I want is a game where small teams play the demon: there's a box with a shutter, molecules show up on one side or the other according to a sequence I hold, opening the shutter at the right moment earns energy chips and at the wrong moment costs them, and the only way to know which moment is to have written the molecule's side down on a memory register made of index-card slots. Registers are short, so you eventually have to reset slots to keep playing, and resetting is what costs you. I'd like the numbers worked out so that a team that never records anything comes out roughly flat, a team that records everything and pays full freight to reset ends up down, and nobody beats the house — please show me the arithmetic that makes that happen rather than just asserting it.

Two wrinkles I'd like built in, if you can make them work. First, half the teams' molecule sequences should be independent coin flips and the other half's should be strongly patterned — long runs, or mostly-repeating — while both decks come out about 50/50 overall, so the difference is structure and not bias. Second, before resetting, a team should be allowed to re-encode their register more shortly and pay only for the short version, provided they can show me how to get the original back. I have a feeling that's where the real lesson is hiding and I can't quite see how to stage it.

Then: a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto the parts of the story (including why cleverer record-keeping only ever gets you closer to break-even), what will predictably go wrong when I run it — I especially want an answer ready for the person who says the reset fee is just a rule I invented and doesn't show the cost is physical — and an honest note on what the game can't stage at all, so I know what I have to say out loud. Also tell me what to do if nobody discovers the short-encoding move on their own.
