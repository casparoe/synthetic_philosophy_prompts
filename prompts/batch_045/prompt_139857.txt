I'm not an academic at all — I do scheduling for a freight company and read biology blogs at night — and I got dragged into a thread on a birding forum that has been stuck in my head for a week. Someone posted photos of a gull hatched this spring in a local colony with a slightly odd, narrower wing feather shape, and a few poeple were saying it probably helps it glide in the windy gap where that colony sits. I asked what I thought was a harmless question: does that feather shape have a function right now, in this one bird, this season, before anything has been selected for or against, before it has left any descendants, while it's just the first individual that happens to have it? Someone answered with Dobzhansky's line — "nothing in biology makes sense except in the light of evolution" — and told me that settles it, come back in fifty generations. It doesn't feel like it settles it, but I can't say why. When I went poking around I found two families of accounts of what a biological function even is, one that ties a trait's function to what it was selected for in the past and one that ties it to what it currently contributes to some bigger system you pick out and analyse (I think they get called something like the selected-effect view and the causal-role view), and my suspicion is that the slogan is being used as a way of quietly helping itself to the first one without arguing for it.

So: what are the different precise claims that sentence could actually be making? I want them as a menu, from the boring to the wild, with a verdict on each — platitude everybody already accepts, substantive and defensible, substantive but contested, or just plain false — and for each one, what it would say about my gull. Some readings I can half-see: that evolutionary history is just a good heuristic for guessing what a part is for (fine, but then it settles nothing); that no trait has a function at all unless an actual history of selection stands behind it; that a trait's function is fixed by its history and not by what it happens to be doing now; that living things don't have purposes in anything like the way a designed object does; that you can't even describe or classify an organism properly without its ancestry; and maybe something stronger still, like every biological explanation has to be a historical one. I'm sure I'm missing some and probably mashing together others.

Two more cases I keep testing readings against, so feel free to use them: a synthetic cell assembled in a lab from a stripped-down genome, where every part was deliberately put there by engineers last year and nothing was shaped by generations of selection; and a structure in some species that originally evolved for one job and now does something completely different, or now does nothing much at all. Oh, and my dad's artery after a stent went in — it still moves blood, but partly because of a bit of metal mesh. If any of your readings answer those differently than they answer the gull, or if one of those cases blows up a reading that looked fine a minute earlier, that's the most useful thing you can tell me. Please don't just pick the reading you like and unpack it — I want the whole menu, I want to know which reading would actually earn that guy his "come back in fifty generations" as opposed to something weaker like "you can't tell yet whether it's adaptive", and I want a guess at which one people who work on this are usually reaching for when they repeat the line.
