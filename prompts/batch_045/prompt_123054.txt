Hey, hope this one's fun rather than annoying. I'm helping a friend put together the comedy issue of a small politics-and-history magazine — very much the register of Private Eye's more bureaucratic pages — and she's asked me to draft one item, which is a government procurement document, and I'd love your help with it.

Set-up: a small, recently independent country (invent whatever geography and history you like — say two or three million people at home and another million or so scattered abroad) has decided that it requires a national tradition, and is putting the job out to competitive tender. So: write an "Invitation to Tender" from its Ministry of Heritage, about 700 words, as numbered clauses with section headings (Background, Scope, Mandatory Requirements, Evaluation Criteria, Exclusions, Pricing, that sort of thing), in the flat, bloodless voice of civil-service-slash-management-consultant English. The humour should come from the theory being right rather than from anyone winking: the thing being procured is an invented tradition, in the sense used by people who argue that a great deal of what passes for immemorial national custom was deliberately formalised and ritualised pretty recently, and usually precisely at moments when older ways of life were being torn up by rapid social change. So the document should worry, in total deadpan, about things like: the tradition has to bind people together *because* it is old, while being brand new; it has to be repeatable and formalisable enough to be taught in schools and performed by the army; it has to be performable simultaneously by huge numbers of people who will never meet, each of whom should nonetheless feel the others doing it at the same moment — let that do the work the "imagined community" idea does, without ever using the phrase; it has to be legally voluntary but socially compulsory; it has to be compatible with a civic rather than ethnic definition of citizenship while still giving citizens the sense that they have always belonged; and it needs to survive two or three generations of forgetting before anyone can safely admit it was designed. I'd also like at least one invented archival reference in a footnote, one anxious clause about what happens if a journalist gets hold of the tender (which is itself classified, obviously), and some quietly horrifying pricing line about antiquity being delivered as an emergent property of the documentation package rather than purchased directly. No explaining the theory anywhere, no stage directions, no essayistic bits — it should read like a real document a firm could bid on, and ideally close with the standard boilerplate about submission deadlines, which is where I'd like the sharpest joke to land. Please write it in British English — organise, colour, "the Department", that sort of thing.
