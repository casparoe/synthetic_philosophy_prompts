I sit on the review board for a distributed storage system, and I want a sanity check on a policy change before I put it to the board.

Our safety case is a conjunction of roughly 400 component claims, one per shard, replication path, and codec. The rule is that a claim may be signed once its probability is at least 0.99. At our last review someone pointed out that, if the component failures are independent, this leaves the probability of the whole conjunction under 2%, so we are signing a document we ourselves expect to be false. From some reading around, I gather that this is just the lottery paradox, and that the preface paradox is the same problem minus the arithmetic: an author who is confident of every claim in the book and also of the note in the front saying that somewhere in it there is a mistake.

My plan is to raise the per-claim threshold to 0.999, which puts the conjunction above 50%, and to deal with both paradoxes with that single fix. Three things I can't settle on my own. First, whether 0.999 is actually defensible, or whether the same reasoning just pushes the required number higher again. Second, whether we should instead give up one of the two things that seem to be causing the trouble: signing off on claims merely because they are highly probable, or treating the safety case as the conjunction of everything we have signed. Third, whether the independence assumption matters here at all, given that about forty of the shards share a single consensus library and would likely fail together.
