I work in programming language theory — I write Agda and Lean most days, and the vocabulary I actually think in is: signatures and inductive datatypes, judgement forms and their derivations, typing and reduction rules, eliminators, universe levels, definitional versus propositional equality, reification and reflection (quote/unquote), and above all the line between what can be said inside the object language and what exists only in the metatheory. My philosophy is two undergraduate courses, long ago.

I want the problem of the unity of the proposition and Bradley's regress reconstructed in that vocabulary as faithfully as you can manage, and then I want the residue report.

The translation I'm after, in order:

1. State the problem first in your own words but at the level of the versions it comes in, not a textbook gloss: why the proposition is supposed to be more than the collection of its constituents; why supplying a further relation to bind those constituents is said to restart the demand; and the difference between Bradley's own use of that regress (relations turn out to be unreal, and the relational picture of reality with them), Russell's worry about the relating relation in the multiple-relation theory of judgement, and Frege's response that concepts are unsaturated.

2. Now rebuild it in my vocabulary. Give me the constituents as terms and types, and show me precisely which move generates the regress: as far as I can tell it is reification — representing a relation as a value, say an element of an inductive datatype of relation codes together with an interpretation function into Type, rather than as the type former `A → A → Type`. Show the regress actually running in that setting: once the relation is data, binding it to its arguments needs an application constant, that constant is itself a term needing to be bound, and so on. Then show what stops it in ordinary dependent type theory and what that stopping move costs.

3. Tell me which candidate in my vocabulary is doing the work of 'unity': the derivation of a judgement (say `Γ ⊢ P type` and `Γ ⊢ p : P`), well-formedness, inhabitation, or something else. I suspect the honest answer is that unity is a property of a derivation and not a thing in the language at all, and I want you to say what follows from that — including what happens when I try to make the proposition itself the object of an attitude, since a type is not a term until I reify it, and reification is exactly what starts the regress again.

4. Say whether the regress, on your reconstruction, is structurally benign or vicious, and make the contrast sharp: a universe hierarchy `Type₀ : Type₁ : …` is infinite and harmless because every level is reached, whereas an application operator that must be applied to itself at the same level is not. Which one is Bradley's? Does a ladder of `apply` at successively higher universe levels even correspond to anything in his argument, or does it change the subject?

5. The residue report. What mapped cleanly (I expect unsaturatedness to land on function types and on the fact that a term of function type cannot be a full-blooded object of the same kind as its arguments). What translated only with distortion — I particularly want your view on truth versus inhabitation, since a false proposition is not obviously an empty type, and on the difference between a proposition and the fact or truthmaker that would make it true. And what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all: the step from 'the relational judgement cannot be made intelligible' to a monist conclusion about reality; the act of judging and its force; and above all the demand that a theory explain unity rather than legislate it, since in a formal system a rule is either primitive or derived and application cannot be derived from anything.

Then the verdict I actually care about. Is the residue a defect of my vocabulary — things it cannot say that a theory of propositions has to be able to say — or is it a sign that the original demand was less clear than it looked? And, bluntly: how much of your translation is translation, and how much is dependent type theory quietly taking Frege's side by making application a rule of the kernel rather than a term? Mark that distinction explicitly wherever it arises, and tell me what a vocabulary would have to look like in which Bradley's own conclusion was expressible.
