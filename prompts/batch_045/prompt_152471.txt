I mod a ~40k-person community in a niche technical hobby (Discord plus a forum, professional and amateur members mixed together), and I also write the weekly roundup that goes out to everyone. Our head mod wants us to start doing a quarterly "epistemic self-audit" of the community and of each of its subforums, and she wants it to be a procedure, not a vibe: something a mod with no philosophy background can run in about half an hour per subforum, using only things we can actually observe from mod tooling and from reading a random sample of threads, producing a short result that has to be signed off by her and then posted publicly — which is a real constraint, because if it reads as "your community is brainwashed" it will start a flame war and get reverted, so it needs to be able to say "this community has this structural feature" without issuing a verdict on any member's rationality. The distinction I want to operationalize is the one I half-remember from a philosophy paper I read a while back (possibly by someone named Nguyen? I genuinely don't remember and I don't need a citation): an *epistemic bubble* is where you simply never encounter the other side, it's a failure of omission, and it pops on contact with outside sources; an *echo chamber* is where you do encounter them but the community has installed a credibility-reduction machine against outgroup sources, so exposure makes things worse rather than better, and the thing to audit is the machine, not the exposure.

Here's the wrinkle I most want the procedure to handle, because I think it's real and I don't think it's in the thing I read: our sister community runs "steelman Saturday," where every week they post the strongest version of the outgroup case *already attached to a rebuttal written by one of their own*, and everyone there thinks this makes them unusually open-minded. My suspicion is that refutation-first curation is a discreditation engine with extra steps — it manufactures the appearance of contact while training members that outgroup arguments arrive pre-defeated — and is therefore worse than simply not posting them, but I want a procedure that can *return* that verdict rather than me asserting it. Relatedly, I want the procedure to distinguish a community that discredits the outgroup *source* ("that lab is industry-funded") from one that discredits the *practice of taking it seriously* ("why are you platforming this," "this is both-sidesing"), and to track the asymmetry between inbound permeability (do outgroup links ever get posted?) and outbound permeability (do members read everything and just filter the uptake?), plus whether ex-members who come back with reports from outside get a hearing or get treated as contaminated. So: give me the questions in the order to ask them, what counts as a sufficient answer to each given only mod-level evidence, where the procedure stops with a hard NO on an intervention — my strong prior is that "just add more outside sources" should be a NO for one of the two categories and a YES for the other, and I want that written into the flow — and where it hands the decision to a person because it's a values question rather than a factual one (e.g. our community does legitimately exist to advance a position, and the head mod will veto anything that requires judging whether an outgroup claim is actually true, so the procedure has to bracket first-order truth and only audit structure). Then run it on three cases: a small genealogy subforum that cites only three in-group sites but is perfectly friendly to outsiders; the steelman-Saturday community; and our own main forum — and I want you to be willing to give me an answer I won't like on the third one, since my honest guess is that we're an echo chamber specifically around whether the mainstream institutions in our field are trustworthy, and that my own weekly roundup is part of the machine. Also tell me which distinctions from the theory have to survive the simplification for it to still be doing real work and which can be dropped, and be blunt about where it will misfire — especially that it can't distinguish a justified refusal to amplify a bad actor from an unjustified one.
