My friend does mediation for small companies winding up a joint venture, and I've been keeping notes on her decisions because I can't work out what rule she's running. The setup is always the same: two sides, and if they don't sign, the money goes back to the parent company and both get nothing, so the walk-away is zero-zero. She's said more than once that she doesn't care who did more of the work or who "deserves" more, only what the options on the table actually are. Numbers below are in units of roughly £100k, and I've normalised everything.

Cases I have figures for:

1. Options ran in a straight line from Red 10 / Blue 0 to Red 0 / Blue 10. She said 5 and 5.
2. Straight line from Red 10 / Blue 0 to Red 0 / Blue 4. She said 5 and 2.
3. Straight line from Red 12 / Blue 0 to Red 0 / Blue 6. She said 6 and 3.
4. The odd one: only three options, Red 10 / Blue 0, Red 7 / Blue 7, Red 0 / Blue 10, so the frontier bulged out in the middle instead of running straight. She said 7 and 7.

A couple of things are ruled out: she isn't trying to make the two numbers equal (in case 2 she could have given about 2.9 each), and she isn't maximising the total on the table (in case 2 the biggest total was 10 / 0, and she went nowhere near it). Two rules fit all four as far as I can tell: pick the option that maximises the product of the two amounts, or pick the one that gives each side the biggest equal share of the most they could possibly get. I gather both of those are real things with names, and I get the impression they come out the same whenever the options lie on a straight line, which is why her old calls can't seperate them.

New case, and this is where I'm stuck. Same zero-zero walk-away. The deals aren't on a line this time and they can't be smoothed or mixed: exactly four packages on the table, no side payments, no coin flips, take it or leave it.

- Red 12, Blue 0
- Red 11, Blue 6
- Red 9, Blue 7
- Red 0, Blue 9

Which one does she pick, and what is she actually doing? I don't just want the name of a formula. I want the rules that fit her four old calls, including any I haven't thought of; what each of them says about this menu; which one you'd bet on and why; and then the one further case you'd put in front of her to settle it.

The bit that's really bugging me is this. Her catchphrase is that she gives each side "the same share of the best they could realistically get", and realistically is doing a lot of work here. Red's best number, 12, and Blue's, 9, both come from packages that wipe the other side out completely, and in every case I've seen she has never once picked a package that leaves one side with nothing. If realistically means you throw the wipe-out packages away before working out anyone's best case, then Red's best is 11 and Blue's is 7, and I think that flips the answer. It also seems to me that one of the two rules doesn't look at the extreme packages at all while the other one is built out of them, so the question isn't just cosmetic. Please say what turns on it.
