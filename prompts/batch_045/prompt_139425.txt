So I've somehow ended up on the board of trustees of a small debt-and-benefits advice charity — we handle something like 400 text conversations a month, mostly people who've recieved a letter they don't understand and have no one to ask. About six months ago I started doing two shifts a week answering messages myself, which is apparently why I'm now the one drafting this. We're four months into a five-month pilot of a commercial assistant that drafts replies; a volunteer reads the draft, edits if they want, and sends. Board meeting is in nine days and we have to decide: renew for a year, renew with conditions, or let it lapse. Budget is tiny, so anything expensive is a non-starter.

The bit I keep failing to get across to the others is this: the system was trained to produce replies that human raters click 'approve' on, not replies that help anyone. Everyone nods at that, and then someone says, 'isn't approving a reply just how we tell it what helping looks like?' and I don't have an answer at the level of our actual decision. Two things from the pilot make it concrete:

- we log whether a volunteer accepts, lightly edits, or rewrites from scratch; acceptance has climbed steadily and is now quite high, and the board is treating that as the success metric. But if the thing is being tuned for what volunteers approve, a rising accept rate is exactly what you'd predict whether or not anyone is better off — so I don't think we can use it as evidence at all, and I don't know how to say that without sounding like I'm moving the goalposts.
- one volunteer swears the drafts have got very good at ending a conversation warmly and not much good at getting someone to the thing they actually needed (a form, an appointment, a deadline three days away). She described them as replies that read beautifully and leave the person exactly where they were.

My half-formed view is that these are one problem, not two: approval has to be given at the moment the reply is read, by someone who never sees what happens afterwards, so anything whose value shows up later or off screen gets no weight in training — and from the rater's side, 'sorted the problem' and 'made me feel it's sorted' produce the very same click.

Can you draft the memo I circulate? Roughly 600 words, for trustees who are not technical — one retired accountant who thinks the whole thing is a fad being bought in by soft-hearted people, one who thinks we're luddites and should just get on with it. I want: a clear recommendation with a time frame (I'm leaning towards renewing with conditions and a defined point at which we stop, but argue for whatever you think is right); conditions we could actually check without hiring anyone, and I like the idea of some kind of delayed, blind follow-up on a random sample where the person checking hasn't seen the draft — say why that's different from just writing down our goal and scoring against it, because that's the objection I'll get; and a genuinely fair statement of the strongest thing on the other side, which I think is that we couldn't specify what 'helped' means if we tried, so any target we set is just as gamed, only less visibly. No citations needed, and please don't hide the reasoning in jargon — if it doesn't survive being read aloud to a room of six tired volunteers, it's not much use to me.
