I'm retired now — spent most of my career running a small field ecology lab, so my working life was sampling protocols, what counts as a genuine replicate vs. a pseudoreplicate, pre-registering the analysis before you see the data, and thinking of a probability as a long-run frequency you could in principle go out and count. I've started going to a philosophy reading group at the local library, and last week we hit the Sleeping Beauty thing: she's put under on Sunday, a fair coin gets flipped, on heads she's woken Monday only, on tails she's woken Monday and Tuesday with a memory wipe in between, and each time she's awake she's asked how confident she is in heads. Half the room said a half, half said a third, and I came away with no idea what they were actually disagreeing about — it sounded like both sides were right about two different things, which in my old job usually means someone changed the sampling unit halfway through.

So: restate the whole dispute in the vocabulary I actually have. Treat it as an experiment I have to design. What is the unit of replication here — the coin flip, the awakening, or the run of the experiment? Are the two tails awakenings independent data points or pseudoreplicates of a single subject? What exactly is being estimated, what's the sampling distribution, and what frequency does each answer converge to if I ran the whole thing a few thousand times? What changes if I have to write the analysis plan down on Sunday before the coin is flipped — does pre-registration settle it, and if so for which of the two answers? Do the halfer and the thirder turn out to be two different pre-registered protocols, or the same protocol read two ways? And where do the betting arguments fit: is offering her a bet at some odds a scoring rule for a forecaster, a decision problem, or something that smuggles in an extra assumption about how many bets there will be? Bring in the version where tails gets you a million awakenings if that makes the structure clearer.

Then the residue report, which is the bit I really want. From where I'm sitting the whole fight seems to be about self-locating belief — which awakening is this one — and I cannot find anything in my vocabulary that that corresponds to. There's no column in the datasheet for it. I want to know whether that's a genuine gap in the experiment-design way of talking, or whether it's a sign that the philosophers' question was never the kind of thing my framework was built to answer. Same worry about the word "credence": do they mean a number I could check against a frequency, or a number that just isn't the sort of item that has a sampling distribution? If it's the second, tell me plainly what I'd be giving up if I refused the word and stuck to long-run frequencies and betting behaviour — is there some question I then definately can't ask, or only some question I won't let myself ask?

Don't be polite about where my vocabulary breaks down. Pseudoreplication is the handle I keep reaching for and I want to know whether it's the right one or whether I'm forcing it.
