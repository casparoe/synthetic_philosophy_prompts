my flatmate (ML engineer, sits in on the same Indian philosophy reading group as me, for some reason) keeps insisting that the four pramāṇas are just a data pipeline, and I cant work out whether its a genuinely useful mapping or whether the conclusion is being smuggled in by the framing.

Quick version of the list as weve been taught it: pratyakṣa is perception, arising from sense contact, and for Nyāya (unlike some of their Buddhist opponents) it terminates in a judgement like this is a jar, with a bare non-conceptual stage before it; anumāna is inference, where you see smoke on the hill and come to know there is fire, via a pervasion like wherever there is smoke there is fire, which is supposed to be exceptionless rather than merely frequently observed; upamāna is the case where someone tells you a gavaya is like a cow, you later see one in the forest, and you thereby come to know that that is what the word gavaya picks out; śabda is coming to know something from the sentence of a trustworthy speaker. Memory is deliberately left off the list, because pramā is supposed to be new knowledge, anadhigata, not something you already had.

His mapping goes: pratyakṣa is the sensor and the raw input stream; anumāna is the model doing inference over the latent state; upamāna is one-shot or nearest-prototype matching, you get one description and then classify on similarity; śabda is the pretrained weights, other peoples perception compressed and installed in you. He then leans on it twice. First, to argue that śabda and upamāna arent autonomous sources at all: upamāna is inference from a single labelled example, and śabda is inference from the reliability of the speaker (his line is that āptatva is just calibration), so the fourfold list is really two sources plus two delivery mechanisms and Nyāya should have gone with the two-pramāṇa reduction its Buddhist critics wanted. Second, he says the Nyāya distinction between non-conceptual and conceptual perception is just the raw-input-versus-post-classifier distinction, so the list is a taxonomy of processing stages rather than of warrants.

My own worry, which he waves off: if its a pipeline, the cache should be a source too, and Nyāya says memory is not one. So either the pipeline picture is wrong or the novelty condition is a bug in the definition of pramā, and he happily says the latter. I also dont think we ever settled what upamāna actually outputs, similarity or the word-world relation, and that seems to matter for whether one-shot classification is even the right gloss.

So: give me the correspondence table, Nyāya term to pipeline component, then audit it. What in the source has no counterpart in the target and vice versa. I am thinking about the fact that pervasion is meant to be modal and exceptionless rather than a learned correlation, the fact that a pramāṇa for Nyāya is the karaṇa, the most efficient cause of a veridical episode, rather than an information channel, and the fact that nothing in a pipeline outputs something that is both truth-evaluable and news to the system. For each of those, say whether it actually bites against the two conclusions hes drawing or whether its noise. Does either conclusion survive the disanalogies, and if the pipeline framing was carrying them the whole time, is there a better analogy, or is the honest answer that this just isnt the kind of thing with a pipeline structure at all.
