I am restructuring the opening fortnight of a graduate seminar on the units of selection, and I want to begin with a specification rather than with the usual tour of positions. Before my students read another defense of gene selectionism, another exposition of the multi-level framework, and another insist that the two are the same claim written in different notation, I want them to know what a correct account of the level or levels at which selection acts would have to be able to do. So: write the desiderata.

Fix the purpose as follows, and tell me in your first section if you think it is the wrong one. The account is to be good enough to tell a working evolutionary biologist what to measure in a given system: which quantities to track, at which level to look for adaptation, and what would settle a dispute about whether some particular case is a case of selection at a level higher than the gene. It is not primarily meant to settle a metaphysical question about whether groups or organisms exist as objects of selection over and above their parts, and I am willing to hear that some putative desiderata are only desiderata under one of these purposes and not the other.

Here are the cases I want each requirement tested against. Two of them I have constructed; that is deliberate, since a list of requirements that only has to fit the textbook examples has not been tested.

(a) A trait that lowers the expected reproductive output of its bearer and raises that of its neighbours, in a population with limited dispersal. This is the site of the familiar claim that the kin-selection and group-selection parameterisations agree on the outcome while disagreeing on the description.

(b) Within-genome conflict: a genetic element that biases its own transmission to offspring while lowering the fertility of the organisms carrying it; and a mobile element that copies itself within a genome at a small cost to the organism.

(c) A clonal cell lineage that is transmitted from one host to another as an infectious agent, persists far longer than any individual host, carries heritable variation of its own, is not part of any host germline, and whose success depends both on how fast it proliferates within a host and on how long it keeps hosts alive and in contact with further hosts. A handful of such lineages are known in animals.

(d) The constructed case: a plant lineage that can no longer acquire nitrogen without a bacterial partner, and a bacterial lineage that can no longer persist outside the plant's root nodules. Each partner reproduces on its own schedule; the plant acquires some symbionts from its parent and some from the soil; the joint output of a pair exceeds what either would achieve alone; and the fitness of each partner depends on traits carried in the other's genome. Is the pair a unit? What would have to be measured to decide?

(e) A major transition: a formerly free-living lineage that is now a part, such as the mitochondrion or the somatic cell, whose own fitness has been reduced to almost nothing by the suppression of conflict within the higher-level unit. What must a theory say about when a level ceases to be a level at which selection acts?

(f) Non-genetic inheritance: a foraging technique that spreads by oblique transmission between unrelated individuals, raises the survival of the groups that have it, and lowers the reproductive output of the individuals who practise it. Say whether your list is meant to cover this, and if not, what marks the boundary.

(g) The bookkeeping worry: a single generation's change in a population can be partitioned across levels in more than one way, the partitions agreeing on the total and disagreeing on the components. A theory that cannot say which partition is correct, or that says the question has no answer, owes us an account of why.

Here is the menu of demands I keep encountering, which I do not equally trust: give a unique partition of total change into level-specific components; be usable with the data a field study can actually produce, such as relatedness, group composition and fitness components, rather than complete causal graphs; reproduce the verdicts of the inclusive-fitness machinery wherever that machinery applies; never invoke a higher level when a lower-level account plus population structure will do; state what has to hold for something to be a unit of selection at all, whether reproduction, heritable variance in fitness, both, or something else, and distinguish units from mere collections and from mere substrates of transmission; permit several levels to be genuine units simultaneously and to conflict; handle within-genome conflict without letting the gene level be absorbed into the organism level; give an account of the appearance and the disappearance of levels; yield verdicts that do not depend on an arbitrary choice among causally equivalent models, or else say what observable difference that choice makes; be formulable in the Price equation, or independently of it; say for each system whether the level question is empirical, conventional or both; say at which level adaptation accumulates, or explain why that is a separate question; handle traits whose group-level benefits are byproducts of individual-level design; be stable under the fact that generations and heritability are defined differently at different levels; and extend to, or explicitly exclude, non-genetic inheritance.

Do not give me a list of everything anyone has ever asked of such a theory. Give me a judgement: which of these are genuine requirements on an account with the purpose I have fixed, which are preferences of one camp presented as requirements, and which are disguised restatements of a substantive conclusion and so beg the question. Add whatever is missing. For each requirement you accept, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement and mark it as non-negotiable, negotiable, or a preference of a camp. Assume no access to a library; everything you need about the cases is above, and I want no references, bibliographies or citations.

Please structure your reply in the following sections, in this order:

1. The purpose — roughly 100 words: accept or revise the purpose I have fixed, and say what changes in the list if it is revised.
2. Desiderata — roughly 700 words: the requirements, in the order of their importance, each with its motivating case or consideration and its status.
3. Joint unsatisfiability — roughly 350 words: which subsets of your own list you suspect cannot be satisfied together, with the argument for each.
4. The decisive requirement — roughly 150 words: name the single requirement that excludes the largest number of candidate theories, and say which candidates it excludes.
5. Scorecard — roughly 250 words: a table scoring gene-level replicator selection, multi-level selection in its two forms, the replicator-and-vehicle framework, and the causal-partitioning approach against your criteria, with no more prose than is needed to read the table.
6. The constructed cases — roughly 250 words: apply your list to cases (d) and (f), and say if either of them is a case your list runs out on.
