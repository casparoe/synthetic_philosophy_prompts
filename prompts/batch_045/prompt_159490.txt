While panicking about it I ran into Benatar's asymmetry argument — the four-case thing where the presence of pain is bad, the presence of pleasure is good, the absence of pain is good even when there's nobody there to enjoy that good, and the absence of pleasure isn't bad unless there's somebody who is deprived of it — and I can't work out whether it's supposed to be decisive for us, or whether it quietly collapses into nothing the moment you try to actually use it.

Two things I keep circling round that I haven't seen anyone deal with, and I'd much rather you engaged with these than gave me the standard for-and-against:

First, the argument is set up as a comparison between creating a person and not creating them, but that isn't our menu. Ours is closer to: start trying now, start trying in a year and a half, or don't. And because the child we'd concieve in eighteen months would be a numerically different person from the one we'd concieve next month — and if we end up going the IVF route there's a whole batch of possible kids of whom exactly one gets to be actual — the "nobody is deprived by not existing" move looks like it applies to every single month of hesitation just as much as it applies to never. So either the asymmetry gives us no guidance at all between those three options, or it ratchets us toward "don't" month by month, and I can't tell which is the fair reading. Is there a formulation of it that actually discriminates between them?

Second, I've been playing with what I think of as the deliberative reading: instead of reading the asymmetry as a claim about what's good and bad in the world, read it as a claim about which considerations are even allowed to count as reasons in deliberation — the absence of pleasure can never be a reason for anything, but the presence of pain always is. Does that reading hold up, and if it does, does it make the argument more or less threatening to the procreative side? I suspect it changes the shape of it quite a lot but I can't see how.
