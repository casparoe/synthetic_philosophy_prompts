hey — could you act as someone who's genuinely done comparative constitutional law but who's also read the philosophy-of-expression literature properly, i.e. someone who can argue the American side and the European side without turning either into a strawman? We need a referee who isn't secretly on one team.

Background: six of us (three Americans, two Germans, one Pole) have been going around in circles for about two months on hate speech, and instead of a third shouting match I want us to try an adversarial collaboration. Could you draft the joint statement that two of our members could both sign — call them the speech-libertarian (First Amendment instincts: content and viewpoint neutrality, counterspeech as the remedy, deep distrust of giving officials discretion over what counts as degrading) and the dignity/militant-democracy constitutionalist (the European style: group-based degradation as an injury to equal standing and to the conditions of democratic membership, proportionality review, democracy is allowed to defend itself)? To keep us from relitigating real-world statutes we all already have opinions about, anchor the whole thing on a made-up jurisdiction I'll call Bellford, which is considering an ordinance with these four features: it covers only public statements asserting that a listed group is subhuman, criminal-by-nature, or deserving of expulsion or violence; it is drafted to be symmetric, so it protects majorities, minorities and any other group on the same terms; before charging anyone, prosecutors must pass a least-restrictive-alternative review showing that counterspeech, civil remedies and platform design changes have been tried or would predictably fail; and the whole thing sunsets in five years with an independent body publishing an enforcement-disparity audit (charges broken down by the politics of the speaker and by which group was targeted) plus non-custodial penalties only.

What I want in the document: a numbered section of agreed claims that aren't vacuous — none of this both sides care about liberty and equality stuff — including agreement about what the ordinance actually does, about why a merely verbal dispute over whether this is regulating speech or regulating a dignitary wrong that happens to be committed by speaking might be partly verbal and which part isn't; then a disagreements table with, for each crux, the speech-libertarian's own formulation, the dignity side's own formulation (each phrased so that side would accept it as their position), the test, natural-experiment or argument both agree would bear on it, and each side's prediction written down in advance. Cruxes I suspect we'll land on: whether the covered category can be applied without viewpoint discrimination in practice (the audit is supposed to test this); whether chilling spills into adjacent contested speech like immigration or crime, and how you'd even measure that against a matched comparison city; whether algorithmic amplification by platforms counts as the kind of thing counterspeech was supposed to answer, or whether it breaks the counterspeech picture entirely; and the nasty one about whether the ordinance survives a change of government — if a genuinely hostile party took over Bellford, does this become a weapon, and does that possibility count against it now or is it a separate argument? For each crux, also say what would count as evidence against each side's own view, and flag honestly any crux you think is unresolvable at the precision we could actually measure.

Please start by brainstorming: throw out a rough list of maybe ten candidate cruxes, cross off the ones that are fake, purely verbal, or untestable, and tell us briefly why you crossed them off, then draft the statement. Take your time and think it through properly. Also — no case names, no citations, no literature review: we all know the cases and we definately don't need the real-world data, since what we're after is the design of the assessments and the predictions registered before anyone sees a result. If you think one of the two positions is simply right, resist saying so in the joint text; note it separately at the very end if you must.
