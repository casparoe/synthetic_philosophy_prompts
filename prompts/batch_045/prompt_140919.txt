I am assembling a problem set for a graduate seminar on belief revision, and I need one case built to order. Its job is to show that the choice of update rule is not a piece of bookkeeping but can change what an agent ought to do.

The literature around the original Judy Benjamin case — the soldier who is uncertain whether she has landed in Blue or Red territory, whether she is inside the Second Company area, and, on some tellings, what her rank is, and who then receives a radio message of the form if you are in the Second Company area then you are in Red territory — has convinced me that the disagreement between conditioning on the material conditional and imaging is genuine. But in every version I have taught, the two rules yield different posteriors and the same recommendation, and the students reasonably conclude that the dispute is cosmetic. I want a case where it is not, and I want it in a fresh setting so that they cannot pattern-match against the case they have already read.

Design me such a case, subject to the following constraints.

Scenario. One paragraph, quotidian rather than military or science-fictional: a courier in an unfamiliar city, a technician diagnosing a machine, a search party in a building. The point of this constraint is that the similarity or closeness structure over states should be readable off the story rather than stipulated; state that structure explicitly anyway, and say which features of the scenario license it.

State space. At most eight states, generated by two or three binary questions, every state with prior probability strictly between zero and one, priors given as small fractions. One binary question supplies the antecedent of the conditional and another its consequent.

Evidence. The agent's total new information is a conditional, learned outright: if A then B. She receives no other news, and no option of delaying or investigating — she must act immediately on her new credences.

Acts and payoffs. Two or three concrete acts, each of which a person in that situation could perform, with payoffs as small integers motivated by the story. Critically, the acts must be probabilistically irrelevant to the states: no act may raise or lower the chance that A, that B, or that the conditional is true. The difference in recommendation has to come entirely from the update rule, not from any causal or evidential entanglement between what she does and how the world is.

Rules to compare. (i) Conditioning on the material conditional, that is, on the proposition not-A or B. (ii) Lewis-style imaging on that conditional, relative to the closeness structure you have stated. (iii) Minimizing relative information subject to the constraint that the Adams-style conditional probability of B given A becomes one; if, as I suspect, this collapses onto (i), say so plainly and tell me what that collapse implies for how I present the choice to students.

Required verdict. The posteriors under (i) and (ii) must disagree, and they must disagree in a way that matters: some act maximizes expected utility under one rule and a different act does under the other. No ties, and no verdicts stipulated — the numbers must generate the flip. Both rules must still agree that the probability of the conditional is now one and that the probability of A and not-B is now zero; the disagreement is to be about where the displaced mass goes, not about whether it is displaced.

Then do the following. Lay out the prior table, the payoff table, and the computation under each rule. Give me the posterior probability of A under each rule and say which rule makes learning the conditional confirm its antecedent's negation. Identify the feature of the case doing the work — I expect it has to do with how much of the prior mass sits in the eliminated cell and how much of the consequent-relevant mass lies outside the antecedent — and show what happens to the divergence if that feature is removed. Report how wide the window is: over what range of payoff values and prior probabilities the flip survives, since a knife-edge case is useless to me in a lecture.

Two robustness checks. First, does the flip survive under the other reasonable closeness orderings for imaging, or does it depend on a particular one? Second, rerun everything on the reading where the message fixes a conditional chance rather than a certainty — if A, then B with something like a three-in-four chance — using generalized imaging against minimum relative information under the corresponding constraint. Say whether the flip survives, and if it survives only with contrived numbers, say that instead.

Finally, tell me how each side would attack the case: the defender of conditioning, who is willing to accept that learning a conditional can raise the probability of its antecedent's negation, and the imager, who treats that as the defect the case exposes. If some part of the specification turns out to be unsatisfiable except by contrivance, tell me which part and why; I would rather know than be handed a case that only looks like it works.
