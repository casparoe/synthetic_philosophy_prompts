ive been going down a rabbit hole on advance directives for a few weeks now (dont ask, it started as an argument about my grandmother) and this literature is driving me up the wall, because every argument is phrased in terms of persons and selves and who has authority over whom, and my brain keeps trying to compile it into decision theory and then segfaulting.

Here is the dispute as I have it. A woman signs a document at 60 saying: if I ever reach the point where I dont recognise my family and cant follow a conversation, then no antibiotics, no feeding tube, no heroics. At 75 she has advanced dementia, seems content, lights up at music, eats fine, and picks up a pneumonia.

- Position A, the integrity side: the document governs. What matters is not moment-to-moment welfare but the shape of the life as a whole; a life is a project, and capping it off with a course of treatment she explicitly rejected damages something that is still hers even though she cant grasp it now.
- Position B, the different-person-now side: the patient in the bed has no access to the values the document expressed, has pleasures and pains of her own, and is not really the subject who signed; the directive is a letter addressed to someone who moved out. Treat the patient you actually have.
- Position C, the compromise: the earlier self gets authority over which life-extending technologies are off the table but not over what makes whatever time is left go well. She can refuse the vent; she cant refuse the ice cream.

And my own wrinkle, which I have never seen anyone address — call it the lucid convert case. A 22 year old writes that if she is everseverely disabled she wants no life-sustaining treatment. At 45, after a spinal injury, she is fully competent, has two kids, and says her earlier self was an idiot who knew nothing about anything. Same reasoning capacity, different values. I want to know how each of A, B and C handles that case. I think it breaks at least one of them, but if it just means my case is confused, say that instead.

My actual suspicion, offered as a suspicion: the whole framing of the authority of the earlier self is a category error, because nobody is binding themselves here. The earlier self is gone or incapacitated; the directive is a string of text fed into an entirely different decision procedure — a surrogate, a doctor, a hospital committee, occasionally a judge — and the real question is what string makes that downstream procedure output something close enough to the earlier utility function. That would make this mechanism design, or writing a spec for an unreliable executor, and not a problem about self-binding or self-ownership at all. If so, most of the philosophical heat is misplaced and whats left is empirical work about how surrogates actually behave.

Vocabulary, so you know the target: I read the FDT paper when it came out and have followed this stuff for years, so updatelessness, precommitment, subagents, corrigibility, and value drift as non-stationary reward are all fine without explanation. I do RL-ish work professionally, so POMDPs, partial observability, reward misspecification, and mechanism design are home turf too. Philosophy-wise I have the sequences and Parfit secondhand (the transporter stuff and the general reductionist line), nothing more.

What I want:

1. Translate A, B, and C into that vocabulary as faithfully as you can, one at a time, without quietly importing decision-theoretic assumptions and calling them translation. Tell me specifically what happens to the word authority: does it map onto anything, or does it dissolve into something else?
2. Restate my convert case in the same vocabulary, and tell me whether it does the work I think it does.
3. A residue report: which claims translated cleanly, which translated only with distortion and what got bent, and which have no expression at all in the target vocabulary.
4. And the judgment I actually care about: is the leftover a defect of the decision-theoretic vocabulary — something it ought to be able to say and cant — or a sign that the original bioethics claims were less clear than they look? Some of each?

Give me the prose I asked for, then end with one machine-readable line, nothing after it: \answer{X}, where X is one of FRAMEWORK_GAP, DEBATE_CONFUSED, BOTH, or NOTHING_LEFT_OVER, whichever your residue report supports.
