ok so I'm on the steering committee of the bike co-op I volunteer at — about 300 members, shared tools, two floors. There's an unwritten thing about where tools go when you're done: the weekday regulars all hang theirs back on the labeled rack by the door, the weekend crowd mostly leaves them on their bench till end of day. Both patterns are stable, everyone in each clique expects everyone else in it to do the same, and honestly it barely matters which one you do as long as the other people in the room are doing it.

A few of us want to post a printed rule (pick one, either way) and one of the older members keeps shutting it down: "a norm is like which side of the road you drive on — nobody chose it, nobody enforces it, it holds because we all expect each other to, so a sign isn't going to make it work and might just kill the thing that was actually holding it together." He's read some Lewis and talks about coordination problems and equilibria and salience.

Two things I think complicate it but I can't tell whether they're the kind of thing that breaks the comparison or just noise. First: some people genuinely prefer that everyone else put tools away and that they personally not have to — they like a tidy shop, they just dont like tidying. Second: turnover is high, and about a year ago a manager did post a rule (everything back on the wall) and maybe thirty people followed it for a week before it quietly died.

Can you audit the driving analogy for me: what's supposed to map onto what (what plays the role of the road, the oncoming traffic, the two sides, the crash), which of those features our shop actually has and which it doesn't, where the mapping breaks, and whether his conclusion — don't formalize, it won't take and might crowd out the real norm — survives once the differences are on the table. Then, if the actual problem is that neither option is salient enough, is there something short of a written rule that does the same work (painting the rack bright yellow, moving it right next to the door), and is that just the same top-down thing wearing a different hat?

Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no intro and no wrap-up.
