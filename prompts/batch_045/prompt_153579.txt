I work on interpretability at a machine-learning lab, and I would like help with a dispute inside my team that I suspect is a philosophical dispute wearing engineering clothes. My training is in computer science; I took one undergraduate epistemology course and remember very little of it, so please translate in both directions rather than assuming I know the philosophical vocabulary.

The vocabulary I actually have: models as functions; internal activations as points in a latent space; directions or features in that space; linear probes; interventions such as patching and ablation that test whether a direction is causally upstream of behaviour; a training distribution and a held-out distribution; deliberate distribution shift, where we evaluate on data nobody trained on; the objective function; and a data-generating process, which in our case we happen to know, because our data come from a simulator we wrote ourselves, with a causal graph over its latent variables.

Our working convention is that a network represents a variable of the generating process when three things hold: a linear probe recovers it, intervening along the matching direction changes behaviour the way our story predicts, and it helps on downstream tasks we care about. One senior member of the team says the third condition is the only one doing real work — that talk of representation, of latching onto real structure, of a model getting the world right, is only a façon de parler for usefulness to us, and that the difference people try to draw between a representation that corresponds to how things are and one that merely works is a difference with no content. She calls this the tool-not-mirror line, and someone told me it is roughly Rorty's antirepresentationalism, from a 1979 book I have not read. The rest of us think there must be more to say.

The case that splits us: we have two networks trained on the same simulator data. M1's features recover the simulator's latent variables one for one — probes are near-perfect, interventions do what the causal graph predicts, and performance holds up under distribution shift. M2 is an invertible reparameterization of M1: it encodes exactly the same information, it matches M1 on every held-out and shifted evaluation we have run, but its features are arbitrary mixtures of the simulator's latents that no probe recovers and that nobody can describe in ordinary terms. Because M2 is informationally equivalent to M1, any claim that M1 latched onto the structure of the world and M2 did not cannot be about how much information either one carries; it has to be about the form of the encoding.

What I want from you:

1. Restate Rorty's antirepresentationalism as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary — objective function, latent space, probes, interventions, training and shifted distributions, the simulator's causal graph — and then restate my team's disagreement in his: the mirror-of-nature picture, representationalism, correspondence versus consensus, and whatever else is carrying the load. A table of correspondences is fine.

2. Run the M1/M2 case through both versions and tell me what each side is committed to saying about it, including whether there is a fact of the matter about which network represents the generating process and which one merely works.

3. Give me the residue report, which is what I actually care about. Which of Rorty's claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression at all in my vocabulary? I have two suspicions I want you to adjudicate. First, that my setup is rigged: I am asking you to translate a position that wants to deflate the notion of representation into a vocabulary whose central technical term is representation, so flag every place where you had to import an assumption my framework does not already contain. Second, that we have something philosophers usually do not — a ground-truth generator that we built ourselves — and that this may make our dispute one that Rorty's position was never about. Tell me which suspicion is closer to right, or if both are.

4. Finally: is my team's disagreement substantive, or does it dissolve once the translation is honest? If it dissolves, tell me what we should be arguing about instead. If it does not, tell me what observation about the models — of a kind we could actually run — would settle it.
