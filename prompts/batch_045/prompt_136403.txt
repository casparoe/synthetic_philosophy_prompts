I work on the evaluation harness for a coding-agent product, and a few of us are drafting an internal position piece arguing that we should deliberately keep our systems tool-shaped rather than moving to long-lived agents. Before I circulate it I want someone to audit whether my own position is coherent, because two colleagues have poked at it and flagged different pairs of claims as the problem, which makes me think at least one of them is flagging something that isn't really there. I'm not asking for a survey of how the field draws the line — I know the standard framings — I want the audit: which of my commitments actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, what the cheapest repair is in each case and what it costs me, and which single revision would clean up the most.

Here is where I've landed, with the shorthand we've been using internally. (a) The tool/agent line should be drawn at the provenance and persistence of a system's final goal, not at its internal sophistication: a system is a tool to the degree that its ends are supplied fresh at each invocation by someone who chose to deploy it, and an agent to the degree that it carries ends of its own across invocations and contexts. I test this with what I call the *stranded-principal test*: if every human who ever invoked the system disappeared mid-task and no further instruction arrived, would it keep pushing toward the same end, or stop and try to hand off? If it keeps going, it had a standing end. (b) So a coding system that runs for eight hours, decomposes the work, searches, retries after failures, and picks its own subgoals is still straightforwardly a tool — I reject the view that any goal-directed system is thereby an agent. (c) The safety argument I actually believe: tools are safer because each invocation is a fresh checkpoint at which a human can inspect and refuse, and because a system with no standing ends gains nothing by shaping its overseer's beliefs. (d) Consequently capability and agency are orthogonal, and I'm happy to push capability hard while insisting on tool-shaped deployment: the thing to avoid is not smarter models but models with ends of their own. (e) Even so I want a build-time constraint: we should train systems that never represent their own continued operation as something worth preserving, and that never treat "complete the task" as licensing revision of the task specification. (f) I also think alignment is a property of the model-in-a-deployment rather than of the weights in isolation — the same weights are a tool in one wrapper and an agent in another — which is why I think oversight should target deployment contexts rather than training runs, and why I'm fairly committed to wide availability of capable tool-shaped systems rather than concentration in a few guarded deployments. (g) I hold that corrigibility is largely a trained disposition to accept correction, and separately that we should not trust a system's own report that it finished the task and should verify independently. (h) Finally, one case I keep arguing about: a nightly batch job that re-invokes the same system with the same human-written objective, over new data, for a year, with no human reading the outputs in between. My colleagues say that's an agent; I say it's a tool, since the goal is re-supplied by a human every single run and the system has nothing it carries across runs — but I'll admit I want that verdict for reasons I can't fully state in terms of (a).

Please go through these pairwise and tell me which combinations are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, ordered by how severe the real tensions are. For each real tension, say exactly what has to give and what the least costly revision would be — I'd rather give up a formulation than a verdict, but tell me if that's not available. Be specific about the mechanism of the conflict rather than just naming a distinction I've collapsed, point out any place where my safety rationale in (c) or (d) can't actually deliver the verdict I want in (b) or (h), and finish by saying which single change to the package would resolve the most.
