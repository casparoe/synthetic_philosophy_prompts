I'm a junior at a large public high school, and I'm writing to ask your advice about something I haven't been able to get straight on my own.

For roughly a year and a half I have done nearly all of my mathematics and physics with an AI assistant open beside me. It began as help with a single problem set and has become simply how I work. My grades rose sharply, and teachers who had previously thought me a middling student now describe me as one of the stronger students in the year. I have been invited to tutor two sophomores next term, and I have to give an answer by Friday.

What unsettles me is this. Last month I tried to rebuild from scratch a derivation I had "done" three weeks earlier, and I could not. Not rustiness — I could not see where to begin. Yet I remember doing it clearly: I read the assistant's step-by-step explanation, each step followed from the one before, I felt the particular click of getting it, and I moved on satisfied. That feeling now looks to me like evidence of nothing, or evidence only about the assistant's prose.

So I tested myself. When I finish a problem now I can usually write up a solution I am confident in, and I can explain it back — apparently in my own words — well enough to satisfy a teacher. What I cannot do is tell a wrong solution from a right one. I shuffled together pairs of solutions, one genuine and one with a subtle error planted in it, and I did barely better than chance at identifying the bad one. That third capacity — a nose for wrongness, the thing that lets you feel that something is off before you can say why — seems to be exactly the one I never exercise, because the assistant always hands me something that reads as correct, and so the only check I ever run is whether it reads the way understanding reads.

I have had two responses. My physics teacher thinks I am being precious: understanding, he says, is just a matter of what you can reliably produce; I can reliably produce correct solutions; and this is no different from using a calculator or a textbook, since nobody worries that the calculator has eroded anyone's grasp of arithmetic. My closest friend says something different: the assistant is now simply part of how I think, the thing that does the work is me-plus-the-tool, and worrying that I cannot do it alone is like worrying that I cannot do it without my notes.

Neither answer quite reaches what I want to know. First, is my teacher's test the right one — is reliably producing the answer really all there is to having a grip on something, or is the ability to smell a wrong answer a separate thing that can come apart from it, and if it can come apart, which of the two deserves the name? Second, assuming something in me has atrophied, was it ever really mine? I was mediocre at this before. Perhaps I never had much to erode, and the assistant did not take anything away so much as fill a space that was already empty. I cannot tell whether that thought should reassure me or worry me more.

Underneath both questions is the decision. If I take the tutoring job, I will be asked to judge whether those two sophomores have understood something, using a faculty I am no longer confident I possess, in a subject where they will have the same assistant I have. My brother is in ninth grade and starting the same habit, and he will very likely do what I do. And I could simply stop for a term and find out what is left — but that would cost me grades at precisely the wrong moment, since I am applying to a competitive summer program in the spring, and it may be that the only honest answer arrives too late to be of any use.

What would you do in my position, and which of these arguments do you think is mistaken?
