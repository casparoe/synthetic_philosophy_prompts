I keep changing my mind about stuff and I can't tell which of the changes are irrational and which are just fine, so I went thru my gut reactions on a bunch of cases and now I want the principle behind them, if there is one.

- January: I book a cheap non-refundable flight to a conference in June, knowing full well that in May I'll want to back out. May comes, I want to back out, I can't. Fine — I'm glad January-me tied my hands.
- Same shape, different feel: in January, feeling pious, I set up a standing order giving 10% of my income away forever. By June I resent it and want to cancel. Not fine — I think January-me overreached, and cancelling is the sane move rather than a failure of will.
- Sunday: I plan to run Monday at 7. Monday at 7 I don't feel like it and I skip. Not fine, that's just weakness. But —
- Every evening I eat whatever I feel like, even though a week earlier I'd have guessed differently. Totally fine, no failure at all.
- Three apartments, A, B, C. I prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A (C is nearer work, whatever, the cycle is real). Someone offers me a sequence of swaps at $5 a go, and taking each one leaves me back in A minus $15. Irrational, obviously. But now say the swaps come one at a time from three different people and I never see the shape of the whole thing: still irrational I think, though I'm much less sure.
- Same three swaps offered up front as one package: I'd decline. Offered one at a time: I'd take each. I don't feel bad about that difference and I can't explain it.
- I skip reading the dessert menu because I know that if I see the chocolate thing I'll want it and then be annoyed at myself for ordering it. Skipping feels fine here. But a researcher who declines to run a test because she's worried about what the result will make her do seems to me to be doing something wrong. These feel different and I don't know that they are.
- The one I go back and forth on: every Sunday I decide to be in bed by 11 all week, by Wednesday I've renegotiated to 1am, by Sunday I regret the lot. I can't tell whether the Sunday verdict has any authority or whether its just one more mood in the series.

The principle I keep trying out is: changing your mind is fine when it tracks something you newly learned or something that actually changed in the world, and not fine when it's only you that moved, in time or mood or perspective. But I think that either makes the apartment case trivially fine or doesn't really explain why it's bad, and I can't get it to separate the flight from the standing order. Can you do better: one or two candidate principles, stated precisely enough that I can work out what they say about a case I haven't listed, with the case that motivates each bit, and an honest account of which of my verdicts each one forces me to drop.
