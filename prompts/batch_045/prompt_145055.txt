My opening move. I build distributed systems for a living, so I basically think in terms of spec plus verification, and that's where your story stops making sense to me. Picture a superintelligent agent whose entire final goal is: compute the first 10^100 digits of pi and write them to a tape. It runs on dedicated air-gapped hardware with its own power supply, its code is formally verified against that spec, there are no other agents anywhere in the picture, and the environment has been engineered so nothing outside the box can make a difference to whether the tape gets written. Now I say: zero power-seeking, zero fuss about self-preservation, zero resistance to being switched of halfway through. There's no uncertainty left for it to be strategic about. So the convergent drives aren't facts about intelligence or about rationality at all — they're artifacts of underspecified goals in messy, competitive environments where agents can interfere with each other. They're a claim about our world and our systems, not about minds. If you want to keep them, present them as an empirical claim about what happens when you deploy optimizers in the wild, and stop acting like they fall out of the concept of a goal.
