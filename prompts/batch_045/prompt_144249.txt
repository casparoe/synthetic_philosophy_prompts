Hey — I'm in a seminar right now (third-year, joint ethics/epistemology thing) and there's something I keep doing that I don't have a word for, and I don't think it's quite the thing we spent the first two weeks on. We went round and round on whether you can get a moral view just from someone telling you — the case everyone uses is you ask a friend whether eating meat is wrong, they say yes, now you believe it, and there's something fishy-feeling about that and everyone had a different story about why. But that's not my case. My case is that I go to someone about something I'm genuinely stuck on and I really don't want them to tell me what to do. What I want them to tell me is which bits of the situation matter. Concretely: my roommate borrowed my bike without asking and it came back dented. I didn't want anyone to tell me whether I was allowed to be annoyed or whether I should say something. What I wanted to know was whether it counts for anything that she'd probably have assumed I'd say yes, or that the dent was small, or that she's done it before and I never complained — like, are those the load-bearing facts here or not? Once a friend told me which of those to actually look at, I could see the answer myself, it stuck, and I could still argue for it a week later. When someone just hands me the verdict, I believe it for about a day, then it evaporates, I can't defend it, and I'd be embarassed to repeat it at dinner as my own view.

It also happens with labels rather than facts. A friend was getting needled by her lab group and thought it was just banter; I said something like that's not banter, that's hazing, and the whole shape of what she should do changed, and it didn't feel like she'd taken my word for anything at all. And sometimes what I get given isn't features or a label but a move — ask yourself how you'd feel if you found out from someone else — and that does more work than any verdict would have. A couple of us argued about this after class and couldn't get clear on what's even being transferred.

So: is there a name for this? Specifically for taking someone's word about which considerations are relevant, or about how to describe the situation, as opposed to taking their word about what to do. And more importantly, is it actually a different phenomenon or the same one in disguise — because whether intent matters here looks to me like a moral question too, so why doesn't deferring on that feel like the fishy case, and does the answer depend on whether I also defer on the verdict? What are the nearest established concepts and how does mine differ from each (I've heard terms floating around — moral perception, moral expertise, thick concepts, that kind of neighbourhood — but I can't tell which if any is the right one, or whether it's just a version of the thing we did in week one). And where's the edge: is it different if what I'm deferring on is a plain fact like whether he actually knew; is it different if I get the frame but still can't get myself to a verdict; is it different if the person I'm asking has no better judgment than me and just has more vocabulary? I'm not after a label or a reading list, I want the carving — what exactly is being handed over in each case, and why only one of them feels borrowed.
