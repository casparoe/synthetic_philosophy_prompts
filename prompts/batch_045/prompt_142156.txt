Hi — I'm a PhD student in philosophy (I work mostly on moral responsibility) and I'm helping write the assessment for our MA module on responsibility and criminal justice this term. I need one essay question on Dennett vs Caruso on desert and punishment, and I can't use the obvious one — 'does the absence of libertarian free will undermine retributivism, discuss' — because every student has a stock answer ready and half of them definately wrote it already for another module last year.

So here's the rough apparatus I want the question built around. I've made up an empirical scenario to stop them just reciting the dialectic. Suppose we had a well-confirmed, fine-grained measure — call it the Uptake Index — of an agent's capacity, at the moment of acting, to recognise and respond to the moral reasons bearing on them. And suppose three things turn out to be true: (1) Uptake comes in degrees and varies within a single person across situations; it's not an on/off switch. (2) Being held responsible in the right way — blamed, praised, asked to justify yourself — raises your later Uptake; the practice partly constitutes the very capacity it responds to. (3) Uptake predicts reoffending, but only where offenders are subsequently addressed in the second person; in systems that process people purely as risks (risk scores, civil detention, no moral address), Uptake atrophies and reconviction goes up.

The question should make them do three things: (a) say whether any of (1)–(3) settles anything between Dennett and Caruso, or whether there's a further question the findings don't touch — and if so, say exactly what that further question is and what would have to be argued to settle it; (b) consider a jurisdiction that adopts Caruso's public-health / quarantine model but adds a compulsory 'address hearing', at which the offender is told in second-personal terms what they did and why it was wrong, and where the hearing has no effect whatsoever on the sentence. Is that arrangement stable? Would Dennett have reason to think desert is satisfied by address that changes nothing, or does desert require that the address make a difference to what happens? Would Caruso have reason to welcome it as mere communication with no hard treatment, or to object that it shapes the offender's motives for the sake of public safety and so uses them as a means? (c) The same jurisdiction keeps awarding state honours on explicitly desert-based grounds while abolishing desert-based punishment. Is that asymmetry defensible on either view?

What I'd like from you:

- The question itself, cleaned up and worded for a 90-minute unseen exam with an indicative limit of about 1,300 words. It should read like a real exam question rather than an invitation to survey the debate, and it should be impossible to answer well by summarising what Dennett and Caruso each think.
- A model answer of roughly 800–900 words.
- A marking scheme out of 20, built around five or six must-hit moves, with the marks for each and a one-line note on how students typically get each one wrong. I already know one of them: a lot of them will say 'Uptake is graded, therefore scepticism is refuted', and that should cost marks, because a sceptic can grant degrees of capacity and still ask whether anyone has the requisite control over having the capacity. Similarly, penalise anyone who reads Dennett as a retributivist who thinks suffering is intrinsically good, or who reads Caruso's quarantine model as unconstrained consequentialism that permits indefinite detention.

Two further constraints. Please don't build the scheme around citations or reading — I don't want them hunting for sources, and an answer that's right should get full marks without naming a single text. And in the model answer, be explicit about which claims are relatively settled among people who actually work on this and which are genuinely contested: I do not want a model answer that quietly presents one contested reading as the consensus, because they'll copy it and I'll spend the whole marking period reading the same essay. In particular there's real disagreement about how much of a realist about desert Dennett is, and about whether Caruso's official position can really do without desert-entailing forms of moral address — so where you're putting a contested interpretation on either of them, flag it in the answer, and say in the marking scheme that a student who spots the same juncture and argues the other way, properly, should get the marks.

British English throughout, please — in your answer as well as in the question and the model answer.
