hey — I'm taking a philosophy of language seminar this term (Austin, Searle, indirect speech acts, we just did the infelicity stuff: misfires where nothing happens at all vs. abuses where the act happens but hollowly, plus the bit where a performative uttered in a play or a joke isn't void so much as parasitic/etiolated) and I also happen to be the recording secretary for my uni's student housing co-op — about 300 members, board meets every Sunday night, minutes have to be posted within 48 hours, the chair signs off before they go up, and then the next meeting rubber-stamps them, which meant that by the time anyone catches a mistake its baked in. And the minutes get read by people who were never in the room: the property management company, the student activities office that controls our funding, members who were at work, next years board. Nobody warned me that minute-taking is basically applied speech act theory — I'm constantly deciding which utterances in that room were *acts* (a motion, a commitment, a resignation, an adjournment) and which were just noise, and whatever I write down is what the act ends up being for everyone downstream.

So could you turn the theory into an actual procedure I can run while writing minutes, preferably at midnight after a three hour meeting? I want an ordered checklist: the questions to ask about each candidate utterance, what counts as a good enough answer to each when I can't go back and ask anyone, the points where the procedure stops with "no act, don't record it as one", and the points where it hands the decision to a human because its a politics or values call rather than a conceptual one. I don't want the procedure to just say "uptake succeeded, act performed"; I want it to tell me which audience's uptake to weight, when the later reader wins over the room, and how to write the thing so the later reader gets it right. (2) *the insincerity asymmetry* — my read of Austin is that an insincere promise is still a promise (an abuse, not a misfire) while a promise by someone with no authority to make it is nothing at all, which implies my job is completely indifferent to whether anyone meant what they said, and the members are going to hate that.
