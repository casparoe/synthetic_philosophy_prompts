I'm trying to work out what I actually think a Dutch book argument establishes, going by my reactions case by case, and I want a principle that sorts them.

1. An agent who assigns 0.6 to P and 0.6 to not-P, and who therefore accepts, at her own fair prices, a pair of bets costing 1.20 and paying 1.00 however things turn out: irrational, full stop.
2. The same agent, except that she declines every bet at every stake: still irrational — the book is a symptom, not the disease.
3. An agent who accepts bets perfectly reasonable given her evidence and loses for sure only because the bookie already knows how things will turn out: not irrational.
4. An agent with cyclic preferences, money-pumped through them: irrational.
5. An agent whose choices across two decision problems violate independence in the Allais pattern: pumpable as well, but I won't call her irrational.
6. Three people, each coherent individually, whose separate commitments a bookie can stitch together into a sure loss for the trio: nobody is irrational.
7. An agent who plans to update by something other than conditionalizing, and whom a bookie who knows that plan can exploit across time: irrational, but this is the verdict I'm least confident about.

Is there one account of what these arguments show — of what has to hold for guaranteed loss to convict — that gets all seven, or do I have to give one of them up? Please also apply it to two cases I haven't settled: a system built out of a language model whose stated probabilities are jointly incoherent and exploitable in aggregate, and an agent with precise credences over A and over B but no credence whatever in A-and-B, whom a bookie exploits through the conjunction.

Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no closing summary: (1) the principle, stated precisely enough to apply to new cases; (2) how it separates the money-pumped agent from the Allais agent, or why it cannot; (3) its verdict on the incoherent model-based system; (4) its verdict on the agent with the gap at A-and-B; (5) which of my seven verdicts it cannot accommodate and what I should replace it with.
