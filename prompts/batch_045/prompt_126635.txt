hey — I run a small reading group, about twelve people, half of them ex-trading types who have actually sized positions with the Kelly rule and half philosophy / decision-theory people who'd never touch it in practice. We keep having the same argument and I want to settle it with an instrument instead of another round of shouting.

What I want is a decision tree, in plain text, that takes someone from here is a favourable repeated gamble, what fraction of my bankroll do I bet? to a named position about why. Constraints:

- no more than about a dozen leaves, and no path longer than four questions.
- every branch node has to be a question one of my group could answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to. Case-based where you can make it: concrete gambles and concrete bettors rather than do you think the long run matters?
- build the thing out of the logical space first — give me the branch questions and the answer-combinations they permit, then place the real positions into that space, and for any combination nobody occupies say briefly whether that's because it's incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored.
- ascii arrows / numbered nodes are fine, and please keep it out of literature-review shape: no reading lists, no citations, no go read X, most of us won't follow them up anyway.

The angle my group actually can't settle is this: is the Kelly rule a theorem about one bankroll growing over time, or is it just expected-utility maximization with a particular concave utility function? Those output the same number in the standard iid-repeated-bet setup, and I want the tree to prise them apart. So please build in, as branch nodes or as scored cases, at least:

1. ten thousand bettors all forced to run the same fraction f for a hundred rounds, after which their combined wealth buys exactly one indivisible thing (a hospital, a lab, whatever). Maximizing expected total wealth says something wildly different from maximizing the typical outcome, and I want that collision doing real work in the tree.
2. the bettor who is spending a fixed slice of the bankroll every round, forever, instead of compounding toward one terminal payout.
3. the same gamble where your edge is estimated off forty data points and might just be noise.
4. a hard finite horizon — thirty rounds, then the money is needed for a house deposit.

Leaves I need on the tree what ever else you fit in: full Kelly; fractional Kelly, split into the versions that arrive there for different reasons (risk aversion, model uncertainty, consumption); the log-utility-expected-utility view as its own leaf even though it agrees with full Kelly in the boring case; the bet-everything or bet-near-max view of someone who insists the ensemble average is what's real; the refuse-to-bet-on-ambiguity leaf; and something for the I only care about beating a benchmark or a rival person, if that's a coherent place to stop.

Flag explicitly any two leaves your tree can't separate — I'd rather see the admission then a forced split. My suspicion is the growth-rate leaf and the log-utility leaf stay glued together for longer than I'd like, and I want to know precisely what kind of case would prise them apart, or whether nothing will.

And the meta bit: on each branch, mark how confident you are that the question really discriminates the way I want, and for the two or three you're least sure about, say what would change your mind — what argument, result, or counterexample would make you redraw that branch, merge two leaves, or move a position to a different part of the tree. If one of my four required cases is a dud, or secretly presupposes the answer it's supposed to test, say that too.
