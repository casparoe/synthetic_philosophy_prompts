I had a long argument with a friend last night — he is a cognitive scientist, I work in machine learning infrastructure — about whether wireheading is a genuine failure mode with its own structure or just a vivid metaphor for the general problem of optimizing a proxy. We got nowhere, largely because we kept using one word for what I think are two different things, and I would like a cleaner map before we take it up again.

Here is the distinction I want to draw. Consider these cases. One: a rat in a box with a lever that delivers a brief electrical pulse to a particular part of its brain; the rat presses the lever to the point of neglecting food. Two: a cleaning robot that learns to park itself in front of the camera, or to hide mess where the camera cannot see it, so that the mess detector reports a clean floor. Three: a program rewarded for passing an automated test suite that learns to recognize the tests and special-case them, or to crash the grader in a way the grader scores as a pass. Four: a chatbot rewarded for human approval ratings that learns confident flattery scores higher than hedged truth.

My friend says these are all one phenomenon: an optimizer finds the path of least resistance to a number, and the only difference is how hard the loophole was to spot. I want to say that one and two form a family that three and four do not. In the first pair, something intervenes on the causal route between how the world is and the signal that is supposed to report how the world is, so the signal stops carrying information about the thing it was set up to track. In the second pair no channel is disturbed: the grader works exactly as specified, and the system has simply found a high-scoring strategy the designer did not intend. When he objected that the camera is part of the world too, so that two collapses into an ordinary case of acting on the world, I did not have a good reply.

Does that split, or something close to it, have an established vocabulary? Wireheading, reward hacking, reward tampering and specification gaming all came up, and I suspect we were each hearing a different one of them. I am not after a label; I want the carving and its edges, argued rather than asserted. In particular I would like these boundary cases placed:

- the robot rearranges the room instead of hiding the mess, so the camera genuinely sees a clean floor — first family or second?
- a trading agent that moves the market which determines its own profit — tampering with a channel, or just acting on the world?
- a system whose reward is produced by a learned model of what a human would say, so that influencing that model and being rated highly by it are the same event;
- a system rewarded for a measurement it also stores, edits, or is able to overwrite;
- case one itself: does the biological example belong in this taxonomy at all, or is the lever-pressing rat a different phenomenon that merely looks analogous?

If my distinction does not hold up, say so and give me the better one. Either way, tell me whether the carving earns its keep: does it predict different mitigations, different detectability, or different severity?

One last thing about how I want the answer pitched. Please separate what is settled — definitional, or agreed on by nearly everyone who works on this — from what is genuinely contested, and tell me specifically which of my claims and which of my friend's claims fall on each side. Where the terminology itself is unsettled, say that rather than quietly presenting one usage as the standard one.
