hey — I need one more piece for a little philosophy-of-physics zine I put together for a reading group, nothing fancy.

Write me a memo, 600-700 words, in the voice of a university Office of the Registrar, addressed to faculty and students, announcing that the university has now accepted the conclusion of Godel's argument from the rotating-universe solution and is reorganising its administrative life accordingly.

For the background you'll need: his rotating solution to the field equations (the whole universe turning, distant matter swirling with respect to local inertial frames, a cosmological constant doing some of the work) contains closed timelike curves, so that in principle you could set out on a long enough trip and arrive back before you left. The further step he takes is the interesting one: the bare existence of such a model — never mind that the universe we actually live in does not appear to be rotating — is supposed to show that temporal passage, or objective succession, isn't real, leaving the picture on which all events are equally real and 'earlier' and 'later' are just a four-dimensional ordering. Do not attribute any actual sentence to him; paraphrase the reasoning rather than quoting it.

What I want is the administration taking that conclusion literally, consistently, and completely calmly. The academic calendar, the tenure clock, the 'five business days' rule, the deadline for filing grade appeals, the statute of limitations on academic-misconduct cases, and the date at the top of the memo itself all have to be redrafted in a way that respects the conclusion. The reductio is that a registrar is trying to tell you when something is due, and it should end on one such instruction that only makes sense if time passes.

Constraints:

- deadpan throughout; no puns about time flying, no pop-culture time-travel references at all, no exclamation marks, no 'of course in practice we still need deadlines' escape hatch — the joke is that they don't take one
- one paragraph on its own has to raise the obvious objection: that model isn't our universe. Observationally the cosmos isn't rotating as a whole, and anyway nobody thinks the stuff around here is swirling. The memo's reply has to be the actual move people make in this dialectic — something about what the space of solutions to the equations tells you about what the theory says time is, and about whether 'physically possible' can quietly be gerrymandered to exclude the inconvenient model — not a hand-wave
- somewhere the fork has to appear explicitly: go modus ponens (the solution is admissible, so give up passage) or modus tollens (keep passage, and rule the solution out as unphysical). Have the Registrar explain, in budget terms, why the university chose the horn it chose
- invent one piece of institutional furniture — a committee, an office, a compliance form — and let it do real work in the memo
- fair enough that someone who knows the literature laughs rather than winces, but still readable for the rest of my group, whose mostly grad students in other departments
