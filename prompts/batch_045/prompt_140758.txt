His verdicts as I remember them. One: the two arrows with the fins at the ends — knowing the lines are the same length doesn't stop one looking longer, so not penetration. Two: white wine dyed red gets described with red-fruit words — not penetration, that's the description changing, not the experience (he kept saying you'd have to show him the experience changed, not the words). Three: a reddish-orange heart shape gets matched as redder once you recognise it as a red heart — also not penetration, he thought the effects were tiny and the methods were a mess and it was probably response bias in the matching task. Four: a hill looks steeper when you're wearing a heavy pack — not penetration, and not even the right kind of case: a tired back isn't a thought, and anyway it's the system that guides your legs. Seven: the thing where you see a mouth say one syllable and hear another and end up hearing a third — no, that's two senses meeting, not thought. Eight: putting people in a sad mood and colours looking drabber — he waffled, said something like if a mood is a cognitive state then maybe, I really don't know.
