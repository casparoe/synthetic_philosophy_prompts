i'm 17 and doing my EPQ on consciousness, and I made up this argument for philosophy club that I think breaks the knowledge argument — except it might also break itself and I cant find where.

(1) Mary knows every physical fact about colour vision, and she's a perfect reasoner, so if anything followed from those facts she'd already know it. She leaves the room, sees red, and learns something new. So there's a fact she didn't know before, it isn't a physical fact, and physicalism is false.

(2) Now run it again with a twist I invented: while she's still in the room, give her the Phenomenal Encyclopedia — a complete list of every fact about what experiences are like, written using exactly the concepts she's going to have after release, including "swatch A looks like THIS" and "swatch B looks like THAT". She memorises all of it and still has never seen red.

(3) When she comes out she still seems to learn something — same surprise, same "oh, that's what red is like". So by the reasoning in (1) there's a fact she didn't know, even though she knew all the physical facts *and* all the phenomenal facts.

(4) So knowing what red is like isn't knowing a fact that any description could state, in any vocabulary, physical or phenomenal — the Encyclopedia already stated all of those.

(5) But if it can't appear in a complete description of reality, it isn't a fact about reality at all. Facts are the sort of thing a full description could in principle include.

(6) So Mary doesn't learn a new fact on release; she just gains an ability, or a new concept, or something that isn't knowledge-that. But then step (1) fails too, and the knowledge argument never gets off the ground.

My friend says (2) and (3) are just step (1) one level up, so if I keep (1) I have to swallow that nobody can ever say anything true about what their experiences are like — which is mad, because the whole argument depends on Mary telling us she learned something. I'd rather keep (1) than (6), but I want to know what I'm paying and which step to give up. My teacher muttered something about it being an ability rather than a fact, and something about "phenomenal concepts", but I couldn't work out which step those were meant to block.
