My protagonist, a fictional Hobbesian named Adrian Vance, defends a thesis he calls the Geometric Parity Thesis. Hobbes treats geometry as the model of a demonstrative science because the objects geometers study—lines, figures, and the motions that generate them—are drawn or described by us, whereas natural philosophy cannot be fully demonstrative because we do not make natural bodies and must instead conjecture their hidden causes. On the same basis, he argues that civil philosophy is demonstrable because the commonwealth is something we make ourselves. For the sake of my novel I stipulate that Hobbes also accepts a kinematic requirement: to make an object in the sense relevant to demonstration is to generate it by a continuous motion that the maker could in principle perform and describe, as a point moved generates a line.
