I build internal forecasting tools at work and have no formal training in philosophy or history. In a recent meeting about how much longer the current dominant great power is likely to keep its lead, one teammate proposed forecasting from a reference class: assemble a dozen past hegemons by hand, take the median length of their primacy as a prior, then adjust for specifics. Someone objected that every one of those cases was different, and my teammate ended the exchange with history doesn't repeat itself, but it often rhymes — which the room treated as settling it. I don't think it settles anything, and I can't say what it commits us to.

Treat that sentence as the object of analysis rather than the debate around it. Lay out, as a menu, the distinct precise theses it could be expressing — from something trivially true about no two events being identical, through claims about structural isomorphism or shared causal mechanism, through a methodological rule about when an outside view should override an inside view, to a claim about how much a small, hand-picked sample can support — and give each reading a verdict: platitude, substantive and defensible, contentious, or false. Then say, per reading, whether it would actually license the specific move my teammate wanted, namely using the median of a dozen hand-chosen cases as a prior.

Two things I want built in, because they are where I expect hand-waving. First, stress-test a rule of thumb I invented and have been pushing on the team: a candidate reference class counts as rhyming only if you can state the mechanism its members share in one sentence that does not mention the outcome being forecast — the idea being that this blocks classes held together only by the very thing you are predicting. Say which of your readings this rule is a version of, and what it would wrongly exclude. Second, separate the reading on which rhymes means similar enough to be informative from the reading on which it means different enough that you must still adjust, and tell me whether the slogan can honestly carry both at once or whether leaning on it that way is just having it both ways. A numbered list of readings with verdicts and a does-it-license-the-move column, phrased so I can paste it into the meeting doc, is what I'm after.
