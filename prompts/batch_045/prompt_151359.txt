hey — we're closing out a small reading-group thing on interpretability plus philosophy of science, half the room wants to talk about circuits and half wants to talk about mechanisms, and we've spent three sessions going in a circle about what it even means for two runs to be "the same mechanism". i'd rather end the last session with a poem than another go-round, so:

write me a sestina about it. six stanzas of six lines, then the three-line envoy, six end-words rotating by the usual rule (the last end-word of one stanza opens the next, and so on round), and each end-word has to shade into a slightly different sense every time it comes back.

the setup it should be about is mechanistic anomaly detection. you've got a model, a reference pile of "normal" runs, and a detector whose job is to flag the inputs on which the model is doing the task by a different route than usual — flagging route-change, not output-change. the case that matters is the one where the answer comes out right but the thing that got you there isn't the thing that normally gets you there. so the detector needs a notion of "same mechanism", and here's the trap i want the poem to actually walk into rather than gesture at:

- if same mechanism = same input-output map, that's too generous: a memorised lookup, or the same computation re-expressed in a different basis, counts as the same thing, and you can never flag the right-answer-wrong-route case at all.
- if same mechanism = same parts doing the same things in the same arrangement, that's too tight: permute the hidden units consistently all the way through the model, or re-express the same linear map in a rotated basis, and every component is different while nothing whatsoever has changed; and then a bit of weight drift on any run is a new mechanism, so "anomaly" stops picking anything out.
- the criterion that actually sounds right is the counterfactual one: same mechanism = same profile under interventions, patch or zero out a component and behaviour moves the same way. but that profile is defined over a distribution of inputs, and the case you built the detector for is exactly the input that wasn't drawn from it. you're asking the criterion for a verdict at the one point where it isn't defined.

plus the granularity thing: any "same mechanism" claim is same-at-a-level, and here the level is just whatever your probes can see.

what i want the form to do: let the six end-words be the parts. their rotation through the stanzas is the permutation — same words, new arrangement — and whether this is still the same poem is the same question as whether that's still the same mechanism. it's fine if the poem notices this about itself. stanzas 1-3 should take the strict criterion seriously on its own terms (the parts really have moved, something is different), 4-6 the permissive one (nothing has been added, the invariant is intact, so what exactly was supposed to have changed?), and the envoy — where all six words arrive at once and the rotation no longer dictates an order — is the off-distribution input: the one where a verdict is needed and none of the three criteria will supply it. leave it open there. i don't want it resolved and i definately don't want a tidy moral at the end.

don't lecture about the scheme, no epigraph, no notes afterwards, and don't let it become a list of the three criteria in verse. it should work as a poem for someone who's never heard "mechanism" used technically, and be right enough that the interpretability people in the room don't wince.
