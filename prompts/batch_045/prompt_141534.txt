The special composition question — under what conditions do some things compose a further thing? — has been argued about since van Inwagen posed it, and each standing answer carries costs that its own defenders concede: unrestricted composition floods the world with arbitrary fusions nobody ever refers to, nihilism gives up tables, the life-view looks arbitrary, and brutal composition gives up on explaining anything. As far as I can tell nobody converts anybody anymore, and the recent literature mostly restates the same costs in new notation.

My hypothesis is that the participants are not really disagreeing about composition but about what a metaphysical theory is answerable to: some treat ordinary and scientific object-talk as data to be vindicated, some treat ideological parsimony as a virtue that can override that data, and some take the real question to be explanatory — why creatures like us quantify over the things we do. On different assignments of what counts as data, the very same argument is a refutation for one side and a cost to be swallowed for another. A colleague of mine holds a version of this I cannot place: call it compositional quietism. There is, she says, no answer to the question, because there is no fact of the matter about when composition occurs, only better and worse things to quantify over for a purpose; but she insists this is not conventionalism, because no purpose whatsoever is served by quantifying over arbitrary scattered fusions, so the universalist's ontology is not merely idle but never even a candidate. Is that a genuinely distinct option, or one of the standard ones under a new description?

I want a diagnosis of the impasse, not a survey of the positions. Is it verbal; is it a clash about what counts as data and about which theoretical virtues are decisive; is a hidden question about vagueness, or about whether the quantifier is restricted, doing the real work; or is it several questions under one name? Say whether my hypothesis survives contact with what each side actually argues. And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, an empirical finding about how object representation works, a change of question — or should no progress be expected, and why?
