So I've ended up in a weird spot in the persistence debate and I'd like someone to try to knock me off it. I'm a perdurantist — things persist by having temporal parts, an object is a worm stretched out in time more or less the way a road is stretched out in space — but it's not the textbook version. Two bits are non-standard. First, I don't think the parts are instantaneous slices; I think the worm decomposes into what I've been calling "career-sized" chunks, the bits answerable to episodes or processes (the candle's burning, the cell's dividing, the friendship's slowly going bad), and my reason is that nothing instantaneous can be moving, or bending, or be in thermal equilibrium, so instantaneous stages can't be the bearers of the properties that made perdurantism attractive in the first place. Second, I don't think there's a single correct decomposition: which chunking you get depends on what you care about, and I'm fine with that, the way I'm fine with there being no single correct way to carve a coastline into bays.

My friend (an endurantist, and annoyingly good at this) says I've built myself a position that can't lose, and that's what I want stress-tested: what would actually count against thick-part, interest-relative perdurantism? Not only the standard anti-perdurantist greatest hits — though tell me which of those still bite given my version and which my version quietly deflects. I want the cases, the intuitions, and the arguments, including the ones I haven't thought of. Concrete things I keep circling: a process with no joints in it at all (a gas cloud dispersing, a ball whose velocity changes continuously) — is the absence of a privileged chunking a real problem for me, or just what I signed up for? A fission case with three successors rather than two, where it really does seem like a fact, not a matter of interest, whether there was one cell or three beforehand. And the thing that worries me most in private: if I say the candle is straight-at-t1 and bent-at-t2 *relative to* t1 and t2, haven't I relativized properties to times anyway, which is the textbook move — so are my chunks doing any work, or am I just a perdurantist with extra furniture? I'd also like the person-level version: whether chunk-perdurantism makes prudential concern and anticipation worse than slice-perdurantism does, or better.

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Tell me which ones a committed thick-part perdurantist should *concede* as counting against them versus which they're entitled to dismiss as question-begging, and what my view predicts that stage theory or endurantism doesn't. And be honest about whether I've qualified the thing into unfalsifiability — my friend thinks the interest-relativity move is exactly that — and if so whether that's actually a problem for a view of this kind or just how ontological disputes end up. The two things that would move me most: a case where every career-sized chunking gives the wrong verdict about a temporary intrinsic while the instantaneous version gets it right, and a clean argument that my chunks are either idle or privileged, with no stable middle. I can see both horns of that and can't tell which one to take.
