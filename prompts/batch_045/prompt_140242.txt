I do health-economic modelling for a public funder — mostly cost-effectiveness analysis: QALYs, DALYs, discount rates, equity weights, the reference-case rule that we only count health effects and ignore costs falling on other sectors. I'm fluent enough in all that; philosophy-wise I've got one undergrad course behind me and its fuzzy now.

I keep bumping into philosophers arguing about speciesism and I want the argument in the vocabulary I actually work in. Take three claims — equal consideration of interests, the argument from marginal cases, and the claim that species membership is morally irrelevant in roughly the way that race or sex is — and translate each as faithfully as you can into my machinery: whose health gains get to enter the calculation at all, what gets weighted and by how much, what an equity weight would even be doing, what work the societal perspective is doing.

Then the bit I actually care about: the residue report. Say which claims only translated with distortion, and which have no expression in CEA at all. My suspicion is that the moral-status question quietly collapses into just a question about which weights I'm allowed to enter, and that something gets lost in that collapse — but I can't say what. A live case that might anchor it: we're costing a zoonotic-disease control programme where one of the arms is culling wild badgers, and the harm to the badgers currently enters only as human willingness-to-pay for there being fewer badger deaths. Is that a bug in my framework, or is it the philosophers asking a question my framework has no way of asking?

Tempting as it is to just tell me what to think about animals, please don't — I want the translation and the residue, not the verdict. And write it in American English.
