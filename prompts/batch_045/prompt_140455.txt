I have to circulate a short position paper before a graduate seminar I'm teaching next term, stating where I stand in the philosophy of mind so that the students have something concrete to attack. Here are the commitments as I've drafted them. I know the package is broadly non-reductive physicalist and that none of it is original; what I want to know is whether it holds together.

1. Token physicalism: every instance of a mental state is a physical event, and the physical domain is causally closed.
2. Mental state types are functional types. To be in mental state M is to occupy some state or other that plays the causal role characteristic of M, specified by its typical sensory causes, its relations to the subject's other states, and its effects on behavior.
3. Type-identity theory is false, and the multiple-realizability argument is what refutes it: one and the same mental kind can be realized by physical states with nothing interesting in common, so no neural, chemical, or computational kind can be identified with it.
4. The openness is empirical, not merely modal. I expect that we will eventually build systems in non-biological hardware that have genuine mental states, and I think a sufficiently good whole-brain emulation of a person would have that person's mental life.
5. Externalism about content: Twin Earth cases are persuasive, and a subject's mental content is not fixed by their intrinsic physical state.
6. But not functionalism about consciousness. Absent-qualia and inverted-spectrum scenarios seem to me genuinely conceivable, and phenomenal character is not exhausted by functional role and may vary independently of it.
7. Moral patienthood: I treat functional organization as the operative criterion. I would extend the benefit of the doubt to a working emulation; I would withhold it from a system that merely simulates the pain role, producing all the same behavior with no corresponding internal causal organization; and I think it would be objectionably parochial to deny pain to a creature whose nociceptive system is chemically and structurally unlike ours, so long as it plays the role.
8. Methodologically, I want the roles read off our best empirical psychology rather than off folk platitudes, since platitudes are too sparse to single out a realizer — and I want to allow neuroscience to correct our mental-state ascriptions.

Please audit the set. Go through the commitments pairwise and tell me which pairs are in real tension and which only look that way; rank the real tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad; for each, say what specifically would have to be revised and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set; and say whether any single revision would clean up more than one. In particular, tell me whether any of the case verdicts in 4 and 7 is one the rest of the set cannot actually deliver, and if a standard repair exists, say what it costs rather than just naming it. Two worries I already have, which you should confirm or dissolve rather than take from me: that the multiple-realizability argument can be run against commitment 2 as easily as against type identity, and that the practical success of neuroscience — interventions on the realizer are what change what a creature feels and does — sits badly with mental kinds being multiply realizable. Please also say how confident you are in each diagnosis, and single out the claim in your answer you are least certain about.
