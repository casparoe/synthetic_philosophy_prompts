Posting here because I've been going in circles on this for weeks and I'd rather be told I'm confused than keep guessing.

Context: I got into forecasting a few years ago, the kind where you put a percentage on whether something will happen and a site scores you on it after. Since then I keep noticing the same shape of argument turning up in totally unrelated disputes, and I can't tell whether it's one argument in different clothes, or two arguments that look alike, or two plus a mistake.

Version one. Someone says: your numbers don't hang together, you'll say 40% that it rains tomorrow and 70% that it doesn't, and if so I can offer you two separate bets, each of which you'd have to call fair or better by your own lights, and you lose money whichever way the weather goes. I get the move. What I get stuck on is how much it needs: I have to take both bets, at the numbers I gave, from someone who somehow knows those numbers. I don't bet. And if a guy on a street corner offered me a pair of bets that was a sure thing for him, I'd of said no. So what is this supposed to establish, that I'm wrong about the weather, or that I'd be in trouble in a situation I'm not in?

Version two, which I think is a different animal. Whatever happens, there was a different set of numbers you could have given that would have scored better. Not probably better, better in every single outcome. No bets, no money, nobody exploiting anybody, nothing you actually lose. The bad thing is just that you did worse on a score than you could have done. This seems to do the same rhetorical work as version one and I can't say what the shared part is.

Version three, the one I really can't place. How much I'd pay for a dollar-if-it-rains depends on the size of the stake and on my rent: 60 cents at a dollar stake, 45 at a hundred, nothing at all at a year's rent. So there's no single number there to build anything out of. And when I try to rebuild the version-one construction with stake-sensitive prices, sometimes I can't make it work against someone whose opinions are visibly incoherent, and sometimes it looks like I can make it work against someone whose opinions are perfectly fine.

A friend said "Dutch book" once but she was half asleep and I think she only meant the first one. So: is there a name for the family, and if there is, does it cover all three? Please don't hand me a list of names and papers, I've got no journal access from where I am right now and I don't want reading suggestions. What I want is the carving: what each version actually establishes, and I mean which of these it is, that someone has a false belief, that they're internally inconsistent, that they can be reliably parted from their money, because those look like three different things; what the shared form is and what the minimum ingredients are for it to have any force at all; and where the edge is, the case where something that looks exactly like this has no force whatsoever. If the general thing has no name, then name it, define it, and tell me what wouldn't count as an instance.
