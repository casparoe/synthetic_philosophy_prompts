You are a professional philosopher who works in metaethics, with a specialism in the dispute between moral particularism and generalism. I want an audit, not a survey of the literature. Before you answer, do a short private pass in which you list every candidate tension you can see in the set below, including the ones you will end up rejecting, and only then rank the survivors. I am relying on you to discriminate rather than to flag everything as equally shaky.

I write novels, so my interest here is professional as well as personal. Roughly, this is where I have landed:

(1) Holism about reasons. No feature is a moral reason in every context in which it turns up; the very same feature can be a reason here, no reason at all there, and a reason against in a third case.

(2) There are no true, finitely statable, action-guiding moral principles. Moral judgment is a trained perceptual skill, closer to a good critic's sense of a passage than to the application of a rule, and it is acquired by apprenticeship to cases rather than by learning formulae.

(3) Realism of a undemanding kind: whether my protagonist acted wrongly is not settled by what anyone would judge, even under ideal conditions of attention, information and reflection.

(4) Supervenience: no moral difference without a non-moral difference. Two situations alike in every natural respect cannot differ in moral status.

(5) Moral understanding travels. A general sentence can carry knowledge into a case it was never drawn from — a child told that she is not to take what belongs to another can thereby come to know something about an apple she has never encountered — and one case can carry a verdict into another, since I am confident in advance that if Anna's betrayal is wrong, the parallel betrayal in the companion volume is wrong too.

(6) The moral content of a novel cannot be paraphrased without loss. What a reader takes from the cases is not extractable into a thesis, and yet, by (5), it is supposed to be usable in situations the book never depicts.

Please tell me which of these commitments genuinely pull against each other and which are merely apparently in conflict, and order the real tensions by severity rather than listing them side by side. For each real tension, say exactly what I would have to give up, what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set, and what the cheapest available repair would be. Then tell me which single revision, if I were willing to make only one, would clean up the most while damaging the view least. I do not know where the fault lies — if I did I would already have fixed it — so please do not assume I have a preferred candidate for revision, and do not tell me which one I secretly want to keep.
