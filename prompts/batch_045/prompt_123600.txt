I work in formal verification and spend most of my days inside a proof assistant, so the vocabulary I actually think in is this: propositions as types, negation defined as implication into the empty type, excluded middle as an axiom I must import rather than something I have for free, decidability as a hypothesis I discharge case by case, canonical versus non-canonical inhabitants, proof irrelevance, the split between the proof-irrelevant sort and the computationally relevant one and erasure at extraction, universe levels and impredicativity, definitional versus propositional equality, parametricity. I have no Sanskrit and no Tibetan, and my only acquaintance with Madhyamaka comes from secondary summaries I half remember, which is why I want a translation rather than a lesson. Please do not name or cite commentaries or secondary literature: I cannot check them, and a doxography would be useless to me.

Here is what I want restated in that vocabulary, term by term and claim by claim:

The catuṣkoṭi, the four-cornered schema, and its best-known application to origination: no existent thing is ever produced from itself, nor from something other than itself, nor from both together, nor from no cause at all, the last of these usually read as the fourth corner, neither of the two. Alongside it: svabhāva, the intrinsic nature a thing would have to possess in order to be what it is independently of its relations to anything else; śūnyatā, emptiness, as the denial that anything whatsoever has svabhāva; pratītyasamutpāda, dependent origination, as the seemingly positive counterpart of that denial; the two truths, conventional and ultimate; the claim that emptiness is itself empty; and Nāgārjuna's claim in the Vigrahavyāvartanī, the work in which he answers objections, that he advances no position of his own and therefore cannot be convicted of error.

Three deliverables, in this order. First, the translation itself, as faithful as you can make it; where my vocabulary forces a choice among readings of the original, say which reading you chose and what the choice costs. Second, a residue report: what comes across cleanly, what survives only after you have quietly supplied an assumption that is yours and not theirs, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Third, a verdict on the residue: for each item that failed to carry, is the failure a limitation of a type-theoretic vocabulary, or a sign that the original claim was doing less work than its grammar suggests?

Two hunches of my own that I want you to rule on, because I suspect one of them is a trap.

The pun first. Dependent origination and dependent types look like the same idea and I think they are not. A family of types indexed by a term of another type is one thing; the asymmetrical, usually causal or mereological dependence of an entity on its conditions is, it seems to me, another. Tell me whether anything of the Madhyamaka claim survives if I translate pratītyasamutpāda as dependent typing, or whether the pun is a false friend that would let me write down theses Nāgārjuna is refusing, and would therefore be a case where the target vocabulary smuggles in exactly what the source denies.

The second hunch matters more, and I want it tested rather than confirmed. In my setting the four corners are not on a par. The both corner, a term of P together with a function from P into the empty type, is trivially uninhabited. The neither corner, formalised as a function from P or not-P into the empty type, is refutable without excluded middle: from such a function I construct the negation of P by applying it to the left injection, and then feed the right injection straight back in. So two of the four corners are provably empty types, and the negations that reject them are free theorems carrying no content, while rejecting the first corner requires me to inhabit a function from P into the empty type, rejecting the second requires me to inhabit its negation, and I cannot do both without inconsistency. If that is right, then the tetralemma is not a four-valued semantics with a designated value, and perhaps not a semantics at all: the uniform surface of the four negations is doing rhetorical work, and what is really being expressed is a refusal to be committed to any of the four as assertible. Tell me where the formalisation is mine rather than theirs, in particular whether the fourth corner should be formalised as I have formalised it, and whether the distinction drawn in the tradition between a bare non-implicative denial and a negation that carries a positive commitment has any counterpart in a setting where negation is just implication into the empty type and decidability is a separate hypothesis.

Finally, emptiness of emptiness. If śūnyatā is itself empty and the withdrawal repeats at every level at which I try to state it, what is the nearest thing in my vocabulary: proof erasure, a proposition about all propositions having to live one universe up, a hierarchy with no fixed point, an impredicative collapse? Say which comes closest, say what each gets wrong, and tell me whether that regress has the same shape as the one my vocabulary handles by stratification, or is a different animal altogether.
