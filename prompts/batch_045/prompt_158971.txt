Hi — I'm helping a small funder place about four researcher-years of money on one narrow question, and I need help deciding which sub-questions inside it actually deserve those years. The question is whether "keep your impact low" / "don't cause side effects" can be made into something an AI system could really optimize without anybody first writing down a full specification of what's valuable and disvaluable about the world. The argument I keep running into — a friend who works on RL safety makes a version of it every time we talk — is that it can't: either the impact measure is so strict that the agent does nothing useful, or the thing that stops it doing nothing is a value function smuggled in through the baseline, and once you've smuggled that in you've given up on the whole point of the exercise. I find that argument persuasive-sounding but I'm not sure it's the *load-bearing* one, and I definitely don't want to fund four years of people re-deriving it in slightly different notation.

Here's the shared example I want us to use, so we're talking about the same thing: a general-purpose assistant agent is told "get me coffee," in a house where the fastest route to the kitchen involves squeezing past a shelf with a vase on it, and where it has the option of (a) squeezing past and risking the vase, (b) taking a longer route, (c) interrupting the human to ask, (d) reorganising the kitchen so coffee is always available, or (e) deliberately keeping itself slow and weak so that it can never restructure anything much before a human notices. Broadly the moves on the table for defining impact without a value spec seem to be: measure deviation not from "agent does nothing" but from what a competent, well-intentioned human with the same goal would have done in the same house (a trustee baseline); weight changes by how expensive they are to undo and by how long the window for undoing them stays open, so that acting slowly and reversibly is low-impact almost by construction; count only changes to parts of the world that have a stakeholder — some kind of "who could this matter to" scan, rather than a ranking over world-states; and require the measure to be legible to a third party (an auditor, a regulator) who can't run the agent in the real world to check it. Each of those seems to relocate the hard part rather than dissolve it, but maybe not equally.
