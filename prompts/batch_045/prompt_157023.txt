I came to the raven paradox expecting a puzzle with an answer, and found instead what looks like a stalemate: the case for saying that a green apple does, in its small way, support the generalisation that all ravens are black strikes me as formally watertight, and the conviction that this is absurd strikes me as just as solid, and as far as I can tell neither side has ever converted the other. I would like a diagnosis, not a survey. My own hypothesis, offered only as a hypothesis, is that one word is doing two jobs, and I have invented names for them: corroboration — the observation nudges the probability of the hypothesis up — and testing — the observation comes from a range of things among which the hypothesis could have turned out false, so that a non-black raven would be a test and a green apple would not. On that reading the paradox dissolves rather than being solved, which makes me suspect I have only relabelled it. So is this a verbal dispute about the word 'confirms', a real disagreement about what evidence is, a disagreement about which cases count as the data — whether anyone would ever learn about ravens by examining non-black things — or a normative question about how a hypothesis ought to be investigated travelling under a formal banner? Commit to one diagnosis, defend it against the others by way of what each side actually maintains, and tell me what would count as progress on it — or argue that none should be expected. Where your diagnosis is most exposed, say so plainly: I would rather have a committed answer with its weak points marked than a balanced menu.
