I'm working on something about the Orwellian vs. Stalinesque distinction — you know the setup: red dot in one spot, then a green dot a few degrees away a beat later, and people report a single dot sliding across that changes color midway, which makes it look like they see the change before the green has actually been shown. Orwellian reading: there was an experience of a red dot moving, and then the record got rewritten. Stalinesque reading: the system sat on the incoming data, staged the whole thing, and there was never an unedited version that anyone experienced.

The usual move at this point is that perfect revision is indistinguishable from no revision, so there's no fact of the matter about which happened. I want an example where that answer stops being satisfying — not because some clever experiment could tell the two apart, but because the distinction itself stops fitting the case.

Here's the variant I keep messing around with. There's no two-flash sequence at all: a single dot drifts smoothly across the screen over about two seconds, and its color at each instant is a moving average over a 150 ms window centered on that instant, so what's on screen at t partly depends on what gets drawn 75 ms after t. Nothing is presented and then replaced; the entire trajectory is smoothed continuously, and there's no frame you could pick out as the original draft. On top of that, the subject's job is to press a button at the exact instant the dot looks green, and the motor system timing that press has an integration window of its own, on the same rough order.

Give me one concrete example of a conceptual issue this kind of case raises — the sort where the Orwellian/Stalinesque framing breaks down rather than just being underdetermined by the evidence. A few hundred words is plenty. Please don't just tell me there's no fact of the matter, and don't reach for "well, some brain scan could settle it" — I want the conceptual problem itself: something about what has to be true of a process for talk of revision, or of a specific time at which an experience occurs, to get any grip at all. And keep it readable for someone who knows the basic color phi argument but definately hasn;t read anything else about it.
