Been lurking on the rationalist blogs and forums for something like fifteen years — Singer's The Expanding Circle, Better Angels, the sequences, a lot of EA forum threads — so I've seen the expanding circle story a thousand times. What I havent seen anyone do is untie one specific knot in it, and that's what I want explained.

Set-up first. It seems to me there are two different things that both get described as the circle growing, and everyone talks as if they come as a package. I'll call the first one widening: the boundary moves, new kinds of beings get counted — outgroup humans, animals, future people, maybe digital minds, whatever comes next. The second I'll call thickening: the beings already inside get taken more seriously. Conceding that an enslaved person is a person is one achievement; also concluding that it's not okay to work him to death is a different one. Those look to me like they might not even be measured on the same scale, and the standard story slides between them constantly.

What I actually want explained is the conceptual status of something I see people gesture at all the time and never define: a kind of conservation law for moral concern. Total concern is treated as roughly fixed, so widening the circle just redistributes a scarce resource and thins out what used to go to whoever was already inside. You can see the gesture in the why care about shrimp when kids are starving move, in the way animal and global-health causes get treated as competing for one pool of do-gooding, in the worry that a person who cares about everything ends up caring intensely about nothing, and in the fact that learning about enormous numbers of distant sufferers reliably produces numbness rather than action.

My question isn't whether that's true as psychology. I want to know what kind of claim it even is. Three parts, roughly:

1. Can total concern deployed be defined at all? Concern for your sister and concern for a billion future people look like they'd have to be measured in different units, and I can't see what the common currency is supposed to be. Does that make the budget idea empty, or just hard to operationalize?
2. If there is a budget, does moral progress stop being a monotone improvement and become movement along a frontier, where widening gets bought at the price of thickening — so a bigger circle isn't automatically a better one, and someone has to say what the right trade is?
3. Does any of this bear on whether the circle story is a discovery story — there's a mind-independent fact about who counts and reason is gradually tracking it better, which is roughly Singer's own framing — or a construction story, where what changed is the membership rule itself and calling it progress needs some independent standard?

I have a feeling the budget talk is secretly an argument for the second reading, but I can't say why, and I suspect the feeling is partly confused.

Please commit. Tell me which reading you think is right, whether the conservation thing is an empirical hypothesis, a conceptual muddle, or a disguised debunking argument, and what would have to be true for a wider circle to count as moral progress rather than mere moral change. Don't hand me a balanced survey and leave me to pick — take a position, give your reasons, and mark clearly where you're guessing or where the question turns on something you're unsure about. American English, please.
