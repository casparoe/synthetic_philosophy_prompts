I have no academic background in philosophy and came to this question on my own, so please define any term you use that isn't in my setup below, and please don't send me to a reading list or to authorities — I'm after your own reasoning.

Here is the case. A physical process will produce either a small world, with about 10^12 observers in total (call this S), or a big one, with about 10^24 (call this B). Physics alone puts me at roughly 0.5 on each. I then learn one further thing: that I am one of the observers produced, with no information about which observer or where I sit in the sequence. One rule I've seen says I should stay at 0.5, on the grounds that I should treat myself as a random draw from whichever population actually exists, and I was guaranteed to exist either way. The other says I should go to essentially 1, on the grounds that my existing at all is 10^12 times more likely if B. The second rule is the one that lets a philosopher announce, from his armchair, that the size of the universe is settled.

The version I cannot stop chewing on is a variant where the two hypotheses differ in population only because of what the inhabitants believe. Suppose S and B are hypotheses about some physical parameter, and that the inhabitants of either world are deciding whether to build vast numbers of additional minds — enough to make the B-world total 10^24 and the S-world total 10^12. The policy is adopted if and only if the civilisation's credence in B passes a threshold. So the population figures that the second rule wants to divide are themselves partly an effect of the credence the rule recommends. If those endogenously created observers count in the ratio, then any group can raise its credence in any hypothesis at all by publicly committing to build 10^24 observers if and only if that hypothesis is true — manufacturing anthropic evidence for free, and, if the hypothesis is about something they can then act on, getting a self-fulfilling certainty. That seems to me unacceptable. What I can't tell is whether it is a reductio of the self-indication rule as usually stated, or only a sign that the rule needs a causal restriction which leaves it untouched in the ordinary case where the extra observers are out there independently of anyone's beliefs.

So here is what I want. Give me your credence, as a single number, in the claim that self-indication-style updating is the rationally required response for an agent whose reference class has been stipulated and whose sole new evidence is their own existence, in the ordinary case where the populations do not depend on anyone's credence. Decompose it.
