I have been reading Simone Weil's argument for abolishing political parties — the part where she says, roughly, that a party is a collective that exerts on the minds of its own members a pressure nobody has decided on and nobody is answerable for. The mechanism keeps turning up somewhere she does not seem to have had in view, and I want to know whether it has a name.

Two cases. (1) A volunteer tenants' association: officers rotate annually, nobody draws a salary, nobody has a career to build. It achieved the thing it was formed to achieve and did not dissolve; it produced further campaigns. Questioning a tactic now draws the coldness that used to be reserved for questioning the aim, while questioning the aim is treated as a private matter. Individual members' stated reasons for each new campaign track the group's current line, and when the line changes the reasons change with it, without any sense of reversal. (2) A small learned society whose positions drift toward whatever most sharply distinguishes it from a neighbouring society it agrees with on nearly everything: the shared theses drop out of its self-description, and the differentiating ones become the ones it is most certain of.

What I am after is narrower than "organizations drift from their purposes," and it is not, I think, the iron law of oligarchy, or goal displacement, or mission creep, or groupthink: there is no oligarchy, no one seems cynical, the mission is never formally revised, and deliberation can be vigorous, sincere and well-informed. Is there an established concept for a group whose doctrines come to be selected for their usefulness in holding the group together and marking it off from its nearest rivals — members' beliefs following the group rather than the reverse — and for the fact that no individual is the author of the result? If there is no such concept, name and define one. Either way I want the carving rather than the label: the mechanism; how it differs from each of the nearby established notions; whether Weil's heavier apparatus (a collective that generates positions no member individually holds and for which no one is responsible) is really needed, or whether a thinner story about membership sorting, the costs of exit, and the need to justify continued association suffices; and what shape of organization would be immune to it, so that the phenomenon has edges.
