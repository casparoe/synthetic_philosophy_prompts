Reading group in two weeks where I have to hand out my own view on perception so everyone else can attack it, and I'd rather find the holes myself first. Here's roughly the package:

1. Perception is cognitively penetrable — what you believe can change how things actually look to you, not just what you're willing to say about them. My three favourite cases are the face that starts to look angry once you've been told the guy is angry, the expert birdwatcher who sees a species difference that just isn't there for me, and the wine that tastes worse once you know it was cheap.

2. Only penetration by a belief about the very thing in front of you should count — call it object-level penetration. Generic stuff like being in a bad mood, or just moving your attention around, isn't penetration at all as far as I'm concerned.

3. But I want to keep that perceptual experience is a basic source of justification: it gives you immediate, non-inferential reasons, and that's what lets empirical inquiry get off the ground in the first place.

4. And I want perception to keep its job as a neutral arbiter — when two people disagree about a theory, they can appeal to what's observable and settle it.

5. I also hold that experiences aren't beliefs: they're not responsive to reasons, you can't revise them at will, and their content isn't propositionally structured the way a belief's is.

6. Methodologically I've been insisting that the only real evidence for penetration is a change in how things look to the subject — reaction times, accuracy, forced-choice reports, anything purely behavioural, proves nothing on its own.

Does this hang together? Some of it I'm confident about and some of it I definately shouldn't be, but I can't tell which. Go through the pairs and tell me which ones are genuinely in tension and how badly, which only look like they are, and for each real tension what the cheapest thing to give up is and what it costs me. Rank them by severity rather than flagging all of them equally, and if there's one revision that cleans up the most at once, say which.
