I write about science and ideas for a British paper, mostly consciousness and AI stuff these days, and there's a move I keep bumping into in arguments among the integrated-information crowd and their critics that I don't have a word for. It's starting to feel like the thing that actually decides these arguments, rather than anything in the theory itself.

Two examples, both from interviews in the last year or so. First: I put to a theorist the objection I'd read about, that by their own mathematics some huge but totally uniform grid of connected elements scores higher than a human brain, which is obviously daft. The answer was that I'd described the thing at the wrong zoom level — sure, at the level of the individual elements there's a lot going on, but at the level of what the whole thing does, there's nothing to speak of. Fine, I thought. Then a critic pointed at a brain region with vastly more neurons than the bits everyone agrees are conscious, and nobody thinks it's conscious; the theorist said, look at the wiring, the connections mostly run one way and are boxed into separate modules, so nothing is genuinely joined up at the scale that matters. And the critic said: you picked the wiring diagram, and the timescale, that gives you that verdict.

That's the pattern. In every case the re-description arrives after you already know which answer you want, and nobody ever says up front which level of description the theory is supposed to be read at — which elements count as parts, what timescale, which wiring diagram. Each individual re-description is perfectly defensible on its own, in the way that each exception bolted onto a bad theory is defensible on its own, so I don't think "unfalsifiable" is quite it. And it isn't quite the ordinary underdetermination-of-theory-by-data thing either, because here what's shifting isn't the data but the description of the object the theory is being applied to.

So: does this have a name? Is there an established concept for the problem, or for the debating move, or for both separately — and if the nearest thing is a technical term inside one theory, tell me what that term covers and how what I've described is wider or narrower than it. If it's really just an instance of something general to do with levels of description in complex systems, say which, and say where the fit breaks down. If there's nothing established, coin a term and define it properly, with a couple of boundary cases, including something that looks like this but isn't — I want to know when re-describing a system is the right move rather than a dodgy one. A bare label won't do me any good; I need to know what the thing is, what it isn't, and where its edges are, because I'm going to have to explain it to readers who've never heard of any of this.

One last thing, since I'll be quoting you or at least leaning on you: be clear about how confident you are. Tell me which parts are vocabulary you'd actually defend as established and which are your own coinage, and tell me what would change your mind — what argument or consideration would make you say "no, that's really just X", or "no, there's no such phenomenon".
