I'm putting together a short series of posts on why thought experiments persuade when they don't really argue for anything — the pejorative sense of "intuition pump," where a vignette makes some verdict feel obvious without supplying any premise that would survive being stated flat. The point I want to make is that "it's just vivid packaging doing the work" is not an adequate diagnosis of a bad pump. I've got the standard cases and I dont want any of them. Build me a new one, to these specs:

- Ordinary setting, one paragraph. No trolleys, violinists, Mary / Chinese room / teletransporters, Gettier cases or fake barns, and none of the cheap affect tricks (unsympathetic protagonist, gross-out detail). It has to work on readers who'd spot all of those.
- It should pump a definite verdict on one contested philosophical claim. Name the claim and the verdict up front, and pick a case where you think the pumped verdict is false, or at least where the pump gives it no real support.
- The hard part: I want a *cold* pump. It should still work after every bit of colour and affect is stripped out and it's restated as bare propositional content — give that stripped version and show it still pumps. So the thing doing the work can't be imagery, emotional valence, or level of detail.
- What should be doing the work instead is a framing choice about how the case carves up the space of possibilities: which contrast class it makes salient, which option it presents as the default, what it treats as the unit of agency or the relevant time-slice. Say which one you're using.
- Then the perturbation: hold every stated fact fixed, change only that framing choice, and the intuition should weaken or flip. If it doesn't, you haven't built a pump, you've built an argument — say so instead of faking it, and tell me which spec you had to drop.
- Then take the defender's objection seriously: that the framing isn't optional packaging but part of the case's content, so your two versions describe different situations and the flip is no surprise. Does that land here?

Two more things. Be honest about how confident you are that this would actually pump on a reader — you can't test it, so say what you'd need to check and where you're just guessing. And separate settled from contested in the background: flag which of your claims about framing effects and the sensitivity of intuitions are things people who work on this mostly accept, versus live disputes you'd be reporting as contested.
