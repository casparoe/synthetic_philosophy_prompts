I am writing a paper for a graduate seminar on modality, and I want a diagnosis rather than a survey, so let me say up front what I already know and what I want from you.

The dispute is the one between counterpart theory and transworld identity as accounts of de re modality. On the transworld picture, one and the same individual exists in many possible worlds, and a sentence like *this podium could have been made of ice* is true because that very object has the property of being made of ice at some world; identity across worlds is literal identity, and nothing is needed to "ground" it beyond the fact that we have stipulated which object across which worlds we are talking about. On the counterpart picture, individuals are world-bound, the sentence is true because some counterpart of this podium at some world is made of ice, and the counterpart relation is a matter of weighted similarity: many–one and one–many, context-sensitive, and in general neither symmetric nor transitive. Both sides, as I understand them, accept a broadly quantificational treatment of possibility and accept that ordinary modal discourse is the thing to be accounted for.

What I cannot see is why the debate has not moved in roughly sixty years. The canonical moves on each side are well known and none of them lands: the transworld side says that a claim about what *I* could have done cannot be satisfied by somebody else's doing it; the counterpart side presses the cost of literal transworld identity and presents its apparatus as at worst a notational variant; the reply is that transworld identification is a pseudo-problem because possible worlds are stipulated rather than discovered, so there is nothing to identify across. Then it restarts. I have three hypotheses and I want to know which, if any, is right, and why the wrong ones are wrong.

First, a separability hypothesis. Counterpart theory as usually presented bundles two theses: that individuals are world-bound, and that a counterpart relation rather than identity figures in the truth conditions of modal claims. The first is a substantive metaphysical thesis with real costs. The second looks, at least in the limit, like notation: require the counterpart relation to be an equivalence relation and you seem to have transworld identity back under another name; relax uniqueness, symmetry, or transitivity and you get a weaker modal logic and something genuinely different. So possibly the live question is a formal one in disguise — a question about which constraints on a counterpart relation recover which modal systems, and about whether there is any *ordinary* modal sentence, rather than a sentence of the formal theory, on which a constrained and an unconstrained counterpart semantics disagree. If there is one, the dispute is substantive. If there is not, I want to know whether its absence really establishes that the dispute is verbal, or whether it only establishes that the disagreement lies elsewhere.

Second, a disagreement about data. Perhaps the two sides differ about whether first-personal de re intuitions are data that a semantics must reproduce or mere reports that it must predict — reports whose speakers would not withdraw them once they accepted the counterpart-theoretic paraphrase.

Third, an ideological disagreement: perhaps the surface dispute is a proxy for the acceptability of concrete possible worlds, world-boundedness, and primitive haecceitistic facts, and no argument about the counterpart relation itself will ever touch it.

To keep the diagnosis from floating, please address the tie case directly: a world containing two exactly similar candidates for being this podium's counterpart, differing in no qualitative respect, with no haecceity or other tie-breaker available, where the podium's being possibly made of ice is intuitively true. Say what each framework delivers, and then say whether that case is genuinely diagnostic or only a replay of the original standoff.

Two further requests. Separate clearly, as you go, what is settled and uncontroversial among people who work on this from what is genuinely contested, from what is your own reconstruction or stipulation — flag any claim you are reconstructing rather than reporting, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. And please write in American English, in continuous prose with your own section headings where they help, at roughly 1,000 to 1,400 words.
