Define the target claim precisely as follows. Take the median human being alive in southwest Asia in 3000 BCE — call her D — and compare her actual life with the life that would have been led by whoever would have occupied D's social slot (say, the eldest daughter of a household in the same river valley) in a counterfactual world in which that valley's inhabitants had remained foragers. Use the same-slot rule: D herself need not exist in the counterfactual, and nobody whose existence depends on the adoption of agriculture gets counted; but everything downstream of the choice of subsistence mode — diet, workload, disease ecology, settlement size, the distribution of power — is in play. My credence that D's life went worse than her counterpart's is about 0.55. A friend puts it near 0.2, and I suspect we are not disagreeing about the same thing. Give me your own number, decomposed over: (a) the empirical claim that early agriculture brought a net decline along the dimensions that matter — nutritional and infectious stress visible in skeletal remains, workload, epidemic disease, exposure to organized violence, the position of women — net of whatever it gained in calorie reliability and buffering against scarcity; (b) how far the counterfactual itself is well defined, given that a forager valley in 3000 BCE would have had a different population density, a different settlement pattern, and arguably no such social slot as D's; (c) whether the comparison should be run in terms of welfare, or in terms of something less aggregative such as autonomy, vulnerability to coercion, or the extent of a life's options; (d) how much weight to give the ratchet, i.e. the fact that once a population's size depends on cultivation, reversion has become catastrophic, so that the initial adoption forecloses the alternatives it was compared with. State a rough probability and one line of justification for each term, say how they combine into your headline number, and then tell me which term you are least confident in, which single hypothetical finding — archaeological, demographic, or conceptual — would move the number most, and where you think the gap between 0.55 and 0.2 most likely lives.
