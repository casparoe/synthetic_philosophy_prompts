I work in machine-learning methodology and have no formal training in epistemology; my working vocabulary is held-out evaluation, leakage, calibration, self-training and pseudo-labelling, weak supervision, distribution shift, and resampling estimators such as Efron's bootstrap. I would like the bootstrapping problem for reliabilist theories of knowledge, and the disputed easy-knowledge verdict that goes with it, restated in those terms.

The position to translate is this: a reliability-based account seems to let a belief-forming source certify itself. The subject consults the source repeatedly, notes that its outputs agree with one another or that they have matched the world in the past, infers that the source is reliable, and thereby comes to know by its lights things she did not know before, at no apparent cost. Set beside it the standard replies: that the track-record inference is an ordinary ampliative induction like any other; that the real culprit is the generality problem, i.e. which process-type the reliability claim is about; and that the circularity involved is only rule-circularity and benign. And set beside those the internalist complaint that such an inference leaves the subject with nothing she could appeal to from her own point of view.

Please render each piece as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary, treating it as a translation rather than a chance to quietly import my framework's assumptions. Then give me the residue report: what maps cleanly, what survives only under distortion (naming the points where you had to cheat), and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. I especially want your verdict on the residue: does it point to something the epistemologists' debate is missing — an analogue, say, of the independence assumption that makes a bootstrap legitimate and that they never state — or does it point to something my vocabulary structurally cannot say, such as the gap between a source's being reliable and its being reasonable to trust?
