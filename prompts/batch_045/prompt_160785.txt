Theres a blog post (one of those big ideas explained type things, i definately cant remember which) about a philosopher named van Inwagen and something it calls the special composition question. Heres roughly what it says, typed out from memory:

Heres the question: when do a bunch of seperate things add up to a single thing? Bricks and mortar make a wall, but my left shoe and the Eiffel Tower dont make anything. Van Inwagens answer is that some parts make a whole only when the parts themselves are alive, when their activity adds up to a life. Nothing else is ever really one thing. So there are no tables, chairs, cars, clouds, planets or stars: there are only particles, arranged table-wise or chair-wise or planet-wise. Philosophers call this nihilism. It sounds drastic but van Inwagen says nothing you believe has to change, because sentences like there is a table in the kitchen are still perfectly true, theyre just a compact way of talking about those particles. One famous consequence is that a dead cat isnt a thing at all: at the moment the animal dies the object goes out of existence and all thats left is particles arranged corpse-wise. The only alternative anybody takes seriously is universalism, the view that any things at all make an object, including your shoe and the Eiffel Tower. Nobody actually believes that, so van Inwagen basically wins by default.
