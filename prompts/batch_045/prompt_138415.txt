In our little reading group we finally got to the quining-qualia paper — you know the one, it opens with that old Chase and Sanborn coffee ad — and the entire session collapsed into the same one-liner being thrown back and forth: qualia are ineffable. The guy running the group used it to do the whole quining move: if you can't say what property you're positing, there's nothing there to posit, so the term should be retired; he kept pairing it with that list of four properties people standardly ascribe to qualia (intrinsic, ineffable, private, directly apprehensible by consciousness) and saying they're fine one at a time but can't be had all together. Someone in my cohort used the exact same sentence to mean the opposite: there's a character to experience that no physical or functional description captures, so it's real, and it's the thing the sciences leave out. I tried splitting it into "nobody can communicate it to you" versus "nobody can describe it in principle" and got told those are the same claim, which I'm pretty sure is wrong but I couldn't say why. Its been bugging me all week, and I'd also like to know why ineffability is supposed to be in special tension with being directly apprehensible — to me those two sound like they go together, so if there's a contradiction I want it pointed at precisely.

So: what are the precise things that slogan could be claiming? I want a menu, not a verdict on the whole debate. Give me exactly five numbered items, no intro and no conclusion, one or two sentences each, ordered from the most trivial reading to the most radical, and for each one say whether it's a platitude, substantive and defensible, or actually self-undermining, plus which of the two uses above it would support (or neither). Please keep separate the ideas of not being communicable to someone who's never had the experience, not being pickable out by any description at all even for the person having it, and not being capturable in the vocabulary of the functional or physical sciences, and say for each reading whether it's a claim about our language and cognitive limits or about the property itself.
