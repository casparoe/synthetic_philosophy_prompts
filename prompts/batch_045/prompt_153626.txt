I'm 71, spent my career as an experimental physicist — mostly signal processing and measurement error, so I've spent a large chunk of my life squinting at ROC curves — and since retiring I've been going to a philosophy reading group at the U3A and generally getting interested in this stuff. I also sit on a volunteer scrutiny panel for our police force and the council, who jointly run a "harm risk" scoring tool that decides which domestic-abuse cases get a specialist visit. We recieve about 400 referrals a month and can do roughly 40 visits, so in practice the score is used to rank, not to set a cut-off: whoever is top of the list that month gets seen.

We've got ourselves into a proper tangle, and here's my understanding of the relevant result. If the two groups we care about (we've been asked to look at ethnicity, and separately at whether the referral came in through the police or through a GP) have different base rates of the outcome, then you cannot have all of these at once: the score meaning the same thing in each group (calibration, I think it's called), the same false positive rate across groups, and the same false negative rate across groups. Something has to give.

Four wrinkles that I don't think the clean versions of this cover, and I want to know if any of them change the picture:

1. We only observe the outcome for cases we didn't visit. If we intervene we never find out what would of happened, so the "positive class" is only defined over the untreated cases — and those are the low scorers by construction.
2. Because the score ranks against a fixed budget, there isn't really a threshold at all. We take the top 40 whatever their scores are.
3. Intervening changes the thing being predicted, and next month's referrals partly come out of the neighbourhoods we visited this month.
4. The force have floated adding a "no recommendation" band: middling scores go to a human panel instead, and the vendor says the fairness statistics only need reporting over the cases the tool actually scored.

So: what are the most important implications of the impossibility results for us, given all that? Order them by how much they should actually change what we put in our report, and please tell me which of the criteria we keep arguing about are the ones we should just stop arguing about, and why. British English please, and don't bother with authors, papers or further reading — I've got no way to look any of it up and it won't help me write the thing.
