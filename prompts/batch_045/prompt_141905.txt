After a long argument with a friend last night about Jeffrey conditionalization I am still unsure who was right, and I would like you to settle the structural question rather than to survey the dialectic.

My friend's objection was that Jeffrey conditionalization is not really conditionalization at all: ordinary conditioning on two propositions commutes, whereas two successive Jeffrey updates on partitions E and F generally do not, and she took that order-dependence as a reductio. I wanted to reply that it is harmless, since an agent whose confidence in E is moved first and in F second has undergone something different from one whose confidence is moved in the reverse order — but I could not say why the difference is benign rather than fatal.

The case that broke us, which I would like you to use throughout, is one where both shifts come from a single indivisible episode: one field report that at once raises a hydrologist's confidence that a contaminant plume is shallow from 0.4 to 0.7 and that it is recent from 0.5 to 0.8, with no fact of the matter about which shift came first. Here the order looks like our artifact rather than the agent's, and my fallback — that a single update should impose rigidity with respect to both partitions at once — seemed, when I tried it, to collapse into the trivial no-change update.

Please explain what rigidity is doing structurally: in particular why it amounts to rescaling each cell of the evidence partition uniformly, and why that is a substantive claim about what the episode does and does not discriminate, rather than bookkeeping. Then tell me whether that collapse is real, and whether the order-dependence in the single-episode case is a defect of the rule, of the representation, or of nothing at all. Take a definite position and give your reasons, and mark clearly where you go beyond what the formalism settles.
