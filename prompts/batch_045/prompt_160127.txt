Our reading group is doing Republic book II next week, the bit where Glaucon hands over the ring of Gyges, and I've now watched three sessions in a row where everybody anwsers a different question than the one asked. So before we go further I want the spec, not the answers: write me the desiderata that any answer to "why be moral?" has to meet to count as adequate, each with the case or consideration behind it. No reading lists or citations please, we already have a syllabus. Some things I think belong on the list, but you sort them and add the ones I've missed: it has to be addressed to the Undisturbed Agent — fully informed, perfectly instrumentally rational, no ignorance, no weakness of will, no confusion — and so must not change the subject by diagnosing him as making some factual or reasoning error; it has to answer the justificatory question (is there a reason?) rather than quietly swap in the psychological one (what gets people to behave) or the genealogical one (how the practice arose); it has to survive the Cost Ladder, meaning a series of cases where doing the right thing costs you a fiver, then your job, then your family's safety, then your life, with no monument and no afterlife, and I want an explicit desideratum about whether the answer must be the same at every rung or is permitted to break somewhere, and if so where and why that isn't a defeat; it has to work in the Ledger case, where every deed is recorded in a ledger that is burned unread at your death, so reputation and self-deception can't do any work; it has to say something in the Reverse Ring case, where you are blamed and punished for good acts you didn't commit; and it has to have an answer for near-total non-compliance (one moral person among ten thousand, where being moral just makes you the mark) and for pure inefficacy (your individual act makes no difference at all to the outcome, as with the single drop in the reservoir).

Tell me which of those are genuine desiderata and which are just one camp's prejudice dressed up as a requirement — I suspect the character-and-integrity one and the "must not be neutral between first-order moral theories" one are both smuggled in, but say which you'd junk. Mark each non-negotiable or a preference of a particular kind of theory. Then flag the pair you think can't be jointly satisfied: my guesses are "don't presuppose that moral considerations are reason-giving" together with "explain why moral demands feel categorical and desire-independent", and "hold at every rung of the Cost Ladder" together with "leave room for genuine supererogation", but make your own case. Also say which single requirement does the most work in knocking out popular answers. Only after that, one or two lines each on self-interest/prudential answers, mutual-advantage or contract ones, constitutivist/Kantian ones, and character-based ones — I'll do the real scoring with the group. Under 800 words, and in British English.
