I'm on the benefits committe at work — there's five of us, at a company of about 70 people, and we're the ones who decide what our self-funded health plan actually pays for. The plan document is from like 1994 and says it covers treatment of disease or injury and doesn't cover cosmetic, lifestyle, or elective enhancement stuff. Years ago our broker told us to write ourselves a one sentence test for what counts as a disease, because otherwise we'd argue about it forever. The test we came up with, and have used ever since, is basically this: it's a medical problem if some part of you is working worse than it normally works in a healthy person of your age and sex, and it's causing you real trouble.

I always thought that was pretty reasonable. This month we had three appeals and now I think it's either wrong or I'm applying it wrong, and I keep flip-flopping between the two.

1) Marcus, 58, drives a forklift. His testosterone came back in the low-normal range for men his age. He says he's wiped out all the time, no sex drive, can't concentrate. His doctor put him on replacement therapy and he says he got his life back. We denied it: nothing in him is working worse than normal, he's just getting older. But his supervisor says Marcus is a different guy since the shots. His age is doing all the work in that sentence and I don't know why age gets to do that.

2) Priya, 34, in accounting. Her husband died in a crash seven weeks ago. She's sleeping three hours a night, lost a lot of weight, can't get through a spreadsheet, and she's asking for short term disability plus coverage for an intensive outpatient program. Our behavioral health vendor says this is normal grief, not a diagnosis, so the program isn't covered; the disability people say they can't approve leave without a diagnosed condition. She's obviously in real trouble and I can't point to anything in her that's malfunctioning.

3) Sam, 41, warehouse. Type 2 diabetes, heavy, drinks most nights, needs bariatric surgery. One of our members said flat out that this isn't a disease, it's a lifestyle, and we shouldn't pay for it. Same guy voted yes last month on knee surgery for someone who blew out his knee playing pickup basketball. When I pointed that out he said that's different, you can't help getting hurt playing ball. I have no idea whether that's a real distinction or if we're just punishing Sam.

On top of all this, Denise on the committee wants to change the test from a healthy person of your age and sex to someone like us, because most of the warehouse works night shift and half of us are carrying extra weight, and if we say healthy person then suddenly everybody's sick. I feel like that makes things worse, not better, but I can't say why.

So: is there a better one sentence test? Or is the whole idea of a one sentence test hopeless, and if it is, what do I actually say at the meeting on Thursday?
