I am a novelist, and I have for some time been unable to write a particular kind of scene. It is a crowd scene: people arrive one at a time in a square outside a house where something is wrongly believed to have happened; each arrives with private reservations; each reads the faces and postures of those already present rather than the facts; by the time the stone is thrown no one present individually wants to throw it, and no one can afterward be singled out as the one who decided. I keep rewriting it as a study in cowardice, and I have come to suspect that cowardice is the wrong explanation.

I have read some popular economics but have no facility with formal modelling, so I would like the standard sequential-observational-learning account of informational cascades restated in the vocabulary I actually work in: scene and beat, point of view, free indirect style, dramatic irony, what a character wants and what she believes, what she can see from where she stands, plausibility, the difference between protagonist and ensemble, the narrator's access, misdirection, the turn.

This is the version of the model I mean, so that the answer need not depend on recall: agents act in a fixed sequence, one at a time, each choosing between two actions; there is an unknown binary state of the world; each agent receives one private signal of known reliability; each observes all earlier actions but not the signals that produced them; each acts to maximize expected payoff given the public record plus her own signal; and the whole structure, including everyone's rationality, is common knowledge. In the simplest such setup the cascade locks in after two consecutive agents have acted alike, after which no further action conveys information; the cascade is wrong with a probability that stays bounded away from zero however long the sequence runs; and every agent in it can know all of this and still rationally conform. Please also keep this apart from the different case in which an agent uses her signal but acts against it because she wishes to appear well-informed, and note that in the model a locked-in cascade is not dislodged by a single contrary act, since by then a contrary act reveals nothing: it takes new public information.

Three things, in order. First, the translation, as faithful as you can make it without importing my vocabulary's assumptions and calling them translation. Second, the residue: what maps cleanly onto scene, point of view and motive; what maps only with distortion — does rationality become plausibility of motivation, and if they are not the same, what is lost? does the reader's position under free indirect style have any counterpart, given that the reader is handed exactly the signals the model treats as private? is a character's private doubt really a signal, when in fiction doubt is often expressive rather than evidential? Third, what cannot be said in my vocabulary at all, and whether that residue indicts fiction or the model.

Two intuitions of mine to test rather than flatter. I want a single truth-teller — the child in the story of the emperor with no clothes — to be able to break the thing, and the model seems to say that a lone deviation is uninformative; is my intuition sentimentality, or does it point at something the model has left out, such as unequal signal reliability, a character known to prefer breaking ranks, or the difference between speaking and acting? And I want to say that the crowd's cruelty is structural rather than a sum of individual failures of nerve: does the model vindicate that, or does it entail that there is nothing there but the sum?

Finally: what would a scene faithful to the model actually look like on the page, and would it be dramatically inert?
