**Enumeration and the inscrutability of reference: a sketch**

1. *The target.* Quine's radical translator arrives without interpreters and must construct a manual of translation from scratch, correlating native utterances with observable stimulation. Where a sentence is keyed directly to stimulation, translation is comparatively stable. For terms and for standing sentences there is no such anchor, and here the famous case arises: the utterance *gavagai*, elicited in the presence of rabbits, can be rendered by *rabbit*, or by *undetached rabbit part*, or by *rabbit stage*, or by the single universal *rabbithood*, and each of these renderings can be squared with any behavioral evidence whatever, provided compensating adjustments are made elsewhere in the manual — to the rendering of identity, of plurality, of the copula, of quantification, and of the numerals. The conclusion drawn is not the modest one that the linguist cannot discover which manual is correct. It is that there is nothing to be correct about: no fact of the matter distinguishes the manuals.

2. *The gap I want to exploit.* The argument is standardly presented as though the linguist's evidence came only in the form of assent and dissent to sentences occasioned by stimulation. But assent and dissent can also be elicited by a performance the linguist stages, and one such performance is counting. The linguist can learn to execute the community's counting routine — reciting the numeral list in order while directing a scanning gesture successively at the items of a presented collection — and can observe, while still translating nothing, at least the following: (a) a fixed and finite list of numeral words, always recited in the same order; (b) a one-to-one pairing between numeral-utterances and scanning acts; (c) correction behavior that distinguishes two different faults, skipping an item and scanning an item twice, together with completion behavior, the natives stopping and treating the numeral reached as the number of the collection; and (d) reuse of that numeral in subsequent transactions, for instance in apportioning the counted items. Call the requirement that a manual preserve the numeral paired with a collection, in every actual and possible counting episode, the enumeration constraint. It is not imported from outside the framework: pairing, correction, completion and reuse are as public as anything else in the linguist's evidence base.

3. *What the constraint does.* Applied to the textbook four-way list it is not neutral. First, *rabbithood* is excluded on the usual reading: if the term denoted a single universal, every collection would number one, yet the natives reach different numerals for different collections, and the difference tracks the scanning acts. To rescue it one would have to render the numeral words differently depending on which count noun is in play; but the numeral list is shared across count nouns, so the repair must be made in the sortals instead, and there it propagates: what began as a proposal about one term becomes a proposal about the whole sortal apparatus at once. Second, *undetached rabbit part* survives only globally. Locally — hold the rest of the manual fixed and swap the term — it fails: in an episode with three rabbits the natives reach three and behave as though they had exhausted the collection, while the undetached rabbit parts present are vastly more numerous; and their practice of correcting skips shows them to be sensitive to omissions of precisely the kind the local swap would require them to make en masse. What survives is the global construction, in which a one-to-one proxy function is applied across the entire language, so that the term comes out as a gerrymandered predicate true of exactly the images of rabbits, and every predicate, quantifier and numeral shifts in tandem. Such a manual is cardinality-preserving and therefore meets the constraint.

4. *The dilemma.* So the thesis has two forms, and they fare differently. Read locally, as the four-way illustration invites, indeterminacy is answered by evidence the linguist can actually gather. Read globally, it survives the constraint but changes shape: the alternatives are no longer rival translations of a term, or even of a language, but gerrymandered systematic reletterings of the entire referential apparatus. My claim is that at this point the remaining dispute is not about evidence at all, but about whether interpretation is answerable to an eligibility or naturalness constraint — whether a manual that assigns the community a predicate which no one could acquire, project, or pair with a scanning procedure is a candidate manual in the first place. If eligibility constraints are admissible, the global thesis is false. If they are not, it is true — but then it is true in virtue of a methodological prohibition rather than in virtue of the argument from the poverty of the evidence, which is the only argument offered. The scandal of *gavagai* would then never have been a discovery about reference. It would have been a consequence of refusing to count naturalness as a constraint on interpretation.
