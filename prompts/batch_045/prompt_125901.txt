I'm a staff engineer on the risk platform at a mid-sized payments company. I have no philosophy training — everything I know about this subject comes from blog posts and from arguments with colleagues. On Friday we are retiring Marlowe, the neural network that has scored our transaction-level fraud risk since 2019, and because my team has a foolish tradition, I have been asked to give a five-minute retirement toast to a model.

About twenty people will be in the room. Six or seven are ML and platform engineers who trained her, retrained her eleven times, and spent years arguing about what her internals actually are. Five are fraud analysts in operations who read her scores all day, every day, and who always talked about her as a colleague: she's being paranoid this week, she doesn't like this merchant, she's tired. The rest are product, compliance, and finance people who know her only as a number on a dashboard and who think the party is strange.

My problem is this. Every tribute sentence I would naturally write attributes representations to her: she learned to spot card-testing, she never trusted a first order from a new device, she understood our merchants better than the merchant database did. And a large part of the room has spent years not knowing whether internal states of a network have content at all, or whether that is a convenient way of talking that happens to predict behavior. I don't want a toast that only works if a contested claim is true, and I also don't want one that quietly takes back everything the operations analysts believed while they were doing their jobs.

Three things from her history that the toast has to be honest about:

In 2021 someone probing her activations found a direction that separated a class of merchant we had never labeled and had no column for. Once we found it we could clamp it and her behavior changed exactly as the label would predict, and it was still there, in the same place, after four years and eleven retrains. Operations named it after an analyst and wrote rules against it. It made them better at their jobs for two years.

In 2023 the direction we had been reading as high-risk geography turned out to be riding on a timezone encoding bug upstream. We fixed the bug; the direction moved; her accuracy barely changed. Whatever we had been attributing to her, she did not have.

Last year, a re-derivation of the feature basis by a different method, from a different seed, produced a set of directions that is equally predictive, equally responsive to intervention, and cuts straight across the old ones. It is not obvious to me that there is a fact of the matter about which set she really had.

One more detail that matters to me: Marlowe never explained herself. The system replacing her is much larger and emits two paragraphs of justification for every score, and nobody on my team believes a word of it.

Here is the half-baked test I have been using: a state in the network earns the right to be called a representation only if intervening on it changes behavior in the way its supposed content predicts, and that pattern survives a distribution shift rather than only holding on the data it was found on. I suspect this is too strong, because people fail it constantly — we mispredict what our own colleagues will do in a novel situation all the time, and I do not conclude from that that they have no beliefs. But maybe the symmetry fails, and if it does I would like to know why.

What I want from you:

One paragraph, written to be readable by someone who cannot follow references — no citations, no suggestions of things to read, no name-dropping — stating what view of representation in neural networks you are writing the toast from, why you think it is defensible, and whether my test is too strong.

Then the toast itself: five or six minutes, warm and specific rather than instructional, built to land for the compliance person who thinks the party is weird as well as for the engineers who will hear the philosophical commitment in almost any line I choose.

Then three or four short notes, for me and not for the room, on which lines are carrying the philosophical weight and what those lines would amount to if I have the view wrong.

The constraints I care about: do not flatten the 2021 case into just a direction in a vector space, do not treat the timezone bug as proof that nothing was ever represented, do not paper over the underdetermination, and do not condescend to the analysts. If you think the honest thing is a toast that declines to say some of the things a toast usually says, write that, and tell me what it says instead.
