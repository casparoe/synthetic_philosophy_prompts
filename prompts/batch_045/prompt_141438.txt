I'm a third-year computer science major, and two of my courses this term keep colliding in a way I can't sort out on my own. In my multi-agent systems course I'm comfortable with the standard machinery: agents and policies, protocols and mechanisms, Nash and correlated equilibrium, incentive compatibility and strategy-proofness, specification and verification — safety versus liveness properties, deadlock, vacuous satisfaction — and the gap between a behavioural strategy, which only says what an agent does, and a program or policy, which says what it is for. In my introductory moral philosophy course we've just spent three weeks on Kant: maxims, the Formula of Universal Law with its contrast between a contradiction in conception and a contradiction in will, the four worked examples of suicide, false promising, the cultivation of one's talents and beneficence, perfect versus imperfect duty, and the Formula of Humanity, with its claim that rational nature is an end in itself and may never be used merely as a means. My section TA keeps insisting that the universalizability test is not the playground slogan about what would happen if everybody did that, and I believe her, but I don't yet have a grip on what else it could be — which is why I want to try restating it in terms I actually control.

So here is the request. Translate, as faithfully as you can, first the Formula of Universal Law, including the distinction between its two kinds of failure, and second the Formula of Humanity, into the multi-agent-systems vocabulary I listed above. My own first pass is that the first formula requires the policy I act on to be one that could be run by every agent in a population — a symmetric equilibrium, or a fixed point under universal adoption — that a contradiction in conception is a specification that becomes unsatisfiable or deadlocks when universally instantiated, and that a contradiction in will is a case where the system runs fine but settles into a state no agent would have chosen to be in. The second formula I cannot even get started on: the nearest thing in my vocabulary is a constraint against treating another agent's capacities as resources to be optimized over without that agent's authorization, and that feels like it drops most of what is being claimed. Please also take on the maxim-individuation problem — the fact that a maxim can apparently be described coarsely or finely enough to pass or fail the test at will. That seems to me to have a real counterpart in specification gaming and in the difference between a policy and the program that generates it, and I want to know whether the parallel flatters Kant or exposes him.

Then the part I actually care about, the residue report. For each formula, tell me what translates cleanly, what translates only if distorted, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all — and where a translation is really a replacement, say so explicitly, especially if it smuggles in an assumption of my framework such as a fixed utility function per agent or a consequentialist way of counting outcomes. My specific suspicions are that the contradiction in will will only come out as something like Pareto-inferiority or ex ante dispreference, which looks like substitution rather than translation; that the categorical character of Kant's law, its holding regardless of what anyone else does, has no analogue in an equilibrium concept, which is always conditional on others' strategies; and that end in itself cannot be stated in a framework whose object language contains only preferences, allocations and incentives. For each item of residue, tell me whether it is a defect of my vocabulary — a distinction multi-agent system design has no use for and therefore no word for — or evidence that Kant's test is less mechanically determinate than my section presents it as being. And give me your overall verdict on how much of the original survives the trip.
