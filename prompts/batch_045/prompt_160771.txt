I work on the backend team at a small nonprofit that runs a volunteer-driver dispatch service — drivers take prescriptions and groceries to homebound people across a metro area — and we're about to put an ML planner (internally "Marlowe") in charge of assigning and reassigning drivers through the day. Which means the thing is going to be making little moral decisions constantly, and two of my teammates have been going at each other in design reviews for about a year now. Priya wants Marlowe to be, as she puts it, a utilitarian: give it a welfare-ish score over outcomes (deliveries completed, wait times, some rough penalty for health-relevant misses) and let promises, privacy and truth-telling enter as costs it can outweigh. Deniz wants hard deontological constraints: a set of hard-coded prohibitions the optimizer can't cross no matter how good the outcome, because the point of the promise is that it doesn't get traded. The fights are now unproductive and our offsite is in two weeks, so I want to run an actual adversarial collaboration and I need the document it would produce.
