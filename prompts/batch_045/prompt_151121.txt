My half-formed idea, which I cant tell is deep or just a relabelling: that one move is secretly doing two jobs with different epistemic status. It feels like the shape is robust: assume the right overall density of primes, plus some no-conspiracy condition (exchangeability? max entropy given the known constraints? invariance under a symmetry group?) and you get the shape back whatever the dependence looks like. The constant seems to depend on the fine structure of the dependence and only gets pinned once you feed in the local obstructions.

So here's what I want to propose: a way of measuring how much independence a given heuristic actually needs. Fix the class of dependence structures that reproduce everything we already know (right density, right residues mod small primes), state the target claim, and ask whether it holds for all of them, for a measure-one bunch, or only for the independence-ish ones. Then sort the standard conjectures into those buckets and see whether the shape/constant split lines up with the bucket split — or whether gap statistics and clustering land somewhere in the middle. Also: what stops an adversary from building a dependence structure that matches every fact we have and still breaks the prediction? If the answer is 'a complexity or symmetry condition', that condition is the real content of the heuristic.
