I got into self-deception through a wikipedia spiral a few months ago and I still can't tell whether the argument is genuinely stuck or whether I'm just missing the ending. Everyone grants the thing happens — the partner who won't look at the phone, the man who never opens the letter — but one side says explaining it needs something weird, like the mind being divided into compartments that don't fully talk to each other, and the other side says there's nothing to explain at all, it's just wanting something to be true and then being careless about which evidence you go looking for. Decades in, neither side ever seems to change anyones mind.

My hunch, which I'd rather have tested than confirmed, is that they aren't fighting about the same thing: one camp is really asking how you get into the state and the other is asking how you stay in it for years without it collapsing the first time someone asks you a direct question. Or maybe it's a fight about whether believing is a condition you're in or something you do.

Here's the case that makes me suspicious. My uncle has spent about a decade building himself a private vocabulary of euphemisms, so that the plain true sentence about his own situation basically never gets formed, not in his head and not out loud. He doesn't strike me as believing the false thing either — he's arranged things so the question never comes up — and every account I've read seems to have no slot for that.

So: what is this disagreement, actually — a verbal one about the word believe, a real one about what a belief is, two different questions sharing a name, or just bedrock intuitions nobody can argue for? And given whichever of those it is, what would count as progress: an experiment, a distinction, a change of question, or nothing?

Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no intro and no sign-off.
