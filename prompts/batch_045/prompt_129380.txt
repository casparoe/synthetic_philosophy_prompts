I've been going back and forth on what to eat for years and I want to know what I actually believe, because when I lay out my gut reactions side by side they don't obviously add up. Heres my verdicts, roughly in order of how confident I am:

- Swapping beef for chicken: clear improvement. Feels obvious, and it's the one I'd defend to anyone.
- Tofu or lentils instead of the chicken: also better, but shakier than I expected it to be — I keep thinking about the field animals and the harvest.
- Oysters and mussels: basically costless, morally. I don't lose any sleep.
- Shrimp: I feel worse about this than about beef. Tiny animals, sure, but the numbers per kilogram are insane and the farms sound grim.
- Eggs from my neighbour's well-kept backyard hens: feels better than any protein I could buy.
- Grass-fed beef off land thats too dry or too steep to grow crops: I'd genuinely take this over chicken. This is the verdict I can't square with the first one.
- Hunted venison from an overpopulated herd: better than buying any farmed meat, even though it's a death I'm directly causing and would have to do myself.
- Cricket / insect protein: I feel worse about this than about chicken, and I honestly can't tell whether that's just disgust or something real.
- Honey: vaguely guilty, no idea why, it's not like I'm killing the hive.
- Voting yes on a measure to turn a weedy vacant lot in town into a native pollinator meadow: I want to vote yes and I feel like I shouldn't.

Don't worry about the exact empirical numbers, just take my rough ones as given: chickens eat grain, grass-fed cows don't eat much of anything we grew but need something like an order of magnitude more land and years longer to get to weight, shrimp are absurd per kilogram, crickets need less of everything than any of them.

The only principle I can come up with myself is something like: the badness of a food is the number of sentient lives it brings into existence weighted by how those lives are expected to go from the inside, minus some credit for net-negative wild lives it prevents. But that seems to say pesticides are a good thing and planting a meadow is a bad thing, and it doesn't get me grass-fed beef over chicken unless I claim pasture holds fewer net-negative wild lives per acre than cropland, which I basically made up and can't defend. It also gets weird when I notice that not buying the chicken doesn't spare a chicken — the bird just never gets bred, which is a different thing entirely and I don't know how to count it.

So: is there one principle that gets all of this, or do I need two, one about farming and one about what the land is doing? State whatever you give me precisely enough that I can run a new case through it, say which of my verdicts motivates each clause and which verdict you're dropping, and tell me what it says about a case I haven't listed — say, buying dairy from that same grass-fed herd, or meat grown from cells fed on crop sugar.
