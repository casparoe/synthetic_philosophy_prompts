A dodgy analogy keeps doing work in discussions at my lab and I want it audited properly rather than just gestured at. The move is "theory choice is just data compression." The version I keep hearing is from a senior engineer on my team: Solomonoff induction settles it — the best predictor is the shortest program that reproduces the data, weighted by 2 to the minus its length, and it converges on the truth, so preferring simpler models isn't a aesthetic taste, it's optimality, and every other virtue people cite (mechanistic interpretability, unifying power, not hard-coding the answer) is either reducible to compression or unprincipled. He uses it to argue we should always ship the smallest model that fits and stop worrying about what goes on inside it.

Can you lay out the correspondence table — what plays the role of the theory, the data, simplicity, the prior, "converges on the truth," and what "best" even means here — and then where the mapping leaks? I want the disanalogies that matter for his conclusion, not the pedantic ones. The ones I half-suspect: for a finite dataset the shortest program is often a lookup table or a brute-force search, which is short to state but is exactly the hard-coding he claims not to like; the choice of machine and encoding is only worth a constant, but a constant is the whole ballgame at our sample sizes; and the convergence result is asymptotic and dominated by a term that depends on the truth itself, so it may license nothing about the next checkpoint.

Where I want to be pushed: I've been toying with the idea that the currency is wrong — that what we want isn't brevity but something more like logical depth, a short description that takes a lot of computation to unpack, so tables and brute force score badly. Does that capture what scientists mean by a good theory better than raw compression does, or does it just smuggle in "we like theories that look like mechanisms"? And if his analogy can't carry the conclusion, what would a better one be — is there anything in the neighborhood that does?
