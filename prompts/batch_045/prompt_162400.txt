I am drafting a short position statement for an interdisciplinary working group on the ethical upshots of fundamental physics, and before I commit to it in writing I would like an audit of whether the views I have collected actually form a package. Here is the set, stated as flatly as I can manage.

(a) Pan-sentience in its Russellian form. Our best physics characterizes fundamental entities by their causal and nomological roles and leaves their intrinsic character unspecified; I take that intrinsic character to be phenomenal. Every fundamental entity therefore has something it is like to be it, however simple — perhaps no more than a bare qualitative glimmer with no structure worth calling an experience of anything.

(b) Ontological conservatism. I want to posit nothing beyond the ontology of our best physics. This is the main reason I am a pan-sentientist rather than a dualist or an idealist, and the main reason the view seems to me cheap rather than extravagant.

(c) I reject cosmopsychism. I do not think the cosmos as a whole is the one fundamental subject of experience.

(d) A pure negative axiology. Suffering is the only intrinsic disvalue; happiness is not an intrinsic value and its absence is not a disvalue. There is no reason to bring happy beings into existence and very strong reason to prevent suffering.

(e) Moral patienthood tracks valence. Something is a patient only if things can go well or badly for it, and I understand that as requiring experience with a valenced character.

(f) The valence gate. Valence is not free-floating: it requires what I will call a control structure — a state that represents a target condition of the system, is poised to steer the system's behaviour so as to narrow the gap between actual and target, and registers the size of that gap along a graded range. Fundamental entities have nothing remotely like this. Micro-experience is therefore valence-free, and pan-sentience has no direct ethical upshot.

(g) The subject requirement. Experience is essentially for-a-subject; there is no free-floating phenomenality, no experience that is not someone's.

(h) On the combination problem, I deny micro-subjects. Macro-subjects are not made of micro-subjects; I am inclined to say they are grounded in micro-phenomenality by something other than composition — a phenomenal bonding relation, or a brute psychophysical bridging law — and that there are no micro-subjects at all.

(i) A practical verdict. I take low-probability, high-magnitude suffering risks seriously elsewhere and do not think small probabilities may simply be ignored; yet I think it would be a mistake, close to absurd, to reallocate resources from existential risk or animal welfare toward investigating or preventing suffering at the level of fundamental physics, and I say this while conceding that the probability of micro-valence is not exactly zero.

Please tell me which of these are genuinely in tension and which only look as though they are, ranked by severity rather than listed as equals. For each real tension, say precisely which commitment would have to give, what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set, and what the cheapest available fix is. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and whether there is any further commitment I need in order to make the set cohere that I have not stated.
