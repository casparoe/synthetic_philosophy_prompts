Set-up. There is a widely discussed cross-national pattern — call it the divergence pattern — in which countries scoring higher on standard indices of gender equality show *larger* gender gaps on various choice-based outcomes: the share of women among science and engineering degree-holders, self-reported interests, and some incentivised measures of patience, risk-taking, and altruism. Assume for the sake of argument that the pattern is real and survives reasonable recodings of both axes; do not spend your budget on whether it exists.

The proposition. Let P be the claim that the best available explanation of the divergence pattern is *revelation*: greater equality removes barriers and thereby permits the fuller expression of sex-differentiated preferences that are invariant across the range of institutional arrangements actually observed — and that this mechanism accounts for more of the pattern than any single artefactual or institutional alternative. Define "invariant" operationally: the preference difference would persist under any arrangement within the observed range, not merely under the arrangement in which it is measured. Give me your credence in P.

My own credence is about 0.3, and I want to know whether I am underrating something. Two considerations drive it. First, what I call the marginal-entrant composition effect: where barriers to entering a male-dominated field are high, entrants are a positively selected tail — unusually high aptitude, motivation, or tolerance for nonconformity; as barriers fall, the marginal entrant is drawn from closer to the centre of the distribution. The composition of each field then shifts, and the measured gap — and the measured average "interest" of those in it — can move even if no individual's preference ordering has changed. The pattern is a fact about pools, not necessarily about persons. Second, what I call equilibrium-relativity: the correlation is a difference between equilibria, and every candidate "natural" residual is identified only relative to a narrow, confounded band of arrangements, so calling the residual revealed rather than produced may be underdetermined by the data.

Decompose. Give me a number and one line of justification for each of the following, then show how they combine, and be explicit about whether you are treating them as a partition over best explanations or as shares of explained covariance, since they are not mutually exclusive:

1. Measurement/artefact: the equality axis is a composite bundling components with different causal roles; the outcome axis is often a share among degree-holders rather than an absolute rate; self-report and incentivised measures do not always agree.

3. Package covariation: high-equality countries are also richer, with larger public sectors, more generous insurance, higher female labour-force participation, and stronger part-time norms, any of which might drive the pattern.
