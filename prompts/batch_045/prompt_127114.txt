Call the first the contingency-profile test. The sea-battle intuition is standardly said to be driven by the future's being open, but "open" can mean at least three things, and they come apart in these cases: (i) a quantum event with objective chance 0.5, say the decay of a particular atom within the hour; (ii) a coin flipped by a machine, deterministic in principle and unpredictable in practice because of chaotic sensitivity to micro-conditions; (iii) a fact that is not contingent at all but merely uncomputed, such as whether the trillionth digit of pi is odd, or whether some unproved conjecture is true. If what drives the intuition is genuine metaphysical indeterminacy, only (i) should feel open. If it is causal unsettlement, (i) and (ii) should feel alike. If it is merely epistemic inaccessibility, all three should feel alike — and then the gap treatment looks like a general skepticism about bivalence for the unknown, which almost no one wants.
