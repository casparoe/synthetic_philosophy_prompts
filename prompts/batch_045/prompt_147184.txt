I teach a final-year undergraduate seminar in aesthetics and am writing this term's final exam. I want one 60-minute essay question on fakes, forgeries and the value of originals that cannot be answered by summarizing Goodman's argument about indiscernible copies or by invoking Benjamin on aura. Build it around this invented case, which I have drafted for the paper:

In 1911 a panel painting catalogued as the work of a minor seventeenth-century Dutch master is transferred: the conservator lifts the whole paint and ground layer off its warped oak panel and mounts it on a new mahogany one. Some decades later the museum re-examines its holdings. Dendrochronology dates the oak panel — which the museum kept, with a thin residue of ground still adhering to it — to the master's lifetime. Pigment analysis dates the paint layer now on display to the 1880s, and archival work shows that a restorer of that period reconstructed the image almost in its entirety from what survived of the original and had the result catalogued and sold as the master's own. So the object on the wall is a nineteenth-century imitation made of nineteenth-century materials; the only physically continuous remnant of the seventeenth-century object is a nearly blank plank in a storeroom.

Please give me:

1. The question as I would print it — the case, plus an instruction precise enough that a strong student knows what they are being asked to settle.
2. A model answer of roughly 600 words.
3. A marking scheme out of 20, organized around four must-hit moves, with the marks attached to each and one line on the error that should cost marks at that point. The errors I most want caught are sliding from "the plank is the spatio-temporally continuous object" to "so the plank is what is worth looking at," and treating "the hanging picture is not by the master" as though that settled what kind of value it can have.

The question should force students to say what sort of property authenticity is and what work it is supposed to do — a historical property, a guarantee of a certain kind of experience, or something else again — rather than to decide which of the two objects deserves a single title. Assume no access to a library, and give me no bibliography; I will attach the readings myself.
