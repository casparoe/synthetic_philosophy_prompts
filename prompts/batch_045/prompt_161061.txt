Please think carefully and work through this in stages: brainstorm the candidate failure modes first, sort them by whether they actually bite on our case, and only then give your verdict. Our committee is splitting a research budget between three programmes: A, scaling an intervention with well-evidenced but moderate benefits; B, a long-shot project with a small chance of averting a genuinely catastrophic outcome; C, capacity-building whose main value is procedural, in that it makes the consent and standing of the affected community matter to how we decide. Our methods consultant keeps saying that moral uncertainty is just factual uncertainty with extra steps. On that picture you have credences over moral theories, each theory tells you what each option is worth, so you take the expectation and get on with it: agonising about whose theory is right is unscientific, and any departure from expected-value weighting is squeamishness about arithmetic. The analogy is being used to license setting aside the objections of two views we take seriously — a suffering-focused view on which the badness of suffering is lexically prior to and effectively unbounded relative to the goods in play, and a consent-based view on which we may not act on any principle an affected party could reasonably reject, and which specifically counts weighting rival theories by our own private credences as such a principle: a second-order instruction about how disagreement itself is to be handled.

Audit the analogy for us. State the correspondence explicitly: in ordinary factual uncertainty, what plays the role of the state space, the probability measure, the outcome function, the utility function, and the shared currency in which outcomes are compared — and what, if anything, fills each of those slots in the moral case? Separate the breaks that matter for our decision from the ones that do not, and tell us whether the consultant's conclusion — expected-value weighting, no squeamishness — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it all along.
