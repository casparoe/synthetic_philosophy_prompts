I want a consistency audit of my own position on inherited wealth, not a survey of the literature. Here is where I have landed:

(1) Equal opportunity is a demanding ideal: it is unjust that a child's life chances should be substantially fixed by which family they are born into, and the state has a duty to dismantle the mechanisms of dynastic advantage.

(2) The central such mechanism is gratuitous transfer at death, so bequests should be taxed at 100% above a modest exemption. I reject the double-taxation complaint: the recipient has neither earned nor been taxed on anything, so nothing is taxed twice.

(3) I nonetheless reject taxing gifts made during life. Parents should be free to spend what they like on their children while alive — school fees, coaching, a flat, an unpaid internship funded by the bank of mum and dad — and it matters to me that the giver is present for the giving.

(4) Unequal outcomes are justified when they track effort and choice; what is unjust is advantage that flows from circumstances. But I read circumstances narrowly: being born into money counts, whereas native talent, health, temperament and the quality of one's upbringing are simply who a person is, and the state owes no one compensation for them.

(5) The tax should carry generous exemptions: the family home the children grew up in, the family farm, and the family firm in which the heir has worked for at least ten years.

(6) Charitable bequests and perpetual family foundations should remain unlimited and tax-exempt, for the sake of pluralism and civil society.

(7) Property rights are robust, and they include the right to give away and to bequeath what one owns.

(8) My preferred instrument is not an estate tax but a lifetime unearned-receipts tax: every gratuitous transfer a person receives over their whole life — fees, deposits, rent-free housing, inheritances — aggregated against one lifetime allowance set at roughly a median lifetime's earnings, and taxed above that at ordinary income rates.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look as though they do, and what is the least costly way of restoring consistency? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, state for each what exactly would have to give, and tell me whether any single revision would clean up more than one of them at once.
