I'm doing a piece for a science-and-ideas mag on whether blame is something we find or something we do, and I want to end it with the document two camps would actually sign if someone locked them in a room for a week. Camp A is Strawsonian: the reactive attitudes (resentment, gratitude, guilt, the demand for goodwill) partly constitute responsibility rather than track it, quality of will is what they're about, and the objective attitude isn't a live option for creatures like us. Camp B is hard incompatibilist: nothing answers to those attitudes once luck and causation are taken seriously, so blame should give way to something like quarantine/public-health management plus moral protest that doesn't pretend to desert. No verdict, no debate transcript — the joint statement. Give me:

1) A numbered agreed-claims section. Include at least what the reactive attitudes are and when they're apt; that we do in fact withdraw them in exempting conditions; that everyday blame tracks quality of will more than outcomes; that "could have done otherwise" is doing less work than lay readers assume.

2) A disagreements table, each row stated twice — once in wording the Strawsonian accepts, once in wording the incompatibilist accepts — with a flag if any row is really verbal.

3) Cruxes, each with a case both sides accept as bearing on it and each side's prediction written down in advance. Use my two plus any you'd add. (a) The triage-model case: a hospital's ML triage system keeps deprioritising one class of patients, and staff end up resenting it, holding a grudge, demanding it do better, while knowing it has no beliefs or will. Is that the attitudes stretching past their addressability condition, or does it show the practice was projective all along? What does each predict about how long the grudge lasts and what replaces it? (b) Engineered serenity: a cheap safe drug blocks resentment and indignation but leaves every belief about what someone was up to intact. Is what remains still blame, or judgement plus management? Each side's answer, and each side's prediction about whether the drug-taker keeps making demands on people.

Two more things: keep the agreed claims non-vacuous (nothing either side would reject as softened into mush), and separate what's settled among people who work on this from what's genuinely contested — and flag where you're inventing a formulation or extrapolating rather than reporting something the literature settles. Don't smooth a live dispute into a consensus for me.
