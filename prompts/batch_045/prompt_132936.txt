I've been reading about moral foundations theory on my own — no academic background, just a few popular books and a long-running argument with my brother-in-law — and one thing about it keeps bothering me. The framework presents itself as a neutral map rather than a verdict: it says it is only cataloguing the innate moral sensitivities people happen to have, the way one might catalogue taste receptors. But it keeps getting used to decide who is right, by explaining the other side's convictions as the output of the foundations they happen to score on, and their reasoning as post-hoc spin for intuitions that arrived first. I can never see why that move should stop short of the people drawing the map.

Please write a satirical policy document of about 650 words: a set of numbered clauses in deadpan civil-service prose, issued by an Office of Moral Foundation Parity in a country that has passed a Foundation Parity Act. Under the Act, arguments made in public are audited for foundation coverage — a letter to the editor, a sermon, or a protest sign that leans only on the two individualizing foundations and leaves the binding ones untouched is classified as monoculture speech, and its author is required to register as foundation-limited. Other clauses should follow the framework's own logic where it leads: because reasoning is held to be the press secretary of intuition rather than its source, audit rulings must be issued on intuition alone, and any auditor caught offering grounds for a ruling is disqualified. A seventh foundation has lately been added to the rubric over the drafters' objections — call it the Symmetry Foundation, the intuition that any norm you invoke against another person also binds you — attributed to a researcher at an institute you invent. Close with a short appendix in which the Office applies its own rubric to the Act's preamble and to the conduct of the audit board itself.

The jokes should come from the theory's real features — the taste-receptor metaphor, the individualizing/binding split, intuition-first moral psychology, the claim to be merely descriptive — pushed one step further rather than replaced with something easier to mock. The target is the practice of using moral psychology to settle moral disagreement, not any named person. It should be recognizably fair to someone who has read the literature and still legible to my brother-in-law.
