I've inherited the case log from the value adjudication group at the lab I just joined, and I have to write the next entry, so I'm trying to reverse-engineer the rule the previous adjudicator was running. Every entry asks the same question: does this preference count as part of this person's coherent extrapolated volition — is it something the system gets to treat as what they really want, or is it noise, an artifact we should discount? (You know the gloss: what you'd want if you knew more, thought faster, and were more the person you wished you were.)

Her rulings so far:

- Smoker, twenty years, says every January that he wishes he'd never started, relapses every February. Ruling: the wanting-to-quit counts, the craving doesn't.
- Woman in a village with no school who insists she never wanted to read; a school opens two valleys over, she learns at thirty, says it's the best thing that's ever happened to her. Ruling: the earlier contentment doesn't count, the later one does.
- Guy who always orders the same dish because he's sure the other one has cilantro in it; he's never actually tasted cilantro. Ruling: the dish he'd order after tasting counts.
- Woman who hated trail running, agreed to try it for six months for her partner's sake, now runs four times a week and says she'd do it again from the start. Ruling: counts.
- Man who finds a certain kind of music repulsive, gets a stim setting (prescribed for something unrelated) that flips it into his favourite genre; he's coherent, happy, and says he wants to keep the setting. Ruling: doesn't count.

New case, which I have to rule on. Man, 45, no coercion, no ignorance, nothing diagnosed. He's walked through twelve small optional changes to how he spends his time and attention over eighteen months — each one offered on its own, each one he tries, likes and endorses, and after each step he's still coherent and would endorse that step again. At the end he's recognisably a different person with different values, and he says this is what he wants and he'd do it again. Three extra facts. At t0, before any of it, he was shown the complete twelve-step itenerary and its endpoint in detail and said no: that's not me, I don't want to become that person, even though I can see each step might be pleasant. At step 6, reminded of the whole thing and where it ends, he said he doesn't want to end up there but doesn't want to undo what's happened either — and when we offered him a one-time restore button he turned it down. And the endpoint self scores better on every coherence measure we use than the t0 self did.

So: what would she have ruled — does the endpoint count as his CEV or not? What rule was she running that fits all five precedents, what other rule fits them just as well and disagrees here, and which one should I put in the log? If the precedents genuinely underdetermine it, say what we'd be committing to with each choice. And what one further fact about this case, or one further precedent we should write up, would tell the two rules apart? I dont just want "it depends on your theory of diachronic identity", I want a verdict and the reason it beats the other one.
