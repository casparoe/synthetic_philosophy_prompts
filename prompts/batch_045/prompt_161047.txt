Retired chemist, came to philosophy late, go to a reading group — we've now spent three sessions on Skolem's paradox and we're going in circles. Everyone grants the standard move: "uncountable" is model-relative, the bijection that makes a countable model of ZFC countable sits outside the model, so nothing formally contradicts anything. Then half the group says that's the whole story and it was never a real paradox; the other half says it only relocates the problem, because now somebody owes an account of what pins down the intended power set of the naturals, and paying that debt with full second-order semantics looks like buying determinacy on credit. Someone mentions Putnam's model-theoretic argument and we start over.

My hunch, offered as a hunch, is that its not really a paradox debate at all: it's the realism-about-sets dispute in a costume, and the Löwenheim–Skolem result adds nothing except forcing the realist to say where the intended interpretation is supposed to come from. But maybe there's a separate question hiding in there about absoluteness, or about whether "there are uncountably many reals" is doing different work inside a model and outside it.

Here's a probe I made up to test this definitly: suppose we were handed, from outside, the genuine power set of the naturals — God's own enumeration of the reals, so the external countability question is just settled by fiat. Would that end the argument, or would the two halves still be talking past each other? My guess is that whatever the answer is tells you which kind of stalemate this is.

So: verbal, a substantive clash about what an interpretation has to be, or two questions sharing a name? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a formal result, a distinction, a change of question — or is "nothing, and here's why" the honest answer? Be blunt about where your diagnosis is most likely to break and what would make you abandon it for a different one; I'd rather have that than a tidy verdict.
