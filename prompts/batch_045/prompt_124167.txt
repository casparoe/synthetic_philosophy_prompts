Write a dialogue, 700-900 words, between two philosophers — call them Mara and Dev — arguing about whether *you* could have come from a different sperm and egg. Mara takes the Kripkean line: no, your origin is essential to you, a person born of different gametes would be someone else. Dev thinks that intuition falls appart once you press on what the sperm and the egg are supposed to be doing in the story. Make them equally good — no strawmen — and have them actually answer each other rather than deliver speeches.

Please have them work through roughly these, not as a checklist but in this order:

1. Same gametes, wildly different outcome: the zygote splits into twins, or the embryo is frozen for twenty years, or the clinic fuses the very same sperm and egg a week later than it actually did. Mara says fine, still you — origin is about which gametes, not about what happens after. Dev asks why the boundary gets drawn right there.
2. Dev's squeeze: suppose a clinic fertilizes a numerically different sperm and a numerically different egg that happen to carry DNA sequences identical to the ones that actually produced you, with the mitochondria matched too, so nothing downstream differs. If Mara says "not you," then what's essential is the bare numerical identity of two cells — a fact with no qualitative or causal footprint on the resulting person. If she says "that is you," her view has quietly become genome-sequence essentialism.
3. The counter-squeeze: same gametes, but one base pair gets edited at the zygote stage, so you have a different genome from the one you actually have. Mara needs a real account of why *which cells* can matter while *which genes* don't, and Dev should push on whether it's stable — how much can the sperm itself change (swap out its atoms one at a time before fertilization) and still be the same sperm?
4. Dev's last jab: fusion is a process, not an instant, so you can't say exactly when the sperm and the egg became one thing — and how would you ever find out which gametes you came from anyway?

Don't end on "we seem to have reached an impasse." Somewhere in 2-4 one of them lands a hit the other can't fully defuse, and the other concedes something specific — tell me what exactly. Then add 150 words max in your own voice on which move actually worked, what the strongest reply left on the table is, and where you come down. Commit to a side; no "both views capture something important."
