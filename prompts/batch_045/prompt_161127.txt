Been chewing on this since it came up in a comment thread months ago and I keep failing to find the exit. I'm a physicalist by default, more or less by temperament, but this argument works way to well on me:

(1) If a scenario is ideally conceivable — you can form a positive conception of it, you know what it would take for the story to be true, not just fail to rule it out — that's good evidence it's metaphysically possible.
(2) A zombie world is ideally conceivable: all the microphysical facts of ours, nobody conscious. I can picture the story being true and nothing in the physics contradicts it.
(3) So zombies are possible, so the microphysical facts don't metaphysically settle the phenomenal facts.
(4) Parity, and in both directions: I can equally form a positive conception of a world microphysically identical to ours where my thermostat is also conscious, or where particles are arranged water-wise but there's no water, or arranged bank-and-ledger-wise but there's no money and nobody owes anybody anything.
(5) So the microphysical facts settle nothing above themselves. Chemistry, biology, tables, debts all float free as brute extra facts, in either direction, and since physics already fixes all the causal structure they'd be causally inert — which is the epiphenomenalism problem the zombie argument already has, except now it applies to money.

I want to keep (2); the gap feels real to me in a way I can't shake. I'd sooner give up (1) or (4) than say water isn't real or that I don't know what I'm looking at when I look at a table. But I can't tell which step is genuinely broken, and every one of them looked fine when I wrote it down. Is (4) doing something different from (2) under a shared label, or is (1) quietly sliding from an epistemic claim to a modal one? And whichever it is, what do I actually pay — what do I still get to say about consciousness afterward, and what do I lose?

Give me your diagnosis in prose, under ~500 words, then a JSON object with this schema:

- "headline": one sentence naming the step you reject or the step that fails, and whether it's a false premise or an invalid/subtle move.
- "per_step": array over steps "1" through "5", each with fields "step", "status" (keep / reject / reinterpret), "why_it_looked_fine", "cost_of_rejecting", "typical_rejecter" (the kind of position that gives this step up — label it in ordinary terms, no names needed).
- "cheapest_rejection": which single step to give up does the least damage, and what you walk away with.
- "phenomenal_vs_water": one or two sentences on what, if anything, makes the consciousness case different from the water/money case.
- "left_with": what someone who pays that cost can still claim about consciousness.
