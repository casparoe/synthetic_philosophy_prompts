hi — you're a professional philosopher of metaphysics who also writes verse, so please take this seriously as both. before you write anything, think it through properly: do a bit of brainstorming about which part of the form is going to carry which part of the argument, settle on your six words, and only then start drafting. i'd much rather have a poem with a worked-out conceit than a pretty one that just manages to drop the word "necessitation" into a stanza.

context: i run a second-year metaphysics reading group and our last session of term is on laws of nature. we've done Humean supervenience — the world as nothing but a mosaic of local matters of particular fact, laws as whatever regularities earn their place in the best system, no necessary connections in nature — against the anti-Humean alternatives: the Armstrong-style move of adding a second-order relation of necessitation between universals, the singularist version (the Dretske/Tooley sort of view) where the relation doesn't need the universals to be instantiated in order to hold, and the powers view where the necessity comes from what the properties themselves are. the two pressure points we keep circling are whether a mere regularity can really be said to govern at all (the accidental generalisation problem), and whether a Humean law can support a counterfactual like "had this match been struck it would have lit" without smuggling in something extra.

what i want to close the session with is a full sestina — six six-line stanzas plus the three-line envoi — read out loud at the end. here's my idea, sharpen it or bin it if you've got something better: the fixed rotation of the end-words is the law. six words just turn up, one after another, in their appointed places; nothing about any single word seems to carry its own position, and yet the pattern is perfectly strict and its what lets you predict where a word will land four stanzas down. that's the whole dispute in miniature — does the rotation describe what the words merely happen to do, or does it make them do it, and is it something over and above the words or just a neat way of summarising them?

things i actually care about:

- pick six end-words where at least three are words both camps use and mean differently (law, necessity, power, govern, chance, disposition, system — that sort of thing), and at least one is a plain concrete noun, a match or salt or a stone or rain, so it isn't jargon the whole way through
- make it work both ways round: a Humean poem if you read the rotation as just how the words happen to fall, an anti-Humean one if you read the words' own natures as demanding their places — without it turning into mush. someone who knows the literature should be able to point at a line and say which side that line commits you to
