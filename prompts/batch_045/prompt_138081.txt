Bit of background: I was an exploration seismologist for about forty years, retired now, and in the last couple of years I've drifted into philosophy through a discussion group here — mostly retired engineers plus one very argumentative chemist. Nobody has any formal philosophy, but they're fine with a bit of probability and they enjoy a fight. Next month it's my turn and I've gone and volunteered to do ninety minutes on counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail.

I don't need the cases explained to me. What I need is a plan for explaining them, and there are four things I'm stuck on.

Order. Do I do counterfactual mugging first and then show XOR blackmail is the same shape with the stakes rearranged, or lead with the blackmail case because everybody has an immediate conviction about it? And where does the "pick a policy in advance and then don't update on the evidence you were always going to get" move belong — handed to them early as a tool, or arrived at late as the answer to a problem they've felt? Tell me what depends on what, and give me one order, not two with their pros and cons.

The carrying example. I want one concrete version doing the work in both cases. I'm toying with a variant where Omega runs its simulation on a copy of you after you've already answered, and tells the copy the coin came up the other way, so nobody can say the prediction causally reached back into your decision — and the coin is loaded nine to one in the mugger's favor, so "it evens out in expectation" can't do any work. Is that the right vehicle or does loading the coin muddy somthing I'd regret? What does the example have to have, and what must it not have?

Misconceptions. They will immediately try to compute an expected value. Someone will say it's just Newcomb's problem with extra steps. Someone will say you should simply be the sort of person who pays, and someone else that you never negotiate with blackmailers. Where exactly does each one have to be headed off, and is there a mistake they'll make halfway through that I should leave in place for a minute on purpose?

Last thing: one question I can put to them at the end that shows me they've understood what's at stake rather than which side they picked, and what I should flatly refuse to get into in ninety minutes. Take a definite position on all of it and say why — and American English, please.
