I'm writing something on how moral norms actually collapse — slavery, duelling, that sort of case — and I keep getting the three usual stories thrown at me as if they were rivals you just pick between: the arguments did it, the material interests did it, honour did it. Before I sign up for one I'd rather have the spec. So: what would a theory of moral revolution have to be able to do to be any use to someone running a campaign today who wants to know which lever to pull? Give me the desiderata, each with the case behind it, and mark which are genuine constraints and which are just one camp's perferences.

Please get on your list (or tell me which of them is a bogus demand and why) these five: 1) a practice that dies out while almost nobody's belief about it changes, because it just got expensive or inconvenient — is that even the same phenomenon, and what does a theory owe us here; 2) pluralistic ignorance, everyone privately finished with the practice and it still fully in place; 3) the timing problem, the argument that fell flat for forty years and then works inside a decade; 4) the honour-holders giving up the thing that made them honourable, i.e. the people whose status was built on the practice being the ones who end it; 5) abolition imposed from outside by a conqueror or a colonial administration, which changes behaviour and maybe changes nothing else.

Then the bit I actually want: which two desiderata on your list you think can't be jointly satisfied — I suspect wanting one mechanism general enough to cover all five cases and wanting each case's local honour economy to do real explanatory work are in tension, but you say which — and which single requirement knocks out the most of the three usual stories. And if you think the Williams-y point, that a revolutionary's reasons aren't reasons for the person she's arguing with so argument can't be the engine, is better handled as a desideratum than as a theory, say where it sits on the list. A one-line scorecard for arguments / interests / honour at the end is fine, but keep the weight on the criteria and their cases.
