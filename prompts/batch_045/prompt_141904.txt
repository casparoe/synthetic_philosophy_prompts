I've been reading Bentham on sexual morality lately — the manuscript he wrote and never published arguing that homosexual sex shouldn't be a crime, and the bits where he says most of what passes for sexual morality is really just disgust dressed up as a reason (that "sympathy and antipathy" thing he keeps bashing), plus his bit about how if the quantity of pleasure is the same, one kind of pleasure isn't worth more than another. I find it definately attractive, so my starting principle is roughly: none of this is anyone else's business, and neither the law nor anybody's blame gets a foothold unless someone's pain goes up.

But my gut on cases won't line up. Here's where I land:

1. Two adults, private, consensual, doing stuff I personally find gross: totally fine, and the law has no business there at all.
2. Two adults having sex on a crowded public beach at midday: not fine, they should be moved along.
3. The same couple doing the same thing in their own bedroom with the blinds open, where a neighbour can see in if they walk past: not fine either, but a lot less bad than 2 — even though the neighbour didn't opt into seeing anything.
4. A 17-year-old and a 24-year-old, no authority relation between them, both genuinely into it: fine, actually, even though it's illegal where I live.
5. A 17-year-old and the 34-year-old who coaches them: not fine, and way worse than 4, even though it's the same age bracket and everyone involved says yes.
6. Consensual sex between adult siblings, private, contraception, no chance of kids: not fine, and I do think the law should prohibit it. But I can't name who gets hurt, which bugs me.
7. An explicit fictional story involving non-consent, written and read in private, no real people depicted: fine, should be legal.
8. A recording of an actual assault, watched alone, never shared, victim never finds out: not fine, should be illegal to possess, and it feels much worse than 7 even though what the viewer gets is the same.
9. Someone generating photorealistic nude images of a real, identifiable ex using her public social media photos, showing nobody: not fine. Nobody was hurt making it, and she never knows.

So 7 vs 8 says the principle has to look backwards at how the thing was produced, not forwards at what it does to the consumer. 9 says it's not about pain at all — it feels like it's about using somebody. And 6 is just a flat fight with my own starting principle.

The patch I've been trying: it's fine unless (a) somebody who didn't opt in gets made into a participant or an audience, (b) somebody was actually hurt producing whatever it is your using, or (c) you deploy someone's body or image without their say-so. But (a) doesn't explain why 3 is still not fine, and (c) doesn't seem to do whatever work 6 needs done, and I can't tell why both (b) and (c) should be in there at the same time.

Can you do better? Give me a principle, or two or three candidates if there isn't one, stated precisely, with the case that forces each clause. I also want to know what it says about two cases I haven't listed: paying for sex where both people are adults and nothing else about the situation is coercive, and somebody who says yes enthusiastically only because they'd lose their housing otherwise, where the person they sleep with had nothing to do with that pressure and doesn't know about it. And please be straight with me about which of my verdicts each candidate makes me give up.
