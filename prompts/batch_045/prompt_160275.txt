Right, so I'm running a grad seminar this term on what emotions are, and two of my best students have spent about six weeks now talking past each other, so I want to force them into an adversarial collaboration instead of another morning of mutual throat-clearing (the room has no wifi, which is honestly a blessing, but it does mean I don't want any bibliography, footnotes or 'as so-and-so argues' — pretend the library is shut and just give me the arguments in your own words). The other holds what I'd call the perceptual or attitudinal line: emotions are experiences that present their objects as bearing evaluative properties, the way perception presents objects as coloured or shaped — contentful, intentional, with correctness conditions of a sort, but not beliefs: not revised just by being told you're wrong, not tracking truth in the belief way, not something you could literally assent to or withdraw, and with a characteristic phenomenology that does the representing rather than merely accompanying it.
