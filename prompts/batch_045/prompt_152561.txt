i'm in a third-year cog sci seminar on embodied/extended cognition and for the last class everyone's supposed to bring one joke that actually lands for the people in the room. I really dont want to do the 'my phone is part of my brain lol' thing, or the one where Otto loses his notebook and everyone goes 'he lost his mind, haha' — we've heard those about a hundred times already.

So here's the wrinkle I want the joke to turn on. Our TA gave us this case he calls the delegated notebook: it's Otto's notebook in every respect you'd normally check — he's carried it for years, he trusts it completely, he consults it automatically, the info is as available to him as Inga's biological memory is to her — except he doesn't write in it anymore. His carer does. Otto just reads whatever's there and endorses it. So all the usual Clark and Chalmers conditions come out satisfied: reliably available and typically invoked, automatically endorsed, easily accessible when needed. The only thing that changed is whose cognition produced the content.

Our TA says this is the case that supposedly seperates the 'if it plays the functional role of memory, it's memory' reading from the 'you also need the right kind of causal history' objection, and honestly I'm not sure which way I'd go.

Write me one joke — a one-liner or a short setup-and-punchline — whose punchline depends on that difference: content authored by someone else versus content that's reliably available and automatically endorsed. It shouldn't just depend on the general point that external stuff can be load-bearing for a mind, and it shouldn't depend on Otto forgetting something. Short enough to say out loud in a seminar, like 20 seconds. Don't write an explanation after it, I want to read it cold and see whether I get it.
