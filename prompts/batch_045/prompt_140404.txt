So I want to know what I actually think. Here's where I land:

- The mouse segregation distorter: heterozygote males pass the thing on to way more than half their pups but sire fewer pups overall, and homozygotes are sterile or dead. Verdict: gene-level selection, no hesitation. The individual mouse is doing worse while the allele does fine; the organism is just the arena.
- A peacock's train. Verdict: individual-level selection, i.e. sexual selection — it's about how many matings that bird gets. I get that you can redescribe it as the long-train allele out-reproducing its alternative, but that redescripion feels empty here, and I want to say gene-level talk is doing no work in this case even though it's true.
- Sterile workers in an ant colony headed by a singly mated queen. Verdict: gene-level again, via relatedness. I don't want to call the colony a unit of selection; I want to say the colony is something the genome builds.
- Cellular slime moulds: amoebas aggregate when they starve and about a fifth of them die to build the stalk that lifts the rest. My prof told us to imagine the slug is chimeric, formed from unrelated amoebas. Verdict: I still want to say yes, this is selection at the level of the slug — between-slug differences beat within-slug cheating often enough that something like the stalk/spore ratio really is a slug-level trait.
- Structurally the same thing inside my own body: cell lineages that defect get policed by apoptosis, immune surveillance, a sequestered germline. Verdict: here I do NOT want to say multilevel selection, with the body as the group and cells as the individuals. I want to say the body just is the individual and cell-level selection is noise that gets suppressed.

Those last two are the pair I can't reconcile, and I think I might be conflating two seperate questions without knowing how to pull them apart. The rule I keep coming back to is a reproduction test: a level is a unit of selection only if things at that level genuinely reproduce — some bottleneck-ish process makes offspring units that resemble parent units — rather than getting reassembled out of whatever happens to be lying around each generation.
