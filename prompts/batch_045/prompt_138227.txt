Putting together a short position paper for a reading group on causation and I'd like someone to poke at where I've landed before I write it up. Roughly, here's the package:

(a) Token causation between particular events just is counterfactual dependence — c causes e iff e depends on c, allowing for stepwise dependence along a chain. And I want there to be one relation here; I really don't want to end up a pluralist about causation.
(b) Causation is objective and mind-independent. It isn't a projection of our explanatory interests.
(c) Causal claims in the special sciences — "the rate rise caused the recession" — can be literally true and genuinely explanatory in their own right, without waiting on a microphysical story, and they stay true even when the underlying mechanisms are wildly heterogeneous from instance to instance.
(d) But I also think the interventionist people get something right: a causal claim is only well-formed relative to a set of variables and contrast values, and the very same pair of events can make "C caused E" true relative to one variable set and false relative to another.
(e) Case verdicts: in early preemption, Suzy's throw caused the shattering and Billy's didn't; in the simultaneous version, both did. And I don't think the theory gets to save itself by re-individuating the effect as "shattered at exactly that instant in exactly that pattern" so that it does depend on Suzy's throw — that move has always felt like cheating to me.
(f) Omissions can be causes, the very same relation as in the positive cases: the nurse's not adminstering the drug caused the death. But the Queen of England's not administering it didn't. And I don't want to get that asymmetry by appealing to what's normal, or default, or statistically typical, since that seems to smuggle our interests right back into the causal relation.

So: where does this not hang together? I have a feeling (b) and (d) fight each other, and I also have a feeling they don't, and I can't tell which. Please rank whatever real tensions you find by how bad they are rather than flagging everything as equally fatal, tell me for each what would have to give and what giving it up costs me somewhere else in the list, and say whether there's one single revision that fixes more than one of them at once. Also flag anything I've assumed is in tension that actually isn't.
