Context: the students have already done Otto and Inga, the parity principle, and the four trust-and-glue conditions (the resource is reliably available, easy to get at, automatically endorsed when consulted, and was endorsed at some point when it was taken up). What they always miss is that parity is a test on the functional profile of the resource and says nothing whatsoever about whose mind is in question. I want a case that makes that vivid rather than me just asserting it.

Three criteria I want the case to separate:

1. Parity plus glue, Clark/Chalmers style: if the external thing plays the same functional role an internal belief would — reliably there, automatically endorsed, guiding action the same way — then it realises a belief, full stop, no further questions.

2. A practice-based or integrationist criterion, roughly the Menary/Sutton end of the literature (don't worry about attribution, I write my own handouts): a resource counts only when it's woven into a learned cognitive practice. The agent was trained into the routine of using it, the using is plastic and skill-shaped, and agent and resource have been mutually reshaped by the routine.

3. My own hobby-horse, which I've never managed to convince anyone of. Call it the revision-profile test: a candidate external state is part of your mind only if a silent change in its content shows up for you as correcting yourself rather than as dealing with a faulty instrument. Contrast discovering you've misremembered something — you revise, the earlier state now seems to have been wrong first-personally, you'd say I was wrong — with discovering your satnav has an outdated map, where you're annoyed at the device and nothing in you needed putting right. Correction or repair: which shape does the mismatch have?

Specs for the case:
