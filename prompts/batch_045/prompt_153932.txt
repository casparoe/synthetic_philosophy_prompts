I help run a very informal philosophy meetup — 10 to 14 adults, mostly no philosophy background, back room of a pub, about 90 minutes, paper and pens only, nobody wants homework and nobody wants real money changing hands. I've been reading about decision theory on my own and I keep coming back to that story where Death turns up in Damascus and says he'll be there tomorrow, and the guy starts thinking about running to Aleppo. I get why it's interesting, but I can't make it land when I just describe it: people say "obviously run" or "its just about free will" and we move on after four minutes. So I want to stage it instead of describing it.

What I'm after: a few short rounds that people actually play in pairs — one person is Death, one is the Traveller, rotate so everyone plays both — where the predictions really are right most of the time rather than guesses, and where by the third round people are arguing with themselves out loud. They should run into two things that feel identical and aren't: "my choice can't change what's already written on the card" and "if I'm genuinely able to pick the other one, that tells me something about what's on the card." And they should feel that the second thought makes choosing unstable, like every plan they form gets cancelled by the fact that it was foreseeable.

Some things I think I want, but you design it and overrule me where I'm wrong:

- A mechanic that makes the Death player's prediction actually accurate without cheating. My idea was to have the Traveller say out loud what they're inclined to do before Death writes anything, then let them choose freely afterwards — but if that wrecks the case, say so and do something better.
- Points, no money, arranged so the Death player genuinely cares about being right (otherwise people scribble nonsense and the whole thing dies) and so the Traveller genuinely cares about surviving.
- An early round where the prediction stays sealed until after the choice, then a round where the Traveller gets to open it first. I want people to notice that this one change flips what feels sensible.
- A round where the Traveller can pay points to hand the decision to a die roll, or to someone in the room who hasn't seen the card. Several of us have the feeling that this ought to help somehow, and I want them to find out whether it does.
- One round where everyone is told up front that Death is only right about 80% of the time, with the running tally on the board, so people can feel where the flip-flopping stops and at what accuracy it starts again.
- Nothing named up front: no "causal", no "evidential", no Newcomb, not the name of the story, and no explanation at the start. They hear the words at the end or not at all.
- It has to survive the guy who says it's just free will — set the rules or the payoffs so that move runs into a wall instead of ending the conversation.

Give me materials, the exact rules and payoffs for each round, a rough minute-by-minute timeline for 90 minutes, what goes on the board and when, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto specific bits of the idea. Also an honest list of what will go wrong the first time I run it (I expect at least: Death players leaking their prediction, people not taking points seriously, someone accusing Death of cheating) with how to handle each, and then the bit I care about most: what the game can't reproduce and I'll have to say myself.

Start your reply with one sentence naming the core mechanism that makes people hit the problem themselves, then give me the rest. No preamble, and don't restate what I asked for.
