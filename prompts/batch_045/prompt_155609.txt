I've been reading LW and ACX forever so I know all the standard moves around this stuff, but now I actually have to do it for real: I'm one of three people on the grants committee of a small animal-welfare funder, and we get ~30 proposals a year aimed at insects where the whole case is a scale argument — the numbers are astronomic, so even a tenth of a percent chance they feel anything makes the EV look enormous. I know why that's supposed to be fishy (the sentience probability isn't indepedent per insect, it's basically one shared hypothesis, so you're not making a million small bets that diversify, you're making one huge correlated bet) but I can't turn that into anything my committee can run on an actual proposal in an afternoon, with no literature review and no expert on call.

So: write me a decision procedure. Questions in order, what counts as an adequate answer to each, where it just stops with a no, and where it has to stop and hand to a person because it's a values call. Specifically I want it to handle:

- unit of account: for eusocial species, is the candidate subject the individual insect, the colony as a superorganism, or both, and how much of the answer rides on that choice;
- interventions that move the population as well as the welfare per insect (a faster-acting pesticide kills more cleanly but might mean more survivors breeding). My suspicion is the sign of the effect flips depending on whether an insect life is net positive or net negative, and we genuinely don't know, so maybe the procedure branches there or punts;
- the case where everything funnels into one correlated hypothesis and the right move might be buying information rather than acting — say when.

Also tell me plainly where the procedure will misfire, i.e. where it hands back confident-looking numbers that are just our priors with a spreadsheet wrapped round them.

Then run it on three: (1) a supermarket-chain campaign to switch to faster-killing pesticides; (2) funding a study on whether some other clade shows the "pessimism"-style judgement-bias markers I half-remember from bees, on the theory the result propagates across a lot of species at once; (3) the bee-pollination welfare project I've already half-promised a big grant to, which I want you to run honestly even if it comes out saying pull out. British English please.
