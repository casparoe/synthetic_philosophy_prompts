I am a novelist, and I would like a short piece of prose built to order.

The situation is this. In the book I am working on, a minor character earns her living deciphering recovered documents: fragments that arrive without titles, authors, dates, or any account of who made them or why. I want to place one such fragment in the text as a block of prose and have my reader's experience of it reproduce hers. So I need a passage that fails in a particular way on a first encounter and succeeds on a later one, and I need the failure to be engineered rather than accidental.

The framework I have in hand is the three-level distinction drawn in Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach: the frame message, the signal that an object is a message at all and that some decoding mechanism is needed; the outer message, carried by the symbol-patterns and structures, which tells you how to build that mechanism; and the inner message, the meaning the sender meant to transmit, the thing that is lost when a text is correctly decoded and yet somehow says nothing to you.

Please construct a passage meeting the following specifications, then show that it meets them.

Constraints on the passage. No more than about three hundred words, printable as a single unbroken block with no title, epigraph, attribution, italics, or framing narration: it must supply its own frame. No typographic or layout devices of any kind, since it has to survive being read aloud in an audiobook. Nothing outside the passage may be needed to get it: no facts about its author, no historical or specialist knowledge, no private code or key. This last constraint matters to me, because the case has to survive the author being dead, unknown, and unavailable as a court of appeal.

Constraints on the failure. First: a competent reader who reads it once, forwards, in the ordinary way, for content, should recover the frame and the outer message correctly and miss the inner message — and the miss must not be a matter of carelessness or of failing to notice something that was there to be noticed. Second, and this is the part I expect to be hard: the passage must be paraphrase-stable. A reader who has missed the inner message can still produce an accurate paraphrase of what the passage says, and once the inner message has arrived the paraphrase is more or less the same. I do not want a riddle whose solution changes the literal content; I want a case where the inner message is real, is missed, and is not additional propositional content. Third: the inner message must be determinate. Readers who get it should converge on it and be able to point to features of the text as grounds, so that it cannot be written off as a private association. Fourth: recovery should come from something the reader does — a second pass, a different tempo, taking the thing to be a different kind of object — rather than from information arriving from outside.

What I want after the passage. Walk through the three levels as your passage instantiates them, and state what each of my two kinds of reader comes away with. Say which single feature of the passage is doing the work, and what happens to the case if you remove it. Say whether the first-pass failure is guaranteed or merely systematic, and what discipline of reading would avoid it. Then take a stand on the diagnosis I cannot settle myself: is the failure one of familiarity, the reader lacking the code that would let a decoded outer message trigger the inner one, or one of procedure, the reader having run the wrong operation on a text she has in fact fully decoded? Give me your reason, and then give me the best reply available to someone who holds the other diagnosis and wants your case not to count.

Finally, since I am going to stake a chapter on this: tell me what would change your mind about your own construction. What evidence about real readers, or what consideration, would convince you that the inner message here is nothing over and above the outer message plus the reader's associations, or that the outcome was stipulated by you rather than generated by the text? And flag the point at which you think the design is most likely to be cheating.
