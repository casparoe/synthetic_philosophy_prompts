I want a designed case, not an illustration. The question I'm using it to probe: whether the standard escape route from money pumps — adopt the best plan ex ante and then stick to it, rather than re-deciding at each offer — is actually available to someone who ranks plans entirely by the outcomes they produce. My suspicion is that for some structures of offers it isn't: once you throw out the plans that are straightforwardly worse than others, what remains cycles, so there is no best plan to be resolute about. I need a case that generates that result from the agent's preferences and the offer structure, not one that stipulates it.

Specifications:

1. One agent, one exploiter, an ordinary setting with money and ordinary goods — no demons, no perfect predictors, nothing science-fictional — and compact enough to state in a short paragraph.
2. The agent's preferences over three options A, B, C are strictly cyclic: A over B, B over C, C over A. Give the cycle a realistic source (comparison along different dimensions, or a contrast or status-quo effect) rather than just stipulating it.
3. The exploiter makes offers one at a time, each a swap of the option she currently holds for the next one in the cycle, with a small cash payment from her to him. The payment must be strictly positive and strictly smaller than each of the three pairwise margins, and you should give actual numbers so I can check this.
4. The agent is fully informed and knows the exploiter's strategy, has no commitment devices or contracts, faces no transaction costs, and never refuses a trade she strictly prefers. Do not block the pump by making her suspicious, forgetful, weak-willed, or unwilling to plan.
5. Show the arithmetic: list the available plans of the form accept k offers and then stop, rank them by the outcomes they produce, eliminate the dominated ones, and show that what survives is a three-way cycle with no maximal element.
6. Then give the verdict of each approach: the chooser who re-decides at each offer, and the chooser who is supposed to pick the best plan ex ante and carry it out.
7. Close by naming the feature of the case that is doing the work, saying what happens if that feature is removed, and giving the strongest reply available to a defender of the plan-and-stick-to-it view.
8. Finally, state how confident you are in each claim above and single out the one step you are least sure of.
