I've been reading interpretability papers for fun — engineering background, no philosophy training — and I keep tripping over the same shape of result without having a word for it.

Here's the shape. Someone picks a property they care about (is this token part of a city name, is the model in a refusal-ish mode, does it "believe" the answer is Paris), finds a direction in activation space that a linear probe reads the property off of, and then shows that pushing activations along that direction changes behaviour roughly the way you'd expect if the property were really in there. So far so good. What bugs me: you seem to be able to pull this off for pretty much any property you can write a label for, and the direction you end up with slides around when you change the layer, the dataset, or the random seed, while the model's actual behaviour doesn't slide at all. Two groups can even find two different directions for "the same" thing, both pass the read-out test and the steering test, and there's nothing in the results that picks between them. And when this gets pointed out, the reply is always to bolt on another condition — it has to be the direction the model itself uses downstream, or it has to be the one a sparse decomposition uniquely recovers — which looks to me like it just relocates the same problem one step over.

I care because this is the kind of evidence people cite when they say a network "really represents" X, and I can't tell whether my worry is about the evidence or about the claim.

Is there an established name for this, and if so what are its edges? I've seen polysemanticity and superposition used for one direction standing for several features, which feels like the mirror image of my thing, and I have a vague sense that "underdetermination" is nearby, but neither is quite it — mine is about the evidence failing to single out one decomposition, not about one neuron doing double duty and not (I think) about a whole theory being underdetermined. If no established term fits, say so and coin one, defined tightly enough that it doesn't collapse into its neighbours. The label really isn't the point: I want the carving, so tell me what it is, what it isn't, and where it stops.
