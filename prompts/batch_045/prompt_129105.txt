I teach a final-year seminar on free will and moral responsibility, and we are spending three weeks on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument. What students keep missing is that the argument, even granting it in full, settles very little on its own: they accept the regress and then assume that no ultimate responsibility has a single obvious upshot. So I want a one-page decision-tree handout that starts exactly where they already are, with the root commitment I grant the Basic Argument's conclusion: no one is ever ultimately responsible, in the desert-entailing sense, for anything. Every node below that root should be a question on which people who all accept that conclusion still come apart, phrased so that a student can answer without knowing which view it leads to, and no node may name a school, an -ism, or a philosopher. Leaves must include at least: the quietism associated with Strawson himself, on which the result is metaphysically true and practically inert because the participant reactive attitudes are not under the will; hard incompatibilism of the revisionary or quarantine sort; the view that accountability or answerability survives without desert; and a reactive-attitudes-only view; add anything else you think belongs. The final branch must turn on what I have started calling double bookkeeping: whether resentment, gratitude and guilt can be kept at full strength while their desert-entailments are explicitly denied, or whether consistently denying desert must, over time, blunt them. Flag any combination of answers that is unoccupied, with one sentence on whether it is empty because incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate. Then give a table with columns leaf_name, one_line_statement, answer_pattern_reaching_it, most_awkward_commitment, and indistinguishable_from, naming the leaf or marking the cell empty.
