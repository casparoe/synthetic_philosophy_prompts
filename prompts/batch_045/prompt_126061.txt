I am writing the requirements chapter of my PhD thesis on updating under uncertain evidence, and before I survey candidate rules I want a specification: what a rule for revising a credence function on the basis of evidence that is itself uncertain has to deliver in order to count as adequate for the job I care about.

The purpose I want the rule good for: a bounded, artificial epistemic agent (think of the update module of a clinical decision-support system) that holds a probability distribution over a large, factored set of variables and receives a stream of items that are not hard certainties. Concretely, its inputs arrive in at least these four shapes, and I want the requirements list built around them rather than around the textbook case of a clean new probability assignment to a partition:

1. report evidence with a stated but disputable reliability — a sensor or a colleague says that p, where the agent's confidence that the source is functioning and being read correctly is itself something like 0.8;
2. nested or second-order evidence — the agent becomes uncertain about what its own earlier evidence was, or about the competence of the process that produced its last update;
3. inexact evidence — the agent is only willing to constrain the new probability of a proposition to an interval, or to constrain an expected value, rather than fix a number;
4. retractable evidence — a later item reveals the earlier source was broken, and the agent must back out the update rather than merely pile a further update on top of it.

Please write the desiderata (8 to 12 of them) as a table with columns: requirement; the case that motivates it and why that case is a requirement rather than a nice-to-have; status (non-negotiable, or a preference of one camp — accuracy-firsters, dynamic-Dutch-book arguments, Bayes-network engineers, imprecise-probabilists); and what the requirement rules out. Alongside the four input shapes above, the cases I want handled are at least: reduction to ordinary conditionalization in the limit where the uncertain evidence becomes hard; no-op when the new probabilities on the partition coincide with the old ones; continuity in the evidential input, so that small error in estimating the input produces small error in the output; insensitivity to how finely the agent carves the space when it encodes the evidence, including what happens when rigidity holds on a coarse partition but not on a refinement; idempotence when the same item arrives twice; order-independence across a sequence of items; and reversibility when an item is withdrawn. Include a requirement about where the evidential input is allowed to come from: whether the rule may take a new probability assignment as a black box, or owes an account of it in terms of likelihoods, and what coherence constraints the input must satisfy.

Two things matter most to me. First, mark which requirements are genuine and which are the prejudices of a camp — I would rather drop a demand than keep one that only sounds obligatory. Second, if some subset of your requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, say so and give the argument: I expect at least one clash and my guess is that it involves order-independence, but show it rather than asserting it, and tell me which requirement you would weaken and what that costs.

Then, briefly — a short paragraph each, not a survey — score against your list: ordinary conditionalization on the report that the source said that p; Jeffrey-style probability kinematics with rigidity on a partition; a constraint-based rule that picks the minimally informative revision satisfying the constraint; and, if you think it belongs, the likelihood-ratio or virtual-evidence treatment used in graphical models. I will supply the citations myself, so refer to positions and rules descriptively and do not give me a bibliography.

Keep the whole thing under about 1300 words. End with a single final line in exactly this form, naming the one requirement that does the most work by excluding the most candidates:

Verdict: <short label for that requirement>
