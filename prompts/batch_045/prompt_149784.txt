I am a novelist finishing a novel in which three characters argue about whether the protagonist — who acted exactly as she wanted to, under conditions that would have been corrected had she wavered — is to blame for what she did. I want their argument to track a genuine fault line in the moral responsibility debate rather than three shades of the same rhetoric, so what I need is a classification instrument, not an opinion: a decision tree that starts from what an intelligent reader who has never heard of Harry Frankfurt would say about such a case, and ends in a named position.

Please build it from the logical space rather than from a list of isms: give me the branch questions first, say which answer-combinations they permit, and only then locate the actual positions in that space. Constraints:

- At most six branch questions and eight leaves. Every branch node should be a single claim or mini-case that separates positions, phrased so my characters could answer it without knowing where it leads.
- The cases I insist on appearing somewhere as nodes: the standard monitor-and-intervener setup; a variant in which the only alternative the agent retains is a twitch of hesitation the monitoring device itself induces, so that the alternative exists but is an artifact of the device; a variant in which she retains only trying-and-failing, or a felt pull she never acts on; and a variant in which she freely arranged, years earlier, to become the sort of person the device would never have had to correct.
- Leaves must include a leeway position that keeps the principle of alternative possibilities, a source-or-actual-sequence compatibilist position, a semicompatibilist position, a source-incompatibilist position, a hard-incompatibilist or skeptical position, and one forward-looking or revisionist position that drops desert but keeps the practice. Name a leaf only where a label is standard; otherwise call it unnamed.
- Mark the combinations nobody occupies and give a one-line diagnosis of each: incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot truly separate rather than inventing a question to pry them apart.

Two things specific to my purpose. First, for each leaf give one sentence on the most awkward thing a character holding it would have to say out loud — I need lines, not summaries. Second, my three characters currently say, roughly: she couldn't have done otherwise, so blame is off the table; the device never touched the actual story, so she is fully answerable; and even if she is answerable, none of us are, really. Tell me where in the tree each of them lands, and which of their disagreements are merely verbal rather than substantive — I would rather cut those scenes than write characters who talk past each other for twenty pages.
