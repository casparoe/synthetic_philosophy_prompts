Hey — I'm giving a works-in-progress talk in three weeks to the joint condensed-matter-theory / quantum-information seminar here, and what I need is Q&A prep much more than slide feedback.

Setup: 25 minutes of talk, 30 of questions, room of about eight people. Roughly half are quantum-information people of the operationalist, resource-theory stripe — they use entropy to mean an asymptotic conversion rate between resources, they're allergic to the slogan that information is physical, and they'll want every claim restated as a claim about what's achievable given free access to a bath at fixed temperature (thermal operations in whatever formalism they like). The other half do stochastic thermodynamics and fluctuation theorems, and think the demon literature was settled decades ago, or that it was never a real problem because for finite systems the second law is statistical anyway. Also in the room: my supervisor, who thinks the only interesting question about the second law is where its initial condition comes from, and one philosopher of science who will keep asking what any of this explains.

Thesis I'm defending: the demon literature runs together two different exorcisms, and only one of them works. Call them the accounting exorcism — find a compensating entropy increase somewhere in the demon's cycle so the books balance — and the no-go exorcism — show from the dynamics alone, without any bookkeeping premise, that no device can run a cycle whose sole net effect is work extracted from a single heat bath. I want to say the accounting version is in good shape but doesn't do the work people want: it tells you where the cost shows up, not why there can't be a demon. And the no-go version, in the strong form, isn't available, because every reconstruction I can build of it smuggles in the premise that the demon's memory starts out uncorrelated with the gas. That premise isn't information-theoretic, it's an initial-condition premise. So the moral isn't that information is physical; it's closer to: you can only be a demon with respect to a system you aren't already correlated with, and being uncorrelated is itself a contingent, costly, initial condition.

Sketch so you know what to hit:
1. Set up a demon that never erases — it keeps its record and uses it to condition its next intervention — and argue that for finitely many cycles there's no erasure cost to point at, so the accounting story either locates the compensation elsewhere or gives up.
2. Argue that what actually runs out is the correlation structure: a record correlated with the gas is a low-entropy resource that gets consumed by being used.
3. General claim: the needed premise is that demon plus memory start uncorrelated with the target, which is the same kind of posit you need for a Boltzmann-style explanation of the second law, not a consequence of any principle about information.
