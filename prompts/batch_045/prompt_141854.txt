Our free will reading group (a Discord server, about thirty people, mostly self taught, we've been grinding through an anthology for nearly a year) has this one guy, Marek, who ends basically every thread with some variant of the same line: "you'd have to be your own cause before any of it could be up to you." He uses it as a full stop. The argument behind it is the Strawson flavoured "basic argument" we did a few weeks ago: you do what you do because of the way you are; to be ultimately responsible for what you do you'd have to be ultimately responsible for the way you are, at least in the mental respects that are doing the work; and you can't be, because any act of shaping yourself comes out of how you already were, so you just get a regress.

Two things got shut down with the one-liner and I want to know whether either of them actually died.

First, the slow-shaping reply: nobody is their own cause, but over years you can deliberately change your own character — habits, what you attend to, what situations you put yourself in — and that partial authorship is supposed to be what responsibility consists in. Marek: unless you authored all of it you authored none of it, and you can't author all of it.

Second, my amendment to that, which I've been chewing on for weeks: responsibility comes in degrees anyway, so I don't need to author my whole character, just enough of it — and "enough" might be very little, because a small authored change can get amplified. Force yourself to show up and act slightly differently, that changes which situations you end up in, which changes what you notice and what you care about, and ten years later a tiny authored nudge has a huge causal footprint on who you've become. So I authored a perturbation rather than a self, and the perturbation is load bearing. Marek: a nudge isn't authorship, and the nudge still came out of how you already were.

A third person in the group says the real escape route is that you only need to be responsible for the narrow set of mental features that actually produced this action, not your whole character, and Marek leaned on the same slogan against that too. Also relevant: one member keeps insisting the argument is supposed to be neutral about determinism, and that on the indeterminist version my self-shaping just turns into luck.

So: can you lay out, as a numbered list I can paste in the channel, the different precise claims that one-liner could be making, from the nearly trivial to the completely unhinged? For each: what exactly it says; whether it's trivial, substantive but defensible, question-begging, or false; and then the bit I actually care about — does it kill slow-shaping, does it kill my amplification version, does it kill the narrow-kernel move. Then tell me which reading Marek has to be committed to for his use of the line to do any work, whether that reading survives, and which reading he's most likely sliding to when I push him. If any of the readings turn on what "responsibility" is even supposed to be (desert vs answerability vs something thinner), flag that explicitly, since we have two members who think the whole thing is a confusion about praise and blame. And please be honest if one of my replies just doesn't work — I'd rather find out here than in the channel.
