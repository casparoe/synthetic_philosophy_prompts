I've drifted into holding something close to the dust-specks conclusion, and I want it stress tested before I say it out loud in a seminar. The version I hold isn't quite the usual one, so here it is.

Instead of one giant leap from a dust speck to fifty years of torture, build a chain of harms where each is only a bit worse than the one before: a speck in the eye for a second, a mildly annoying itch, a pinprick, a dull headache, a bad headache, a toothache, a migraine, a broken arm, a kidney stone, a day in solitary, a month of it, fifty years of torture. Premise: for every adjacent pair in that chain there is some finite number of instances of the lighter one that is at least as bad as one instance of the heavier. Chain them and you get some finite — possibly absurdly large — number of dust specks that is worse than the torture. Call it the substitution-chain argument. I think it's sound, and I think every way out costs more than the conclusion does: denying the premise at some step, positing incomparability, a lexical threshold, a special rule that sub-perceptual harms don't aggregate, giving up transitivity of worse-than, or going person-affecting.

So: what would count against it? Not a survey of the debate, please, and no reading list. A few things I specifically want:

Which step in the chain is actually the weakest link, and what would show that premise false rather than merely unappealing? If the conclusion is false, then there's a largest number of specks that isn't worse than the torture and a smallest that is — where is the cut-off, and is there any non-arbitrary story that locates it? Is there is no fact of the matter available as an answer here, and if it is, does it kill my argument or only the determinacy of its conclusion?

Does the argument get easier or harder if you keep everything inside one life — the same person getting one speck per year for a billion years, no bad moment and no accumulating memory — compared to spreading them one-per-person across a huge population? I suspect the interpersonal version is quietly leaning on something the intrapersonal one doesn't give me, and I want to know what.

Split the defeaters into ones a defender of my side would concede count against it and ones they'd rightly dismiss as just restating the intuition they're trying to explain. Rank the whole set from fatal to merely awkward. And be honest at the end about whether the view has been insulated: can anyone actually produce the number, is there any evidence that could bear on this at all, and if not, is that a problem for a claim of this kind or just what claims of this kind are like?

What would move me most personally: an account of why tiny harms fail to aggregate that doesn't lean on a threshold nobody can locate.
