I'm renovating a small kitchen and my contractor charges a flat $60 "revision fee" every time I change a materials decision once he's already started on it. I've now went back and forth four times and I'm sitting here at like 1am with the same three samples on the table: laminate, butcher block, quartz. The thing is, whenever I compare two of them head to head I know exactly which one I want — quartz over butcher block (it basically lasts forever), butcher block over laminate (it looks about ten times better), and laminate over quartz (it's less than half the price and the money genuinely matters to me right now). Each time my contractor has offered me a swap I've said yes and paid the $60, and I've ended up back with the sample I started with, $240 poorer. He's a smug guy and he says this proves I'm irrational: that being exploitable like this just *is* what irrationality is, and that if I'd picked one on day one and stuck to it I'd have the counter I want and $240 more.

I have a few half-formed comebacks and I can't tell which of them are definately any good. One: every swap looked clearly worth it to me at the time — I'd rather have quartz and pay $60 than be stuck with butcher block — and I could obviously just say no to the next one, he can't make me accept anything. So if each individual choice was fine when I made it, and I can always walk away, where exactly is the mistake supposed to be? Two: his profit doesn't seem to prove much by itself, since he'd also make $60 off me for a change that was a straight improvement — he's charging for revising, not for going in circles. Three: even granting I should have committed on day one, committing would have meant day-one me forcing week-three me to live with something week-three me didn't want, and I'm not sure that's obviously better. I also kind of like keeping my options open and am happy to pay something for that — is that just another taste, or is it part of the problem?

A friend from the one econ class I ever took says none of this is even a real issue: preferences aren't anything over and above what you actually pick, so if I chose the quartz then I wanted the quartz, and there's no deeper fact about what I "really" prefer for me to have gotten wrong. That feels like it dissolves the whole question but also like it's cheating somehow. I'd rather not just be told to make a pro/con list or to sleep on it — I want to know whether there's a genuine mistake here and what kind of mistake it is. Is my contractor right? And concretely: should I commit to one tonight and refuse every further swap, keep re-deciding as I go, or do something else entirely?
