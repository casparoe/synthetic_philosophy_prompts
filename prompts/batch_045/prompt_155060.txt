Hi — quick favour: I need a case built to order for my seminar on the precautionary principle, and I'm sick of the standard nanotech-and-GMO examples everyones already read.

Construct a scenario — one paragraph, realistic and contemporary, a hospital or a city council or a school board, not the fate of the species — in which the precautionary principle forbids an action that straightforward expected-utility maximisation recommends. The catch: the disagreement may not come from any of the things that normally do the work. Probabilities are known and agreed by everyone involved, so no ambiguity, no Knightian uncertainty, no "we just don't know yet." No outcome is catastrophic or irreversible. Utility is linear in money and in health. The same population bears the risk either way, so it isn't distributional. And dont get there via risk aversion, ambiguity aversion, or by quietly swapping in maximin. Whatever's left — the burden of proof, the trigger condition, the act/omission asymmetry, who counts as the proponent — has to be what carries the verdict.

One more constraint: applying the principle symmetrically, i.e. exercising precaution against not acting, must not simply reverse the verdict, so I don't get the standard precautionary-paradox shrug.

After the scenario, do the check: the verdict each of the two delivers and why it delivers it, the single feature doing the work, what happens if I remove that feature (does it collapse into plain cost-benefit analysis, or into the paradox?), and the best line of attack available to a defender of the principle who wants to say the case is rigged or misdescribes her view.

The point of the exercise is to find out whether the principle has any content left once the epistemic and stakes-based rationales are stripped out — so smuggling ambiguity or catastrophic downside back in counts as cheating, and so does stipulating the verdicts instead of deriving them from the two positions.
