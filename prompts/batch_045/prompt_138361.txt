hi — hoping you can help with something that's been bugging me at work and that I don't have a word for. I'm a field archaeologist, mostly commercial work, so I spend a lot of time reading write-ups of deposits where the interpretation is doing a lot more work than the finds.

First version of the thing. Pit, early iron age, contains a complete pot, some animal bone that shows no butchery marks, and three small polished stone objects nobody can find a use for. It goes in the report as "structured deposition, probably ceremonial". Next site, same region, same century: another pit, but this one is cut into a midden, the pot's smashed, the bone is mixed in with ordinary domestic rubbish. That one also goes in as ceremonial, because placing it with the refuse was itself the point. I have now watched both a careful deposit and a careless one get counted as evidence for the same conclusion, and the two reports cite each other.

Second version, which might be a different thing. The confidence seems to run backwards. The three odd stones: no parallels, no wear traces, therefore "clearly non-utilitarian, almost certainly ritual". If we'd found four hundred of them in a workshop with debitage, they'd be beads and they'd get one line. The less there is to compare an object to, the louder the claim about what it meant. Certainty is highest exactly where checking is hardest.

I can't tell if that's one phenomenon or two, and I don't know if either has a name. It isn't plain unfalsifiability — nobody says no possible find could refute the ceremonial reading, people do state conditions. It's that the conditions get restated at a different level each time: the object, then the deposit, then the site, then the whole region. And it isn't circular either, not really, because each step on its own looks fine and leans on something independent. It's the total that won't sit still.

So: is there an established concept for a category that holds its extension while flipping its own application criteria as you move up a level? Half-heard things I can't place: god of the gaps, "residual category", that business of a hypothesis being protected by tacking on exceptions, something about different processes leaving the same traces. Which of those actually fits, which only looks like it fits, and how would you tell this apart from the honest case where a category is just broad (tool, food waste, rubbish)? Please don't hand me a label and stop — I want to know where the edge is: what a genuine test of a ceremonial reading would look like, what kind of reading would be immune to this complaint, and if there really is no term for it, coin one and define it with a boundary case. And tell me whether my two versions are the same thing wearing different hats.
