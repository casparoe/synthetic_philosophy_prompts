After a career in experimental biology I have taken up philosophy in retirement, and I keep circling back to a discomfort with the literature on the evolution of cooperation. Fix the standard frame: two players, simultaneous choices, payoffs ordered T > R > P > S with 2R > T + S, so that defection dominates in the one-shot game while reciprocity, reputation or assortment can stabilise cooperation when the same pair is likely to meet again. My unease is not with any step in that argument but with what is being treated as the thing to be explained.

In my old discipline one never asked why a trait persisted without first fixing a null model, and it seems to me that this literature runs two different null models together without noticing. The first is a population of unconditional defectors who already interact: the game is on, as it were, and we ask why anyone ever cooperates within it. The second is a population in which the interaction itself is not yet in place, because organisms need not meet, or because seeking a partner and interacting carries costs of its own. Call these the defector baseline and the solitude baseline. Conditional strategies such as tit-for-tat, the shadow of the future, and the various mechanisms of assortment all look to me like answers relative to the first baseline; under the second, the prior question is why the interaction exists at all, and I suspect that question has a rather different structure.

Write an essay on whether the prisoner's dilemma, and the repeated prisoner's dilemma in particular, is the right formal frame for the evolution of cooperation, or whether its centrality has quietly distorted the field by taking the interaction as given. Weigh the case on both sides, make use of my two baselines if they help and set them aside if they do not, then reach a verdict and defend it; I would rather have a defended position I can argue with than a survey ending in the observation that both frameworks have their uses. Attach to each load-bearing claim a brief indication of how confident you are, and say plainly where your verdict rests on a stipulation about what is to count as cooperation in the first place. And if my baseline distinction is confused, or the controversy as I have set it up rests on a caricature of what theorists actually do, tell me so directly rather than answering the question politely on my terms.
