I've somehow ended up on our Faculty Senate's Governance Review Committee (philosophy, so I got voluntold), and I need to get a memo out before Monday's meeting. The issue is, as far as I can tell, a problem about rules that change the rules for changing rules, and nobody else on the committee seems to think that's the interesting part.

Here's the setup. Two years ago the Senate replaced the old Faculty Handbook with a Governance Charter. Article V of the Charter is the amendment article: any amendment needs a majority of senators present and voting, nothing is entrenched, no referendum required. Last spring, at a thinly attended meeting — 34 of 61 senators there, quorum is 30 — the Senate passed, 21 to 13, what everyone now calls the Stability Clause. It says that any future amendment to Article V itself requires a two-thirds vote of the entire Senate plus a ratifying referendum of the whole faculty, and — this is the bit causing the fight — it declares that it is not itself subject to further amendment except by that same supermajority-plus-referendum route. The minutes are unclear on whether two-thirds means of the full membership or of those present and voting.

So: 21 senators, about a third of us, appear to have made it so that 41 senators plus a faculty referendum — and faculty referendum turnout has never once hit 40% — are needed to touch Article V again. There is now a live proposal to restructure the committee system, which a clear majority of the Senate supports (call it 36 of 61) and which has no chance under the Stability Clause.

Half of us think the Clause was never validly adopted: a rule can't bootstrap itself out of the procedure that created it, or at least can't do that and bind everyone downstream, because a bare majority shouldn't be able to shrink the power of every later majority. The other half says that is exactly what an amendment is — the Senate had whatever authority the Charter gave it, and if we start voiding amendments we don't like, the Charter is just a suggestion. And one faction has now floated the obvious workaround: dissolve the Charter entirely and adopt a successor document by majority vote, on the theory that the Clause protects Article V of *this* Charter, not of whatever comes next. I have no idea whether that's a legitimate move or a transparent dodge, and I suspect the answer tells us something about what a rule even is across time.

What I want from you is the memo itself, 900–1200 words, addressed to the Senate:

1. A recommendation on the Stability Clause — in force, void, or something in between (if there's something in between, be concrete about what it is).
2. What follows for the live proposal.
3. A standing meta-rule for how the Charter should handle amendments to its own amendment rules from here on. I've been toying with an asymmetry — that tightening a threshold should cost more than loosening one — and with the thought that amendments which change *who has to say yes* (adding the faculty as a veto player, redefining the denominator) are a categorically different thing from amendments that move a number from 50% to 67%, and should be handled differently. Tell me if that distinction holds up.
4. The strongest version of the case against whatever you recommend, written so that it would actually survive contact with the people who disagree with me. I don't want my own view handed back to me.

Two constraints. Keep it in English a chemist and an associate dean can both read — no jargon that needs a seminar to unpack, and don't pad it with references to constitutional-law literature; I want the argument, not a reading list. (I do keep half-remembering something about courts voiding amendments that destroy a constitution's basic structure, which feels adjacent but maybe isn't — if it's doing something different from what I'm asking, say why in a sentence rather than building the memo around it.) And please don't rest on the fact that the Board of Trustees could override us anyway; I know that. I need an answer at the level of what the Senate is obligated to do, given that it is the body actually making the rules it is bound by.

I'll recieve it fine either way, but definitly get it to me by Sunday night if you can.
