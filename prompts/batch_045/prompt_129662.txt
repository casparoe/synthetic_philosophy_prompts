Help me think through a what-would-he-say question. Set-up first: 10,000 people each get a letter saying that a $10 million prize will be split equally among everyone who replies, but that if more than 1% of the recipients reply, nobody gets anything at all. Everyone is told the letters went to people selected for being good at this sort of reasoning, that there's no second round, and that there's no way to communicate with the others. (It's a Hofstadter-ish superrationality set-up: one-shot, no chance to build up any convention over time.)

What would David Hume say — Hume of the conventions, the bit about two men pulling the oars of a boat falling into a common rhythm by convention without ever having promised each other anything? Say he's been walked through how the payoffs work. Two questions: (1) does he have any resources that could tell a recipient what to do here, or is his whole story about convention tied to repeated interaction and a gradually built-up sense of common interest, so that he'd just predict the thing collapses; (2) would the "we were all picked because we reason well" detail count for anything with him, and if so in which direction — does he take it as a real basis for expecting the others to land on the same reasoning, or does he treat it as exactly the kind of self-flattering story people tell themselves about why they're special?

I'd rather have a committed answer with the reasoning than a survey of possible readings. Under 500 words, and no need for citations.
