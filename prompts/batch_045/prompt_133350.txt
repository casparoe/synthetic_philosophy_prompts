Analyse the removal of Terri Schiavo's feeding tube in Florida in 2005 as a case study for the distinction between doing and allowing harm — and, more narrowly, between killing and letting die. The facts I'll take as given: after a cardiac arrest in 1990 she was diagnosed as being in a persistent vegetative state; she was not terminally ill, and with the tube in place she could have lived for years; the tube was removed in March 2005 at the request of her husband acting as her guardian, after years of litigation and against the wishes of her parents, and she died of dehydration roughly two weeks later.

I don't want a general defence or critique of the distinction — I want it mapped onto this case. So: (i) identify the agent, given that the decision was spread across a guardian, a long series of state and federal courts, legislatures that intervened without changing the outcome, and the physician who actually removed the tube, and say whether 'doing' versus 'allowing' is even well defined once the act is distributed across that many hands; (ii) apply the two standard ways of drawing the line — the causal picture, on which a doing starts a lethal sequence while an allowing merely fails to stop one the patient's own condition is already producing, and the 'would have died anyway' baseline test — and say which side the removal falls on, including what to do with the fact that over the window in which dehydration kills she was not otherwise dying, while over a long enough window everyone is; (iii) say whether the distinction is doing any independent work here, or whether the load is carried by a prior judgement about her best interests and the value of her continued life, with the distinction reached for only because people disagree about that judgement; (iv) say whether the intending/foreseeing distinction would handle the case more cleanly; and (v) say what the episode does to the theory — does it confirm the distinction, is it an anomaly for it, or is it the kind of case the distinction was never built for?

Answer as exactly five numbered points, matching my five questions in order, at most two sentences each, with no introduction and no summing-up, in British English.
