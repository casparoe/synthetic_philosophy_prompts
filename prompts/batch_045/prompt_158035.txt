I am drafting a defence of lexical threshold negative utilitarianism (LTNU) and would like your help with one step in it. I have been stating the view as follows. There is a threshold level of well-being — the neutral point — such that (i) lifting someone from below that point up to it, or preventing them from falling below it, matters lexically more than any amount of improvement above it, and (ii) no sum of pleasures above the threshold can compensate for even the briefest episode of suffering below it.

My difficulty concerns how to motivate (i) and (ii). The strongest support, it seems to me, is the incommensurability of pleasure and pain. The two are phenomenologically distinct, admit no shared unit, and cannot be placed on a single cardinal, interpersonally comparable scale. So there is no quantity of pleasure of which it could be said that it outweighs, balances, or offsets an episode of pain. Crucially, this is not the claim that suffering is worse; it is the claim that the two are not the sort of thing that can be weighed against one another at all. Priority for the negative then follows as the only rational policy: where two outcomes are incomparable, the choice is settled by the pessimistic ordering, and that is exactly what lexical priority delivers. The threshold also becomes easy to place, since it need only be drawn at the phenomenological boundary between the pleasant and the unpleasant, rather than defended as a location on a scale that does not exist.

The case I want to apply all this to is the following. A health authority must choose between a programme that removes a year of severe chronic pain for a thousand people, who remain below the neutral point afterwards, and a programme that lifts ten million people who are already content from contentment to mild delight. I want to say that it must fund the first, and that it must say so not because the first outcome is better but because the second is not even a candidate for comparison.

Is this the right way to set the argument up? In particular, should the incommensurability claim be presented as entailing the lexical claim or as independent support for it, and how would you answer the critic who says that if pleasure and pain really did share a single cardinal scale, LTNU could not be formulated in the first place?
