I spent about thirty-five years fitting models to spectra, so I arrived at philosophy late and mostly through the worry about overfitting. Lately I've been reading about Bayesian confirmation theory and the so-called problem of old evidence, and I've ended up somewhere deflationary that I'd like stress-tested.

Roughly my view: the problem isn't really a problem for Bayes at all. It's the old novel-prediction-versus-accommodation point in a technical costume. Conditionalization stalls only because the probability of the evidence is already one; fix that by letting the background carry a tuning ledger for each theory — a list of the results the theory was built, tweaked, or parameter-fitted to reproduce — and confirmation then flows only from evidence that isn't on that list. General relativity getting the anomalous advance of Mercury's perihelion right is the sort of case I have in mind: not something the theory was engineered to match.

The bit I've added myself, and am least sure about, is that a ledger is a historical object, so it can be revised. If it turned out that some result everyone thought was derived blind had actually been fitted to — or the reverse — then on my account the confirming force of that evidence changes after the fact, with no change in the theory, the data, or the likelihoods. I half like that consequence and half find it wierd.

So: what would count against this? Give me the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones a defender of my sort of view should concede rather than wriggle out of. I care most about cases where a theory nailed old evidence beautifully and nobody thinks it was confirmed, and cases where a theory was visibly tuned and yet we still do count the fit as evidence — those are where I already have firm intuitions to check the view against. Also tell me honestly whether I've built something that can't lose: if the ledger move has qualified the position into unfalsifiability, say so, and whether that's defintely fatal for a view of this kind or just unattractive.
