ok so some background: I'm half Swedish, half American, and every christmas I end up in the same argument with both halves of my family — my American relatives think the Scandinavians have a pathology about success, my Swedish relatives think the Americans worship greed — and I've realised I don't actually know where I stand, I just argue whichever side I'm facing. So I sat down and wrote out what I think I believe, and I'd like you to audit whether it hangs together rather than tell me which side is right. Here's the list: (1) I'm broadly pro-celebration — societies that visibly admire and reward high achievers seem to get more of the things people want, and I don't want to live somewhere where standing out is embarrassing. (2) I think tall-poppy cutting and the Jantelagen "don't imagine you're anyone" reflex is, most of the time, envy moralised as egalitarianism, a tax on aspiration paid by everybody, and I want none of it in my politics. (3) But I also think a big slice of status competition is genuinely positional: my extra acclaim is somebody else's relative demotion, so it's an arms race that can leave everyone worse off, and I'm happy with institutions that damp that. (4) I don't think inequality of outcome is bad in itself — only when it comes from a rigged or unfair process. (5) I support much steeper progressive taxation than either country actually has, and I feel no guilt about it. (6) Here's the bit that's my own invention and that I'm most suspicious of, I haven't read it anywhere: a two-currency rule, be generous with praise and honours and public admiration because those are cheap and abundant, be stingy with money and power, celebrate the person and tax the reward. I use it to hold (1) and (5) together. (7) I think most large successes are hugely luck-dependent, which is a reason not to treat successful people as morally special, but not a reason to confiscate what they made.

What I want: which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? I have vague guesses and they're probably wrong so please don't treat them as hints — for instance I can't tell whether (3) is quietly handing me the justification for levelling that I disowned in (2), or whether the two are about different things entirely. I also can't tell if (3) and (4) are fighting about why inequality matters at all: if some inequality is bad because it's an arms race rather than because the process was unfair, then (4) looks false, and if (4) is true then (5) looks unmotivated. Two things I suspect might be fake tensions: (5) looks to my Swedish relatives exactly like tall-poppy cutting under a boring name, and (1) and (3) look like they say opposite things about admiration. Maybe both dissolve, maybe one doesn't.

Rank whatever real tensions you find by how severe they are, tell me for each what would have to give and what that would cost me, and then say which single revision would clean up the most. In particular I want to know whether my two-currency thing in (6) survives (3) at all — if praise is itself a positional good, being generous with it might just relocate the arms race rather than end it, and then (6) is doing no work. If it helps to have a case: my old department was deciding whether to spend a big donation on one named chair for its star researcher or on six junior fellowships, and I could argue myself into either position using this list within about a minute, which is usually a sign the list isn't doing any work.
