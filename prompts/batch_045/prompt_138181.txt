So I keep going round in circles on the lottery and preface paradoxes and what I want is a diagnosis, not a verdict.

Everyone agrees on the cases. In the lottery one you have overwhelming statistical grounds that your ticket loses and it still feels wrong to say you believe it loses. In the preface one the author has proper case-by-case evidence for every claim in the book and still rationally writes that he's sure at least one of them is false. Both have been chewed on for 60-odd years and the two camps haven't budged: one says keep the individual beliefs and give up the thought that rational belief survives conjunction, the other says keep the set consistent and deny that you can ever believe on base rates alone, because belief needs something more — evidence that discriminates, or a belief that wouldn't be wrong in nearby cases.

My hunch is that these aren't the same problem wearing two hats. I think the variable doing the work is whether the high probability comes from a statistical base rate or from case-specific evidence, and that each camp has quietly made one of those constitutive of rational belief without any argument for the choice. To test that I've been playing with a hybrid: an author with genuine case-specific evidence for each of 100 claims who also has a statistical reason of exactly the same strength that at least one of them is false. Call it the ticketted preface. Does it separate the camps, or does it just reproduce the standoff one level up?

So: is my hypothesis right, is this a verbal dispute about 'rational belief', is it a hidden disagreement about what belief is for (tracking truth versus guiding inquiry and assertion), or two questions under one name? Diagnose it off what each side actually commits to, and tell me honestly whether anything would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, an experiment on folk ascriptions, or just dropping the question.

Give me the prose diagnosis first, then a table with these columns: axis of disagreement | what the fix-the-belief camp says | what the fix-the-set camp says | kind of disagreement (substantive, verbal, different questions, hidden empirical, or bedrock intuition) | what would count as progress. One row per axis, plus a final row for what the ticketted preface does or doesn't show. British English throughout, and feel free to tell me my hunch is wrong.
