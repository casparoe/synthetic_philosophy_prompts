I am writing an interactive sidebar for a science magazine on the Orwellian–Stalinesque distinction, and I need a decision tree that sorts a scientifically literate reader with no philosophy training into a position on what actually happens in the colour phi phenomenon: two brief flashes, red at one location and then green at a nearby one, timed so that observers report a single dot travelling between them and turning from red to green about two-thirds of the way along.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of isms: fix the questions and the combinations of answers they permit first, then locate the actual positions in that space, and mark any combination nobody occupies with a one-line note on whether it is empty because incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Eight to ten branch nodes, six to eight leaves.

Every node must be a concrete case or a claim about one, phrased so a reader can answer without knowing where it leads. No node may use the words Orwellian, Stalinesque, representationalism, intentionalism or qualia, and none may ask whether consciousness is such-and-such. These cases must all be used: the standard two-flash set-up; a variant in which observers reliably report the dot passing through a hue that neither flash contained; a variant in which the second flash is cut off after 20 ms and replaced by a mask, so nothing is ever seen at the second location, yet the mid-path change is still reported; a variant with a speeded keypress at the moment of the change whose latency places registration of the change after the second flash begins; a hypothetical TMS result in which a pulse over hMT+ timed to the mid-point abolishes the motion report but not the colour-change report, while a pulse over V4 does the reverse; and a variant in which the same observers, asked not what they saw but what was on the screen, correctly reply that the first dot was red and never turned green. Treat these as stipulations, not as published findings to be checked.

The leaves must include, under whatever names you prefer: the change was experienced and then the record was overwritten; the change was never experienced and the report was inserted after the fact; there is no determinate fact of the matter about when anything was experienced; the question dissolves because experience just is the final representational state; the answer is feature-relative, so motion and colour are revised differently; and the whole dispute is empirical, settled by identifying the neural vehicle of the change-representation and its timing. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate, and say what further commitment, rather than what further question, would separate them.

Begin with the first branch node itself, with no preamble, no framing paragraph and no restatement of this brief, and put every note on coverage, empty combinations and unseparable leaves after the tree. Write in British English throughout.
