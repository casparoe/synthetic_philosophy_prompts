Here's where we're stuck. Everyone agrees on the cases: a glider moving across a cellular automaton grid, a chess engine's evaluation of a position, a thermostat's "goal," the outline of a shadow, constellations, the digits of pi versus an encrypted bitstream, a neural net's learned internal features. Everyone agrees that some of those do causal work (the glider's future positions depend on its being a glider) and some don't (the shadow doesn't make anything happen), and everyone agrees on the basic arithmetic of compression: describing the glider's trajectory in glider-talk is shorter than listing cell states. The two camps nonetheless haven't budged. The pattern realists say reality just is compressibility — there's something to be right or wrong about, you can't fit a parabola to noise, so "real" earns its keep. The projectivists say compressibility is doubly relative, to a vocabulary and to whoever is doing the compressing with whatever measurement budget, so "real" adds nothing to "cheap for us," and the honest move is to drop the metaphysics and talk about prediction cost. Nobody disputes a single case, and nobody moves.

My hypothesis, which I want you to test rather than nod along with, is that there are three or four different questions under one heading, possibly with a hidden formal question doing the work. First, a straight verbal dispute about "real." Second, a real but unstated question about whether compression is meant in raw, idealized terms or in resource-bounded terms — where you count computation time, memory, how expensive the measurements are, and how far into the future the pattern stays reliable — and if it's the resource-bounded version, whether the whole thing becomes an empirical question about which predictors actually exist, which would at least make it the kind of thing that could be settled. Third, a disagreement about what a stance has to earn its keep by: successful prediction, explanatory unification, or giving you a handle for intervening and controlling the system. Fourth, maybe bedrock and unarguable: whether a question about what is real can be indexed to an agent at all. I've also been playing with a criterion of my own — call it the budget-relative exploitability test: a pattern is real-for-a-predictor if predicting via the pattern beats the best available non-pattern predictor on total cost, where cost is measurement plus computation plus how long the pattern keeps working — and I can't tell whether this dissolves the argument or just relocates it one level down, so that the fight becomes one about whose cost metric and whose budget instead of about patterns.
