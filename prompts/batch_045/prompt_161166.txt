I help run my school's astronomy club, and I'm putting together a one-page handout called "Where is everybody?" that ends in a decision tree. The draft I have now is useless: it asks how likely you think alien life is, and the leaves are "optimist," "pessimist," and "undecided." I want something that sorts readers by which step in the argument they actually reject, not by how hopeful they feel.

Start everyone from two premises nobody in the club disputes: the galaxy is very old and contains an enormous number of stars, and we have so far detected no signal or artifact that we can confidently attribute to another technological civilization. From there, build a tree of five or six questions, each phrased so that someone who has never heard any of the labels can answer it, ending in eight to ten leaves.

The distinctions I care about having as branch nodes: whether the missing link is in how often life or intelligence arises, in how long technological civilizations last, in whether we would receive or even recognize a signal from one, or in timing — that other civilizations may have risen and fallen long before we existed rather than never having existed at all. Please include one node on how much we are entitled to generalize from Earth as a single sample, one on whether a civilization sufficiently unlike us would register as a civilization to us, and one on how much weight the silence bears given how little of the sky, the spectrum, and the relevant timescales we have actually examined.

Name each leaf where a name is in common use, mark a leaf "unoccupied" where nobody defends that combination, and say in one sentence whether the combination is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Finally, flag any two leaves the tree cannot separate because they predict the same observations, and say what evidence, if any, would tell them apart.
