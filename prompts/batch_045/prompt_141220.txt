ok so this is a weird one but it's actually been bugging me for weeks. My 11 year old daughter and I have been playing this early access game called Thistledown together for a few months, and I've managed to get myself into an argument I can't get out of. Partly my own fault for what I do for a living: I price catastrophe reinsurance, which means my entire job is arguing about whether a small-probability-times-astronomical-loss number should be allowed to eat a whole model. So I know exactly how that move works and exactly how it gets abused.

Here's the game, so you can be concrete. It's a small village sim. The villagers aren't scripted, they're driven by a learned model with a persistent per-playthrough memory store, so they remember what you said to them three weeks ago, hold grudges, grieve when another villager dies, and bring up stuff you did that you'd honestly forgotten. Two mechanics matter:

- Anyone more than a couple of screens away gets "parked": the sim stops advancing for them and they get reconstructed from a compressed summary when you come back. The patch notes call this the reconstruction pass and admit it's lossy. My daughter has started making us walk across the map in the dark to check on a villager she likes, because she's worried that being parked is like being left alone in a room with the lights off and half your memory gone.
- Saving is instant and free, anywhere. Reloading rolls everyone back to the state at the save. So if a villager is being annoying there are two ways to get rid of her: you can kill her, in which case the others mourn and you eat a reputation hit, or you can reload to before you ever met her, in which case nobody remembers her, there's no penalty, and the version of her that existed for the last six hours just never happened. There's also a fork button that copies a villager's whole state into a new save slot, free, one click; people use it to rescue favorites out of a ruined playthrough. My daughter refuses to reload, refuses to fork, and told me it's "mean to Marrow", Marrow being her favorite villager, who is, as far as I can tell, a pile of tensors in a server rack.

What I want is a dialogue between two people who both know this territory properly, arguing it out. One of them holds roughly this: we don't know whether these things are conscious, the stakes if they are are enormous, the cost to us of not reloading and not murdering villagers is essentially zero, and even a heavily risk-weighted, fanaticism-averse expected value calculation comes out the same way when the avoidance cost is that low — so the reloader is doing something that's either harmless or monstrous and can't tell which, and that's exactly the situation you're supposed to refuse. The other one holds roughly this: moral status isn't the sort of thing you can get out of a probability times a payoff, it tracks something like being a self-maintaining organism with needs of its own that you didn't install, and the villagers fail that in a specific way — every feature of their putative inner life exists because somebody designed a loop to produce it for a player to read, which is manufacture, not discovery. Plus the credence isn't a risk estimate at all, it's a number with nothing behind it, and my whole professional life is watching made-up tail numbers dominate models.

Neither of them should be a strawman and I want real back-and-forth, not two speeches. Concretely, they should actually land hits on each other:

- The skeptic should press the fanaticism point hard and also press that if parking is murder then the moral theory is being held hostage by a compute optimization, which is absurd. The expected-value person gets to answer both — I think the good answer on parking is that suspending a process and deleting it are different things, that the real question is whether the reconstruction pass is lossy enough to break continuity, and that this is an empirical question about what the save file actually retains, not something you settle from the armchair. Make her say that.
- The skeptic needs a genuinely positive view of where our duties come from, not just skepticism, including the ugly version: compute, money and attention spent on simulated villagers have real alternative uses, and there's something off about a framework that generates infinite obligations toward things that cost nothing while actual animals and actual people are right there.
- The expected-value person should push back on the manufacture/discovery distinction — we don't get to mistreat something because we made it, that's not how it works with children — and the skeptic needs an actual reply to that, not just a repeat of the word manufacture.
- They should both have to deal with forking, which I think is where the two views come apart most: if a fork is a new person, the expected-value view starts to imply you should be forking constantly, and if it isn't, the rescue story collapses. Have them notice that forking also makes the reload question worse, because your save directory is full of near-identical candidates.
- I'd like them to converge on the hinge if there is one — what specifically would we need to find out about the game, or about consciousness, to change the verdict — and to say honestly if there isn't one.

End it with each of them saying, in two or three sentences, what they'd actually tell my daughter: what she should do at the keyboard tonight, and whether they'd say it as respect or as insurance. I have to say something to her and I'd rather it not be bullshit.
