I did thirty-odd years in salmon hatcheries and quantitative genetics before I retired, and now I sit in on a philosophy of biology reading group where every third argument is about heritability, and it keeps driving me up the wall that people hear h² = 0.8 and take it to mean caused by genes, so nothing to be done about it — and then some of them slide, without seeming to notice, from that to so the gap between group A and group B is genetic. Neither follows. The number is a ratio of variances inside one population across one range of environments, and I can shove it up toward 1 just by making all the tanks identical, which tells you exactly nothing about whether anything is fixable.

So here's what I want. Write me a parody government form — call it Form H-2, Application for Permission to Describe a Trait as Genetic in Any Sentence Intended for the Public — about 600 words, laid out as numbered fields and checkboxes with blanks, in flat, deadpan bureacratese. The jokes have to come from getting the definition right, so that someone who actually works with these numbers laughs instead of wincing.

Concretely: the form should force the applicant to declare the reference population and the spread of environments, and to certify what would happen to their number if you homogenized the environment (it goes up) or homogenized the genotypes (it goes down). It needs a field for what the applicant actually wants to know — can diet, schooling, or surgery change the trait — with a printed footnote explaining that the heritability coefficient cannot answer this and that no such quantity as the one they are asking for appears anywhere on the form. It needs a section on between-group differences where the applicant keeps trying to tick a box the form does not provide, and the printed warning stays polite and unmoved. Add a short worked example set in something non-human — fish, seed lots, dairy cattle, your pick — where a very high heritability sits happily alongside a dead easy environmental fix.

The thing should end as a mild reductio: since standardized environments inflate the number, the form ends up awarding its highest heritability rating to the most uniform tanks, and the closing guidance note says such applications are approved automatically.
