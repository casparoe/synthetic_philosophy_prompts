I am working through Wittgenstein's On Certainty and trying to state, without metaphor, what makes a proposition a hinge rather than a merely very well confirmed empirical claim. Below are my verdicts, arrived at case by case rather than derived from any account I hold.

1. That I have two hands: hinge.
2. That the earth existed for a long time before I was born: hinge.
3. That my name is the one I have always used: a hinge for me, even though it could be settled by a birth certificate and is, for you, an ordinary empirical matter.
4. That water boils at 100 degrees Celsius at sea level: not a hinge, since we have a procedure for settling it and we cite readings as reasons.
5. That the river has always flowed past the mill: not a hinge, merely extremely well confirmed, even though everyone in the village accepts it, nobody has checked, and nobody would think of checking.
6. That our senses are broadly reliable: hinge. That the instruments in this lab are calibrated: not a hinge.
7. That I am in pain right now: not a hinge, being closer to an avowal than to a standing commitment, although for me it is as groundless and as stable as anything in 1 or 2.
8. What a committed conspiracist treats as fixed, namely that the official account of any disaster is a cover-up: not a hinge, only dogmatic; and yet for him nothing would count as evidence against it, in the way nothing counts as evidence against a hinge.
9. That the sun travels around a stationary earth: a hinge in a medieval village and not one now, with the heliocentric claim that displaced it being an established result rather than a hinge either.

My tentative principle is that a proposition is a hinge for a community just in case the community has no procedure for settling it and its inquiries presuppose it. That seems to count 8 as a hinge and to come out wrong on 7 and 5, but I would like to keep verdicts 3, 8 and 9 if any account allows it.

Can you state one principle, or two rival candidates if the choice is genuinely forced, that captures as many of these verdicts as possible, naming the case that motivates each clause, and then say plainly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate and why those are the ones that ought to be abandoned? Please also apply it to two cases I have not listed, namely that there are other minds and that I am not now dreaming. Finally, I would want the account to specify what attitude we bear to hinges, since I find myself wanting to say both that Moore did know he had hands and that knowledge requires grounds, and I suspect the principle will have to dissolve one of those commitments.
