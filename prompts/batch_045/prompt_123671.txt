I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about emergence in science, and I think the only honest way to do it is to dump my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and see if anything covers them. So here goes, no particular order.

- Temperature of a gas is mean kinetic energy of the molecules. Reduced, done, and the micro story basically swallowed the macro word — nobody needs "temperature" as a separate posit. Not emergent.
- Transparency and liquidity of water. Also reduced, just a nightmare to actually compute. Hard is not the same as emergent; I'm confident about that one.
- The wave that goes round a stadium. Not a thing. Just a pattern over people standing up in sequence. I don't feel any pull to call it emergent.
- A phantom traffic jam that travels backwards while every car is moving forwards. This one I do want to call emergent. It has its own kinematics, it survives any kind of vehicle, and — this is the bit I lean on — the jam makes her brake. It acts back on its own parts.
- The Second Law. Micro dynamics are time-symmetric, the arrow isn't written in the parts at all, it needs something about the boundary condition. Emergent, and this feels like the deepest case on my list.
- The fact that a fluid near its critical point and a magnet near its critical point share a critical exponent — wildly different parts, same macro number. I call this emergent too, but for what feels like the opposite reason from the traffic jam: the details don't matter at all. That makes me suspicious I'm using one word for two things.
- Genes. Molecular biology didn't eliminate genetics; "gene for" talk still does explanatory work you can't do in the vocabulary of base pairs. So: autonomous. But I also don't want to say genes are something over and above the DNA, which feels like it contradicts the previous sentence.
- My own toy sim: 10k agents, three rules, a business cycle shows up. Perturb the rules a dozen ways, the cycle comes back. But freeze a random 5% of the agents and it vanishes completely. I want to call it emergent and the fragility makes me think I'm looking at an artifact of my own code.
- The one I flip-flop on: a glider (or a self-copying loop) in Conway's Game of Life. It persists, collides, does things to other patterns. But it's one inference away from the update rule, so calling it emergent feels like cheating — and calling it "just a pattern" like the stadium wave feels like it throws away something real.

The pair I genuinely can't reconcile: I think the multiple-realizability argument is convincing when it's aimed at pain — pain isn't a brain state because it could be built out of anything. But a door can be built out of anything too, in infinitely many ways, and I don't think door-hood is emergent for a second. What's the difference supposed to be? And behind that: I say "the jam made her brake" and I also say nothing macro ever does anything over and above the parts. Those can't both stay in the form I've stated them.

My tentative principle, for what it's worth: M is emergent relative to a micro story iff (a) no difference in M without a difference in the parts, (b) substrate-neutrality — the laws of M hold however you build it, (c) the dynamics of M can be stated without quantifying over the parts, (d) my "replug test": you can unplug the micro story, plug in a different one, and the same macro story keeps working, and (e) M makes a difference to what the parts do.

Problem I can already see: temperature passes (b), (c) and (d) and I still refuse to call it emergent. So either the principle needs a clause it doesn't have — something like "no bridge law available", or "no derivation of the macro from the micro" — or my temperature verdict has to go. Repair it, or tell me which clause to cut and what that costs me.

I'd rather get two or three candidate principles with their prices attached than one tidy one that quietly drops a case. For each: say which case motivates each clause, and be straight about which of my verdicts it can't save. Then tell me what the best candidate says about three I haven't listed: a bank's "risk appetite" causing a crash, a trophic cascade after wolves get reintroduced, and a capability in a neural net that shows up all at once past some training scale and then generalizes.
