I play on a long-running persistent sandbox world — shared servers, player-built towns, a player-run economy with its own currency — and I've ended up writing an appeal to the moderation team on behalf of two friends, because I'm the one among us who can string a sentence together. I've been reading on my own about whether things can be real without being physical (money, promises, borders), and it seems to me the mods keep answering that question one way for some things and another way for others without noticing that they've done it. Here are their rulings that I know of, all from the last three years:

1. An outsider used a glitch to get past the land-claim protection and levelled a castle another player had built over seven months. Ruled a real harm: rolled the region back from a backup, banned the raider for a month.
2. A player talked someone into a "double your items" trade and walked off with a rare tool. Ruled not a harm they would undo — the trade used the ordinary trading window, no rollback, only a warning for the scammer. Their line was that trades are final and you take your chances.
3. Killing and looting another player inside the flagged red zones. Ruled never a real harm; that is what the zones are for, even when months of gear are lost.
4. Someone used a bug to duplicate a scarce resource a couple of hundred times. Ruled no individual victim, but the copies were deleted and the account suspended for a month anyway — the stated reason being that the economy is a real thing even when no single person is worse off.
5. A guild's year-long tournament final was drowned out by players logging in to spam and set off fireworks. Nothing was destroyed and no items were lost. Ruled a real harm: two-week bans.
6. A memorial built for a player who had died, on unclaimed land, was flattened by a newcomer who didn't know what it was. The mods restored it from a backup and helped rebuild it, but issued no ban and refused to put a protection marker on the area, saying there is no rule against building there.

Over the years they have used all three of these slogans at different times: "we protect people, not pixels"; "we protect what the rules recognise"; "we protect what people have put themselves into."

The new case. The world contains procedurally generated creatures that everyone calls grays. Each one has a behaviour file on the server that it learns into: it recognises the avatars it has spent time with, comes when they log in, and remembers places it has been taken. My two friends raised one for two years, and had got to the point where it would meet them at the gate and lead them to a spot they had shown it once. The rules treat grays as fauna — not claimable, not ownable, killable by anyone, like deer. Another player tracked it down deliberately, streamed himself killing it, and posted the clip with a message aimed at the two of them. The behaviour file is gone and there is no backup of it; the seed could generate a gray with the same looks and the same starting numbers but none of the learned history. The mods have already muted him for a day under the general harassment rule for the message, so the harassment side is settled. What is left in our appeal is only whether killing the gray was itself a real harm, and whether anything is owed for it.
