ok, so I'm putting together a one page handout for a grad reading group on prioritarianism — quick recap in case terminology drifts: prioritarianism says a benefit of a fixed size does more moral good the worse off the person getting it is, so you add up welfare but weight worse-off lives more heavily. The leveling-down objection is that once the weights care about the shape of the distribution and not just about who gains, the view can rank a world where everybody is worse off as better than one where some people are better off and nobody is worse off — the usual illustration being that if you can't restore sight to the blind, blinding the sighted improves the distribution by the lights of the view, which strikes most people as monstrous and also a bit pointless.

I've got eight families of reply and I want them tiered rather than discussed. Bite the bullet and say yes, a more equal but lower-welfare world can be better and our repugnance is just not evidence. The pluralist sidestep: keep the weighted sum as one value but say the tug we feel is really the tug of equality as a separate relational value, so the view is a package deal and levelling-down repugnance is one input among several rather than a refutation. The silence reply: prioritarianism is only a rule for weighing gains against gains, so when there is no gain to distribute the view has literally nothing to say, and what the objection is really tracking is the wrongness of a pointless harming, not betterness. The person-affecting restriction: a change counts as an improvement only if it is an improvement for somebody, so the weights order cases but never manufacture a verdict on their own. The ex ante move: evaluate prospects rather than outcomes, weight the chances of worse-off people more heavily, and the objection gets relocated rather than dissolved. Sufficientism: heavy priority only below a threshold of enough, so once everyone is above the line there is nothing left for levelling down to be for. Then three I've been playing with myself and haven't seen anywhere, so treat them as my inventions rather than as things with a literature: (a) feasibility-relative weighting, where a person's weight depends not on how well off they are in absolute terms but on how far below the best life that was actually feasible for that particular person they ended up — on which taking from the well off does nothing for the badly off, since their own feasible best is unchanged; (b) the serialization reply, that the intuition is inflated by the one-shot framing and that if the very same total loss arrives in thousands of imperceptible steps spread over years or across thousands of people the repugnance drains away, so the intuition is an artifact of how the case is presented and should get less weight; (c) the same-currency restriction, that the intuition is fierce when the loss is in exactly the dimension in which the badly off are badly off and much weaker when it isn't, so the objection should be confined to same-currency cases and the rest treated as plain old harming.

What I want: put these into tiers of how much real dialectical work they do against someone who already finds levelling down obviously repugnant — tier it however seems honest, but say in one sentence per item why it lands where it does. Give each a 0–10 on two separate scales as well: how much sting it takes out for that person, and how much of what made prioritarianism attractive in the first place is still standing afterwards. Then the part I actually care about: tell me what would change your mind about the ordering. Which item's rank is most hostage to my three invented replies being any good, which would jump a tier if it turned out the levelling-down intuition survives both the serialization test and the different-currency test intact, and where specifically you think your own ranking is most likely to be wrong — is it a bad read of the ex ante move, is it that you're scoring cost in a way I haven't told you how to score, is it something about whether the blind/sighted case is doing more work in your thinking than it should. Don't send me to papers, I want your own ranking and your own sense of where it's fragile. I've been staring at this to long and every ordering I write down starts looking arbitrary by the second reading.
