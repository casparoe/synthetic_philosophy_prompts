Hey — I'm a journalist. I cover science and ideas for a daily, and I also sit on our standards desk, so a solid chunk of my week goes into arguing about indvidual words. That's the background for what I need.

We're drafting a house rule for labeling mass atrocity — internally we call it the ladder. Rungs, roughly: massacre, atrocity, crimes against humanity, ethnic cleansing, genocide. Each rung carries (a) an evidentiary threshold, (b) a rule about who has to have said the word first before we can use it unattributed — a court, a UN inquiry, two named human-rights organizations, a government? — and (c) a downstream consequence, meaning which rung gets it on the front page, which gets a planned follow-up, which gets a sidebar explaining the word itself. The ladder works fine for the lower rungs. It falls apart at the top one, and I think that's because I only half-undersand the concept up there.

So: restate Lemkin's genocide in desk vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. My side I'm completely fluent in — news peg, lede, attribution, "allegedly," balance and false balance, news values, hard versus soft news, correction-and-retraction norms, the reflex that we don't use the word until a court does, the wall between news and opinion copy, and the plain fact that a story needs an event and a named person to work at all. On Lemkin's side I'm a well-read generalist: I know he minted the term, that he meant it to cover a composite of techniques aimed at a group as such and not just killing, and that the intent element is where all the difficulty lives.

Then I want the residue report, in three buckets: what maps cleanly onto an evidence-and-labeling problem, what fits only if I bend it, and what has no expression in desk vocabulary whatsoever. My guesses at the untranslatable parts: the victim here is a collectivity, but my stories need an individual face; most of the techniques he actually cared about — closing the schools, killing the language, breaking up the economic base — are non-events, so they generate no peg; and the concept is built to fire before the thing is finished, while every norm I have is built to fire only after someone authoritative has ruled. Tell me whether those are the right guesses and, more usefully, what I've left out.

Then the verdict on the residue. Is what won't translate a defect in newsroom vocabulary — something we genuinely can't say — or is it a sign that the concept is doing less work in a headline than we pretend it is? And concretely: should the top rung of my ladder exist at all, or should the rule be written so that it names the techniques and leaves the g-word to quoted sources?
