I've been reading LessWrong and the blogs adjacent to it for something like fifteen years, and next month I'm giving a talk to a meetup whose demographics are almost exactly that readership: mostly self-described consequentialists, maybe a quarter of them working in or adjacent to effective altruism, several with decision-theory or economics training, a couple of committed longtermists, one or two people who take contractualist and person-affecting views seriously, and essentially nobody with any appetite for rights-talk. Appeals to intuition will land badly. Expected-value framing, Harsanyi-style aggregation results, and the repugnant conclusion will all be in the room by minute two.

The thesis I want to defend is not that utilitarianism is false. It is that the separateness-of-persons objection has been conducted at the wrong level of description, and that once it is moved to the right one it stops being a refutation of aggregation and becomes a parameter.

A sketch of the argument, so you can aim objections at it: (i) what the objection turns on is not the number of persons but a structural feature I'll call non-fusion — the fact that goods located in distinct loci of agency do not fuse into a single larger good that anyone enjoys, even when those loci are unified in every other respect; (ii) this feature appears where there is no second person, in fission and gradual-replacement cases and, on a reductionist view of personal identity, in the relation between earlier and later stages of a single life; (iii) it fails to appear where there are two persons, if the case is built so that there is no fact of the matter about whose good is whose; so persons is a proxy for the property rather than the property; (iv) the upshot is that the dispute is best modeled as a dispute about a single scalar, the substitution rate at which a gain at one locus may be traded for a loss at another, with ordinary total utilitarianism as the rate-one limit and no-aggregation views as the rate-zero limit, and with intermediate rates being coherent rather than evasive.
