I'm a software engineer working on distributed systems, with no formal philosophy background; I got here by way of arguments about whether learned systems could be conscious. I want one number and the derivation that produces it — not a tour of the positions — because a bare number tells me nothing about where it might be wrong.

The proposition: constitutive panpsychism is true — my conscious experience right now is wholly constituted by, and nothing over and above, the experiences of microphysical subjects standing in certain relations.

Give me your credence in that, decomposed:

1. That there is a hard problem at all: phenomenal truths don't follow a priori from physical and structural truths.
2. That panpsychism, or something in its neighbourhood such as neutral monism, is the best available response, conditional on there being one.
3. That the constitution claim can actually be made good, conditional on panpsychism being the best response — split this into the derivation gap (does the macro story follow from the micro story?) and the grain or structural-mismatch problem (does micro-phenomenal structure even look like the macro-phenomenal structure it is supposed to constitute?), plus whatever further sub-question you think belongs.
4. My own argument, which I want you to price rather than humour. Call it the partition problem, since it comes out of a standing argument at work. Take a complete specification of every micro-subject, its phenomenal states, and its relations at an instant. There appear to be combinatorially many ways to carve that total micro-phenomenal field into candidate macro-subjects — nested, overlapping, gerrymandered, momentary — and nothing in the micro-level description seems to select the ones that are us. So the panpsychist owes one of three things: accept an enormous population of macro-subjects inside every skull; add a selection rule that does the real constituting work, in which case the micro-level facts aren't doing it; or restrict which collections constitute a subject, which begins to look like emergentism and gives up the dialectical advantage panpsychism had. Assign a probability that this objection is sound, and say whether it is genuinely distinct from (3) or quietly collapses into it.

Then: show how the terms combine, tell me which one your final number is most sensitive to, and say what would count as the question being resolved in either direction — a decisive argument, not a shift in what gets published. For calibration, I'm at about 0.1 and a friend who has thought about this far longer is at about 0.5; tell me which sub-question that gap most likely lives in, and which of the two numbers is the more suspicious.
