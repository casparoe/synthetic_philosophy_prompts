I have been working through the pragmatist accounts of truth with a small reading group, and I keep tripping over a configuration that I cannot locate in the literature. I would be grateful for help identifying it and, if it has no name, for a definition tight enough to have boundary cases.

The configuration, in two instances.

First. Two financial analysts are each given the same two-hundred-page dossier on a company that failed: every document, in a different shuffled order. Each is required to write an interim conclusion after every twenty pages and a final verdict at the end. They endorse incompatible verdicts. They then exchange dossiers and re-read the other's pages in the other's order, and neither can recover the other's reading: the memo that struck one as a decisive admission reads to the other as boilerplate, and placing it after the other pages does not restore the force it had on first encounter. What has been fixed is not their confidence but which proposition they take the body of documents to support; each is now maximally confident, and each finds the other's certainty unintelligible.

Second. Two experienced clinicians are shown the same set of images from a long and ambiguous case, again in different sequences, again with a written impression after each block. They reach different diagnoses, and a joint review of the whole set in either sequence does not move them; the later images were already seen as confirmations or as puzzles, and that seeing cannot be undone by seeing them again.

What I think I am not describing: the familiar order effects on judgment, where the same verdict is reached with a shifted threshold or a distorted confidence. Here the divergence is in content, and it survives full disclosure of everything both parties saw. Nor is it a difference of prior commitments brought to the material: had the pages or the images arrived in the same order, the two would have converged.

One member of the group says this is only the well-known fact that sequential belief revision need not commute, and that it therefore has nothing special to say about truth. I am not convinced, because of how it bears on the pragmatists. Peirce's way of cashing out truth in terms of what inquiry is fated to settle on in the limit looks committed to saying that this order-sensitivity washes out, that the settled opinion is a fixed point independent of the route taken to it. James's looser talk of a belief's practical value looks as if it has room for the divergence, but I cannot tell whether that room is his pluralism taken seriously or a sign that the account has stopped being an account of truth.

So my questions. Does this configuration have an established name, in the pragmatist literature or in epistemology generally? If it does, I need more than the label: how is it normally stated, and is it normally taken to be an objection to convergentist accounts of inquiry? If it has no name, please coin one and define it; say how it differs from order effects on judgment, from underdetermination of theory by total evidence, from the non-commutativity of belief revision, and from the radical incomparability discussed under the heading of incommensurability; and specify one case that falls just outside it, so that I can check whether I have drawn the boundaries where I think I have.
