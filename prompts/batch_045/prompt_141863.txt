I'm in a graduate reading group that has spent the term on Wittgenstein's late notes on certainty and on the hinge epistemology that has grown out of them, and I'm leading next week's session, which has turned into a fight about an image rather than about an argument. Please answer as a professional philosopher who works in contemporary epistemology and has taught these notebooks: take a moment before you write to sketch out the competing mappings on paper, and only then draw your conclusions.

Here is the situation. Almost everyone in the literature, and the people in my group on both sides, leans on Wittgenstein's comparison of the propositions that are exempt from doubt to the hinge on which a door turns. One member of the group uses it to do three jobs: first, to show that hinges cannot be true or false and so are not beliefs and are not rationally appraisable, which is supposed to get the view off the hook of sanctioning arbitrary dogma (if there is nothing to appraise, there is nothing we are being unreasonable about); second, to show that the class of hinges is small, local and mostly invisible, so the objection that hinge epistemology absurdly multiplies certainties misfires; and third, to underwrite the claim that the whole picture is not epistemic relativism, because a door does not swing freely just because it hangs on a hinge. His opponent in the group reaches for a different image from the same notebooks, the bed of a river whose banks are hard rock in places and shifting sand and shingle in others, and uses it to argue the opposite: that what is exempt from doubt changes over time, that there is no clean line between hinge and ordinary empirical proposition, and that the view therefore is a form of relativism with a reassuring name. A third person, trying to broker it, has floated an image of her own that I think is new to this debate: hinge commitments, she says, are like the ballast in a ship's hold. Ballast is not cargo, because it is not there to be delivered anywhere; it is not part of the hull, but it is not merely inside the ship either, since without it the vessel capsizes; it can be pumped out and re-stowed mid-voyage, though doing so is a delicate operation and not something the crew does on a whim; what counts as ballast depends on the vessel and the voyage; and where the ballast currently sits is not something anyone on board normally thinks about at all. She thinks this captures both the fixedness and the changeability and so dissolves the disagreement.

I don't trust that it does, and I don't even know whether my two combatants are disagreeing about the notebooks or just about which picture each of them is picturing. What I want is an audit, not a survey. Give me the correspondence table explicitly, with the three images side by side and a row each for what plays the role of the hinge, of the doubts and questions that turn on it, of the person who has the certainties, and of whatever corresponds to the door, the water and the ship. Then separate the disanalogies that matter for the three jobs listed above from the ones that are merely decorative: a door hinge is manufactured for a purpose and is a single discrete point, a riverbed is made of the same material as the sediment it channels and has no sharp boundary, and ballast is stowed by someone and can be inventoried, and I want to know which of these features each image quietly imports into the target and which are idle. Two other images float around the same passages and may be relevant: the scaffolding of our thoughts, and reaching bedrock where the spade turns. Say whether either helps, and where it breaks. Most importantly, tell me which of the three conclusions the door-hinge image is being used to support actually survives once the disanalogies are on the table: does the arationality of hinges block the charge of arbitrary dogma, or does it invite it? Is the non-relativist reading carried by the image or by the argument? And is there a better composite image, or is the honest answer that no single object has both of the features this debate needs? Finally, keep your confidence calibrated and mark your distinctions: I need you to separate what is uncontroversial about these passages and this literature from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it, and in particular not to present the reading on which hinges are not beliefs at all as if it were the consensus view, since I have a suspicion the dispute in my group is downstream of exactly that assumption.
