hey, i need a hand with something for my pragmatism seminar. we've spent the last three weeks on Dewey's political stuff and i keep having the same reaction in class: it all sounds gorgeous when we say it out loud and then completely unusable the second any actual institution tries to run on it. my prof keeps saying that's a failure of imagination on my part, but i think it might be a real soft spot in the view, and rather than write yet another essay arguing about it i'd like to make fun of it. could you write me a piece of satire?

what i have in mind: an internal announcement — memo plus an FAQ section, around 800 words — from something like the "Office of Institutional Inquiry" at a university, announcing that the faculty senate and student government are being dissolved and replaced by "continuous experimental inquiry," a standing process in which every disputed question is reformulated as a hypothesis and tested. the voice should be the warm, flat, jargon-saturated voice of an administrative email: nothing snide in the register, no winking, all the comedy in the content. and the comedy has to come from getting Dewey right rather than from a cartoon version of him. concretely, i'd like the jokes built out of: (a) the move from democracy as a set of institutions for aggregating pre-existing preferences to democracy as a mode of associated living, so elections get retired on the grounds that voting is the kind of mechanism you'd only insist on if you'd misunderstood what democracy is in the first place; (b) his idea that the ends a policy is supposed to serve are themselves ends-in-view — hypotheses about consequences, revisable in light of what actually happens — exploited so that nobody ever has to say what a policy is for, because the end is always provisional and the Office decides which consequences get measured; (c) the means–ends continuum, used to argue that the process of inquiry isn't merely a means to an outcome outside itself but is part of what's being aimed at, so no bad outcome can ever count against the process; (d) the claim that a public is constituted by the indirect consequences of transactions that people can't control, so the Office gets to decide which consequences are salient and therefore who counts as affected and gets a say — the staff made redundant by the restructure turn out, conveniently, not to be part of the public; (e) the absence of any real stopping rule, since no one can say when an indeterminate situation has become a determinate one, so everything remains permanently under inquiry, which happens to suit the people already in charge; and (f) warranted assertibility quietly spreading to places where nothing remotely like experimental inquiry is going on, e.g. the budget.

two constraints. first, keep it fair: my flatmate hasn't read a word of Dewey and is going to read this, so it has to be legible without footnotes, and the target should be the genuine vulnerabilities in the position rather than a strawman — someone who actually likes Dewey should feel stung rather than misrepresented. second, please don't put any actual Dewey wording in quotation marks; paraphrase his vocabulary instead of quoting, since i don't want to pass around something with a half-remembered line in it. oh, and if you can avoid ending on a character who explains the joke, that'd be great — the thing should land on its own. thanks!
