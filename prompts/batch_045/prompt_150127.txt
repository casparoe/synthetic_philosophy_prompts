You're a philosopher of physics who's actually worked through both sides of the Maxwell's demon literature. Take that role, and think carefully before answering: sketch two or three candidate diagnoses in your first pass, then commit to the best one.

My reading group has been going round in circles on the demon for two months and nobody has moved. We all agree the demon can't extract net work from a single heat bath; the disagreement is entirely about why. One camp gives the erasure answer: sorting costs nothing in principle, but the demon's memory fills up, and resetting one bit in a bath at temperature T is what you pay for, so the second law survives by charging for information processing. The other camp says that's a detour — do the fluctuation bookkeeping honestly for an actual mechanism and the demon fails for ordinary thermodynamic reasons, with no principle about information doing any work. A third view is that its verbal: information means thermodynamic entropy in one mouth and Shannon entropy in the other.

The case that finally broke us is a variant I made up to block the obvious reply: the demon never measures anything. It arrives with a tape of bits already correlated with the gas, prepared off-stage by some earlier process, uses them to decide when to open the door, and drops the spent bits in a bin without erasing them. Inside the box it pays nothing per bit and can keep running until the bin fills. The erasure camp says the cost has merely been relocated, to whoever prepared the tape and whoever eventually resets it. Their critics say relocating a cost explains nothing unless you can say what the cost is a cost of, and if the answer is only: somewhere in the cycle, then the slogan that information is physical has collapsed into a bookkeeping identity rather than a mechanism.

So what kind of stalemate is this? My hypothesis is that the sides are answering different questions — one about what must hold of any implementation that fails, one about what the failure consists in for a given mechanism — but I definately can't tell whether that dissolves the dispute or just relabels it. Diagnose it with reasons, tell me which part is substantive and which is noise, and say what would genuinely count as progress here: a distinction, a toy model, a theorem, or abandoning the question. Under 500 words, British English, and commit to a diagnosis rather than surveying the options.
