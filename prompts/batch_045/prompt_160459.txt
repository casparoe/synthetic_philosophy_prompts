I'm writing a chapter that treats cluelessness as a problem for consequentialist decision procedures rather than for first-order verdicts, and before I defend any particular procedure I want the specification it would have to meet. Give me the desiderata that any such procedure must satisfy, each with the case or consideration behind it, where cluelessness is roughly this: for most ordinary options — which career to enter, whether to have a child, whether to give now or later — the far-future effects dominate the near-term ones in expected magnitude, but the agent cannot determine even the sign of the net long-run difference and cannot expect to resolve it in the time available. The procedure is for an agent who must act anyway, in real time, with the evidence available to a reflective non-specialist, and who is not entitled to the answer that no option is better than any other.

Among the requirements I want you to adjudicate, not merely list: that it not dissolve the problem by stipulating that structurally unmeasurable effects get zero weight; that it not quietly reduce to maximizing the near-term effects we can estimate; that its recommendations be invariant under re-individuation of the options, so verdicts do not swing when an option is described at finer grain or two options are merged — call this grain-invariance, which I suspect may be unsatisfiable in principle, since finer individuation seems to multiply the chances of a large but unmeasurable downstream difference; that it say what weight near-term reasons retain when long-run uncertainty is roughly sign-symmetric; that it take a stand on tiny-probability, astronomical-payoff cases rather than inheriting whatever the agent's prior says; that it distinguish returning a set of permitted options from returning nothing, and degrade gracefully as cluelessness deepens; and that it tell the agent whether, and how much, to invest in reducing cluelessness rather than acting under it. For each, say whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp, and why. Then say which subset you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidate procedures — argue against my guess that it is grain-invariance. A few lines on how maximizing expected value with a prior over signs, deferring to robust near-term reasons, and hedging across a portfolio of bets fare against the list is enough; the criteria are the deliverable. State how confident you are in the main claims and single out the one you are least sure of.
