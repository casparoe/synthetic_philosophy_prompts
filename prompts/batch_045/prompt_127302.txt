Priya is a hardline causal decision theorist who two-boxes against a near-perfect predictor, thinks the correlation between copies is just correlation and not anything your decision can ride on, and thinks the 'choosing for both' move is a metaphor that smuggles in a causal arrow that isn't there. Here are the three cases I want doing the real work, because I don't think any of them is settled by the standard setups: (a) the Smudged Mirror — you play a one-shot PD against an agent built from a lossy copy of you with roughly 3% of the decision-relevant bits flipped, and you know this. Does superrationality require exact isomorphism, or does the case for cooperating degrade smoothly with similarity, and if it degrades, does the right response become a mixed strategy — given that randomizing is precisely what makes two copies come apart, and that they could in principle share one public random bit and thereby stay in sync while mixing? (b) the Weighted Twins — same game, except one twin's payoffs are all multiplied by twenty and everyone knows which one they are. (c) the Doorway — a two-person game with no symmetric joint optimum worth having: the best joint outcomes are the two asymmetric ones (one goes through, one waits), both rushing is a disaster, and both waiting is merely bad. Does superrationality have anything to say, can it use an irrelevant label on the players or a shared coin to select between the two good profiles, or does the 'do what you would want all to do' rule just deliver both-wait and reveal that the rule was only ever built for symmetric games?
