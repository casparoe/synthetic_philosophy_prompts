I moved here from Australia about four years ago and got my citizenship last spring, so this november was the first federal election I was actually eligible to vote in. Back home voting is compulsory so I'd never had to decide whether to do it. I've been turning something over since then and I want to know whether it has a name, because it keeps showing up and I don't have a word for it.

Here's what I mean. In the weeks before the election everyone told me some version of: your vote won't decide anything on its own, obviously, but democracy only works if everyone adds theirs, so you have to turn out. Fine — I get it, the individual act is a drop in a bucket and what matters is the bucket. Then the day after, the same people started talking as if my vote were the thing that put the winner in office. Texts to friends who stayed home along the lines of "hope you're happy," texts to people who voted along the lines of "you did this," and to me specifically, "you voted for him, so you don't get to complain about what he does now." But that seems to need the opposite picture of what my action was: not a drop in a bucket but a lever I pulled, one that makes me into an author of whatever comes next.

The reason I don't think I'm just describing hypocrisy, or people grabbing whichever argument suits them, is that both halves sound true to me when I hear them on their own. My vote really wasn't going to break a tie. And it's also true that if enough people like me had stayed home the result would have been different, and that I did add mine to the pile that produced it. It flips inside my own head too: before I voted I told myself it didn't matter so I could vote for the person I actually wanted, and afterwards I felt like I'd done something I was on the hook for.

Things I've been told it might be, and why I think they're nearby but not it. A philosophy friend mentioned Parfit's harmless torturers case when I described it: lots of acts each of which makes no perceptible difference, big aggregate harm, no clean individual culprit. But there the puzzle is how harm can exist with nobody responsible, and here the puzzle is more like responsibility being handed back to me after I was told I wasn't a cause. I've also seen people online use "expressive voting," but as far as I can tell that's about voting in order to say something rather than to change the outcome — this is different, because here somebody else is deciding what my act said and booking it as support. And I'm not asking whether there's a duty to vote or whether lesser-evil voting is corrupting; those are separate questions I can find plenty on.

One more thing that might be the same phenomenon or might be a second one: voting is treated as giving consent, but not voting isn't treated as withholding it. Nobody says "you didn't vote, so the government doesn't speak for you." The consent seems to run in one direction only.

So: is there an established concept for the switch — the act being described as causally negligible when it's being demanded from me, and as causally mine when it's being credited to me? If there isn't, I'd like you to name and define it, and either way I want the carving more than the label: what exactly it is, what it isn't, and where its edges are. Specifically: does it need a large electorate? In my union local there are about forty of us and the last contract vote was 21–19, so a single vote genuinely can decide it, and the flip doesn't seem to operate there, or at least doesn't feel like the same thing. Does it need a secret ballot, or a two-party race, or a winner who then claims a mandate? Does it extend to other things people do collectively — signing petitions, eating meat, flying — or is there something special about voting? And is the one-way consent bit the same phenomenon or a different one? I'll know an answer is good if it tells me which cases don't count. Don't bother with reading lists or citations, I just want the distinctions straight.
