I've been reading about van Fraassen's Reflection principle on my own — I have no philosophy background, I'm a sysadmin — and I think I get the gist but I've tied myself into a knot.

The way I understand it: my confidence in some claim right now ought to match what I expect my confidence in it to be later. If I'm sure that tomorrow I'll be at 70% on p, then I should already be at 70% today, not tomorrow. I started calling this pre-trust and using it as a general rule: never hold a confidence you expect to abandon.

Two things came up that I can't sort out.

First: I do 48-hour on-call shifts, and by hour 40 I'm foggy and useless. Everytime I get convinced that somthing I'm building is doomed, or that a friend is quietly furious with me. I know this happens, I know these beliefs evaporate after I sleep, and I know they're not tracking anything. But by pre-trust, shouldn't I start believing them now, scaled to how confident I expect fogged me to be? That seems obviously wrong, so either my rule is wrong or real Reflection has an off switch for future selves you don't trust. My guess was that there's a clause like as long as future me isn't worse at this than present me, but that feels circular — I'd have to already know which future selves count, and that was the thing I wanted the principle to tell me.

Second: pre-trust seems to make evidence pointless. If my confidence today has to equal my expected confidence after I open the lab results, then opening the envelope does nothing — I should already be sitting at the average of where I'd end up. Same for reading a paper. But then no one could ever rationally plan to change their mind, and every Bayesian says updating on new evidence is the whole point. So pre-trust either makes learning worthless or it just collapses into conditionalization saying the same thing twice, and neither of those can be right.

Also, does it generalize past me? If the lesson is defer to the better-informed version of you, shouldn't I defer to my colleague who's actually read the literature on whatever we're arguing about? And does it run backwards — should rested Monday me trust what Thursday-night me concluded?

So what is Reflection actually saying, and is any version of pre-trust salvageable, or is the whole rule I invented just a misreading?
