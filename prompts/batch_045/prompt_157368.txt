Had a long argument with a friend last night about the units of selection, and I realised halfway through that we werent actually disagreeing about the same question, so what I want now is the spec, not another position paper. He's gene's-eye all the way: genes are the only things copied faithfully generation after generation, organisms and colonies are just vehicles that get rebuilt each time, so genes are the units, end of story. I kept saying that can't be the whole story, partly because of traits that look individually costly and collectively beneficial (reproductive restraint in a group that would otherwise crash), and partly because it feels to me like the phrase is doing three different jobs at once: which thing has heritable variation in fitness, which thing's fitness differences actually explain why the trait spread, and which thing the trait is apparently designed to benefit.

So write me the desiderata any theory of the units of selection has to meet, with the case behind each one. Purpose clause: I want a theory good enough to tell a working biologist which level to look at and what to measure, not just a metaphysical verdict. Things I want on the list, at minimum: it has to say which of those three jobs it's answering, or give a principled link between them; it has to handle individually costly / collectively beneficial traits, and say what would settle whether the group is really a unit rather than just redescribing the case; it has to handle a gene that sabotages the gametes carrying its rival and spreads while making its bearer less fertile, without letting the fact that genes are what gets transmitted do all the work; it has to be non-vacuous, since you can always recast a gene-frequency change as a group-level change and always partition the covariance across levels, it needs to say what makes level L is a unit here a claim that could be false; it has to handle the two ways of defining a collective's fitness (the average of its members' fitness versus the collective's own reproduction, like a colony fissioning or a nest founding a new one) and say whether the verdict can flip depending on which you use; it has to say what counts as heritable variation at the collective level without just stipulating that groups aren't faithful enough; it can't collapse the level selection acts at into the level the trait is for (byproduct cases); and it has to say something about the boundary cases, eusocial colonies and hosts with obligate symbionts, and about what evidence for a level would look like that isn't just inferring the level back from the outcome.
