ok so this stopped being a seminar question for me about seven months ago — my daughter was born in Febuary — and then it turned into a work question too, because I'm the one who has to write the part of our spec that says when the assistant should just do what a user says they want and when it should treat that as a bias and quietly not comply. Every draft collapses in the same place and I've decided the collapse is in me, not in the prose. So before I write another one I want an audit of where I actually am. Here's the profile:

1. Favouriting your own kid isn't a bias, it's a preference, and any moral view with no room for agent-relative reasons like that is defective. I don't think I owe anyone a justification for it.

2. Speciesism is a bias. Favouring humans over animals merely because they're the same species as me has the same shape as racism, and the fact that nearly everyone does it is no defence.

3. My working test is a swap test: take the property that's doing the work and ask whether i'd still hold the ranking if I'd been assigned the other side of it. If the hospital had handed me a different baby I'd love that one and defend it just as hard, so what's doing the work is the relationship and not the genes — preference. If I'd been born a pig and still thought pigs counted for less, that's bias.

4. But I also believe the debunking stuff. A lot of what presents itself to me as a moral preference is an evolved or inherited response wearing a reason's clothes, and near-universal agreement among people is weak evidence of anything.

5. On magnitudes I trust the debunking story completely: the pull of an identifiable victim, scope insensitivity, and the rate at which I discount people in the far future all look like biases and I try to correct for them — I move money to effective charities instead of local ones and I think we should be spending far more on the far future. At the same time I think it's fine to spend a large sum on my own child's health instead of on a stranger's child's life, and not fine to spend it on a nice holiday for us.

6. I'm uneasy about how much more the flooding near us gets to me than the worse flooding somewhere else, and I think favouring compatriots in immigration and aid policy is mostly bias — but the swap test in (3) seems to hand me nationalism as a preference, since of course I'd favour whichever country I happened to be born in.

7. I need the test to be usable from the inside, in real time, by a person in the middle of a decision — but biases are exactly the sort of thing that feels like a preference from the inside, so first-person feel can't be the test either.

Which of these genuinely clash and which only look like they do? I want them ranked by severity rather than all flagged as equally bad, and for each real clash I want what would have to give and what that costs me. Then tell me which single revision cleans up the most. Please don't just tell me to become a utilitarian or to give up on the distinction — I need the distinction for the spec, so I'd rather keep as much of it as possible. And: is there some distinction I'm simply not drawing — I keep suspecting there's something like the difference between what I'm aiming at and how much weight I give the things I'm aiming at — that would dissolve more than one of these at once?
