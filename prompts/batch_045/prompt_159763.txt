i want to practice defending the line that string theory is still a rationally pursueable research programme even though it hasn't predicted anything we can actually test — and i want to do it against someone who knows methodology and won't just roll over. so please play Imre Lakatos. it's 1973, you're at the LSE, and a visitor has just handed you a one-page briefing on what happens in fundamental physics after you die. be the sharp version: you can concede small points but you never give up the core (science is rationally reconstructible, programmes get graded by progressive vs degenerating problem shifts, naive falsificationism is a corpse and you're the one who buried it). use only concepts you had in 1973 plus what my briefing says. you don't get to ask me factual questions about the future — the briefing is all there is — but you can say what else you'd need. about a paragraph a turn, stay in character till i say stop, and if i say "switch", flip: i defend, you attack.

the briefing:
- by the mid 80s a programme called superstring theory is trying to unify all the forces including gravity; its hard core says the basic objects are extended strings in ten dimensions.
- it has produced zero confirmed novel predictions in physics.
- it did produce novel conjectures in pure mathematics. the famous one is mirror symmetry: string theory told mathematicians how many rational curves of a given degree sit on a quintic threefold, and the mathematicians later proved those numbers right.
- the Large Hadron Collider found the Higgs particle and found no supersymmetry, no extra dimensions, nothing else new.
- in the 2000s its own practitioners concluded the theory has an enormous number of low-energy solutions — the figure usually quoted is around ten to the five-hundredth — and a lot of them started calling that the landscape and treating the resulting multiverse as a prediction rather than an embarassment.
- philosophers of science now argue for non-empirical theory assessment: the no-alternatives argument (people have tried for decades and nobody has produced a consistent alternative quantum gravity), the meta-inductive argument (earlier theories in this lineage made predictions that got confirmed much later), and unexpected explanatory interconnections.

my opening: professor, here's why i think your apparatus has nothing to say about this case. your whole grading scheme bottoms out in confirmed novel facts, so take those away and the categories go dead. consider a programme that is mathematically progressive and physically degenerating: the physics side only ever adds auxilliary hypotheses after the fact — compactifications, flux vacua, the landscape — to swallow each null result, while the maths side keeps kicking out brand-new conjectures that mathematicians then prove. i say you have no grade for that, and here's why that's your problem and not just mine: in your Euler-characteristic dialogues you worked out in detail how mathematics actually grows — conjectures, refutations, monster-barring, lemma incorporation — and you never gave mathematics anything like the progressive-or-degenerating test you gave physics. so: is my mixed programme progressive, degenerating, or outside your jurisdiction? and if you say degenerating and abandon it, then you owe me a rival, because you've argued that it can be rational to keep working inside a degenerating programme until something better comes along and even after. which means your criterion has no teeth in exactly the case everybody actually fights about. one more thing while you're at it: is the landscape move a positive heuristic doing real work, or is it monster-barring with a better name?
