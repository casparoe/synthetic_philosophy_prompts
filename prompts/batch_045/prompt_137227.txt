I run a tiny reading group — four of us, two on each side — that spent most of last year on the causal exclusion argument and got exactly nowhere, so we've now agreed to do an adversarial collaboration and I got volunteered to draft the joint statement. Which I'd like you to do instead, because my own attempts keep getting recieved as slanted by whichever side I wasn't writing for that week. One side is the exclusionist: every physical effect that has a cause has a sufficient physical cause, mental properties aren't (at the level of types, anyway) physical properties, distinct sufficient causes don't systematically overdetermine the same effect, so the mental as such does no causal work, and the honest options are reduction or some weaker surrogate. The other side holds that mental properties do genuine causal work as difference-makers at the grain the special sciences actually use, and that the argument only bites if you smuggle a very particular picture of causation into the premises — single, sufficient, productive, and indifferent to how the effect is specified. Assume we've all read the standard material and nobody needs to be told who argued for what: no citations, no paper titles, nothing resting on the exact wording of a published argument, please. I want the claims, not the footnotes.

What I'd like: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims — say five to ten, each genuinely load-bearing rather than the sort of thing nobody denies ('causation is contested' — I'll throw the document out); (2) a disagreements table with these columns: the crux, the exclusionist's own formulation (worded so they'd sign it), the non-reductivist's own formulation (same), the single case or argument both sides accept as bearing on that crux, and each side's prediction about how it comes out, written down in advance like a pre-registration; (3) an explicit note on which parts of our dispute, if any, turn out to be verbal, with the substantive version of each reformulated disagreement or an explanation of why there isn't one; (4) a short closing note from you as the drafter: which of the agreed claims you'd bet least on, what result or argument would actually move one or both camps, and where you think this whole exercise is most likely to fail — e.g. that the cruxes aren't independent, that one side wouldn't really accept the wording you gave it, or that the test cases can't discriminate between them.

Two things to build in, because they're what my own drafts kept breaking on. First, I have a hunch the deep crux is whether 'sufficient' in the closure premise means sufficient for the *occurrence* of the effect or sufficient for the *difference* the effect consists in — tell me whether that distinction dissolves anything or just relocates the fight one step back. Second, here are the cases I want used as the shared probes; add your own if they don't discriminate. Locked Route: an agent's reaching is produced by neural route R1, which realizes the relevant intention-plus-belief state M, and there is a hardwired backup route R2 that doesn't fire but would have produced the very same reaching had R1 failed — did M cause the reaching? Scatter: across a population the same mental property M is realized by many different physical states, each of which produces the behavior, with no single physical-state type present in every case — did M cause the behavior, and does it matter that only M is invariant across the population? Grain Shift: the very same event described as 'the hand moved at t at velocity v' versus 'she reached for the glass' — does the exclusion pressure change with how the effect is specified, and if so which specification is the argument entitled to? For each, give me both camps' verdict and, more importantly, what each would count as having gone wrong if the other's verdict is right.
