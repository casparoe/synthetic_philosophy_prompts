I'm taking an undergraduate seminar on the ethics of death and human extinction, and my final paper defends a version of the afterlife conjecture: the badness of human extinction is not exhausted by the deaths and losses it involves, because my valuing of anything at all — my projects, the worth of what I do, the point of activities like science or art — depends on there being a human future after my own death, so that extinction would take something away from me now rather than merely deprive others of what they would otherwise have had.

Before I commit to the view I want to know how exposed it is, so I'm asking for a map of its defeaters rather than a defense.

Two things I want you to engage with instead of smoothing over. First, I suspect the conjecture silently runs together a backward-looking claim — the value that what I do already has would be retrospectively emptied or undone by there being no future — and a forward-looking one — my very capacity to go on valuing here and now presupposes a future that my valuations are answerable to. My hunch is that most of the intuitive pull comes from one of these while most of the good objections land on the other, and I want to know which is which.

Second, take a successor case: humanity is not annihilated but gradually replaced by descendants, biological or digital, who inherit our archives, languages, institutions and some of our concerns, but who are no longer Homo sapiens and are unmoved by much that moves us. My view as stated gives no clear verdict here, and I think that's because it never says whether the afterlife my valuing needs is a matter of species membership, of causal and cultural continuity, or of the persistence of a community whose valuing overlaps enough with mine. Each answer makes it a different view, so I want to know what would settle which answer is right.

So: what would count against the view, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and split into what I as a defender should concede counts against me and what I may reasonably reject as resting on premises I don't accept? What empirical or introspective findings — including cases of people who fully accept that there will be no human future and whose valuing seems intact rather than hollowed out — would actually move me, and which would I be entitled to set aside? And finally: has the view been insulated by its own qualifications, so that any apparent counterexample gets absorbed by redescribing the relevant future more loosely, and if so is that a problem for a claim of this kind or an acceptable feature of it?
