I've been chewing on this for about two months and I need someone to find the bad step for me, because I can feel that there is one and I can't put a finger on it.

Context: I'm a diesel mechanic, no philosophy or econ classes past high school, and I got into this after watching our food co-op board approve an expansion we definately couldn't afford. Eight sensible people, one at a time, all deferring to the two guys who spoke first. So I started reading about this herding idea in a pop-economics paperback I found at an airport, and then I built my own version of the argument, and now it says things I don't believe.

Setup: 200 people in a line. Two options, A and B, exactly one is right. Each person gets one private hint that's right more often than not, say 60%. Each person sees every choice made before theirs, but not anyone's hint. Everyone wants to be right, nobody gets paid for being original.

(1) Rational people treat other people's behavior as evidence, since behavior was caused by what those people knew.

(2) If I know the rule someone was following, I can run that rule backwards and recover at least part of what they privately knew from what they did.

(3) Evidence from different people stacks up. Enough public evidence pointing at A will outweigh whatever my one private hint says.

(4) So there's a tipping point. After enough people have gone A, the rational move is to go A whatever my own hint says.

(5) The moment I do that, my choice tells the people behind me nothing about my hint. And once that starts it never stops, because everyone after me is in a strictly stronger version of the same situation.

(6) So the total information a crowd like this will ever produce is just the handful of hints owned by whoever happened to stand before the tipping point. Call it two or three people's worth. Everyone after them is decoration, and adding more honest people later adds nothing.

(7) Now swap in any other group that reached agreement partly by watching each other: scientific consensus, market prices, what's popular, what everyone knows. Same structure, same result. The length of the line of agreement isn't evidence. Ten thousand experts agreeing is worth roughly two guys guessing.

(8) And since private hints stop being expressed once the thing is rolling, how long a belief has lasted isn't evidence either. Something everybody has believed for four hundred years has no more going for it than the same thing on day three.

(9) But (7) and (8) are insane. If they were true I'd have to stop trusting nearly everything I believe on other people's word, including stuff like germs causing disease and my brakes needing doing. And I'd have no reason to think this herding story is true rather than just fashionable, which my friend thinks is a clever point and I think is a cheap trick.

Here's my own wrinkle, which I can't evaluate. Suppose instead of just picking A or B, each person has to write down a number from 0 to 100 for how confident they are, or is allowed to abstain, or the group does one secret ballot before anyone speaks. My gut says the lock-in from (5) breaks, because now there's more room in what you say for your own hint to show through. My friend says it doesn't break, it just moves: whatever rule you give them, I can still invert it, and the public stuff still eventually swamps the private stuff, and the cascade re-forms one level up. I genuinely can't tell which of us is right, or what it would even look like to settle it.

So: which step do you throw out, and what does throwing it out cost me? I'd rather keep (1) through (6) if I can — (6) is the part I started out wanting, it's literally what happened to our board — and give up (7) and (8). But I need to know whether that combination is stable, or whether keeping (6) drags me into (7) anyway. If I do have to give up (6) too, tell me what I'm left with and what I have to pay for it. And if there's a real line between a crowd whose agreement carries almost no information and a crowd whose agreement carries a lot, I want to know what the line is made of, not just that it exists.

Please don't send me to any papers or books, I can't get to a university library and I wouldn't know what to do with them anyway. Just the argument. And please write in American English.
