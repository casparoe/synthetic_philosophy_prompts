So we're starting from the same page rather than from you trying to recall a paper, here's the anti-epistemic argument I have in mind, in my own words. Say every preparation procedure comes with a spread of possible underlying physical states, and suppose the underlying state fixes the probabilities of any outcome of any measurement you might do, so that once someone hands you the underlying state the quantum state tells you nothing more. If two different pure-state preparations had overlapping spreads, there'd be an underlying state compatible with both. Now prepare two systems seperately, one from each procedure: if the underlying states of seperately prepared systems are independent, you can land on a pair where each system's underlying state is compatible with either preparation, so all four products are still live. And there's a joint measurement on the pair whose four outcomes are each assigned probability zero by one of those four products — so conditional on that pair of underlying states, every outcome has probability zero, which can't be. Conclusion: the spreads don't overlap at all, and the quantum state behaves like a property of the single system. I'm willing to grant all of that except one step, which is where I want to start: it assumes the two preparations are physically independent, that nothing in their shared past correlates their underlying states.

My opening move is exactly that step. Suppose both preparations trace back to a single event in their common past — one source, one batch, each system's microscopic details fixed a year earlier in a way no one can track in practice — so their underlying states come out correlated in precisely the pattern that fakes the overlap, with no action at a distance needed, because the correlation was baked in at the source long before anyone chose a measurement. On that picture the argument above only rules out information-views that also promise you a clean independence assumption, and the view survives by refusing the promise. My question: is refusing it any more disreputable than the superdeterminist dodge in Bell's theorem? From where I'm sitting both look like the same move in different clothes — a correlation laid down in the past, invisible in practice, that makes the statistics come out. If you think mine is worse, tell me precisely what the extra cost is, and don't just wave at "conspiracy": say what would have to be true of the source for my loophole to work and why that's more embarrassing than what the Bell-denier has to believe.
