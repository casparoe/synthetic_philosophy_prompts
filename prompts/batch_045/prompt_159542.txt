I help run a small giving circle — roughly $40,000 a year — and we allocate it using the importance / tractability / neglectedness framework popularized by 80,000 Hours. I have been trying to make our use of it more rigorous, and I have run into a problem I cannot resolve.

My reading is that ITN is an expected-value decomposition: importance is how much value would be created by fully solving the problem, tractability is how much of the problem gets solved per additional dollar, and neglectedness is one over the resources already committed to it. On that reading the product is a genuine quantity with units, so I have put our estimates on ratio scales, converted each factor to a 1–5 score after a log transform, and taken the geometric mean rather than the raw product, so that a very low score in one column does not automatically zero out an otherwise strong cause.

But the decomposition now looks internally inconsistent, and I think neglectedness is the culprit. If neglectedness is inversely proportional to resources already committed, then any cause's score falls as you fund it. Apply the formula iteratively — give a dollar to the top-scoring cause, recompute, repeat — and you end up spreading money across causes in proportion to how much they already receive, which is the un-prioritized diversification the framework was supposed to replace. It also looks like neglectedness double-counts: if tractability already measures progress per dollar and returns diminish, then how crowded a cause is should show up in the tractability column already.

So: am I right that one of the three factors is redundant? If so, should I drop neglectedness, or absorb it into tractability as an elasticity — I have been experimenting with replacing it by the exponent in a fitted power law of returns to funding? Once the redundancy is removed, is there a principled normalization, or is the geometric mean overcorrecting? And how should I handle the fact that our scores do not seem comparable across levels of description, when one row is bednet distribution and the next is catastrophic risk reduction?
