So I got drafted onto the norms working group for Plover, the open source project I contribute to — the previous chair left kind of suddenly and somone had to finish the contributor guide before the summit in five weeks, and that someone is apparently me. The bit I'm stuck on is sorting every informal practice in the project into three buckets: enforced rules (CI or code-of-conduct backed, break one and you get sanctioned), conventions (we describe them, we don't enforce them — break one and you're not wrong, just confusing), and habits (we don't mention them at all). The bucket actually matters, bcs calling something a convention is us publicly promising not to punish it.

Our old chair was a hardline Lewisian about this stuff — a convention solves a recurring coordination problem, the solution is arbitrary among the alternatives, and it holds together bcs everyone conforms, expects everyone else to conform, and conforms conditionally on the others conforming. I assumed that would make the sorting mechanical. It doesn't, and part of the problem is that the minutes from the last two years are full of rulings I can't derive from one clean rule. Roughly:

- Everyone opens PRs against main even though develop exists and either would work fine; nobody can say why main won. Ruled: convention.
- The weekly call is at 15:00 UTC; any slot would do if we all moved together; people show up bcs other people show up. Ruled: convention.
- Commit messages in the imperative mood: basically universal compliance, everyone expects it — but the linter auto-flags anything else, so we ruled it not a convention. Same shaped regularity, wrong sustaining mechanism: the bot, not each other.
- Reviewing PRs within ~48 hours: high compliance, people who lag get publicly nudged, and most of us would do it anyway to get our own stuff merged. Ruled: not a convention.
- Running the test suite locally before pushing: near universal, but each of us would keep doing it alone — it catches our own bugs. Ruled: not a convention.
- 'Fixes #123' lines in PR bodies. Everyone believes the founder decreed this back in 2016; she didn't, it started with one person and got copied. Ruled: still a convention.
- Everyone thinks everyone else reads the monthly changelog; almost nobody actually does. Ruled: not a convention — expectations without behaviour don't count.
- The docs team writes 'click' and the API team writes 'invoke'; stable in both, no interaction between the teams. Ruled: not a project convention, and the group point blank refused to say whether each team has its own.

Now I've got two new ones to rule on next week and I genuinely can't tell which way our own precendents push.

1. We migrated to a new tracker three weeks ago and design talk split between a Forum and a Chat, both equally workable. Within three days basically everyone was in the Forum, nobody proposed it, and each person says they went where everyone else was. There's no history anyone can appeal to — the Forum is three weeks old.
2. Everyone posts a one-line 'shipped:' note to #done at the end of the day. Nobody reads #done, nothing of anyone's depends on anyone else doing it, and if half the project stopped the rest would drop it inside a week. But everyone knows everyone does it and everyone would feel like the odd one out being first to quit.

Help me out: what two or three rules fit all eight earlier verdicts? What does each predict about 1 and 2? Which bucket should we put each in if we want to be consistent with ourselves, which old ruling is the closest analog in each case, and what principle are we committing to either way? And is there a third case — or a question I could put to the group at the summit — that would tell the surviving rules apart? I don't need references or reading lists, I just need the pattern in our own minutes.
