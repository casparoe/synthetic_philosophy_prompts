A couple of people in my department have been running an informal reading group on self-locating belief and anthropics, and it's my turn next week to present where I actually stand on Sleeping Beauty. I'd definately rather find the holes in my own package before they do. So here it is, as a list. (1) In the standard setup — fair coin, one awakening on Monday if heads, awakenings on Monday and Tuesday if tails, memory wiped, no clock, full knowledge of the protocol — I'm a thirder: on waking, my credence in heads is 1/3, and I get there with the boring counting rule, i.e. weight each possible world by its prior probability and then spread my credence evenly over the awakenings in that world (equivalently: expected number of heads-awakenings divided by expected number of all awakenings). (2) I also think that rule gives the right answer in variants, and a friend bet me I couldn't design one where it spits out something other than 1/3, so here's mine: exactly as before on heads, but on tails you're woken Monday and then woken Tuesday only if a second independent fair coin comes up heads — so tails yields one awakening half the time and two half the time. I get 2/5. (3) What I don't think is that the number should move just because the experimenters do something different with the awakenings without telling me: whether they offer a bet at every awakening, or instead choose one of the awakenings uniformly at random and offer the bet only at that one, I want to say 1/3 either way, since in both cases everything I know when the bet is offered is just "I am awake and someone is offering me a bet." I'd also like my credences to line up, at least roughly, with the prices I'd bet at. (4) In the two-subject version — two people exist the whole time, on heads only one of them is woken, on tails both are woken simultaneously in different rooms, and I don't know which room I'm in — I want to say 1/2, on the grounds that both people exist either way and no world has "extra people" in it in the sense that matters. (5) I'm comfortable saying both that Beauty learns nothing new when she wakes up (she knew the protocol, she's told nothing) and that her credence in heads moves from 1/2 to 1/3. (6) Methodologically, I reject SIA, the self-indication assumption that a world containing more observers is intrinsically more likely to be actual — the "presumptuous philosopher" style conclusion seems like a flat reductio to me — and I'm at least half sympathetic to the Doomsday argument, which as far as I can tell runs on the opposite idea, that you should reason as if you were a random sample from the observers in your reference class. And I take it as a point in favour of all this that P(heads | it's Monday) comes out at 1/2.

What I want is an audit, not a survey of the positions people have defended: which of these commitments actually pull against each other, and how badly? Please don't flag everything as equally problematic — I specifically want to know which pairs only look like they're in conflict and dissolve once you're careful, and which are the real ones. Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I genuinely don't know whether the trouble is in (3), or in the rule I stated in (1), or in (6), so please don't read me as having already picked the one I'd sacrifice.
