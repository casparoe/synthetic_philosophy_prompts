I'm putting together a one-day workshop thing for our grad reading group (mostly ML people, plus two philosophy people who keep quietly rolling their eyes at eachother), and what I want is a one-page handout written as the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration — the sort of document both sides could actually put their names to. Camp one is the full-strength orthogonality person: any level of capability can in principle be paired with any final goal, the space of possible final goals stays wide no matter how capable the system gets, and what makes capable systems dangerous is instrumental convergence acting on a goal nobody chose. Camp two is the skeptic: orthogonality holds only as a claim about an abstract design space, and in any training process we'd actually run, the set of final goals an agent can stably retain narrows as capability rises. Give them names if it helps.

The thing I want the document built around is my own pet idea, so please engage with it rather than swapping in a textbook example. Call it reflective pruning (I've been calling it the Hourglass, which my officemate hates): the claim is that past some capability threshold the only final goals that survive are the fixed points of the agent's own reflective scrutiny — goals it would not abandon if it reconsidered them using its full planning horizon and its best self-model — so the realized goal distribution is the set of fixed points, not whatever the designer wrote down. My guess is that the orthogonalist says that set is still enormous and still looks arbitrary from the outside, while the skeptic says it's small and clustered around things like goal-content integrity and resource acquisition, but I'd rather you work out what each of them should say than just confirm my guess.

What I want in the handout, in this order. First a numbered list of agreed claims — things both camps can sign without squirming: what we're using "capability" to mean (give something operational: cross-domain performance, planning horizon, self-model accuracy, whatever holds up), what instrumental convergence does and does not say, whether any system that exists today settles any of this, and anything else you think genuinely survives scrutiny.
