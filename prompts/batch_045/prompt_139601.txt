I run a small reading group that grew out of the local rationalist meetup, and we have spent the last three sessions stuck on the same stretch of Hofstadter: the imaginary "careenium" substrate and its "simmballs" — a made-up medium whose states group into larger configurations, which in turn combine into still larger ones, until you have something that behaves like a symbol-system riding on top of a physical substrate, and Hofstadter asks whether those symbols ever get to *push the substrate around*, or whether it is substrate pushing substrate all the way down and the symbol story is merely a gloss we find convenient. Everyone in the group agrees about every concrete case we can invent, and then disagrees about the word "cause", so another evening of talking is not going to help. What I want instead is an instrument: a decision tree of eight to ten branch nodes and eight to twelve leaves, starting from a premise all of us accept — that a complete microphysical description of the careenium at one moment fixes its microphysical state at the next — and sorting a person into a named position by asking only about cases they can judge without knowing what the positions are called.

The cases I want the nodes built from, in whatever order makes the tree come out cleanly (and please add your own in the same spirit if a junction needs one): the *inverted-interpretation twins*, two careenia intrinsically identical at a given moment whose symbols nonetheless mean different things because of different use-histories in different surroundings — does the substrate's subsequent evolution diverge, and if you say yes, what carries the difference?; the *graft*, where a simmball configuration is lifted out of one careenium and installed in another where it comes to play a different role — does it go on doing what it did at home, or what it means here?; the *clamped symbol*, where an experimenter holds a configuration in place against the substrate's own tendencies and the substrate visibly reorganises itself around it — is the symbol the cause, or only the experimenter's hand?; the *counterfeit careenium*, a system engineered to reproduce the same substrate trajectories and the same outward symbol behaviour by brute tabulation, with no genuine symbols anywhere inside — does it have symbols at all, and if not, what exactly did the real one have that it lacks?; *leak-away*, a careenium whose own dynamics erode every configuration within some fixed span, so the symbols' grip is real but partial, temporary and constantly in need of repair — does partial and temporary grip count as pushing?; and *re-graining*, where the very same careenium is described at a finer or a coarser grain, so that what counted as substrate now counts as symbols and vice versa — does the original question then collapse into a question about which description we happened to pick up?

Please map the logical space before naming anyone's view: set out the combinations of answers your nodes actually permit, then place the real positions inside that space and, for any cell left empty, give a one-line diagnosis of why (incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unoccupied). As leaves I want at minimum: the reductionist or identity answer; the exclusionist answer on which the symbol level is a causally inert shadow; non-reductive physicalism in both its overdetermination-tolerant and its causal-inheritance (realisation-without-overdetermination) forms; a strong downward-causation or emergentist answer on which the micro-laws are not the whole story; the interventionist answer on which symbols count as causes precisely because holding them fixed changes what the substrate does; the descriptivist answer on which symbols constrain without causing, so the original question is a category mistake; an interpretation-relativist or intentional-stance answer; and a tangled-hierarchy answer of the sort I take Hofstadter to be reaching for, on which the symbol level is real and efficacious because the system's self-representation loops back down onto the substrate. Also flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely cannot separate — I suspect the causal-inheritance view and the descriptivist view may agree on every case I can think of, and I would much rather you said so than invented a node to split them apart — give each leaf one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and finish with a short scoring key so that somebody who answers in order can be told which leaf they landed on and which single answer they would have to change to fall into a neighbouring one. One practical note: we argue in a pub with no library and no signal, so please do not send me off to read anything — no citations, no paper titles, no suggestions for further reading; just the distinctions and the cases.
