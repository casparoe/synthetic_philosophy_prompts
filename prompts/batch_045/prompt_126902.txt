Its been stuck in the same spot for decades, as far as I can tell, and I want to know what kind of stuck it is. I've been reading about the qualia dispute on my own, no philosophy training, so sorry if I butcher the vocabulary.

My hypothesis is that its mostly verbal. The pattern I keep running into is that whenever the anti-qualia side accounts for some feature — what it is to experience red, how we tell experiences apart, why we can report on them — the other side says no, that's not the part I meant, and the target slides to somewhere the account can't reach. That's roughly the quining complaint in the Chase and Sanborn paper, I think that's the one. If the term is defined so that any account you offer automatically misses it, then of course nobody moves, and the fight is about the word rather than the world.

But here's the case that makes me doubt my own hypothesis. Forget definitions and imagine we could actually do the silent swap: overnight your red and green responses get switched, and nothing changes in your behaviour, in your memory of how things used to look, or in what you say. Now take a nastier version — the swap flips back and forth every hour, so there isn't even a stable earlier state for you to compare against and nothing for you to remember. My sense is that the realist still wants to say there's a further fact about whether anything changed for you, and the denier wants to say there is no further fact, and that is just what having nothing to say looks like from the inside. That reads like a genuine disagreement about a possible case, not a quibble about a word — which would mean my verbal-disagreement story is at best half the story.

So: is this one dispute or two sharing a name — a definitional one that really is verbal, and a substantive one about whether introspection is a source of evidence no third-person account can touch, or about whether what it's like can in principle be fully described? Diagnose it using what each side actually argues rather than surveying the positions, and then tell me what would count as progress: a distinction to draw, the flip-flop case actually being run, abandoning the question, or nothing at all. And please say how confident you are in your diagnosis and single out the part of it you're least sure about.
