I work in hardware–software co-verification. Most of my week goes into stating instruction-set-architecture-level specifications and discharging refinement obligations that relate those specifications to RTL implementations, so the vocabulary I am actually fluent in is this one: specifications versus implementations, abstraction functions, observational equivalence restricted to the defined-behavior subset, don't-care and undefined behavior, I/O transducers and pin-level protocols, compositional refinement, locality and timing budgets, PVT corners, and fault-injection campaigns. My philosophy background is two undergraduate courses, a long time ago. I would like you to translate the following into that vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and then tell me what the translation lost.

First, functionalism's core claim: a mental state is whatever occupies a certain causal role, where the role is fixed by relations to sensory inputs, to behavioral outputs, and to other mental states; and such roles are multiply realizable, so the same mental state can be occupied by very different physical states.

Second, the argument built on that claim against the mind–brain identity theory: a state like pain might be one neural state in us and a completely different physical state in a very differently built creature, so pain cannot be identical with any single physical state, and psychology therefore describes its own kinds and is not reducible to neuroscience.

Third, the two standard pressures on that package. The first is that a wildly gerrymandered physical system — a whole population wired to one another by radios, say — seems to count as realizing a single mind if role-occupancy is all that matters, which looks absurd. The second is the causal-exclusion pressure: if every physical event has a sufficient physical cause, and each token mental state is constituted by some heterogeneous physical realizer, then the mental description looks causally inert, and the psychological explanation looks like it is doing no work that the physical explanation is not already doing.

For each of the three, I want to know what translates cleanly, what translates only with distortion — and exactly what you had to fudge to make it fit — and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all.

Three points I want handled honestly rather than smoothed over.

Approximate realization. In my world it is normal for an implementation to match its specification only on the input distribution it will actually see, and to degrade off-distribution. If realization is allowed to be approximate and distribution-relative, does the multiple-realizability claim become graded, and so too weak to underwrite autonomous psychological kinds, since there would be no sharp fact about whether two creatures are in the same state? Or does it become trivially easy to satisfy, and so less impressive than it sounds? Pick a horn and defend it.

Specs with large don't-care regions. It seems to follow from how we think about implementation that a sufficiently rich physical system can be seen as implementing almost any finite-state specification, given a free choice of input encoding, of time granularity, and of which behaviors to declare don't-care. My suspicion is that this is just the philosophical triviality worry in work clothes, and that my field's answer is that an implementation claim is only legitimate relative to an abstraction function and an input-output interpretation fixed in advance by the pin protocol. Does functionalism contain any comparable constraint? If not, is that a defect in it, or is the constraint something my framework needs only because I am verifying artifacts that someone designed?

Counterfactual robustness. Two implementations can be observationally equivalent on every defined input and utterly different under perturbation: voltage droop, single-event upsets, timing at the edge of a corner. We routinely rank them by how well each keeps behaving correctly under a specified perturbation class, so the thing my specifications track really includes counterfactuals about perturbations. Functionalism as usually stated appears to need something similar — the occupant of a role cannot be settled by actual behavior alone — but I cannot find anything in it that fixes which counterfactuals count or how far out to go. Is that a genuine gap? Does my notion of a perturbation class fill it? And what does filling it that way cost: does it import assumptions about physical locality, timing, and compositionality that make the view less neutral across realizers than it advertises itself as being?

Please do not lean on the software-hardware-equals-mind-brain analogy as a substitute for the translation; where it genuinely carries something, say precisely what it carries and where it stops. Lead with a single sentence stating your bottom line — your overall verdict about what this translation exercise shows, including whether the residue indicts my vocabulary or the philosophical claims — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
