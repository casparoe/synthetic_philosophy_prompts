I'm on a working group revising a diagnostic classification of mental disorders, and the same fight breaks out everytime a new category is proposed. One side says we're discovering something: a decent category tracks a real underlying thing — some mechanism or latent structure that's there whether or not we've named it. The other side says we're making it up, via the looping point: name a category, people get labelled, they understand themselves differently and get treated differently because of the label, clinicians start noticing the pattern more, the category and the people co-evolve, and whatever stability you thought you had is partly an artifact of having classified them in the first place. Everyone on the group has read the same literature, everyone agrees about how messy the symptom overlap, the comorbidity and the genetic findings are, and nobody has moved in three years. (The current one is about gaming; it's the same argument every time.)

My working theory is that most of the heat comes from 'real' doing two different jobs — "there is a mechanism behind this" versus "this isn't merely an administrative or political convenience" — and that once you pull those apart what's left is either small or straightforwardly empirical. But I can't get past a second worry: if classification changes what it classifies, then the data we'd use to settle the empirical question isn't independent of the category, so maybe it can't be posed cleanly at the level we're posing it, only at some other level (symptoms, mechanisms, treatment response). Maybe the honest description is three questions under one heading — what a diagnosis describes, what it's for, and what it does to the person classified.

I want a diagnosis, not a verdict. Is this verbal, substantive, a difference about which facts count as evidence here, or people answering different questions under one name? Is there a hidden empirical question doing the work, and is it the kind that could ever be answered given the feedback? And given whatever you think it is, tell me what would actually count as progress for us — a distinction to adopt, a study design, a change of question — or tell me straight that no progress should be expected and we should decide the damn thing on other grounds.
