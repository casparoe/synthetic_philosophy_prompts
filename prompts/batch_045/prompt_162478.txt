So I had a genuinely two-hour argument in the pub last night with my friend Mo and I'm still going round in circles on it this morning. Mo's position was basically Hardin's old lifeboat essay: we're the passengers, there are vastly more people in the water than berths, and if sentiment gets the better of us and we start hauling people aboard we all get swamped and then nobody is saved — so, two families done properly rather than five done badly. I said that has Singer's drowning-child-in-the-shallow-pond thing exactly backwards: nobody is at risk of being swamped, the marginal cost of one more family is roughly the cost of the holidays and takeaways we'd otherwise buy for ourselves, and "we'd have to give up our nice things" is not the same kind of reason as "the boat capsizes". He said I'd just changed the subject from survival to comfort.

What I want from you is an audit of the lifeboat picture as an argument, because I have to turn this into a one-page note the rest of the committee can actually use.
