I have an argument that seems to force semantic externalism into a dilemma I don't think it can escape, and since I can't accept the conclusion I'd like a diagnosis of the reasoning rather than a survey of the literature.

(1) Externalism: the content of a speaker's term is fixed by facts outside the speaker's skin — the stuff she is causally hooked up to, and the causal-historical chain running from her use back to it.
(2) Whatever fixes content is content-constituting: any difference in the fixing facts is a difference in content. Deny this and externalism shrinks to a causal story about how we come by the contents we would have had anyway, which no internalist need resist.
(3) The chain for an ordinary term runs through the particular samples the speaker met, the tokens she overheard, the experts she deferred to, and their samples in turn.
(4) No two speakers share a chain in all those respects, and my chain today is not the chain I had at eight.
(5) So no two speakers ever mean quite the same thing by an ordinary term, and I do not now mean what I meant at eight.
(6) Genuine disagreement requires two speakers to take incompatible attitudes toward one and the same content, and successful communication requires the audience to recover the very content the speaker expressed.
(7) Both are commonplace: I understood the paper I read yesterday, and I disagree with my eight-year-old self about plenty.
(8) So externalism destroys content — unless we add that content is coarse-grained, so that many differences in the fixing facts leave content untouched.
(9) But the Twin Earth difference is then just one more difference in the fixing facts, H2O versus XYZ. If content survives the differences in (4), it survives that one too, and the Twin Earth argument shows nothing an internalist could not grant.
(10) To block (9) the externalist owes a principled distinction between differences content tracks and differences it ignores. To sharpen that demand I gave my seminar the Shard case: a planet whose lakes are chemically uniform within a lake but vary continuously from lake to lake, so that neighbouring lakes are indistinguishable to the inhabitants while the two ends of the gradient are as unlike as H2O and XYZ. There was no single baptismal sample and, apparently, no fact of the matter about which kind the community's word for the clear drinkable stuff latched onto.

The conclusion I can't swallow is that externalism either leaves us no content to share or buys shared content at the price of being unable to say what was ever special about Twin Earth — with the Shard case suggesting it cannot even secure determinate reference where it looked most at home.

Which step do you reject? I would keep (1) and (7) over anything else, and I would sooner give up (6) than give up externalism, but I want to be told what that costs. Commit to a single step and defend it against the nearest alternative instead of laying out the menu for me, and say plainly what would change your mind.

Reply in exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no preamble and no closing summary: the step you reject; why that step looked compulsory; what rejecting it commits you to; why the best alternative rejection is worse; and what the Shard case does to your diagnosis.
