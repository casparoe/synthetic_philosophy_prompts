Two people in my reading group got stuck on each other last night over Dennett's ladder of kinds of minds — Darwinian creatures (hard-wired, no learning), Skinnerian ones (learn by trial and error out in the world), Popperian ones (generate candidate actions internally and prescreen them, so the bad options die in their heads instead of them dying in the world), and Gregorian ones (use tools, especially mental ones like words, so their brains get restructured by the culture around them — the level named after the psychologist Richard Gregory). The fight was about where actual mindedness starts on that ladder: where things start mattering to a creature, where there's a perspective rather than just control.

Jonah: only Gregorian creatures have minds in the sense that matters. A Popperian creature prescreens, sure, but it has no way of representing its own reasons, so there's no subject there — nothing it is like to be it. Words are what turn a control system into someone.

Priya: that's moving the goalposts. The real threshold is where you can't explain or predict the behavior anymore without attributing inner representations of the world, and that's the Popperian step. Gregorian-ness is a power upgrade, not the dawn of subjectivity.

Jonah: but the whole point of the ladder is that each level's inner machinery is still just more designed kit, a pile of tricks. Without words there's no self-construction, no loop that makes a self out of a controller. Attributing experience to a wordless Popperian is just us projecting.

Priya: then your test quietly becomes a verbal-report test, which makes a pre-linguistic toddler, or an adult who never aquired language, mindless. That isn't just uncomfortable, it's wrong — obviously there's something it's like to be a one-year-old.

Jonah: fine, the criterion isn't current language use, it's being the kind of thing built to soak up cultural tools. A toddler is a Gregorian creature by design: she's about to be scaffolded by language and by other people's tricks. A chimp never gets past the Popperian ceiling.

Priya: so mindhood depends on community membership and on what's going to happen later? A chimp raised with signing gets promoted and her wild cousin doesn't? And it gets worse: a large language model is marinated in human text and runs on tools humans built. On your criterion it's more Gregorian than any ape. Do you really want to say it has more of a mind than a chimpanzee?

Jonah: no, and I don't have to. Gregorian-ness is necessary but not sufficient. You also have to be an agent with your own endogenous ends — something whose goals weren't installed by somebody else for somebody else's purposes. A language model is Gregorian the way a library is full of words.

Priya: now you've smuggled in a substrate condition, which cuts against the whole functionalist spirit — thelevels were supposed to be defined by what a creature does with information, not by what it's made of. And if Gregorian-ness isn't sufficient, maybe the ladder never carved mindedness at a joint at all. Maybe consciousness, agency, rationality, selfhood and mattering-to come apart across these levels, and one ladder conflates all of them.

Jonah: maybe. But then the project of ranking kinds of minds was muddled from the start and we need to say which of those properties we were ever tracking.

A third person then threw in a hypothetical I haven't stopped thinking about: a creature that does internal preselection, but whose candidate generator and evaluator are both innate and never updated by experience — Popperian without ever being Skinnerian. Does the ladder have room for that, and does its case help either of them?

They turned to me to referee and I honestly had nothing. Please go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about what — where each of them is clearly right, where each is reaching for something their own framework can't give them, and what both of them are missing about the structure of the ladder itself (including whether "mind" is even the sort of thing that has a first rung).
