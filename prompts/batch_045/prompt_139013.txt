Me and a friend got into this on a long car ride and I've been going in circles ever since. I never did any physics past school, so sorry if I mangle the terms. His argument, roughly:

1. Special relativity says there's no fact of the matter about which distant events are happening "now" — that gets fixed by your reference frame, not by the world.
2. Penrose's Andromeda thing: because even a tiny change in speed swings your "now" slice by whole days at that kind of distance, whether the invasion fleet has already launched depends on which way I happen to be walking across my kitchen.
3. So if what's real is what's simultaneous with me in my frame, then reality itself is frame-relative — for one of the two walkers the launch is already real and done, for the other one it isnt yet.
4. But reality cant depend on which way someone is walking. Me pacing toward the kettle does not make a war start on the far side of the galaxy.
5. So "now" isn't picking out anything real, and reading what exists off simultaneity is a dead end.
6. The block universe argument (Rietdijk-Putnam, I think its called) runs on exactly that move: some far-off observer drifting the right way has my tomorrow inside their "now", that observer is inside my "now", and since what's real isn't just my private perspective, my tomorrow is already real too.
7. So that argument has nothing left to stand on, the future is genuinely open, and nothing that will happen is sitting there yet.

I can't accept 7, and I really can't accept that anything depends on how I walk, but I also dont see how to deny 1 or 2, and 4 feels like the most solid line in the whole thing. Which step is actually broken, and once I give it up what am I stuck believing instead? If I have to choose I'd rather keep 1 and 2 than 7.
