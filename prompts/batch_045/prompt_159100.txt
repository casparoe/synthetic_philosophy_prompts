my brother-in-law works at one of those quantum computing startups and at a family cookout last weekend he offered me a bet that I can't stop thinking about, and I honestly can't tell if the guy is conning me or if he's onto something.

here's the setup. I pay him $600 up front. Then I get in his machine, which does one of those genuinely random quantum coin-flip things where physicists say the numbers are 90/10. Except — and this is his whole pitch — he says there aren't really odds at all, because both results happen; the world just splits, and the "90/10" is really just how much of me ends up on each side. Then his machine does something extra: if the 90 side happens, one guy wakes up in the room and gets nothing back. If the 10 side happens, the machine prints nine guys who all wake up in the same room, and each of them gets handed $700.

his argument: forget the 90/10. ten guys wake up, nine of them are up a hundred bucks and only one is down six. nine out of ten of your futures are good ones. and he says that even if you insist on weighting the sides his way is still fine, because the thin side produces nine times as much total happiness — $6,300 of it — so even discounted it beats the $600.

my wife, who works in neither quantum nor anything adjacent, says this is garbage. the 10 side is ten times thinner than the 90 side no matter how many guys you print on it, and you can see that because if his machine printed a *million* guys on the losing side, his way of counting would make the losing side a dead cert, which is insane.

I think she's right but I can't quite say why, and there's a wrinkle: he admitted the nine copies aren't equally thick — eight of them are apparently almost vanishingly thin, and one of them carries nearly all of that side's weight. he says that's irrelevant if what you care about is how many futures go well. I'm not so sure; it feels like "nine out of ten" is misleading in a second, separate way if eight of the nine are barely there. he also floated a version where the machine prints nine copies on *both* sides, and says that removes the copying weirdness and settles it in his favor.

so: should I take the bet, and whose reasoning is actually right? please also tell me how confident you are in whatever you claim and single out the bit you're least sure about — I'd rather know which part to push on than get a smooth answer.
