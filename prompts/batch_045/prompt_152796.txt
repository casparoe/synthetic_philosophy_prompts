I spend most of my working life designing and certifying flight-control systems, and a large part of the rest of it writing incident attributions: reports that trace some undesired behaviour of an autopilot back through the loop — plant and disturbance, sensors, controller gains, the adaptation law that set those gains, the outer scheduling logic that selected the adaptation law, and the requirements document someone signed off further upstream. My professional reflexes are therefore all backward-tracing. Whenever anyone says that some component is at fault, I immediately ask which level of the stack authored the behaviour, and in practice the answer is always one level up, and then one level up again, until I arrive at a document written by a committee that was itself following a standard. I have no philosophy beyond a first-year survey course twenty years ago, and every time I read a summary of Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility I cannot tell whether I am looking at a deep result or at a piece of wordplay with the word responsible.

So I would like a systematic translation rather than a summary. Please restate the argument, premise by premise and in particular its regress structure, in the vocabulary I actually use: plant and controller, control law and its parameters, disturbances, noise injection and dithering, gain scheduling, inner and outer loops, adaptive and self-tuning control, credit assignment, counterfactual robustness, and the forward-looking question an incident report is really written to answer, namely what to change so this does not happen again. Two features matter to me especially, because I think they are where the argument either earns its keep or collapses. First, Strawson's claim that the argument goes through whether or not determinism is true: in my terms, that a controller whose output is fully determined by its parameters and inputs is in no better position, with respect to authorship, than one into which someone has injected random noise. Second, his claim that the regress cannot be stopped by adding levels: in my terms, that a tower of meta-controllers each tuning the one below never reaches a level that grounds authorship, and that an infinite one is not buildable. Please also translate, or say plainly that you cannot, the distinction between the strong notion of responsibility that Strawson says is impossible and the thinner, forward-looking notions of accountability and remediation that my incident reports traffic in happily.

To make the translation do some work, I have been trying to formalise authorship over one's own update rule in three grades, from strongest to weakest: (A) you set your own parameters outright; (B) you did not set them, but you chose the update law by which they change; (C) you chose neither, but your present parameters are an attractor of your own dynamics — perturb them, re-run, and you re-converge on roughly what you are now — and you now reflectively endorse both the parameters and the law, in the sense that you would not accept an intervention that changed either. Grade A is plainly unavailable to anything built by someone else, and I take it that is Strawson's target. The interesting question for me is whether the argument has the resources to reject B and C without simply helping itself to the conclusion that authorship cannot be inherited or accumulated, so please say, for each grade, whether Strawson's premises reach it and what the standard compatibilist-style moves against the argument would look like when aimed at that grade specifically. Then give me the residue report: which parts of the debate survive into control-engineering vocabulary unchanged, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart there at all — desert, praise and blame, guilt, the reactive attitudes, the phenomenology of being an originator. For the residue, tell me whether it indicates that my vocabulary is missing a primitive it ought to have, or that some of what Strawson and his opponents are arguing about is less determinate than it sounds. Do not leave me with an agnostic shrug; commit to a verdict on where the translation breaks and why.
