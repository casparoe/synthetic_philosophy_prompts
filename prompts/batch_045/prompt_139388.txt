I'm in a metaphysics seminar on actual infinities, and my term paper defends a modest finitism: no concrete actually infinite collection is possible, while the natural numbers and the continuum are perfectly respectable as abstract objects. The argument I have built for the first half of that claim seems to run straight through the second half, and I cannot find the break. It goes like this.

(1) If any actually infinite collection of concrete objects is possible, then Hilbert's Hotel is possible: rooms numbered 1, 2, 3, and so on, one guest in each, and no other rooms.
(2) Let the guests in rooms 3, 4, 5, and so on check out. Infinitely many guests leave, and two remain.
(3) Instead, let the guests in rooms 2, 4, 6, and so on check out. Infinitely many guests leave, and infinitely many remain.
(4) In both cases the manager does the same thing to the same starting population: removes infinitely many guests. Yet the remainder differs. So how many remain is not settled by how many there were and how many left.
(5) An arithmetic of quantity in which subtraction yields no determinate result for a determinate pair of quantities is incoherent, and no real collection can be governed by an incoherent arithmetic.
(6) Therefore no actually infinite collection is possible.
(7) Nothing in steps (2) through (5) uses the fact that the guests are concrete. Run the same steps on the natural numbers, on the points of a line, or on the instants of an endless future.
(8) Therefore there are only finitely many natural numbers, the continuum is not actually infinitely divisible, and the future is not endless.

Step (8) I cannot accept: the rest of the paper depends on there being infinitely many naturals and on Cantor's theorem. But I want to keep step (6) for concrete collections, and I would rather give up almost anything else than give up either.

A classmate has offered a patch I do not know how to assess. She says (4) is false as stated, because the two check-out lists are not the same operation under any description available to a hotel whose rooms have not yet been numbered: no permutation of the room numbers maps the list in (2) onto the list in (3), since their complements differ in size, so the lists differ structurally and not merely in which rooms get named. She proposes a constraint: if two check-out lists are related by a renumbering of the rooms, the remainders must be equinumerous. Hilbert's Hotel satisfies that trivially, and on her reading (5) is never triggered at all. What I cannot tell is whether her constraint is a truth about cardinality talk in general, in which case my paper is over, or a substantive thesis about concrete collections in particular, in which case it is exactly the restriction I need but I owe an argument for it that does not collapse back into (7).

Please tell me which step to reject rather than just that the argument is unsound, and price the options: what does each rejection commit me to about infinite totalities, about cardinality as a measure of size, and about whether the concrete and the abstract can be pulled apart here? If the honest answer is that no version of this argument gives me concrete finitism without taking (8) with it, say so and tell me what a defensible replacement argument would have to supply.
