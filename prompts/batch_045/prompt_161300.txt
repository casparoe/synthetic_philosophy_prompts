I am writing a paper on what exactly the cogito establishes, and before I commit to a reading I want the specification rather than another literature survey: what would a reading of Descartes's cogito have to deliver in order to count as adequate? Fix the purpose so that adequacy is relative to something. The job I want it to do is foundationalist: it should be the one item that survives the radical doubt, and then it should bear weight — serve as a premise I can reason from, across time, in an extended argument that eventually re-establishes the trustworthiness of clear and distinct perception and gets me back an external world. So give me the desiderata, each with the case or consideration behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable given that job and which are merely preferences of one camp (strict fidelity to Descartes's own system, say, or a general taste for thin, deflationary readings). Tell me also which distinctions a reading has to respect rather than collapse — for instance, indubitability-for-me versus knowledge, and having a deliverance versus knowing how much it covers.

My provisional list, which I want you to correct, prune, and extend: (i) independence — the deliverance must not quietly borrow from logical inference, from the general reliability of clear and distinct perception, from the meaningfulness of one's own words, or from memory, since the doubt is precisely what puts those in question; (ii) transfer — it must yield something usable as a premise, which raises the therefore problem: if the cogito is a deductive step it seems to need a major premise to the effect that whatever thinks exists, and the source and status of that premise are themselves in dispute, while if it is not a step at all but something closer to a self-verifying avowal, it is unclear what it transfers to; (iii) ownership — a reading must say whether what is given is that thinking is going on or that I exist and that the thinking is mine, without helping itself, at the instant of insight, to a persisting thinking substance; (iv) non-triviality — the deliverance must be informative rather than a disguised tautology; (v) demon-proofness, in the sense that a deceiver could not make it false while I have it, together with an account of whether that is bought by thinning the content and whether that purchase is legitimate; (vi) stability across iteration and time — whether each run covers only the instant of its own run, whether intervening doubt erodes it, and whether re-running restores it in full. To that I want to add one requirement of my own, which I have not seen discussed and which I suspect is the one that matters most for the job I have fixed: call it detachability, or the memo problem. Whatever the cogito gives me is given in the act of thinking itself, but the project I want it to ground is extended in time — I have to write the result down, read my own note later, re-identify myself as the same thinker, and then reason from it. So a reading must either explain how the deliverance detaches from the occasion of its occurrence without inheriting only memory-grade certainty, or explain why a foundation that decays on detachment cannot do the work I am asking of it.

The part I care about most is whether some subset of these is jointly unsatisfiable. I have two suspicions and want your verdict on both, plus any further tension you find: first, that independence and transfer pull apart, since every way of making the cogito premise-free seems to make it non-transferring, and every way of making it transfer seems to import a premise it cannot yet afford; second, that independence plus non-triviality plus detachability cannot all be met at once, because the thinner and more occasion-bound the deliverance is, the safer it is and the less it can bear the weight of a system, which would make the list itself the interesting result rather than any reading's score on it. Then, briefly: which single requirement does the most exclusionary work, and which requirement on my list is really just a prejudice of one camp that I should drop? Finish with a short scorecard — three lines each, no more, since the criteria are the deliverable and not the scoring — against the four readings I keep running into, described here so that nothing depends on the scholarship: the reading on which it is a deductive argument with a suppressed premise; the reading on which it is not an argument at all but a self-verifying performance; the thin reading on which all that is given is that thinking is occurring, with no subject; and the reading on which it is an intuition of the intellect in which a universal principle is seen in a particular instance.
