I'm enrolled this term in a graduate seminar on rational choice and the foundations of decision theory, and we have reached the part of the syllabus where the room divides into two camps that mostly argue past each other. For my term paper I want to attempt the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between them would actually produce — not a verdict on who is right, not a staged debate, and not an account of why the literature has stalled.

Camp A treats the coherence arguments as genuinely normative: an agent whose preferences violate the structural axioms is criticizable, the sure-loss constructions and the money-pump arguments are what exhibit the defect, and the representation theorems describe what coherent preference looks like from the outside, namely expected-utility maximization with respect to some probability function and utility function. Camp B treats those theorems as measurement results rather than norms: they say how to read probabilities and utilities off preferences that already satisfy the axioms, and so they cannot tell the agent who violates them that she has made a mistake. On Camp B's view the only real requirements are thin ones such as statewise dominance, and the arguments that reach violations of independence smuggle in premises that are themselves in dispute — that willingness to accept a book of bets separates across its component bets, or that preferences over plans must line up with preferences over the acts that compose them.

Please draft the document both could sign, with these components.

1. A numbered agreed-claims section, at most eight items: what both sides accept about the formalism, about what the theorems do and do not deliver, and about what a successful normative argument would have to look like. Nothing vacuous — each claim should be one a skeptic in either camp would resist if it were weakened.
2. A disagreements table. Each row should give: the crux, understood as a sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side; the statement of that camp's position phrased so that camp itself would accept the wording; the case, argument, or hypothetical both sides agree bears on it; and each side's prediction about how that case comes out, recorded in advance.

Four things I want the document to confront rather than skirt. First, the difference between synchronic exploitability — accepting a package that guarantees a loss by your own lights — and diachronic exploitability, where each individual trade is locally licensed by your preferences at the moment you make it but the sequence bleeds you; say whether the second is a defect for an agent who can bind herself or who declines to evaluate plans act-by-act. Second, a test rule: the lower-decile rule, on which you rank acts by the tenth-percentile outcome in your own ordering of outcomes, breaking ties at the twentieth percentile and so on. It is complete and transitive and it respects statewise dominance, but it violates independence. Does any money-pump argument reach it, and if so which premise does the work? Third, the empty-representability worry: if utility may be state-dependent and the state space may be refined at will, is the existential claim that some probability function and utility function represent the agent close to vacuous, and what would each side add to make it bite? Fourth, whether some of the dispute is verbal — in particular whether the two camps are using rationality to mean internal coherence on one hand and instrumental success or immunity to exploitation on the other — and, if so, which rows of the table survive the paraphrase.

Please also include one empirical crux on which the two camps make opposing predictions and would both accept the result as evidence: for instance, what happens when subjects who violate independence in a one-shot incentivized choice are then walked through the corresponding sequential trades.

Format the piece as the numbered agreed-claims section followed by the disagreements table, with a short closing note on which parts of the dispute you take to be merely verbal and why. Keep the whole document under 800 words, table included.
