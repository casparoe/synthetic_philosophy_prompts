In my immigration reading group one guy keeps shutting things down with 'you can't have open borders and a welfare state' — most recently against my claim that a rich country could unilaterally open its borders and keep its current tax-and-transfer system more or less as it is. I can never tell whether he means something boring and empirical (too many people come, the budget blows up), something about solidarity (open borders erode the support redistribution needs), or something conceptual, like that a welfare state just is a scheme of cooperation among bounded members and the two are incompatible by definition. Can you give me the menu of precise claims that one-liner could be making, with a verdict on each — platitude, plausible, contested, false — and say which readings would actually do the work he needs?

Two things I want built in. First, split welfare into contributory/earned stuff (earnings-related pensions, unemployment insurance) vs means-tested or unconditional transfers; the slogan might only be about one of them. Second, a fix I've been playing with: vesting — a migrant's claim on the non-contributory tier ramps up with years of residence and taxes paid, near zero on arrival, full after say ten years. Under vesting, which readings still bite?
