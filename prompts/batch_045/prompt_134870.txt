The setup, so we don't talk past each other. Harsanyi's aggregation theorem says roughly: if each individual's preferences over lotteries on social outcomes satisfy the vNM axioms, if social preferences do too, and if the social ordering respects Pareto indifference (when everyone is indifferent between two lotteries, so is society), then social welfare is representable as a weighted sum of individual vNM utility functions. Add a symmetry/equal-treatment condition and you get equal weights; fix the population and you get average utility. The usual way of making it intuitive is the impartial-observer version: imagine you have an equal chance of turning out to be anyone in the society, keep your own tastes but not your own position, and choose. Maximize expected utility and you are maximizing average utility.

An economist in my department sat in on my graduate seminar on aggregation last week and used exactly that to close down discussion afterwards. His line: this is not a separate ethical argument at all, it is just ordinary decision under uncertainty, the state variable happens to be who you are, it is on a par with buying fire insurance, and anybody who accepts expected utility for individuals and also cares about impartiality is already committed. On that basis he said prioritarianism, maximin, and the rank-weighted stuff we had been reading are just irrational risk-aversion dressed up as morality, and the seminar should have ended in week two.

I think the analogy is carrying the whole thing, and I want the audit before I answer him. My own suspicion, which you should push on rather than agree with: the mapping quietly treats who-you-are as just one more coordinate of the state, alongside the weather and the interest rate — call it identity-as-another-coordinate — and the entire interpersonal-comparison problem is smuggled in with that one move, because in ordinary risk the state doesn't determine which utility function applies to the outcome while here it does. I can't tell whether that is genuinely better or whether it just builds the anti-utilitarian conclusion into the frame.
