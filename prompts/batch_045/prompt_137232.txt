My friend and I had a three-hour pub arguement about the units of selection that went nowhere, because we kept saying things like "obviously the gene's eye view is right" and "no, group selection is real" and meaning totally different things by them. So I want to build a decision tree that sorts a person by what they say about cases, not by which label they like. Roughly ten nodes, I'm going to print it out and go through it with him.

How I want it built: start from something we all agree on (a population of things that vary and leave different numbers of descendants), then branch on concrete yes/no claims or cases, each phrased so you can answer it without knowing what position it leads to. So not "do you think groups are units of selection?" but "in this scenario, what spreads, and what would have to change for something else to spread."

Cases that have to be in there:
1. a sterile worker that dies defending a colony of close kin;
2. a meiotic-drive allele that sweeps through a population even though every male carrying it is half-sterile;
3. cancer — cell lineages that win inside a body while killing the body;
4. one I made up, precisely because it isn't in the literature: a colonial species where next season's germline cells are not descended from this season's germline at all — they get recruited fresh each cycle from whichever cell lineage won a competition inside the colony. Colonies whose internal lineages are less competitive make more propagules; within any one colony the most competitive lineage takes the germline slot. Nobody has written about this, so just handle it on the merits.

Leaves that must appear: genic/gene's-eye selectionism; kin selection / inclusive fitness; multilevel selection in both flavors (trait-group style with no group-level reproduction, and groups-as-individuals with their own heredity); the major-transitions / hierarchy view; the "Darwinian individuals" or "Darwinian populations" approach; and the deflationary "it's all the same bookkeeping, pick your partition" position. Add leaves if the logical space has room.

One node should turn on whether a level needs to reproduce as a lineage in order to count as a level of selection at all, versus just aggregating effects from below. At the end, tell me which answer-combinations are unoccupied and whether that's because they're incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored, and flag any two leaves the tree can't actually pull apart (I suspect the deflationist is hiding somewhere).
