I convene a twice-yearly briefing on penal policy for legislative staff who actually draft criminal-justice bills, and I want to replace the ritual opening exercise — where everyone places themselves on a line from "tough" to "soft" — with an instrument that predicts something. The observations I want to explain are these: the same staffer will often vote to shorten a sentence and to make conditions harsher, or to build a Halden-style facility (unarmed staff, private rooms, an explicit principle that the punishment is the deprivation of liberty and not the conditions of confinement) while extending a renewable preventive-confinement track for a small category of offenders. "Punishment versus rehabilitation" does not cut along the lines the disagreement actually runs on.

So I want a classification instrument: a decision tree or scored quiz that sorts a person by what they say about cases and trades, not by which label they accept. My proposed skeleton is that there are three separate dials and that most of the confusion comes from people assuming they are wired together:

1. the intake dial — how many people are confined at all, and for which conduct;
2. the duration dial — how long they stay;
3. the conditions dial — how hard the stay is, and how closely it is allowed to resemble life outside.

Build the logical space from these dials and from whatever further splitting questions you think do real work, then locate the named positions in it — desert-based views (including the version that only sets upper and lower bounds and leaves the rest to consequences), deterrence, incapacitation and risk-management, the rehabilitative or "medical" model, communicative or denunciatory views, restorative justice, decarceration, and abolitionism, plus at least one leaf for the position that humane conditions are owed independently of any view about sentence length. Eight to twelve leaves. Where a cell of the space is occupied by nobody, mark it "unnamed" and commit to one of four diagnoses: incoherent, merely unattractive, collapses into a neighbouring leaf, or genuinely unexplored — and say which, with a reason. Where two leaves cannot be separated by your own questions, flag the pair and either supply the extra case that would separate them or say that no case will.

Some constraints on the diagnostic questions, because these are what the whole thing stands on:

- No question may use the words retribution, deterrence, rehabilitation, restorative, incapacitation, or abolition, and none may ask the respondent to endorse a school, a theorist, or a country. Each question is a case or a forced trade, answerable by an intelligent non-specialist in under a minute.
- I want at least eight questions, and I want these cases among them, or better replacements if you think a case fails to discriminate: an offender who actively wants to be confined and prefers the prison to what is outside; an offender whose community would treat a long sentence as an honour; a sentence served under guard in a comfortable apartment, and whether that counts as punishment at all; a punitive burden the offender never notices; a victim who demands a sentence far longer than any risk assessment supports; a rehabilitative programme that works but is effectively coerced; the mirroring problem — if conditions inside are supposed to approximate life outside, what do you do when life outside is very unequal; and the conversion question, i.e. the exchange rate between harshness and duration: how many months of humane confinement someone would accept in place of one month of punitive confinement, and what it means about them that the rate is finite, infinite, or undefined.
- I also want the instrument to say something about the Halden-versus-American-mass-incarceration comparison specifically, since that is what my attendees arrive talking about: at minimum, each leaf should carry one line about what it predicts for a bill that simultaneously (a) improves conditions and (b) leaves sentence length untouched, and for one that does the reverse.

If you think one of my three dials is the wrong joint, replace it and tell me explicitly which one you replaced and why.

Please be committed rather than even-handed: pick the joints, choose between rival splitting questions, delete any leaf you judge incoherent instead of describing it neutrally, split any standard position you think is really two positions, and then name the three editorial choices of yours that you think are most worth arguing with, with your reasons. Where a leaf is defensible but you find it wrong, say so in one line rather than withholding.

Format. Give me: (1) a short prose preamble, under 250 words, stating the dials and joints you chose and what the instrument is and is not sensitive to; (2) a table of questions with columns id | scenario (one sentence) | options (short, lettered) | axis | what each option routes toward | confound risk; (3) a table of leaves with columns id | name or "unnamed" | answer pattern | core claim in one line | most awkward commitment | predicted stance on the two bills above; then (4) a single JSON object with these keys: "dials" (array of objects with id, name, values, why_it_is_separable_from_the_others), "axes" (array with id, splitting_question, why_this_splits_positions, replaced_my_question as boolean, and reason when true), "questions" (array with id, scenario, options as an array, axis_id, routing as an object from option to next axis or leaf id, confound_risk, and nonspecialist_checked as boolean), "leaves" (array with id, name_or_unnamed, pattern, core, awkward_commitment, bill_prediction, halden_stance), "empty_cells" (array with pattern, diagnosis drawn from exactly ["incoherent","unattractive","collapses_into","unexplored"], and explanation), "indistinguishable_pairs" (array with pair as two leaf ids, why, and separator_question or null), "scoring" (an object with rules as an array of strings, mixed_patterns describing how you score respondents whose answers land them in no clean leaf, and known_failure_modes as an array), and "editorial" (an object with choices_made as an array of strings, most_contestable as an array of exactly three strings, and positions_split_or_merged as an array of strings).
