Me and my friend Dev spent about three hours in the pub last night argueing about moral trade — that thing where two people with different values each do what's cheap for them and valuable to the other, without either of them endorsing the other's values. (I give $200 to the wetland trust Sam cares about, Sam gives $200 to the malaria charity I'd have given to anyway.) Dev's position was basically pure gains from trade: if each of us prefers the package deal to no deal at all, do it, end of story. When I pushed back she added something like a veil-of-ignorance clause — the arrangement should be one you'd agree to without knowing which side of it you'd end up on. I sort of liked that but I'm not sure it earns its keep.

We got nowhere because we kept fighting about cases, so here are mine, going by gut:

- Sam, $200 each to the other's cause. Obviously fine.
- Guy from my climbing gym, young-earth creationist, raising money for a museum. He'll give $500 to my charity if I give $500 to the museum. Not fine. I think the museum makes the world worse and I want no part of financing it.
- The priest at the church on my street: he'll give $200 to my charity if I give $200 to their soup kitchen. Fine — even though I think some of what that church teaches does real harm to real people, and even though the money is just as fungible here as it is in the museum case. I can't tell you what the difference is between these two, and I'm not sure there is one.
- Marcus: he's about to buy a huge truck. He'll buy a small one instead and give the difference to my charity if I lay off meat for a month. Fine.
- Marcus, different conversation: he says he'll spend Saturday keying SUVs unless I lay off meat for a month. Not fine at all, and it doesn't even feel like the same kind of thing, even though the shape is identical — he does something I like, conditional on me doing something.
- A local campaign: they'll give $300 to my charity if I sign their petition, whose framing I think is straight-up dishonest. Not fine. I won't put my name to something misleading for $300 — even though I'd hand them $300 out of my giving budget, or just redirect it from my own cause, without blinking. That asymmetry probably doesn't survive scrutiny, but it's what I feel.
- Scale: I'd happily trade $200, and I'd happily trade $2,000, but if someone offered to move most of my annual giving into trades on genuinely great terms I'd balk. No idea whether that's cowardice or a real point.
- Future me: I sign up with a site that takes my $1,000 and burns it, or gives it to a cause I'm indifferent about, unless I've made my yearly donation by December. Fine — enthusiastic, even. Dev says that's not a trade at all, just a commitment device, and my principle shouldn't have to cover it.

And here's what I genuinely can't decide: trading with someone who's just wrong about the facts — the guy who thinks his pet charity saves a life for a couple hundred bucks when I'm pretty sure he's off by an order of magnitude, and who'll fund my cause if I fund his PR push; trading with a stranger on a forum where neither of us can check whether the other actually did their bit; and a matching platform where everyone deposits an "I'll do X if matched" pledge and an algorithm assigns counterparties, so I end up serving some cause I've never heard of and can't evaluate.

So: is there a principle, or a short list of candidates, that captures my verdicts? State it precisely enough that I can test it, tell me which clause is doing the work in which case, be honest about which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and which one you'd throw out, and run it on the three I can't decide. If gains from trade plus Dev's veil thing is basically right and I'm the one who's confused, say that, and say where. One more: is the "you can't verify compliance" issue a moral problem or just plumbing? Dev says plumbing. It feels more moral than that to me, but I definately can't say why.
