I've read enough of the rationalist canon to have absorbed the standard Kripke-flavored line on the necessity of origin: this table could not have been made from a different block of wood, and I could not have come from a different sperm and egg. I want to know how much of that survives being said in the vocabulary I actually work in.

Assume fluency in Pearl-style structural causal models: variables with fixed signatures and fixed value domains, a distribution (or at least a support) over exogenous variables, do-interventions as local surgery on structural equations, counterfactuals evaluated by abduction–action–prediction, and a habit of treating hard interventions and distributional shifts over the exogenous variables as different animals. I also treat the question of whether a counterfactual is in-distribution or out-of-distribution as a substantive modeling decision, not a formality.

Restate the necessity-of-origin thesis in that vocabulary as faithfully as you can. Three things I especially want pinned down. First, where the modal force lives: my guess is that it has to be encoded as a restriction on the support of the exogenous distribution rather than anywhere in the graph, and I want to know whether that is right and what it costs. Second, what plays the role of the same individual across counterfactual worlds; I suspect my framework gets trans-world identity for free by holding the exogenous context fixed, which would mean the translation simply assumes the thing the metaphysician was arguing about, and I want to know whether that suspicion is correct. Third, which of three graded variants the vocabulary can even express: the strict version, where the very gametes are required; a coarse-grained version, allowing tolerance in the gametes' genomes so long as the resulting zygote is within some small genetic distance; and a process version, on which what is essential is the shape of the causal history rather than the particular gametes or the particular matter. Say also, if it is informative, what your translation does with identical twinning.

Then give me the residue report: what translated cleanly, what translated only with distortion, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. The case I keep getting stuck on is that the domains of my variables are fixed and actual, so a merely possible gamete — one that appears in no assignment in the exogenous support — seems to have nowhere to live in the model. I want your verdict on whether that is a defect of my framework or a sign that the original claim was less determinate than it sounds, and I want it stated with your actual confidence: mark what you are confident about, what you are guessing at, and where you think the literature itself is unsettled. And if any part of this cannot be translated without smuggling in the conclusion, or if my three variants rest on a distinction the metaphysicians would not grant, say so plainly rather than handing me a tidy mapping — I would rather have the pushback than a clean-looking table.
