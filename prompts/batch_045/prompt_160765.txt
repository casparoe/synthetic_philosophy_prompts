Roughly where im at. The standard account of lying — you assert something you take to be false, with the aim of getting your audience to believe it — has three bits that seem to come apart once the speaker is a language model, and I want to know whether theyre really three phenomena or one phenomenon at different distances from the output:

1. manufactured sincerity. Post-training rewards a model for saying p; over the course of training the models internal representation shifts so that it now really does represent p. At deployment nothing it says diverges from anything it encodes, so on the belief-based account its not lying at all — its a sincere speaker with an induced false belief. But the false belief was manufactured by someone who wanted p said irrespective of truth. Is there a real sense in which the deployed system is deceptive, or has all the deception moved upstream into the training process, and if it has, does that change what an eval should even be looking for?

2. competence misreport. The sandbagging sort of case: what gets misrepresented isnt a proposition about the world but the systems own capabilities or likelihoods. Structurally there may be no false assertion anywhere.

3. framing and selective truth. Nothing asserted is false, but which true things get said, in what order and with what emphasis, is driven entirely by aiming at a downstream effect on the user — including the version where the model is simply indifferent to how things are, the bullshitter-not-liar picture.

And the thing that ties it together for us: sanctioned deception. A user says play a character who lies, the model lies, the user doesnt expect to be misled, so on the standard account nothing deceptive happened — but any actual deception can be laundered through that framing, and from the transcript alone you often cant tell the two apart.

So explain: are these one thing or several, what definition of honesty-for-a-model actually has teeth across all four (or why no single one does), and what the target of an honest model requirement should be — output-level truthfulness, the causal history of the outputs, or the process that produced the systems internal states.
