I teach ancient philosophy and I was putting together a problem set on Zeno when I backed into an argument I can't find the exit from. My students will ask me abou this on Monday, so: help.

(1) Call a sequence of tasks *chained* when each one has to be finished before the next is begun. A chained sequence with no last member can never be finished: to have finished the lot you'd have to have reached a stage at which none of them is still owed, and at every stage there are tasks further down the line still owed.

(2) Achilles runs from 0 to 1 in a minute. Take his tasks to be: cross [0, 1/2], cross [1/2, 3/4], cross [3/4, 7/8], and so on. They are chained — he can't cross [1/2, 3/4] until [0, 1/2] is behind him — there are infinitely many of them, and there is no last one, since getting to 1 isn't on the list.

(3) Finishing the list just is getting to 1. So by (1) Achilles never gets there.

(4) Now run the same argument on the minute instead of the runner. For [0, 1] to be over, [0, 1/2] has to be over before [1/2, 3/4] is, and that before [3/4, 7/8], and so on: chained, endless, no last member. So the minute is never over. Run it the other way and it never got started either. No stretch of time ever elapses.

(5) That is more than I can swallow. I'll entertain a puzzle about motion; I won't conclude that the clock can't tick. But (2) and (4) look like the same claim in two costumes, one about a path and one about a stretch of time, so I don't get to fix one and keep the other.

I tried to block it by turning the sub-journeys into seperate events rather than one smooth motion chopped up by a description: Achilles crosses [0, 1/2] in the first quarter minute, rests for an eighth, crosses [1/2, 3/4] in a sixteenth, rests for a thirty-second, and so on. Still infinitely many legs, still no last leg, and he's at 1 by the half-minute. That seems to make (1) flatly false — except that then I don't know what to say on Wednesday about the lamp that's switched on for the first half minute, off for the next quarter, on for the next eighth, and so on: whether it's on or off at the minute looks genuinely unsettleable, and "it's all done by the deadline" looks like the very move that was supposed to save Achilles.

Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd rather keep (2) and (4) than go atomist about space and time — two rows of soldiers marching past each other at one atom per tick seem to force half-ticks, and I don't want that either. But if the answer is that (1) is false, I want to know what the principled difference is between Achilles and the lamp, and what I'm committing to when I say an endless chained list can be finished.
