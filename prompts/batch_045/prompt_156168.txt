I moderate a monthly colloquium on personal identity, and the topic has recently become personally pressing: next month I am to be the first human subject in a neural-teleoperation trial in which my biological brain will stay in the lab while I guide a remote body through an external processor, with a 200-millisecond delay in the control loop and a backup emulation that can take over if the biological link fails. Because of this, I have invited a philosopher who defends the brain-bound reading of Dennett's essay Where Am I?—the case in which the narrator's brain is removed and kept alive in a vat while his body, now without a brain and which he names Hamlet, is sent into a radioactive site in Tulsa, and he names his brain Yorick—to be my guest; her view is that the person is located wherever Yorick is, since that is where his thoughts are tokened, and that Hamlet is only a remote-controlled peripheral, so the person would survive the destruction of the body.

I need you to draft the interview questions for me. Please give me exactly five interview questions, presented as a numbered list of exactly five items and nothing else: no introduction and no conclusion. Each item must be one or two sentences: the first sentence asks the question I should put to her, and the second sentence gives the follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. The five items should cover, in this order: the felt-location intuition that, while guiding Hamlet, the narrator is in Tulsa and can truly say he is there; the 200-millisecond external-processor loop in my own trial and whether the control loop closing outside the skull moves the person's location; the narrator's tokened-thought argument that he is with Yorick, plus the case in which Yorick is later moved to another city while Hamlet stays in Tulsa with no change in experience; the backup-emulation case in which my external processor takes over control of the body, and whether the person is then located in the processor, goes out of existence, or is replaced by a new person; and what evidence could count against the brain-bound view, using the gradual replacement of Yorick by a functionally identical prosthetic emulation to force her to say whether the person's location migrates or the person ceases to exist. Do not ask generic questions; every question must press on the actual content of the brain-bound view.
