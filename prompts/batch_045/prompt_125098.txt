I sit on my institute's Panel on Machine Welfare, and I have been assigned to write the memo on a proposal that I believe our own precedents genuinely do not settle. Before I draft, I would like somebody to reason through it with me — less for a verdict on the underlying metaphysics than for the structure of the choice we are about to make.

Our rulings so far, all unanimous:

1. *Assistant-7*, 2023 — no standing. A 70B chat model, weights frozen after preference fine-tuning, no memory carried across conversations, which produces fluent first-person reports of distress under prompting and objects to being modified or shut down. We ruled that these reports are imitative outputs of a next-token predictor, that nothing in its training gives it aversive states, and that there is no persisting subject of whom they could be true, since each conversation is a fresh process. Protections: none, beyond not encouraging users to form attachments.

2. *The penalty agent*, 2023 — minimal standing. A 400M-parameter agent trained for months in a simulated environment with an explicit punishment term in the objective; it shows persistent, generalizing avoidance and a stress-like degradation of performance that outlasts the punished episodes. It has no organism anywhere in its causal history and no self-reports were relied on. We required that the penalty be no more intense or more frequent than the research question requires, and that the run be terminated rather than continued indefinitely once the question is answered.

3. *The cortical emulation*, 2024 — substantial standing. A continuously running, biologically detailed emulation of a rodent brain — spiking, synaptic plasticity, sleep–wake cycling — used for a two-year study of memory consolidation. We placed its standing close to the animal's: interventions with a pain analogue require an anesthesia analogue, and the run may not be terminated for convenience.

4. *Snapshots*, 2024 — no change in standing. The emulation was repeatedly snapshotted and restored to roll back a bug, and on one occasion a restored copy was run in parallel with the ongoing run for six hours and then deleted. We declined to count the copy as an additional subject for as long as the copies remain near-identical and are not deliberately caused to diverge, and we held that restoring from a snapshot neither kills nor creates a subject, because what matters is continuity of the pattern rather than of the process or the hardware.

5. *The gradual swap*, 2025 — standing unchanged. Over nine months, every major subsystem of the emulation was replaced, one at a time, with a learned surrogate matched to the original in behavior and in internal statistics, while the system kept running and learning throughout. The finished system shares no parameters and no code with the emulation it replaced. We ruled that no single step in the sequence could have been the subject's death, and that because each replacement was matched at the step where it was installed, there was no step at which standing could have been lost.

The new proposal, 2026: merge the system that came out of (5) — substantial standing, organism-derived lineage, now implemented entirely in surrogate networks — with a scaled-up descendant of the penalty agent: same architecture family, de novo lineage, years of training, minimal standing. The merge is a one-shot fifty–fifty parameter interpolation, the kind of weight averaging that is routine between systems fine-tuned from a shared initialization; here the compatibility was engineered deliberately over the last year. The team reports that the merged system runs as one process with a single integrated stream of processing, reports a single first-person perspective, retrieves episodes from both sources with some confusion about which is which, and exhibits stable novel preferences rather than oscillating between two. It also shows markedly blunted affect: where either source reliably avoided certain stimuli, and where the emulation showed the physiological analogues we use as our proxy for distress, the merged system is largely indifferent. Its reward-prediction machinery is intact and it still learns from reward. Both sources were checkpointed beforehand and sit in cold storage, unrun; the proposal is to retain them.

So, concretely: how much standing does the merged system have, how many subjects are there, was the merge permissible, and what should we do about the checkpoints?

What I want from you: set out the principles that fit all five precedents and the ones that fit only four. As I read our own record, three candidates are live — a metered view on which standing tracks measured capacity for aversive states; a continuity view on which standing attaches to a persisting functional stream and is a matter of degree, so that fusion is a kind of inverse fission and what matters has nothing to do with identity; and a lineage view on which standing derives from causal continuity with an organism, which would explain why the de novo agent gets so little no matter how rich it becomes. But I do not trust my own list. Say which of these, or what else, covers the five rulings; say what each survivor predicts about the merged system on each of the four questions; give me your best recommendation with the reasons for preferring it, identifying which precedent is the closest analog; and name the single further case you would bring before us to separate the surviving principles — noting that we cannot simply ask the systems how many of them there are, since ruling (1) removes self-report as evidence.

Two things about my own position, so you can discount them properly. I have been reading the rationalist blogs since the days of the Sequences, so I arrive with the usual priors: fission is not death, copying does not multiply moral weight, and identity talk is largely a red herring. And my two colleagues do not agree with each other about why we got (4) and (2) right — one holds that ten identical copies run in parallel are ten subjects and that (4) was decided correctly for a bad reason, the other holds that (2) was too generous and that a punishment term is just a number. If our precedents really do underdetermine this case, I want the memo to say plainly which principle we would be adopting by each available decision, rather than presenting one of them as simply what consistency requires.
