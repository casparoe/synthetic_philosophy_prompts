My brother relapsed in March and I've more or less stopped blaming him for it. Galen Strawson's Basic Argument did most of that work for me: you do what you do because of the way you are, to be truly responsible for what you do you'd have to be responsible for the way you are, and you can't be — you'd have to have chosen your own character, which would need a prior character you were responsible for, and so on forever. So there's nothing to blame. Fine, I can live there.

But a friend of mine who works on epistemology ran an extension past me on Sunday and I can't find the exit:

(1) The Basic Argument is sound: no one is ever truly morally responsible for anything.
(2) Nothing in it is specifically moral. Its premises are about action, character and causation in general. So if moral blameworthiness needs ultimate self-origination, then every other kind of desert needs it too.
(3) Epistemic credit and blame — being reasonable, dogmatic, closed-minded, careful, credulous — is just desert applied to belief-forming, and you form beliefs the way you do because of the way you are.
(4) So no one ever deserves credit or criticism for any belief at all.
(5) Which means no one is ever criticisable for dogmatism or wishful thinking, and no one — my friend and I included, right now — deserves any credit for getting an argument right, so the argument can't even be offered as something we have reason to accept. It eats itself.
(6) That's absurd, and not in an interesting way.

I want to keep (1), or something close to it — I need it for my brother. I also want to keep saying that some reasoning is bad reasoning and that some people really are closed-minded. So which step do I give up? Explain why it looked fine, and price me the alternatives: what does each rejection cost me? Please answer in British English, and no reading lists or citations — I've got no library access out here, so just work the argument itself.
