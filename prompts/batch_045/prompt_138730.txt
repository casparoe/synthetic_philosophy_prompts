I've been reading about moral worth informally, and there is a pattern I keep running into that I can't name. Two cases first. A man donates a kidney to a stranger; asked afterwards why, he says he never really weighed it — the hospital sent round a note about a match, he was a match, and that was that. Nothing in his account looks like a reason he acted on, and yet everyone I describe this to thinks his action is morally better, not worse, than that of a second man who spent a fortnight deliberating and gave because he concluded it was what he had most reason to do. Second: a woman refuses to lie for a friend, and when pressed for a reason is briefly at a loss and then says only that she doesn't lie, while a colleague in the same situation gives a fluent account about autonomy and trust, and would have lied in a nearby variant of the case where the balance came out differently. Neither contrast, I think, is a contrast in sincerity.

What I keep noticing is that whether these agents had the right reason seems to depend on who is asking and in what mode. The donor's reason can be supplied by an observer — that a stranger would otherwise die — and he would accept it if it were offered to him, but he was not moved by it under that description, and had he stopped to ask himself whether it was a reason he might have produced something cruder. The colleague's reason is fully present in her own deliberation and yet seems to explain nothing about what she does, since some equally fluent rationale would have been available to her either way. So there appear to be two standpoints: the reason as attributable to the agent from outside, and the reason as operative in her own deliberation. Moral worth seems to want the first in one pair of cases and the second in the other — or perhaps to want both at once in each, which would be worse.

Does this have an established name, or an established literature behind it? I have seen talk of acting for the right reasons and of reasons-responsiveness, but as I understand them those concern whether the reason is the right one and whether the agent's behaviour tracks it, not which standpoint the reason has to be present in. A bare label won't help me: I want the nearest concepts and, for each, exactly how the thing I have described differs from it, and which fits best — or, if nothing fits, a coined term with a definition plus the boundary cases that would settle a hard instance. Please also mark where you are going beyond the record: separate what is settled in this area from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it, and don't present a contested view as the consensus. No bibliography and no citations — I will chase those up myself — but do give me the term if there is one. Exactly five numbered items, at most two sentences each, with no introduction and no closing sentence.
