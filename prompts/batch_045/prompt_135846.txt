I'm a philosopher in a department that mostly does logic and decision theory, and I'm currently supervising a third-year dissertation on the doomsday argument — the version where you're meant to treat yourself as a random draw from everyone who will ever have lived, notice that your birth rank is unremarkably early given how fast the population has been growing, and conclude that the total probably isn't going to be much bigger than what's already happened. I'm a bit out of my depth once cosmology enters the room, but I've now sat through three supervision meetings where the same dialectical shape kept turning up, and I can't find a word for it. The first half of it is this: attempts to block the conclusion by narrowing who counts as a sample don't seem to defeat the argument so much as relocate it. Say I retreat to humans who have actually thought about the doomsday argument, or to people born after 1900, or to academics, or to readers of a particular book-length defence of it from the nineties — the same reasoning just runs on that class instead, and now it tells me that *that* class will end soon, which is a different prediction and, in just about every case we tried, a sillier one. It behaves less like an argument being answered and more like an argument sliding sideways into whatever category you retreat into; I've been calling it the hydra property in my notes, because narrowing the reference class doesn't kill it, it grows you a new one.

The second half, which may or may not be the same phenomenon: the one move that does seem to kill it — treating your own existence as evidence that the class is enormous, so that finding yourself early in a huge class is exactly what you would have expected — appears to commit you, elsewhere, to inferring the size of the universe or the number of civilisations from the bare fact that you exist, which is the case everyone treats as a reductio. So the absurdity looks like it can be moved around but not reduced: one setting buys you an imminent-end prediction nobody believes, the other buys you an existence-proves-vastness inference nobody believes. My colleague, over a drink after a research seminar, pointed out that the same trick would predict our departmental seminar series ends this term, since we're four weeks into an eight-week term, and I couldn't immediately say why that was funny. I also half-remember a well-known paper doing something like this with how long a wall, or a run of a show, was going to last, but I can never remeber whose it was or even which examples were in it — and I can't tell whether that's the same argument in disguise or a different one that happens to look identical from the outside.

So: does this have a name? I've tried to convince myself it's just the ordinary reference class problem — the one where a 55-year-old smoker's chance of dying before seventy depends on which class you file him under — but that isn't it, or isn't all of it, because there the choice of class changes the number and how confident you're entitled to be, whereas here it manufactures a fresh first-order prediction about the world that you then either have to believe or explicitly disown. It isn't helped by people waving at two sampling assumptions whose acronyms I can never keep straight, since the pattern I'm after survives that choice rather than being settled by it. There's a family resemblance to the old-evidence worry in confirmation theory, something about the prior you were supposed to have had before you learned where in the sequence you were, but I can't get the details to line up and I suspect the real issue is that the information is indexical. I've got a works-in-progress slot next month and I'd much rather talk about this than about whether the argument is sound, so I won't be satisfied with a label and a pat on the head: I want the carving. Tell me what the phenomenon is, what the nearest established concepts are and exactly where each diverges from it, whether the conservation bit is genuine or an artefact of how the prior is shaped, what the boundary cases are — does it show up in fine-tuning, does it show up in cases where we know the mechanism like the seminar series, is there an analogue anywhere in the non-identity literature — and if there's genuinely nothing on the shelf, what you'd call it, how you'd define it, which near misses you'd rule out, and whether my hydra and conservation-of-absurdity labels are any good.
