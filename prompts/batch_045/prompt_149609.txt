Here's roughly how it went, compressed:

1. Me: perdurantism doesn't really give you persistence, it gives you a four-dimensional sum of temporal parts, and nothing in that picture is ever wholly present at a time. But when I say I'm the same person who made a terrible decision ten years ago, I mean that *I* did it, not that some earlier segment of a worm did. So perdurantism quietly swaps identity for something weaker and then insists the substitute is just as good.

2. Priya: that's the standard complaint, but endurance has a worse version of the same problem — the problem of temporary intrinsics. If I'm wholly present while bent and wholly present while straight, and intrinsic properties are had outright rather than relative to a time, you either have incompatible properties or you have to index them to times (bent-at-t), at which point you've admitted the time-relativity that was supposed to separate the two views, and the whole dispute starts to look verbal.

3. Me: that only bites if intrinsicness can't tolerate temporal indexing, and bent-at-t isn't a relation to some *other* object, so it's still intrinsic in the sense that matters. Anyway perdurantists themselves need temporal locations for their parts, so they haven't avoided the commitment, they've just relocated it.

4. Priya: then take fission — one person, two equally continuous successors. Endurance has to say both are the original (which breaks identity being one-one), or that neither is, or that one arbitrarily is, and all three are bad. Perdurance just says there were always two overlapping chains of temporal parts that share everything up to the split, so both successors were there all along.

5. Me: clean, but that's a science-fiction case doing enormous work in a theory of ordinary objects, and the price is that before the split there were two people sharing all their parts who differ only in their future — two thinkers with identical pasts, which explains nothing. Worse, on your view *I'll be in pain tomorrow* isn't about me at all but about a later temporal part or counterpart, so prudential concern and ordinary anticipation start looking like errors.

6. Priya: the worm theorist answers that straight up — I *am* the whole worm, the tomorrow part is literally a part of me, so concern is fine; you only get the counterpart version if you go stage-theoretical, and you don't have to. Dev, at the end, the bit that shut us both up: why not say an object is three-dimensional and wholly present at every instant it exists, but additionally has, as proper parts, a growing trail of its own earlier phases — so at t the thing is wholly there and it also carries a trail that can later branch. Call it the accordion view. You'd get perdurantist machinery for fission (the trail branches) without giving up that the thing you're talking to is wholly present.
