I run a reading group in philosophy of physics for people who know undergraduate quantum mechanics but have never read the literature on the probability problem in no-collapse interpretations. I want a decision tree I can hand out: a flowchart that takes someone from the starting commitment "there is no collapse, and the post-measurement state contains multiple outcomes of sharply unequal squared amplitude" to a named position about where probabilistic talk comes from and why amplitude-squared should track anything an agent cares about.

Constraints on the tree:

1. Build it from the logical space, not from a list of familiar views. Give five or six branching nodes first, each phrased as a question about a concrete case that someone who has never heard of the debate could answer, and only then locate positions at the leaves.

2. The cases I want doing the diagnostic work: a branch of squared modulus one in a billion in which you win something enormous — is it worth paying a real cost to bet on it; a branch in which you survive a hazardous procedure but with badly degraded cognition for a year before recovering — does that branch's weight count fully in your deliberation; an agent who knows she will not care about anything after tonight — does she still apportion her expectations by squared amplitude; an agent whose branch is about to split non-uniformly among successors — does what matters to her now depend on her current amplitude or on the measure of the branches downstream; and a case in which the amplitude is spread over what look like continuously many outcomes, so that no finite counting of branches is available.

3. Six to nine leaves. Label each descriptively ("amplitude-squared is recovered from the agent's own preference structure", "amplitude-squared is a primitive physical weight, not a probability"), attach the conventional label only if you are confident one exists, and write "unnamed" otherwise. At least two leaves should be unoccupied-but-coherent cells rather than positions anyone defends — I especially want one on which squared amplitude is a real thickness of a branch but carries no probabilistic significance on its own, and one on which rational credence across branches is simply indeterminate. For each unoccupied cell say whether it is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

4. Every leaf gets one sentence naming its most awkward commitment, including the leaves that reject the project altogether — restore collapse, add hidden variables, or accept that the theory has no probabilities and let that count against it.

5. Flag any pair of leaves your questions cannot in fact separate, rather than inventing a further question to pry them apart.

Number the nodes, write the tree as nested "if yes, go to N" lines rather than prose, and give no references, attributions, or reading suggestions anywhere.
