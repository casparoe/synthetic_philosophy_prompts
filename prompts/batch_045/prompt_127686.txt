The fellowship I'm applying for requires a two-page proposal for a single dissertation chapter, and the chapter is on the ethics of judging historical agents — specifically on the "man of his time" defence, the move that does most of the work in the public arguments about Jefferson, Churchill, and Columbus. I don't want the chapter to be a case study of any of them; the cases are what makes the question matter, not the material I'd argue from. What I need from you is the proposal, not the argument.

Here is the half-formed idea I want turned into one.

The defence is standardly read as an epistemic excuse on the model of "he couldn't have known better" — the true moral belief was not available to someone in his position. But in the cases that actually generate controversy that is false in any flat sense: the true belief was held by people alive at the time, was often stated in print, and was sometimes pressed on the agent directly. So the flat reading can't be what is doing the work. I want to split the notion into two and see whether the defence survives on either:

- *availability*: someone in his epistemic situation could have arrived at the true belief; the evidence and the arguments were, as it were, in circulation.
- *attainability*: he could have arrived at it without costs of a sort we do not normally require an agent to bear as a condition of being blameworthy — social ruin, the collapse of a self-conception or a way of life, sustained effort against his own motivated reasoning.

My suspicion is threefold, and I don't know which part is the real claim. First, that the defence is plausible only on an attainability reading, so that what looks like an excuse is really a demandingness point: he fell short of a demanding standard rather than below a normal one. Second, that the defence is asked to do two different jobs that come apart, and that it may succeed at one and fail at the other — judgments of culpability (was it wrong, is he blameworthy, how much) versus what I'll call commemorative posture (is public honour fitting here, should the statue stand, should the building keep his name). The reasons for withdrawing honour need not be reasons about his blameworthiness, in which case a successful excuse would leave the practical question exactly where it was, and the people who invoke the defence are answering a question that was not asked. Third, I can't place the diachronic symmetry argument: if the defence is good for them it is good for us judged from our successors' vantage point, and people divide sharply between treating that as a constraint any account must satisfy and treating it as an emotional reaction to be discounted. I want the proposal to decide what role that argument plays rather than gesture at it.

Please write the proposal so that it contains:

1. A statement of the question narrow enough that one chapter could answer it, and stated so that a reader could say in advance what would and would not count as an answer. If my three suspicions are three questions rather than one, say which one the chapter is on and why that one first.
2. Why the two standard families of position leave it open — roughly, that historical distance makes judgment of past agents defective or inappropriate, and that it makes no moral difference at all — with the emphasis on what they share that I am rejecting. If they share only a framing assumption, name it.
3. The method, month by month across twelve months. I have no archival training and no access to manuscripts, so the project has to make progress without new empirical history; say why these questions are answerable from the armchair, and if any part of what I've sketched is not, say so and cut it in the proposal.
4. What a result would look like for someone who is not a philosopher and is actually deciding about a statue, a building name, or a school curriculum — even if the deliverable is only the deletion of a bad reason.
5. The possible conclusions and the considerations that would decide between them, then an honest account of what would count as the project having failed, and of the most likely way it would turn out to be too large for a year.

Do not include citations, references, or a bibliography; I'll supply those. Please write the proposal in American English.
