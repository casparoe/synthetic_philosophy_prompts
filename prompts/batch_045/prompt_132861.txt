I'm in an undergraduate seminar on formal systems and self-reference, and I've been asked to lead the final session, which is on the MIU system and on what it means to "jump out of the system." The system, as we've been using it: axiom MI; rules — append U to any string ending in I; double everything after the M; replace any occurrence of III with U; delete any occurrence of UU. MU is not a theorem, and the reason is invisible from inside the system: count the I's. Every theorem has a number of I's that is not a multiple of three, because doubling can't turn a non-multiple of three into a multiple of three (2 is invertible modulo 3), III→U removes exactly three, and the two U-rules leave the count untouched. MU asks for zero I's, and zero is a multiple of three. Our TA calls any quantity that all of a system's rules are blind to a "conserved residue," and the seminar's line is that jumping out just is finding one — which makes the jump less an escape from formality into intuition than a move to a second system in which the first one can be described.

I'd like a poem to close the session with: terza rima, six tercets plus the single unpaired closing line — nineteen lines — in loose iambic pentameter, in one voice throughout. The voice is someone who has been inside the system for a long time, applying the rules honestly, and who really does believe that persistence will reach MU; it should not turn into a different, wiser speaker at the end. It should stop applying rules and start counting, which is the entire point.

Use the form to carry that. Tercets one through four stay inside: each should end on a string that is a genuine theorem — I will check every one against the four rules, so no invented derivations; MUI is fine (MI → MII → MIIII → MUI), MUUII is fine (double to eight I's, then turn two adjacent blocks of III into U), but nothing with an I-count divisible by three is. Let the strings lengthen across these stanzas, and let near-misses accumulate — theorems that begin MU and then go on being something else — while the bare string MU itself is withheld until the verdict. Terza rima's chain, in which each stanza is generated from the last by a fixed rule, is itself the trap: the poem should feel like it is being derived. The turn comes in the fifth tercet, where the voice stops deriving and counts. The sixth delivers the verdict: the count never changes, and the target asks for zero. The closing line should be the only line in the poem that contains no string at all and is not part of a three — in a system where three is exactly what collapses into one — and it should speak about the standpoint the voice now occupies: open the question whether that standpoint is itself a system whose rules the voice cannot see from inside, and leave it open rather than settling it.

Two accuracy constraints, please. No name-dropping: no "Gödel," no "incompleteness," no "metamathematics." The MIU system is decidable and the underivability of MU has nothing to do with incompleteness; blurring the two is the mistake our professor keeps flagging. And the arithmetic has to be right, not gestured at — someone in my seminar should be able to reconstruct the whole argument from the last three stanzas and check every string you name, while someone who has never seen the puzzle should still find it a poem. No prose headnote, gloss, or epilogue: I want to hand it out cold.
