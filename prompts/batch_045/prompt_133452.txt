Hey — I'm on the board of my co-op and I have to vote on this next week and I cant tell whether my discomfort is a reason or just a reflex. Here's the setup: our town requires each household to clear its own sidewalk within 24 hours of a snowfall. In practice maybe a third of people actually do it and the bylaw officer has never once written a ticket. The proposal is to let a household pay $40 and have the town send its own crew instead. Two wrinkles: the $40 is set at exactly what the crew time costs, the town neither profits nor loses on it, and the crew is already out salting the bus stops anyway, so in most storms no extra worker gets hired — the payment isn't really purchasing anyone's labour, it's closer to reimbursing a cost. And whoever pays is still recorded as having complied.

One neighbour says this is just hiring a substitute for your share of a shared obligation, like paying someone to sit your jury service, and that it converts something neighbours owe each other into a service you can buy your way out of. Another says the opposite: that $40 is trivial for the people who'd use it and real money for the people who never would, so it's a way for the better-off to quietly exit a burden everyone else still carries. I feel the first one but I'm not sure whats being protected when the status quo is a rule nobody follows and nobody enforces.

I'm in political theory so Ive read around in the general markets stuff — please skip the 101 and the balanced pro/con list. What I want is: what feature of this particular design is actually doing the moral work, are my two neighbours making the same objection or different ones, and would changing that feature (pricing it at cost vs. at market, capping how many times a household can use it, making the payment anonymous, routing the money somewhere else) actually answer it — or does the objection survive every version of it?
