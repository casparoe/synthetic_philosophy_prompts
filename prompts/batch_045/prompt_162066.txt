I am writing the second chapter of a monograph on "information" in molecular biology, and before I score the existing accounts against each other I want the specification itself: what would a theory have to deliver for it to be literally true, and not merely a façon de parler, that DNA carries information — that the genetic code is a code?

Fix the purpose as follows: the theory has to be strong enough to settle a live dispute rather than to paraphrase textbook talk. The dispute is whether the developmental-systems and niche-construction critics are right that "information" can be redistributed onto methylation marks, maternal cytoplasmic gradients, and features of the nest, or whether something about DNA specifically survives that parity argument.

So give me the desiderata, each with the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a taste. The cases I expect handled include: a degenerate mapping in which several codons specify one amino acid and translation cannot be run backwards from protein to sequence; a mapping that is nearly universal yet has variants, including organellar reassignments and the recoding of a stop codon into a twenty-first or twenty-second amino acid; the gap between the codon–amino-acid mapping and what a whole genome says, given that protein-coding sequence is a small fraction of a mammalian genome and that one stretch of sequence has different effects under alternative splicing, a shifted reading frame, or parent-of-origin imprinting; the gap between what a sequence says and what happens, as in misincorporation and frameshift; and templating that copies without coding, as in prion conformations. I also want the theory to say which of the available metaphors it is endorsing — code, template, program, blueprint, law — and why.

Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a camp; tell me which pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidates. Skip the scorecard of named positions; I will do that myself.

Last, and most important to me: say what would change your mind about the list. Which finding or argument would make you drop or demote a requirement you have called non-negotiable, and where is your reasoning about the unsatisfiable pair most likely to fail?
