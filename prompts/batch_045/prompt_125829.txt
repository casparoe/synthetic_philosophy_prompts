Suppose Nietzsche were shown a scholarly arrangement that could not have arisen in his own time: a comprehensive Mnemonic Archive that has ingested every digitized text, returns any passage in a fraction of a second, and—unlike a human mind—forgets nothing, so that no text, event, or minor figure can ever sink below the horizon of the present. Students and scholars no longer read or memorize; they query it. Suppose, further, that the archive ranks whatever it retrieves by frequency of prior repetition, so that the texts it presents as monumental are simply the ones most often cited.

Drawing on his distinctions among monumental, antiquarian, and critical history, together with his claims about the necessity of forgetting and of a bounded horizon, how would Nietzsche diagnose this situation? Would he treat the archive as the consummation of one of his three modes, as a pathological fusion of all three, or as something his typology cannot capture—and if the latter, what fourth mode would he need to introduce? In your answer, keep separate (a) his account of what the archive does to a culture and (b) what he would take a genuinely critical history to require in response; and say whether he would think the faculty of forgetting could be restored by an act of will once such an archive exists, or only through the destruction of the archive itself.
