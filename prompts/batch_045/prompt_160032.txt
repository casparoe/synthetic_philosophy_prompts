Stuck on something and it's turning into a monster. Call a market a "fishbowl" if every trader's signal, model and order is visible to everyone, so nobody has private information and the way anyone came by their information is common knowledge.

(1) No-trade: if traders share a common prior, the structure by which they acquire information is common knowledge, and the initial allocation is ex-ante efficient, then nobody can profit from private information by trading — any bid you make reveals what you know and gets impounded into the price before anyone accepts it.
(2) A fishbowl is that assumption set turned up to eleven, so in a fishbowl no information-motivated trade happens at all.
(3) The epistemic case for markets is that prices are special: they compress scattered private knowledge into one number that nobody individually had.
(4) So at full transparency the price stops carrying information.
(5) Full opacity is no better — if nobody can see or respond to anyone else's quotes, there's no number for private signals to get pooled into.
(6) So the price's epistemic value peaks somehwere in the middle: mandate enough disclosure and you destroy the very thing that made the price worth reading.
(7) And (6) isn't really about markets. Any institution that's supposed to know more than its members — a forecasting panel, a refereeing process, a committee — pools by having members respond to each other's revealed positions, so making the process fully transparent should wreck it the same way.
(8) But (7) is nuts: open data, published models, pre-registration are supposed to make institutions more reliable, not less.

Which step do you reject, why did it look fine, and what do the other exits cost? If I have to choose I'd rather keep (1) and (2) than (7) — I'm attached to the idea that a fishbowl really would freeze — but I don't want to end up saying disclosure rules make the collective dumber.
