I've got no academic background at all — I run a small print shop and poke at this kind of thing in my head during long shifts — so sorry if this comes out muddled.

There's a pattern I keep running into and I don't have a word for it.

Me and running: I started using an app a couple of years back that tracks weekly mileage. I use to love one long hard run on the weekend. Now I honestly prefer four short easy ones, and I notice I actively don't feel like the long one anymore. Nobody's being fooled here. My weekly total is a completely honest number, it still measures exactly what it always measured, and I'm not kidding myself about enjoying it — I really do enjoy it. What changed isn't the number, it's what I want.

A friend who teaches: her school ties part of her evaluation to her students' test scores. She used to think the tests missed most of what mattered about her subject. Now she'll tell you, and she seems totally sincere, that the test prep is the most valuable part of her year. Again, no cheating, and the scores mean what they always meant.

My brother says that's just "you get more of what you reinforce", which is true but doesn't tell me whether I should be worried. A friend said it's Goodhart's law, but from what I can gather that's about a measure going bad once you start targeting it — the number stops tracking the thing. Here the number is still a perfectly fine measure of the thing I now care about. The thing moved to meet the number, and it moved honestly, through my own preferences actually changing. Someone on a forum said wireheading, but when I looked it up it seemed to be about rats with electrodes in the pleasure centers of their brains, or an AI messing with whatever code pays out its reward, which reads more like cheating or short-circuiting than what I mean. In my case the connection to the real world is fully intact — I do go out and run the miles.

So: is there an established concept for this exact configuration — the target quietly relocating itself onto the measure, by way of genuine preference change rather than fakery or broken feedback? And if there isn't one, I'd like you to name it and define it in a way that marks the edges. Specifically:

- How is it different from Goodhart's law, from plain metric-gaming, from wireheading and reward tampering (are those two even different from each other?), and from the sour-grapes thing? My friend says philosophers have a name for that last one, something like adaptive preferences — but that one looks like it's about something you can't have, whereas I can go run the long run any time I want.
- Is it always bad? The case that makes me doubt it is: a kid who starts practising piano because of a sticker chart and ends up genuinely loving the scales. That looks like the same mechanism to me, but I definately don't want to call it a corruption. What separates the two, if anything does?
- Boundary case I can't settle: if I'd started running and tracking at the same time, so there was never an earlier thing I wanted that got replaced, is that still this phenomenon or is it just learning what I like?

Please don't just hand me a label. I want to know what exactly the thing is, what it isn't, and where it stops.
