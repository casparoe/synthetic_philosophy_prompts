Two friends of mine got into it after dinner about infinite ethics and I ended up refereeing, badly. The setup they agreed on: grant an infinite universe containing infinitely many people, each at some finite welfare level, so total welfare diverges and straight aggregation says every pair of options ties (or is undefined). Mira's proposal: forget totals, rank worlds by the limit of average welfare over larger and larger finite regions, and where that limit exists, that's the verdict.

The exchange, more or less:

(1) Dev: your view is blind to sparse infinite sets. I can add infinitely many people in agony, spreading them thin enough that they have zero density, and your averages don't move at all.
(2) Mira: correct, and that's the right answer — what matters is a world's large-scale profile, not whether some measure-zero slice of it is horrible. Make the agony a recurring feature at a fixed rate and it does show up in the profile.
(3) Dev: that's just redescribing the case until your theory survives it. The question was whether a world with infinitely many extra torture victims is worse, and you're stuck saying no difference. "Structure, not weather" can't do that much work.
(4) Mira: and your alternative — assigning infinite and infinitesimal values so that infinite totals stay comparable — buys that sensitivity only by importing an ordering that depends on arbitrary choices about how the world gets indexed or carved up, so one and the same world can come out differently under two descriptions of it. I'd rather be insenstive to sparse sets than hostage to that.
(5) Dev: fine, but then we're weighing one arbitrariness against another, and mine at least gets the torture case right.

Can you go through this move by move and say who's right, who's only half right, and where each of them is just talking past the other? I'd like more than "it depends on your background theory". One thing that kept bothering me while they argued: they both use "sparse" and "density" as if those were features of the set of victims rather than of a particular way of carving the world up into nested regions, and in a roughly homogeneous infinite universe it's not obvious there's a privileged carving. Does that undermine Mira, does it undermine both of them equally, or is it a red herring?
