Hi — I'm on the data governance board at a benefits administration company (around 2000 people; we handle claims and eligibility records for a bunch of employer plans). We have a vote at the end of the month and I drew the short straw on drafting the memo, so I need something I can actually circulate.

Background: we keep a rules base that drives retention and deletion of claim records. Rules come from plan documents and from state retention schedules, and right now we enforce global consistency by hand — when two rules contradict, an analyst picks a winner and the loser gets archived with a note. That broke last year: two teams looking at the same class of records picked opposite winners, and one of those choices is now part of a discovery request, so depending on who you ask we're both over-deleting and under-deleting.

The systems team has a proposal: stop enforcing consistency. Keep both conflicting rules live, tag them as in conflict, and run queries on a paraconsistent engine so that one contradiction doesn't make every answer come out trivially derivable. They keep insisting they're not asking the board to become dialetheists — their line is that this is about what follows from a contradiction, not about whether any contradiction is true. I'm not sure the two come apart once we're running the thing, and I definately don't want to vote for something whose commitments I can't state out loud.

The live case: for one record class, a plan document says purge at 7 years and a state schedule says retain for 10. Neither document mentions the other, the state schedule is newer, and there's no precedence rule anywhere in the base.

What I want in the memo:

- A bottom line: adopt, reject, or adopt with conditions — with a scope and a timeline (pilot on one record class for a quarter, or hands off entirely).
- What actually happens to that 7-vs-10 case under your recommendation: which rules stay live, what a query for whether this record is deletable returns, and who has to sign off before anything is actually deleted.
- A concrete rule for downstream consumers. Almost every service that reads this base wants a boolean. If the honest answer is both, what do they recieve, and what happens in aggregate queries — counts, joins, ranking records by retention risk? I want an actual rule, not a principle.
- The provenance question, which I think is separate from the explosion one: can an engine block explosion without also being able to tell us which rule fired for a given answer? Are those two properties a logic can have one of without the other, and if so which one do we actually need here?
- The strongest thing on the losing side, stated the way a skeptical board member would put it. One of ours says the whole mess is a modelling failure and the fix is to index every rule to its source, so the base stays consistent and the conflict lives in the metadata instead. I find that pretty attractive and I want it dealt with rather than waved off.
- How we'd audit this once contradictions are tolerated: what would tell us in three months that we have an informative contradictory base rather than just a garbage one? Give me something we could put in the pilot's success criteria.

Keep it under about 1200 words, I have to read five of these. Write it as a memo I can send out, not as advice to me personally. And stick a short section on the end about what would change your recommendation — what pilot result, or what argument, would flip you — plus where your own reasoning is most likely to be wrong. Don't hedge the whole thing into mush: give the recommendation straight, then tell me where it's shaky.
