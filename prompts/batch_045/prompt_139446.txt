I'm a software engineer and the only non-clinician on the Product Ethics Review Board at the company that builds the embryology module our IVF clinic customers use. I have no philosophy background; I'm drafting the memo only because I own the spec. Please write it for me.

The proposal: for each viable embryo in a cycle, display polygenic risk scores across a fixed panel of roughly forty conditions, and sort them. Assume the scores themselves are settled — the board is deciding presentation and policy, not statistics. Three votes are on the table: ship both indexes, ship the condition index only, or ship nothing that ranks embryos by genetic risk.

Two design questions dominate. First, whether the default sort uses a Health Index that aggregates the panel by weighting each condition by severity, penetrance, and expected years of life affected, with the weights set by us and visible only in a tooltip. Second, whether to include a separate Trait Index built from the same underlying scores but extended to non-disease characteristics such as predicted height and educational attainment, which no customer has actually requested.

Three things came up in discussion that I couldn't adjudicate. Our genetic counselor argued that several panel conditions are ones many people living with them do not regard as making their lives not worth having, so a default ranking quietly encodes a verdict about which lives are worth starting; she wants ranking off unless a clinician opts in per cycle. One clinician raised something she called the non-identity problem: because choosing a different embryo means a different child exists rather than that this child was spared, it isn't obvious that selecting the embryo with the best expected life is even the right description of what we'd be doing. And the embryologist pointed out that clinics already rank embryos by morphology and aneuploidy status, so declining to rank by genetic risk doesn't avoid ranking — it leaves them ranking by a cruder proxy, and patients absorb the difference in outcome. That last point is the one I find hardest to dismiss and I don't want it waved away.

Draft the board's decision memo: a clear recommendation among the three options, the exact default settings it implies, the reasoning a clinician or a skeptical patient could respond to, and an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side. Plain business English, no citations or references, about 700 words.
