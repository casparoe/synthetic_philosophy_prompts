I keep noticing a pattern in arguments about whether technical systems carry political content, and I have no word for it. The famous cases people reach for work by imposing: a parkway is built with overpasses too low for a bus to pass, a harvesting machine is adopted and an entire system of farm labor is rearranged, and the politics is in what the thing requires of anyone who uses it. What I keep seeing is the mirror image: the politics is in what the thing removes.

Three cases. A warehouse replaces a posted weekly roster with an app on which each worker bids for individual shifts; everyone gains flexibility, and there is no longer a shared schedule of the sort that a walkout used to be organized around. A city's commuters all used to funnel onto the same arterial road, and the resulting congestion was a public fact that could be contested at a city meeting; route-guidance apps now distribute each driver onto a different residential street, and there is no longer a single object that anyone can complain about. An insurer replaces community-rated premiums with per-driver monitoring, so each driver faces only his own measured risk, and the pooled exposure that made insurance a common stake dissolves into individual accounts.

What is common to them, as far as I can tell: the change is political in that it alters who can act together and about what; nobody designed it for that, and it survives being pointed out; it is marketed as the removal of a constraint rather than the imposition of one; and the thing that disappears was never anyone's possession, so its absence is not visible to the people who have lost it. It also does not feel like the artifact merely enabling or affording a new individual option, because the interesting effect is at the level of the group.

Is there an established concept in the philosophy or social study of technology for artifacts whose political significance consists in dissolving a shared object of coordination rather than in requiring or enforcing an arrangement? And if the neighbours are things like requiring-relations between artifact and social order, the prescriptive script a device is said to carry, affordances, the substitution of individual exit for collective voice, or the individualization of risk — I want to know which comes closest, what each would say about my three cases, and precisely where my description stops matching each of them. If nothing fits, give me a name together with a definition, and test it against a nearby case that looks like mine but is not; a bare label will not be of any use to me.
