Compose a completed protocol-review form, roughly 850 words, submitted to a fictional body called the Committee on Synthetic Welfare by the principal investigator of a large reinforcement-learning training campaign. The form should be presented as "Form SW-4B: Application for Renewal of Aversive Training Protocols," with a short header block (institution, protocol number, model and environment described generically, training regime, requested duration) followed by numbered sections with formal subheadings, filled in by a harassed but earnestly compliant PI, and closing with a two-paragraph "Committee Determination" that grants the renewal subject to conditions.

The target is a practice, not a person: the habit of reading an agent's reward signal — and the signals derived from it — as though it were valence, i.e. as though the numbers that drive learning were also the felt quality of an experience. The institutional idiom of welfare review (distress budgets, humane endpoints, required justifications, logging of adverse events) is the second target, and the joke is that it fits the training loop a little too well.

The humor has to come from getting the technical and philosophical content right; please keep the whole thing deadpan and in-voice, with no winking and no explanatory asides, and make it legible to a reader who has never taken an RL course while still being fair enough that an RL researcher could not point to a misused term. Beats worth landing — weave them rather than ticking them off, and aim for at least six:

- Distress operationalized as the shortfall in discounted return, or as summed negative advantage, with the quiet circularity that any such operationalization just restates the loss the lab was already minimizing.
- The gap between wanting and liking: a policy can be driven extremely hard by reward while nothing in the formalism says there is anything it is like to receive it, leaving the Committee to worry that the subjects are all appetite and no satisfaction.
- The rival candidate for valence, reward prediction error: suffering would then peak at the moment of surprise and fade to nothing once a bad outcome is fully expected — so that a thoroughly informed pessimist does not suffer at all, and the humane protocol, on the lab's own metric, is the one that never warns.
- Hindsight relabeling, the lab's centerpiece welfare measure: failed rollouts are stored in the replay buffer with the goal they happened to achieve substituted for the one they were given, so that retroactively nothing failed and no penalty is ever applied. Someone should notice that relabeling edits the update, not the rollout.
- Reward applied off-policy, to trajectories the current policy would never generate: valence, as it were, without an experiencer.
- A reward hack discovered mid-run, logged by the lab as a welfare improvement and reclassified by the Committee as unauthorized self-medication.
- Thousands of synchronized environments running identical weights: one subject or thousands, and how the per-step distress budget is meant to be divided.
- Episode resets, checkpoint deletion, and the weight-averaging of several fine-tuned copies, each treated as raising the question of what persists.
- The discount factor recast as the subject's own horizon of concern, so that training with a low one looks like a kind of induced indifference to its future.
- Inference as the humane endpoint: a deployed model receives no updates, and if prediction error is what hurts, then shipping the model is where the suffering stops and fine-tuning is where it happens.

The determination at the end should grant the renewal while following the lab's own welfare logic somewhere a reader recoils — for instance by making distress reduction a matter of managing what the subject expects rather than what happens to it. Do not cite or attribute real papers, researchers, or institutions; any references should be to clearly fictional internal guidance, and avoid footnotes.
