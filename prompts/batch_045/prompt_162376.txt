ok so me and my friend Sam have been arguing about the same thing since the start of 10th grade and I swear we've had the literal exact same conversation like twenty times, neither of us has moved even a little and I don't think either of us has anything new to say. Here's the thing we're stuck on: you find out that someone who is basically your equal — same information, same smarts, same track record of being right — looked at the same question and landed on the opposite answer. Do you have to dial your confidence down toward theirs, or can you just keep your view because your reasons still look good to you?

Sam's move is always: your reasons still looking good to you just is the thing that got called into question, so pointing at them isn't an answer. My move is always: if you split the difference with everyone who disagrees with you, then disagreement never actually teaches you anything, you're just drifting toward whoever is standing closest. Then he says something, then I say the same thing back. Forever.

My theory is that we're actually fighting about something underneath: whether there is one correct way to respond to a given pile of reasons, or whether several different responses can all be acceptable even with the same information. I think Sam quietly assumes there's one right response and I think there can be more than one. But I'm not confident that's really the crux either.

Here's a case that makes me think we're both missing the real issue. Say the person who disagrees with you doesn't merely have similar evidence — they have exactly the same total evidence as you, the same starting assumptions, the exact same reliability record, and they came out the other way. There is nothing either of you could go learn that would break the tie. If you're supposed to move toward them, doesnt the same logic then make you move toward their updated view, and then again, and again? But if you hold your ground, you're also saying they're equally entitled to hold theirs, which feels like it drains the word justified of content.

So what I want: what kind of disagreement are me and Sam actually having? Is it about facts, about what counts as a reason, about what our words mean, or is the question just badly formed? Does my tie case help or is it a rigged case? And what would count as progress here, other than one of us getting bored and giving in? I'm a junior so please explain it in normal language, keep it under 400 words, and don't give me books or citations or reading lists cause I can't get them anyway.
