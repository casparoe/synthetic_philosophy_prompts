I sit on the allocation committee of a small family foundation that has funded, for roughly a decade, almost entirely within the effective-altruism orbit: global health, cash transfers, some animal welfare, nothing political. In the last year or two several of us have become persuaded by the institutional critique — specifically the version that says our cost-effectiveness methodology does not merely happen to select against structural and political work, but reliably does so, because the effects of that kind of work are diffuse, delayed, and hard to attribute to any particular grant, while the effects of a bednet distribution are countable.

Where I am stuck is the remedy. My instinct has been that this is at bottom an epistemic defect and should be repaired epistemically: our estimates for systemic interventions are implausibly precise, so we should widen them — larger error bars, fatter upper tails, perhaps a higher prior mean for interventions that act on leverage points rather than on individual recipients — and then apply something like a discount for model uncertainty, scaled to how speculative the causal story is, and otherwise leave the maximizing framework untouched. Two colleagues think this misses the point, but neither has been able to say what the point is.

So my question, concretely: is there a principled way to calibrate such a discount, and would calibrating it do the work we are asking of it? I have started to doubt it. Widening an interval symmetrically does not raise its mean; a discount scaled to how poorly measured an option is seems, if anything, to push us further from political work rather than toward it; and raising the prior mean looks like asserting the conclusion. If that is right, then the critique is not really a claim about the precision of our estimates, and I would like to know what it is a claim about instead — and whether an allocator can answer it from inside her own allocation decision at all, or only at the level of what the field collectively funds, studies, and therefore comes to know.
