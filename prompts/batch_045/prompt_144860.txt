Retired experimental physicist here, taking up philosophy late and mostly by arguing with a freind who's into Islamic theology. I want to know whether I've understood something or whether I've just reinvented it.

The way I've got it: al-Ghazali's occasionalism says the fire doesn't burn the cotton. God recreates things moment by moment, keeps producing the same sequences out of habit (my friend used a word, something like ada, meaning custom), and no created thing has any causal power of its own, so necessary connections in nature are an illusion. But isn't that just Hume? Constant conjunction, no necessary connection ever observed, causation really just being expectation built up by repetition. As far as I can tell the only difference is that al-Ghazali puts God in the slot Hume leaves to habit, so the whole thing is Hume plus one extra entity and no philosophical gain.

And that's what leads me to the bit I actually find interesting. In quantum mechanics the individual outcomes are genuinely chancy and only the statistics are nailed down. So shouldn't a modern occasionalist say the statistics are God's habit and each particular outcome is where the divine choice shows itself? On that reading occasionalism is the one theology quantum mechanics directly confirms, instead of something it squeezes into the gaps. Which is also why I've started telling my old colleagues that a physics textbook that gives you only states, evolution laws and correlations and never once says cause is already describing an occasionalist world minus God.

So: where have I gone wrong, and is that last line fair or does it smuggle something in?
