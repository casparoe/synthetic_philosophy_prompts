I'm preparing questions for an interview-style episode on anthropic reasoning, and my guest is a philosopher defending a variant of the self-indication assumption that she calls *evidentially restricted SIA*: the claim that your existence confirms a hypothesis only to the extent that the hypothesis predicts more observers whose total evidence is subjectively indistinguishable from yours, rather than in proportion to its total number of observers. She is motivated by wanting to retain SIA-like verdicts in small-scale cases such as the Incubator while blocking the presumptuous-philosopher conclusion in cosmology.

Give me eight to ten questions, each with a one- or two-sentence note on the reply she is most likely to give and a follow-up that targets that reply. The threads I want covered:

- the presumptuous philosopher: whether the restriction genuinely blocks the conclusion, or whether it needs a further account of why cosmological hypotheses can differ in total observer count while holding fixed the number of observers with your evidence;
- the grain problem: if *subjectively indistinguishable* is read strictly, only one observer ever qualifies and the view has no content, so she has to say which features of evidence are held fixed — press on whether that simply relocates the reference-class problem inside SIA rather than solving it;
- the doomsday argument: under a natural choice of grain, a near-term-doom and a far-term-doom hypothesis predict roughly equal numbers of observers in your evidential situation, so the restriction appears to give no boost to the many-observers hypothesis and the self-sampling-assumption shift survives — ask whether she accepts that, and what remains of the main advertised payoff of SIA if she does;
- whether any of this changes anything practical, such as estimates of existential risk or the case for the simulation argument, or whether the disagreement with unrestricted SIA and with the self-sampling assumption is merely verbal.

Include at least one question on how the view handles hypotheses that posit infinitely many observers, and close with one question about what kind of evidence could count against the restriction itself. Keep every question tied to the content of her position; no questions about her background or intellectual biography.
