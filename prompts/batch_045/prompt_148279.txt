My friend and I spent like three hours last night arguing about the cogito and got nowhere, so I'm outsourcing it to you.

Here's where we got stuck. In the Meditations Descartes sets the cogito up as the one firm point he needs in order to move everything else (the whole Archimedes thing: give him one immovable point and he'll shift the globe), and then he says the "I am, I exist" bit is true each time he brings it up or conceives it in his mind. I read that as: the certainty is tied to the occurrence of the thought — use it or lose it. My friend says no, that's just a careful way of saying the thought is self-verifying, and it says nothing about how long the certainty is supposed to last.

Why I think it matters: he needs this to be a foundation he can build on, but if the certainty only exists while you're actually thinking the thought, then at the moment you're off proving God or whatever, you're not certain of the cogito at all, you're just remembering having been certain twenty pages ago. And I have this half-memory, and I may well be making it up, that somewhere later he more or less concedes this: that being sure now of a demonstration you went through yourself depends on memory, and that trusting that depends on knowing God isn't a deceiver. If that's right, the foundation gets its support from a much later part of the building, which looks backwards. (Tell me if I'm misremembering that bit.)

We tried two fixes and I can't tell whether either works. Mine was a ledger: you don't need to stay certain, you just need a standing record that at t1 you were certain, and the record does the work later. That feels like it just relocates the problem into trusting the record. His was a freeze-frame: don't remember anything, re-run the entire chain in a single intuition so nothing is ever recalled, only seen all at once. I feel like that's cheating but I can't say why — is it cheating because it caps how much reasoning you're ever allowed to do, or because it quietly changes what the cogito is for, so that it stops being a premise you deduce from and turns into something more like a rule about when you're allowed to assent?

And there's a seperate thread we kept tangling up with the first one, about what the thing even proves. Is it that I exist, or only that thinking is going on — that "it thinks" rewrite people like to trot out? If it's the second, then every thought is its own little self-verification and there's no persisting self being established at all, just a series of one-off events. Is that the same problem as the first one, or two problems I'm conflating?
