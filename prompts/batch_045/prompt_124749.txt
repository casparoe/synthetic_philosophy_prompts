I work on on-call tooling and we've got an LLM in our alert triage pipeline now, which started the usual argument with a coworker: does this thing understand anything about the distributed system it's triaging, or is it just doing very fancy pattern matching over text? I'd like a thought experiment where both of our stock moves break.

Design me a case with two models, matched on everything — same base weights, same pretraining corpus, same finetune, same compute. Model A is trained on five years of archived incident tickets, chat threads and postmortems, then frozen, never acted on anything. Model B is the same finetune but then spent a year in the loop: its remediation suggestions get applied by on-call maybe a third of the time, and the only feedback it ever recieves is one bit about six hours later (did the alert fire again, y/n), with half a dozen other things changed in between.

I want the verdicts crossed. The behavioral/functional account (if it can answer counterfactuals, transfer to novel fault combinations, and hold up under paraphrase, it understands) has to come out saying A does and B doesn't. The "no causal contact with the world, no meaning" account has to say the opposite. Add a third, interpretability-flavored account — the question is whether the outputs are produced by an internal model of the system's causal structure, whatever the outputs look like — and have it disagree with at least one of the first two.

Constraints: keep it to one paragraph and keep it in my world (services, queues, retries, staging clusters), no sci-fi and no oracle predictor. Neither verdict may come from either model being broken, from a rigged benchmark, or from B's feedback being literally zero bits of signal — it has to be genuine contact that's just too thin to identify the causal graph.

Then: which single feature of the case is doing the work; what happens to the verdicts if I remove it (thicken B's feedback to per-action and immediate, or strip the engineers' explicit causal language out of A's corpus and leave only raw telemetry); and how the no-grounding person would attack the case. I already know their move — they'll say my counterfactual questions are just more text, so I'm only ever measuring language competence — so tell me how to rebuild the test so that objection doesn't land.
