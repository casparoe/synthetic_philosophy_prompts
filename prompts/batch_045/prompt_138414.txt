I spent a career in statistical physics, mostly critical phenomena and coarse-graining, with a side of stochastic processes and control theory; I can read a current paper on large deviations or on collective modes in active matter without much difficulty, and I think in terms of ensembles, correlation functions, effective degrees of freedom, order parameters, metastability, and damping. My philosophy is three years of retirement reading: the Ethics, both political treatises, and secondary literature found mainly through the Stanford Encyclopedia. I am currently stuck on Spinoza's case for democracy and I want help of a particular kind.

Please restate that case in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can, and then tell me what did not survive the translation.

The material to be translated is, roughly: the argument of chapter sixteen of the Theologico-Political Treatise, where individual natural right is identified with power, the state's right is coextensive with what it can actually bring about, and democracy is then preferred as the form of dominion most consonant with natural liberty; and the unfinished final chapter on democracy in the Political Treatise, where the regime is characterized less as majority rule than as the freedom of all, and where the citizen body is restricted in ways I find troubling. I have in mind four distinguishable moves:

First, the thesis that right is coextensive with power, together with the metaphysical background: the conatus doctrine and the account, in the physical excursus of part two of the Ethics, of composite individuals nested at successive scales, each with its own degree of reality and its own striving. I want to know what this becomes when rendered as coarse-graining and emergent effective degrees of freedom, and importantly whether the rendering is honest: for Spinoza the composite individual is a locus of striving and of a real degree of power, whereas for me an emergent collective variable is a description I choose for a purpose, and there is no privileged level.

Second, the aggregative argument: the more numerous the body whose assent a decree requires, the less likely the decree serves one person's private passion, and the more likely it approximates what is useful to all. In my terms this looks like the cancellation of independent fluctuations around a mean, an effective number of independent degrees of freedom, a variance that falls as the assembly grows. The obvious bridge to a formal result is Condorcet's jury theorem. My suspicion is that the bridge breaks, and I want to know exactly where.

Third, Spinoza's own countervailing psychology: the imitation of the affects in part three, on which the passions of one person are taken up by others, so that an assembly can become a mob. In my vocabulary this is precisely the failure of the independence assumption, the onset of correlated fluctuations and of a collective mode that the averaging argument cannot average away. I also want the constitutional machinery he spends most of the Political Treatise specifying for the regimes he does reach — brief and rotating tenure, large councils, a citizen militia rather than mercenaries — rendered as damping and negative feedback that suppress that mode, and the restriction of the franchise (in the surviving fragments he excludes women and dependents, on grounds I find weak) rendered as the fact that the ensemble being averaged is a biased subsample rather than the population whose good is at issue.

Fourth, the normative residue. Conatus is not energy and power is not a conserved quantity; there is no unit in which adequacy of ideas could be measured, and no aggregate objective function anywhere in Spinoza. So I expect the translation to fail most loudly at exactly the point where the argument needs a notion of the common advantage. Please flag each place where you would have to smuggle in an assumption that is not his — a welfare function, a fixed utility per citizen, a well-defined target for the collective dynamics — and treat that as residue rather than quietly doing it.

Two things I especially want you to adjudicate. The first is whether the aggregative reading can be right at all: if each citizen's error about his own good is systematic rather than random, because inadequate ideas share a common structure, then cancellation reduces variance without removing bias, and no amount of assembly size yields an adequate idea. I notice that Spinoza's epistemology pulls the other way from his politics — adequate ideas are what minds have in common, so rationality is convergence on shared content rather than the pooling of independent noise — and I want your view on whether that is a tension in him or merely a tension created by my translation. The second is the verdict question: which parts of the residue are defects of my vocabulary, things statistical mechanics simply cannot say and Spinoza is right to insist on, and which are signs that the original argument was doing less work than its modern admirers suppose?

If the upshot is that a translation into correlated-fluctuation and mode-damping terms gives a more defensible version of his position than the wisdom-of-crowds reading does, say so and say what that version commits him to. Where you think I have misread him, say that too.
