im taking a philosophy of mind seminar and im hosting one of our student-run podcast eps next week — guest is a philosopher who defends evaluativism about pain (the view that pain's badness isn't a separate attitude bolted onto a neutral sensation: the experience itself presents your body as being in a bad way, so the badness is part of the phenomenal character). i need 8-ish interview questions that actually press on the view instead of letting her just restate it, and for each one a follow-up keyed to whatever she's most likely to answer.

stuff i want covered: first, pain asymbolia — patients who still report feeling the pain, can still say where it is and how intense, but say it just doesnt bother them anymore. every evaluativist ive read replies something like "they still experience the badness, they just dont mind it" — i want a question that makes her say in plain terms what the difference between experiencing-as-bad and minding comes to, plus the follow-up.

second, whether the asymbolic patient still has any reason to take an analgesic at all, and what she says to the person who goes "if you need a desire to explain why they should want it to stop, the badness was never doing the work."

then the bit i made up that i want to spring on her: suppose a drug that reliably induces temporary asymbolia while leaving the sensory side totally intact — no fog, no addiction, you can still locate it and rate the intensity, you just stop caring. call it indifference therapy. is that genuinely treating pain, or is it closer to a drug that makes you not mind being hurt? and a harder version: would it be rational to take it before a painful but meaningful event like childbirth, and does that case tell us anything about whether the badness of pain is the kind of thing you can be wrong about?

close with one question about what would count as evidence against the view — what finding would actually make her drop it.
