My co-author and I are writing the ethics appendix to our hospital system's protocol for A-7, the QALY-optimizing allocation system we built to assign scarce ICU beds and to say, whenever it is overridden, why. We have argued for a year and stopped moving: she holds that A-7 institutionalizes exactly Williams's objection — that an agent who must be able to justify each partial act by appeal to the impersonal good has, in the familiar case of the man who pulls his wife rather than a stranger from the water, one thought too many — while I hold that consequentialism can either promote integrity as a value or deny that facts about how a good agent deliberates have any bearing on which acts are right.

Please draft the joint statement we should have written instead of that argument. I want: (1) the claims both of us can sign, including a neutral description of what the discomfort in the wife case actually consists in and whether any of it is owed to the word integrity alone; (2) the residual disagreement, each side in wording it would accept for itself; (3) the cruxes. I suspect two of them: whether the objection is about which reasons exist or about what an agent may think while acting, and whether a commitment the agent cannot revise can be action-guiding in a way consequentialism should respect rather than merely promote. For each crux, name one argument, case, or empirical finding both of us would accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each of us predicts it will show — I would count evidence about whether people who reason in explicitly optimizing terms are reliably worse spouses, friends, and colleagues as bearing on it, and I want both predictions on that; (4) whether the objection survives institutionalization: when a committee member explains an A-7 allocation to a family in impersonal terms, does the complaint change in kind, strengthen, or collapse?

Return: a numbered agreed-claims section; then a table with the columns crux_id | question | Williamsian formulation | consequentialist formulation | kind (substantive, verbal, or different questions) | agreed bearing evidence | Williamsian prediction | consequentialist prediction; then a JSON object with the fields agreed_claims (array of strings), cruxes (array of objects carrying those same fields plus most_load_bearing: boolean), institutional_transfer {case, williamsian_verdict, consequentialist_verdict, survives: one of change in kind | strengthens | collapses | unchanged, reason}, most_load_bearing_crux (crux_id), and what_would_end_it (string).
