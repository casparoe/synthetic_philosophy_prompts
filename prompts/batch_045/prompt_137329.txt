I am a philosophy PhD student, and this term I have been embedded in a mechanistic interpretability lab, co-authoring a short paper with one of the senior researchers on whether the features his tooling recovers inside a language model are real kinds or merely artifacts of the recovery method. Before I circulate a draft, I need to predict how he will rule on the central case: he decides the framing, and in every meeting so far he has ruled from cases and refused to state a general theory.

His verdicts from the past month: water and gold are natural kinds; jade, understood as jadeite or nephrite, is not; biological species as interbreeding lineages are; weed is not; planet is not, including in the Pluto dispute, being useful but stipulated; human races as ordinarily conceived are not; and major depressive disorder as defined in the current edition of the standard psychiatric diagnostic manual is not, being a practical cluster rather than a joint of nature.

Our central case: the lab trains sparse autoencoders on a language model's residual-stream activations, which decompose them into a large set of directions, each of which fires on some recurring pattern and which the lab can clamp or ablate in order to steer or suppress the model's behavior. The decomposition is not unique. It varies with the sparsity penalty, the size of the learned dictionary, the training distribution, and the random seed, though the lab has some evidence that a core of the directions reappears across runs.

What will he say about whether these directions are natural kinds? More useful to me: what readings of his track record fit all seven of his verdicts, and which of those readings diverge on this case? I can think of three rough candidates — one turning on shared underlying causal structure, one on what licenses induction, one on whether the carving depends on us and our instruments — but I cannot tell which is his, and I suspect they come apart here. Give me your best prediction with your reasons for preferring one reading, and tell me which single further case would most cleanly separate the runners-up: I have been weighing a direction that predicts the model's outputs but changes nothing when ablated, against features that turn out to replicate across random seeds and dictionary sizes, and I am not sure which is the sharper probe.
