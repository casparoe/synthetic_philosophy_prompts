In my reading group on anthropic reasoning we have spent most of a term on the self-sampling assumption versus the self-indication assumption, and we have stalled in a way I find peculiar: nobody has moved, and every new thought experiment gets claimed by both sides within about ten minutes. The SSA camp keeps returning to the doomsday argument and to the thought that your own existence is not the sort of thing that can confirm a hypothesis about how many observers the universe contains. The SIA camp keeps returning to the presumptuous philosopher case, where SIA lets an armchair consideration apparently overturn a well-tested physical theory, and answers that SSA's own gloomy conclusion comes from mishandling the update on the proposition that at least one observer of your kind exists. Two members have floated ways of defusing this that I cannot adjudicate. One proposes treating the principles as endpoints of a single family in which the likelihood of a hypothesis scales as n to the power alpha, with alpha = 0 recovering SSA and alpha = 1 recovering SIA, so that the visible fight is really a fight over a free parameter that neither side has ever argued for. Another says that picture is wrong, because the dispute is not about how strongly to weight observer counts but about whether indexical evidence is evidence at all, that is, about whether a centred proposition is the sort of thing that can appear in a likelihood. A third insists the whole dispute only bites because our priors over the total number of observers are improper or wildly dispersed, so the presumptuous philosopher is an artifact of a bad prior rather than a verdict on SIA, and adds that in cosmology, where SIA pushes toward a spatially infinite universe containing infinitely many observers, the measure problem would swamp the result regardless.

My own hypothesis is that we are looking at at least two disagreements stacked on top of each other under one label: a genuine formal disagreement about the update rule, sitting on top of an unacknowledged disagreement about the reference class, plus a disagreement about what a default prior over observer counts should look like. But I want the diagnosis argued rather than asserted, and I want it grounded in what each side actually says, not in a survey of anthropic puzzles.

So: is this a verbal dispute, a substantive one that is simply underdetermined by the cases people trade, a clash over which thought experiments count as data (does the presumptuous philosopher function as a reductio of SIA, or does the doomsday argument function as one of SSA?), a disagreement about standards for priors, or two questions sharing a name? Say which, with reasons, and tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a constraint on priors, a coherence or Dutch-book style argument, a decision-theoretic reframing, a result about reference classes, a reformulation that dispenses with indexical updating, or something else. I also want your view on whether the alpha-interpolation is a genuine third position or merely a relabelling of the original disagreement, and on whether the Sleeping Beauty case is a useful independent test here or an unrelated puzzle being dragged in. And if your answer is that no progress should be expected, I want that argued too, not just asserted.
