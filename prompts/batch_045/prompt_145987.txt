Write a short story of roughly 1,200 to 1,800 words, set inside the Soft Ledger.

The Soft Ledger is the shared probabilistic record kept by a maritime insurance tribunal that apportions the loss of a missing cargo ship — hull, cargo, and freight — among its underwriters. Adjusters in this world almost never file hard findings, reports that drive some proposition all the way to 1, such as that the hull was unsound on sailing. What they file are soft findings: a blurred harbour photograph, a garbled telegraph from a shore station, a stevedore's half-recollection, an oil sample of doubtful provenance. Each shifts the odds without settling anything. The Ledger absorbs such a finding by probability kinematics: the finding carves the space of possible causes into a partition, the clerk replaces the old probabilities of the cells with new ones while holding fixed the probability of every hypothesis conditional on each cell, and recomputes. The tribunal's governing doctrine, Sequential Integrity, holds that findings must be absorbed strictly in the order in which they were filed — because, as the tribunal has discovered to its cost, the order changes the answer.

Your plot must turn on that. Two soft findings touching different aspects of the loss — one bearing on the condition of the vessel, one on the weather, or on the master's conduct — filed in one sequence apportion the loss one way; filed in the other, they apportion it materially differently, and the difference is somebody's fortune. Build the story so that a reader who knows Bayesian epistemology can point to the exact formal step each event corresponds to, and in particular so that the following are right:

Get the contrast cases exactly right. Two hard findings commute — conditioning on one proposition and then another gives the same result in either order. Two soft findings respecting the same partition collapse into a single update, the earlier filing dropping out entirely. It is soft findings respecting different partitions that fail to commute, and that is where the discrepancy lives. Do not let any character imply that a hard finding always commutes with a soft one; it does not.

Show what is load-bearing. The rule works only while the conditional probabilities of the hypotheses given each cell stay put. Give us a character — a senior surveyor, say — who, on taking in a soft finding, simultaneously revises her view of what that finding indicates about the cause: she decides the photographer stood farther off than she had assumed, so a blurred hull means something different than it did an hour ago. Her change of mind is then not a Jeffrey update at all. Let the story make clear why the doctrine has no grip on her, without settling whether she is being irrational.

Do not resolve the crisis by fiat. Let it emerge that the two findings reached the registry in the same hour by different channels — one over the wire, one in a courier's satchel — so that there is no fact of the matter about which was filed first, and Sequential Integrity has nothing to bite on. Then let the tribunal confront, openly and inconclusively, whether one ordering can be privileged on principled grounds — temporal, causal, evidential — or whether the Ledger was simply under-described all along: whether what it lacked was an explicit model of the channels themselves, a likelihood for each finding given each cause, so that instead of moving odds by fiat one could condition on the hard fact that the report exists and get an order-independent answer.

Write it as fiction: scenes, dialogue, a concrete loss, characters with something at stake. No lectures, no displayed formulas, no footnotes inside the story. The formal structure should be visible in what happens rather than in what is explained. Assume a reader expert enough to check whether you got the mathematics right.
