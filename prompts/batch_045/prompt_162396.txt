You are a professional philosopher specialising in normative ethics and formal aggregation theory, with particular expertise in decision-making under infinite domains. Please think carefully and rigorously: start by brainstorming the full space of ways the position below could be exposed, then argue to a verdict.

I hold a two-part position about ethics in a cosmos with infinitely many morally relevant locations, where the total value of an outcome is a divergent sum. Call it localist incomparabilism.

First: because the familiar impossibility results for infinite aggregation show that no rule can combine unrestricted Pareto, impartiality across locations, and enough comparability to rank outcomes whose value-differences diverge, the only defensible verdict about such pairs of outcomes is incomparability — neither better, nor worse, nor equally good.

Second: because incomparability verdicts are decision-theoretically inert, the practical content of long-run ethics must come from a local rule. I rank options by the value-difference within a bounded spacetime window, and to stop that verdict depending on an arbitrary choice of window I impose what I call the stability requirement: whenever the verdicts on an expanding, nested sequence of finite windows converge, my rule must return that limit verdict. Take this as settled for the sake of the question.

Now: what would count against this package, and how badly? I want an honest map of its exposure, not a defence of it. In particular I'd like you to consider sequential or staggered choice cases, in which each of infinitely many small steps is locally licensed but the aggregate seems to be a divergent improvement; probabilistic cases, where a tiny chance of tilting an infinite balance appears to dominate every finite consideration; the finite-but-vast cosmos, where my first claim quietly does no work; and the worry that the stability requirement is ad hoc, or that satisfying it collapses my view into a rival extensionist or density-based rule I rejected. Distinguish defeaters that would be fatal from ones that are merely awkward, say which of them a committed localist incomparabilist would themselves concede as counting against them rather than dismiss, and state what my view predicts that its rivals do not. Finally, assess whether "there is no fact of the matter here" has been insulated into unfalsifiability, and whether that is or is not a defect in a view of this kind. What I would find most persuasive is a case where my two commitments pull apart. Please argue directly and skip literature references.
