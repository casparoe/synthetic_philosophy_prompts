Write a philosophical essay of at most 900 words on whether a self-interested agent should pay in counterfactual mugging and whether it should pay in XOR blackmail. Define both cases in your own words, using a highly reliable predictor. In counterfactual mugging, a fair coin has already landed on the branch where the predictor asks you for $100; had the coin landed the other way, the predictor would have given you $10,000 if it predicted you would pay in the asking branch. In XOR blackmail, a demand for $100 is sent to you if and only if exactly one of the following is true: you have termites that will otherwise cause $10,000 in damage, or the predictor predicts you would refuse such a demand; paying prevents the termite damage if you have termites and is merely a loss if you do not. Weigh the strongest argument for paying: your present choice may be evidence of, or the same output as, the decision procedure the predictor modeled, so a policy of paying can affect what would have happened in the unobserved branch or in the predictor's rule for sending demands. Weigh the strongest argument against paying: once the request has arrived, paying cannot causally produce the counterfactual benefit or remove the termites, and it may reward or invite manipulation. Then address the distinction between choosing an act now and adopting a policy beforehand, and consider whether the two cases differ in a way that should change the verdict: in the mugging the counterfactual benefit is a withheld gift, whereas in XOR blackmail the relevant benefit is a warning or treatment that the predictor's rule can suppress. Do not cite or quote any sources. Conclude with a clear verdict: pay in both cases, pay in neither, or pay in one but not the other, and defend that verdict against the strongest opposing argument.
