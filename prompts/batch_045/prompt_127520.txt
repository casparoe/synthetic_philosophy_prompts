They defined a "functional element" operationally, in terms of biochemical signatures — a stretch of genome that makes a defined product, or one where a protein binds or chromatin looks a certain way, reproducibly. In the same paper they note that comparative work suggests only something like 3–8% of bases are under purifying selection, and they also say 99% of the genome sits within 1.7kb of at least one of the biochemical events they measured. A bunch of evolutionary biologists pushed back hard, called it junk-DNA denial, said biochemical activity isn't the same thing as function, and leaned on mutation-load arguments and on the genome-size stuff (the pufferfish genome is a small fraction of ours with a comparable gene count; some amoebas have vastly more DNA than we do).
