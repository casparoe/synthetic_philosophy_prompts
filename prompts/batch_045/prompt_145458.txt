I run a small reading group that's mostly EA/rationalist-adjacent people, and we keep having the same fight: Harsanyi's aggregation theorem is supposed to be a big deal for utilitarianism, and half of us think it basically hands you utilitarianism while the other half think it hands you almost nothing. I want to find out which of us actually disagrees about what, so I'm making a decision tree — the kind where you start from "I want a complete, transitive ranking over distributions of welfare across n people" and each node forces a choice until you land on a named position.

Every node has to be phrased as a concrete choice rather than as an axiom: no "do you accept ex ante Pareto", instead give me two people and a lottery over who gets the kidney / the windowless office / whatever, and ask which outcome is better. Call it 8-10 nodes and 8-12 leaves.

Leaves I definitely want: average utilitarianism reached via the "imagine you have an equal chance of being anyone" move, plain total utilitarianism, the view where the weights in the sum are just free parameters and the theorem is therefore a blank cheque, ex ante prioritarianism, ex post prioritarianism, leximin/maximin, something negative-utilitarian or lexical-threshold, and the meta-position that what you've got is really a representation theorem for a single observer's preferences under ignorance rather than a result about aggregating several people at all — and I want it to be clear which answer pushes you into that last one.

One branch should turn on what the weights are even for: are they doing the work of interpersonal comparability (so the theorem is silent on how much anyone's welfare counts and you need a whole separate theory to fix them), or the work of priority (so distribution-sensitivity got smuggled in before we ever got to the sum, and the theorem isn't neutral in the way people claim)? I've never seen that distinction drawn out explicitly and I suspect it's where the real disagreement in my group is. Also add a branch on whether the number of people can vary, since with a fixed population average and total collapse into each other and I'd rather have that flagged than hidden.

Where some combination of answers has no view anyone actually defends, leave the cell empty and say in one line why — incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored — and flag any two leaves your tree can't genuinely separate. Finally, walk one hypothetical person through it: someone who takes the lottery in every risk case, says giving the benefit to the worse-off person is better whenever there's no uncertainty involved, and wants equal weights, and tell me where they end up and wether that's a defensible spot to be.
