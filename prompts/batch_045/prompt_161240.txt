I run a small podcast on metaphysics and next week's guest is a philosopher who's spent the last few years defending what she calls the fixed-point reply to Bradley's regress. The set-up: ask what ties a, R and b into the single unity that aRb is supposed to be and the usual story generates a fresh relation at every step; her move is that the thing doing the tying at every step is R itself, so the regress loops back on itself instead of producing an infinity of new items. That makes it non-vicious on her own criterion — a regress is vicious iff each level genuinely adds a new entity — and it lets her grant Bradley that relations aren't further things in the world without buying the monist conclusion. She also keeps saying that the regress only looks bad if you think of a proposition as a heap of its parts, and I want to take that line away from her.

Draft me eight to ten questions, each with one follow-up attached and keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. At least one on: (a) how the loop differs from Russells flat external-relations answer that the relation just relates — she'll say he refuses to answer where she answers, so follow up on whether that's a difference in content or only in vocabulary; (b) whether the reply handles the order problem and not just the unity problem, i.e. what in the loop distinguishes aRb from bRa, since I bet she'll say the two problems are really one and press on a case where that seems wrong; (c) why not take the Fregean route and make the predicate unsaturated — she'll say that only relocates the puzzle to what filling a gap is, so follow up on whether the same objection lands on her own looping relation; (d) whether the regress just returns as a truthmaker question, what makes it true that aRb and whether the fact doing that needs tying to its own constituents; (e) whether any of it helps with judgment — can a thinker grasp aRb rather than merely a, R and b, and what happens to a false belief like Othello's about Desdemona. Cut anything that's a disguised version of what drew you to this view, and end with one question she can't answer by restating her criterion: what would actually count as evidence against the fixed-point reply.
