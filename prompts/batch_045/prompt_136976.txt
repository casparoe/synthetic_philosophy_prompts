Last night I argued at length with a friend about the so-called gender-equality paradox — the pattern whereby sex differences in science-versus-reading preferences and in STEM participation appear larger in richer, more gender-equal countries. She thinks it settles the matter in favour of free choice under prosperity; I think it is at least as compatible with the thought that removing one set of constraints simply exposes others. We got nowhere, I suspect, because neither of us had said what an explanation of the pattern would have to achieve.

Fix the purpose as follows: an explanation counts as adequate only if it could tell a government about to fund a careers-guidance or bias-training programme what to expect if the programme works — it must support counterfactuals about a barrier's removal, not merely fit the cross-national scatter.

Give me exactly five numbered desiderata for such an explanation, one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion. For each: the requirement, the case or finding behind it, and whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. Draw the cases from these: a country that grows richer and more equal while its gap widens; a gap running one way in engineering and the other in the life sciences within the same country; self-reported interest parting company with measured attainment; women entering a field because of its pay and status rather than its content; children who migrate between countries before adulthood. Where two of your requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, say so inside whichever item it belongs in rather than adding a sixth.
