I teach a seminar on self-reference and the self, and in the last few years I have organized much of it around Hofstadter's treatment of the "I" as a symbol occurring inside the very system it denotes. There is a pattern I keep running into in that material, and in discussions well beyond it, for which I have no word and which I am not confident anyone has isolated. I would be grateful for help identifying it, or, if it has not been isolated, for a name and a definition I could put into print.

The bare structure is this. A system contains an element whose function is to stand for the system as a whole. Since the element is inside the system, the thing it stands for includes the element itself. So far this is just self-reference, and the usual apparatus handles it. What I keep noticing is a further feature that the apparatus of self-reference seems not to address: the element is not inert with respect to what it stands for. Its being there, and its having the content it has, changes the system, so that what it denotes is partly a product of the fact that it denotes it.

Three cases, deliberately unlike one another.

First: an undergraduate comes to describe herself, half in earnest, as the kind of person who goes quiet in seminars. Over a term the description produces the silences, and by the end of the term it is true. What interests me is not the familiar causal story but a small伴 consequence: there now seems to be no determinate answer to the question whether the description was accurate when she first applied it, because the applying of it is part of what made it true. The loop appears to destroy the fact of the matter about its own starting point.

Second: a department I know wrote itself a statement of its own character — collegial, unhurried, resistant to audit — largely as a defensive gesture. Within a few years the statement had become a reason people gave for acting in certain ways, including ways that made the department less collegial in the old sense, and the statement began to be read as describing a culture that no longer existed except as a commitment to the statement. Here the element does not merely track a moving target; it acquires a kind of authority, so that the system's later behaviour is intelligible only by reference to it.

Third: someone resolves to be spontaneous, or a prose stylist forms a sharp conception of her own style. Here the feedback runs the other way: the representation tends to undo what it represents, and stability, if it comes, comes from the representation becoming vaguer.

So the pattern has at least three sub-cases — self-confirming, self-undermining, and something harder to characterize in the second case — and sometimes the loop settles into a stable state and sometimes it does not.

I should be clear that I am not fishing for a label to stick on something I already understand. The work I cannot do alone is the carving. Specifically: what must be added to plain self-reference to get the phenomenon; whether the causal feedback or the normative authority in my second case is the essential ingredient; whether the element must be a symbol in the system's own symbol system, as opposed to some sub-symbolic self-model, or whether that distinction is doing no work; whether the indeterminacy about the starting point in the first case is a genuine feature of the phenomenon or an artifact of how I described it; and which of the three cases is central.

I am aware of several things it is not, or not only, and I would want the differences drawn precisely rather than asserted: the social-scientific literature on predictions and classifications that feed back on the people they concern, where the classifier is normally outside the classified and the medium is different; the literature on self-regulating systems, where nothing is semantically about the whole; fixed-point treatments of self-referential truth, which handle reference but not the way the referring changes its object; and Hofstadter's own tangled hierarchies, which seem to me to be chiefly about reference and level-confusion rather than about this constitutive feedback. Tell me if I am wrong about any of those, if the phenomenon collapses into one of them, or if it has no home and needs one.
