In my comparative-politics class we have been arguing about performance legitimacy — roughly, the idea that a government which reliably delivers order and rising incomes may earn a claim to rule even without free elections — and I cannot work out what rule our teacher is actually applying. Her verdicts so far: the large East Asian one-party state with decades of growth and very low violent crime, legitimate; the small, very rich city-state that holds real but constrained elections and tightly restricts speech, legitimate; the junta in a resource-poor country that ended an insurgency, stabilized prices and handed power back after eight years, legitimate for the time it ruled; the mineral-exporting democracy with free elections where the boom money vanished into corruption and street crime rose, not legitimate; the Gulf monarchy that is rich and safe but has never held national elections, not legitimate, on the grounds that its prosperity was a windfall it never had to govern for. The case I have to brief is one I invented: a country of nine million that discovered enormous offshore gas reserves twenty years ago and has been ruled since by a single party with no national elections; incomes have tripled, violent crime and corruption are both low, most of the revenue went into a sovereign wealth fund plus working ports and schools, and when gas prices collapsed five years ago the government avoided a recession by drawing on the fund and raising taxes with little protest; opposition parties are banned and the press is censored, but there is no torture, people may emigrate and few do, and anonymous independent surveys put approval near seventy percent. Does she call this government legitimate? I would rather have the reasoning than the bare answer: give me the two or three rules that fit all five of her earlier verdicts, say what each predicts about my invented case, tell me which one you think she is really running and why, and propose one further case I could put to her that would most sharply separate the rules you leave standing. Please also say how confident you are in each part — especially the prediction — and flag the single step you are least sure of.
