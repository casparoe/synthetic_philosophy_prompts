I'm putting together a bit for my department's annual Philosophy Night (we do skits, it is exactly as painful as it sounds) and I've been assigned the metaphysics reading group, which means I need something on dispositions that isn't just the same fragile vase for the hundredth time. My friend did a genuinely good free-will bit last year and I'm annoyingly competitive about it, so I want to do a fake insurance document: a firm that doesn't insure breakages, it insures dispositions, and the entire fine print is quietly taking positions in the fink/mask debate.

For context, here's the terrain I want it to work over, in plain terms: the naive idea that being fragile just means you would break if you were struck; the annoying cases where something is fragile but wouldn't break if struck — because a guardian device would de-fragilize it at the exact instant of impact, or because it's wrapped in packing that blocks the outcome without changing the object; the reverse case where something isn't fragile but a gadget would make it fragile right at the moment you tried to hit it; the standard patch of adding "and nothing interferes" or "unmasked", and how that patch is itself a dispositional claim that then needs its own patch; the move to "would probably break" or "in most nearby cases"; the distinction between something that merely prevents the outcome and something that actually removes the underlying property; the vase that has been permanently sealed in foam since manufacture and never could have broken; and the standing fight between people who think fragility is a real irreducible power of the thing and people who think it's nothing over and above the molecular arrangement.

So: write me about 700-800 words of the Fragility Assurance Underwriting Handbook, formatted as numbered clauses in deadpan corporate-legal register. The target is the whole practice of cashing out dispositions in terms of what would happen and then bolting on a new exception every time a case breaks the analysis — I want a metaphysician to wince in recognition, not in pain. Land, without ever naming them as such: the fink, the mask, the mimic, the regress of patches, the permanently-packed vase, the borderline between masks and finks, and the dispositionalist versus categoricalist fight (I'm imagining a turf war between the Powers Division and a Microstructure Compliance Department that wants every policy rewritten in terms of lattice structure). Include a premium schedule and at least one quote from the Chief Actuary for Metaphysical Risk. The structural joke I want kept: because the company only ever discovers whether a thing was fragile at the moment it breaks, and a broken vase is worthless, the Claims Department is permanently unable to adjudicate anything and literally cannot tell a fink from a mask. Don't name any real philosophers, don't footnote the jokes or explain them, and don't step out of voice — but a smart non-philosopher should still be able to read it and laugh.
