Here's the setup I have in mind: a fictional jurisdiction, Meridian, has officially abolished retributive punishment and adopted something close to Caruso's public-health-quarantine model, meaning any hard treatment is supposed to be purely preventive — like quarantine — and not imposed because the person deserves to suffer. But because the public still wants some ceremonial expression of blame, Meridian adds a non-custodial accountability proceeding: offenders must attend restorative-style hearings, listen to victims' statements, and formally acknowledge that their action was wrong, while the state insists the acknowledgment carries no extra hard treatment and is not retributive. Now take a defendant I'm calling a responder-in-remission: someone whose capacity for reasons-responsiveness is usually intact thanks to medication, but who stopped taking it, had a violent flare-up, and committed an assault during that period; by the time of the proceeding they are back on medication and fully reasons-responsive. The crux is whether this non-retributive blame expression is legitimate on each view, and when exactly desert or blame is supposed to attach — at the time of the offense, when the agent was impaired, at the time of the proceeding, when the agent is responsive, or across the person as a diachronic agent.

Could Caruso treat the proceeding as permissible moral addressing or forward-looking rehabilitation, or would he say it is a cosmetically retributive trap that sneaks desert back in? Could Dennett say the acknowledgment expresses or tracks the compatibilist desert that Caruso denies, and does Dennett need the agent to be reasons-responsive at the time of the offense to have that desert, or is responsiveness at the proceeding enough?
