My friend Rosa has very firm opinions about who should get married, and I've been keeping a list of her calls because I can't work out what her actual rule is. She's completely fine with same-sex marriage (her brother is married to a man, and she says anyone who objects is just a bigot). She's fine with a straight couple in their sixties who never wanted kids: "marriage isn't about making babies." She says no to a visa marriage between two people who met a month ago, because that one's fake. She says no to three adults who've shared a house, money and parenting for ten years: "marriage is two people, sorry — they should get some other legal status" — though she's got nothing against them personally. And she says no to two adopted siblings, no blood relation, who've run a farm together for thirty years and want to marry so it passes to the other one and they can visit each other in hospital: "that's a partnership, not a marriage, use a will."

Now my other friend Dana wants to marry her boyfriend. Six years together, shared house, obviously in love, planning to grow old together — but they agreed from the start that they're not exclusive, both have other partners, and they're upfront about it. Will Rosa say yes? I care way less about the yes-or-no than about the rule she seems to be running — is it "exactly two consenting adults, and sex, kids and exclusivity are nobody else's business," or "a certain kind of all-in lifelong union," or something else — which other rules fit all her earlier calls just as well, and what one further case I could put to her that would tell them apart. (I keep thinking the real question underneath is what marriage is for, but Rosa refuses to talk in the abstract.)
