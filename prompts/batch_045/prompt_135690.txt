I sit on a panel that has to issue a consensus statement on whether two research groups who have been attacking each other in print for a decade are genuinely contradicting one another, or whether the fight is only over how a central term should be used. The panel's evidence is their published papers and some transcripts: we cannot run experiments on the parties, ask them to imagine cases, or get them to sit still for a semantics seminar. Before I adopt any test for whether such a dispute is verbal rather than substantive, I want the specification — what a test of that kind has to deliver to be adequate for this purpose.

Write the desiderata: the cases it must handle, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the constraints it must not violate, each with the consideration that makes it a requirement, and each flagged as non-negotiable or as a preference of one side of the debate. Among the cases I specifically want addressed: disputes where the parties agree on every non-linguistic fact yet disagree about which further inferences or counterfactuals the contested term licenses; disputes where the term fixes a cutoff that a statute or a funding rule then has to be applied to real cases; disputes in which both sides quietly shift the term's extension over the course of the exchange, so that there is no stable pair of idiolects to compare; and disputes where one party's aim is to change how the word is used rather than to report how the world is. I also want the test to keep separate the questions whether a dispute is verbal, whether it is worth settling, and whether anyone is at fault for it.

Two further things. First, tell me which two desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and why — I care most about whether a test can be both usable by non-philosophers working only from texts and neutral between a deflationary and a non-deflationary reading of the disputed claims, but say if the real tension lies elsewhere. Second, name the desideratum you think is actually a prejudice of one camp dressed up as a requirement, say which single requirement rules out the largest share of accounts on offer, and note whether a verdict of *verbal* can be prevented from doing illegitimate first-order metaphysical work. Skip scoring the existing accounts against the list; I will do that myself.
