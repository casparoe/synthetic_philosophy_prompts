For my applied ethics seminar I need to hand in a dialogue instead of a paper, so could you write one for me? Two students from the same seminar arguing about whether I have any reason not too buy the chicken sandwich. Priya says no: the canteen's supplier restocks in fixed batches, so one less sandwich sold this week won't change any order, and the same goes for every week after — my contribution is causally inert, and she thinks the usual expected-utility reply just papers over the fact that nobody knows what the actual probability is. Sam says yes, and he brings up a mechanism we invented in section: a "carryover rule", where the supplier's system logs each unsold unit in a buffer and cuts the next delivery by a whole crate whenever the buffer passes a set size — so my skipped purchase isn't lost the moment it fails to trigger anything, it's stored and can later combine with other people's skipped purchases to do something none of us did alone. They should really engage with each other rather than just trading speeches: Sam has to explain why a stored contribution that never on its own crosses the threshold still counts as *my* doing something, and Priya should both push back with the rounding worry (if the buffer only records whole crates, my single unit is deleted the second it's logged) and say whatever she thinks is wrong with the fairness-style "just do your share" replies. Make them equally sharp, no strawmen, and end on whatever real disagreement is left over instead of a tidy resolution.
