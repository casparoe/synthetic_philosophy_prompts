A friend of mine has read a fair amount of the philosophical literature on whether the numbers count in rescue cases and has come out firmly on the side that says they don't, but her actual verdicts are more complicated than that summary, and I want to know what she will say about a case we haven't discussed. Here are the verdicts I have from her.

1. A ship goes down. One stranger is in the water to port, five to starboard; she can reach one group in time and not the other; anyone she fails to reach drowns; there are no relationships, no other differences, and no further consequences. She says neither group has a stronger claim, that either rescue is permissible, and that she herself would toss a coin so that each group gets an equal chance.

2. The same shape, except that the one would drown while each of the five would lose a leg. She says: save the one, and no coin toss. The five losses do not add up to a death.

3. The same shape as 1, except that the one is her son. She says she would save him outright and would not think she had done wrong, but she also says she would not condemn a stranger in that position who tossed a coin instead.

4. The same shape as 1, except that the rescue of the five has a 60 percent chance of success while the rescue of the one is certain. She says: save the one. A gamble on five is not better than a certainty of one if the numbers don't count.

She has also said in conversation that she suspects there is some ratio at which her view breaks down and she would simply save the greater number, but she has never said what that ratio is and refuses to name one.

The new case is the same shape as 1 — all strangers, both rescues certain, no relationships, no side effects, nothing else at stake — except that it is one person on one side and ten thousand on the other.

What I want is not only the prediction. Tell me what she will say and why; set out the two or three rules that fit verdicts 1 through 4 equally well and say what each of them predicts about the ten-thousand case; tell me whether her coin toss is doing the work of a requirement or only of a tie-breaker she happens to like, and whether her real principle is about numbers or about how much each individual stands to lose; and, since I can put exactly one more case to her, tell me which case would best separate the rules you cannot eliminate here.
