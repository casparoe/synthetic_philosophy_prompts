Me and two others from a reading group I'm in (we've spent a term working through a pile of papers on thought experiments in ethics and personal identity) have drifted into a position that I now want stress-tested properly. Roughly: a thought experiment earns its keep only through what I've started calling its *bleachable* content — the formally specifiable structure of the case, i.e. whatever your background theory already has a rule for (causal topology, a decision matrix, dominance relations, identity of the players across time) — while every bit of non-bleachable content is pure rhetoric: that it's set on a spaceship, that a surgeon is cutting up a patient to save five, that it's written in the second person, that the death is described as painless, the order the two options come in, how many named individuals there are. Rhetoric does motivation, zero justification. Hence the bleaching test: re-describe the case with the flavour stripped and the structure held fixed; if your verdict flips, the original verdict was an artefact of the pump rather than a finding, and the case carries no evidential weight until someone shows the bleach altered the structure and not just the mood. I've got fairly invested — I've even sketched a taxonomy of flavour types (affective valence, genre, narrative perspective, granularity of the described mechanism, number of named individuals) on the assumption that each can shift verdicts independently of anything a theory cares about.

So: what would count against it? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and split into ones someone holding my position should concede versus ones they'd rightly dismiss as missing the point. Here's what I keep circling on. One: is there *load-bearing flavour* — detail that looks like pure mood but is actually the only way to get a reader to notice a structural feature the bleached version leaves implicit? Concreteness can disambiguate a causal claim that the abstract version leaves dangling. If so, bleaching sometimes destroys evidence instead of isolating it, and my test has a systematic bias. Two: do bleached cases have artefacts of their own? Very abstract cases are strange, and strangeness might push verdicts around as much as gore does — in which case bleach-invariance isn't testing for evidence at all, it's just measuring agreement between two idioms, and I've quietly privileged the dry one. Three: how much trouble is it that nobody can say in advance which details are structural? If anyone defending a case can always reply that your bleach changed the structure, the test can never return a verdict against anything, and I'd rather be told plainly that I've qualified it into unfalsifiability — and then told whether that's actually a problem for this kind of claim, given that it's methodological rather than empirical. Four: what does my view predict that its rivals don't? The rivals I have in mind are the view that thought experiments exercise some intuition faculty delivering defeasible evidence, and the deflationary view that they're just arguments with the premises left implicit and are only ever as good as those arguments. If my view predicts nothing those two don't, I've probably just redescribed one of them. Five: I half-remember experimental work claiming verdicts track things like whether a side effect is good or bad, how up-close-and-personal the described action is, or what language you read the case in — I don't trust my memory of any of it, so lean on it only where you're actually confident, and tell me which findings, if they hold, would genuinely discriminate between my position and its rivals rather than just showing verdicts are malleable, which I already believe.

Be straight with me about how exposed the position is, and mark how confident you are in each ranking rather than sounding evenly sure about all of it. What would move me most is something showing that flavour and structure don't carve at the joint I think they do — that bleaching isn't merely hard to apply but the wrong target altogether. And if the whole framing rests on something muddled, or if "what would count against this" just doesn't have a good answer for a claim like mine, say that instead of being polite about it.
