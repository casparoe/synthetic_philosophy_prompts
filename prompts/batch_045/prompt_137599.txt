I work as a machine learning engineer, mostly on distribution shift and domain adaptation. My working vocabulary is this: a model as a conditional distribution p(y|x); training as empirical risk minimization under a loss, including cross-entropy against soft targets, label smoothing, and distillation; deployment shift decomposed into covariate shift, label shift, and concept drift, where under label shift the class-conditional p(x|y) is assumed stable and only the class prior p(y) moves, and the new prior is typically re-estimated by EM and then imposed on the model; relative entropy both as loss and as regularizer; calibration; and a loose Bayesian vocabulary of priors and posteriors that I do not use with any rigor. I know nothing about formal epistemology beyond what I have picked up informally.

A colleague tells me that there is a philosophical update rule, Jeffrey conditionalization or probability kinematics, that generalizes Bayesian conditioning to cases where evidence is itself uncertain, and that it is mathematically just the minimum relative entropy projection of a prior onto the set of distributions matching some new marginals. She also says the rule behaves badly in ways philosophers care about: that it is sensitive to how finely you partition the space you are updating over, and that two such updates applied in sequence do not in general commute. If the identity with minimum-entropy projection is right, it sounds like something my field already does under the name of prior correction, and the bad behavior sounds like things we already live with. I want to know how much of that is right before I rely on it.

Please restate the following in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can: the contrast between strict conditionalization on a proposition learned with certainty and Jeffrey conditionalization on a partition with new probabilities assigned to its cells; the rigidity condition that does the real work in the rule; and the role of the partition in determining what the update even is. Where my vocabulary has several candidate renderings, say which is the honest one and which smuggles in an assumption of mine rather than translating. Then give me the residue report.

In particular I want four things addressed. First, check the minimum relative entropy claim: is it correct, does the direction of the divergence matter, and is characterizing the rule as minimal change a justification of it or only a redescription? Second, partition sensitivity. In my setting, imposing a new marginal over classes and imposing one over a finer grouping, say class crossed with a subgroup or cluster, give me different models. Is that the same phenomenon as the philosophers' sensitivity, or a different one that merely looks like it? Third, order dependence. My procedures are already order sensitive, so I cannot tell whether the non-commutativity of two soft updates is the same complaint I already handle empirically, or a distinct one that my vocabulary makes invisible. Fourth, and what I find strangest: the rule seems to require me to be certain of the new marginals while its whole motivation is that experience gives nothing with certainty. In my setting the target marginal is estimated from a finite sample, so I have uncertainty about a quantity the rule treats as given. Say what the philosophical literature does with that, and whether it translates into anything I could do differently.

Candidates I would guess at: a notion of evidence as opposed to data, and a notion of an update as rationally required rather than as one procedure among others chosen for its performance. For each item of residue, say whether you think it reveals a gap in my framework or a lack of clarity in the original debate, and do not flatter either side.
