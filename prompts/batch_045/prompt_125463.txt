I write compilers for a living (mostly type checking and a couple of optimisation passes) and mess around in Lean on weekends, so the vocabulary I'm actually fluent in is: object language vs metalanguage, ASTs and well-formedness judgements, inference rules as code in the kernel vs propositions as data, quotation and quasi-quotation, eval and self-hosting interpreters, LCF-style trusted kernels, soundness proofs that are themselves written in whatever logic I happen to trust that morning, and compiling a rule into silicon so the machine never has to look at a representation of it.

I keep getting pointed at Lewis Carroll's little dialogue where the Tortoise won't grant Z even after he's accepted A, B, and the hypothetical that if A and B then Z — he just keeps making Achilles enter one more hypothetical in his notebook, each one saying that if all the previous ones are true then Z must be, forever. I've seen the one-line answer (rules aren't premises, inference isn't just another assertion you can take on board), and it feels obviously right and at the same time like its hiding something. So I want the real translation, not the slogan.

First: restate the regress in my vocabulary as exactly as you can. My own guess is that the Tortoise is demanding that the inference rule be reified — turned from kernel code into an object-level datum, a term of type Prop or a row in a table — and that every time Achilles obliges, something still has to apply that datum, and that thing is code again, so you get the same shape as the fact that you can't write a self-interpreter without a base language somewhere underneath. If that's roughly the right mapping, say where the fit is exact and where I'm flattering myself.

Second: I think there are two worries here that people run together and I want them kept apart. One is the Tortoise's: why can't a rule simply be one more thing I accept. The other is: suppose I want a reason to think my deductive apparatus only ever outputs truths — can I argue for that without already leaning on the apparatus? In my world the second one looks like proving type soundness, which I do in the metalanguage using plain informal maths, and nobody treats that as scandalous or circular in a bad way. Does that mapping really hold, or does the philosophical version depend on something about consequence or truth that my metatheoretic argument silently doesn't have to supply? And are these two worries even the same kind of thing, or is one a point about what inference is and the other a point about how it could be vindicated?

Third, which is the part I actually care about: the residue. My machine is the one place in this story where the regress genuinely stops, and it stops in an unsatisfying way — the CPU doesn't represent the rule it's executing, it just transitions, and I baked the rule into the hardware and declared victory. Does the logical case have any analogue of that bottoming out, or is saying we just do infer, and there it ends a change of subject rather than an answer? Concretely: imagine a variant Tortoise who says, fine, I'll never dissent from any instance of the rule, I simply won't take the step myself — I'll wait for each instance to be handed to me. Is that position coherent? Does it have a clean counterpart in my vocabulary (rule compiled in vs. rule consulted from a table)? And if my vocabulary can describe that position but has no way to say what is wrong with it, is that a gap in my vocabulary, or a sign that the philosophers' complaint was never really about the mechanics?
