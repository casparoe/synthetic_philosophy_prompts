Hi — hoping someone here knows the consciousness literature better than me. I'm putting together a small study (reaction times plus a short verbal report after each trial) and I wanted to run it in the heterophenomenology style, since I like the idea of a method that doesn't do violence to the subject's own experience. My plan was to add what I've been calling a validation stage: after the session the subject reads back the transcript of what they said and marks which bits they 'stand behind' as true descriptions of what it was actually like for them, and we throw away the rest. That way the data we analyse is only the part the subject themself vouches for, which seems like the obvious way to take first-person authority seriously.

A reviewer shot this down, saying something like: in heterophenomenology the subject's authority is already total by stipulation, so a check like that can't add anything, and besides I'm smuggling in a Cartesian picture Dennett explicitly rejects. I don't really get it. If the whole selling point of the method is that it takes what people say about their experience seriously, why is it a problem to ask them to confirm it? And doesn't the method get the wrong answer in cases like blindsight, where people insist they saw nothing — if you take the reports at face value you'd have to conclude there's no visual experience going on at all, which surely can't be right?

Also, slightly separate: my advisor keeps saying heterophenomenology isn't the kind of thing that can be tested against theories like global workspace theory or IIT, and I don't see why not. Isn't it definately just the most careful version of the report-based methods everyone already uses? Would love to know where I'm going wrong — or if I'm not.
