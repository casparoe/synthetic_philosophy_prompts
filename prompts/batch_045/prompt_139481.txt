A friend in my reading group on African philosophy keeps running this argument at me and I cant find the loose step, but the conclusion is crazy, so there has to be one. We've been reading relational views of personhood — the idea, associated with Ubuntu, that being a person isn't something you have in yourself but something you have in virtue of how you stand to other people.

(1) On the relational view, what makes a human being a person is her standing in the right kind of relations to others — being recognized, being part of a community, being in relations of mutual care — and not any property she has on her own.

(2) If what makes X an F is X's standing in relation R, then X is an F only while X stands in R. Take away R and youve taken away the thing that was doing the work.

(3) Whether X stands in R to others isn't settled by anything intrinsic to X. Relations have two ends: the people around her can withhold recognition no matter how disposed she is to give it.

(4) So whether X is a person isn't settled by X's intrinsic facts. Someone intrinsically just like a person — same memories, same capacities, same psychology, same body — could fail to be one, and a person could stop being one without changing in herself at all.

(5) And if it's other people's conduct that settles it, then other people can bring it about, by their own acts and without touching her, that X is not a person.

(6) Deliberately bringing it about that a person is not a person is about the worst thing you can do to somebody. It's close to what makes killing someone the wrong it is, and maybe worse.

(7) Now take a case: a village that, without laying a finger on anyone, permanently stops treating one of its members as one of them. Nobody speaks to her, nobody counts her, nobody will be her kin or her neighbour. Call that total ostracism.

(8) By (5) and (6), total ostracism has the moral shape of murder, just without the corpse.

(9) And worse: a regime or an enslaver that successfully withholds all recognition from a group has, by (4), made it true that those people aren't persons. So the dehumanizer's verdict is self-certifying, and there's nothing left for the wrong of dehumanization to consist in. Try condemning chattel slavery in these terms.

(10) But (8) and (9) are absurd. Being cast out is a serious harm, and there are real literatures on social death, but it isn't murder. And if anything is a wrong done to persons, dehumanization is.

(11) So the relational view is false and personhood has to be something the individual has on her own.

My friend says the target is (1) and I should just go back to an individualist view. I dont want to: the whole reason the relational view appealed to me is that individualist views of the Locke-ish sort seem to kick out infants and people with advanced dementia, and the relational view handles those cases beautifully. I'd much rather give up (2) or (6). Is one of them actually broken, or is the rot somewhere else — and if it's (2), I don't see what the relational view is even claiming anymore.

Which step do you reject, why did that step look fine, and what does each of the other available options cost me? Under 500 words and lead with the step.
