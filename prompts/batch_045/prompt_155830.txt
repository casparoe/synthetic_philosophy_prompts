I am writing a paper on collective responsibility for harms produced by loosely structured and partly automated groups, and before I commit myself to a framework I want the standard of adequacy stated explicitly. What I need from you is that specification, not a survey of the literature: the list of things a theory of collective intentionality and joint action has to deliver in order to be usable for my purpose, each with the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and a verdict on which requirements are non-negotiable and which are only preferences of one camp.

My purpose fixes what counts as adequate. The theory has to be able to tell me, and to show me how it tells me, whether a group some of whose members are not human (a clinical team together with a diagnostic model, an editorial collective together with a ranking algorithm) can be said to act together at all; whether attributing a shared intention to such a group is ever anything more than shorthand for the attitudes of its human members; and whether a group of that kind can owe something to someone, such that there is a point in holding it to account.

Here are my candidate cases, which you should use or replace with better ones: two co-authors who work together smoothly but take themselves to be producing different things, one a manifesto and one a parody of a manifesto; contributors to a shared artifact who never exchange a signal and cannot track one another's intentions; a group that persists through complete turnover of its members and is nonetheless asked to answer for what it did; the participant who goes along only because exiting is costly, and who would prefer the activity not to happen; and the case in which one member has the standing to settle what the group is doing without the concurrence of the others. I also have candidate requirements whose status I do not trust: that a shared intention be analysable without residue into individual attitudes and the relations among them; that an account explain the normativity of joint action, the standing to rebuke and the obligation one owes one's co-participant, and not merely the coordination; that the theory not entail that prelinguistic children and other animals cannot act jointly; that it keep joint action, shared belief, and group agency apart; that it distinguish intending that we do something from intending to do one's part of what we do; and that it capture what joint action is like from the inside. Tell me which of these are genuine desiderata and which are the scruples of a single school.

I also suspect that some subset of whatever list you produce is jointly unsatisfiable. My guess is that persistence of the same we across total turnover of members, obligations owed between participants, and refusal to posit anything irreducibly group-level cannot all be had together; confirm that or relocate the conflict.

Respond as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, and give me nothing before the first item and nothing after the last: the desiderata in the order in which I should be least willing to surrender them, each with the case behind it and whether it is negotiable, and as the fifth and final item the subset you take to be jointly unsatisfiable, which of them I would have to give up, and which single requirement does the most work in excluding otherwise attractive theories.
