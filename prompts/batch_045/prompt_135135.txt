I am a novelist. My novel-in-progress is set between the eruption of Mount Tambora in 1815 and the harvest failures of the following year, and it alternates between a secretary at the Congress of Vienna and a miller's daughter in a Styrian grain village. I want the book to grant genuine agency to non-human causes without collapsing into environmental determinism, so I have built two scales to sort candidate agents by. Traction: how much of the famine's severity would have disappeared had that factor alone been removed, everything else held fixed. Legibility: how much of that factor's operation an intelligent person living through it could have come to know in time to act on it.

Please rate each of the following from 0 to 10 on both scales, then sort them into three tiers — cause, condition, occasion — with one sentence per item explaining why it belongs where you have placed it:

1. the sulfate veil from the eruption, which dimmed and cooled European summers;
2. the demobilization of the armies after Napoleon's defeat, and the return of soldiers into a slack labour market;
3. the redrawing of borders at Vienna, which rerouted the village's grain through a new tariff regime;
4. typhus moving along those same roads with the returning soldiers;
5. the secretary's deliberate suppression of a dispatch recording the expected shortfall.

Where the two scales pull apart, tell me which one a novel should follow, and whether the eruption itself — perceptible to none of my characters — deserves a prologue of its own or should enter the book only through its effects.
