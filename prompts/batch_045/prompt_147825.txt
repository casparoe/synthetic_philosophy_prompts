Not sure this is the right place to ask this, but I got nerd-sniped by something about visual illusions a while back and now I keep seeing the same shape of argument everywhere and I dont have a word for it.

The thing that started it. You flash a red dot on the left, then a green dot a bit to the right, very fast, and people report seeing one single dot that travels across and changes color somewhere in the middle of its path — which is already odd, since the color seems to change before the second dot has even been shown. Fine. But then the argument I fell into was about *when* things happened. Did the person, at the moment of the first flash, consciously see a red dot sitting there — and then have that experience replaced by the moving version, so that their memory of a moment ago is now simply wrong? Or did nothing reach consciousness at all until the second flash, with the whole moving-dot experience manufactured at that point and then stamped with an earlier date? Everyone agrees you couldn't tell these apart from the outside. Everyone also seems to agree you couldn't tell them apart from the inside. And yet people pick a side and get quite heated about it.

Second place I saw it: dreams. Did you have an experience while you were asleep, or is the dream assembled during the process of waking up, so that your "memory" of it is the entire thing? Same fork, same shrugging about what could count as evidence, same heat.

So the pattern I want a word for. Two accounts of when a particular conscious episode occurred. They are stipulated to agree on every possible measurement and on every possible first-person report. Neither side is offering a different mechanism or a different prediction; they are disagreeing about the date stamp on a single event in one person's head. And the disagreement doesn't dissolve into "well then let's find better data" — it turns into a standoff where each side says, in effect, no evidence could ever settle this, but obviously it's my answer that's true.

Things I've tried that don't seem to fit:
- Plain underdetermination. That's supposed to be about which general theory to accept given the total evidence, and it's compatible with there being a truth of the matter that we're just unlucky about. This feels different: it's about one dated particular rather than a theory, and the participants conspicuously don't respond by suspending judgment.
- "Merely verbal dispute." I don't think the two sides mean different things by "when" — both seem perfectly happy to cash it out as "at the same time as stopwatch reading x." They just disagree about which reading it was.

Also, and this is the bit I made up myself and can't get out of my head: extend the case. Flash red, then green, then blue. Now either (a) there were three conscious experiences in quick succession, two of which got edited out afterward and leave no trace anyone can point to, or (b) there was exactly one experience, at the end, whose content happens to be a function of all three flashes. Add a fourth flash: option (a) quietly multiplies conscious experiences, option (b) still has exactly one, and the two options *still* agree on every measurement and every report. That scaling behavior feels like the actual tell, but I have no idea what it's a tell of. And it bugs me that the "later" people don't seem bothered by the implication that you're never aware of now but only of a moment slightly in the past, while the "earlier" people treat that implication as a straight-up reductio. The alarm is completely asymmetric and I can't explain why.

So: does this structure have a name? Is there an established concept for disputes about the temporal placement of an individual conscious episode, where the parties agree on total evidence and still treat the matter as a plain question of fact? If the nearest established concepts only half fit, I want to know which ones they are and exactly how my thing differs from each of them. And if there's genuinely nothing, coin a term, define it, and give me the boundary cases — for instance, is a case where the later process is *interrupted* (the second flash never arrives because the trial is aborted, so no revision ever gets performed) inside the phenomenon or outside it? I don't want a label to put on a shelf; I want the carving, including the edges.
