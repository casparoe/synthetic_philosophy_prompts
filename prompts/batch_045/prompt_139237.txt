Hey — I'm on the programs committee at a small animal-advocacy funder and we're trying to settle our aquaculture strategy for next year. The three things on the table are: fund welfare standards and slaughter/stunning research for farmed fish and shrimp, fund campaigns that try to shrink demand for farmed seafood, or put the money into plant-based and cultivated seafood instead and leave farms alone. Every meeting we have goes in circles because people are answering different questions, so I want a decision tree we can walk through together on a whiteboard.

What I'd like: a tree where every branch node is a claim written as a plain yes/no or a pick-one, phrased so that someone with no background can answer it without knowing which position it leads to. About five questions, eight to twelve leaves. No branches like "do fish matter?" — make them bite on things like these instead:

- whether welfare should be added up per individual animal or per tonne of product (a tonne of small fish is thousands of animals, a tonne of a big one is a handful — so the two ways of counting give opposite advice about which species to push people toward);
- whether uncertainty about sentience should scale the moral weight down, and if so whether you'd accept the same move for a vertebrate whose sentience you're shakier about, or only for shrimp and other decapods;
- whether a life that contains more good than bad but ends in a bad death makes the practice net positive overall, or net negative;
- whether buying a higher-welfare version of the same product can make things worse by making the industry look respectable;
- whether the thing to fix is how the animals live, how they die, or how many of them there are.

Requirements on the leaves: each one gets a name (or "unnamed" if nobody really holds it), one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and one sentence on what we should actually fund if we end up there. Make sure these are all somewhere in the tree: the "no moral status, but reform anyway as insurance / because it's cheap goodwill" position; the reform-entrenches-the-industry position; the small-bodied-species position where the number of individuals per unit of food is the dominant consideration; the net-negative-lives position where reducing headcount beats improving lives; the position where the lives are net positive and aquaculture could in principle be expanded to displace chicken or beef; and at least one leaf where decapods get treated separately from finfish.

Build it from the logical space first — lay out which combinations of answers your five questions allow — then place the real positions in it, and tell us which cells are empty and why (incoherent? unattractive? just unexplored?). Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate rather than inventing a question to split them; my guess is that the insurance-style leaf and the discount-by-probability-of-sentience leaf end up recommending the same thing in practice, so say so if that's what you find.

One last thing: at the last meeting someone pointed out that some of this reasoning, if you follow it far enough, seems to imply we should be thinking about wild fish populations too, and that freaked people out. Please mark which leaves carry that implication and which ones stop short of it. Keep the language plain enough for a board of non-philosophers, but don't water down the awkward bits — I'd rather we argue about the awkward bits than about labels.
