I'm part of a small reading group — mostly philosophy of biology people plus two computer scientists — that has spent a term working through self-replication: von Neumann's self-reproducing automaton, Hofstadter's Typogenetics from *Gödel, Escher, Bach* (the formal system in which strings over A, C, G, T are simultaneously the data and, once translated into enzymes that cut, copy, move and insert on other strings, the programs, so that a strand can bring about the production of the very enzymes that act on strands), the actual molecular biology of template replication and translation, and the quining programs the CS people produce every time the discussion turns to codes. Before we read any more I want the specification rather than another theory. What I am asking for is the desiderata list for an account of what it is for a physical system to be a self-replicator, adequate for one job: to decide, without appeal to intuition, whether some novel system counts as one, and to say precisely what the DNA–Gödel isomorphism — the claim that the genetic code plays the role in replication that Gödel numbering plays in the incompleteness proof, letting a string inside a system reach out and act on the system that contains it — actually establishes and what it merely gestures at.

Give me the requirements with the case or consideration behind each: the phenomena any such account must cover, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the results from chemistry, thermodynamics and computation it is not allowed to contradict. The cases I want stress-tested, each either handled or excluded with a stated reason, are these: a single DNA strand in a tube of activated nucleotides with no primer and no polymerase, where nothing happens, versus the same strand with both present, versus the same strand with a primer the experimenter supplies; a mirror-image cell, every molecule of the opposite handedness, sitting in ordinary nutrient broth where nothing can copy it and which divides happily once we add a mirror polymerase, so that the system is a replicator only relative to a medium that need not exist; a quine, which reproduces its own source only because a CPU happens to be executing it, and a virus, which reproduces only because a host cell supplies ribosomes, transfer RNAs, energy and membrane; a 3D printer with a robot arm that prints and assembles the parts of a 3D printer from a CAD file held in its own memory, and the variant where the file lives on a server and is downloaded each cycle; a photocopier that scans and prints its own parts but assembles them only because a technician does; crystal growth and a spreading fire, which everyone agrees are not replication but nobody excludes by a reason that generalizes; a prion, which imposes its fold on other copies of the same protein, with heritable strain differences, but with no sequence and no code; and an autocatalytic reaction network in which every member is produced by some other member and nothing is plausibly the entity being copied. I also want the following near-misses classified: a mule, a plaster cast taken from a statue through a mould, and a transposable element that is copied by machinery to which it contributes nothing. Please don't give me a reading list or citations; we have more than enough of those, and it is the conceptual work I'm after.

Two things I especially want. First, mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the preferences of a camp: that replication essentially involve a description or code; that it be intrinsic to the object rather than to an object-in-a-medium; that it admit heritable variation of unbounded complexity; that it be statable without singling out a privileged level of description; that the interpreter of the description be produced by the replicating system itself rather than supplied by the environment — this last one is where the analogy with Gödel numbering bites, since a formal system interprets its own numerals by its own rules whereas DNA is read by polymerases the cell had to build. Second, if some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, say which and say what has to give; my suspicion is that you cannot have both a criterion intrinsic to the object and one that handles the primer case and the mirror cell, but I'd rather be told I'm wrong. Tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding the accounts people actually hold. My own hypothesis, offered as one, is that the isomorphism is exact at exactly one narrow point — the double use of one string as instruction and as copied data — and that the further claims made on its behalf, that replication is essentially self-referential or that a replicator must supply the semantics of its own description, are either false or are really claims about evolvability rather than about replication. A compact table scoring the von Neumann description-used-twice picture, the Hofstadter strange-loop picture, and the chemical-autocatalysis picture against your list is welcome at the end, but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; I can argue about the scoring with the group myself.
