Hey -- I'm on the Research Reproducibility Board at my univeristy (we hand out small internal replication grants), and we've got one case I have to decide by Friday. I need you to draft the decision memo I'll circulate to the board Thursday night.

Here's the deal. A psych lab applied for money to run a multi-study replication of a flashy social-priming effect. Their proposal says they'll use a Bayesian sequential design: collect data in small batches, peek after each batch, and stop as soon as the Bayes factor hits 6, which they treat as strong evidence. No fixed sample size, no p-values, no correction for peeking. Our board's current rule is that any funded project has to keep the overall false-positive rate under 5% across the whole line of studies, because we're still freaked out about the replication crisis and the file-drawer problem.

The twist: the applicants know about that rule, and they've proposed a homebrewed fix they call a 'sequential evidence escrow.' As I understand it, after every batch they'll log the Bayes factor with our staff, and at the end they'll apply a 'peek discount' -- they multiply the final Bayes factor by 0.85 for each interim look, and if the discounted Bayes factor is still above 6 they'll call the effect replicated. They argue this discount gives them long-run error control without having to pretend their prior is true or lock themselves into a fixed sample size. Half the board says this is just p-hacking with extra steps and a made-up fudge factor; the other half says it's a goodfaith bridge between frequentist error control and Bayesian learning, and that forcing fixed-N frequentist rules would throw away the whole point of going Bayesian.

Please write the board's decision memo: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a clear bottom line and a concrete timeline (what they must do before funds are released, and what they have to report at the end). Give reasons the researchers could actually respond to, and take the losing side seriously -- don't just strawman the people who hate the peek discount. I'd rather have something usable than perfect. Also, write the whole answer in American English, please: use 'behavior' and 'analyze' style spellings, not the British ones. Thanks!
