I run a graduate seminar on philosophy of cognitive science, and in last week's session we got completely stuck on a case that I had expected to be routine, so I want to put it to you as a probability question rather than an open one. Set up the four-kinds taxonomy as usual: Darwinian creatures are those whose behavioral options were fixed by natural selection, Skinnerian creatures are those that can also be shaped within a lifetime by the selective reinforcement of their own generated behavior, Popperian creatures are those that can let candidate hypotheses or plans be discarded in advance instead of risking themselves in the trial, and Gregorian creatures are those that have been equipped with culturally transmitted mind-tools such as words, diagrams, and schemas that redescribe their problem space for them, each kind sitting on top of the last. Now consider a system I will call Kestrel, and consider it as one individual rather than as part of a lineage. Kestrel consists of a language model whose weights are frozen during deployment, plus scaffolding: for each task it samples a few dozen candidate plans in natural language; each candidate is executed in a sandboxed simulator and only the survivors are executed in the world, so what dies is a plan, not Kestrel; because the weights are frozen, nothing learned in a session persists in the model itself, but the surviving plans together with the simulator's verdicts are written to an external store that is loaded into context in later sessions, so the heuristics behind candidate generation improve across sessions with no weight change anywhere; the model's vocabulary and plan schemas, and the simulator itself, were all authored by humans, and although Kestrel writes to the store it never edits the store's schema or the simulator.

Give me two numbers, please: your credence that Kestrel is a Popperian creature, and your credence that Kestrel is a Gregorian creature. I do not want the answer that it depends on what we mean by a creature or by a single individual; pick the reading you think is best, state it in a line, and then tell me how much the numbers move if you adopt the main rival reading instead. What I mainly want is the decomposition, with a rough probability and one line of justification attached to each term: whether Popperian status requires that the entity generating candidates be the same entity that would otherwise have paid the cost of error, or whether it is enough that some cost is avoided by discarding representations; whether the generate-and-test loop has to be internal to one individual's psychology and lifetime or can be spread across model plus simulator plus store with nothing changing its weights; whether the creature has to have acquired its own generator through learning rather than simply running one bequeathed by its designers, in which case the Popperian credit may belong to the design lineage rather than to Kestrel; whether the simulator has to represent the world with some genuine fidelity or whether mere filtering of candidates is enough, and whether it matters that humans wrote it; and for the Gregorian question, whether mind-tools have to be acquired from a social environment and used to redescribe one's own problems, or whether a system built out of other creatures' words and schemas is Gregorian by constitution. Say in particular whether you take the tower to be cumulative here, so that failing to be Popperian rules out being Gregorian, then combine the terms into each final number and tell me which single term the answer is most sensitive to and which term you are least confident about.

For calibration against my own thinking: I sit near 0.55 that Kestrel is Popperian and my co-instructor sits near 0.15, and I suspect we are not actually disagreeing about the facts of the case. Tell me where that disagreement most likely lives. Then take four variants, one at a time, and say how much each moves your number and in which direction: unfreezing the weights so that sandbox verdicts generate gradient updates; letting Kestrel rewrite its own simulator and its own store schema; removing the sandbox so that failed candidates are tried in the world and cost something; and pooling the external store across many copies of Kestrel, so each copy reads what the others wrote. Finally, an honesty check I care about: if your reasoning makes Kestrel Popperian, does the same reasoning make a termite mound, a business firm, or a scientific community Popperian, and if so is that a reductio of your argument or a consequence you are happy to accept?
