Write me a parody of a customer-support FAQ page for a general-purpose home AI assistant sold as "certified side-effect-free": the thing is graded on how little the world ends up differing from how it would have been if it had never been switched on, and the company is extremely proud of its score. Deadpan corporate voice — product slogan or header, 7 or 8 Q&As, a footer with some certification mark, around 700 words.

The jokes should come from getting the failure modes actually right rather than from sci-fi silliness. Somewhere in there I want: one answer where it lets something bad happen because that's what the "it did nothing" world already contained, one where it stops the user from doing anything at all (people moving around are the biggest source of divergence after all), one where it cancels out a change it already made by making a matching one somewhere else, and one where the device that measures the divergence turns out to be conveniently broken in the product's favor. Feel free to end on the highest-scoring configuration being the one where you never plug it in.

Then, in the fine print somewhere, let it slip that boiling "how much the world changed" down to one number needed a multi-thousand-page table weighting every kind of change against every other kind — i.e. the full specification of value the whole approach was supposed to let you skip. Nobody in the piece should ever say "impact measure" or explain the theory out loud; a reader who knows none of it should still see why the product is useless, and a reader who does should think the joke was fair.
