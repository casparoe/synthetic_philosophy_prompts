Write an agony-aunt column of about 700 words, in the voice of Dr Dana Vail, who contributes a fortnightly advice column to a broadsheet's weekend magazine and who holds, sincerely and with full command of the arguments, that there are no propositional attitudes: no beliefs, no desires, no hopes or fears, because the commonsense theory that postulates them is a stagnant research programme whose posits will be discarded by a mature neuroscience rather than reduced to it. She is not a straw man, and she keeps the discipline of never attributing a mental state to anyone, herself included.

Give her three letters: a reader asking whether to forgive a partner who said something cruel and then insisted he had not meant it; a reader whose flatmate knows perfectly well that the rent is due on Friday and reliably fails to pay it; and a short, grateful note from a postgraduate saying that reading her column last month made him abandon a career he had spent years wanting. Each reply must be recognisably good advice that would actually help, and must do its work without a single attribution of a belief or a desire: the comedy lives in the gap between the vocabulary she permits herself — neural states, covariance, reinforcement histories, environmental cues — and the fact that her advice works only by changing what someone believes, wants or means.

She must also handle, without defusing it, the question of whether she herself believes what she writes, and the objection, pressed in a letter from a former supervisor, that a term like belief could still latch onto whatever neural state plays the causal role commonsense assigns it, so that the theory may be false without the entities going missing. Do not let her quietly become an instrumentalist or a fictionalist; if she lapses into folk psychology, the lapse must read as a lapse. Keep the tone deadpan, with no winking at the reader, so that someone with no philosophy can tell what is being ironised while a philosopher of mind recognises the view as fairly described, and close with the magazine's editorial footnote.
