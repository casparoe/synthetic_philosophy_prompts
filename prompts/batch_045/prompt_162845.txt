I'm a science journalist working on a piece about what emotions actually are, and I've been polling my own gut on cases. The pattern isn't what I expected:

- Horror movie: genuinely scared, grabbing the armrest, enjoying it, and I don't for a second believe I'm in danger. Real fear.
- Flying: I know the safety statistics better than most people (I've reported on them) and I'm still terrified. Real fear, and there's something wrong with it.
- A drug that produces exactly the bodily signature of grief — tight chest, tears, lethargy — with nothing in my life I'm sad about. Not grief. A side effect.
- Same adrenaline surge: rollercoaster is a thrill, car swerving at me is fear. Different emotions, and the difference seems to be what I take to be going on.
- A colleague insults me, I register it fully, I think "I should be furious" — and I feel nothing. I'd say I'm not angry.
- I'm angry at the airline's rebooking algorithm. It has no intentions and can't wrong me. Still: real anger.
- I'm furious at the rain that wrecks my reporting trip, and at a jammed drawer. On reflection that's not anger, just frustration. But isn't that basically the algorithm case?
- Sunday-evening dread with nothing it's about: feels like fear from the inside, but I want to call it a mood.
- Reading a statistic about kids dying in a famine I'll never see: real grief, with no image of anyone in my head.
- The AI assistant I've talked to every day for a year gets switched off. I'm upset, and I don't think it was conscious.

My own stab: an emotion is a feeling that has an object, where the object is supplied by perception, memory, or imagination, and it's the kind of state that can be fitting or unfitting relative to that object, not merely caused by it.
