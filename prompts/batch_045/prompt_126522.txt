My friend and I spent three hours last night arguing about whether my family group chat counts as an echo chamber, and I've been going in circles since. We ended up drawing a line I half believe: a bubble is when you just don't hear the other side, a chamber is when your group has a standing story about why outsiders can't be trusted, so that hearing them makes you more sure instead of less. Here's where I land on cases.

My uncle, who watches one cable channel and literally never encounters the other side: not a chamber, just uninformed, and not really blameworthy — sending him articles should help. My old subreddit: a chamber, clearly — every mainstream outlet was "captured," anyone who left had been paid off, and the more we read the opposition the more certain we got. A bunch of epidemiologists who refuse to engage with anti-vax researchers: not a chamber, even though the structure looks identical to the subreddit. My book club, which reads only women writers: not a chamber either, and I actively don't want it fixed. A cancer support group I sat in on: total trust in the oncologists about medicine, but a firm sense that only another patient can tell you what it's like, and non-patients get quietly tuned out on that one topic: a good thing, honestly, though by my own test it looks like a chamber. Me, now — I left that scene and I've basically swung to believing whatever the people who used to drive me crazy say: not better than where I started, and maybe worse. Worst of the lot, I think, is a media-literacy group I know that reads everything, including all the stuff they disagree with, but only ever as specimens to diagnose.

Is there one principle here, and does it have to mention whether the belief is true? Which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? And what does it say about a feed that just shows you more of what you already clicked, with no community and no story about outsiders at all?
