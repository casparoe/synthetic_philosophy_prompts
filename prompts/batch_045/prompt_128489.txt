I work in facilities at a community college district — about 9,000 students, one central kitchen and two little satellite cafes — and somehow I got put on our Sustainable Food Working Group, which has to hand the Board of Trustees a recommendation by the 14th about whether to sign a three-year "lower-carbon menu" contract with our food service provider. I'm not an academic at all; I got into this because my brother-in-law spent Thanksgiving telling me that nothing any of us does makes any difference, it drove me up the wall, and now I'm the one who has to write the recommendation and I've slowly realized he might be partly right, which is annoying.

Here's the mess of facts I'm working with. The new contract costs roughly $190k a year more than just renewing what we have, and that money would otherwise restart the peer tutoring program that got cut two years ago, so it's not free money in any sense. What we'd get: the provider cuts the district's beef purchases by about 40 tons a year, plus something they call a "demand signal" clause. But their buyer explained (a bit too honestly, I thought) that they actually order through a regional distributor on a rolling forecast, so a lot of any reduction just gets rerouted — leftover units get offered first to other institutional customers at around a 15% discount, and only the units nobody else takes don't get reordered the following quarter. Then there's this detail I can't stop thinking about: the distributor's ordering system buys in whole pallets and rounds to the nearest pallet, and our current volume sits about 4% above a pallet boundary, so a 40-ton cut (maybe 7% of what we buy) definitely flips a pallet, but a 3% cut would do literally nothing at all unless someone else's change pushed us past the boundary anyway. Meanwhile the student union is voting next month on going beef-free in its own cafe, which is only about 2% on its own. So I genuinely can't tell whether our cut is what does the work, whether the union's smaller change is, or whether the pallet would have flipped anyway because of something happening three states over. Oh, and the provider let slip that our participation would show up in the sustainability reporting they do for other clients, but wouldn't say whether that actually changes anything downstream.

What I need from you is the memo itself, not a philosophy lesson — please don't point me at papers or books, I won't read them. Something around 800–1000 words that I could circulate under my name: a clear bottom line (sign it, sign it with conditions, or don't sign), a timeframe, and at least one thing we can actually go back and check in a year with a stated trigger for revisiting the decision. Please take the strongest version of my brother-in-law's argument seriously instead of just dismissing it — including the version where the beef reduction really does change the number of animals raised by zero — and tell me which of our reasons would still survive if that turned out to be true, and which ones quietly collapse. Also: our group has eight members and the chair only votes to break ties, and one member has already said he's going to abstain because his one vote will never decide anything, which is my brother-in-law's argument in miniature, and I'd like the memo to say something about that too. If you think the whole thing turns on a fact we should go find out before the 14th, tell me which fact and who to ask.
