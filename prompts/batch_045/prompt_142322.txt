Write me an essay on whether something that doesn't happen can be a cause — and please land somewhere at the end, I want an actual verdict with reasons, not "it depends what you mean by cause".

Some context for where I'm coming from: I'm retired, spent my career as an experimental physcisist, and only started reading philosophy a couple of years ago. My own hunch, which may well be wrong, is that physics never has to cite an absence. The state of a system is made of the stuff that's there, and the equations take you from one such state to the next; there just don't seem to be negative events in the furniture of the world. So "he didn't call and that's why the house burned" looks to me like shorthand for a pattern of dependence plus some background norm about what he was meant to do.

Here's the case that makes me doubt that. I made it up, but the structure is what matters. A seed bank's cooling vault has a failsafe: the control computer dumps liquid nitrogen into the vault unless it recieves a signed "all clear" ping from the duty officer's console at least once in every 30-minute window. The cooling itself is working fine. One night Ravi, the duty officer, is supposed to ping every 20 minutes; he falls asleep two hours in and sends nothing. His relief Mara is supposed to take over automatically whenever Ravi misses a ping, but the handover page goes to a phone with a dead battery, so she sends nothing either. The nitrogen dumps and a tray of irreplaceable seed accessions is destroyed by over-freezing. Either one of them pinging would have been enough to stop the dump. Neither did.

So: did Ravi's not-pinging cause the loss? Did Mara's? If you say yes, then what about the night cleaner, who also didn't ping — and me, and a rock in the car park, since none of us pinged either? Say something about why the list of "omissions" doesn't balloon until it's useless. And tell me whether your answer shifts if the question is "what explains the loss" versus "who is to blame" versus "what should we change so this doesn't happen again", and whether it matters that the story also contains a perfectly ordinary positive event, the dead battery.

Finally: if my physicist's instinct is wrong, say so plainly, and tell me what I should put in place of the absence — what is the thing that's actually doing the causing?
