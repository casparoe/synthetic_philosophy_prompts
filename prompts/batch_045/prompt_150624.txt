My flatmate is doing an MSc in computational linguistics and we got into an argument about machine translation last night that she definitely won, but for reasons I don't think are the right ones. We started from Hofstadter's complaint that statistical translation is shallow — that it shuffles words around using surface patterns without having any idea what the words are about. I said that complaint is now just outdated: modern systems aren't phrase tables anymore, they're neural, trained on absurd amounts of text, they handle idioms and pronouns and register, and when I fed one a paragraph with a genuinely ambiguous pronoun it picked the right referent. So whatever shallowness used to mean, better output settles it — understanding just is being reliably right about what a sentence means, and if a system is right about as often as a tired human translator, then the debate is over and Hofstadter lost. I also said that since errors are now mostly the *same kinds* of errors humans make, there's no principled difference left to point at.

Then we tried to make it harder. Turkish — and languages like it — mark in the verb whether the speaker witnessed the event or is only reporting or inferring it, a distinction English doesn't grammatically mark. So we gave the system a few dozen short English sentences like *the train was late* or *she left her keys on the table*, with nothing in the prompt indicating how the speaker knows this, and asked for Turkish. Most of the time it just picked the witnessed form and said nothing, which on my reading means it's quietly inventing a fact about the speaker's situation that isn't in the source. But then, when I added one line saying *I heard this from my sister*, it switched to the reported form perfectly. My flatmate says that's the whole point: it tracks context, it isn't stuck on a default. I say the switching is the interesting bit and shows it isn't shallow. (Going the other direction, Turkish into English, it never flagged that the distinction was vanishing — I said that's a separate bug, she said it's irrelevant, and we left it there.)

What I'd like from you is a straight answer about where this argument goes wrong, because I suspect I'm conflating two or three things and can't tell which of my claims is the load-bearing mistake. Is output quality even the right thing to look at, given what shallowness is supposed to be a claim about?
