I've somehow ended up as the mediator between two colleagues in my department who have been going at each other about predictive processing for about three years, and they've finally agreed to do an adversarial collaboration — with me writing it up, which I'm doing badly, because every formulation I produce gets rejected by one of them as a strawman within ten minutes. So draft the document for me. On one side, the active-inference / prediction-error-minimization person: perception is inference over an internal generative model, action exists to make the model's predictions come true, and both drop out of minimizing a bound on surprise. On the other side, the enactive / ecological / dynamical-systems person: you get perception and action out of coupled agent–environment dynamics and affordances, and internal models, predictions and error signals either add nothing explanatory or aren't even coherent as descriptions of what the system is doing. I want three things: a numbered section of claims both can genuinely sign — including about the formalism itself, about what the free-energy principle is even a claim about (every self-organizing system at a non-equilibrium steady state? just brains? just certain cortical circuits?), and about what everyone takes the experimental record to have actually shown so far; then a table of the remaining disagreements, each one stated twice, once in wording the predictive-processing person would accept for their own view and once in wording the enactivist would; then a crux table, with for each crux a concrete case or result both would concede bears on it and each side's prediction written down in advance, before anyone knows how it comes out.

Some terms of reference, because I think they've been talking past each other. Please keep apart three things they keep sliding between: the free-energy principle in its very general form, predictive processing as an architecture for the whole brain, and predictive coding as the narrower circuit-level story about which pyramidal layers carry predictions and which carry errors — I suspect a good chunk of their "dispute" is really about which of those is on the table today. Please also make both of them address the dark-room objection (if what you do is minimize surprise, why not go sit in a dark cupboard forever?), along with the standard reply in terms of prior preferences and expected uncertainty, and have them say bluntly whether my enactivist friend is right that the reply is ad hoc rescue. On the triviality worry, make them say what would count as a system that has a Markov blanket but doesn't minimize free energy, and what either of them would conclude if someone cleanly showed that some plainly non-neural system — a convection cell, say — satisfies the formalism. And I want both of them to respond to a landing zone I've been toying with and can't get either to engage with, which I'm calling update-rule instrumentalism: the generative model and the free-energy bound are just a compact way of writing down the update rule the system's dynamics approximate, so a system can count as "doing inference" in that thin sense while containing nothing at all that plays the role of a model, a prediction, or a representation. The predictive-processing person will say that makes the theory unfalsifiable; the enactivist will say it makes the theory trivial. Have them say exactly that, and then have each say whether they'd accept it as a place the debate could end or whether it just relocates the fight somewhere else.

Two housekeeping things. First: no citations, no reading lists, no "see Friston 2010" (or whatever year it actually is) — I want the arguments, not the literature, and I'd rather you reconstruct the position from scratch than gesture at papers. Second, and this matters more: tag the agreed claims for me. I want to know which ones are genuinely uncontroversial among people who work on this, which are just things these two happen to agree on, and which are contested in a way where even the state of the controversy is unsettled — I'm teaching this next term and I don't want to present a live fight as the standard line, or vice versa. I've been teaching bits of it on and off for years and I'm not longer sure which parts I actually understand, so be explicit about your own confidence too.
