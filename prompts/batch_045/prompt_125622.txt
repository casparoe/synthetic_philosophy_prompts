I want you to read Bush v. Gore as a case about vagueness instead of as a case about federalism. Bones of it, so you don't have to lean on shaky recall: Florida law told canvassing boards to count a ballot if it showed the voter's clear intent, the punch-card machines left hanging and dimpled chads behind, different counties drifted into different chad rules, the certified margin was in the hundreds of votes, and in December 2000 the Supreme Court stopped the statewide recount 5-4 largely on the ground that counties applying different standards for discerning intent violated equal protection, with the federal safe-harbor deadline a couple of hours away.

The mapping I have in mind: treat the admissible chad rules as admissible sharpenings of that intent standard. Supervaluationism then says a ballot is definitely a vote iff every admissible rule counts it, possibly a vote if some do, and that the number of legally valid Gore votes in Florida was indeterminate rather than merely unknown; epistemicism says there was a sharp cutoff somewhere and a determinate count, and the whole mess was an epistemic one about a fact that was there.

Do the mapping properly and then push back on it. In particular: what would each theory say the Court should have done, given that it had to produce a winner by a hard deadline; does the distinction between local and global supervaluational consequence do any real work here, or does requiring one uniform statewide standard versus letting each county pick its own admissible rule make no difference to the set of definitely-valid ballots; where does the higher-order vagueness bite, given that which chad rules are admissible looks vague too; and does either view have room for a fractional remedy, counting a dimpled chad as part of a vote rather than all or nothing, or is that only available to a degrees-of-truth view neither of them endorses. Finally, tell me honestly if I'm just pattern-matching: the equal protection complaint is about voters being treated alike, which is a normative claim about fairness, not a semantic one about indeterminacy, so say where the vagueness lens actually explains the holding and where it only redescribes it, and where both theories are simply silent.
