I'm revising the framing chapter of a dissertation on accuracy-first epistemology, and before I commit to the package in print I want it audited for consistency. Roughly, here is where I've landed:

(1) Veritism: accuracy — closeness to the truth — is the sole fundamental epistemic value; evidential support, coherence, and understanding matter, if at all, only as means to accuracy.

(2) The inaccuracy of a credence function over a partition is to be measured by the Brier score, and I take the usual case for it (strict propriety together with additivity) to settle the measure on purely alethic grounds: nothing pragmatic is entering the framework at the level of measurement.

(3) The central diachronic norm is vindicated by maximizing expected accuracy as judged from the agent's own current credences — conditionalizing is what does best by the agent's own lights, and that is enough to make it the norm for updating.

(4) Permissivism: given sparse and symmetric evidence — a coin of unknown bias that has come up heads once — some range of credences in heads on the next toss is rationally permissible, and no accuracy-based argument should collapse that range to a single point.

(5) A credence can be irrational even when it happens to be maximally accurate: someone who lands on a true proposition by wishful thinking is not, for that reason alone, rational.

Which pairs of these are genuinely in tension, and which only look as though they are? Please rank the real tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally serious, and for each say what exactly would have to be given up and what that costs. Then tell me whether any single revision would clean up more than one of them at once, and which commitment is doing the most load-bearing work in the package. I don't need anything attributed or cited — I want the argumentative structure, not the literature. Maximum 600 words.
