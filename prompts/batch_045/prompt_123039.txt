I cover science and ideas for a national newspaper, and I'm finishing a long piece that uses the argument about whether the symbols inside a language model mean anything as its spine. Before I build a feature on top of a position, I like to find out whether the position is one thing or several things wearing the same coat. So I'd like an audit of what follows, and I'd rather you found the cracks than told me the position is reasonable overall.

The commitments, roughly as I'd put them in print:

1. The symbol grounding problem is a genuine problem, and not just the Chinese Room argument in different vocabulary. A system whose training input consists entirely of symbol strings cannot acquire meanings for those symbols from that training alone, however much of it there is and however well the system performs on our benchmarks. Meaning requires some non-symbolic causal contact with what the symbols are about.

2. The usual remedy is not a remedy. Attaching a camera or a microphone and letting the system learn that a given token fires when the sensor is in a certain state does not ground that token, because covariation with a proximal stimulus does not settle which distal thing the token is about; and adding more sensors and more effectors does not, in principle, change that.

3. Most of my own vocabulary was acquired from text and testimony. I have never seen a quark, a velodrome, or an ion channel; I do not think my uses of those terms are empty, and I do not think they borrow their content from some specialist who has seen the things.

4. In any case, meaning is largely a matter of what a symbol goes with. What makes my use of a term the use it is is its inferential and distributional relations to the other symbols in the system, not any pointing relation between it and the world.

5. There is no bright line between grounded and ungrounded systems. Grounding, whatever exactly it turns out to be, admits of degrees, and there is no fact of the matter about how much is enough for a symbol to count as meaning something.

6. I do not think today's large language models understand what they produce, but I think the interesting explanation is their training objective: they are built to predict text rather than to be right about the world. On this telling, the missing-grounding complaint about them is secondary, or a special case of something else.

7. Even a fully grounded system would not thereby have beliefs. For a state to be a belief it must be the sort of thing that can misrepresent, that can be revised for reasons, and that can figure in the system's own deliberation; none of that follows from causal contact with an environment.

Two devices I've been testing these against, both of my own construction, since I couldn't find equivalents in the literature and I want the piece to move rather than survey:

Case A. Two systems are trained on the same corpus. The second also has one extra input channel: a rooftop light sensor wired into its input stream, so that one particular token fires whenever it is daylight where the machine is running, and never otherwise. One channel, one distal-ish property, no labels. My instinct is that the second system is barely more grounded than the first, in any sense of grounded I can reconstruct, and that this instinct is evidence about commitment 2 rather than about how much hardware you need.

The override test. A symbol is grounded for a system to the extent that the system will, without retraining, allow non-linguistic input bearing on the same subject matter to defeat what the corpus had led it to expect — to the extent, that is, that the world and not merely the text retains the right to correct it.

What I want from you:

- Go through 1 through 7 pairwise and tell me which pairs are in real tension, which only look like it, and which are simply consistent. I'd rather you discriminated than flagged everything.
- Rank the real tensions by how badly they damage the package, rather than listing them as equally serious, and for each one say precisely which commitment has to give and what the cheapest revision would be — and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set.
- Two I especially want checked. Do 1 and 2 together entail that nothing at all is grounded, including ordinary speakers, and if they do, is that a reductio of 1, of 2, or something I should simply accept? And is 3 genuinely a problem for 1, or does the standard reply — a small stock of grounded symbols plus testimony doing the rest — dispose of it, and does 4 then take that reply away again?
- Tell me whether the override test is doing independent work or is only commitment 2 or commitment 4 dressed up as a criterion; and, relatedly, whether a system that passed it would count as grounded by my own lights given everything else I've said.

I don't know which of the seven I'd drop, and I'd rather you didn't assume I have a favourite. If the honest answer is that two of them are the same claim counted twice, or that one of them is doing no work at all, say so.
