I convene a cause-prioritization reading group for the public-policy PhD students in my department, and last session two members spent the whole hour on the importance–tractability–neglectedness framework without either of them giving ground. I would like a move-by-move adjudication; I genuinely could not tell who was ahead.

(1) Jonas opened by saying ITN double-counts: tractability, if it means anything, is the good done per dollar at the current margin, and where that margin sits is just a function of how much money the cause already attracts — which is what neglectedness measures. So the product multiplies one quantity by a proxy for itself, and flatters causes that look cheap only because no one has tried to scale them.

(2) Miriam replied that tractability in the framework is read at achievable scale, not at the margin of the next dollar, while neglectedness stands in for how far the funding curve runs before returns decay; on that reading the product crudely approximates the area under a marginal-returns curve rather than compounding two independent facts.

(3) Jonas said that if this is what ITN is doing, it is not a ranking rule at all: with a fixed budget you spread money across causes until marginal returns equalize, and the top-product cause can deserve almost none of it if its returns fall off a cliff after the first million. Worse, comparing products is precisely wrong when decay shapes differ — a cause with lower initial returns but a long flat tail beats a steep one at any budget worth arguing about.

(4) Miriam granted the portfolio point but said ITN is a triage device for one more grant decision at the margin, and there the product ranking is roughly right; decay shapes only matter for large reallocations, which is a different decision with different inputs.

(5) Jonas said the retreat fails: the margin in question is a real grant, not an infinitesimal, so shape matters there too; and tractability estimates are themselves uncertain, so where uncertainty is high part of the return to funding is buying the information that places the next grant. A low-expected-ITN, high-variance cause can then be the better marginal bet — call it option value or call it a fourth factor; either way ITN as stated cannot see it.

(6) Miriam answered that this is a fourth factor smuggled in to manufacture a defect: if unmeasured considerations count against a framework, none survives, and practitioners already handle exploration by earmarking money outside the framework.

(7) Jonas said that concession is the whole point — a framework that cannot represent the main reason to fund uncertain causes is a tool for comparing knowns, not for prioritizing — and added that neglectedness itself needs a counterfactual reading: what matters is what the rest of the funding ecosystem does with money you do not spend, so a neglected cause with one committed billionaire behind it is not neglected at your margin, and the usual total-resources proxy can invert the ordering.

(8) Miriam agreed about the counterfactual reading but called it a measurement problem rather than a conceptual one, and said much of their dispute is verbal: Jonas is testing ITN as a decision procedure while she is defending it as a checklist of considerations that any such procedure has to combine.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then rate each of the eight moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right with a brief justification, say who is more right overall, and close with what both of them are missing.
