Putting together a short position statement for a reading group on AI and understanding, and I'd like someone to poke holes in it before I present it. Roughly where I've landed:

1. Understanding is a capacity, not a state — I understand something only if I can do things with it (debug it, vary the case, explain it to someone else), not if I just happen to have true beliefs about it.
2. So delegating to a model is fine as long as I actually check the output. Production doesn't matter; verification is where the epistemic work is.
3. Long-term reliance does erode the underlying skill though, and that's genuinely bad, not just nostalgia for doing things the hard way.
4. I don't think depending on a model is categorically different from depending on a sharp colleague or a good textbook — testimony and division of cognitive labor are normal, not shameful.
5. I also think the model doesn't understand anything, and yet I do count reading its explanations as learning.
6. Concretely: last month I shipped a module I could not have written myself. I read the diff carefully, it looked right, the tests passed. I don't think I did anything wrong there.
7. I'm not personally worried about erosion, because if my skills were sliding I'd notice — and anyway I deliberately do some tasks the slow way as maintenance.

One more thing I've noticed but can't place: when I'm unsure whether a generated function or proof is actually correct, the only check I have left is to ask the model to re-derive it a different way and see if it agrees. That feels circular, but in practice it works fine.

Which of these genuinely conflict, and which only look like they do? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say exactly what has to give and what that costs me. Please don't just announce that everything is in tension with everything else — I want the two or three that actually bite. Keep it under 500 words and lead with the worst one.
