On Thursday I'm giving a paper at a small one-day workshop on value and its objects — title is something like "Moore's Two Ideals: Beauty, Friendship, and the Scale of Isolation." I need a question-period prep sheet and I'm bracing myself a bit.

Thesis in four sentences. Moore pairs the enjoyment of beautiful objects and the pleasures of human intercourse as the two greatest goods we know of, but his own method of isolation pulls them apart: if a beautiful world with no one in it is genuinely better than an ugly one with no one in it — his own move against hedonism — then the value of aesthetic appreciation isn't located in the subject's consciousness. And the same pressure applies to friendship, where a relationship whose other half turns out to be simulated is worth far less even holding the lover's experiences fixed. So I propose an anchoring condition: a good's value is anchored in whatever the good cannot survive the removal of. Beauty is object-anchored, friendship is other-anchored, a drug pleasure is self-anchored — and Moore's talk of states of consciousness is a slip that his isolation test doesn't actually support. Second half of the paper, the bit I'm least sure about: the test never tells you what the unit of isolation is, whether you isolate the friendship or only one friend's consciousness, and I argue the unit has to be set by the nature of the item itself, which means isolation can't deliver the single determinate verdict Moore wanted from it.

The room is about twenty-five people. Three early analytic specialists who care most about getting the 1903 book right and who'll treat the later 1922 essay as the mature view; two consequentialists of broadly hedonic sympathy who think the whole "anchoring" apparatus is hedonism with extra steps; one Aristotelian who works on friendship and will think I've asked the wrong question by starting from value at all; and a handful of grad students, one of whom always asks about the metaphysics.

Tell me what I should concede in the first five minutes rather than be forced into later, and what the one reply is that has to land if the paper is going to survive.
