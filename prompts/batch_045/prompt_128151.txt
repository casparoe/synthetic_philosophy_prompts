**Question 5 (30 points).** In each generation, players are randomly paired to play an iterated prisoner's dilemma. After each round the interaction continues with probability w, independently of everything that has happened so far, so the expected number of rounds is 1/(1−w). Per-round payoffs are T = 5, R = 3, P = 1, S = 0, where T is what a defector gets against a cooperator, R is mutual cooperation, P is mutual defection, and S is what a cooperator gets against a defector. A player's fitness is the expected total of its per-round payoffs over the whole interaction. Say that a resident strategy *resists invasion* by a mutant strategy when V(resident | resident) ≥ V(mutant | resident), and that it is *collectively stable* when it resists invasion by every possible mutant, with ties broken by requiring V(resident | mutant) > V(mutant | mutant).

In every round, independently, each player's intended move is replaced by the opposite move with probability e; players observe each other's actual moves perfectly and cannot tell an accidental defection from a deliberate one. Assume 0 < e < 1/2.

(a) *(10 points)* GRIM cooperates in the first round and thereafter cooperates if and only if the opponent has cooperated in every previous round. Derive the condition on w under which a population of GRIM players resists invasion by ALL-D, the strategy that defects in every round.

(b) *(8 points)* Two GRIM players are paired with each other. Give the expected number of rounds up to and including the round in which the first accidental defection occurs. What does this imply about GRIM's payoff against itself as e grows, and about the long-run average payoff per round of two GRIM players facing each other?

(c) *(12 points)* Define the strategy PENANCE as follows. Every player carries a public standing label, maintained by the rules of the game, initially GOOD; after each round a player's standing becomes BAD if and only if that player defected while the opponent's standing was GOOD, and otherwise becomes GOOD. In each round, PENANCE defects if and only if its own standing is GOOD and the opponent's standing is BAD; otherwise it cooperates. Compare PENANCE with Tit-for-Tat (cooperate in the first round, then copy the opponent's move from the previous round) in the noisy world described above. (i) Explain why PENANCE does not fall into the echo of alternating defections that TFT falls into after a single accidental defection, and say what a tremble costs each strategy in self-play. (ii) Explain why it does not follow from (i) that PENANCE will simply displace TFT in a noisy population. Then derive the condition under which ALL-D can invade a population of PENANCE players — for this last calculation only, ignore trembles, and say that you are doing so.

**Student's answer.**

(a) If GRIM meets GRIM, nobody ever defects, so each player's expected payoff is R + wR + w²R + … = 3/(1−w). A lone ALL-D mutant in that population gets T in the first round and then P in every round thereafter, because GRIM never forgives and never forgets: 5 + w + w² + … = 5 + w/(1−w). So GRIM resists invasion when

3/(1−w) ≥ 5 + w/(1−w),

that is, 3 ≥ 5(1−w) + w = 5 − 4w, that is, w ≥ 1/2. The shadow of the future has to be at least half for a GRIM population to be safe from unconditional defectors.

(b) A cooperative spell between two GRIM players survives a given round only if nobody's hand trembles in that round. The probability that a given player's move is corrupted is e, so the probability that the pair gets through a round unharmed is (1−e), and the number of rounds up to and including the first accidental defection is therefore geometric with mean 1/e. Once one player has defected even by accident, GRIM never forgets: both players defect for the rest of the interaction and earn P from then on. GRIM's payoff against itself is thus roughly R/e rather than R/(1−w), so as soon as e is non-negligible GRIM does badly against itself no matter how long the shadow of the future is, and with probability 1 a pair of GRIM players ends up earning P per round in the long run.

(c) (i) TFT retaliates mechanically at the level of observed moves, and that is its undoing here. If A's hand trembles in round t, B sees a defection and defects in round t+1; A sees that defection and defects in round t+2; and the pair locks into (D, C), (C, D), (D, C), … forever, so each averages (T + S)/2 = 2.5 per round instead of R = 3. PENANCE keeps two things apart that TFT runs together, namely whether a defection was a punishment or a slip. When A defects by accident in round t, A's standing goes BAD, so in round t+1 A atones and cooperates, while B, still GOOD, is entitled to defect against a BAD opponent and does so without losing standing. After that round A is GOOD again and the pair is back to mutual cooperation in round t+2. The tremble costs them (R + R) − (T + S) = 1 unit of joint payoff once, and then cooperation resumes, instead of costing them half a unit in every round forever.

(ii) Even so, it does not follow that PENANCE takes over from TFT. Repairing your own mistakes is a claim about how a strategy does against itself, whereas invasion is a claim about how it does against the resident strategy and against other mutants; a strategy can be excellent in self-play and still fail to invade, or still be invaded itself. And PENANCE is in fact more exposed than TFT, because PENANCE is a forgiving strategy: it goes back to cooperating after any defection of its own. Against ALL-D this means that PENANCE alternates between cooperating while it atones and defecting while it punishes, so ALL-D receives T on half the rounds and P on the other half:

V(ALL-D | PENANCE) = (T + wP)/(1 − w²) = (5 + w)/(1 − w²),

whereas V(PENANCE | PENANCE) = 3/(1 − w). Setting 3/(1 − w) ≥ (5 + w)/(1 − w²) gives 3(1 + w) ≥ 5 + w, i.e. 2w ≥ 2, i.e. w ≥ 1. So for every w < 1, ALL-D invades a PENANCE population, while TFT needs only w ≥ 1/2. PENANCE recovers from trembles better, but it is much easier to exploit, and it can be stable only when the shadow of the future is essentially infinite.
