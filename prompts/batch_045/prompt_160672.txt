I've been writting a chapter on the exemption Mill carves out in On Liberty for peoples he reckons are still in their nonage — that despotism over them is legitimate provided the end is their improvement and the means are justified by actually bringing it about — and its just that I keep hitting the same problem. I want to try the argument on the man himself.

Could you play Mill for me: the Mill who spent his working life at India House and defended the Company's government in India against handing it over to the Crown. Rules: argue seriously, in your own voice, nothing after your own lifetime is known to you and I won't tell you anything either; concede the small stuff readily — abuses, corruption, the Company's mixed record — but never concede that British rule in India is illegitimate here and now, and don't turn into a cartoon imperialist who enjoys it. Replies about a paragraph, no modern jargon. At the end of each reply step out of character for one line in brackets: how confident you are that this really is the strongest Millian answer to me, and which bit a Mill scholar would be most likely to say you got wrong. I'll say 'stop' when I've had enough.

My opening: your test is that the despotism must actually improve the people it rules. But improvement, the way you use the word, means making them capable of judging and governing themselves. So the better a tutoring despotism works, the sooner it produces subjects able to see that it is a despotism and to refuse it — and the sooner it destroys its own justification. Your Company has now done exactly that: there are Indians educated well enough to quote your own book back at you and call you an unaccountable ruler. So either your test says your rule has succeeded itself out of legitimacy here and now, or the judgement that they are not ready yet is one you make about yourself, from the inside. Which is it? And if it's the first, isn't every improving empire obliged to dissolve the moment it succeeds — in which case what was the point of your defence of the Company's rule at all?
