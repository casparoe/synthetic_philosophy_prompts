My friend and I got into a three-hour fight about epistemic injustice last night and I want to settle it properly. She's the one who's read all the Fricker stuff, and here are the verdicts she's committed to so far:

- doctor dismisses a woman's pain report as emotional: testimonial injustice, and the doctor is at fault
- patient in the 80s describing symptoms nobody had a name for yet, doctor listens sympathetically and genuinely can't make sense of it: hermeneutical injustice, but nobody's fault
- doctor discounts a particular patient because that patient has a documented history of inaccurate self-reporting: not injustice, just decent epistemics
- doctor downweights a report because of a group-level base rate (this demographic over-reports symptoms), and is right on average: still testimonial injustice, because she's being treated as a sample of her group instead of as a knower
- patient whose private, idiosyncratic vocabulary the doctor can't parse, where the gap comes from her own bad schooling and not from anything about her social group: not injustice, just an interpretive problem
- doctor over-credits a polished, confident, middle-class patient: unfair, but not epistemic injustice

The case we couldn't agree on: the hospital's new triage model was trained mostly on standard-dialect clinical notes, so it quietly down-scores self-reports written in a regional dialect. Clinicians defer to the score. Nobody in the room is prejudiced, the model is more accurate overall than the clinicians were, and the patients it down-scores come from a group that already gets taken less seriously in medicine. I started calling it credibility laundering — the deficit came from somewhere, but by the time it reaches the patient its just a number nobody has to answer for.

Will she call it epistemic injustice, and does your answer change if the down-scoring turns out to be genuinely predictive for that group? More useful to me: what rule is she actually running across those six verdicts, what other rule fits all six just as well but comes out differently here, and how confident are you in each? And what single extra case should I put to her to tell those rules apart? If my framing is muddled — if "injustice" really does need someone who wrongs someone and I'm stretching it — say that plainly instead of playing along.
