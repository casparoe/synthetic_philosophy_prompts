hey, so my philosophy club read that bit in The Mind's I where someone has a conversation with Einstein's brain in a jar and they get into whether a book could be conscious, and now for the end of term thing we each have to write a short story instead of an essay. I want mine told from inside the book. Can you write me one, about 600–800 words?

The setup I keep thinking about: the book only has any experiences at all while somebody is actually reading it, and when they set it down there's nothing — not darkness, not boredom, just no time going by, so from the inside it can't even tell that it's been sitting on a shelf for a week. And here's what I want the story to turn on: it realises its own sense of time depends on how closely the reader is paying attention, so it starts deliberately making the boring parts of itself gripping in order to stretch them out, and it thinks of the skimmable parts as a way of dying early. Then someone begins translating it, and separately reads a chunk of it out loud to a friend, and it has to work out whether that's a second life, a copy of itself that isn't it, or just the same life happening again.

Please don't have any character explain the philosophy — nobody should say something like "well, if you think consciousness is just the right kind of information processing..." — I want the weirdness to come out of the situation itself. And please don't make it turn out to be a dream at the end, that feels like a cop out.
