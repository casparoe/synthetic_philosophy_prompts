I have recently become the person who decides how much of our capital goes into each of a stream of small, repeatable bets on behalf of a scholarship fund, and the more I read the more I suspect the textbook setup doesn't describe us. Three mismatches worry me. The fund must make fixed real payouts every year, so wealth is being drawn down along the path rather than simply compounded. It also receives top-up gifts from a donor who is, if anything, more generous in the years we have done badly, so our bankroll is replenished in a way that is anti-correlated with our own returns. And the opportunities themselves are awkward: lumpier than the theory assumes, with a minimum cheque size, an edge that decays if we wait for a better price, and the occasional payout in something we cannot sell for years.

I have roughly a year, and the equivalent of two junior researchers, to spend on getting our rule right rather than on picking bets. Which open questions should that year go to? I care most about the ones whose answer would actually change the fraction we commit, so for each please say how much you expect it to move that number, how tractable it is in a year with pencil-and-paper plus simulation, and which questions are upstream of which. Please also say which of my three worries — or of the standard ones, such as how much to haircut for uncertainty about our own edge, whether the time-average and expected-utility framings genuinely come apart in a case like ours, and what to do when the true model may not be in the set of models we are averaging over — is overrated, in the sense that settling it would leave our rule essentially unchanged. Finally, what result in the next six months, ours or someone else's, would move something up or down your list?
