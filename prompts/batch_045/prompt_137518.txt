I'm writing a methods handout for a first-year PhD seminar on historical explanation, and I want a decision tree that takes a student from the rough question — why did sustained, self-reinforcing economic growth and modern science take off in Britain rather than in China, the contrastive question at the centre of the Great Divergence debate — to one precisely specified research question. Every branch node should be a choice about how the contrast is fixed: what the relatum is and whether the contrast runs symmetrically (does the question change if you invert it and ask why not China rather than Britain?); what the unit of comparison is (England, Britain, northwest Europe, a particular Chinese region); which time-slice is used and whether the gap is one of levels or of growth rates; whether the contrast class is a population of comparable units or a single counterfactual pair; and whether the question asks for a difference-maker or for the causal history that produced the outcome. Give me eight to ten leaves, each a precisely worded question named for the diagnosis of the original that it embodies. Two leaves should differ only in whether they presuppose that take-off was to be expected, so that the Chinese side is an absence standing in need of explanation, or treat it as a surprise requiring explanation on both sides; and I want at least one leaf for the view that the original contrast is ill posed and should be recast as a question about the timing of a single transition that, once underway, closed off the alternative. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, and note any coherent combination of answers that no historian actually occupies, with a one-sentence diagnosis of whether it is empty because incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Finally, say briefly which leaf you think best captures what historians are really arguing about when they use the question.
