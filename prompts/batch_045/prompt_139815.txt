hey, I've been messing around with the old sea battle problem in my spare time and I wrote up a short piece that tries to do something a bit different from the usual "is it true now or not" exchange. Draft first, asks after. Comments would be great.

**Hardening**

§1. Take B: there is a sea battle at noon tomorrow. The view I'm interested in — call it the gap view — says that right now B is neither true nor false, and that if the battle happens B *becomes* true at noon. Call that transition hardening. I want to know what hardening is supposed to be, because I think the natural answers either collapse the view into something else or generate a regress.

§2. First answer: B hardens when its truthmaker shows up. The battle occurs, and that's what makes B true. Problem: the battle's occurring at noon is a fact that obtains at noon, and whether it obtains doesn't depend on when you ask. So if truth-at-a-time just tracks the truthmaker, then B's status never changes: it was already the case, at every earlier time, that the battle occurs at noon. That isn't hardening, that's determinism about truth with extra steps. So the gap theorist can't lean on truthmakers; whatever makes t1 different from t2 has to be something about t1's relation to t2, not about the battle.

§3. So: what makes the future open at one time and closed at another? The only story I can see is the branching one — at t1 several histories run through t1 and then diverge, and B is true on some and false on others. But that's basically the Ockhamist picture, and it already gives you a perfectly good truth predicate: true at a time on a history. Once you've got that machinery the gap theorist's motivation starts to look thin, because the thing she wanted to avoid (a fact of the matter about which way it goes) is either back — as the thin red line marking which history is actual — or it was never really the issue, since the branching structure was doing the work all along. Call this the convergence argument. This is the section I'm least sure about.

§4. Second problem, and the one I care about more. Take H: B hardens at noon tomorrow. When does *H* harden? Three options. (a) At noon, along with B. Then at every earlier time it's unsettled whether B hardens at noon — which requires an unsettled fact about the structure of settling itself, and you get the same question again one level up, forever. (b) Earlier, say at midnight. But then it was settled at midnight that B closes at noon, and I don't see how that's compatible with its still being unsettled at midnight which way B goes: a timetable that says "the question closes at noon" already excludes the histories on which the question closed at 6am, and those are exactly the histories where something about the battle got settled early. (c) Never — H has no hardening time, it's just always settled or always not. That's a dodge unless you say which.

§5. The way out I want to try: accept (c) in a specific form. Call it Scheduled Settling. There is a determinate timetable saying when each future contingent proposition hardens, and the timetable is settled from the start; but the timetable says nothing about which way anything hardens. What grounds it: causal structure — a proposition hardens when the causal preconditions of its truthmaker are complete, and under indeterminism that's compatible with either outcome. So it can be settled now that B closes at noon without its being settled now whether B closes *true*. Settledness of the schedule, openness of the outcome.

§6. Two things I'm worried about. First, this looks like a thin red line of a new kind: a thin *schedule*. If I'm allowed one piece of privileged structure, the Ockhamist will ask why she isn't allowed hers. Second, early-hardening cases: if the admiral signs an irrevocable order tonight, isn't B settled tonight even though its truthmaker shows up tomorrow? My causal story says yes, the schedule should read "closes at 9pm" — but then schedule and truthmaker come apart, and I'm not sure I can say what the schedule is about anymore.
