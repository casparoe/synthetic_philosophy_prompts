I'm taking a "Life in the Universe" seminar this term and I keep hitting the same wall. Everyone in section agrees on the setup — a galaxy old enough that a technological species could have crossed it many times over, tons of stars, and yet no signal, nothing in the all-sky infrared data that looks like waste heat from a species running on a big fraction of a star's output, no unambiguous artifact parked in the solar system. But the moment someone says this is evidence that the great filter is ahead of us rather than behind us, someone else shuts it down with "absence of evidence is not evidence of absence" and the room moves on. I want to know what that sentence could actually be claiming, because I think it's doing three or four different jobs at once and they dont seem to me to be equally strong.

The readings I think I'm conflating: (a) the pure point about search power — if the search wouldn't have found them even if they were there, we've learned nothing (I've started calling it the candle-in-a-cave case: ten thousand candles in a galaxy-sized cave, each lit for one night per million years, and you're almost never looking at the right one at the right moment); (b) a claim about who bears the burden of proof, i.e. which way the default should sit before anyone updates; (c) the empirical-ish claim that our actual searches are so incomplete that the likelihood ratio is basically 1; (d) the much stronger claim that silence is never evidence of anything, which sounds false to me for any detector with nonzero sensitivity. Our searches are incomplete, sure — a beacon deliberately aimed at us for a few decades is a completely different object from our own 1970s TV leakage, which I'd guess fades out within a few hundred light years — but they arnt zero either.

Can you lay the readings out as a menu rather than picking one, tell me which are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, and which are straight up false, and then say which of them people actually mean when they say it out loud? The part I really care about: my TA used it to conclude that the silence gives us no update at all toward the filter being ahead of us. Which readings would support that, and does the strongest one he could get away with actually deliver it? I want something to bring to section instead of everyone quoting the slogan at each other.
