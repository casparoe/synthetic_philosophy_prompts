I have been asked to circulate a two-page position statement ahead of a graduate reading group on the intentional-stance programme and the notion of a real pattern, and before I send it round I would like an audit of the commitments I am proposing to defend. I do not know where the weak points are, and I would rather find them now than in the session. Here is the profile, roughly in the order in which I would argue for it.

1. The three stances — physical, design, intentional — together with mixtures of them exhaust the systematic strategies available for predicting a system's behaviour. There is no fourth basic stance.

2. By the physical stance I mean something specific and demanding: you take the system's actual micro-state, you evolve it forward under the laws, and you read off the outcome. Prediction by coarse-grained extremal principles — maximise entropy, find the equilibrium, find the fixed point of a flow — is not that, whatever else it is.

3. And those extremal methods are not mere conveniences. For some systems — a gas, a traffic network, the dispersion of prices across a market — they deliver the best predictions we have: better than micro-simulation, better than any functional story, better than any belief-desire story. They would go on being the best thing available even with arbitrarily much computing power, because their point is to discard information rather than to gather it.

4. A pattern is real just in case there is some description under which the data compress, that is, a description shorter or more tractable than an enumeration of the data points. Real patterns are not projections of our interests, and they are not ontologically second-class relative to whatever fundamental physics describes.

5. But compressibility is relative to a scheme. Whether one description beats another depends on the coding language, the grain of description and what is counted as a repetition. No scheme is metaphysically privileged, and there is no scheme-independent measure of how far a finite body of data compresses.

6. Where the intentional stance beats its rivals, the system really does have the beliefs and desires attributed to it. Applied to a large language model: if attributing beliefs and desires is the most compressed and most reliable way to predict what it will say next, then it has beliefs and desires, and it is not merely as if it did.

7. Attributions of expectations to aggregates are a different matter. The market expects a rate cut; the university has decided. These are useful ways of talking, and they are not true in the way an individual's expectation is true. There is no real intentional pattern there.

8. No stance is metaphysically privileged. The physical stance is not what is really going on with the others as mere shortcuts.

9. At the same time, physics is causally complete: every event that happens is determined, at the level of physical description, by prior physical conditions and the laws, and nothing extra is ever needed to make anything happen.

What I want from you is an audit rather than a lecture. Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look as though they are. For each genuine tension, rank it by severity, state precisely which claim has to be modified or abandoned, and say what that modification would cost me elsewhere in the profile — in particular, whether a given fix would stop me from saying what I want to say about large language models in (6) or about aggregates in (7). Where a tension is only apparent, explain why it dissolves. If any item is ambiguous in a way that determines whether a tension is real, say which reading you are assuming. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and flag anything that is not in tension with anything but is simply undefended as I have stated it — an argument I have gestured at rather than made.

I have no settled view about which of these I would give up, so please tell me which is cheapest to lose rather than which is most fashionable to defend. Argue from the commitments themselves: I know the surrounding literature and will add the references myself, so I would rather have a blunt answer about which item has to go than a survey of the positions people have taken.
