My reading group has been going round in circles about illusionism about phenomenal consciousness for two years now — four of us: two philosophers, a psycholinguist, and me (I do ML, so I'm mostly there to be confused) — and I want a diagnosis, not a verdict, because I'm not even sure what kind of argument we're having.

The setup, roughly: the illusionists (Frankish, and Dennett in his later work) say there are no phenomenal properties in the strong realist sense; what's really there is just the ordinary, scientifically respectable functional and neural stuff, and our powerful sense that experience has ineffable, intrinsic, directly-grasped qualities is itself the thing that needs explaining. The realists say that's not a theory of consciousness but a theory of our talk about it: there's still something it's like to see red, Mary still learns something when she leaves the black-and-white room, the explanatory gap stays open no matter how much functional detail you pile on, and conceiving of the zombies still seems to do work. And the realists have one move I've never seen an illusionist answer head on: if it introspectively seems to you that you have qualia, that very seeming is itself something it's like, so you've only relocated the datum. The illusionist reply is basically that the realist is just producing more reports, and reports are exactly what's in dispute.

Nobody in the group disputes any third-person fact — the neuroscience, the discriminations, the lesion cases, the fact that people say they're conscious. Everyone agrees the word is useful. And nobody has budged an inch in two years.

My hypothesis, which I've started calling the detector-or-generator question, is this: everything turns on whether introspection is a faculty that detects a feature of our states — in which case its deliverances are data, and any theory that contradicts them is refuted by the appearances — or a subsystem that produces representations in its own proprietary vocabulary, representations that can misdescribe their objects the way vision misdescribes the lines in a Müller-Lyer figure. If it's a detector, the realist basically wins by stipulation; if it's a generator, the illusionist does. And then the whole literature is two groups answering different questions under one heading.

Is that anywhere near right? Or is the real structure something else — a verbal dispute about the word 'consciousness', a methodological one about what an explanation has to deliver, a clash of starting intuitions neither side can argue for, or a hidden empirical question (something like whether introspective conviction can be dissociated from first-order discrimination) that would actually settle it? Someone mentioned that Chalmers has floated the 'meta-problem' — explaining why we think there's a hard problem — as neutral ground both camps can work on; would solving that count as progress here, or does each side just swallow whatever result comes out? I dont want a survey of who holds which view. I want to know what kind of knot this is, what (if anything) would untie it, and — if the honest answer is that nothing would — why not.
