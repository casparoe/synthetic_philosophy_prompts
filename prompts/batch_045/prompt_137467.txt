Draft the joint statement that two theorists of meaning could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on where a message's meaning is located. Take as shared vocabulary the threefold distinction Hofstadter draws in *Gödel, Escher, Bach*: the frame message, whatever makes a receiver treat an object as a message and set about decoding it; the outer message, the physical signal as it awaits decoding; and the inner message, the content a successful decoding yields. Call the first party frame-first: she holds that nothing is determinate about a message's content beyond what its frame and outer message, together with a receiver's actual decoding practice, fix. Call the second content-first: he holds that the inner message is a genuine and possibly unrecovered content, namely whatever any correct decoding must preserve, and that it is the same content across every frame that succeeds in triggering decoding.

Both parties accept the following as the common case. An interstellar probe carries a gold-alloy disc bearing a single spiral groove. Its casing, metallurgy, and trajectory are unmistakably artificial, so every receiver recovers the same frame message; but the groove admits two mutually inconsistent decodings, each internally coherent and each equally well supported by the groove's physical structure. On one decoding the disc reports the elemental composition of the senders' star; on the other it gives instructions for assembling a particular molecule.

Produce: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims, including what each side concedes about the frame message's role in triggering decoding, about whether meaning must be recoverable in principle, and — if any part of the dispute turns out to be merely verbal — which part, and why; (2) a table of the residual disagreements, each stated twice, once in the wording its own side would accept; (3) the cruxes, among them whether underdetermination of decoding leaves a fact of the matter about the inner message, and whether invariance under translation is the right test of what the inner message is; (4) for each crux, a further case or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction about its outcome recorded in advance. Consider in particular one case both may treat as decisive: a physically identical disc encountered by a community whose frame differs, who also recognize it as a message but as a musical performance to be heard rather than a statement to be believed — does that community recover the same inner message, a different one, or none? Do not adjudicate between the parties; the deliverable is a text both could put their names to.
