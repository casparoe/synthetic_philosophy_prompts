I work in proof engineering — Lean 4 mostly, some Coq, and enough categorical logic to be careless with it. My modal logic is thin: I know Kripke semantics at the level of the box quantifying over reachable worlds, and I know S5 as what you get when reachability is an equivalence relation. My metaphysics is one undergraduate course, taken a long time ago and mostly spent on other things. What I do have is a working feel for what a proof assistant will and will not let you get away with, and I want to use that as a probe.

What I want from you is a translation, not an explanation. Take the ontological argument in its modal form — the Anselmian version that runs from the definition of God as that than which no greater can be conceived, through existence in the understanding, to existence in reality — and, at greater length, the standard reconstruction of Gödel's version: a primitive notion of positive property, Godlikeness as possession of every positive property, necessary existence counted among the positive properties, and S5 carrying the final step from possible necessary existence to existence. I do not care about textual fidelity to Gödel's manuscript or to Anselm's; translate the argument as it is actually discussed, not as it was written down.

Restate both, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually use: types and their inhabitants; contexts and judgments; definitions versus axioms; impredicativity and proof irrelevance of the sort of propositions; definitional versus propositional equality; indexed families and dependent functions; preorders and algebras of predicates; classical principles declared as postulates rather than assumed silently; normalization and canonicity; and the distinction between a theory and its models. Where the translation needs a piece of machinery I do not have, say so explicitly instead of paraphrasing around the gap.

Concretely, I want four things mapped, and I want the mapping to be honest about where it strains.

First, the notion of positivity. As I read the axioms, positivity behaves like an ultrafilter on the Boolean algebra of properties: closed under conjunction, closed under necessary entailment, and exactly one of a property and its complement positive. Say whether that reading is right, translate it into a structure over predicates on a type of individuals, and tell me which classical principle its existence requires and where in the resulting term that requirement becomes visible. In particular: does the argument survive in a type theory without excluded middle, and if not, does that fact show up as a philosophical commitment or merely as an engineering one?

Second, necessary existence. In a single-world, fixed-domain setting, to say that a type is necessarily inhabited seems to collapse into saying that it is inhabited. I take it the standard way out is to reify a type of worlds and index the individual domain and every predicate over it, so that necessity is a dependent function over worlds. Do that, and then tell me what the reification costs me: which distinctions the encoding buys, and which it forecloses.

Third, S5. I have a suspicion I want tested. If necessity is encoded as quantification over a world index, S5 appears to come for free — I get the box's characteristic principles from the shape of the dependent function rather than from any assumption about what metaphysical necessity is like, and weaker modal logics are then expressible only by adding an accessibility relation back in as extra structure. If that is right, say so, and say whether it means the choice of S5 is doing more work than the choice of the other axioms. If you think it is also expressible as a comonad on the type of propositions plus an extra law relating the comonad to its dual, tell me what that extra law has to be and which of S5's axioms it corresponds to.

Fourth, the objection that the axioms are too strong — the one where the theory proves that every true proposition is necessary, rather than proving anything about God. I want you to distinguish two things I will name myself: derivational collapse, where the implication from a proposition to its own necessity is a theorem of the theory, and semantic collapse, where every model verifying the axioms has all worlds mutually accessible. Tell me whether these come apart, and then the part I actually care about: does a proof assistant register either as a defect? My impression is that it registers inconsistency and nothing subtler — that a theory which is consistent but whose axioms are jointly unreasonable raises no error at all in my framework, because my framework has a type for false and no type for unmotivated. Confirm, correct, or complicate that.

Then give me the residue report, graded in three: what translated cleanly, what translated only with distortion (naming, for each, the assumption that got smuggled in at the point of distortion), and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all.

Two standing constraints. I have no library and do not want one: no citations, no titles, no dates, no attributed positions, nothing in quotation marks; if a step depends on something someone argued, give me the step. And do not tell me which premise you would reject — tell me what my vocabulary can and cannot say about rejecting it, and flag every place where you were tempted to settle a philosophical question by a choice of encoding, whether that is world-indexing, a classical postulate, proof irrelevance, or a universe level. I want those surfaced as choices.

Close with a verdict in both directions at once: which items in the residue you would chalk up to a limitation of dependent type theory as a language for metaphysics, and which you think are signs that the original argument was less clear than it looks when stated in its own terms. If there is one item that would most change a philosopher's mind if they saw it stated this way, name it and say why.
