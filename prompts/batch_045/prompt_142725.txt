You are a philosopher of physics specializing in the metaphysics of time, and you are writing a one-page decision-tree handout for a graduate reading group on the Rietdijk–Putnam argument. Work carefully: before drawing anything, map the logical space, and build the tree out of the distinctions that space allows rather than out of a checklist of -isms.

The shared starting commitment your tree begins from is: I accept special relativity as empirically adequate, and I accept that simultaneity between spacelike-separated events is frame-relative. Every internal node must be phrased as a concrete case or claim a student can answer without knowing which position it leads to — no node may ask whether the respondent is a presentist, an eternalist, and so on. Label each branch with the claim that splits it, and present the tree as an indented list or in ASCII.

Requirements on the nodes. At minimum you must use: (1) the walking-pace Andromeda case — for someone strolling down the street, is there a fact of the matter about what is happening now in a galaxy two million light-years away, and if there is only a relative fact, what is it relative to: an observer's inertial frame, a single spacetime point, or a world-line? (2) An explicit non-transitivity case — three events such that the first is co-real with the second, the second is co-real with the third, and the first is not co-real with the third — used to force a choice about whether "is determinate for" can be the same relation as "exists". (3) A case about whether the sum total of what is real can change. (4) A deliberation case: you are deciding whether to accept a job and you are told that a complete description of the block exists in which your answer is already inscribed; do you still weigh reasons, and why does your answer matter for the ontology?

Leaves: at most nine, each with the standard name of the position (or "unnamed" if nobody holds it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. These must all appear: the block universe; the growing block; presentism; presentism with a dynamically undetectable privileged foliation; the moving spotlight; becoming relativized to a point; and the deflationary view that the disagreement is only about the word "exists".

Two further things I want. First, place my own candidate cell rather than joking it away: the "sliding window," on which reality's future edge grows and its past edge erodes, so that events stop being real after a fixed span. Give me at least two unoccupied cells in total and say, in one sentence each, whether each is empty because the combination is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Second, flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot actually separate, and name the further question that would separate them.
