I'm putting together the closing item for a seminar session on Otto Neurath — the session covers both his picture language and his holism — and I'd like a villanelle that does some real work rather than just name-dropping.

Background for the prompt, stated so the task doesn't depend on recall: Isotype, first called the Vienna Method of Pictorial Statistics, was developed at the Social and Economic Museum in Vienna in the late 1920s and early 1930s, chiefly by Neurath with the artist Gerd Arntz and the transformer Marie Reidemeister. Its first rule is that a greater quantity is shown by more identical same-sized signs, never by a bigger one, because a repeated sign has a fixed value you can count, while a scaled-up sign leaves it unclear whether you are comparing height or area; perspective and shading were avoided for the same reason. Neurath never claimed it was a full language — he called it a helping language, a language-like technique, always accompanied by words. Alongside this sits his boat image: we are sailors who must rebuild our ship plank by plank on the open sea, never able to put it into dry dock and reconstruct it there out of the best materials.

The poem: a 19-line villanelle about that ship, in which the two refrains are built the way an Isotype sign is built — short, concrete, countable, identical every time they return, with no intensifiers, no comparisons of size, no stronger word substituted on the third or sixth appearance. Any increase the poem needs to express should be expressed by adding another identical unit, not by scaling one up. Let the refrains shift in meaning only because the stanza around them has shifted, the way a fixed sign takes its sense from the chart it sits in. Then let the poem arrive at the place where picture languages stop: it has to say not, or perhaps, or because — the things a chart of repeated figures cannot show on its own — and let the form register that reaching-for-words as a cost rather than a relief. Someone who knows Neurath should be able to say which refrain is the sign and which is the ship, and why the poem cannot end in dry dock.
