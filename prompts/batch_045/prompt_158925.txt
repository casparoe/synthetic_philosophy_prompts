Bit about me so you know what you're up against. I'm retired, spent most of a career on dose-response work in radiological protection, and I've been getting into philosophy the last couple of years and definately making a mess of it. Which is where my opening comes from. In my old field we had a habit everybody half-believed: take a dose-response curve measured over maybe two decades of dose, assume its linear all the way down with no safe floor, then multiply a genuinely trivial dose by an enormous population and read off a predicted number of cancers. Fine as bookkeeping, and we all quietly knew the bottom-end number was fiction — a model calibrated where you can actually measure doesn't license arithmetic ten decades below that calibration, and multiplying by headcount doesn't manufacture the missing signal. Your argument looks to me like exactly that shape: a disutility scale you can only read where subjects can report, extrapolated down to one dust speck, times 3^^^3.

So here's the positive version, and I want you to kill it if you can. Call it the least-count view. Every instrument has a grain and a noise floor; a mind's unpleasantness scale has one too, and a dust speck sits under it. Not worth zero — just unresolvable against the background of ordinary affective noise, and what can't be resolved can't be summed, not even across very many different subjects. And note I'm not positing a cliff: I'm not saying there's a bright line where aggregation stops, a noise floor is fuzzy and real at the same time. Your move.
