My supervisor has spent something like fifteen years arguing that we don't need a substantive property of truth, and she has built up a pretty consistent track record of saying which bits of truth-talk her version of the view can absorb and which bits force her to admit something more. I'm writing the eight-page intro to a volume of her papers (due in March, she keeps asking whether it's done, it isn't) and I want to state the through-line correctly rather than flatteringly, so I've been trying to work out what rule she is actually running. Her verdicts so far, as she has stated them in various places: plain disquotation for a small finite language, nothing substantive needed; blind generalization of the sort where you say everything in the inspector's report is true, or every theorem of some formal system is true, still nothing substantive needed, because on her telling the predicate is there to do expressive work and not to name a property; truth at a world in modal semantics and truth in a model in model-theoretic semantics, also nothing substantive, those are devices defined recursively over a structure and not descriptions of a property; the claim that truth is the norm of assertion and the aim of belief, nothing substantive; the move where someone says our best scientific theories are successful because they are true, still nothing substantive, since on her view you can strip the word out and the explanation loses nothing. Where she does say something substantive is needed: truthmaking, as in it is true that the meeting was cancelled because the meeting was cancelled, where she thinks truth is on the receiving end of a real explanation that the equivalences don't capture; and the Liar and its strengthened variants, where she thinks you cannot have a consistent and complete unrestricted schema, so you need a hierarchy or truth-value gaps or a weaker logic, and whichever you pick you are saying something about the structure of truth that minimalism doesn't say.

The case I can't figure out is vagueness, and specifically the supervaluationist-looking package: take a language with vague predicates, consider all the admissible ways of sharpening the vague vocabulary into precise vocabulary, call a sentence true when it comes out true on every admissible sharpening, and let the truth predicate itself behave perfectly classically, so that a borderline sentence like 'Nils is tall' is neither true nor false while 'it is true that Nils is tall' is straightforwardly false. Assume quantifiers and self-reference are handled some other way, say by a hierarchy, so paradox is off the table and the only issue is the vagueness. Would she say this forces her to admit a substantive property of truth, or is it another device? A friend of mine has been pushing something he calls sharpening-relative minimalism, where you keep the equivalence schema but index it to a sharpening and then define the real truth predicate as truth on all sharpenings, and he claims this shows the whole thing is deflationarily kosher; I can't tell whether that is a genuine extension of her view or just relabeling what she already rejects. So: predict her verdict, and say which rule she is running. The two I keep coming back to are that she needs substance exactly where the equivalence schema cannot be had in full, unrestricted and transparent, versus that she needs substance only where truth does metaphysical or explanatory work or where the flat list is outright inconsistent, with any relativized or parameterized schema counting as still deflationary the way models and worlds do. Give me the other rules that fit her seven verdicts just as well, tell me which of her earlier verdicts is load-bearing for the prediction you make, and name the further case that would actually tell these rules apart. My own guess is that the test is a variant where the truth predicate itself is vague, so the equivalences never fail but bivalence does, but I am not sure that separates them in the right direction, and it might be that the real crux is which relativizations of the schema she is willing to count as deflationary at all. I don't need any citations, I know the literature badly enough already; I just want the shape of the argument.
