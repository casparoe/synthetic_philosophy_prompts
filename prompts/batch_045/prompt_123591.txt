I keep noticing the same move in arguments and I don't have a word for it. Hoping there's an established one, or that someone can coin a good one.

I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background, and I got dragged into a dinner argument about Malthus. The claim was: population compounds, food production only adds, so famine. Someone pointed out that the famine didn't happen, and in fact the opposite happened. The reply was that the mechanism was right but the timeline was wrong — we were "right but early" — because of stuff nobody could have foreseen in 1800: fertilizer, mechanization, the green revolution. Then the date gets moved out.

Here's what I can't sort out. I've seen three versions of this and they feel different but I can't say why from the outside.

Version one, which seems fine: at work we said the service falls over at 10x traffic. It didn't. But we could name the specific thing that changed — we'd put a cache in front of it since — and we could redo the arithmetic with that variable included and get a new number that we then actually tested. Nothing sketchy there.

Version two, which seems bad: a prediction fails, and the response is just a new date. No new variable named, no re-derived number, nothing added to the theory except "later." And when that date passes, another one. What gets me is that this doesn't feel unfalsifiable in the plain sense. These people keep making testable dated predictions and they keep being wrong, and being wrong costs them nothing. Each individual miss gets explained by something reasonable on its own — unforeseen tech, a policy change, a war — and nobody ever seems to add up the fact that it's happened four times in a row. It's not that the claim can't be tested. It's that it can be tested forever without ever losing credibility.

Version three, which I think is a genuinely different thing: the prediction fails because the prediction was heard. Everyone gets scared, goes and builds the thing that prevents the outcome, and so the outcome never arrives. From outside that looks exactly like being wrong, but it feels like the predictor deserves credit rather than blame. Did Malthus get that? No idea.

A friend with a philosophy minor waved at "unfalsifiable" and something about "immunizing" moves, but she couldn't remember details and neither seems right — an immunizing move is supposed to protect you from ever being tested, and these people definitely get tested, they just survive it.

So: is there a name for version two specifically, the serial date-shift with no new content? If there are established concepts near it, tell me which ones, how my thing differs from each, and which fits best. If there's nothing, coin a term and define it with its boundary cases. I don't want a label — I want to know what to look for, from the outside, to tell version two from versions one and three, since the people doing all three say basically the same sentence.

Also: whatever you settle on, say what would change your mind about it, and where you think your own distinction is weakest. And I'd rather have a confident answer with the weak point flagged than a list of nine things it might be.
