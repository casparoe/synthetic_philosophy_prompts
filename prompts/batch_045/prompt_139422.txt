here's what i want it to get right, since the whole point is that it should actually be true: a chaotic system is deterministic and can be extremely simple — the randomness is in us not knowing the starting conditions exactly, not in the system. and the error doesn't fade in gradually, it roughly doubles over some fixed stretch of time, which means each extra decimal place of precision buys you a fixed extra slice of forecast time, not a proportional one: to double how far ahead you can see you'd have to square how precisely you started. so perfect prediction isn't just hard, it's impossible without infinitely precise starting data. i also think people get the free will thing backwards — some kids in my class said chaos means the universe isn't clockwork after all so there's room for us in it, but if what you do turns on a detail you can't even perceive, you're less in charge, not more. i want the poem on my side on that, whatever that turns out to mean for a poem.
