My dad has vascular dementia, and a few months ago we sat in a neurologist's office looking at his MRI while my brother, trying to be helpful, said afterwards that there is just less of him in there now. I've been chewing on that ever since. Obviously I want to say that brain volume has nothing to do with how much my dad matters — but I've come to realize I don't have a clean way of saying why, and I'd rather know what would actually settle it than keep reassuring myself.

The position I've landed on is this: brain size — total volume, neuron count, whatever measure you like — is never morally relevant in itself. It's only ever evidence about the underlying capacities that do the real moral work. And when I try to say which capacities, the thing I keep coming back to is something I've started calling the reach of a standpoint: how far into the past and future a creature's concerns extend, and how much of its own life it can hold in view at once. So on my view the atrophy is only bad because of the reach it took with it, and a scan is just a very indirect way of guessing at that.

What I want is what would count against this, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I don't need references or reading lists — I want the arguments.

Some things I've been turning over: cases where size and reach come apart, which is where I'd expect the view to be tested — a creature with an enormous brain and a very short reach, or a tiny system with a long one, or a brain emulation you could in principle compress, or slow down by a factor of a thousand, without changing what it does at all. The worry that my view quietly collapses into the one I was rejecting: if reach tracks neuron count closely enough across every case we actually encounter, then I am just a size person with extra steps. The pressure from continuity, where I have to draw a line somewhere between a worm and a person and I'm not sure my line isn't motivated. And the possibility that what matters is how much welfare there is rather than who has it, in which case a longer reach might mean more of the thing that matters and not just evidence of it.

Be honest about two things especially. First, which of these a defender of my position would concede as really counting against it, as opposed to what critics say counts against it. Second, whether the it's only a proxy move has made the view unfalsifiable, and if it has, whether that's actually a problem for a view of this kind. And I'll tell you what I'd find most persuasive, because it isn't any of the above: it's the possibility that at his next scan, with more atrophy showing, I'll want to say he matters less for a reason that has nothing to do with what he can still do, and I wont be able to tell whether that's a defeater or just grief.
