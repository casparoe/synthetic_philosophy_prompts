Hey — I run a fortnightly reading group on AI safety, about 14 people, mostly CS grad students plus some phil folks, 75 minutes, one big table, post-its and index cards available, no projector. I want an activity where they *feel* why "just avoid side effects" won't cash out unless you've already smuggled in a story about what's valuable.

Rough idea, which you can throw out: pairs. One is the system, one is the world-builder. The world-builder lays out a room of ~8 items (vase on a shelf, laptop charging, plant, sleeping cat, window latch, kettle, open notebook, box of matches) and secretly marks two as the things that actually matter. The system is told to do one small task — say, get the laptop onto the desk — and is scored on doing it *minus* a "disturbance" penalty. The catch: the group has to write the disturbance formula on the board before anyone sees the hidden marks, so they commit to what counts as a change, what "unchanged" is measured against, and over what time window, without knowing what matters. Three rounds, reveal the marks after each, let them rewrite the formula.

The bit I care most about: in round 3 the world-builder has to include something that was going to happen anyway if the system sat still (the plant wilts, a colleague opens the window at noon), so people hit the baseline problem in their gut instead of being told about it.

Give me rules, materials, the scoring, a round-by-round timeline for 75 minutes, and a debrief that maps specific moments onto (a) the choice of baseline, (b) the choice of state representation / distance, (c) the time horizon. Say what'll predictably go wrong first time (I'm guessing someone says "just count every object" and someone tries to negotiate with their partner) and how to handle it. Be honest about which part of the argument the game can't stage and you'd just have to tell them.

Don't hedge between designs: commit to one baseline rule and one distance metric, say why, and tell me plainly which part of the "no side effects without a value spec" argument you think the game actually carries and which part is just your own view.
