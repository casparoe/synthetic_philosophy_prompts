My opening. I've spent six years on a novel. Two thirds in I get what looks like a strictly better idea: new book is better, I write faster now, the old one will probably flop. Forward-looking numbers say start over, so I start over. Eighteen months later it happens again. Do that a few times and I die with a drawer full of brilliant openings. Now kill the audience — put me alone on an island, no readers, nothing to signal, no one to impress — and the same thing still happens. So this isn't information and it isn't signaling; it's about what a bounded agent can actually implement. Here's my argument: your own theory, done properly, tells me to commit — backwards induction on the switching problem says stick with the project unless the case against it is strong. But commitment needs a mechanism, and for someone with no external commitment technology, the only mechanism on offer is a disposition to weight what's already been spent. So honoring sunk costs isn't a fallacy at all; it's the psychological form the correct forward-looking policy has to take in a creature like me. I call it the implementation argument. Which bucket does that go in, and what's your reply?
