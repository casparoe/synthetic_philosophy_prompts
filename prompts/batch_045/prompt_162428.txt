ok, im stuck on this fight about whether qualia are even a coherent thing to posit, and I keep going in circles. The version I get is: qualia are the what-its-like of an experience, over and above what the experience does, and the inverted spectrum case is supposed to show theyre real because the functional role stays the same while the qualia flip. The other side says the whole posit should just be quined.

heres what I actually dont get. What kind of difference is even on the table in that case? Call it a loud inversion if everything else shifts along with it — your discriminations, your memory associations, what youd say about the tomato. Call it a silent inversion if nothing else moves at all: same judgements, same discriminations, same reports, same sense of noticing that something changed, just the qualia flipped.

Is someone who believes in qualia committed to silent inversions being genuinely possible? Because if only loud ones are available, then the qualia just look like the functional/discriminative stuff again and theres nothing left to argue about. But if they are committed to silent ones, then the posit looks like something no evidence — not even my own introspection — could ever bear on, and I think that IS the incoherence complaint rather than just a we-cant-know complaint.

Can you explain where the disagreement actually lives: what each side has to say about the loud vs silent distinction, and whether the anti-qualia side is basically running a dilemma that squeezes you onto one horn or the other? Ive seen the pop versions of this debate, I want the one where that distinction does real work.
