I am putting together a handout for a graduate reading group on the wrong kind of reasons problem, and I want the centrepiece to be a decision tree that sorts readers by their reactions to cases rather than by which label they like. Assume the reader knows only the basic set-up — that some considerations (a demon's threat, a wager, the moral costs of holding a bigoted belief that the evidence marginally supports) seem to favour *having* an attitude without bearing on whether that attitude is fitting or correct — and nothing about how theorists have carved up the space.

Please build it as follows:

- Derive the tree from the logical space first: give the yes/no questions that generate it, note which answer-combinations are actually occupied by a defended position, and for each empty combination say in one line whether it is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
- Six to nine leaves. Among them: evidentialism about reasons for belief; pragmatic encroachment, whether about knowledge or about what one ought to believe; moral encroachment; an object-given/state-given (value-based, buck-passing) treatment of reasons for desire; a monistic view on which all reasons for belief are practical reasons; and an involuntarist view on which there are no reasons to believe at all, because belief is not up to us. Name a leaf 'unnamed' if you think nobody holds it.
- Every node must be a concrete case, not a slogan, and must be answerable without knowing where it leads. Include the standard demon variant plus at least three of: a threat directed at someone else (does the reason 'inherit' to you?); a threat you cannot comply with directly (a reason to take a belief-inducing drug rather than a reason to believe); a case where the consideration seems to make the belief merely permissible rather than required; and a case involving an attitude other than belief or desire, such as admiration or hope. No node may simply ask whether the right/wrong kind distinction is real.
- Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate, and say what further question would separate them, if any does.
- The handout's angle: I want the tree to separate those for whom the distinction marks two different normative relations, those for whom it marks a difference in what the reason is a reason *for*, and those for whom it is an artefact to be dissolved. If your tree cannot cut the space along those lines, say so and tell me what it cuts along instead.
- Close with a scoring key: for each leaf, the answer pattern that lands there and one sentence on that leaf's most awkward commitment.
