i keep bouncing off papers on infinite ethics because each one seems to fail a different test, so before I read any more I want the spec instead. Imagine I'm designing a decision procedure for an agent who isn't sure whether the world contains infinitely many morally relevant locations (persons, person-moments, whatever the unit turns out to be) and who can directly affect only a small finite patch of it. What would that procedure have to get right to count as adequate? Give me the requirements, each with the case behind it: the addition-paradox style cases where reshuffling changes everything or nothing, worlds where net value's limit flips depending on the order you sum in, comparisons between worlds with the same infinite cardinalities of good and bad lives, and verdicts about acts rather than worlds given partial control. I also want the distinctions it can't collapse, like between "no verdict here" and "these are equally good." And three requirements I made up, which you should keep only if they're real: invariance to how coarsely spacetime is carved into locations; still saying something when the agent is one of infinitely many structurally identical agents running the same rule, so it can't just retreat to "you only affect finitely much"; and graceful degradation when the agent is only ~70% sure the world is infinite. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's taste, flag any subset you think is jointly unsatisfiable (that's the part I care most about), and say which single requirement eliminates the most candidate views. Skip the scorecard and skip names and dates — I can look those up.
