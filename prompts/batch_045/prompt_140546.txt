ok so this has suddenly stopped being hypothetical for me. I'm on the board of a 340-unit housing co-op, we've been going in circles for two years about whether to sell the strip of land behind the laundry block to a developer who'd put up about forty units, and someone on the board got excited about futarchy and now we are genuinely talking about running a pilot on this one decision: pick a welfare proxy for the co-op, open a conditional market on it, let the price decide which way we go. We've already agreed on more then I expected — the metric is going to be a composite of the median self-reported well-being score from our annual member survey eighteen months out and net assets per member at the five-year mark, and contracts on whichever branch we don't take get voided with everyone's stake refunded.

I was open to this and then I talked myself out of it, and I want to be argued with properly before the next board meeting. Please play a real futarchy advocate — someone who thinks this is the best available tool for exactly this kind of decision, knows the mechanism inside out, will happily concede that particular implementations break in particular ways, and will not concede that a board debate or a one-member-one-vote referendum does the job better. Not a caricature and not a salesperson: if I land a point, say so, then tell me how you'd engineer around it. Keep it to about two paragraphs a turn and stay in character until I say stop. One hard constraint: assume neither of us can look anything up, so no citations, no paper titles, no telling me some paper or pilot exists and I should go read it — argue from how the mechanism actually works.

My opening move. In an ordinary prediction market I get paid for being right, so I trade until the price is right. This isn't that. I get paid for my branch winning, and if it loses I get my money back, because the contracts on the losing side are voided. So my downside from lying about the branch I want is capped at whatever I'm willing to tie up for a couple of years, while my upside is the whole decision — forty units, or not, forever. With 340 members, maybe fifteen of whom will ever actually trade, the book will be thin enough that the three or four who care most can push the conditional price wherever they want. And the reply I keep getting, that they have to pay for the privilege and the money is just a transfer to whoever takes the other side so it's really a bribe that enriches everyone, doesn't reassure me at all here: taking the other side means putting up cash our members mostly don't have, on contracts that may never resolve at all. So isn't this just letting a small, rich, intense minority buy the decision — and worse than voting, because voting at least caps everyone at one share?
