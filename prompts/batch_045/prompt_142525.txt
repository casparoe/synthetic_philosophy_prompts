Hi — novelist here, stuck on a scene, and I need a bit of conceptual engineering.

At the centre of my book is a workplace AI system, and two characters fight about it. One says it's a tool: a hammer with good manners. The other says it's an agent, and that "tool" is just the word people reach for when they don't want to say who's responsible. I want both of them honourable and both with an argument my reader can follow, so the disagreement has to be doing real work rather than being about two people meaning different things by a word.

What I think I need is a case where the two obvious tests for the tool/agent line come apart:

- the goal test: is the system's output shaped so as to bring about some downstream state of the world — does it do anything like means–end reasoning over a horizon longer than its own answer? If yes, agent.
- the handoff test: for each consequential thing the system does, is there a person who saw it coming and could have stopped that thing? If yes, tool.

Build me a scenario where the goal test says agent and the handoff test says tool. I think that's the hard direction; if you think I've got it backwards, say so in a line and do the other one instead. Constraints, because I dont want a cheat:

- today's tech, somewhere a reader already knows (a hospital if you can, otherwise a newsroom or a county benefits office), nothing sci-fi, no self-modification.
- the goal-directedness has to be in daylight: an objective somebody chose, and a system that searches or plans toward it. No secret agenda, no deception, no reward hacking. If the thing is frightening it should be frightening on the surface.
- nobody is careless, rushed, or under-staffed. The human genuinely reads what they approve, and genuinely would understand it taken on it's own.
- the system's output should be short prose a reader could see on the page.
- one paragraph.

Then do the check: what each test gives and why, the single feature of the setup that's doing the work, and what happens to the disagreement if I take that feature out. And give me the best line for the character I'm going to have lose — how would the side you rule against attack the case?

Last thing, since I have to decide how far to commit in print: which side do you actually think is right, where is your reasoning most likely to break, and what's the one consideration that would make you switch?
