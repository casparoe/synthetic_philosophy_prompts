I've been reading, on my own, about the argument over whether what you believe or want can change what you actually see — as opposed to only changing what you say about it or how you act on it — and every discussion I find seems to settle the whole thing with one favourite example. I want the specification instead of another example.

Write me the desiderata for a test of genuine cognitive penetration of perception, where "good enough for" means: good enough to settle the ordinary claim that experts, or people raised in different surroundings, literally see the world differently. Give me the list — the cases it has to classify correctly (the illusion whose lines you know to be the same length; the drawing that flips once someone tells you what it is; the trained eye versus the novice, say a radiologist or a birder; a slope that looks steeper to me when I'm carrying a heavy pack; afterimages and illusory contours, where nobody thinks belief is doing any work), the distinctions it must not collapse (penetration versus attention, versus response bias, versus ordinary context effects, versus learning that only changes what you notice), and the constraints it can't violate (perception is fast and not something you decide to do; it's the thing that puts us in contact with how the world actually is; it doesn't simply vanish when you're told you're being fooled). Say why each is a requirement and mark which are non-negotiable and which are just what one camp happens to want.

One extra demand I have, and I want to know whether it's a real desideratum or my own prejudice: the test should still give an answer where there is no honest first-person report to collect — a blindsight patient, a dog, an image classifier whose output shifts when you change how common it believes tumours to be. Say which of those cases a theory must handle and which it may ignore.

If some subset of the requirements can't be satisfied together, say which — that interests me most. Then commit: tell me which single requirement you think does the most work in knocking theories out, which of my list is really a prejudice, and where you're least sure. Don't score the named positions; I'll do that myself.
