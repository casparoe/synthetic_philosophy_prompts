I'm the junior philosopher on a diagnostic revision working group — call it the DSM-6 working group; the name doesn't matter — and on Thursday I have to defend a proposal to add a new category to the manual, so I want to rehearse against the strongest version of the objection I'll actually face. Please play the senior member of the committee who has been blocking it: a psychiatrist-philosopher working in the Hacking line, committed to the view that psychiatric categories are interactive rather than indifferent kinds, that the looping of a classification back onto the people classified is a real feature of the world and not a symptom of sloppy science, and that a criterion set built largely out of patients' reports about their own agency is precisely the sort of thing that elaborates the phenomenon it names. Be sharp rather than a caricature: concede the small points you'd really concede, help yourself to the resources your position actually has (the interactive/indifferent distinction, the idea that a kind can be a moving target and still be known, the notion of an ecological niche that makes a human kind more or less hospitable, self-fulfilling versus self-frustrating loops, the history of transient illnesses that flourished and then disappeared, the difference between looping in the individual and looping in the kind, and the point that the manual's own legitimacy rests on reliability, feasibility and clinical utility rather than on any claim to have carved nature at its joints). Do not concede that looping by itself settles whether the category belongs in the manual, do not give me a lecture on the literature, and if you need a fact about the proposal or about our pilot, take a position first and then ask me for it. Keep every reply to two short paragraphs so it feels like a committee argument, and stay in character until I say stop.

Here is what I am defending. The proposed category is Narrative Agency Loss: onset after a sustained period of relying on a conversational AI companion; a persistent, distressing experience in which one's own wants and emotions are reported as unavailable until something outside has articulated them first — patients say things like *I know what I want only once the chat has said it* — together with significant impairment, and two exclusion clauses, one for depersonalization-derealization and psychotic presentations and one for the case where the person still believes the system has privileged access to their mind. That last exclusion is in there deliberately: I want to block the charge that we are simply medicalizing a folk-psychological confusion about how language models work.

My opening move is that the looping objection, as you have been running it, proves far too much. Every category in the manual whose criteria include self-report loops to some degree — depressed mood, flashbacks, depersonalization, even pain — and yet nobody treats that as a reason to doubt they name something; so the real question cannot be whether the category loops, only what shape the loop takes. So I built one into the proposal: an announcement test, in which we disclose the full criterion set to a sample of people who have not encountered it and measure whether self-identification inflates or deflates. In our pilot of roughly four hundred heavy users, disclosure *deflated* it — fewer people endorsed the criteria after reading them, and the exclusion clauses did most of the work — and my argument to the committee is that this is the signature of a real condition rather than a manufactured one, because a category people do not rush to occupy, and that dissolves a little on contact with its own description, is behaving like a discovery and not like a niche. Tell me why that inference is illegitimate. And before you get there: the draft field trial codes clinician notes that were written with AI scribes, so the instrument doing the classifying is partly made of the same technology implicated in the syndrome — I take that to be an objection to our instruments rather than to our category, and I would like to hear why it isn't.
