So I'm in this group chat — half rationalist-adjacent, half startup people — and we're trying to help a small nonprofit-ish org drop degree requirements from its hiring and use a work-sample/portfolio thing instead. One guy in the chat, who reads a lot of evo-psych, more or less shut the discussion down with: "a signal is like an advertisement. The money you spend on the ad is thrown away, and that waste is the whole point — it's what makes the message believable. If it didn't cost anything, everyone would run the ad, and then the ad carries zero information. So your cheap take-home test can't work. The waste has to be somewhere; you're just moving it around." He then reeled off peacock tails, stotting gazelles, Rolexes, expensive weddings, and said the handicap-principle math guarantees this in general: honest signalling requires waste, full stop. Nobody had a good answer and its been bugging me for a week.

My own half-baked pushback, which I'd rather you stress-test than just agree with: as I understand it the handicap condition isn't "resources get destroyed," it's closer to "the signal has to be differentially costly — it has to cost a worse sender more than it costs a better one" — which is a claim about what a *lower-quality* sender *would* pay, i.e. a counterfactual, not about anything actually being burned up in equilibrium. And if that's the real condition, there ought to be signals where nothing is consumed at all: posting a bond or a hostage that you get back if you turn out to be telling the truth, or a credential that's free to hold but gets retroactively yanked plus a penalty if the holder underperforms — the cost is real and deters the fakers, but nobody ever pays it on the equilibrium path. Call them hostage signals versus burnt-money signals if that helps. If hostage signals genuinely work, then the ad analogy is smuggling in waste where the theory only needs differential cost, and my friend's "you're just moving the waste" is false rather than deep. But I also suspect there's a catch — something about needing someone who can verify quality after the fact, or about a single bond only sorting people into two bins instead of a smooth scale, or about the bond having to be big enough to sting the marginal type while top types post it for free — and maybe that's exactly where the waste creeps back in.

So: audit the analogy properly. Give me the correspondence table (what plays the role of the ad budget, the product's actual quality, the customer, the repeat purchase, the rival firm that would love to lie), then separate the disanalogies that matter for the use he's making of it from the ones that are just decoration, and tell me whether "waste is load-bearing" survives once you put hostage-type signals next to burnt-money-type ones. If the ad picture is the wrong one, give me a better analogy for what a signal actually is. Assume for concreteness that the org gets ~30 applicants per role, can observe job performance reasonably well about six months after hiring, and can't pay anyone to sit through anything long. Write in American English, and keep it concrete rather than literature-flavored.
