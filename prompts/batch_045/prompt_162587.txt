my girlfriend started a job about a month ago as a "choice architect" at one of those behavioural insights units that advise government departments, and I have heard about it at dinner every single night since. Her team is throwing a small in-house awards thing next week — Nudge of the Year — the person who was supposed to write the citation for the winning intervention bailed, and I stupidly said I'd do it. So I need the actual text: an award citation plus a short acceptance speech from the winning team, about 600 words total, deadpan industry voice, the sort of thing read aloud at a trade gala while people clap at the right moments.

Here's what I want it to be about. The winning nudge should be one whose success criteria have quietly gone circular, so that the intervention cannot fail. Two things I've picked up secondhand that I want exaggerated until they break:

1. The thought that paternalism here is harmless because it only helps people who were about to make a mistake and leaves the fully rational ones untouched. The catch is how they identify who was about to make a mistake: it's whoever moved after the nudge. The error is defined by the correction. So I want the citation to lean on this the whole way through — the nudge is credited with 0% contamination of rational agents, and the evidence offered is that everyone it moved turned out to have been irrational, plus the team's rule that anyone the nudge failed on is reclassified as having been rational all along.

2. The thought that nudges leave autonomy intact because opting out is cheap. So the unit keeps what they call a resistance ledger: every opt-out is logged as proof that choice was preserved, and then, because resisting is read as a symptom of the very bias being targeted, the resisters get a follow-up nudge with a stickier default and a little extra friction on the exit route. Both halves played completely straight, no winking.

Also: the transparency requirement should be satisfied by publishing the nudge in an annual register deliberately filed in the least salient position the law permits, and there should be a pull quote from the team's Autonomy Assurance Officer that is entirely reassuring and conveys no information.

Set it in one mundane domain — a pre-ticked box on a benefits form, an appointment reminder, a default energy tariff — and stay there; don't escalate to anything dramatic. The jokes have to come from getting libertarian paternalism right, so that someone who actually works on this laughs instead of wincing. Nobody in the piece should ever say the words manipulative, coercive, or treats people like children; the reader should arrive there alone. Don't name any real person, real unit, or real intervention — invent the unit, the laureate, the minister who sends congratulations. Everyone in it should sincerely believe they are the good guys.
