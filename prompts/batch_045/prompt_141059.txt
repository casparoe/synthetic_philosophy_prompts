hi — so I'm a junior and I'm one of two students on this advisory committee our school board threw together, and we have to say something at the meeting next thursday, so I've been trying to write down where I actually stand instead of just nodding along. Please take this seriously and act like a professional philosopher who works on this stuff for a living — like, actually brainstorm through it before you answer, go commitment by commitment, don't just give me your own view on statues. And plain American English please, I'm 17 and not writing a paper. If you use a jargon word explain it.

The situation: our school is Whitcomb High, named after Governor Silas Whitcomb in like 1898. He's on the school because he built the state's public school system — that is literally why it's named after him. He also ran the campaign that rewrote the state constitution to take the vote away from Black people, and the history teacher says the two things were the same political project, same coalition, the disenfranchisement is partly how he got the school money through. There's also a statue of him downtown on the courthouse lawn, put up in the mid-1950s by a local committee, right after the Supreme Court said school segregation was unconstitutional, with a plaque about him being the defender of our schools. The board's compromise idea is that we keep the name but "re-aim" it, so that Whitcomb High now honors the students instead of the governor.

Where I've landed:

1. Commemoration is about us, not about him. He's dead, he can't be wronged, so "does he deserve it" is the wrong question. The only question is what the name and the statue do here and now.

2. But I also think it's genuinely unfair to judge someone from the 1890s by 2025 morals, and he wasn't unusual for his time — most white politicians in the state went along with it. That feels like it should count for something and I don't want to give it up.

3. My rough rule: if the thing he's honored for is tangled up with the bad thing, then it comes down. If the bad thing is basically unrelated to why he was honored, keep it and add context.

4. Names and statues are different things. The school name is forced on people every day — it's on the diploma, the fight song, we say "Whitcomb pride" at assembly — so it's active endorsement. The statue is just something you drive past and can ignore. So: rename the school, keep the statue, put a real plaque on it.

5. Who decides: the people it actually hurts — Black students and families in this town — should get way more say than the alumni association that just hates change. If the people it hurts want it gone that's close to the end of the argument for me.

6. And I don't buy the "if we take it down we're erasing history" thing at all. But I also sort of do think hauling the statue off is a way of making the problem disappear instead of dealing with it, which is part of why I want the plaque version. Also the "re-aim the name" idea feels fake to me but I can't say why.

So: does this hang together? Go through the pairs and tell me which ones are really in tension and which only look like it, and rank the real ones by how bad they are. For each real one, say what specifically would have to give and what it'd cost me, and then tell me which single change would clean up the most. Also be honest if one of these is just confused rather than in tension with anything. I'm not attached to any particular one of them, I genuinely can't tell which is the weak one, and I'm not asking you to fix my verdict for thursday so much as to tell me where I'm kidding myself.
