hey, so our school philosophy club has this thing where before each discussion you write down like 5 or 6 things you actually believe about the topic and then everyone else tries to find the contradiction. this months topic is karma as a theory of moral desert — and I mean the real version, not the "what goes around comes around" version: the claim that what happens to you is what you *deserve*, and that this is a natural law, so there's no judge handing it out. i grew up around this (my grandparents are buddhist) so i care about getting it right more than about winning the session, but i also dont want to just repeat stuff i heard at home. heres my card:

(1) karma is a desert claim, not just a causal one. suffering isn't only *caused* by past bad acts, it's *owed* because of them. the backwards-looking part is the whole point, otherwise it's just consequences with a moral label stuck on.

(2) there's no permanent self. a person is a causal stream of mental and physical events, and what carries on is the stream — not a soul, and not memories.

(3) but i also think it's wrong to say someone deserves to suffer for something no stage of them remembers doing and that left no dispositions or character traits behind. causal continuity on its own isn't enough for desert, you need something psychological. to test it i made up this case: someone does something awful at 20, then at 25 a procedure wipes every memory of it and every disposition it formed — they're not guilty, not calloused, not anything — and the only link left is that the 25 year old is causally downstream of the 20 year old. i think the 25 year old deserves nothing for it. (i know some versions of buddhism say there's a store-consciousness that carries seeds across, and i think that's kind of cheating.)

(4) even though i said desert, i think the actual point of karmic suffering is educational: it's supposed to wear out the disposition, not balance a ledger.

(5) i want the theory to explain things: no suffering is undeserved, so the distribution of suffering in the world is in principle fully accounted for, even though we can't see which past acts are hooked up to which.

(6) transfering merit to other people is bunk. responsibility is individual.

(7) there's supposed to be an exit — the stream can actually stop, not just get recycled forever.

the guy who runs the club says (5) plus (1) just amounts to "everyone who suffers had it coming" and that that's the real problem with the whole thing. i said that's not a contradiction, that's just a consequence you don't like, and we sort of stalled there. i also half suspect (5) has a regress thing going on — if there's no first karma, what does the first suffering answer to — but idk whether that's (5)'s problem or (7)'s.
