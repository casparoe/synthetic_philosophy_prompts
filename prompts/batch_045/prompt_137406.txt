I want to pressure-test a position I have been defending about embedded agency. Call it the regulator-parity thesis: the hard part of embedded agency is not really about agency. It is the general problem of a system that is smaller than, and causally coupled to, the system it has to track — a thermometer thermally coupled to the bath it measures, a regulator that must contain a model of what it regulates, in each case a proper part carrying information about a whole that includes the part. On this view the familiar catalogue of embedded-agency puzzles — the impossibility of a complete self-model, the breakdown of the clean input/output boundary on which Bayesian and causal-decision-theoretic framings depend, the grain-of-truth or realizability problem, predictor cases where another system models the agent — are all instances of that one difficulty, and the right research response is to build a theory of self-modeling subsystems (sufficient statistics, coarse-graining, consistency conditions on partial models) rather than a new decision theory. What would count against it? I want defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I especially want the ones a defender of the thesis should concede rather than the ones a critic would reach for first: is there any embedded-agency puzzle that survives if you simply hand the agent a perfect sufficient statistic of its environment? Do cases where the agent's own deliberation is among the things it must predict — self-fulfilling prediction, a fully transparent predictor, an agent deciding whether to rewrite its own decision procedure — have a structure with no analogue in the measurement case? Does an adequate treatment of the measurement case secretly need a notion of directedness or of a standpoint that only a goal-directed system has, so that parity collapses at the crucial point? And has the thesis been stated loosely enough — the same difficulty, a part tracking a whole — that no result could refute it, and if so is that a problem for a claim whose point is to redirect research effort? Finally, say how confident you are in each of your main claims and single out the one you are least sure of.
