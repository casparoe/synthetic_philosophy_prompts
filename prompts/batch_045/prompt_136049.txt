I chair the release review board at my company, and we have to decide by Friday whether to ship our new assistant model to enterprise API customers. The safety case in front of us is statistical: the evals team ran red-teaming plus a deployment simulation and puts the rate of catastrophic outputs (their taxonomy, roughly: actionable help with a clearly illicit goal) at about 1 in 10^7 conversations, and argues that at ~300 million conversations a month that's ~30 incidents, of which monitoring catches a decent fraction. Two things bother me. One: the eval prompts were sampled from our current consumer traffic using our current model, but enterprise customers will hit us with their own system prompts and their own resampling loops, and anyone who actually wants the bad output can just try a few thousand times, so the rate that matters may not be the rate on the distrubtion we measured. Two: it's a mean, not a bound, and our own red team found a single jailbreak template behind most of the hits, which makes me think the tail is where basically all the risk sits.

Priya on the board says nothing ships without a worst-case bound, and the formal methods group backs her, but they also say the soonest they could deliver one is 18 months out and it would be over a narrow formally specified property that probably isn't the harm we actually care about, which makes me suspect her standard comes out vacuous in practice. The CPO says waiting for it means not shipping at all.

Draft the memo I'll circulate: approve, hold, or approve with conditions and a date. Pick one and argue for it, be straight about the strongest point on the side you're voting against, and flag where your recommendation rests on a judgement call rather than on anything in the packet -- I want a definite bottom line, not a menu.
