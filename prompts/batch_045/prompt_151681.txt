Hi — I'm a volunteer giving advisor for a smallish family foundation, and I keep tripping over something in how the effective-altruism-flavoured funding world actually behaves. Could you answer in British English?

Here's the practice. Nearly every serious funder in that space expresses value in one unit: you work out how much good a programme does, then state it as a multiple of simply handing the same money to someone in extreme poverty. Unconditional cash transfers are the yardstick — the one thing everyone treats as known — so the case for deworming, insectide-treated nets, vitamin A, whatever, is that it's some multiple better than cash. Second: what actually gets funded is overwhelmingly the stuff with a randomised trial or a clean metric behind it, a DALY here and a test score there, while things without a number attached — organising, movement building, policy and institutional work, most systemic bets — mostly don't get a look in however plausible the story is. And this has got more pronounced as the money has got bigger and the grantmaking has professionalised. Third: the very same people will defend cash by saying poor people know their own needs best and we shouldn't impose our guesses on them, then turn around and fund a health intervention precisely because recipients weren't going to choose it and needed a subsidy, a nudge or a free delivery to take it up.

So what does that package presuppose? Does using cash as the zero point commit you to a substantive view about what makes a life go well, or is it only a convention? Is the preference for measurable things a moral claim about what's valuable, or an artefact of foundations and grant officers having to justify themselves to a board? Is the cash-knows-best / nets-need-a-nudge pairing actually inconsistent, or can one theory carry both? And what would have to change in how they did things if the implicit view were swapped for a rival one?

Be clear about which parts the practice strictly requires and which merely sit comfortably with it, and about where several quite different theories would all license exactly what they do. And please separate what's genuinely settled among people who know this material from what's contested — flag the contested bits as contested instead of smoothing them into a consensus. I've read enough of the blog posts to know a lot of this is argued about.
