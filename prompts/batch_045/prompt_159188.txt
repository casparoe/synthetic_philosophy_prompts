I have no formal training in physics — I'm a retired actuary who got hooked on this through popular books and has been working through the demon argument on my own — and I've been asked to write up where I've landed for a small reading group. Before I do, I'd like a consistency check. Please answer in American English, and please skip any general exposition of Maxwell's demon or Landauer's principle; I know the standard story.

Here is the package I've arrived at:

1. The demon's real problem is not watching the molecules but clearing its notes: resetting one bit costs at least kT ln 2, and that is what keeps the second law intact.
2. Observing costs nothing in principle; the whole bill comes due at the clearing.
3. The second law is statistical, not exceptionless — in small systems over short times entropy genuinely can fall, it's just overwhelmingly unlikely.
4. Entropy is best understood epistemically: a measure of what an agent doesn't know about the microstate, not a property the system carries on its own.
5. Information is still physical — a bit has to live in something, and physics constrains what you can do with it.
6. What has to be tracked is not the number of bits in the notebook but how much the notebook says about the gas; I've started calling this the correlation budget, and I think the clearing cost should scale with that mutual information rather than with a raw bit count.
7. A demon with an endless supply of blank tape that never erases anything is no counterexample, since nothing is ever reset.
8. A demon that reversibly compresses its notebook before clearing it can sometimes pay less than one bit's worth per entry, and I don't think that breaks anything.
9. Resetting a memory whose contents I already know — I watched it get set to all zeros — should cost nothing, and I don't think that's a counterexample either.
10. The direction of explanation runs from the erasure bound to the second law, not the other way around; the bound isn't just the second law restated in information language.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? Please rank the real tensions by how serious they are rather than listing them as equals, say for each what would have to be given up, and flag any that are merely verbal. Then tell me the single cheapest revision that cleans up the most, and what it costs me elsewhere in the set.
