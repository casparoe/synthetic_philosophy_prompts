I want a sidebar for the piece: a which-decision-theory-are-you-actually-running-on quiz for readers, plus a small flowchart I can print next to it. Readers are smart but have no philosophy background, and I want the whole thing to take under two minutes.

What I need from you:

Eight questions, each a tiny concrete scenario with two or three answer options. No question may use the words evidence, correlation, causation, rational, utility, or predict-as-such, and no answer option may carry a theory name — a reader should be able to answer without knowing that there is a debate. The cases I want used (rework the details freely, but keep the structural point of each):

1. The baseline: you're in Damascus, Death is a very reliable predictor of where you'll be, fleeing costs you a small amount (a train fare, say), and the payoffs are set up so that staying is better if Death goes to Aleppo and fleeing is better if Death comes to Damascus.
2. The same case with the small cost turned into a small bonus, so that whichever option the don't-treat-your-own-choice-as-news answer picks flips. I like the hook that the entire answer turns on the train fare, so please build that in explicitly.
3. A version where the predictor is only 90% accurate rather than near-perfect, to see whose answer moves continuously and whose flips.
4. A version where, the night before, you can pay a modest fee to have your choice locked in for you: would you pay?
5. The same scenario every week for a year, Death predicting each week fresh.
6. A version where Death reliably predicts your coin as well, so arranging to randomise buys you nothing.
7. A version where you get a message telling you where Death is, but only after you've already committed — and then a bookie offers you a bet on where Death is. Same odds you'd have taken before you left, or not?
8. A control with no predictor at all: same numbers, but Death's location is just settled by a coin toss nobody foresaw. I want this one to catch the readers who are answering on vibes or who are extremely survival-motivated, so I can throw out inconsistent answer sheets.

I'd also like one pair of questions that are the same case reframed (say the 90% one described once as right nine times in ten and once as a ten percent chance of escape) so I can see how many readers are just frame-sensitive.

Six leaves, and I want the tree built from the logical space first — the questions and the answer combinations they allow — and only then the real positions located in it. The leaves:

- Your own choice isn't news about anything; what matters is what would happen if you did each thing.
