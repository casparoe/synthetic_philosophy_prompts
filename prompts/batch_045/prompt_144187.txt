I'm applying for a nine-month dissertation fellowship and need roughly two pages of proposal text for a project on Maxwell's demon and the physics of information. No bibliography or literature review — I'll fill in citations myself. What I want from you is the conceptual work: turn a half-formed idea into a question that has a determinate answer and could be finished in the time available.

The half-formed idea. The standard resolution holds that the demon's record must eventually be erased, and that erasure is where the thermodynamic bill gets paid. But that seems to me to conflate two separable claims. One is a bookkeeping claim: any mutual information the demon acquires about the gas must be offset by an entropy cost somewhere in the closed system, and erasure is only one of the places that cost can land. The other is an embodiment claim: information is useful to the demon only to the extent that it is physically realized in a system that can actually be coupled to the gas — a demon that an external observer credits with lots of knowledge but that has no way to act on it extracts nothing.

To prise the two apart I've been sketching a demon with an N-bit memory that never erases anything and instead writes each measurement record onto an ever-growing tape, running for M cycles. Here the bill looks like it is paid in the tape's rising entropy rather than in any erasure step, and the quantity of interest becomes a tradeoff between memory size, cycle count, and work extracted per cycle. If that sketch survives, the erasure story would be one special case of a more general principle about where the entropy has to go, and a good deal of the philosophical argument about 'information being physical' would have been conducted on an unrepresentative architecture.

What I need in the proposal: the question stated precisely enough that a negative answer is possible; why the existing framings leave it open; the method, and why that method rather than the obvious alternatives (pure information-theoretic bookkeeping on the one hand, or a general metaphysics of information on the other); the two or three conclusions the project could reach and which considerations would decide between them; and an honest statement of what would count as the project failing, including whether the toy model is likely to collapse into an instance of something already settled.
