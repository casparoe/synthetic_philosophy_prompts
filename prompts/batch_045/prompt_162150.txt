hey — I want you to compare two ways of spelling out "the kind of free will that's actually worth wanting".

Menu view: freedom is a property of your option space — how many live alternatives you can represent as reasons-bearing and really select, and how resistant that space is to being quietly narrowed (by compulsion, by manipulation, or just by an algorithm feeding you choices you'd never have thought of on your own). Trace view: freedom is a property of the action's actual causal history — it has to run through your own reasons and deliberative machinery in the right way, and whether alternatives were ever open to you is beside the point, so even a Frankfurt-style intervener sitting ready to redirect you takes nothing away.

Two cases to seperate them with. (1) Shrinking menu: over years someone's option space gets narrowed bit by bit, and the narrowing never once changes what they actually pick. Menu view says their freedom drains gradually and is a comparative matter ("less free than she was"); trace view says nothing happens at all until the narrowing finally bites. (2) Weighted randomiser: huge, vivid menu, but the selection is made by an internal coin-flip whose weights are set by the agent's own reasons. Menu view says maximal freedom; trace view has to decide whether a reasons-weighted coin is the right *kind* of history.

Which of the two is closer to whatever it is we care about when we want free will at all, and which handles the shrinking-menu case better? Does the answer split depending on what the freedom is wanted for — blame, self-creation, meaning in life? Pick one as primary and say what the other one is still good for.
