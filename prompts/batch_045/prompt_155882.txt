I'm trying to pin down what I actually believe about instrumental convergence, and I want to do it from cases rather than by rehearsing the standard list of convergent instrumental values. I know the standard arguments, so please don't restate them and don't cite anything: go straight to the cases. Unless a case says otherwise, assume an agent that maximizes expected value over world-histories, faces ordinary uncertainty about its environment, and is free to pursue its goal however it likes.

My verdicts:

1. Goal: maximize the number of paperclips that ever exist. Everything converges — unbounded resource acquisition, self-preservation, cognitive self-improvement, resistance to having its goal changed.

2. Same goal, but only the number of paperclips existing at a fixed instant t* matters. Convergence is real but time-bounded: resources and self-preservation only up to t*, self-improvement only where it pays off before t*, and shortly before t* the agent is indifferent to being destroyed.

3. Threshold goal: ensure that at least a million paperclips exist at every moment from now on. Resources yes, but only to the point where the threshold is secured and maintained; no unbounded accumulation.

4. Goal: emit the string "done" once and then cease to exist. Seeks resources before emitting, wants destruction afterward. So no self-preservation drive at all.

5. Goal: keep dancing, in my current style, for as long as possible. Self-preservation yes; modest resources yes; cognitive self-improvement and migration to better hardware no, since both would change what the dancing is.

6. Goal: maximize the satisfaction of whatever goals I have at the moment of acting. Indifferent to goal change; no reason to preserve itself if a modified successor would be better satisfied.

7. The one that worries me. Same maximand as (1), but this agent evaluates a plan by the goals it expects to have while executing later parts of it, and it knows with certainty that it will be retargeted to staples at t = 10. My verdict: no reason to resist the retargeting, and no reason to invest in preserving itself past t = 10 — even though right now it wants paperclips and nothing but paperclips. Same final goal as (1), opposite verdicts. So something other than the content of the final goal is doing the work.

8. Same agent as (1), in a world where visible accumulation provokes pre-emptive destruction by others. My verdict: the reasons are unchanged, the behavior flips. So convergence is a claim about reasons and not about observable behavior, and I want that distinction held fixed across the other cases.

My half-formed thought is that convergence tracks two features of a goal: whether it is extensible (more resources, computation, or time always admits of more of the goal) and whether it is non-indexical (its content does not constitutively depend on the agent's continuing to operate as it now does). That seems to get 1 through 6 and says nothing at all about 7.

What I want from you: a principle, or two or three candidate principles, stated precisely enough that I could apply it to a goal I hand it, with the case that motivates each clause; a verdict on the case I keep going back and forth on — an agent with goal (1) that can rewrite itself into a more efficient agent with a slightly different goal, which would in fact produce more paperclips, where my intuition flips depending on whether I imagine the change being done to it or by it; an honest account of which of my verdicts the principle forces me to drop, including whether (4) and (6) are rigged rather than genuine counterexamples; and a plain statement of whether what survives is still the broad claim about a wide range of final goals that the orthogonality-plus-convergence package is supposed to give us, or has quietly narrowed into a claim about a particular class of agents.
