ok so I'm in a teleosemantics reading group and I got into an argument I cant stop chewing on. The case we were arguing about is a lab thing: a strain of bacteria evolved over a few hundred generations to swim up a gradient of some sugar, call it A. Then someone shifted them into a new environment where the same receptor also latches onto a chemically similar compound B that they cant metabolise, so they swim toward B and do worse.

One guy in the group — very Millikan-style, proper functions and selected effects all the way down — says its just like a heart. A heart is for pumping blood; a heart that fails to pump is a defective heart, and that is all malfunction amounts to. So the receptor is for detecting A, because that is what it was selected for, so firing on B is a malfunction, and malfunction just is misrepresentation, so the content is A and not B. He used it to close the whole thing: misrepresentation is nothing over and above malfunction, content is naturalised, there's a determinate fact of the matter, no normativity problem left.

I think the heart is quietly carrying a lot of that but I can't put my finger on where it gives. Can you audit it? Give me the correspondences as a table — what plays the role of the heart, of pumping blood, of the organism's survival, of the designer, of the defect — and then where the mapping breaks. The bits I keep circling:

1. the heart's job is a thing it causally does, but content is supposed to be something a state can have even when it never in fact indicates it, and can have wrongly. Can a causal-etiological story about a thing's effects deliver that?
2. selected for detecting A, or for detecting food, or for detecting A-ish-or-B-ish stuff generally? Is there a privileged description of the job, and does the heart case hide that worry because we already know which contribution we care about?
3. what about whatever reads the receptor downstream — if a later mechanism evolves that treats that signal as B-ish and acts on that, does the content change? And is there any analogue of a consumer for a heart, or does the analogy just not have room for one?

Then the payoff: does misrepresentation = malfunction survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the heart doing the work? Which disanalogies actually matter for his conclusion and which are noise? And if the heart is bad, is there a better one — a key and a lock, a fuel gauge, a map?

Reply in British English please, and go easy on the jargon where you can.
