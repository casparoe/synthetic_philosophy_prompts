Hey — I need a draft of a joint statement, and every time I revise it myself it gets blander, so I'd rather just hand it off.

Setup: two colleagues of mine (one does psychophysics of time perception, one does philosophy of mind) have been arguing about color phi for a couple of years and have agreed to do an adversarial collaboration before they start just repeating themselves. The case: a red flash at location A followed roughly 100 ms later by a green flash at location B produces a single percept of one dot moving from A to B, changing color partway along the path. The awkward bit is that the experience as reported seems to have content — the color change at the middle of the path — at a time before the second flash could of been processed, so something about the timing of experience has to give. The two standard hypotheses: Orwellian, on which there was a genuine earlier conscious episode and then memory of it was rewritten; Stalinesque, on which there was never an earlier draft at all, the brain produces one finished episode later, and the apparent earlier timing is just how that episode gets dated.

The two camps in the actual argument aren't those two, though. Camp 1 holds that no empirical test could distinguish the hypotheses, so there is no fact of the matter about when the experience occurred, and what the subject consciously experienced at t is settled by the subject's report and nothing more. Camp 2 holds that there is a fact of the matter and that it is at least in principle discoverable — from neural timing, or from an intervention that lands between the putative experience and the report. I want the document those two could both sign.

What I want in it:

- A numbered agreed-claims section first. Not things nobody denies — the load-bearing ones: what the verbal reports actually establish, whether the anomaly generalises to the auditory and tactile analogues, what the existing designs do and don't show, and what both camps owe to each other's best case.
- Then a disagreements table. For each row: the disagreement stated neutrally, then each camp's own preferred formulation of its own view, in wording that camp would actually accept rather than wording I've imposed on it.
- The cruxes. I think there are at least three: (i) whether report has any authority about the timing of experience as opposed to its content; (ii) the circularity worry — candidates for the neural correlate of a conscious episode get picked out by correlating them with report, so can neural evidence ever be independent here; (iii) whether the Orwellian/Stalinesque pair exhausts the space at all, or whether the honest answer is that the experience's timing is indeterminate over an interval — in which case part of the argument has been about whether indeterminate timing is even coherent, and possibly part of it has been verbal.
- For each crux, a concrete case or design both camps would agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction written down in advance. Ones I'd like used: a TMS pulse or masking flash delivered at variable delays after the second flash; an involuntary index that needs no report at all (smooth-pursuit onset relative to the apparent path, say); and a stretched version where the second flash is delayed well past the usual integration window, so that the anomalous dating has to either scale with the delay or break.
- Each camp should also say what would count as evidence against its own view, and whether there is anything both agree would actually move the debate rather than just restate it.

No citations, I'll add those myself. Aim for something like 1000-1400 words, don't pad it, and don't let Camp 1 quietly win by defining their claim so that nothing could ever touch it — Camp 2 should get to notice if that's happening. If you think the cruxes are fewer, or different, overrule me and say why.
