Write a dialogue of about 900 to 1,200 words between two political philosophers of equal ability who disagree about whether one act counts as corruption.

The case: Nkechi is a district water engineer with discretion over the order in which villages are reconnected to a repaired main. The official priority list ranks her uncle's village fourth; she connects it first. No money changes hands and she gets nothing material out of it. She acts because her kin network secured her the post and she would lose its support if she refused. The list itself was drafted by an external donor agency, is posted on the office wall, has never been enforced, and is regarded by neither officials nor residents as legitimate. Call rules of that kind phantom rules: on the books, unenforced, and acknowledged by no one subject to them.

Okoye holds that this is not corruption. There is no entrusted public power worth the name where the state barely delivers, and her act is a transfer inside a network of reciprocal obligation; the impersonal standard is really the ethics of a particular institutional package — secure salaries, career civil service, administration that actually reaches people — that her country lacks, and it is unfair to demand that individual officials bear the cost of a legitimacy the state has not earned. Lindqvist holds that it is corruption: the wrong-making feature is the deflection of an office's purpose toward partial ends, not any payment, and phantom status is only an enforcement failure, which cannot turn a breach into something permissible.

Requirements: neither side may be a straw man; each must answer the other's actual argument rather than restate a position, and each must concede at least one point. Lindqvist must meet the scaffolding objection without relying on anything Nkechi's state currently provides. Okoye must face a relabeling test: if the very same material flow, recast as constituency service or as a gift between personal friends, would flip her verdict, then her view seems to track presentation rather than substance. By the end, let them establish whether they are disagreeing about what corruption is or only about how much wrongness the word should carry.

Write in American English, in dialogue form with speaker names. Finish with a single line, enclosed in \answer{...}, naming the one consideration on which the disagreement actually turns.
