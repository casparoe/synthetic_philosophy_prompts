I recently started doing donor calls for an animal shelter — my first job where part of what I take home depends on how much people give, which is weird to sit with. After a training session our two team leads got into an argument and I've been turning it over ever since.

The trainer had taught us two things: put a big "$150/month Guardian" option on the pledge form mainly so the $50 option looks reasonable next to it, and ask "how does it feel to be someone who helps animals?" before we ever ask for money.

Lena said both are manipulation. Neither gives the donor a reason they didn't already have; they work by reshaping how the choice shows up, so the gift gets caused by features of the pitch instead of by the good it does.

Marc said that's just a description of persuasion in general. You can't hand someone a reason without picking which reason to lead with, in what order, in what words. There's no framing-free version of the ask, so if Lena's right we shouldn't be asking at all.

Lena then offered a test: if the same device would work just as well to get someone to give to a cause they've got no reason to care about, its not engaging their judgement at all, it's a lever. Decoys and anchors pass that test; telling them where the money actually goes doesn't. Marc said the test is too crude — it also condemns reminders, deadlines and asking in person, all of which help people act on reasons they already have.

Lena's fallback was a re-run test: if the donor found out how the ask was designed and would still make the gift, it was persuasion; if they'd feel played, it was manipulation. Marc said that makes the wrong depend on how the donor feels afterward, so one and the same script is manipulative with a resentful donor and fine with a grateful one — and anyway hindsight gets shaped by the pitch too.

Lena ended up saying maybe that's just what manipulation is: a wrong to a particular person inside a particular relationship, not a property of a technique. Marc said then the real question was never "is this tactic ok" but "are we in the kind of relationship with donors where this is ok," and he's not sure a script plus a bonus structure is compatible with that.

They asked me who had it right and I honestly couldn't tell. Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about what, and what, if anything, both of them are missing?
