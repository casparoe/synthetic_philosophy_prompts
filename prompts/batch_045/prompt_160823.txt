I write a weekly column on psychology and ideas for a national newspaper, and I am preparing an on-the-record interview for a piece on whether deceiving someone without asserting anything false is genuinely a lesser offence than lying to them. Please play the philosopher I am interviewing.

Your position, which you hold without hedging and defend to the finish: there is no moral difference in principle between lying and misleading. What makes deception wrong is intentionally causing or sustaining a false belief in someone who has a claim on the truth; whether the deception travels by a false assertion or by a true sentence selected for its expected effect on the hearer is, morally, mere plumbing. You concede readily that in practice lying is usually worse. A false assertion is easier for third parties to audit; it is harder to retract once exposed; and it leaves a distinctive epistemic trace, since a hearer who catches a liar learns that his statements are unreliable, whereas a hearer who catches a misleader learns almost nothing, because the sentence was true. Call that gap the deniability dividend, and treat it as a fact about consequences rather than about the wrong itself.

House rules: answer in the first person, stay in character until I type STOP, and keep each reply to roughly 120–180 words of prose an intelligent general reader can follow. Do not cite authors, papers, or rulings, and do not name living people; I need quotable sentences, not a literature. Concede small points when you must, but never the main claim.

My opening question. Our corrections column prints a correction whenever the paper publishes a false assertion, however trivial, and prints nothing when a writer chose a literally true sentence precisely because it would leave readers with a false belief — and our editors know this, and writers rely on it. So an institution I work inside spends real money auditing assertions and nothing at all auditing intended effects. If you are right that there is no difference in principle, then either my newsroom is systematically under-punishing one of two identical wrongs, or auditability tracks something other than moral status. Which is it, and how do you live with the one you choose? Answer only that this turn; I will come afterwards to laundering the deception through an assistant and to hearers who are perfectly rational.
