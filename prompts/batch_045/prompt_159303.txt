**The hard problem as a problem of measurement, not of metaphysics**

The usual framing: there are the "easy" problems (discrimination, report, attention, sleep-wake control), which we know how to attack because they're questions about structure and function, and then the leftover question of why any of that machinery is *accompanied by* experience at all. I want to argue that the reason the leftover question feels different in kind is not that it's about a spooky extra ingredient. It's that the evidence available to us has a symmetry that no amount of third-person data can break.

Call it *interface invariance*. Take every measurement we can in principle make of a system from outside: behaviour, reaction times, verbal reports, neural dynamics, lesion effects, counterfactuals about what it would do in other circumstances. Now take the qualities the system supposedly instantiates — the reddishness, the ache, the whatever — and permute them, but do it in a way that preserves all the relations among them: same similarity structure, same discriminability, same report-discriminations, same influences on behaviour. Every one of those third-person measurements comes out identical. The evidence is invariant under the permutation. This is the old inverted-spectrum worry, generalised: not just "your red might be my green" but "the whole qualitative labelling might be swapped around wholesale, and nothing we can measure from outside could tell".

If that's right, then the hard problem has the same *shape* as underdetermination of theory by evidence in ordinary science. In my own field you reconstruct a current field from a handful of moorings and the reconstruction is never unique; you pick among the candidates using extra criteria — smoothness, simplicity, consilience with other measurements, some symmetry-breaking assumption. The disanalogy, and this is the bit I think is load-bearing, is that in science those tie-breakers are at least in principle checkable later, by adding instruments. Here they look permanently uncheckable, because the only instrument that could register the difference is the subject itself, and it reads out in the very vocabulary we're trying to calibrate. We're trying to calibrate a detector using the detector.

So there seem to be three ways out.

One: deny the symmetry. Say there's no further fact about which quality is where, because there's nothing quality-ish over and above the functional/structural story — the illusionist-ish route. Then there's nothing to be underdetermined and no tie to break.

Two: accept the symmetry and treat the mapping from structure to quality as a posit with no evidential support at all — a bit like a gauge choice in physics. On this view, questions like "is *this* quality the same one as *that* one" aren't factual questions, they're conventions. I find this attractive for about five minutes and then it seems to slide into option one, which I don't want.

Three: accept that there is a further fact and that we have non-inferential access to it — acquaintance as a measurement channel — and pay the epistemic cost of admitting a channel that isn't publicly checkable. Which sounds awful, except that every measurement chain bottoms out somewhere in a calibration against something we just take as given, so maybe it's less fatal than it looks.

My own tentative conclusion is annoyingly unambitious: we don't currently know whether the symmetry is a real feature of nature (a genuine gauge freedom) or an artefact of having only the instruments we've got. And that suggests a two-part research programme rather than a single leap. First, map the relational structure as completely as possible — a psychophysics of similarity and difference relations, not of qualities. Second, hunt for *covariance leaks*: cases where reports about quality come apart from the relational structure we've mapped, which would give a bridge law something to bite on. The obvious irony is that reports are themselves part of the structure, so we may end up mapping only the report-interface and congratulating ourselves for having measured the quality.

One more thing I'm unsure about. If interface invariance holds, does it quietly undermine the conceivability argument for zombies? If qualities are only ever specified up to their relations, then the gap between "all the structure" and "also experience" isn't a clean gap between two determinate states of affairs, and I'm not sure the conceivability move gets the purchase it's supposed to. But I may be misdescribing what the zombie argument is meant to be doing.
