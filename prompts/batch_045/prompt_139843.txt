So my friend and I got into a stupidly long argument last night about the necessary a posteriori and ended up pulling up Frege's 1892 paper "On Sense and Reference" (in an old translation) on a phone at 1am, which definitately made things worse. The bit we were shouting over is the opening move, where he's trying to motivate sense in the first place:

"The reasons which seem to favour this are the following: a = a and a = b are obviously statements of differing cognitive value; a = a holds a priori and, according to Kant, is to be labeled analytic, while statements of the form a = b often contain very valuable extensions of our knowledge and cannot always be established a priori. The discovery that the rising sun is not new every morning, but always the same, was one of the most fertile astronomical discoveries. Even today the identification of a small planet or a comet is not always a matter of course. Now if we were to regard equality as a relation between that which the names 'a' and 'b' designate, it would seem that a = b could not differ from a = a (i.e. provided a = b is true). A relation would thereby be expressed of a thing to itself, and indeed one in which each thing stands to itself but to no other thing. What is intended to be said by a = b seems to be that the signs or names 'a' and 'b' designate the same thing, so that those signs themselves would be under discussion; a relation between them would be asserted. But this relation would hold between the names or signs only in so far as they named or designated something. It would be mediated by the connexion of each of the two signs with the same designated thing. But this is arbitrary."

and then, a few lines later, on the star case:

"The reference of 'evening star' would be the same as that of 'morning star', but not the sense."

My friend's claim is that the whole passage collapses once you accept rigid designation: "evening star" and "morning star" pick out the same thing in every world, so the identity is necessary, and the only thing left for Frege's cognitive-value point to be about is how we find out — which, she says, means the a = a / a = b contrast is doing no work on modality at all and Frege simply didn't have the necessity/apriority distinction in view. My claim is that Frege never says these identities are contingent, that he's only saying they're not knowable a priori, and that Kripke's examples are therefore compatible with everything in the passage except a reading nobody holds. We went nowhere.
