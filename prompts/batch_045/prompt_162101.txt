I'm on the review panel for a foundation's call on forecasting political instability. One proposal — four years, about $4M — comes from a cliodynamics group. They want to extend their historical database of agrarian states (variables like real wages measured against population, elite overproduction, state fiscal health) into the present, fit what they call a secular-cycle model, and then publish a quarterly "structural pressure" score for roughly twenty countries: they insist this is not an event probability, just a reading of how far into the crisis phase a polity currently sits. Half the panel calls it phrenology with a database; the other half points out that insurers, central-bank risk units and peacebuilding funders currently have nothing at all and would use even a noisy reading. Two complications. Their track record is entirely in-sample — parameters tuned on the same handful of cases the model then retrodicts, and there are only about fifteen of them. And their pilot leaked a three-country score to some hedge funds, one country's sovereign spreads widened, and that finance ministry has now written to us. My own worry runs the other way from the leak: a published warning might get acted on and prevent the crisis, which would then look like a failed forecast, so I'm not even sure what "was the model right" would mean here. Please draft the memo I circulate on Friday: fund, reject, or fund with conditions. If conditions, say concretely what they owe on out-of-sample validation (I've been toying with requiring that the country set and the coding rules be locked in advance, including a few countries the modellers never touch until the scores are frozen) and on who sees the scores and when — embargo, aggregate-only release, or no public release. Give reasons the applicants could actually respond to, give the option you're rejecting its best day in court, and keep it under about 700 words.
