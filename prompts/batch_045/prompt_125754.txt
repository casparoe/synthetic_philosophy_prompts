I have been going back and forth with a friend over an argument he uses to defend wavefunction realism, and I have come to think it establishes a good deal more than either of us is willing to accept.

(1) Call a ψ-surrogate any object in a theory which, together with the Hamiltonian, fixes the probabilities that theory assigns to the outcomes of every possible measurement. Call a theory empirically adequate if it matches the predictions of quantum mechanics to the accuracy to which they have been tested.

(2) Every empirically adequate theory must contain a ψ-surrogate. The predictions themselves have a structure that cannot be generated without something doing that job, and the constraint comes from the predictions rather than from anyone's choice of notation, so it survives any reformulation of the theory.

(3) If a quantity is indispensable to every empirically adequate theory in the way just described, then it does not merely encode someone's ignorance; it answers to something in the world. Otherwise the predictive success of the theory would be left unexplained, a coincidence on the scale of a miracle.

(4) So the wavefunction is ontic, and the psi-epistemic view is false.

(5) Now run (2) and (3) again elsewhere. Any empirically adequate treatment of a gas must contain a distribution surrogate, a probability density over microstates without which its predictions cannot be stated. It satisfies (3) in exactly the same sense that the ψ-surrogate does.

(6) So the distribution over microstates is ontic too, and is not an encoding of ignorance about which microstate obtains.

(7) And the pattern generalises: take any theory and any quantity without which its predictions cannot be stated, and (2) and (3) apply to it. Nothing in any scientific theory represents ignorance, the distinction between psi-ontic and psi-epistemic views is empty, and every view is ontic.

(8) That cannot be right. A distinction that could not possibly be false is not a distinction worth drawing.

Of the claims above, I would most like to keep (4) and to reject (6), but I cannot see where the reasoning goes wrong: (2) and (3) each look like ordinary scientific realism, and (5) looks like a genuine parallel rather than a parody. Which step should I give up, why did that step look sound when it is not, and what does giving it up cost me? In particular, is there a way of reaching (4) that does not also reach (7), and if so does it rest on something more specific than indispensability?
