hey — hope this is the sort of thing you can help with. I'm in a small reading group going round in circles on whether self-deception is even possible, and I've started to think it's because everyone is leaning on the same analogy without ever laying it out.

The analogy: deceiving yourself is just like deceiving a mate of yours, except both parts are played inside one head. There's someone who knows the truth and would rather you didn't have it, someone who ends up with the false belief, a motive for wanting that, a bit of stage-management — steering the conversation, not opening the letter, keeping the receipt in the drawer — and presumably a moment where the trick lands. One person in the group uses it to argue that self-deception is perfectly possible but *only* given a genuinely divided mind: no single unified agent ever pulls this off on itself, so any real self-deception is a claim about mental architecture, not just about what someone believes. Someone else uses the exact same analogy to argue the opposite — that self-deception is impossible, because in the ordinary case the deceiver has to know precisely what the victim doesn't, and one person can't at once know and not know the same thing, any more than they can be taller than themselves. Same picture, opposite verdicts, which usually means the picture is smuggling something in.

So could you audit it properly? Give me the correspondence table first — what in the one-person case plays the deceiver, the deceived, the true belief, the lie, the motive, the tell, the moment of success — and then where the mapping actually breaks. I especially want the disanalogies that matter for the job the analogy is being asked to do, separated from the ones that are just noise. Things I suspect are load-bearing but can't sort out: interpersonal deception needs a channel and some shared code; the deceiver acts at a time, with a stable intention, and can give it up; the victim is ignorant of something they could in principle be told, and can be disabused by someone else; deceiving someone is the sort of thing you can be blamed for, and the blame doesn't evaporate if the victim was complicit. Which of those are essential to the analogy's conclusion and which can be dropped?

The bit I actually care about, though, is that I don't think the analogy has room for the cases I keep noticing, where nothing internal needs to be divided at all. Take someone who doesn't so much hide a fact from themselves as arrange that the fact never gets assembled: they let their phone do the remembering, they curate what comes back at them, they hand the journalling to a chatbot that only ever sees the cheerful version, they never open the letter but they never quite decide not to either. All the information is available in some sense, but nobody ever puts it together, and if you asked them directly they'd sincerely say nothing is being concealed. Call it outsourced self-deception if you want a label. Is that self-deception on this analogy, or does the analogy force us to say it isn't — and if it forces us to say it isn't, is that a verdict about the case or a defect in the analogy? Does the outsourced version need a divided mind? Does it need an intention to deceive at all, or only an intention not to look?

Then the bottom line: does the claim that self-deception is possible if and only if the mind is divided survive once the disanalogies and the outsourced cases are on the table, or was the analogy carrying that conclusion the whole time? And if the interpersonal picture isn't the right one, give me a better analogy if there is one — I've been toying with something like a badly indexed archive, where the file is intact and perfectly findable but the label on the drawer sends you somewhere else every time, but I can't tell whether that's a real alternative or just the same problem moved into a room with better furniture.

Oh, and please write it in British English. Around 900 words, no citations or reading lists — we've read the literature, we want the mapping taken apart rather than a tour of who said what. Definately don't just list famous positions at me; if one of the branches amounts to the whole thing dissolving into wilful ignorance, or akrasia, or just ordinary motivated reasoning with no paradox in it, tell me which branch I'd be taking if I went there and what it costs me.
