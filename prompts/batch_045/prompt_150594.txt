Hi — I teach a second-year metaphysics module and I'm writing a model answer for our essay on time, and I'd like you to write it. I dont want the standard presentism-vs-eternalism weigh-both-sides thing that students can half remember from the internet, so I've put a twist in the question, and I want you to answer that.

The setup: everyone agrees the dispute is about which times or events exist, and then they fight about truthmakers for ordinary claims like "there were dinosaurs", and about whether relativity leaves any room for an objective present. Now suppose a student proposes: drop the objective present but keep the presentist-sounding ontology. Call it indexical presentism. "Now" is treated exactly like "here": a context-sensitive indexical, so there is no fact of the matter about which time is absolutely present, only about which time is present relative to an utterance or a thought; and what exists is whatever is simultaneous with that context. So "there were dinosaurs" is true at our context, "dinosaurs exist" is true at a Jurassic context, and there's no contradiction, because the existence claim is context-relative too. One selling point: unlike ordinary presentism, it doesn't seem to need a privileged global simultaneity slice.

Write the essay, and work through the following rather than asserting it: does the view actually solve the truthmaker objection, or does the objection just reappear one level up, as a question about what makes it true that at a Jurassic context dinosaurs exist? Can a context-relative existence still underwrite what presentists actually cared about — real passage, an open future, the intuition that the future isn't already sitting there? And is there any argument, or any conceivable observation, that would tell indexical presentism apart from plain eternalism — or is the residual difference merely verbal?

Then commit to something. Is indexical presentism a genuine third option, eternalism in disguise, or presentism with the spine removed? And whatever you decide, say what it shows about the original debate: if the view turns out to be stable but indistinguishable from eternalism, does that make the presentism/eternalism dispute less substantive than it looks? Take a side on both questions and defend it; don't close by saying more work needs to be done. Aim it at a good second-year: define terms as you go, and no citations or reading lists — I want the argument itself.
