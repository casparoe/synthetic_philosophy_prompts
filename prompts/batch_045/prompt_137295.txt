I take part in a small reading group on Hofstadter's strange-loop account of the self: the idea, as we have been reconstructing it over the first few sessions, that what I call by the name *I* is not an extra ingredient added to the brain's machinery but a self-referential symbol mounted inside the very system it denotes, a symbol that gets activated whenever the system represents itself, whose content is something like the system as a whole rather than any part of it, and which comes in degrees — on our reading of him, people have more of an I than dogs, dogs more than mosquitoes, and there is no sharp line anywhere. At our last meeting a member of the group, a video artist by trade, said that the whole thing is much clearer if you think of pointing a camera at the monitor displaying its own output. The camera sees an image of itself seeing an image of itself seeing an image, the nesting runs off toward an apparent infinity, the loop has a threshold of total gain below which no stable image appears at all and above which the tunnel of nested images locks into a self-similar structure, and there is a delay at each pass that keeps the whole thing from short-circuiting. He then used the picture to argue for two things he takes Hofstadter to be committed to: first, that the self is nothing but a pattern sustained by an ongoing process, so there is no deep further fact about whether I persist and nothing about death to fear beyond the stopping of the pattern; second, that the I is causally downstream of everything the brain does — an intricate but epiphenomenal flourish — since the image glowing on the screen does not reach back and swivel the camera.

I would like an audit of this analogy rather than a verdict on Hofstadter. Please give the correspondence table explicitly: what plays the camera, the monitor, the image-within-the-image, the loop gain, the delay, the closure of the loop, and the threshold; and say for each correspondence whether it is doing real work or is decoration. Then separate the disanalogies that bear on his two conclusions from the ones that are merely true, and tell me, separately, whether each conclusion survives once the load-bearing disanalogies are on the table. Three worries of my own that I would like addressed rather than patted on the head. One: in the video case the self-reference is causal and pictorial, while the I is supposed to be a symbol that denotes the whole system; what would have to be added to a feedback loop for the loop to carry genuine aboutness, and does adding it cost the analogy its elegance? Two: the video's nested images are self-similar copies, but the I, on Hofstadter's telling, is not an I inside an I inside an I — the point of calling the hierarchy tangled is precisely to avoid that regress — so does the fractal look of the video picture smuggle back in the regress the view exists to avoid? Three: the gain threshold seemed to give his graded picture of selves unexpected support, but is that a structural parallel or an accident of the medium, given that gain is a single continuous scalar whereas having an I looks like it requires a rather specific representational achievement that does not obviously come in continuous degrees?

Finally, if the camera-and-monitor loop is the wrong picture, say what the least misleading one is and give the same mapping for it — a control system whose set point refers to the system doing the regulating, a program whose source code contains its own description, a you-are-here arrow drawn on the map of the mall, or something better of your own — and say what it shows that the video loop hides and what it in turn conceals. Please commit to whether the artist has Hofstadter right, and if the analogy turns out to be carrying one conclusion but not the other, say which, and why the asymmetry.
