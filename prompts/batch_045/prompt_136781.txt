My understanding of the standard story is that you get an arrow by adding a boundary condition instead — the universe starts in a ridiculously low-entropy state (I think people call this the past hypothesis) — and then everything else, memory and causation and the sense that one end is fixed and the other open, is supposed to piggyback on the entropy climb.

Two cases I want you to work through rather than gesture at:

1. A two-tailed world. The low-entropy point sits in the middle, entropy rises in both temporal directions away from it, and there are agents on both sides who all remember the middle as their past. One direction of time here, or two?
2. My actual sticking point. In an ordinary one-tailed world, a sealed box undergoes a big downward fluctuation: a gas spontaneously unmixes over the course of an hour while entropy in the rest of the universe goes up as usual. Put a small record-keeping agent inside the box. Does time run backwards for that agent? Do the states inside it that reliably carry information about the outside world point toward our future? Is the agent confused about which way causation runs, or are we?

Then make the call and defend it: either (a) the direction of time is one global thing fixed by the dominant entropy gradient, so the box is just a local statistical accident and its occupant isn't living backwards at all; or (b) the direction of time is local and agent-relative, so that agent really is time-reversed relative to us and there's no further fact about who has it right; or (c) something better — in particular, if you think the direction of time is really several asymmetries that normally coincide and come apart in case 2, say which one deserves the name and why the others ride along on it.

Please don't close by saying it all depends on what we mean by time: commit to one of the three and argue for it. Skip the generic preamble about the second law, I already get that bit, and no citations or reading lists since I can't check them — argue from the cases. Around 800 to 1000 words.
