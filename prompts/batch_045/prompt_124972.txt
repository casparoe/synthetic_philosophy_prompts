Next week I'm presenting at a joint stat-mech / philosophy-of-physics seminar and I need Q&A prep. My thesis is that the standard exorcism of Maxwell's demon — the demon has to record its measurements, it has to erase the record to complete the cycle, erasure costs at least kT ln 2 per bit, so the Szilard engine can't beat the second law — is incomplete: nothing stops the demon from writing each new measurement into fresh, previously unused memory and simply never resetting. My claim is that the cost doesn't disappear, it moves — a supply of blank, known-state bits is itself a low-entropy resource whose preparation costs what erasing would have — so the point is better stated as a resource-accounting claim about wherever the record actually gets written, not as a claim about one erasure step tacked onto the end of the cycle. The room: two quantum-information people who will want everything recast as a statement about state conversion under reversible operations, a statistical mechanist who works on fluctuation theorems and will want to know what 'indefinitely' means probabilistically, and six or so philosophers of physics who are agnostic and mostly want to know whether its an empirical or a conceptual claim I'm making. Give me the objections in roughly the order I'll get them, in each person's own vocabulary, with what to concede, what to hold, a reply I can actually deliver in about two minutes, and where that reply is softest. Include the one I'm hoping nobody asks — I suspect it's something about whether I've quietly swapped the second law for a finite-resources assumption, which would be much weaker than what I'm claiming. And mark clearly which parts of what you hand me are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who work on this, plus anywhere you're unsure of the physics yourself, because I don't want to walk in presenting a contested reading as the standard story.
