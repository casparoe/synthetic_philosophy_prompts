Ok so I've been chewing on this for like a year and going back and forth, and I want you to actually pick a side — not one of those "it depends on the details of the training setup" non-answers.

Setup, so were on the same page. In iterated amplification plus distillation (the IDA thing everyone on the Alignment Forum talks about, roughly the HCH-ish scheme): you take a task that's too hard for your current model, break it up into subtasks the model *can* do, have copies of the model do those subtasks, compose their answers into an answer to the original task, train a new model on that composed behavior, and repeat. The pitch is that competence compounds across rounds and alignment gets inherited from the human-ish judgments doing the composing and the decomposing.

Here's the distinction I want you to work with. I havent really seen anyone make it explicitly, but it feels load bearing to me:

Call a task *frame-closed* if doing it well never requires pushing back on the question itself — the question already fixes what would count as a good answer, and the ideal solver just fills in the blank. Call it *frame-open* if doing it well sometimes requires noticing that the question rests on a false presupposition, or is confused, or is simply the wrong question, and saying that instead of answering. Frame-open work is everywhere in real life: the doctor noticing the patient's self-diagnosis is wrong, the assistant noticing the boss's request assumes a result that isn't true, the model noticing the operator's instruction is based on a factual mistake, or that the goal as stated is trivially gameable.

The position I want you to argue: IDA can only compound *frame-closed* competence, and it actively trains frame-openness out, via two mechanisms that reinforce each other. First, the decomposition step is frame-closing by construction — whoever writes the subquestions has already committed to the top-level frame, and each subquestion is by design a fill-in-the-blank thing. A top-level move like "you don't actually want X, you want Y, here's why" has nowhere to live in the tree; there's no node whose output is "reject the framing." Second, distillation makes it worse: the training signal rewards producing the composed answer, and any node that balked, rescoped, or refused either got overridden upstream or dropped out of the final dataset, so what gets distilled is the shape of compliance rather than the disposition that generated the good answers in the first place.

Then say why this bites on the *alignment* half of the pitch specifically, not just competence. Argue that the situations where you most need the system to be aligned are disproportionately frame-open ones — the request rests on a misunderstanding, the spec is Goodhartable, the human is asking for something that will hurt them for reasons they haven't thought through, the operator is confidently wrong. So if the above is right, IDA buys you something that scores great on the distribution it was amplified on and turns into a very capable yes-machine exactly when the frame is broken, which is the case everyone is actually worried about.

Don't strawman the objections; these are the three I think are strongest and I want them engaged properly:

1. Just add "check whether this question is well-posed, and say so if it isn't" as an explicit subtask at every node in the tree.
2. The humans in the loop themselves model frame-open behavior all the time, so the amplification data contains it, and distillation only has to copy it.
3. Distillation trains dispositions, not just outputs: a model trained on answers produced by a process that involved frame-checking inherits the disposition to frame-check, the same way it inherits "do the arithmetic carefully" without anyone ever giving it an arithmetic-checking subtask.

Give me a real verdict at the end. Does IDA as described preserve alignment and competence, or does it run into a wall at frame-openness? And if you think it hits a wall, is there a variant that gets past it — bolting on debate, or recursive reward modeling, or adversarially chosen decompositions — or is frame-openness just not the sort of thing you can decompose your way to? Commit to something.
