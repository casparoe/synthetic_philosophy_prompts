Hey — I TA for a first-year seminar that got Frankensteined together out of an astrobiology class and a synthetic-bio class (enrollment was low, long story), and for the last session I want to hand out a 'which defintion of life are you actually smuggling in?' quiz. Every version of this I can find online just asks 'is a virus alive, yes or no' and then sorts you into boxes by whichever label you already liked, which is completely useless to me — I want something that sorts people by what they say about cases, and only afterwards tells them where they landed. Also, the group is a mix of biosciences majors and one very stubborn philosophy minor, so the cases need to do the work, not the jargon.

What I'm after:

- Start from the logical space rather than from a list of names. I think the separating questions are roughly: does being alive require self-maintenance/metabolism of some kind; does it require heredity plus the capacity to evolve by selection; does life have to come in discrete individuals or units of selection, or can it be a process, a lineage, or a collective; and is 'alive' a binary property we discover or a cluster/threshold thing we stipulate for a purpose. Use those (add or drop as you see fit), show which combinations of answers are available, then locate the real positions in that space.
- Leaves I want to show up at least: the working defintion astrobiology uses (a self-sustaining chemical system able to undergo Darwinian evolution); a self-maintaining-organization / autopoiesis-style view; a three-subsystem chemoton-ish view; a Darwinian-population or individuality-first view; a replicator-first / informational view; a metabolism-first or 'proto-life shades into life gradually' view; and a deflationary 'cluster concept / useful fiction' leaf. If a leaf has a standard label use it, otherwise write 'unnamed' and characterize it in one sentence — I don't want any citations or who-said-what, so please don't turn it into a literature survey, and don't hedge every leaf into mush either.
- Roughly ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the leaves. Give me a short blurb I can paste at the top of the handout too.
- Cases to build the questions out of, mixing real ones with ones I made up: a mule; a seed that's been sitting in a drawer for two hundred years, or a cryptobiotic tardigrade; a prion or some other self-propagating protein fold; a spreading fire front or a growing crystal; a HeLa cell lineage; a lab RNA lineage kept in a chemostat that evolves beautifully but collapses within the hour if the pump stops; a microbe from a hypothetical Mars sample that has metabolism and heredity but runs on some non-DNA genetic polymer; an ecosystem of self-replicating machines on a dead planet, built long ago by an extinct civilization, running on sunlight and evolving by selection but originally designed; a population of digital replicators evolving inside a simulation nobody is maintaining; and a biofilm mat that only ever reproduces as a whole, whose individual cells can't survive alone.
- No question may just be 'is this alive?' in disguise — each should force one of the distinctions above (e.g. ask whether the answer changes if you imagine the system persisting for a million years without us, or whether it matters that its chemistry isn't ours). Each leaf gets one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
- Finally, note the combinations in your space that no position occupies and say in a clause why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored), and flag any pair of leaves the quiz genuinely can't separate instead of pretending it can — I already suspect the cluster-concept leaf and the 'it's a stipulation' leaf will come out identical on cases, so tell me if there's a question that could pull them apart or whether I should just merge them.
