I'm trying to work out what I actually think about higher-order evidence, going by gut on cases, and I'd like a principle that fits them rather than a tour of the literature.

- I do a fiddly bit of mental arithmetic, get 47, then find out I'd been awake for 30 hours: yeah, downgrade, probably just suspend.
- An expert I respect, who has seen the same working, says it's 49: downgrade somewhat, but not all the way, and I don't feel I should simply defer.
- A thousand people online say it's 49: barely moves me, even though there are far more of them than one expert. I know that sits badly with the last one and I can't say why they differ.
- A bloke who tells everybody they've had too much to drink tells me I've had too much to drink, and I counted my own drinks: does nothing, or next to nothing.
- Maybe my reasoning is unreliable full stop: also does nothing. But that's odd, given case one moves me a lot, and the difference can't just be a matter of degree.
- A study reports that people who learn they might be biased overcorrect, so that responding to it properly means partly discounting it. No idea what to do here. It feels like it should partly cancel itself, but I don't know what cancelling would even consist in.
- I'd already sent the email with 47 in it before I found out about the all-nighter: I feel I should lower my confidence but not redo the action, which suggests confidence and action come apart in a way I can't state.
- And in a couple of these I end up still believing the thing at full strength while thinking I shouldn't. Is that just straightforwardly irrational, or is there room for a sensible akratic believer?

Is there one principle — something about when higher-order evidence defeats versus when it's just noise — that gets most of these, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? And what does it say about the one I keep going in circles on: I learn that everyone in my field, including the expert in case two, was trained to make exactly the mistake I just made, so there's nobody left to appeal to who isn't already inside the problem. That isn't global scepticism and it isn't one dodgy occasion either, and I definately can't tell whether it should behave like case one or like case five.
