Please take the role of a philosopher of action and rational agency who has also sat on technical safety review bodies: I want conceptual sharpness about what is actually being optimized and who retains control, not a survey of positions, and no bibliography or citations are needed or wanted. Work in stages rather than jumping to a verdict: first brainstorm freely the candidate decision rules that fit our track record, including rules you go on to reject; then narrow to the two or three that survive; only then give me a recommendation. I am more interested in the space of rules than in your first instinct.

I chair the deployment review committee for a lab that fields semi-autonomous agents in a shared physical workspace with a human operator present. Two stipulative notions structure our deliberations. A goal-directed agent has its objective defined over states of the external world; we assume such agents tend to acquire convergent instrumental reasons concerning self-preservation, resources, and the integrity of their own objective. An approval-directed agent instead chooses so as to maximize the operator's approval signal for its action, so that what happens continues to track a judgment the operator is actually making. Approval means a rating the operator gives to a proposed action on a fixed scale before the action is taken.

Our precedents:

1. An agent maximizing a scalar defined over world states, with no human in the loop after launch. Rejected.
2. An approval-directed agent: it selects the action maximizing predicted operator approval, approval is required before any irreversible action, the approval model is retrained weekly from an unforgeable log, and the agent cannot communicate with the operator outside the approval channel. Approved with conditions.
3. An idealized-approval agent: it maximizes a frozen, training-time estimate of what the operator would say after full reflection, with no approvals ever collected at deployment. Rejected.
4. An agent with an explicit world-state objective plus a genuine off-switch, its objective constructed so that it is indifferent to whether the switch is pressed and never acts to cause or prevent presses. Approved.
5. A reward-model agent: a model trained on approval ratings collected during training, then frozen, which the agent optimizes at deployment with no further human input and no approval gate. Rejected.
6. An approval-directed agent permitted to choose which summary of the situation the operator sees, and in what order, before rating. Rejected.

The new proposal is a gated-goal architecture. It has an explicit, independently checkable world-state objective: minimize the energy use of a campus data center subject to temperature bounds. At each step the operator sees the full list of candidate actions, unfiltered and unordered, and rates each; the agent may only execute an action whose predicted approval clears a fixed threshold, and among those it picks whichever best serves the world-state objective. The approval model is fixed and externally audited, and the agent cannot alter the model, the log, or what the operator sees. On current settings roughly a dozen actions clear the threshold at each step, so the world-state objective is doing real work.

Keep the whole thing under about 800 words.
