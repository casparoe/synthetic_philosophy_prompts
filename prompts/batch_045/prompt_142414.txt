for the last session of my early modern reading group I want to close with a villanelle in the voice of Descartes meditator, on what the cogito actually gets you. Refrain A carries the certainty: that I am, that I exist, is true every time I bring it to mind, and true in the bringing rather than by inference from a premise. Refrain B carries the catch: that the certainty is indexed to that act of attending, so the moment I look away all Ive got is a memory that a proof once happened, and memory is precisely what a deceiver could corrupt, which is what shoves you back to needing a non-deceiving god and so into the circle. Each return should land in changed company, so a reader feels every recurrence as something done afresh rather than something possessed; hard rule, no line may use a word that implies the thinker persists across the stanzas (no still, no remain, no again in that sense, nothing like it) — each I am has to be earned inside its own line. Someone who knows the performative vs the argumentative reading of the cogito, and why the memory move is the thing that lets the demon back in, should be able to tell which refrain is which and why they have to end up beside each other in the final couplet. Just the poem, 19 lines, no notes or explanation after it. British English please.
