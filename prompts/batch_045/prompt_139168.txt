hey so i need help with a thing for school and its not a normal homework thing, it's real, and i've been going back and forth on it all week and my brain is fried

I'm a junior and I'm one of two students on Northgate's Culture & Traditions Committee. It's 6 students, 3 teachers, and the vice principle (Ms. Devereaux) runs it and she votes only to break ties. We normally do dumb stuff — spirit week themes, whether the hallway banner can say "Hoco" — but Friday we're voting on what to do with the memory case and people are actually mad about it, like parents emailing the principal mad.

The case: a glass display thing in the main hall by the office, put up in 2011. Right now it has three names on a little plaque — Danny Ruiz (would've been class of '12, died in a car crash), Mr. Halloran (English teacher, died in 2011), and Simone Alvarado (class of '13, died in 2012) — plus a lacrosse stick, some photos, a program from the assembly they had, that kind of thing. It was put up by a teacher who has since retired and nobody has really been in charge of it since. There's dust, the photos are faded, one of the letters has curled up.

Now: last year the district got a mental-health grant and made a Wellness Committee, and they've asked us to retire it. Their argument, mostly from Mr. Brandt who's the new wellness coordinator: the school shouldn't be in the business of curating grief, a permanent display tells 900 teenagers that the expected thing is to stay sad forever, and there's research-ish stuff about how constant reminders pile up on kids who are already struggling. He uses words like "closure" and "closing the loop," and he said in the meeting that the kindest thing a school can do is let people be finished. Also: nobody currently in the building knew any of the three, so he says it isn't anybody's grief anymore, it's just a mood in a hallway.

Ms. Okonkwo (history, been here 22 years, she's the one who's loud about keeping it) says something different: a school either has a memory or it's just a building where classes happen, and the case is how we say these people were ours. She made a point I keep thinking about — we already have the yearbooks and the archive, so the case isn't storing information, it's doing something, and if we stop doing it then we've decided we're the kind of place that doesn't do that. She also says taking it down isn't neutral, it's its own decision, and we should notice that we're the ones making it. I think she got some of this from a book by a philosopher, I looked it up and it might be Avishai Margalit? I can't pronounce it, and honestly I don't need the book, her point stands on its own.

Complications: Kayla Mbeki, class of 2021, died in a crash last October, and her parents have asked for her name to go in the case. So we're not only deciding keep vs. remove, we're deciding who counts. Someone also started a thing to add Mr. Ferreira, the night custodian here for 31 years, who died in 2019. Half the committee thinks obviously yes, a couple of people including Ms. Devereaux quietly think its a different category, and now I don't know what the rule is even supposed to be. And Danny's older sister, who's like 30 now, emailed the VP saying she wishes the case would come down: her brother has been 16 in that hallway for fifteen years and that's not who he was. One of the other families moved away and has never been asked. Danny's mom apparently still comes by on the anniversary. So "the families" are not on one side and I don't want to fake that they are.

My idea, which I floated and it went badly: keep the case, but every year some student group has to sign up to be its custodian — they clean it, decide what goes in it, write a short card, and they're the ones who check in with the families. Sign-up deadline October 1. If nobody signs, it comes down that month and the contents go to the families and the district archive. My thought was that way it stays only as long as people are actively choosing to keep it, instead of sitting there forever just because removing it is awkward. Okonkwo hates it: she says making remembrance depend on whether a club feels like filling out a form turns a duty into a popularity contest, and that whether someone is owed remembering can't be the kind of thing that expires. Brandt likes it, which makes me suspicious of it, because I think he figures nobody will sign up and it'll quietly die in two years.

What I need: a written opinion I can put my name on and hand to the committee Thursday. A page or a page and a half, real paragraphs, since it's going in the packet — so not bullet points and not textspeak, but I still want it to sound like a person wrote it and not like a press release. Put the bottom line at the top — keep it, retire it, do the custodian thing, or something better you've thought of — with an actual timeline and dates in it, something the committee could turn into a motion and vote on. Then the reasoning. Then give the strongest version of whichever side loses, the way Okonkwo or Brandt would actually say it, not a watered-down version I can knock over in one line.

Two more things. One: tell me what you actually think, I'm not looking for backup here — I kind of want to keep it but I keep losing the argument in my head, and if retiring it is right I'd rather find that out now than be the one person voting no on Friday. Two: no quotes and no sources in the thing itself. We can't cite anything in the packet and I'm not going to pretend I read a book, so just make the argument.

Also Ms. Devereaux mostly wants whatever will cause the least noise, so if your recommendation is going to set off the parent email chain, say so and tell me how to handle that part too.
