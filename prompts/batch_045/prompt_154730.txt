Hi — I write novels (five out, I teach a craft workshop most summers) and my philosophy is podcast-level at best. So I want a translation job, not an explanation and definitely not a reading list. I can't look anything up, so please don't hand me names, titles, or papers I'd have no way to check.

Here's the disagreement I keep tripping over. Someone in my reading group pushes a view called tranquilism: roughly, that the only thing that matters is the absence of unpleasant conscious states — pain, yes, but also craving, restlessness, unsatisfied wanting — and that positive states (joy, absorption, the good bits) add nothing of their own; they matter only insofar as getting them removes or prevents an itch. Standard hedonism says no, positive pleasure is intrinsically valuable. If you think I've got the view wrong as stated, correct the statement first and then translate the corrected version.

My vocabulary is the one I actually use: tension and release, pacing, the itch to know what happens next, a payoff that's earned versus unearned, the page-turner versus the book you slow down and savor, flow, catharsis, the ache a bittersweet ending leaves you with, and the thing I tell students — value of a scene is roughly the size of the itch times the quality of the scratch. That last formula is my own and I'm attached to it, so take it apart if it's muddled.

Please translate the disagreement into that vocabulary, claim by claim, and then run the translation on these cases:

- the savor-book: almost no suspense, lots of positive texture. On the tranquilist side, is it worth anything beyond warding off boredom, and does the answer depend on what I'd otherwise be doing — lying awake restless versus sleeping?
- the spoiler test: if I already know the ending, the itch is gone, so on this view the book should be near worthless. But I reread books and get a lot out of it. Is that a counterexample, or does the view have a reply?
- manufacturing the itch: my whole craft says end chapters on questions, withhold, cliffhang. If only removed craving counts, is building the itch to scratch it a wash, a gain, or a theft — and does it change if the sequel never comes and the itch is never scratched at all?
- the unresolved ache: readers pay good money for an ending that leaves them sore and don't want it fixed. Is that pure cost on the view, or does craft talk even have the resources to say "an ache worth having"?

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want. What carried over cleanly? What only carried because craft words are doing double duty — I suspect "satisfying" means both relief-of-tension and a positive glow, and that my vocabulary can't seperate the two, which may be exactly where the philosophers are disagreeing. And what has no counterpart at all in how novelists talk? For each bit of residue, say whether you think it's a blind spot in my vocabulary or a sign that the original claim was less clear than it looked — maybe it isn't even introspectable whether a pure positive state with zero craving is definately something I've ever had.

Be explicit about confidence: mark where you're reconstructing what someone on the tranquilist side would say versus where you're sure, and tell me what would change your mind. And push back plainly if the setup is bad — if "suspense is craving" is just a wrong identification, or if my itch-times-scratch formula smuggles in the hedonist answer it's supposed to be neutral about, say so instead of politely going along with it.
