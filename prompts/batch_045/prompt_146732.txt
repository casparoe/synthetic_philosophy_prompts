I want you to write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two people who've been arguing about the continuum hypothesis for years and have finally agreed to do it properly. Side A: there is, in some sense still to be made precise, a single intended universe of sets; CH has a determinate truth value; the right response to independence is to look for better axioms, not to give up on the question. Side B: the multiverse picture is the right one, forcing shows there isn't a fact of the matter about how many reals there are beyond what a given universe fixes, and the whole "settle CH" project is something like a category error. I don't want a verdict on who's right, and I don't want a dialogue. I want the document they'd both put their names on.

Structure it as: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section, (2) a disagreements table, (3) a cruxes section.

For the agreed claims, start from my candidate list and fix or reject anything I've got wrong: ZFC neither proves nor refutes CH; forcing preserves ZFC and CH is flippable by forcing; the usual large-cardinal axioms are preserved by small forcing (the Levy–Solovay kind of result) and so don't decide CH either; L satisfies CH, and whether V=L could ever count as a new axiom is itself contested; CH is a claim about objects one level above the reals, where our shared intuitions are much weaker than they are for the reals themselves.

Two bits of vocabulary I want you to actually use, because I keep going in circles without them. Call a candidate axiom continuum-indifferent if it was motivated without any eye to the size of the continuum but pins it down anyway — the forcing axioms of the PFA/MM family are, I think, like this, since they come out of a genericity intuition and I believe they force the continuum to be ℵ2, correct me if I'm misremembering. Call it continuum-entangled if settling the continuum is part of what it's for, like just adopting 2^ℵ0 = ℵ2 outright. Then the question for the table: is a continuum-indifferent axiom that lands on an answer better evidence than a continuum-entangled one, the way a novel prediction is supposed to beat an accomodation in science? Or is that analogy doing all the work, and should Side B reject it? I want each side's own answer in its own words.

Second thing to stress-test rather than just assert. Here's an argument I half-remember and half-invented: since CH can be flipped by forcing, no sentence whose truth value is invariant under all set forcing can settle CH, because you could force CH to fail in an extension, and if the axiom's truth survives the forcing you get a contradiction. So nothing forcing-absolute decides CH. If that's right it's a real constraint: whatever settles CH has to be forcing-fragile itself, which sits badly with the genericity/maximality intuitions that motivate the axioms either side actually likes. Put it in the agreed claims if both can sign it, or in the table with the exact step one of them won't grant — and if it's just broken as stated, say where it breaks instead of quietly fixing it.

Third, I want a short section on whether they even disagree about the continuum. My suspicion: Side B's real claim is that "all subsets of the naturals" isn't a determinate collection and CH is indeterminate because the power set operation is indeterminate, while Side A thinks the power set operation is fine and the indeterminacy is only in our axioms. Call it the inherited-indeterminacy diagnosis, and put it wherever it belongs — shared claim or genuine disagreement — and say what would tell these two readings apart.

For the cruxes: give me three or four, each with something both sides accept as bearing on it and each side's prediction written down in advance. Some candidates: whether a multiverse view needs a background universe to define its own multiverse (I have in mind the move where you take the multiverse to be all the countable transitive models inside a background V — check that before you lean on it, I may be mangling it), or whether that's smuggling in the very thing it denies; whether convergence between two independent research programs on the same value of the continuum would count as evidence, and what each predicts about whether such convergence will happen; whether V=L being "restrictive" is an objection to it or just a description of one region of the multiverse.

Also flag anywhere the dispute is merely verbal, and try rephrasing it so it isn't. And no reading lists or citations — I've read the survey material. Names are fine only where they're doing real work; if you're not confident who argued what, drop the name and keep the position rather than inventing one, and tell me in a line what you weren't sure about.
