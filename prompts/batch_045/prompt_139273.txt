(1) There can be a moral reason against doing something only if doing it makes some difference to what happens; if what happens is the same either way, there's nothing to weigh.
(2) Our corner shop reorders in crates of 60 birds and bins whatever's left on Sunday, and nothing carries over to the next order. So whether I buy one pack or not, the number of birds raised and killed this week is already fixed: my one pack isn't going to move a crate boundary.
(3) So my buying it makes no difference to how much suffering there is.
(4) So by (1) I have no reason not to buy it.
(5) But (2) is true of every customer that week, not just of me.
(6) And the order gets placed because people buy; the crate is nothing but the sum of those seperate purchases. So the harm is produced entirely by a set of acts each of which is, by (1) and (5), fine.

(8) There obviously is something wrong with what's happening — and if there isn't, then each of 500 people throwing a stone at a man who was going to die anyway has also done nothing wrong, and nobody has ever had a reason to vote.
