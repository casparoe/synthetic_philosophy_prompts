Setup: three things that didn't exist in his lifetime. (1) A weekly injection, cheap and effectively side-effect-free, that switches off hunger and food craving altogether, and that most adults are now on. (2) A continuous glucose monitor plus an app that scores every meal and tells you what to eat. (3) A view that's currently big in one corner of the wellness world, call it appetite-as-oracle: a craving is a kind of non-propositional knowledge of what the organism needs, so systematically overriding your appetite is self-mistrust, an alienation from the body — roughly the inverse of the default rationalist line that hunger is a noisy signal you should mostly ignore or hack around.

Now drop Nietzsche into that world. Would he take the injection? Would the guy who spent an absurd amount of his pages on diet, climate and digestion think a society where everyone's on it is ascending or declining? Is appetite-as-oracle an ally, or just one more version of the thing he spent his career attacking? I want you to actually pick a side instead of hedging: does his contempt for ascetic self-denial beat his contempt for comfort and softness, or the other way round, and why? Where does the CGM land — is being told by a device what to eat a new form of the discipline he praised, or priestly self-surveillance in gadget form? And a seperate question people always skip: if he said yes to the drug, what would he say it's *for* — health, or something else entirely?
