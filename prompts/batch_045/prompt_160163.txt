Two colleagues in my department have been going at each other for about a year over the so-called gender-equality paradox — the finding that in countries that score high on gender-equality indices, women's share of STEM degrees (and more broadly the split between people-oriented and thing-oriented jobs) is *more* gendered, not less. One of them reads this as decent evidence that some of the gap tracks biologically-influenced average differences in interests. The other thinks the correlation is basically an artifact of what those indices actually bundle together and of how rich and secure a country is. They've finally agreed to do an adversarial collaboration, and because I opened my mouth in a department meeting I'm the note taker. So what I need is the document they should produce, not another essay about the topic.

Give me a numbered section of claims both of them could sign, and I want the interesting ones, not the filler — e.g. that a cross-national correlation is descriptive, not the counterfactual anyone cares about (the question is what the *same* girls would have chosen in a less egalitarian version of their own country, and no country was randomized); that a population of unconstrained choosers with even small average differences in interests can sort *more* sharply than a population where everyone gets pushed down the same channel, so more freedom and more segregation aren't in tension; that "egalitarian" is doing three different jobs — non-discrimination and equal treatment, material security and a welfare state, and a permissive self-expression culture — and the correlation might be riding on the second and third rather than the first; and that the indices partly contain outcomes (labour-force participation, representation) rather than just policy, which makes the direction of causation murky.

Then a table of where they still differ, each row phrased so that its own side would accept the wording: the crux, each side's formulation, and — this is the part I actually need — a test or case both would accept as bearing on that crux, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Candidate cruxes I'd guess at: whether the correlation survives once you separate equality from wealth, welfare-state security and postmaterialist values (does it hold within countries over time, or across regions, or only between them?); what happens with second-generation immigrant girls raised in a high-scoring country by parents from a low-scoring one — does field choice track the host country's level or the parents' origin country's level; whether the gap is moving in self-assessed ability and interests or in the costs and returns of entering a field (pay, hours, flexibility, hiring bias) — do female shares shift when a field's pay or flexibility changes, or when self-concept measures change; and whether the people-vs-things split and the STEM-enrolment split are even the same phenomenon.

There's also a justice question they keep sliding past, and I want it in the same document rather than bolted on. Suppose the biosocial story is basically right: what follows, if anything? Include as cruxes whether the target of justice is opportunity and option-availability or outcomes; whether preferences formed under background conditions that were themselves partly unjust can count as the person's own (the adaptive-preference move); whether there's any aggregative or externality argument left once everyone is choosing freely; and whether a state may nudge toward desegregation when the targets of the nudge would, by hypothesis, freely choose otherwise. And the mirror image: if the artifact story is right, does the paradox *help* egalitarians by showing there's no natural floor here?

Two more things. Say which parts of their fight are verbal — I suspect half of it is "nature" meaning genetic versus merely "not caused by discrimination," and "culture" meaning social versus malleable. And have each side state what it would count as evidence against itself, then tell me honestly whether anything either could actually get hold of settles the dispute, and if not why not. No citations, no reading list, nobody is going too look anything up — I want the formulations, not the literature.
