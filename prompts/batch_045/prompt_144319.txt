I work on compiler backends — mostly LLVM, years spent in register allocation, SSA construction, DWARF/debug info, SIMD and bitfield packing, plus the occasional decompiler and taint-analysis hack. No philosophy training beyond blog posts. Last week I got into a long argument with two interpretability people about whether a trained network actually *has* representations or whether that's just a convenient way of talking, and I kept feeling they were fighting over something I have a perfectly good vocabulary for and just couldn't hear it in their terms. Their anti-realist side said things like: the only thing the training process constrains is input-output behavior, so any decomposition of the internals into features is a projection we add, there are always many decompositions equally compatible with the behavior, and a feature is real only in the sense that it's useful for predicting or editing what the network does. One of them also pulled out the basis point — in the stretches of the network that are just a linear map, you can stick an invertible rotation in and undo it in the next layer's weights, the function computed is unchanged, but every direction anyone called a feature has moved. The other side insisted some decompositions are better than others, not merely more convenient — they support interventions that do what you expect, they generalize off-distribution, they let you say the internal state was *wrong* about the input rather than merely different.

Here's what I want. Restate that whole dispute in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can: binaries and machine semantics, the as-if rule and observable behavior, SSA values and def-use chains, register allocation and packing several logical values into one physical register, debug info that maps machine state back to source variables, decompiling a stripped binary, taint/provenance tracking, canonicalization and optimization passes that destroy source structure. Give me each of the following translated one by one, and be explicit where you're quietly helping yourself to something my vocabulary doesn't contain: (1) the claim that only behavior is constrained so internals are up for grabs; (2) the no-privileged-basis / rotation argument; (3) the realist reply that some decompositions are the ones you can intervene on and get the counterfactual you predicted; (4) superposition — more candidate features than dimensions — which in my head looks exactly like several logical values sharing one register with no instruction where any of them individually lives; (5) the claim that a representation can misrepresent, i.e. that an internal state can be incorrect about the world and not just causally different.

Then the part I actually care about, the residue report. Which of those five translate cleanly, which only survive after you distort them, and which have no expression at all in compiler terms? I have two specific suspicions I want you to press on. First, decompilation only makes sense because there was a source program: debug info is checkable against it, and a decompiler's output is right or wrong relative to it. Interpretability has no source — there's a training distribution and some I/O traces, and that's it — so I suspect the realism debate is partly a fight about whether a source exists to be recovered, and I want to know whether the fight even makes sense once you notice there isn't one. Second, my whole framework is instrumental: a compiler is allowed to do anything that preserves observable behavior, and nothing internal is ever correct or incorrect, only faster or slower. So tell me honestly whether that's a defect in my vocabulary — something real about representation that as-if thinking structurally cannot say — or whether it's evidence that the interpretability people's realism is thinner and less substantive than it sounds, and the debate is really only about which decompositions are useful for what. If it's the latter, say so; don't rescue their realism by smuggling a spec or an intention into my framework and then calling the result a translation.
