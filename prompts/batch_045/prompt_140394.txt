hey — I want to practice attacking teleosemantics, so I need you to play the other side properly and not as a strawman. You're a committed teleosemanticist of the etiological sort: you hold that the content of an inner state is fixed by the proper function of the mechanism that produces it, and that proper function is a historical business — settled by what actually caused that mechanism's lineage to be favored or maintained, not by what the state happens to correlate with nowadays, and not by what some ideal interpreter would read off it. You'll cheerfully concede that misrepresentation is real and common, that a few real cases are indeterminate, and that appeals to "normal conditions" do more work than anyone likes to admit. You will never concede that content is fixed by anything ahistorical. Keep it to about two paragraphs a turn, argue in plain American English, no citations or reading lists, just your own reasoning. If the case I give you is underspecified, fill in something reasonable and say what you assumed. Stay in character until I say stop.

Here's my opening. Stipulate a frog-ish critter with a snap-detector: the detector is there because ancestors that snapped at small dark moving things got fed. Now shift the world one tiny step per generation for ten thousand generations, until the descendants' detectors are being kept around because they catch something completely different — say falling seeds — and no fly has existed for nine thousand generations. My argument:

(1) On your view, the content of a detector at generation n is fixed by the selection history up to n.
(2) Selection history differs only slightly between adjacent generations.
(3) So the content at adjacent generations can differ only slightly, if at all.
(4) But the content at generation 1 (roughly: small dark moving thing nearby) and at generation 10,000 (roughly: falling seed) are not slightly different — they're just different contents.
(5) So somewhere along the sequence content jumped — yet nothing else in the sequence jumps, so there's no fact of the matter about where.

Either your theory has to swallow vague or graded content, or it has to say the critters are all still representing flies and getting their world wrong for nine millennia, which makes content look like a fake posit you invented to save the theory. Which horn do you take and what does it cost you? I've got a second case ready about an old mechanism getting reused for a brand new job, so don't dodge into generalities. go.
