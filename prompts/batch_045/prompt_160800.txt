hi — i'm a 3rd year phd student in corpus pragmatics and ive somehow become the person who breaks every tie on our lab's annotation project. The data is ~12k comments scraped from a hobbyist forum; we label each one for (a) does it contain a personal attack and (b) is it on-topic for the thread. Two undergrad RAs label independently and i adjudicate. Volume is brutal: 60–90 seconds per disputed item, ~40 a week. The RAs are sharp but have no theory background at all, so whatever i hand them has to fit in two pages and be runnable by someone who has never thought about anything. Stakes: the corpus gets released publicly, two of my chapters and someone else's paper hang off it, and a reviewer will definately ask why item 4021 is yes and 4022 is no — so i need a decision log, not vibes.

I fell into a rabbit hole reading about the sorites paradox (the heap thing: taking away one grain never turns a heap into a non-heap, iterate that, and there are no heaps) and it feels like exactly the right diagnosis of what keeps breaking in our manual. What i can't do is turn it into something my RAs can actually run. So: give me the procedure.

Things i think the procedure has to contain — push back if any of this is the wrong shape:

- a step that happens before any threshold is set, fixing what the label is *for* (the downstream use) and the comparison class. I've noticed our boundary slides depending on whether we're training a classifier vs. informing a moderation policy, and that feels like it should be a feature rather than a bug, but I don't know how to write it down so it survives contact with an undergrad.
- a rule about which items are allowed to serve as anchors. My suspicion is that items sitting right next to the grey zone look like clear cases but are terrible anchors, and that's been quietly poisoning our calibration. If that's right, say what qualifies as a real anchor instead.
- a rule about how *not* to reason. The thing my RAs do constantly is: if 4021 is an attack then 4022 has to be too, they're basically the same. That looks like exactly the tolerance move that generates the paradox, so the manual needs to forbid it in a way they'll accept. And then I need to know what to do when following the rule produces a verdict that feels wrong for precisely that reason — is that a bug to fix or a cost to just eat?
- the grey zone itself: when to declare an item penumbral instead of forcing yes/no, what to do with penumbral items at release time, and how wide we should *expect* the zone to be. If 35% of my data comes out penumbral I want to know whether that's normal or evidence my manual is broken.
- the regress problem. Any rule i write for the grey zone seems to grow its own grey zone. I need a rule that stops: at this point you pick, deterministically, you write down what you picked, you move on. And I need to know which of those stopping points are matters of fact and which are genuinely value calls I have to own — and whether the PI signs off on those or I do.
- a test for telling RA *error* apart from genuine borderline-ness when they disagree. We currently treat every disagreement identically and I'm fairly sure that's wrong.

Separately: several items are vague on more than one dimension at once (affectionate mockery, an insult aimed at a third party, a slur used as in-group reclamation). I don't know whether to threshold on a composite severity score or decompose into sub-features first. If you have an opinion, argue it, and say what the decomposition costs.

Then run it on three items, and please include at least one where the procedure gives me an answer I won't like:

1. A pair that differs in exactly one small respect, where the RAs split and the if-one-then-the-other pressure is strongest. Concretely: A is *that wiring is a crime*; B is *that wiring is a crime, did you learn to solder from a youtube short*. Thread is someone posting a photo of a badly soldered pedalboard.
2. An item everyone agrees is a clear attack but which sits one step from the grey zone — is it allowed to be an anchor?
3. An item where the two ends of the severity chain disagree about where the grey zone starts: climbing up from banter it reads as friendly, coming down from an unambiguous slur it reads as an attack. (Ours is a reclaimed slur between two users who banter constantly.)

Finally, be straight about what this procedure can't do — in particular whether my existing 12k labels are salvageable or contaminated, since that decides whether i have to redo the whole thing before i can write chapter 3.
