I am assembling teaching materials for an upper-level seminar on computational creativity, and I need a scripted dialogue that stages a dispute my students keep re-running and that I have never been able to referee. Please write it as a dialogue of roughly 1,200 to 1,700 words, with speaker tags, minimal stage directions, and no closing narrator's summary or verdict.

The dispute concerns Douglas Hofstadter's picture of creativity as variations on a theme: a creator works inside a style space with many implicitly known knobs; creative acts are turns of those knobs, sometimes small, sometimes very far, sometimes on a knob nobody had noticed was there; and turning a knob far enough produces slippage, in which the knobs themselves change what they mean. The opposing picture holds that some creative acts are not movements within a space at all: they change the space, adding a dimension that no amount of twiddling could have reached, so that no continuous path of mutually recognizable variations connects the earlier works to the new one.

The two speakers are colleagues who have co-taught this seminar for years and know each other's arguments better than their own students do. Rhea Vance builds generative systems that interpolate in learned latent spaces and defends the knob picture. Tobin Ilg works on open-endedness and novelty search and defends the discontinuist picture. They must be equally competent: neither may win by attributing a silly view to the other, and at least twice one of them must state the other's position in a form the other endorses before replying to it.

Rather than argue over real history, have them argue over material I have stipulated. The Vessan song-cycle is an invented tradition; nothing here is to be looked up. Every song in its nine-hundred-year corpus is fully specified, as far as anyone has found, by six knobs: (1) mode, one of four; (2) beats per line, any odd number from three to eleven; (3) drone, plucked or bowed; (4) the interval at which the second voice enters, which is a unison, a third, or a fifth; (5) what the refrain does with the opening line, which is repeat it verbatim, invert it, or omit it; (6) the ratio of sounding line to silence after it, which is 1:1, 2:1, or 3:2. Songs also have a schedule: a specification of which knob changes value at which section boundary and which knobs stay fixed throughout. The corpus contains eleven recurring schedules, and every known song uses exactly one schedule for the whole song.

Two recent finds drive the argument. The Verrat Fragment uses only attested knob settings and only the eleven attested schedules, but it selects the schedule section by section according to a rule: whether the preceding section ended on the mode's final, on the fifth above it, or on some other degree determines which schedule governs the next section. The Oss Fragment turns the entry-interval knob outside its attested range, to the just interval 7:5, and in doing so alters what the other knobs mean: with a 7:5 entry, a piece in the second mode is heard by competent listeners as being in the first mode, and the inverted refrain no longer sounds like an inversion of anything.

The dialogue must work through the following, in its own order, in a way that arises from the characters rather than from a checklist being ticked off:

First, the Verrat Fragment. Is a rule that re-selects the schedule at every section boundary a new dimension of the space, or is it the same knob set at a finer grain — a knob that was always present but frozen at one setting for the duration of a song, now un-stuck? Rhea should be allowed the un-sticking move and Tobin should be allowed the symmetric reply: that any genuinely new knob can always be retro-described as an old knob un-stuck, which would make the gradualist's claim trivially true.

Second, the Oss Fragment, where the knobs turn out not to be independent. The space is only locally a product of separate knobs. Both must say what happens to the distinction between moving within a space and changing the space if the knobs are not globally defined in the first place.

Third, the computational material. A system trained on the corpus reproduces every known song and interpolates fluently between them, but by construction cannot produce a setting outside the span of its training data; given an architecture that can add latent units, however, it can also represent the new dimension after the fact. Both speakers must say what this does and does not show about the nature of the act, and each should concede a genuine limit of the computational analogy. Note that each fragment cuts against the speaker who finds the other one easy, and neither may simply take the convenient case and ignore the inconvenient one.

Fourth, the retroactivity problem. Once a rupture succeeds, the tradition re-describes it as a variation: the 7:5 entry becomes just another setting, the second-order rule becomes a twelfth schedule. That makes the gradualist's claim look unfalsifiable; symmetrically, contemporaries experience many ordinary variations as ruptures, which makes the discontinuist's claim look cheap. Each speaker must answer this objection for their own side, and neither may answer it by asserting how things really are.

Close by locating the residue precisely: say what evidence about the corpus or about competent listeners would actually move the question — how quickly listeners come to hear a fragment as in-genre, whether later songs cluster near it in the attested knob settings, whether the slippage stabilizes or spreads — and say where the disagreement bottoms out in a choice about what we mean by the space, and whether that residual question is substantive or merely verbal. Each character should end having conceded something they did not concede at the opening. Do not settle the dispute by fiat, by a sudden conversion, or by the phrase that both are partly right.

Keep the dialogue self-contained: no citations, no reading lists, no further name-dropping beyond Hofstadter, and no real historical examples except in passing service of the invented case, where the other speaker is free to push back on the comparison.
