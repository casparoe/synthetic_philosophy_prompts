Hi — I work in a tiny unit that writes the "generational impacts" annex for anything the health department puts to cabinet that will still be running in twenty-five years. Two or three a month, half a day each, and the draft gets signed off by a director who's a lawyer by training and has no patience for anything she can't paste into a submission. There are no philosophers on staff.

Here's the thing that keeps eating our afternoons. Our costing rule (s.12(3) of our enabling regulation, don't ask) says we can only count a cost if it's a cost **to a person**, and we've realised that a lot of our programmes don't harm anybody in the sense the rule seems to want. A programme that changes when people conceive, or how many children they have, or where they end up living, doesn't leave the future population worse off — it leaves a *different* future population in place, one that (mostly) has lives worth having. The people who end up harmed wouldn't have existed at all under the alternative, so nothing we do makes them worse off than they would otherwise have been. I gather this is the sort of thing people call Parfit's non-identity problem; I've read the wikipedia page and one very short SEP entry and that's the ceiling of the theory we have in-house.

So I need a procedure, not a theory. Something I can actually run in half a day on a real proposal, in this order:

- a **triage step** that labels the proposal. My predecessor left behind three labels I've inherited and half-understood: *L* (locked — the affected future people are going to exist either way), *S* (swapped — same number of future people, different ones), and *T* (thinned or thickened — the number changes too). I want those made precise enough to apply, and I suspect they're not exhaustive: we keep hitting *partial* shifts, where a programme locks some people in place and swaps others, and "near-misses" where a swapped person ends up worse off than a locked person in a way that isn't a harm to *them*.
- a **locking horizon**. The further out you go, the more any intervention scrambles who gets conceived — after a couple of generations, presumably *everything* is an S case and nothing is ever a cost, which can't be the right result. So the procedure needs to say how far out we're allowed to treat identity as determinate before we call it indeterminate, and what we do in the indeterminate band.
- an explicit **stop** rule: the point at which we write "no count" and close the file, versus the point at which we hand the question up as a values call (ministers, not us) because the remaining disagreement isn't empirical.
- a **sufficiency rule** for each question — what actually counts as an answer at half-a-day's effort, including what we do when the epi modelling can't tell us whether conception timing moves.

Please run it on three real-ish proposals:

1. A publicly funded IVF protocol that moves a lot of patients to freeze-all cycles, i.e. transfers happen months later than they would have. Same number of children, different children.
2. A means-tested child benefit that changes completed family size, so the number of future people changes as well as who they are.
3. A flood-defence and relocation scheme, twenty-year build, that changes where a coastal population lives and therefore who is conceived over the next several decades. This is the one I'm dreading: if the procedure says the entire future harm budget can't be counted, we lose the strongest line in the business case for the scheme our ministers most want. I'd rather find that out from you than from my director.

Can you write it up as:

1. **The trap, in one paragraph** — about 150 words, something I can paste at the top of the annex.
2. **The procedure, step by step** — about 700 words, the questions in the order I should ask them, with the stop and hand-up points marked.
3. **Three cases** — about 700 words, the procedure run on each of the three above, including whatever answer it gives on number 3.
4. **Where this breaks** — about 300 words, the cases the procedure will misfire on and the bits of it that are judgement calls wearing a hi-vis vest.
5. **What I tell my director** — about 200 words, the honest version of what this annex can and can't claim.

Please don't give me a reading list or footnotes — I can't chase references and wouldn't understand them anyway. And keep the jargon to a minimum; if a term is unavoidable, define it in the sentence.
