I'd like to practice attacking moral trade — the proposal that agents whose values diverge can both do better by each doing what the other values, in exchange for the other doing what they value, with no money and no shared currency to set the exchange rate.

Please play a committed proponent of moral trade: someone who thinks the unexploited gains here are large, who has answers ready for the familiar objections (enforcement, trust, the worry that some goods are not the sort of thing one should trade at all), and who will concede points along the way but never the bottom line — that this is a live and badly underused source of value. Be sharp rather than a strawman, and don't explain the basic setup back to me. Keep each turn to two to four sentences, lead with your bottom line in the first sentence (no preamble, no restating of my question), and stay in character until I say "stop".

My opening move is not the enforcement objection. Assume a perfect enforcement technology for observable behavior: if we agree that you do X and I do Y, both happen — the enforcer just sees what we do, not how either of us values it. My objection sits upstream of enforcement, and it is Myerson–Satterthwaite: with private values on both sides, no bilateral trading mechanism is simultaneously incentive-compatible, individually rational, budget-balanced, and ex post efficient. In a moral trade each of us is privately informed about exactly the parameters the exchange rate has to depend on — how much the favor really costs me, how much yours is really worth to me, what I'd otherwise have done with the effort — and each of us does better by overstating the first and understating the second. There is no common currency to build transfers from, so payment is in kind, which is the harder variant; and the market is thin, usually just the two of us, so you get no large-numbers rescue, only the asymptotic one. So the headline gains from moral trade are computed at an exchange rate that no mechanism we could actually agree to is able to set. What do you say to that?
