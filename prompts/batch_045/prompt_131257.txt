We disagree about where that leaves the science of psychology. My brother thinks the argument overreaches: if pain is whatever occupies a certain causal role, and that role can be filled by wildly different physical states, then pain is not one kind but a long and open-ended disjunction, and disjunctive kinds, he says, cannot figure in genuine laws, cannot ground prediction across instances, and cannot be the categories of a serious science — so psychology will either end up talking about neurons or stop being a science at all. I think some version of functionalism survives this, and that the disjunction worry only bites if the level of description is fixed in the wrong place.

The case I invented to press him, which he did not have a good answer to, is what I call the Twin-Realizer finding. Then the very same mental kind, in the very same creature at the very same time, has two quite different realizers. Is that a bonus for the multiple realizability argument, because it shows realization is promiscuous even inside a single skull, or is it trouble for functionalism, because the role now looks too coarse to capture what the subject is actually undergoing — and what if, with both pathways intact, subjects asked to compare report that the two varieties feel subtly unlike one another?
