I am drafting the specification section of a paper on message design for receivers with whom we share nothing: no language, no conventions, no artifacts in common, no prior contact. What I need is a theory that says where the meaning of a message is located, and specifically one good enough to guide the construction of an artifact from which such a receiver could actually extract content, rather than merely to sort messages into kinds after the fact.

The starting point is the three-way distinction familiar from Hofstadter: a frame message, the bare fact that an object is a message and is meant to be decoded; an outer message, which tells the receiver what apparatus to build or how to set it in order to decode; and an inner message, the content that shows up once that apparatus is running. His own illustration is a phonograph record whose casing carries a diagram of a stylus in a groove: the casing supplies the outer message, the music is the inner one. I want the requirements such a theory must meet, and I want each one argued for rather than asserted.

Draft ten or twelve requirements. For each, give the case that motivates it, and mark it as non-negotiable or as the preference of one camp. Among the cases I want squeezed for requirements:

- the artifact that carries its own decoding instructions on the outside, versus one that does not. Does the theory owe us a verdict on whether an unassisted artifact can be a message at all, or is withholding that verdict precisely what a theory of this kind should do?
- contested framing: a surface covered in marks that one community treats as ornament and another as writing. What must the theory be able to say here, and is it permitted to say that the question has no answer prior to someone treating the object as a message?
- a receiver who decodes successfully by its own lights and recovers a coherent content that the sender never put there. Does an account that locates meaning in the message have the resources to call this a failure, and does an account that locates it in the receiver have the resources to call the sending a failure at all?
- the canary: messages that include a fragment whose only function is to let the receiver confirm that the decoding was performed correctly, such as a passage of known plaintext, a checksum, or an image of the thing the text describes. Is this part of the outer message, part of the inner message, or evidence that a three-way scheme needs a fourth category?
- recursive decoding: a message whose content is itself instructions for assembling the decoder for a further message. Where, if anywhere, does the scheme bottom out, and should a theory be required to say?
- signals with no sender: tree rings, geological strata, structure in the cosmic background. Must a theory of the location of meaning handle these, or is excluding them a legitimate consequence rather than a cost?
- hostile framing: an object engineered so that a receiver is induced to treat it as a message and is damaged thereby, a trap whose inner message is reachable only by building the apparatus that injures you. Does the distinction of frame, outer and inner survive being turned into a weapon, and should it?

Three things beyond the list itself. First, tell me which pairs of requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, and in particular whether demanding that meaning be recoverable from the artifact alone is compatible with insisting that it depends on an apparatus the artifact does not contain; if the list is inconsistent, say so and say which requirement should go. Second, identify the single requirement that does the most work in excluding candidate theories, and briefly score against it the three obvious families, a table is fine: views that put meaning in the message, views that put it in the sender's intention, views that put it in the receiver or in the practices around the receiver. Third, flag any requirement that is really a camp's metaphysical commitment dressed as a constraint drawn from a case. Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases behind them; a sentence or two per family on the scorecard is enough.
