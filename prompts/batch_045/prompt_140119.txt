I work in health outcomes research, and I keep running into a two-sided move that I don't have a word for. I want the carving more than the label.

Here is the pattern. When people's own reports about how their lives are going come out more positive than our measures say they should, the reports get discounted: people accommodate, expectations drift, they don't know what they are missing, so the report is treated as a symptom rather than as evidence. When the reports come out more negative than the measures say they should, they get discounted too, but by a different route: the distress is real but is said to be about the wrong thing — a status injury, an adaptation not yet finished, expectations set by comparisons that should not be setting them. Both mismatches get absorbed, and the measure never does.

What makes this more than ordinary two-directional suspicion, it seems to me, is that the two moves are usually made by different people. Clinicians, in my experience, discount the negative reports; the advocacy-minded discount the positive ones. Nobody is individually selective, but across a field a first-person report can apparently only ever confirm the measure, never challenge it. By contrast, when we trim both suspiciously high and suspiciously low answers out of a valuation survey, that two-sided trimming reads as ordinary rigour rather than as a trick, even though it has the same shape.

So: is there an established concept for the distributed version — a pair of individually reasonable discounting moves, each aimed at a different direction of mismatch, whose net effect is that no first-person report could count against the theory? If there is, tell me how it relates to the nearby things I only half-know about: unfalsifiability, confirmation bias, the idea that preferences adapt to circumstances, and the idea that some speakers are unfairly denied credibility. If there isn't, coin it, define it, and tell me what would distinguish a field that is doing this from a field that is simply applying two well-founded corrections, one in each direction. Please also mark clearly which parts of your answer are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who work on this, and flag anything that is your own coinage rather than something with a literature behind it.
