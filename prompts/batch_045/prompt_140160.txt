ok so I help run a tiny student philosophy podcast attached to our metaphysics of religion module, and next week's guest is a philosopher who's written a short book defending a Gödel-style modal ontological argument with one twist I keep loosing the thread on. Instead of taking positive property as a primitive, she takes excellence as primitive: roughly, an excellence is an intrinsic property such that having more of it can never make a being worse, and — this is the bit she thinks is new — the excellences are supposed to be mutually compossible, so that the step from each excellence being possibly instantiated to all of them being possibly instantiated together is meant to come for free instead of being a separate possibility premise you can just deny. She also drops the axiom saying that for any property either it or its negation is positive, because she thinks that axiom is what produces the collapse result (the one where the axioms seem to entail that every truth is necessary, so nothing is contingent at all), and she's made a bit of a name for herself by deflecting every objection with some version of you're not objecting to the argument, you're objecting to the axioms, and the axioms just unpack what excellence means.

Could you draft me eight to ten numbered interview questions for her, in British English please, and under each one a couple of lines on what she'd most likely answer and then the follow-up that catches her on that answer? Our listeners have done Anselm, Gaunilo's lost-island style parody and one lecture on Plantinga's possible-worlds version, and I've read the SEP article on Gödel's version twice and still can't tell wich axiom is doing the work, so please keep the questions pitched at that level and don't have her name-drop papers nobody will have read. I definitely want: one on whether the compossibility clause is just the possibility premise in fancy dress (and if it is, whether the argument is any improvement on just asserting that a greatest being is possible); one on the parody where you run the whole thing with defects instead of excellences and get a necessarily existing worst being — she'll probably say defects aren't compossible with each other, or that worse isn't just betterness flipped, and I want the follow-up to whatever she actually says; one on how, if she's dropped the axiom that every property or its negation is positive, she still gets a *maximal* being at the end rather than merely a very good one; one on whether her version still collapses modality or whether she's quietly become a necessitarian; one on the S5 step from possibly necessarily to plain necessarily, and whether that step alone is doing more work than all her axioms; and one on what observation could count against the argument, i.e. what would make her give up an axiom rather than reinterpret it.

Also — me and my flatmate had a proper row about this after the seminar — could one of the questions press her on whether the whole excellence machinery is just Anselm's argument with possible worlds bolted on, i.e. whether anything is actually gained by going modal, and could you make sure at least two or three of the questions are built so that her you're objecting to the axioms move gets progressively harder to use rather than her getting to say it once and coast? End with one question that's fair but that she probably doesn't have a good answer to, since I'd rather the episode land on her being stuck than on her being smooth.
