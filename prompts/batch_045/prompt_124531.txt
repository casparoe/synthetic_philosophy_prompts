Fix a date if I've mangled it, but don't make the timeline the answer.

So: read that 1 in 73 million through frequentism, then through subjectivism (degree-of-belief / betting-odds style), then through a Popperish propensity view. The bit I actually go back and forth on: call a probability auditable if the interpretation that licenses it also lets you, after the fact and for that one event, say whether the person quoting it was right. My sense is subjectivism can do that via proper scoring rules, frequentism can only do it by dragging in a reference class the event may not belong to, and propensity can't really do it at all since you observe outcomes and never chances. Is that fair to all three, is auditability a demand any of them should have to meet, and does thinking in those terms change how the case looks?
