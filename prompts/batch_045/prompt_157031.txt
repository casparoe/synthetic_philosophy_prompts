"There's this case where a company chairman is told that a new program will raise profits and will also hurt the environment, and he says he doesn't care about the environment at all, he just wants profit, so they go ahead with it. Afterwards people are asked whether he hurt the environment intentionally and most of them say yes. Run the exact same story with the program helping the environment instead — and he still doesn't care about it either way — and most people say no, he didn't do it intentionally. The only thing that changed is whether the side effect was good or bad; in both versions he foresaw it and didn't aim at it. So the only real explanation is that ascriptions of intentional action get hijacked by our moral reactions. People hear that the environment got damaged, they want somebody to blame, and intentional stops describing his mental state and just means he's the sort of guy who did a bad thing. That's Knobe's point — it's a bias, basically an optical illusion for concepts.
