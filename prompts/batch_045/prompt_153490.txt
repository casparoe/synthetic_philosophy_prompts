My case, which has recently stopped being hypothetical for me. My father died seven months ago. Since then I have re-read, perhaps a dozen times, one chapter of a novel in which a son sits with his dying father. What comes over me there feels like the grief I have in ordinary life: same object, my father; same belief, that he is dead; nothing pretended or hypothetical. But three features of it seem to leave your account nothing to work with. I am not supposing or imagining anything — not that I am the son, not that the scene is taking place. The chapter still works now that I know it well enough that I could not recite a sentence of it afterwards, so it is the movement of the prose doing the work, not what it says. And the grief stops when I close the book, which is exactly why I go back to it. So I have come to think the paradox of fiction is misdescribed: nothing in me is aimed at the character; the novel is a switch that turns on a real grief with a real object.
