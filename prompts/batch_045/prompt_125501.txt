No reputational consequences, no repeat interaction, nothing further hangs on it — paying now is pure cost. Question: is it rational for you, in town, to pay?

Here's the wrinkle I want to add, because I think people run these two together. Say the trait she reads at the roadside could be one of two quite different things:

(i) the akratic payer — the trait is something like not fully registering that the situation has changed. You pay because the fact that arriving dissolved your reason doesn't really land. She picked you up because you're the sort of person who doesn't notice.

(ii) the principled payer — the trait is a disposition to keep your agreements for a non-instrumental reason, and it survives you noticing, perfectly clearly, that every prudential reason to pay is now gone. You pay in full knowledge that it costs you and gets you nothing.

Is paying rational in each case? And separately: at the roadside, is it rational to want to have the trait — i.e. can it be rational to deliberately build up or preserve a feature of yourself whose whole job is to make you worse at responding to reasons later?
