I belong to a small reading group that has been working through Wittgenstein's late notebook *On Certainty* on our own, with no one leading us, and I have been trying to use what we read on a real disagreement. I should say up front that I am not asking for anything to read: no authors, no papers, no recommendations. I want the argument itself.

Two members of the group, Nadia and Peter, argued for most of an evening about a mutual acquaintance named Colin. Colin holds that the world may have come into being very recently, with our records, fossils, and memories already in place, and he therefore treats the age of the earth and the common descent of species as matters of commitment rather than evidence. I have reconstructed the exchange as faithfully as I can and numbered the moves. I genuinely cannot tell who is ahead.

1. Nadia: Colin's doubt is not a discovery, it is a doubt detached from anything it could disturb. Doubts need somewhere to stand: to doubt one claim you must leave in place the practices by which claims get checked. Some propositions are not supported by evidence and do not need to be, because they are what makes evidence possible — the hinges on which the rest turns. That the earth existed long before us is one of them, so the demand that it be proved is not a demand we failed to satisfy; it is a demand that does not make sense.

2. Peter: Then anything can be protected by calling it a hinge. You have to say which propositions get the status and why. Either the test is psychological — what people happen not to doubt — in which case Colin's circle has hinges of its own and is as well off as we are; or it is normative — what it is correct not to doubt — and then you owe an account of correctness that is not simply our side winning.

3. Nadia: Neither. It is a role, not a feeling and not a certificate. A proposition functions as a hinge for a given inquiry when it is what you would have to presuppose for anything to count as evidence for or against the question at issue; you cannot measure a ruler with itself. And that is not a licence: the recent-creation story is not a rival hypothesis at all, because a hypothesis earns its keep by saying that some observations would have gone differently, and this one is built so that none ever will.

4. Peter: You have explained why Colin cannot be answered, not that he is wrong. And you must choose. Either he has a different set of fixed points, in which case there is nothing to say to him and this has collapsed into relativism; or he has the same ones we have and is simply wrong about a matter of fact, in which case all the talk of hinges was irrelevant to the disagreement we were actually having.

5. Nadia: Both, and that is not a contradiction. The fixed points are not a list, they are a practice, and practices are shared without being statable. Colin shares nearly all of ours — he does not wonder whether the street outside is still there — and the sentence he dissents from governs nothing in his life; he is not certain of its denial in the way he is certain that his car is where he left it. So it is not a rival framework so much as a sentence he says. And the framework needs no proof: it is the one we are all already using.

6. Peter: The one we are all using is a headcount, not a reason. If the authority of the framework is just that it is ours, this has quietly turned into a sociology of knowledge, and my actual question — is Colin making a mistake, and what am I meant to say to him — has been dropped. Besides, you also said the fixed points can shift, like a riverbed. If they shift, they are revisable; if they are revisable, they answer to something; and we are back to needing to say what.

7. Nadia: They answer to the friction of going on, not to evidence. A framework that answered to nothing would leave you unable to act, to find your way, or to finish a sentence. That is a third position between groundless-because-unjustifiable and groundless-because-arbitrary.

8. Peter: That third position is pragmatism wearing Wittgenstein's vocabulary, and you should say so. And it costs you your reply to Colin: if tracking is measured by whether a community can go on, then a community that goes on perfectly well inside Colin's framework is in exactly as good a position as ours.

They stopped there and neither had moved. I would like three things from you.

First, go through the eight moves one at a time and say, for each, what is right in it, what is wrong or too quick, and where a move succeeds only if something left unsaid is added to it. I am not interested in who wins overall if that means skipping the moves that fail.

Second, say what both of them are missing: which question or questions have gone unasked, so that they keep talking past each other. My suspicion is that more than one thing is being run together here — something like the difference between how a certainty comes to be held and whether one has any right to hold it, and perhaps also between several things one might mean by saying a proposition cannot be doubted — but I cannot make it precise, and I would rather have your version than my guess.

Third, take up a case they never reached, which is the one I actually care about. Consider a conflict inside a single person's own commitments, between two things that each seem to play the hinge role for me: that my own memory is broadly trustworthy, and that the recorded word of others — instruments, archives, expert reports — is broadly trustworthy. Usually they agree. But suppose they come apart, and my memory of some episode is vivid and steady while the records flatly contradict it. Then I cannot settle the matter by appeal to either commitment, because each of them licenses one of the two sources and discredits the other. My questions are these. Is there any sense in which one of these commitments is higher than the other — a rule saying which hinge gives way — and if there is, is that rule itself another hinge, so that we get a regress or a circle? Someone in the group has suggested to me that a proposition is only ever a hinge relative to some inquiry, so that the same statement can be the fixed point of one investigation and an ordinary testable claim in another. If that is right, does it give Peter the criterion he was asking for, and does it stop the conflict case from arising — or does it make hinge-hood so cheap that Colin can claim one for his own project? And finally: is the case of conflict within one person's commitments structurally different from Colin's case, or is it the same problem in different clothes?
