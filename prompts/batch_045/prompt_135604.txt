For the past two years I have been sitting in on a metaphysics reading group at my old university. I am a retired solid-state chemist; I came to it wondering how philosophers handle the identity over time of the sort of thing I used to spend my career growing in vessels, and I have stayed because our convenor is unusually systematic. She has declined, more than once and with evident amusement, to say whether she is an endurantist or a perdurantist, on the ground that the labels matter less than the pattern of verdicts. So I have started recording the verdicts. Her rulings to date are these.

First: a seed crystal of potash alum, suspended in supersaturated mother liquor and grown over a season to ten thousand times its original mass, with the lattice orientation uninterrupted throughout. She holds that this is literally the same crystal at the end as at the beginning, notwithstanding that all but one ten-thousandth of its ions were not present when it was seeded.

Second: a man of eighty, none of whose cells were present at his tenth birthday, and who now has two prosthetic joints. Literally the same human being.

Third: the familiar vessel whose planks are replaced one at a time. Literally the same ship. The vessel subsequently reassembled out of the discarded planks is not the same ship, but a duplicate that came into existence later, however intrinsically similar it may be.

Fourth: a river whose water is wholly replaced every few weeks and whose lower course was diverted two centuries ago. Here she denies that there is any one thing persisting across that span; talk of the same river is, she says, a convenient way of speaking about a causal-historical sequence.

Fifth: the pilot light on a boiler that has burned continuously for thirty years, its matter wholly turned over countless times. Derivative in the same way: a process rather than a continuant.

Sixth: a lump of clay modelled into a figure and afterwards rolled back into a ball. The lump persists through both operations; the figure is a distinct thing, brought into existence by the modelling and destroyed by the rolling.

Seventh: a crystal dissolved completely in water and recrystallised from the same solution when the water was allowed to evaporate. A new crystal, she says; the old one ceased to exist at the moment of dissolution.

The case I want to put to her comes from my own former laboratory, and she has not ruled on it. A crystal is held in a flow cell across which a small temperature gradient is maintained, so that material dissolves at the warmer face and is deposited at the colder one. Over three weeks the crystal travels two hundred micrometres across the cell, turning over its entire material more than once while preserving a single lattice orientation and an unaltered external habit. The flow is then stopped and the gradient removed; the crystal, now threaded with dislocations, cleaves along a plane into two pieces, and each piece goes on growing as a separate crystal of the same orientation.

My questions, in order of how much I care about them. Will she say that either daughter is literally the crystal that had been migrating? If not, is the original best described as having perished at the cleaving, or as having continued in some weaker but still privileged way? And how many crystals were there in the cell in the week before the cleaving — one, or two that had not yet come apart?

What I would value more than the answers, however, is the principle behind them. I should like two or three candidate rules, each of which reproduces all seven of her earlier rulings, together with an account of exactly where they come apart on the new case; then a best guess at her verdicts, with the reasons for preferring it; and finally a further case — one I could pose to her at the next meeting, ideally drawn from something a chemist can produce — that would separate the surviving candidates cleanly. I should add that I have no interest in being told the dispute is merely verbal. If that is in fact the right thing to say, I should like to be shown why seven rulings delivered without apparent hesitation do not already settle it.
