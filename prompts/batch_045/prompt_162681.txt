I'm writing a short paper for a third-year philosophy of biology seminar (so you don't need to explain what the accounts are, but do say which version of each you're using — Millikan-style or Neander-style selected-effect, Cummins-style causal-role, or a contribution-to-fitness / goal-contribution variant — and then stick to it). The paper pushes one objection: if proper function is fixed by actual selection history, then the very first bearer of an adaptive novelty has no function at all, and that seems like the wrong thing to say. A friend in evo-bio says this is just Swampman-for-organs and that the selected-effect people have stock replies to it; I want a case that isn't only that.

So build me one. Specs: a single paragraph of realistic natural history — real-ish species you could name, no sci-fi, no engineered organisms, nothing where a human breeder or experimenter intended the trait. In it, a trait has no selection history whatsoever and is, right now, making a causal contribution to the organism's survival and reproduction that a working field biologist would call its job without hesitating. The verdicts have to fall out of the accounts' own formulations rather than be stipulated: selected-effect says no proper function at all — not "a different function", not "a former function", not "not yet, wait a few generations"; causal-role (or goal-contribution) says yes, function F, and it is performing it properly right now. Please design around the traps I keep falling into: my first attempt was a mule's hybrid-vigour heat tolerance, and it died three deaths — a mule is made by people, the trait isn't really novel (the etiologist just says the function comes down from the homolog in horse or donkey), and someone will say the trait is there because a human wanted it. So: the trait has to be genuinely novel, not a modified version of anything in the lineage; it has to arise with nobody intending it; and no exaptation (it wasn't selected for something else), no spandrels, no vestigiality, and no appeal to the environment having changed since some earlier selection episode — the novelty should be carrying the load, not an environmental mismatch.
