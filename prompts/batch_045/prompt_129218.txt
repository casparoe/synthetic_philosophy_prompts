I keep running into one shape of argument and I have no word for it, which is making it hard to think about. I would like the established term if there is one, and whichever way it goes, help carving the thing at the joints.

Three instances from the last year. First: a friend trying to talk his brother into forgiving their father listed some considerations — you would sleep better, the family would stop being miserable at Christmas, the grudge is costing you more than it costs him. The brother granted every item on the list and then said they were still the wrong sort of thing: reasons to want to forgive, or to behave as if he had, or to get himself into a forgiving frame of mind by some indirect route, but not reasons to forgive; and if he produced the feeling from those considerations alone, it would not be the thing he was being asked for. Second: a colleague told me I should be impressed by our new director, on the grounds that being impressed would make my working life easier and would show in how I spoke to her. Same shape. The consideration is genuine, it tells in favour of something, and yet citing it in favour of the reaction felt like a category mistake. Third: a man offered me a hundred dollars if by Friday I could come to believe his startup would work. Here I do not even want to say the money is a weak or second-rate consideration; I want to say it is not a consideration of the relevant sort at all, only a reason to want the belief, or to act as if I had it.

Two things I think I am not after, so you do not spend the answer on them. It is not the familiar observation that belief and admiration are not directly under the will — my friend's brother is not claiming he is incapable of working himself into a forgiving mood, and people do manage it. And it is not the point that practical considerations do not affect what is true; he never denied that his grudge was harmful. The recurring thing is narrower: the consideration seems to tell in favour of a neighbouring state or action while failing to tell in favour of the reaction itself, and offering it in deliberation seems somehow to spoil the very thing it is offered in favour of.

Two cases I cannot sort, which is why I do not trust my own description. If I tell myself to trust my co-founder because the company will fall apart unless we trust each other and she has given me no positive evidence of untrustworthiness, that has the bad shape, and yet it strikes me as a perfectly respectable reason. And if I tell myself that starting out admiring someone will make me notice merits I would otherwise miss, and that admiring her is therefore a good idea, is that the bad shape too, or just an ordinary case of a consideration operating indirectly?

So: does this configuration have a name — a label for considerations that count in favour of a reaction without bearing on what the reaction is about, or that look like reasons for wanting the reaction rather than for having it? If the nearest established concepts fit only imperfectly, tell me which they are, how each differs from what I have described, and which fits best. If nothing fits, coin the term yourself, define it, say what does the explaining (two kinds of reason? one kind plus a separate standard that reactions answer to? or not reasons at all, but causes and incentives wearing the costume of reasons?), and give me the boundary cases any definition would have to get right. I will not be satisfied with a bare label; I want the carving.

Please put your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of what I have asked.
