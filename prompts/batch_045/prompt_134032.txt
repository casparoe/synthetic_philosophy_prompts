I am revising a paper on whether Dutch-book reasoning can tell us anything about rationality outside a fixed space of possibilities, and a friend has handed me an argument I cannot take apart. He calls it the unbidden-hypothesis argument, and it seems to me to prove far too much. I would like you to find where it breaks.

Set the terms as follows. A book is a finite set of bets, each offered to an agent at a price that is fair or very slightly favorable by her own lights at the moment of offer, and assume she accepts such bets. An agent is book-immune over an interval if there is no book, offered in that way, that guarantees her a net loss in every outcome. I am happy to grant the two standard results: that synchronic book-immunity requires probabilistic coherence, and that diachronic book-immunity requires every change in credence to be conditionalization on new evidence. I know the diachronic result carries the caveat that the agent must be confident she will carry out her plan; I do not think the caveat matters here, but tell me if it does.

The case. Clara is a physician who partitions the possible causes of a disease into miasma (M) and contagion (C) and assigns them 0.7 and 0.3. She is perfectly coherent. A few years later she encounters the germ theory of disease, in which a mechanism she had never been able to formulate — call it K — becomes available. Her hypothesis space is re-cut, she acquires opinions about M, about C together with K, about C without K, and so on, and her confidence in miasma falls.

The argument, as he gives it:

(1) Book-immunity is a necessary condition on rationality. (2) Conservation lemma: if no genuinely new evidence arrives and the later algebra refines the earlier one, book-immunity requires the later credence function to agree with the earlier one on every proposition expressible in the earlier language — for if her price for a bet paying one dollar on P was 0.7 then and 0.6 now, sell her that bet for 0.7 and buy it back for 0.65 now (the margins can be made as small as you like), and she is down a nickel whatever happens. (3) A new hypothesis is not, however, a refinement whose division of the old credences is settled by anything: when K arrives, nothing in Clara's earlier credences and nothing in her evidence determines how the 0.3 she gave to C divides between C-with-K and C-without-K, so her new numbers are underdetermined relative to her old ones. (4) In the cases that matter the old hypotheses do not survive the re-cut at all: after germ theory, miasma is not a candidate that keeps a smaller share of her confidence, it is a category revealed to be empty or degenerate, and there is nothing left for a new function to agree with. (5) But if her old categories were that inadequate, her earlier confidence in them was misplaced, and rationality requires her confidence in every proposition of the old language to fall: the arrival of an unbidden hypothesis is reason to distrust the space, not merely to subdivide it. (6) So by (2) and (5) she is not book-immune, and by (1) she is irrational. (7) Conceptual change of this kind is not a rare accident — any agent who has not already arrived at the scheme inquiry would end with will undergo it, or would undergo it if she lived long enough — and the obvious escape is closed: a catch-all hypothesis of the form "the truth is something I have not conceived" either receives a sharp credence, which is arbitrary, or receives none, in which case her attitudes are not a probability function and the synchronic result has already convicted her. (8) Therefore rationality requires conceptual finality — occupying now the hypothesis space inquiry would terminate in — and every agent who has not reached the end of inquiry is thereby irrational, which is either false or useless, since a verdict that lands on everyone discriminates between no one. So one of the steps has to go.

What I want to keep is (1) and (2); the whole project was to use exploitability as a handle on rationality, and the conservation lemma looks to me like arithmetic. I am least attached to the brush-off of the catch-all in (7), though I cannot see how a catch-all helps when the old categories do not survive to receive a share. I have three inarticulate worries and cannot rank them: that the nickel bet in the proof sketch may not be available in the case that actually matters; that (3) says her new numbers are underdetermined while (5) says they must go down, and I do not know what bridges the gap; and that the bookie who runs this may need to be conceptually ahead of Clara, which would make the whole thing a version of the old complaint that being exploited by someone who knows more is not the same as being irrational. Tell me which step fails and why it looked sound, and price the alternatives for me — in particular, whether I can hold on to (1) and (2) and still have anything to say about conceptual change, or whether holding them means the Dutch-book framework falls silent exactly where the interesting epistemic action is.
