I'm on the advisory board for a mid-sized natural history museum, and we're stuck on the new permanent gallery covering the Late Bronze Age, the Classic Maya, and Rapa Nui. Our curators have come out hard against our working title — "Collapse" — saying it's factually wrong in all three cases and that it tells fifty thousand visitors a year that these people failed, when what actually happened is that settlements shifted, political systems got smaller and less centralized, the language and a lot of the religion carried on, and in one of the three cases the population crash came after Europeans showed up. Our education staff say the word is the reason anyone will buy a ticket and that it carries exactly the warning we want people to leave with. Two complications: the foundation that gave us the money has the word written into the gallery title in the gift agreement, and one board member has floated a compromise — keep the word, add a question mark, and put a live tally on the exit wall where visitors vote on whether each society collapsed.

I also don't think the three cases are even alike: my impression is that our Aegean material looks much more like a genuine break than the Maya material does, so a single verdict for all three seems wrong to me.

Draft me the one-page opinion I'll circulate before Tuesday's vote. It should say what we should actually do — keep the word, drop it, or something else — with a date by which the label copy has to be locked, and it has to take the curators' objection seriously rather than treating it as marketing fallout. One thing I'd like settled for me: are we arguing about how the word works, about what the word means, or about whether a museum ought to be delivering warnings at all? Because which one it is definately changes what we should do.
