Context: I'm an ML researcher and I think the taxonomy dissolves under pressure. Here are my two cases.

Case one, the surrogate critic. An RL agent with no world model it can plan inside. During training we fit a second network to predict reward from state plus proposed action; at test time the agent scores candidate actions against that critic and takes the best one. Nothing in the environment ever dies in the course of choosing: the agent never tries the rejected action and suffers for it. On the usual way of drawing the Popperian line — that in a Popperian creature hypotheses can die in its stead rather than it dying in theirs — this thing qualifies. But the critic itself was shaped only by real reward events, and wherever the critic is wrong the agent just dies. So is this a Popperian creature, or is it a Skinnerian one with extra machinery bolted on?

Case two, the well-read hermit. A model pretrained on essentially all of human text, then frozen and dropped into a single episode where it emits one action sequence. No feedback ever reaches it, it never revises its own hypothesis space, and no thought of its own ever dies. It is saturated with cultural tools, but nothing got installed in it by a cultural process it took part in, and it doesn't use the tools to build a better hypothesis-testing machine — it just is one, baked in by gradient descent. Is it Gregorian? Or is being Gregorian partly a matter of acquiring and wielding tools within a lifetime, in which case the hermit is a Gregorian brain stuck in a Skinnerian situation?

My opening move: both cases show the taxonomy is really only about mechanism, mechanism comes in degrees, so there are no threshholds at all — just a smooth gradient from hard-wired to culturally tooled, which my field already describes better in its own vocabulary. Tell me which of my two cases actually breaks your taxonomy, which one you think I've misdescribed, and where you draw the line that I'm claiming isn't there.
