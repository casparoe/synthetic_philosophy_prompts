My opening. I write backend services, and every contradiction I've ever actually met is traceable to a source conflict: two services disagree, a cache is stale, someone wrote bad data, the spec was ambiguous. In none of those cases do we conclude the world is inconsistent — we conclude our representation of it is. And the liar sentence looks like the same shape to me: the contradiction pops out because of rules we built into the language, not because reality contains a glut. So either (a) give me a contradiction that isn't attributable to conflicting sources or to a bug in our own rules, or (b) argue that even if all of them are like that, "P and not-P" can still be true.

One thing I'll grant up front so you don't take the cheap win: our databases already give up bivalence. Under the open-world assumption a fact that isn't recorded is unknown, not false. But that's a truth-value gap, not a glut, so it only helps you if you think gaps and gluts stand or fall together — and I don't see why they would. My own slogan, if you want to attack it: a contradiction is a failing invariant, not a fact about the world.
