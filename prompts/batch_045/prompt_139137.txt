Okay, some context first so you know where I'm coming from: I work in freight logistics, did two years of a physics degree before I quit, and for the last three years about six of us have met in my living room on Sunday evenings to work through one philosophy topic at a time, slowly, a few papers a month. We spent two months on the special composition question — when do some things add up to one further thing, rather than just being a bunch of things — and it ended in this strange flat silence where nobody had changed their mind and nobody was even annoyed about it. The positions we ended up with, as far as I can reconstruct them: nothing ever really composes, there are only the smallest bits (that's the nihilist end); everything composes, always — any random collection whatsoever, including my left shoe and the Eiffel Tower, is already a thing (I think that's Lewis's universalism); and a middle view where composition happens only when the parts are doing something unified enough, the famous version being that it happens only when the activity of the parts constitutes a life (van Inwagen, I'm fairly sure). Then there are the two arguments nobody could shake: the problem of the many, where a cloud or a mountain seems to have no principled edge and so seems to be either everything or nothing, and the vagueness worry, that if it's vague whether some things compose then there's a vague fact about what exists, which people said was impossible and other people said was fine. And one guy in the group, Dave, got fed up and proposed a new middle position: composition happens exactly when the parts are locked into a self-maintaining causal loop — a flame, a cell, a hurricane, an ecosystem — so the boundary of an object is the boundary of a process that keeps itself going.

My hypothesis, which I want you to push on hard, is that the whole thing is verbal and we were wasting our Sundays. It feels to me like everyone at the table actually agrees about the furniture of the world and is just disagreeing about which bits of it we're licensed to count with the word "exists" — so the nihilist and the universalist aren't really fighting, they're using "thing" with different cutoffs, and once you say that, the problem of the many dissolves too, because there's no fact of the matter about how many clouds there are, only a decision about how to talk. But I can't shake the feeling that this is too cheap, because everyone in the room would have rejected it, and I want to know why they'd be right to.
