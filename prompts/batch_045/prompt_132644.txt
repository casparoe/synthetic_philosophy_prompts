hey, I need a piece of satire for a reading-group thing — please write it in American English, casual is fine, aim for about 700-800 words.

Write it as a leaked technical due-diligence report from a consulting firm (invent one; deadpan corporate voice) that was hired to audit the human genome as if it were a codebase a client is thinking of aquiring. Give it section headings, severity ratings on the findings (Critical / High / Medium), a Recommendations section at the end, and a short appendix on why the semantic part of the audit could not be completed.

The firm grades candidate "codes" under an invented in-house standard, the Semiotic Compliance Framework v3: C1 is a mere mapping between two sets of physical states, C2 is a mapping plus a decoder that isn't itself part of the message, C3 is a mapping with an author and an intended reading. The client wants C3. The joke is that the auditors keep trying to promote the genome grade by grade while their own findings block them every time.

What I care about most is that the humor comes from the actual biology and the actual philosophy, not from "wow, spaghetti code lol." Each finding should break one specific software analogy:

- the decoder problem: the tRNAs, synthetases and ribosomes that do the "reading" are themselves encoded in the thing being read, so there's no compiler or runtime outside the machine
- the instruction set isn't fixed: the same codon gets read differently depending on context (recoding, or the variant code used in our own mitochondria)
- the same stretch of sequence gets read in different frames, spliced different ways, or serves as part of two overlapping genes, so one string has several "meanings" depending on cellular context
- there's proofreading and repair, but nothing checks the sequence against a specification, because there is no specification
- most of the genome isn't coding sequence at all, plus the fight over "junk" DNA after one of the big genomics consortia announced that most of it is functional
- transposons: pieces of the "source" that copy themselves around, the one place the software metaphor sort of works and also the place where it turns out the author has no interest in the organism
- the author of record is listed as Selection, Inc., which is not an author — no intentions, no foresight, no design documents, just a filter

Two rules: nobody in the report is allowed to say "it's just a metaphor," and the report should never quite admit out loud that DNA isn't a code — but a reader should finish it knowing exactly why "the code of life" talk is doing work it hasn't paid for. The target is that style of genomic discourse, not any person. Aim it at someone who knows the molecular biology and is tired of the whole "book of life" thing; they should definately feel the satire is fair rather than that I invented a dumb version of the view.
