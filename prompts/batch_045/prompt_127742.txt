Someone in my reading group uses the same move every time the question comes up whether LLMs understand anything: text-only training is just the classic "learning Chinese from a Chinese-only dictionary" case — every word is explained by more words, nothing ever touches the world, so the whole symbol system floats free. He uses it to get two conclusions: (a) today's models don't mean anything by their tokens, and (b) more text can never fix that, only sensors and acting in an environment can.

I think there's something to it but I can't tell where it breaks. Three things I keep circling on:

- a monolingual dictionary really is a closed circle of paraphrase relations, but a training corpus was written by people who were looking at and doing things, so the text isn't sealed off from the world the way a dictionary is — it's more like a shadow cast by it
- I invented a case to test him with: strap a geiger counter to the model's input channel and let it predict click streams alongside text. Now there's a genuinely non-symbolic transducer in the loop. Is "click" grounded and "democracy" not? Does the analogy have any principled way to say that's the wrong answer?
- words like "if", "electron" or "inflation" don't seem to get grounded by anyone pointing at anything, so grounding can't be sensorimotor all the way down even for us

Can you audit the analogy: give the correspondence table (what plays the role of the entry, the definition, the learner, the world), say which features it smuggles in from the dictionary case that a language model doesn't have, which of my three points actually bite versus which are red herrings, and whether (a) and (b) survive separately — I suspect one does and the other doesn't. If there's a better analogy for whatever is missing in text-only training, say what it is.
