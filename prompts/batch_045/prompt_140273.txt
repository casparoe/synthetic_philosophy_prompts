Im writing a piece for a science-of-ideas column and I want to run the 2006 IAU vote on what counts as a planet through Hofstadter's stuff on fluid concepts and conceptual slippage — the letter-string microworld, slippability hierarchies, the temperature parameter that governs how randomly the program commits to a reading. Treat "planet" as a concept under pressure the way "the rightmost letter" is under pressure in abc → abd: what's the conservative reading, what's the high-slippage one, and which one actually won in Prague? Then the part I care about. I've been kicking around a notion I'm calling co-slippage: when one concept slips in a tightly coupled neighborhood, its neighbors have to slip too or the local web goes incoherent. Pluto dragging Eris, Ceres and Charon along with it looks like a textbook case — but is that notion doing work beyond the bland holism claim that meanings are interdependent, or is there something real in it? And what does the episode do to the theory? Hofstadter's cases are one-shot puzzles settled in a millisecond by a single architecture; this is a centuries-old public category settled by a show of hands with telescope time and textbooks riding on it. Say where fluid-concept theory just goes quiet — and is it quiet on whether we should have kept Pluto, or is there a recommendation in there that doesnt boil down to "be less rigid"? Skip the "Pluto was always a Kuiper Belt object" line, I know that one.
