hey, I could use a sanity check before I hard-code a bunch of agents. I'm TAing a small seminar on rationality and choice — mostly econ-adjacent undergrads plus a few philosophy people — and I'm building a toy simulation where they each have to pick an apartment. 60 listings, each with 15 binary features, true value of a listing is a weighted sum of its features with the weights drawn i.i.d. from an exponential (so two or three features do almost all the work and most barely matter), features mildly positively correlated within a listing, and the agent knows all of that generative structure — the distribution, the correlations — but not the realized weights. Every single cell of the 60×15 matrix costs 0.1 "match points" to look up, the whole thing is rescaled to 0–100, you have 150 lookups, and your final score is the true value of the listing you pick minus what you spent looking. So it's a real search-cost problem, not just a hard cap. If any parameter there is underspecified for something you want to say, just pick a sensible value and tell me which one you picked.

I've sketched seven procedures and I want them rated, not just discussed. Give each one (a) a number 0–100 for expected net score as a percentage of what the best possible procedure with the same budget and the same information would get — so 100 means "nothing does better", and (b) a number 0–10 for how much it's genuinely *satisficing* as opposed to just expected-utility maximization with a search-cost term and a fake moustache: 10 = a real threshold/aspiration thing that can't be reconstructed as optimization, 0 = obviously just maximizing in a trenchcoat. And flag which two or three numbers you're most unsure about, because I'd rather know where your guesses are soft:

- myopic value-of-information: each step look up the single cell with the highest expected gain given your current posterior, stop when no cell is worth 0.1, then take argmax of posterior mean value over everything you've seen
- fixed aspiration: set the bar at the 85th percentile of your prior over listing values, go through listings in random order, look up that listing's features in decreasing order of expected weight, bail the moment you know it clears the bar or the moment you know it can't, never revisit a listing
- declining aspiration: same, but the bar starts at the 95th percentile and drops five percentile points for every 10 lookups you've burned
- elimination by aspects: take the highest-expected-weight feature, look it up for every surviving listing, throw out everyone who's 0 on it, repeat with the next feature, until one listing is left or the budget dies (then take the best surviving posterior mean)
- winner-stays-on: draw two listings, look up all 15 features of each, keep the better one, then keep challenging it with a fresh random listing — full 15-feature profile each time — and switch only if the challenger wins
- screen-then-drill: spend the first quarter of the budget sampling single cells more or less at random across lots of listings, rank everything by posterior mean on what little you've seen, then spend the rest drilling into the top three
- regret-threshold: take the first listing for which your posterior probability that *some unseen listing beats it by 5 points or more* falls below 20% — a threshold rule, but the threshold is on expected regret rather than on value

Two more things. My co-TA insists that the three aspiration-flavoured ones (fixed, declining, regret-threshold) are all just maximization once you price the searching properly, and that only winner-stays-on and screen-then-drill are doing anything a straight optimizer wouldn't do — where does that go wrong, and does your 0–10 scale back him up or not? And: which pairs swap places in the performance ordering if (i) I triple the budget, (ii) I flatten the weights so all 15 features matter about equally, or (iii) the listings are generated by a platform that knows your procedure and is tuned to get you to sign a high-commission lease rather than a good one? I mostly care about the reasoning behind each number, but I do want the numbers.
