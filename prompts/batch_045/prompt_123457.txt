I am the honorary secretary of the Association of Regional Archivists, a learned society of about 2,300 members founded in 1911, and one part of the job I have no training for is this: within ten days of the deadline for motions each spring, I must give our governing Council a written recommendation on every proposed amendment to our Rules — whether the motion is in order, and under which of our amendment procedures it must be voted. There are usually twelve to twenty motions a year, nearly all housekeeping, but every few years one turns on the *shape* of the Rules rather than their content, and those are the ones that get argued from the floor of the annual general meeting, where I have to defend my recommendation cold, in front of members who are archivists rather than lawyers. We can afford an opinion from the Association's solicitors about once every two years, so most of the time my triage stands or falls on its own reasoning; and the stakes are not theoretical, because a motion passed under the wrong procedure is void, and an object clause amended in a way the charity regulator won't accept can put our charitable registration at risk.

The difficulty is that our Rules seem to be arranged in a shape I have no word for. Rule 41 is the ordinary amendment rule: any Rule may be amended by a two-thirds majority of those voting at an annual general meeting, given sixty days' notice. Rule 42, inserted in 1974, provides that notwithstanding anything else in the Rules, Rule 1 (our name) and Rule 3 (our objects) may be amended only by a three-quarters majority at an AGM followed by the assent of two-thirds of our regional branches meeting separately. Rule 42 is not itself inside its own protected list, so it appears to be amendable — or repealable outright — under ordinary Rule 41. And Rule 43, inserted at the same time and likewise unprotected, says that Rule 42 ceases to have effect fifty years after its adoption unless reaffirmed by a two-thirds vote at an AGM; at our 2024 AGM the reaffirmation motion was carried by 61 per cent, the officers split publicly over whether that was enough, and the question has never been settled. So the rule that limits entrenchment is a weaker rule than the entrenchment it limits, while the entrenchment declares itself immune to everything else — each, so far as I can tell, purporting to be the hand that draws the other.

A friend who reads philosophy tells me this has a name: a tangled hierarchy, the thing pictured in Escher's lithograph of two hands each drawing the other, and that what such systems lack is an *inviolate level* — a level genuinely outside the system, which the system cannot reach or rewrite from inside, and which therefore stops the regress of "who authorises the authoriser." I believe he said the phrase is Hofstadter's, but I may be misremembering, and I would rather you not lean on the source at all: take the idea only as I have sketched it, roughly that no rule can confer authority on itself and that a hierarchy whose levels fold back into one another has no stopping point inside itself. What I need is that idea made into something I can run on a motion in about twenty minutes with the Rules in front of me. Please write me a triage procedure: the questions to ask, in the order I should ask them; what counts as a sufficient answer to each; the point at which the procedure simply stops with a no; and the point at which it hands the decision to a person or to Council because the remaining question is one of politics or values rather than of the Rules. Include a test for the indirect route — a motion that never formally touches a protected Rule but changes the Rule that defines the protection, or achieves in practice what it could not achieve directly — because I can see members attempting exactly that. The procedure must be in plain English, fit in two pages, and be usable by someone who is not a lawyer, since it will be read out loud at the AGM by people looking for loopholes. Then run it on the three live motions before me: (a) a Membership Committee motion to lower Rule 41's threshold from two-thirds to a simple majority; (b) an Ethics and Standards Committee motion to add a new Rule 44 providing that the Association shall not endorse or oppose candidates for public office and that this Rule shall not be repealed or amended — they want it entrenched, and I want it entrenched too, so tell me plainly if the wording cannot do the work they think it does; and (c) a motion to change our name from "Provincial" to "Regional," which I personally favour for recruitment reasons and which therefore needs the least sympathetic reading you can honestly give it.

Finally, two structured deliverables I can paste into my report. First, a table of the procedure's steps with these columns: Step | Question asked | What counts as a sufficient answer | Verdict if the answer is not forthcoming | Judgment call? (yes/no, and why) | Who signs off. Second, for each of the three motions, a JSON object with these fields: motion_id (a, b or c); classification (one of: ordinary, amendment-of-the-amendment-rule, purported_self_entrenchment, externally_anchored, unsettled); governing_rule; verdict (one of: in_order, in_order_with_conditions, not_in_order, refer_to_council); one_sentence_reason; what_I_wanted_versus_what_the_procedure_gives; judgment_calls (an array); escalate_to; residual_risk. And after the worked applications, tell me what the procedure cannot do — the cases it will systematically misfire on, and which of my questions it has no business pretending to answer.
