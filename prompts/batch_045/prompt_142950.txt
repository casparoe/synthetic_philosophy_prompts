Here's my opening, two prongs, tell me which is the weak one. First: this looks like an induction from one exotic solution. Sure, in Gödel's model there's no global cosmic time — but that's a model with rotating dust and a cosmological constant tuned against it, and our universe isn't like that; in the standard cosmology the matter distribution itself picks out a preferred foliation (comoving slices, and you can operationally find that frame by the CMB dipole), so there's an objective cosmic time here after all, and the radical conclusion is an artifact of a toy model. Second, and this is the bit I actually care about: even if you take away a global 'now', I don't think you've touched becoming, because I want to locate becoming locally rather than globally — my proposal is that what it is for time to pass for an observer is just that records accumulate along their worldline, i.e. that there's an asymmetry between the traces the world contains of one direction and of the other, so a local arrow of records is enough for real temporal passage and it survives the loss of a global slicing intact. Take both apart.
