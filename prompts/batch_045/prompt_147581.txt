The ratchet asymmetry (my term, define it in the dialogue however you like): steps toward centralization are much easier to take than to reverse, because the gains from coordination show up immediately and land on identifiable people, while the costs of dissolution are delayed, diffuse and mostly borne by people who can't vote yet. Cosmetic multipolarity (Dev's worry): the number of member states, vetoes and formal veto-holders is a terrible proxy for where power actually sits, and it's a proxy that a would-be singleton can game — you end up with the reality of a singleton and the accountability of a committee, which is worse than an honest one.
