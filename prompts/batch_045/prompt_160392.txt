Hey — I'm one of the five members of my city's Board of Zoning Adjustment, and I need a draft of the written opinion I'm going to circulate before our hearing on the 12th. Keep it to something I could actually hand to people (two pages-ish), and make it come down on one side.

Background: last year the council passed the Block Choice Ordinance — everyone calls it 'little local option.' It lets the households on a single block face (contiguous parcels on one side of a street) petition to rezone their own parcels, up or down. It needs signatures from owners of 60% of the parcels, plus a separate 'resident assent' vote in which renters count as half a household; that half-weight bit was a compromise the tenants' union forced through at about 2am. The Board has to grant the petition unless it finds that the change would cause 'substantial harm to the distinctive interests of households outside the petitioning block face' — a phrase nobody defined and which is now doing all the work.

The case: the east block face of Halsey between 4th and 5th — 20 parcels, mostly 1920s duplexes plus one legal-nonconforming six-flat. Fourteen owners signed. The resident assent vote passed 16–4, but if renters counted as full households it would have failed 11–9. They're asking for R-4, fourplexes by right. Two of the signing owners are landlords who've already filed preliminary plans for fourplexes; a community land trust holds a purchase option on two other parcels that expires in 90 days and says rezoning will push assessed land value past its acquisition budget. The ordinance lets a block face re-petition every three years, so anything we grant can be taken back in 2028 — the land trust says it can't finance against that horizon, which raises whether we should attach some kind of durability or vesting condition, and whether we even have the authority to.

The opposition is the Rowan Street Association, roughly 140 households on the two cross streets and the opposite face. They say the block face is the wrong unit: street parking, the elementary school catchment (enrolled at 102% of capacity), and the combined sewer all spill over onto them, and they get no vote at all. Their strongest version, I think, is the all-affected principle from democratic theory — everyone with a stake gets a say — and here the people with the biggest stake are outside the voting unit by construction, since what a block face exports is exactly the spillover. Complicating it: the four dissenters on the block face include three renters in the six-flat, there's no relocation assistance anywhere in the ordinance, and the six-flat's owner has said he'd 'evaluate his options' if the zoning changes. Complicating it the other way: the state housing agency has tied an $11M sewer-and-streets grant to the city demonstrating net added residential capacity by June 30, and staff say Halsey is the only project that can plausibly be permittable in time.

The thing I keep going back and forth on, and what I most want the opinion to take a position on, is objector consistency — call it zoning clean hands. The Rowan Street Association has formally opposed every upzoning in this city for six years, including two clear across town, and it filed the appeal that killed a 40-unit project last year. Should that record discount their standing now? My instinct is yes: a group that has spent six years pushing housing onto other neighborhoods shouldn't get full-weight sympathy the moment the spillover lands next door. But I also think that argument, once we endorse it, turns us into a board that keeps a scorecard on the parties instead of judging the merits, and it's available in reverse too, since the city has downzoned plenty and will again.

So: draft the opinion. Recommend one of — grant; grant with conditions (say which, who enforces them, and by when); deny; or deny without prejudice and remand for a vote across a wider affected area. Give a real bottom line with dates, and give the strongest version of whatever loses: if you land on granting, state the Rowan Street spillover-and-standing objection, and the renter-assent problem, as well as the association's own lawyer would, and tell me what you'd concede; if you land on denying, do the same for the grant clock and the clean-hands argument. Don't send me cases, citations or a reading list — I can't look anything up and staff won't read them anyway. Just the reasoning, in plain language, with the judgement I'm supposed to defend on the 12th.
