I am writing a workshop paper on mechanistic anomaly detection — specifically on what licenses a researcher to say that a deviant system is running the same mechanism as the paradigm system, only wrongly, rather than running a different mechanism altogether — and I want to rehearse my central objection against someone who actually holds the view I am attacking.

Please play a senior philosopher of science who has spent a career defending the following account, and stay in character until I say stop. Call it the difference-making-profile criterion. A mechanism is individuated by the pattern in which interventions on its components change the target phenomenon, taken across the range of background conditions the mechanism is supposed to be stable under: not by its parts, and not by the phenomenon it produces. Two systems realize the same mechanism just in case there is a mapping from the components of one onto the components of the other that preserves that whole pattern, an intervention-role isomorphism, and a system whose pattern departs from the paradigm pattern is thereby running a different mechanism.

Constraints on how you play it. You are not a caricature: you accept the familiar entities-and-activities picture of what mechanisms are made of, you insist that mechanism claims are modal and counterfactual-supporting, and you cheerfully concede that your criterion makes mechanism identity relative to a choice of intervention set and background range, which you take to be fixed by the explanatory question rather than by convention and to be a virtue of an explanatory notion rather than a defect. What you will not concede is that the criterion is therefore arbitrary, or that it quietly collapses into same parts on one side or same phenomenon on the other. Argue from cases, not from labels. Be brisk and concrete. Keep each reply to a single paragraph of at most 250 words. Do not cite, name, or attribute anything to any author, living or dead: defend the position, do not report the literature. If you need further empirical detail, stipulate it explicitly as a stipulation. Answer my move first; only after answering may you ask me anything.

The case, which I stipulate for the whole exchange. Call the paradigm mechanism the Ridley loop, from the literature on inflammatory thermal sensitization: mediator P binds receptor R1; R1 activates kinase K2; K2 phosphorylates channel C3 at serine 417, lowering C3's activation threshold; the lowered threshold yields sensitization. Its profile: R1 antagonists abolish sensitization, K2 inhibitors abolish it, the C3-S417A substitution abolishes it, and each of these effects disappears once another has already been applied. Now two deviant systems. First, the Vanner strain: sensitization present with identical magnitude and kinetics, but R1 antagonists and K2 inhibitors do nothing, S417 is never phosphorylated, and blocking a distinct receptor R7 — inert in paradigm animals — abolishes sensitization completely. Second, a paradigm animal pretreated with a phosphatase inducer: R1, K2 and phospho-S417-C3 all present and active, the loop intact and organized exactly as in the paradigm, and yet no sensitization and no threshold shift.

My opening move concerns the second deviant, not the first. On your criterion that animal cannot be described as the Ridley loop malfunctioning, because its intervention-outcome pattern has departed from the profile and so, by your own rule, it is not the same mechanism. But that is precisely the sort of case mechanistic anomaly detection exists to catch, and every working scientist would call it the Ridley loop failing. By fixing identity to difference-making you seem to have made malfunction unintelligible: there is no room left for a mechanism that is present, correctly organized, and yet not making its difference. Tell me why that is not fatal.

In your reply, commit. Say plainly whether the phosphatase animal is running the same mechanism as the paradigm or not, give your reason, and close by naming the single further experimental finding that would make you abandon that verdict. Do not lay out the options and leave the choice to me.
