hey, so im a junior and I run this little philosophy club at school and we totally derailed on epiphenomenalism last week. the view, the way I mean it: your feelings and experiences dont cause anything at all, they just ride along while the neurons do all the actual causing.

the thing I cant let go of is this: if my back pain causes nothing, then my saying "my back hurts" wasnt caused by the pain. so how is it a report *about* the pain, and how am I supposed to be justified in believing I have experiences at all — or in believing epiphenomenalism, since that belief obviously isnt caused by my experiences either? our club president calls this the paradox of phenomenal judgment and says its no big deal.

he dissolves it with this analogy he uses for everything: consciousness is like the steam whistle on an old locomotive. the whistle doesnt drive the engine, but the same steam runs both, so the whistle still tells you what the engine is doing. he says the shared cause is enough — my judgment and my pain both come out of the same brain state, so the judgment can be true and reliable and count as knowledge without the pain doing any work. case closed, epiphenomenalism costs us nothing.

I tried to push back with my own version and it went nowhere: I said pain is more like the heat coming off a laptop — real, caused by the work being done, does zero work itself, and its the only thing that would ever tell anybody the laptop is straining. he says that's the same analogy wearing a different hat. I think mine is diffrent bc the heat is the only channel anybody has, and it still cant tell the laptop anything.

can you audit this for me? I want exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no intro and no wrap-up at the end:

1. the mapping table — on his reading, what plays the engine, the steam, the whistle, the sound itself, and the person standing there hearing it
2. where the mapping actually breaks, and which of those breaks matter for the paradox and which ones are just decoration
3. what the whistle picture quietly assumes that epiphenomenalism doesnt get to have (I think something about there being a hearer standing outside the train, but I cant say why that matters)
4. whether "costs us nothing" survives once those disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying that conclusion the whole time
5. a better analogy if there is one — what it'd have to keep from the whistle and what it'd have to drop — plus whether my laptop-heat thing is really a different analogy or just the same one in a hoodie
