I picked up one of those airport pop-science books on the way home from a conference (the chapter was called something like "Why You're Probably Near The End") and now me and Sam have an actual bet riding on it, so I want to know how much of the chapter to trust. Its version of the doomsday argument ran roughly like this:

"Treat yourself as a random draw from everyone who will ever live. You're about the 100-billionth human, and if there were going to be trillions more it would be a miracle to find yourself this absurdly early — so the total is probably not much bigger than the number born so far, which means the human race most likely ends within a few thousand years. The maths is just Gott's delta-t rule: assume you're at a random point in any ongoing process, and with 95% confidence its future duration is between about a fortieth and about forty times its current age. The argument rests on the self-indication assumption — the idea that your own existence is evidence that there are lots of observers like you — and that's also its weak spot, because if you change the reference class from all humans to, say, all mammals the argument gives you nothing at all, which most people now agree refutes it. Either way the upshot is hard to ignore: we should be spending far more on things like asteroid detection, because the argument tells us we're finished whatever we do."
