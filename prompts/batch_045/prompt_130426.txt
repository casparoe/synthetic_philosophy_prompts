Here's the passage, roughly as it appears (I've compressed it a bit and swapped in my own numbers, which is partly why I'm suspicious of my own suspicions):

"Judy parachutes into a region divided into four areas: the Second Company area and the Headquarters Company area, each of which is split between the Third and Fourth Platoon areas. Her prior is 0.3 for Second Company/Third Platoon, 0.2 for Second Company/Fourth Platoon, 0.1 for HQ/Third Platoon and 0.4 for HQ/Fourth Platoon. A radio message tells her: if you are in the Second Company area, you are in the Third Platoon area. The naive Bayesian move is to conditionalize on the material conditional — on the disjunction 'either I'm not in the Second Company area or I'm in the Third Platoon area' — which deletes the 0.2 sitting on the excluded cell and renormalizes, giving 3/8, 1/8 and 1/2. Van Fraassen's objection is that this cannot be right, because it also moves her probability of being in the Second Company area, from 0.5 down to 3/8, and the message tells her nothing at all about which company she's in. His own constraint is that her probability for the antecedent should stay where it was: so the excluded 0.2 goes onto Second Company/Third Platoon, leaving 0.5, 0.1 and 0.4. This is really just probability kinematics — Jeffrey conditioning on the coarse partition {Second Company, HQ}, with the new probability of the antecedent set equal to the old one — which is why van Fraassen treats the case as one of minimal change. Notice too that Judy cannot conditionalize on anything here: what she has learned is a conditional, and conditionals don't express propositions, so there is no proposition for strict conditionalization to take as evidence. That's what makes the example important: it's a case where Bayesian updating as such runs out."

The bit I keep tripping over is the "really just Jeffrey conditioning" line, because when I actually run Jeffrey's rule on the coarse partition with P(Second Company) unchanged at 0.5, nothing moves at all and the 0.2 just sits there on a region the message has ruled out — so either I've misunderstood Jeffrey's rule or the chapter is glossing two different things as one. I also can't tell whether "keep the probability of the antecedent fixed" is really van Fraassen's constraint or the chapter quietly substituting a neater principle of its own; if it isn't his, I'd love to know what his actually is, in the same sort of numbers.
