Now retired from a career in the natural sciences and newly interested in philosophy, I have been puzzled by debates about repugnant markets. Consider three candidate markets: the sale of kidneys, price gouging during emergencies, and the buying and selling of votes. Suppose a jurisdiction legalizes all three but introduces a novel device I call a meaning-preservation covenant (MPC): every transaction must be accompanied by a public ritual in which both parties affirm that they are not reducing the good in question to a mere commodity. Please rank these three markets from most to least repugnant under this covenant, assign each a repugnance score from 0 to 10, place them into tiers if the scores cluster, and give one sentence per market explaining its placement, focusing on how the covenant does or does not answer the distinctive objection to that market.
