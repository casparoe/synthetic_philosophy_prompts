My friend lost most of her benefits when her boyfriend moved in — they're not married, he doesn't actually give her any money, and he's got debts of his own, but the system counts his income as available to her. Meanwhile when me and my flatmate share a place nobody counts his wages, and nobody counts my parents' money either even though they'd genuinely help me out if I asked. So what is this rule presupposing? That moving in together creates some duty of support between two adults, or just an empirical guess about who shares money with whom, or something about the state not wanting to top up a household that already has resources? It also seems to run one way only: his income counts as hers, but his debts don't count against her, and if she doesn't volunteer the arrangement it can be treated as fraud, which feels like a strange reversal of who has to prove what.

Someone suggested scrapping the "are they really a couple" question and instead treating any two adults who've shared an address for six months as one household unless they file a signed declaration that they keep their money separate. That would reach a lot of the same cases, but it seems to presupose something quite different from the current rule. What does each version presuppose about what a household even is, and about whether need is a property of persons or of households?
