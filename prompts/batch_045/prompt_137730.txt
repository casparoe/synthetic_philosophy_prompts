In most cities the remedy adopted over the last decade for a contested monument has not been destruction but relocation: the statue is lifted from its pedestal and offered to a museum or stored in a depot, the plinth is sometimes left empty or fitted with an explanatory plaque, and the whole operation is described as recontextualization rather than erasure. When the contested object is a name rather than a statue — a street, a school — the remedy is characteristically the reverse: not removal but addition, an honorary co-designation mounted beneath the official sign, which leaves every address, map, and legal record untouched. Some commemoration commissions are now discussing a third option for new monuments: a commemorative authorization granted for a fixed term, renewable only if a standing body votes that the honoree still merits civic honor, with any monument whose authorization lapses transferred to an off-site archive open by appointment.

What view of what a monument does does this family of practices presuppose? I want three things pressed in particular. First, does relocation-plus-contextualization presuppose that a statue's honoring is a function of its setting and its framing — so that the same bronze, in a gallery behind wall text, has stopped honoring and started only teaching — and is that defensible, or does it merely move the difficulty somewhere else? Second, what explains the asymmetry between statues, which get taken down, and names, which get added to: does the practice presuppose that a name's honorific force is detachable from its use as an address, and is that distinction stable enough to bear the weight placed on it? Third, does the renewable-authorization scheme presuppose that honoring is an ongoing act performed by the present polity rather than a completed act of a past one or a piece of testimony about what happened — and if honoring is ongoing in that sense, what follows for the default: does an un-renewed monument become a kind of speech, and do private commemorations, a family stone or a donor plaque in a hospital corridor, need authorization as well?

Say finally where the practice is underdetermined: which rival theories of commemoration would license the same policy, so that nothing the commission is doing forces a choice among them.
