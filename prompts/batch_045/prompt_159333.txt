My opening. Say the billionaire gets fed up that nobody ever collects and rewrites the terms: no more all-or-nothing. At midnight he pays out $1M × p, where p is the probability that a panel of well-calibrated forecasters — who know the whole setup and know that I know it — assign to my drinking the vial the next morning. Continuous, no threshold, money already in my account, and I never have to drink.

I say I can collect a big fraction of that without ever forming an intention to drink. I take the day off so being sick is cheap, put the vial next to the coffee, tell four friends I'm going to do it — which isn't just cheap talk, it gives me a reason at 9am that I don't have in the original setup — and stop pushing when the next bit of p costs more than its worth in dollars. Call the state that leaves me in "intending to probably drink." That puts you on a fork: either your constraint only bites on certainty, in which case it has no grip here and the money is collectible in degrees; or it bites on credence, in which case it looks ad hoc, and it also seems to imply that a smoker who knows the relapse rate and resolves to quit anyway doesn't really intend to quit. Which horn do you take, and what happens to the original puzzle if you take it?
