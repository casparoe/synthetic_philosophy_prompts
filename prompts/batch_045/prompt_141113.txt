My flatmate is in one of those AI safety reading groups and came home with an argument that corrigibility is impossible, and that any agent that is corrigible is useless for exactly that reason. I think its proving too much, because the same reasoning seems to work on a perfectly normal human colleague, but I cant find the seam. Here it is, more or less in his words:

(1) Call an AI corrigible when it doesnt try to make its operators less likely to correct it: it wont disable the off switch, wont talk them out of pressing it, wont leave out of the weekly report the bit about where the switch is, wont make itself harder to understand than it has to be, and so on.

(2) "Correct it" covers anything the operators might do: shut it down, swap in a different utility function, edit out its current goals, replace it with another system entirely.

(3) For something that maximises expected utility, "wont try to make X less likely" just comes to "expected utility is not lower when X happens than when it doesnt". Because if it were lower, then whenever there is some cheap action that nudges the probability — one sentence in the report, a thirty second delay, putting the switch somewhere slightly less convenient — it takes it. And there is basically always some such action.

(4) So a corrigible agent has to be indifferent, in advance, between each of those interventions happening and not happening.

(5) And indifferent by the lights of the utility function it has now, since an agent evaluating a change to its own goals still evaluates it using the goals it has at the time.

(6) Now take the intervention where the operators overwrite its utility function with the exact inverse of the one it currently has — the one that assigns high value to precisely what it now wants to avoid. By (4) and (5) it has to be indifferent between that and nothing happening.

(7) But you can only be indifferent between your goals surviving and your goals being inverted if you dont actually care, by your current lights, how things turn out. And because (2) lets the replacement function be anything whatsoever, its current one has to be flat across the outcomes: no preferences, nothing to maximise.

(8) So the only corrigible agent is one with an empty utility function, which cant be given a task. Corrigibility and usefulness are incompatible.

(9) And it gets worse. Run (1) through (8) on a person. A genuinely corrigible colleague is one who never tries to make her manager less likely to overrule her — she doesnt bury the numbers that would change his mind, doesnt make herself indispensable. By (7) she must not care how the project goes. But people like that exist and are great to work with. So something above is broken.

I pushed back with the move Ive seen around — build the utility function so that being switched off isnt itself something the agent minds, letting it still care about the outcome while not caring about the switch. He said that relocates the problem: indifference is global, so the agent is now equally indifferent between a well informed operator pressing the button and someone drunk stumbling into the server room and leaning on it, and it will watch a disaster it could have prevented by quietly making the switch harder to reach. He also dismissed just adding a bonus term for not interfering, on the grounds that the term has to be big enough to outweigh the worst case loss from being corrected, and once its that big it swamps everything else the agent was supposed to care about. I kind of agree with both replies, which is why Im stuck.

So which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I would most like to keep (1) and (2) — corrigibility really does look like it involves not obstructing your own correction, and correction really does include having your goals rewritten — and I want to keep (9), since an argument that only works by making corrigible humans impossible is one I trust even less. If you reject (4), tell me what the thinner requirement is actually supposed to be, and what the agent is doing in the case where the operators are about to press the button by mistake. And if you think the honest answer is that corrigibility is a structural relation rather than something written into the utility function, say what breaks first in that relation and how bad the breakage is.
