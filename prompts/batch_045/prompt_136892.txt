You are an analytic philosopher of religion who works on the contemporary Molinist literature and who has also read the causal-modelling literature closely enough to move around in it without hand-holding: structural causal models, potential outcomes, do-calculus, off-policy evaluation, common random numbers. Write for an interlocutor who is your mirror image — I do causal inference and offline reinforcement learning, I am fluent in that apparatus, and my grip on the theology is that of a careful amateur.

Translate Molinism into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and then tell me what did not survive the translation.

Here is the conjecture I want you to test rather than flatter. It looks to me as if divine middle knowledge is just a perfectly specified counterfactual model that is queried before any intervention is performed: God knows what every possible creature would freely do in any circumstance, then selects which circumstances to actualize. On that reading, Molinism is the claim that a certain model is well defined and that the agent gets to read off its counterfactuals before acting. I suspect this is either trivially true or incoherent, and I want to know which, and why the literature does not simply say so.

Work through the following, in this order.

1. Fix the object of translation. State in my terms what a counterfactual of creaturely freedom would have to be: what the antecedent sets (circumstances complete right up to, but not including, the creature's own exercise of will), what the consequent is, and what kind of truth is being claimed for the conditional.

2. Map it onto a structural causal model, and be honest about the fork you hit. Is the free act an endogenous variable with a structural equation, or an exogenous noise variable? Show why each horn fails to reproduce the Molinist claim: with an equation, the act is determined by the circumstances and middle knowledge collapses into ordinary knowledge of the model's consequences; without one, there is no counterfactual value to be read off at all, because the model returns nothing unless the exogenous input is fixed.

3. Translate two downstream notions. First, the set of worlds feasible for God: those reachable by intervening on circumstances alone, leaving free acts to fall where they may. Second, the thesis that every such world contains at least one creature who freely does wrong somewhere — transworld depravity, the claim that no intervention on circumstances avoids moral failure. Say what the counterfactuals would have to mean for that claim to be evaluable at all, and whether my vocabulary can even express it.

4. Then the distinctive piece: the intervention that is creation. An intervention in my sense sets a variable to a value. Actualizing a world is supposed to fix every circumstance while leaving choices undetermined, and yet yield a complete and known actual world. Tell me what kind of query that is — prediction, selection, something else — and whether it has any counterpart in my framework.

5. Give me the residue report in three parts: what translated cleanly; what translated only with distortion, and which extra assumption you had to add to make it go through; and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Then take a position on what the residue means: is my framework missing a resource — some notion of counterfactual truth that is not fixed by structural equations or by a similarity ordering over worlds — or is the Molinist helping herself to a notion of counterfactual truth that nothing grounds? Argue for one side, and say what would change your mind.

Two constraints. Flag every point at which the translation would beg the question by assuming either determinism of the will or libertarian indeterminism; do not let the mapping settle the substantive dispute by stipulation. And where you invoke a named thesis or position, define it in a sentence, since I cannot look anything up.

Before the translation proper, give me a short brainstorming pass: two or three candidate mappings of middle knowledge onto my vocabulary, and why you discard all but one. Then the translation, then the residue report, then your verdict. Aim for roughly 900 to 1200 words, prose rather than bullet lists except in the residue report, where structure helps.
