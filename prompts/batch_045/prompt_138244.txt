After forty years in experimental geophysics I have started auditing a graduate seminar in practical ethics, and at last week's meeting a fellow student closed a discussion of whether settling other worlds is a moral priority by remarking that we should not keep all our eggs in one basket, as though the proverb settled the matter. I would rather press on it.

Please lay out, as a numbered menu, the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making here: about what the eggs are (individual persons, the species, a civilisation's accumulated value), what counts as a basket, over what time horizon, what kind of loss is at stake — extinction, civilisational collapse, or something considerably less — and whether a settlement can be a source of new existential risks rather than merely a hedge against old ones. For each reading, say whether it is close to trivial, whether it survives scrutiny, and whether it would actually deliver the conclusion it was being used for.

I am most interested in what the slogan silently assumes about the independence of the risks to the two baskets. The case I keep returning to, which the seminar has not addressed: suppose the planned settlement is projected to reach self-sufficiency in roughly a century and a half, and that throughout that interval it remains dependent on a single Earth-side supply chain for one critical input — a class of radiation-hardened electronics, say, or a pharmaceutical precursor whose manufacture requires an industrial base only Earth possesses. Does the diversification argument still apply, and if so in what attenuated form? And is the eggs-and-baskets picture the right one at all when the colonisation programme and the terrestrial risk-reduction measures it would displace draw on the same finite funding, expertise, and political attention, so that the second basket is purchased by thinning the first?

Finally, tell me which reading you think people who deploy the proverb this way usually have in mind, and what each reading would have to assume — statistically, causally, and morally — to carry the urgency claim.
