Im a retired field entomologist who got bored and started sitting in on the philosophy of biology reading group at the local university. One member, Hal, a retired evolutionary biologist, has a habit of ruling on every trait we discuss: adaptation, spandrel, or exaptation. His calls so far:

- vertebrate camera eye: adaptation, and he said he wouldnt need the fossil record to tell
- human chin: spandrel, and he waved off the chewing and speech stories as just-so
- pandas radial sesamoid thumb: adaptation, clumsy as it is
- giraffe neck: adaptation, and he refused to be drawn on whether it was for browsing or for fighting
- lactase persistence: adaptation
- malaria resistance in the sickle-cell heterozygote: adaptation
- red colour of blood: not an adaptation, just the chemistry of haemoglobin
- the blind spot where the optic nerve exits the retina: not an adaptation
- the oversized antlers of the Irish elk: spandrel, an allometric consequence of body size, until someone shows otherwise
- the appendix harbouring gut bacteria that reboot the microbiome: exaptation, not an adaptation
- feathers: he refused to rule

Now the new one. In one of those digital evolution setups where self-replicating programs compete for CPU time, a lineage picked up a duplicated block of instructions as a pure copying accident. The duplicate does nothing by itself, but it happens to stop a register being overwritten, so its carriers replicate about four percent faster. It has been spreading ever since, and it is slowly getting shorter and tidier as selection trims it.

What would Hal say about this block of code, and what about the same block two hundred thousand generations later once selection has really molded it? Im less interested in the right answer than in the rule he seems to be running: something historical about what selection was for, something current about what the thing does for fitness now, or something about special design being the only evidence he will accept. Lay out the candidate rules that fit his track record equally well, say what each predicts about the code block early and late, guess which one he is actually on, and tell me which further case would best tell them apart.
