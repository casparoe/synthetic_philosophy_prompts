Hey — I'm on the safety committee for our regional transit authority and we have to vote next month on whether to let a driverless shuttle pilot expand from one loop to the whole downtown, and I keep flip-flopping, so I'd find it genuinely useful to have a draft memo to argue with rather than my own notes.

The case, briefly: fleet per-mile crash rate in the pilot data is roughly half the human baseline. But there was one crash that nobody can pin on anybody. The remote operator was watching nine vehicles at once; her only intervention is a halt button that the system itself surfaces as a suggestion, and pressing it would not have helped. No maintainer missed a check. The vendor met spec. The failure came out of an edge case that wasn't represented in the training distribution, and the logs don't support a counterfactual — you can't say what the shuttle would have done if the sensor had been clean, so you can't say that any agent could have prevented it. The vendor's proposed fix is contractual: keep the remote operator role, designate her in the contract as the responsible party, pay her a differential for carrying that designation. Our union rep calls it a moral crumple zone with direct deposit. One committee member says there is no gap here at all, this is exactly what strict liability is for, put it on the vendor and stop being spooky about agency. Another says that if we demand meaningful human control we're just buying back the human error rate we're trying to get rid of, so we're being forced to choose between safety and having anyone left to blame. My outside view says every institutional fix in this space turns into blame-laundering; my inside view says a no-fault compensation fund plus mandatory counterfactual-legibility requirements for the logs is actually fine, and I can't tell which of those is doing the work.

Draft the memo I'd circulate before the vote: a concrete recommendation — approve, reject, or approve with named conditions — with a time frame, that takes both of those members seriously instead of just recieving the standard two-sides-have-a-point. Two things I specifically want it to handle, because I don't think the standard moves handle them well: first, whether responsibility gap is even the right diagnosis, or whether what we're short of is a counterfactual — an epistemic problem about what the black box recorded rather than a metaphysical problem about who acted; and second, if we go the no-fault route, whether foreclosing blame ex post changes anyone's incentives ex ante, given that the designers and monitors now know in advance that no one will be found at fault. Give me something I can put to a vote, not a survey.
