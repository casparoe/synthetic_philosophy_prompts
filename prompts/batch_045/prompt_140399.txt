I work on AI safety policy, and before a grant panel asks me to defend it I want to write down exactly what standard of assurance I think frontier labs should have to meet. Here is where I have landed.

(1) A safety case for catastrophic risk should be worst-case: it must not rest on the assumption that the inputs a system meets in deployment resemble the inputs it was evaluated on.
(2) Evidence from today's systems does carry over to future ones: the fact that current models mostly do not deceive when deception would not pay is real evidence that gradient descent does not produce deceptive agency by default, and I want to lean on that when I claim that deceptive alignment is not near-term.
(3) Defense in depth is the right architecture: several layers — monitoring, access control, capability thresholds, human oversight — each of which only has to be reliable on average, since failure rates multiply across independent layers.
(4) The object of evaluation is the deployment stack: model plus scaffolding plus monitors plus sandboxing, not the model in the abstract.
(5) Adversarial evaluation and red-teaming, scaled up, are our main instrument for getting a handle on rare failures, including fitting a statistical tail model to red-team scores in order to extrapolate how bad the worst case is.
(6) None of this should depend on claims about the model's internal goals or intentions; behavior and structural constraints are enough.

Someone recently pointed out to me that there may be two different worst cases in play here: worst case over the inputs a human might supply, and worst case over what the system could find if it did the searching itself. I have been treating the first as the demanding standard, but I am not sure my commitments are indifferent between them.

Please audit this package. Which pairs actually conflict, which only look as though they do, and for each genuine conflict what precisely would have to be given up? Rank the conflicts by severity, give the cheapest revision for each, and tell me if one of these commitments is doing load-bearing work while another is there mainly because it makes the rest feel safer.
