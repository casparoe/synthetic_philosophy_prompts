Every month I run a twenty-five-minute ideas slot at my team's Friday engineering meeting, and next month's is linguistic relativity — the Sapir–Whorf material. I have no philosophy or linguistics background and my team, about a dozen backend engineers, have less. Nobody in the room will have anything to look up, and I would rather not name-drop studies, so whatever the claim turns out to be, it has to be motivated from cases I can put on a whiteboard.

What I want is not an explanation of linguistic relativity but a plan for the twenty-five minutes: the order the pieces have to come in and what depends on what; the one example that carries the whole thing and what that example must have and must lack; the misconceptions to expect and where in the sequence to head each off; and, the part I would most like help with, which misconception my chosen example will manufacture that a standard example would not.

My instinct is to build it on the claim that programming languages shape how programmers think, since that is a version my team already half-believes and will argue about. If that is the right vehicle, tell me exactly where the analogy stops tracking the real claim and how I make that break the main teaching point rather than an awkward caveat; if it is the wrong vehicle, say so and give me the one you would use.

Two misconceptions I already expect: the many-words-for-snow version, where the claim is about vocabulary limiting thought; and the belief that the whole topic was settled and refuted by the color-naming research of the late sixties. I also expect my engineers to arrive with a computational frame — thought as language-independent data, language as a lossy encoding of it — which I suspect makes the interesting claim unstatable rather than false, and I do not know whether to attack that frame early or borrow it.

Finally: a check for the last five minutes that shows whether they have the idea rather than whether they are convinced. I was thinking of handing them a language with a feature none of them has encountered and asking what would have to be true of its speakers' non-linguistic behaviour for the claim to hold — improve on that or tell me why it fails — plus an explicit list of what I should refuse to cover at this level and say so out loud.
