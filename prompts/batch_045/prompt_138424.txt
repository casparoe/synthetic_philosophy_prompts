Last night my friend and I argued for three hours about voting and got nowhere, so I want to turn the stalemate into a piece of satire.

Her position is that voting can only be justified by what it causes: the chance that a single ballot breaks a tie in a large electorate is somewhere near one in a million, so the expected causal payoff of my walking to the polling station is smaller than the walk, and any duty to vote has to be grounded in something other than outcomes. I kept insisting that voting is expressive: what I do in the booth is go on record, declare which side I am on, and that is why I would rather vote for a hopeless candidate I believe in than hold my nose for one I despise. She replied that if the point is to go on record I could do it with a bumper sticker, and that I am attached to the booth only because I have not admitted to myself that I am cheering rather than choosing. We also could not settle the lesser-evil case: she thinks voting for a candidate you despise is complicity you elected to incur, and I think the people who tell you that a vote for anyone else is a vote for the worse man are threatening you, and that there is something servile about rewarding the threat.

Write the piece I would write if I wanted to settle this by mockery. Form: a mock official government document, Application for Retroactive Abstention, Form EV-4, with a short cover letter, issued by an elections agency in a jurisdiction that has taken both of our arguments entirely literally. Deadpan bureaucratic voice, numbered sections, 600 to 700 words, signed by an invented official with a title like Deputy Commissioner for Non-Pivotal Ballots. No real parties, no real politicians, no actual election.

What the form should quietly do:
- Since a ballot's causal contribution is nil unless it is pivotal, voters may apply after the count to have their vote reclassified as an abstention; the application is refused where the margin was one vote or less, in which case the agency congratulates the applicant on having caused everything the winner has done since.
- A separate schedule for the expressive content of the ballot, which cannot be severed from the act: applicants may withdraw the statement their vote made but may not revise it, and there is a checkbox for voters wishing to certify that they meant it sarcastically.
- A lesser-evil section requiring a declaration of whether the vote was cast in response to a threat of a worse outcome, with a note that such threats are recognised only when made by a party the applicant was not already inclined to support.
- A provision for spoiled ballots, treated as the purest kind and assessed at a higher fee, plus one worked example for a voter in a safe district whose ballot is 100 per cent expression by weight.

The jokes have to come from getting the philosophy right, not from election trivia: the tiny pivot probability, the reply that if everyone like you stayed home, the difference between causing a result and endorsing it, cheering versus choosing, and the structure of a lesser-evil threat. Someone who knows this literature should feel fairly mocked whichever side they are on, and should be able to reconstruct the real disagreement from the form alone; someone who does not should still find it funny. Keep it deadpan throughout, and do not let it collapse into democracy is a sham: the target is the shape of our arguments, not voting.
