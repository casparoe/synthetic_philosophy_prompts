I'm a philosophy postgraduate working on Dennett's three stances — physical, design and intentional — and on the compressibility criterion he attaches to the notion of a real pattern: roughly, that a pattern is real if there is some description of the data that is more efficient than a bit map of it. In a seminar last week I floated a fourth stance, and the discussion went in enough circles that I now want a number rather than another exchange of intuitions. Call it the accountability stance: you predict a system's behaviour by working out what it would be fitting to blame, praise, excuse or sanction that system for, and then predicting that it will behave as a responsible agent would, including in its responses to actually being held responsible. My claim is that this is not a special case of the intentional stance, because responsibility vocabulary carries regularities of its own that belief–desire vocabulary does not obviously capture: reactance when blamed, manufacture of excuses, reputational repair, strategic confession, deflection of blame onto whichever part of an organisation is cheapest to lose. On my view those are compressible under descriptions framed in terms of responsibility, and not compressible under descriptions framed only in terms of beliefs and desires, so the accountability stance tracks patterns the intentional stance misses.

Here is the proposition I want a credence on. Fix a well-defined forecasting domain — say, predicting the publicly observable behaviour of mid-sized firms over a five-year horizon — and imagine a properly designed forecasting tournament in which one team is trained to use only physical, design and intentional vocabulary and another is trained to use only accountability vocabulary, with both scored on identical strictly behavioural predictions, pre-registered in advance. My claim is that the accountability team would do measurably better, by more than could plausibly be attributed to luck. Give me your credence that this is so. I do not want it depends what counts as a stance; I want a number to two significant figures and the decomposition that generates it.

Please decompose it, with a number and a one-line justification for each term, then show how the terms combine: the probability that the three stances really are jointly exhaustive of predictive strategies over a system, as opposed to a useful but incomplete taxonomy; the probability that, if they are not, attributions of responsibility are the sort of thing that would fill the gap rather than something the intentional stance already contains implicitly; the probability that there are behavioural regularities of the kind I listed which genuinely admit of more compression under responsibility descriptions than under belief–desire descriptions; the probability that, even granting that, five-year firm forecasting is a domain where such regularities are frequent and strong enough to emerge above the noise; and whatever further terms you think belong, including how much weight you are placing on the possibility that predictive compression is simply the wrong test for whether something is a stance at all. Then tell me which single term you are least confident in, which term the final number is most sensitive to if that is a different one, and what one piece of evidence — an experiment, a formal result, a conceptual distinction — would move your number furthest, and in which direction. Finally: do you think such a tournament would actually settle anything, or could someone who denies that accountability is a stance absorb a win for the accountability team into the intentional stance after the fact? If you think they could, say so, and say what would block the move. My own credence is 0.55 and my supervisor's is 0.2; I would like to know where you think that gap lives. Please answer in British English.
