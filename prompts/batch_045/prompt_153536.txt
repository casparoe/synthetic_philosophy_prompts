I got backed into a corner at my philosophy club last week and I've been going in circles since. I'm an origin essentialist of the boring Kripkean sort: I came from a particular sperm and a particular egg, and I couldn't have come from a different pair. Then someone laid out this argument and now I can't tell what to drop.

(1) Origin essentialism (OE): a person couldn't have originated from numerically different gametes than the ones they actually came from.
(2) My father produced billions of sperm. Plenty of them differed from the one that actually won by essentially nothing — a crossover landing at a slightly different spot, a few hundred base pairs out of three billion, no effect on any trait, same kid coming out nine months later. Its a molecular coin flip which one got there first. Surely I could have been the result of one of those.
(3) If a difference of a few hundred base pairs doesn't stop the resulting child from being me, then neither does one more base pair, and one more after that. There's no principled number where the difference suddenly starts to matter, because every step is just "one more."
(4) So chain far enough and I could have originated from gametes carrying basically any human genome — including sperm from a man my mother never met and an egg from a woman who isn't my mother.
(5) But then there's no content left in "I couldn't have been a different person." I could have been anyone at all. That has to be wrong.

I want to keep (2) — it really does seem like a fluke which sperm won — and I really don't want (5). So something in the middle has to break and I can't see what.

One wrinkle that came up, which I think is load bearing: suppose a clinic had used a molecular duplicate of the actual sperm, same base sequence but different carbon atoms. If that child is me, then OE isn't about the token cells. If it isn't me, then OE is somehow about the gametes' matter, which is odd given that the sperm's own material mostly gets dismantled at fertilization anyway. Both ways seem to cost me something.

So: which step do you reject, why did it look fine, and what does each of the other exits cost? And if you think (2) is the thing to give up, tell me what I'm supposed to say about molecular coin flips without sounding like a maniac.
