hey — I run a small podcast (just me and one guest, ~40 min) on philosophy-adjacent questions in AI, and next weeks guest is a researcher who thinks you can define "avoid side effects" for an AI system without first writing down what's valuable. The whole episode is going to be about whether that's really possible, and I want to press her on it rather than let her restate her setup for half an hour.

Her position, as I understand it from her blog posts and a talk: forget about ranking outcomes as better or worse. Instead, at each timestep you look at how well the agent could still do a broad spread of placeholder goals if it wanted to — she calls this its reachability profile — and you charge it for the distance between the profile after its action and the profile it would have had if it had just done nothing at that moment. That distance is the impact. Two parts are supposed to be her own contribution: (1) it's a hard budget per episode, not a penalty subtracted from reward, because a penalty is just a price and any price gets paid if the task reward is high enough; (2) when she computes the "did nothing" branch she holds everyone elses behaviour fixed at what they actually do, so the agent dosn't get charged for the human moving a vase across the room. Her line in the talk was "we're not specifying value, we're specifying a budget on change," and she claims this gets around the usual objection that any impact measure has to smuggle in a weighting over which changes matter.

Can you draft me eight or nine questions? Rules: nothing that just asks her to explain the measure again, since listeners will have heard the setup in the intro. No "how does this relate to X's paper" questions either — she'll namecheck the literature herself and I'd rather the questions work on the content. Each question should be speakable out loud and self-contained for a smart non-specialist, so if a question needs a concrete example, build the example into the question. And for each one give me the follow-up keyed to whatever answer she's most likely to give.

Things I definitely want covered:

- Whose options? She charges the agent for changes in the agent's own reachability. Why should anyone care about the agent's options? And if she switches to the human's reachability, she needs to know what the human can bring about, which looks like a value specification with extra steps. Follow up on the obvious move — that a broad spread of placeholder goals is just a prior, and priors are cheap.
- What fixes the grain? The same physical change registers as basically nothing on a coarse description of outcomes and as huge on a fine one, and the metric on profiles is doing similar work. Have her say where the grain and the metric come from, then follow up.
- Status quo bias: on her baseline, a death or a forest fire that would have happened anyway is part of "nothing changed," so preventing it gets charged as impact. Is "leave what would have happened alone" a value-free principle, or is it conservatism about the actual world including its disasters?
- Her own fix opening a loophole: if other agents' behaviour is held fixed in the baseline, and the human presses the off-switch because the agent did something obnoxious, then the press shows up in the baseline for free and the agent escapes the charge exactly by provoking predictable responses. Ask whether her baseline is even well defined when the other agents are responding to her agent, and follow up on whatever she says about interventionist counterfactuals.
- Salami slicing: per-step budgets let a long sequence of tiny steps travel arbitrarily far; a cumulative budget over an episode makes behaviour depend on the horizon and gives reasons to hoard the budget or blow it at the deadline. Whichever she picks, follow up.
- Many agents, one baseline: if N agents each compare against the same "nothing happens" branch, then either each one's individual delta is tiny because the others would have done it anyway, or the same change gets charged N times over. Which way does hers go, and why isn't that fatal?
- Is a low-impact agent just a weak agent? If she charges reachability gains as well as losses, she's charging the agent for becoming more capable, which sounds like safety through incapacitation; if she only charges losses, she's built a power-seeking incentive into the impact term. Make her pick a side.
- Something at the end that isn't a gotcha: what would actually change her mind, and what would she count as evidence that the approach works, given that there's no agreed metric for "few side effects"?

Order them so the episode builds instead of jumping around, and put a line or two after each saying why you aimed the follow-up where you did.
