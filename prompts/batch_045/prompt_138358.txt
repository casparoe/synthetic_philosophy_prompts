Study group argument that I can't find the hole in. We've been going round on the problem of old evidence and one of us wrote this down:

(1) On the Bayesian account, E confirms H for an agent A at a time iff learning E would raise A's credence in H — i.e. P(H|E) > P(H).
(2) Once E is old — once it's known — P(E) = 1, so P(H|E) = P(H), and no confirmation is possible.
(3) The usual repair is to evaluate the ratio counterfactually: what matters is the "ur-prior" A would have had before she learned E, not her actual credences now.
(4) But "what A would have believed had she not known E" is badly underdetermined. Take away her knowledge of the perihelion data and she might believe all sorts of other things instead; different ways of subtracting E seem to give different, even opposite, verdicts about whether E supports H.
(5) So confirmation by old evidence isn't just hard to model, it's arbitrary — there's no fact of the matter.
(6) And old evidence isn't a special case: every observation is old the moment after it's made, and scientists always build theories with the data already in hand.
(7) So on any Bayesian view, whether E confirms H is arbitrary, full stop.
(8) But whatever else is true, evidential support isn't arbitrary — the perihelion data really did favour GR over the Newtonian account.
(9) So Bayesian confirmation theory is false, and we need support to be a two-place relation between E and H that doesn't run through anybody's credences.

I'd most like to keep (8) and give up whatever else I have to, but I can't tell which step is doing the damage, and the discussion has definately stalled. One of us says the real issue is whether H was *built to fit* E rather than whether E was known, so (2) and (6) are aimed at the wrong target; another says (2) is false anyway for evidence that's recorded but nobody has ever looked at — data sitting in a ledger for fifty years is old for the world but not for anyone's credences.

Where's the break? Name the step, say why it looked fine, and price the alternatives for me: the counterfactual repair, the "what you actually learn is that H entails E" repair, and the no-credences option in (9). Also separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this — don't sell me a contested fix as the consensus. Under 700 words, prose, no headings.
