I'm a journalist preparing a feature on the renewed claim that brains and artificial neural networks are, in the same literal sense, computers, and I want an interactive sidebar that sorts readers by walking them down a decision tree rather than telling them what to conclude. Please build that instrument for me.

Structure: every reader starts from the same thin commitment — that neuroscientists' descriptions of neural circuits track real causal structure — and ends at a named position. Six or seven branch questions, no more, and at most ten leaves. Audience is educated general readers with no philosophy background, so each branch must be a concrete case they can answer without knowing any label, and no branch may ask whether they are a computationalist, a functionalist, or a mechanist.

The splits I want driving the tree: (i) an ordinary rock, under some mapping of its physical states onto program states, satisfies every program there is — does the reader grant that it computes? (ii) a circuit damaged so that it now issues systematically wrong outputs — is it still computing the old function, a new one, or nothing at all? (iii) the same intact circuit satisfies many mutually incompatible program descriptions at once — is the computation the one useful to us to describe, or one singled out by what the system is for or how it got here? (iv) must the states being manipulated be about something, or is structured vehicle-level causation enough for cognition? (v) if every neuron were replaced one by one with a chip matching its input-output profile, would the cognition survive?

Leaves must include: the position that the whole question is empty because everything computes; the position that it is a substantive empirical claim about what neural mechanisms do; the position that brains compute but not over sentence-like symbols; the dynamical-systems rejection; and the position that computation is a description we impose rather than something a system does intrinsically. For any combination your questions permit but nobody occupies, say so and diagnose whether the vacancy is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or neglect. Flag any pair of leaves your questions genuinely cannot separate rather than inventing a distinction. Each leaf should carry its standard label (or "unnamed") and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
