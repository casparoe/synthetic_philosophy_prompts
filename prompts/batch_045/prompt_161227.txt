A population axiological theorist whose case notes I follow rules on one comparison at a time. Her well-being scale runs from −100 to +100 with 0 as the neutral point, a life at 0 being neither good nor bad for the person living it, and in every case so far everyone within a population sits at the same level. Her published verdicts:

- 2 billion people at +90 is better than 1 billion at +95.
- 1 billion at +95 is better than 500 billion at +5; she regards this as the place where she rejects the repugnant conclusion.
- Adding a single person at +95 to an existing population makes the outcome better.
- Adding 10 million people at +5 to a population of 1 billion at +90 makes it worse.
- Adding 10 million people at +55 to that same population makes it worse, while adding 10 million at +59 makes it better.
- Every addition case so far has used a population at +90 as the base; she has never ruled on adding people to a population whose members are below +50, and has never compared two populations of very different size at the same average level.

Here is the open case. Take 10 million additional people, each at +45. Does adding them improve a population of 1 billion at +90? Does adding those very same people improve a population of 1 billion at +30? And is the size of the change the same in the two comparisons?

I can think of two families of rule that reproduce the six verdicts: one that measures each life against a fixed critical level sitting somewhere around +55 to +59, and one that works off average well-being with numbers counted less than proportionally. I suspect there are others, including hybrids of the two. Tell me which rules genuinely fit the whole track record, what each of them predicts about the two additions and about whether the magnitudes match, which single rule you would bet on and why, and which one further case would separate the surviving candidates most cleanly. Please be explicit about the points where her precedents underdetermine the answer rather than settling on one rule too quickly.
