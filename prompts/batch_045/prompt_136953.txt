I've been working through the endurantism/perdurantism literature and it has the shape of a dispute that has stopped moving: the argument from temporary intrinsics, the argument from special relativity, the fission and reassembly cases, the grounding objection, and the standard replies to each are all on the table, everyone has read them, and nobody I can find actually converts. I want a diagnosis rather than a survey. My hypothesis is that the question how objects persist is not one question with many arguments attached but several questions sharing a name: one about the semantics of temporary predication, one about whether the dispute already presupposes eternalism or can be restated for a presentist, one about whether persist is a term of art whose reference each side fixes by a different paradigm case. And I suspect the load-bearing part may not concern persistence at all but change, since for an entirely changeless object the two views seem to say exactly the same thing and the labels stop doing any work. Add that the perdurantist label covers both the worm view and the stage view, which disagree with each other about whether anything literally persists. Tell me which of these is doing the real work, whether any part of it is merely verbal, whether the dispute as standardly posed is well-posed, and what, on your diagnosis, would count as progress: a formal equivalence or inequivalence result, a distinction, a verdict on eternalism, a different question. Commit to the diagnosis you actually favor, give your reasons, and say what would disconfirm it.
