I'm RAing for my supervisor, who's finishing a paper on which measures of inaccuracy are admissible in accuracy-first epistemology — three-world partitions throughout, credence functions p = (p₁, p₂, p₃), inaccuracy of p at world w written I(p, w) — and I've been handed the section on a new measure she scribbled on the whiteboard after last week's seminar and told me to work out whether its admissible. I'd rather not spend a fortnight writing up a verdict she's going to overrule, so I want to predict it first.

Her verdicts so far. Admissible: the Brier score Σ_v (p_v − δ_vw)², her benchmark; and the cubic score 2Σ_v p_v³ − 3p_w² + 1, which surprised me because it isn't convex in the credences, but she says that's fine. Inadmissible: the absolute-deviation score Σ_v |p_v − δ_vw|, about which she said something like jump to a corner and you beat your own credences; the boosted quadratic Σ_v (p_v − δ_vw)² + 0.4(1 − Σ_v p_v²), which is bounded, continuous, symmetric and convex but as far as I can tell rewards sharpening; the hedged quadratic Σ_v (p_v − δ_vw)² + 0.6 Σ_v p_v², which nudges you toward the uniform; and the weighted quadratic Σ_v c_v (p_v − δ_vw)² with c = (2, 1, 1), which she concedes is strictly proper but rejects because she isn't going to let her measure of inaccuracy care which world is which. She has never ruled on an unbounded measure — whenever the log score comes up she says let's not open that can of worms and changes the subject.

The new one (call it the one-third measure): let a_v = max(p_v − 1/3, 0), and set I(p, w) = Σ_v a_v² + (2/3) Σ_v a_v − 2a_w + 4/9. I've messed around with it numerically. Its bounded between 0 and 4/3; it's 0 when you put all your credence on the actual world; it's 4/9 at the uniform credence function no matter which world is actual; and when I compared credence functions that differ only in how they split their credence among worlds they both rate at or below a third — (0.5, 0.3, 0.2) against (0.5, 0.25, 0.25), say — their expected inaccuracy by the lights of either one came out exactly tied. I cant find a single case where you do strictly better than your own credences, only ties.

So: will she admit it, and why? What I actually want is the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her six verdicts just as well, what each of them predicts about this measure, which one you'd bet on and why (does it matter that the payload of the paper is a dominance argument for probabilism plus an expected-accuracy argument for conditionalizing?), and what one further ruling I should try to extract from her — given she still hasn't said a word about the log score — that would settle which rule she's really running.
