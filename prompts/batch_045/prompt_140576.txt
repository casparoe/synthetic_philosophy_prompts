I have a half-argument that misleading is definately the worse of the two here, for three reasons: it's deniable, it leaves the deceiver's credibility untouched (the running joke of the play is that everyone calls Iago honest, and it's not only a joke — his reputation as a blunt truth-teller is exactly the credential he spends), and it makes the victim co-author of the false belief, so Othello ends up unable to say he was lied to in any way that lets him be angry at the right person.
