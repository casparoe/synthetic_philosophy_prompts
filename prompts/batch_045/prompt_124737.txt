I'm a quantitative psychologist — my daily work is item response theory, structural equation modeling, measurement invariance testing, and signal detection theory applied to perceptual discrimination; I teach a graduate seminar on construct validation, and I'm fluent enough with d-prime versus response criterion, reflective versus causal-formative indicators, identifiability arguments, and the treatment of instrument noise that you can use all of it without explaining it. A philosopher friend at a conference dinner dragged me into the debate over illusionism about phenomenal consciousness — the Dennett–Frankish line that there are no phenomenal properties, only a systematically misleading way in which a mind represents its own states to itself, against the realist who insists that the what-it-is-like-ness of experience is a genuine feature of the world that any complete account must include. I have read some of each side since then, and I came away with the sense that neither camp is saying anything I could test, and, worse, that I cannot tell whether that is because their claims are empirically empty or because my entire toolkit is built to describe relations between a measured quantity and its indicators and simply has no slot for the thing they are fighting over.

So here is what I want. Take the illusionist's central claim and the realist's central claim, one at a time, and restate each as faithfully as you can as a claim in my vocabulary: latent variables and their indicators, reflective versus formative measurement, construct validity and what would count as evidence for it, identifiability, measurement invariance and differential item functioning, trait variance versus measurement error, d-prime versus criterion, calibration, and above all the distinction between a quantity that exists but is measured badly and a quantity that has no value beyond what our instruments return. Where the translation requires you to add an assumption my framework does not itself supply — in particular the assumption that every well-formed quantity has a determinate value that measurement merely estimates — say so explicitly and mark it as smuggling rather than translation. Then give me the residue report: which claims of each side come out as straightforward empirical or conceptual theses in measurement terms, which come out as claims about the design of the introspective report channel that my methods could in principle adjudicate, and which have no counterpart at all in anything I can say.

To make the residue concrete, I will stipulate two results, which I want you to imagine as already obtained and then use as probes. First, resolution saturation: on a stimulus continuum whose physical dimensionality we can raise without limit, subjects' forced-choice discriminability of their own sensory states keeps improving, but their trial-by-trial introspective ratings and their free descriptions stop distinguishing states once dimensionality passes some modest threshold, becoming a deterministic function of a low-dimensional summary statistic — the fine-grained structure is present in the discrimination and absent from the report. Second, report-channel clamping: an intervention that leaves forced-choice discrimination fully intact while fixing the report channel at a single output, so that the subject discriminates perfectly while consistently saying that nothing is present and denying that anything has changed in how things seem. For each stipulated result, say what the translated realist and the translated illusionist each predict about it, and where the disagreement, if any, now lives. Then the real question: is whatever remains untranslated a defect of psychometric vocabulary — something measurement theory cannot say and ought to be able to say — or is it a sign that the philosophical dispute was, at exactly that point, less substantive than it looks? I would especially like your view on whether the realist's residual notion of appearance as such, as opposed to belief or disposition to report, can be expressed as anything other than a claim about a second and better instrument, and on whether the illusionist's notion of illusion is doing work that my vocabulary would classify as a measurement artifact or work that it would classify as nothing at all.
