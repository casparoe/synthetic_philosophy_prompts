I have one fifty-minute seminar to move second-year undergraduates from the simple conditional analysis of dispositions — x is fragile iff x would break if struck — to seeing finks and masks as two different problems rather than two instances of one problem. That schema and one lecture's worth of motivation is all they have; no one has read the literature. Don't explain the debate to me; design the session.

Give me the order the components must be introduced in and what depends on what — in particular whether the reverse-fink or mimicker case belongs before or after the mask case, and what breaks if you swap them. Give me one central object with two or three variant epicycles rather than three unrelated examples, and say what that object must have and must lack to do the work: I tried a live wire wired to a cut-out device and found that the machinery upstaged the point, so I want to know what feature made it fail. Then give me the misconceptions to expect, including that a mask is just a fink we happen to approve of, that adding a ceteris paribus clause repairs the counterfactual, and that the cases show dispositions are really relations to environment — and name the exact point in the sequence where each should be pre-empted rather than corrected afterwards. End with a single novel case that discriminates between students who have the distinction and students who have only learned the labels.

Two things I want built into the plan rather than attached to it. First, the students will keep identifying the disposition with its causal basis and thereby collapsing the debate into a verbal one: tell me whether to fight that at the outset or let it surface and put it to work. Second, they must leave able to handle an interference mechanism that is part of what makes the object a well-functioning instance of its kind — a rifle whose safety prevents firing when the trigger is pulled, and which seems more, not less, disposed to fire for having it — since I suspect that case quietly breaks the fink/mask contrast as it is usually drawn, and I want it used as a stress test, not set aside. Finally, say what I should openly refuse to explain in fifty minutes.
