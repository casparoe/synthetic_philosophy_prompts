One of the POV characters is a curate who reads the Essay on the Principle of Population the year it comes out and never stops believing it, even as the factories go up, the enclosure gets worse and then better, and his own parish visibly gets richer. My problem isn't whether he's right; it's that I've agreed to run a forty-minute session for a historical-novel reading group -- smart adults, no economics, all of them have quietly absorbed that Malthus was disproved by the Green Revolution -- and I need them to get why a reasonable person could go on holding the view, not just conclude the man was stubborn.

Don't write the session, plan it. What has to be in place before "right but early" even means anything? I assume they have to see the mechanism -- more labor applied to a more or less fixed land base, diminishing returns, numbers pressing back down toward subsistence -- as a different object from the forecast, but you tell me the order and why. What one example should carry the whole thing, and what does it have to have and lack? I was gunna use yeast in a vat of sugar, but I worry that smuggles in the crash as inevitable. Then the misconceptions, in the order they'll form, and where exactly to head each off: I'm guessing "he set a date and it didn't happen", "food output grew faster than arithmetic so his ratio was just false", and the big one -- that "right but early" is only unfalsifiability with better manners. For that last one I want something sharper than "it depends": my own cut would be that a theory is early when the condition suspending it is one the theory itself named in advance, and its running an alibi when the condition gets bolted on after the failure and would have explained any outcome whatever. Is that the right place to split it? And where does the stock-versus-flow point go -- the 19th-century escape running on one-off inheritances (New World acreage, coal, guano) rather than a permanently raised ceiling -- before or after the alibi distinction, and does it actually help, or does it just leave them thinking I predicted collapse by 2050? Give me one question at the end that shows me they got the distinction instead of just liking my curate, and say plainly what I should refuse to get dragged into at this level -- I dont want to end up reading real-wage series aloud or arguing about Boserup.
