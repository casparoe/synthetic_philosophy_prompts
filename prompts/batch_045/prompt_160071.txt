yo, I need a decision tree and I keep tieing myself in knots trying to build it, so build it for me.

Background: I write the internal training handouts for a small team of analysts who have to produce single-case risk numbers — flight-medical recertifications, long-term disability claims, occasionally "will this clinic still be operating in three years" for a lender. They're numerate, they're not philosophers, and right now they pick a reference class by vibes.

What I want is a decision tree that starts at "I have exactly one individual and someone is demanding one probability" and ends at a named position on where that probability is supposed to come from.

The rules I care about:

- Every branch node has to be phrased as a question someone could answer straight off a case file, without already knowing which position it leads to. No "do you think probabilities are propensities?" — ask it about the case.
- Nine leaves maximum. Must include, in whatever order the logic actually gives you: (1) there's no single-case probability at all, only rates in classes; (2) take the narrowest class you still have reliable statistics for; (3) take the class that can't be subdivided in a way that changes the rate; (4) any class is fine as long as you're coherent; (5) there's a genuine single-case chance and the class is only evidence about it; (6) refuse one number, report the spread the admissible classes give you; (7) there is no question-independent right class, so pick it by what the number is going to be used for and what the two kinds of error cost.
- Four cases have to appear somewhere in the tree. Run the pilot one through the whole thing: 52 years old, grounded after an arrhythmia, applying to fly again — note that he can comply or not comply with medication and can simply decide to retire, so he partly picks which class he's in. Then: a three-year-old startup's chance of surviving five years; a claimant's chance of ever returning to work; and a case where the class with by far the best statistics is one we're legally not allowed too sort people by (sex, ethnicity, postcode), with a proxy variable sitting right there.
- Two branch nodes are non-negotiable, because the tree always dies on them for me: what do you do when the candidate class is partly defined by the outcome you're predicting; and what do you do when two classes both pass every test you have and disagree.

Label leaves with the usual names where you're confident about them and just describe the move in your own words where you're not — I don't want a bibliography, and I don't want anyone's name attached to a view they didn't hold.

Sections, in this order:

1. "The tree" — the whole thing, nested or numbered, 500-700 words.
2. "The leaves" — two or three sentences each, ending with the most awkward thing that position is stuck with.
3. "Empty cells" — combinations of answers your nodes allow that nobody actually occupies, and your guess as to why, ~150 words.
4. "Where it breaks" — nodes that don't really separate anything, or cases your tree handles badly, ~250 words.
