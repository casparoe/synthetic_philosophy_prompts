I am working on the metaphysics of future contingents, and specifically on a question the sea-battle argument tends to run together. I would like a case built to order that pulls two notions apart. Please work with the following four positions as I define them here, rather than importing other formulations.

- **Ockhamism**: future-tense claims are bivalent and determinately true or false at the time of utterance, because there is a privileged branch — the actual future — that settles them. (I do not think the hard/soft fact distinction will do any work in the case I want; if you think it does, say where.)
- **Occurrence-based openness**: a future-tense claim about an event that has not yet occurred has no truth value at the time of utterance; determinacy arrives with the event's occurrence or non-occurrence. Negation is internal, so the corresponding denial is likewise indeterminate beforehand.
- **Settledness-based openness**: determinacy tracks causal settling rather than occurrence. A future-tense claim is determinately true as soon as the total state of the world at the time together with the laws nomologically fixes the outcome, and determinately false as soon as they exclude it. Outcomes not yet settled remain gappy, exactly as on the occurrence view.
- **Assessment-relativism**: a future-tense claim receives a truth value only relative to a context of assessment, understood as a time together with a branch or world. One and the same utterance may be true relative to one such context and not true relative to another, and there is no assessment-independent verdict.

These last three views coincide on the standard textbook cases, which is why I need a non-standard one.

**What I want built.** An ordinary case — a clinic, a repair shop, a school, a warehouse, an architect's office; no deities, no prophets, no time travel, no exotic physics; nothing melodramatic; one paragraph — in which a professional gives a checkable assurance to someone who will rely on it, concerning an event at a later time. The case must satisfy the following.

1. *Present fixity without occurrence.* At the time the assurance is given, the outcome is already nomologically fixed by the state of the world then plus the laws, but the event has not yet occurred. The fixity must be intervention-proof: no agent's subsequent choice, and nothing anyone could have done afterward, is part of what settles it, and nothing anyone could have done would have changed it.
2. *Fixity that is not disguised ignorance.* The facts doing the settling must not have been ascertainable at the time by any means then available. State what those facts are and why they were hidden. If they are merely unknown-but-available, the case is an epistemic one and does no work.
3. *One sentence, one sense.* All four views are to evaluate the same ordinary future-tense sentence in the same sense. No trading on scope, vagueness, or a shift between "will" and "is going to."
4. *The required verdict pattern.* Design the assurance so that it is the one that comes out false. The Ockhamist and the settledness-theorist must then both say it was determinately false when given, while the occurrence-theorist must say it was indeterminate then and became determinately false only later. Derive these verdicts from the positions; do not attach them by stipulation.
5. *A downstream question that needs a positive verdict.* Build into the case a question that anyone would care about — which of two incompatible assurances the record should canonise, whose judgment an internal review should find sound, which adviser can be said to have been right at the time — such that the question demands attributing truth at the time of utterance to one of the two assurances. I want this because "not true" will not answer it: a verdict of mere indeterminacy, reached by external negation, cannot single out the assurance that was correct. The occurrence-theorist should therefore be left unable to deliver what the practice demands, or able to deliver it only by changing the subject, while the other views deliver it directly. If you think the practice can be satisfied after all by an external-negation reading, say so and tighten the case; if it cannot be tightened, say that instead.

**What I want back.**

- The scenario, followed by each view's verdict on the assurance with a sentence of reasoning, and then each view's answer to the downstream question.
- The single feature of the case that is doing the work, and what happens to every verdict under the minimal perturbation that removes that feature.
- An extensionality check: the smallest change to the case that breaks the agreement between the Ockhamist and the settledness-theorist, together with confirmation that the Ockhamist's own verdict is invariant under that change. That asymmetry is the signature I am after, and I would like it stated explicitly.
- The strongest available reply from an occurrence-theorist. I have two in mind and would like both addressed: first, that an assurance such as the one in your case carries implicit ceteris-paribus conditions whose fulfilment is itself a future matter, so that the outcome was never settled at the time after all; second, that the downstream practice ought to track fault, expected value, or assertibility rather than truth-at-the-time. Tell me which is stronger and whether either actually works.
- Two or three sentences on where assessment-relativism lands and whether your case causes it any difficulty.

**What the case is for**, so that you can tell when you are cheating: I want to know whether the "open future" that the sea-battle argument requires is openness as non-occurrence or openness as unsettledness, since the two theses cannot be distinguished by any case in which the outcome is settled only by occurring. A case that merely labels the verdicts, one that gets its fixity from a future choice, one that reduces to somebody's ignorance, or one that secures the pattern by choosing the sentence to fit the theory, is of no use to me. If some part of the specification is jointly unsatisfiable, tell me which part and why rather than quietly relaxing it.
