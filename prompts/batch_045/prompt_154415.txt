I work in mechanism design — mostly sequential screening and dynamic contracting with limited commitment — and I have been trying, and failing, to get a grip on updateless decision theory. I don't need to be convinced that it is interesting; I need it said in my own vocabulary, and then I need to know precisely what that vocabulary cannot say. So: translate first, report the residue second.

The vocabulary I have available: a mechanism as a message space together with an allocation rule and a transfer rule; types drawn from a distribution F that the design problem takes as given; strategies as functions from types to messages; Bayes–Nash equilibrium, in which each player's strategy is common knowledge among the others; the three grades of incentive compatibility — ex ante, interim, ex post — and the gap between them; the revelation principle; the principal moving first and anticipating the equilibrium strategy; commitment, limited commitment and renegotiation-proofness; sequential rationality at information sets that are never reached, plus the refinements that constrain beliefs there; and, from information design, a sender's signal structure together with the receiver's obedience constraint. Assume I already know the standard causal and evidential verdicts on the usual cases; only re-derive them where the translation needs them.

Translate the thesis, not just the cases. State, in those terms, what UDT claims — something in the neighbourhood of: evaluate the strategy once against the prior, and never re-optimize it at the interim stage. Then test that formulation on four cases: the ordinary Newcomb problem with an accurate predictor and already-packed boxes; the transparent-boxes variant; the counterfactual mugging; and the hitchhiker who cannot pay the driver unless driven to town. Where UDT and functional decision theory come apart, flag it, but keep UDT as the subject.

Be exact about what updatelessness refuses to condition on. Three readings occur to me, and I can't tell them apart from the literature I've managed to read. First: it refuses interim evaluation — the agent maximizes ex ante and treats the realized type as irrelevant to what it should now do. Second: it refuses indexical conditioning — the strategy is scored as though its output were realized at every input in its domain simultaneously, weighted by prior measure over inputs, including inputs it knows it won't receive. Third: it refuses something my framework has no name for, because my framework identifies the agent with the type-conditional action and UDT identifies it with the whole function. Which reading is right, and does the choice change any verdict?

The case I actually care about, which I have not seen discussed. Suppose the Predictor cannot read my strategy and does not simulate me in the situation I'm in. It has black-box query access to my decision procedure, a budget of exactly one query, and it spends that query on a decoy input: it runs me on the input where I have been shown, veridically and transparently, that the box is empty. I know I will not face that input; the real setup is opaque. The decoy's own payoffs are stipulated to be zero — the run is terminated the moment it returns an action. If the decoy returns the one-box action, today's real box is full. Does UDT have a determinate verdict about what my policy should return on the decoy input, and is it the same as its verdict on the input I will actually get? My suspicion is that everything distinctive about updatelessness is packed into this case: whether the decoy's output counts as an output of my decision procedure for the purposes of the prior-weighted sum, or whether the policy may treat it as a separate, payoff-irrelevant computation. I also want the mechanism-design translation to render its own verdict here, because I suspect it has one — via sequential rationality at unreached information sets and constraints on off-path beliefs — and I can't tell whether it is the same verdict or merely a similar-looking one.

Then the residue report, in three buckets. What translated with no loss. What translated only after I had to alter something in the target framework or in UDT — say which, and say who moved. And what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all: I nominate two candidates, the notion of one algorithm instantiated in several places with uncertainty over which instance I am, and the use of a prior over the outputs of a computation I perform myself. For each item in each bucket, give me your view on whether the residue indicts mechanism design or shows that UDT was underspecified where it looked precise.

One last thing, since I expect it to be the load-bearing point: my framework takes the type distribution to be exogenous to the mechanism, and Newcomb's problem is exactly a violation of that. Is refusing to relax that assumption a discipline my framework is right to maintain, or the specific reason it cannot see what UDT is doing? Don't settle for telling me it is a modelling choice.
