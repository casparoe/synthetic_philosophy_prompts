I have no academic background in philosophy — I got into this stuff on my own, mostly arguing online — and I've ended up defending a strong suffering-first view, stronger than most people I argue with are willing to go.

The part I think is my own (I havent seen it put this way, maybe I just havent read enough) is a thing I call the veto test. A pleasure's claim on us seems cancellable by the person who would have it: if you decline it, nothing is left over, nothing is owed, no residue. But a pain's claim doesn't seem cancellable by the person who has it: the boxer who agrees to be hit, the person who consents to painful surgery, even the masochist — their pain still counts against the world in a way that a declined pleasure never counted for it. So suffering makes a demand that doesn't need anyone's endorsement, while happiness makes one that does. From that I get three things: suffering is where moral reasons come from in the first place, happiness matters only downstream (by preventing or soothing suffering, or by being partly made of its absence), and there's a lexical edge — past some threshold of severity, no amount of happiness trades off against it.

So: what would count against this? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want the ones a suffering-first person would actually concede separated from the ones they'd be right to shrug off. Include the ones that have arguably already happened — real cases where the veto test returns an answer I'd hate (consented pain, pain people seek out and are glad about afterwards, maybe just intense effort), and cases where the no-tradeoffs part looks like it breaks. And be honest about whether I've quietly built something unfalsifiable: is suffering doing enough hidden work in my argument that nothing could ever count against it, and if so is that fatal for a view of this kind, or just what foundational moral claims are like? What would move me most is something showing the veto test doesn't really track the thing I care about — not another downstream consequence I have to swallow.
