I sit in on a fortnightly epistemology reading group, and after last week's session on Nozick's tracking account of knowledge — the account in *Philosophical Explanations* (1981), on which a belief counts as knowledge only if it varies with the facts in both directions: were p false, S would not believe it by that method, and were p true and S to use that method, S would believe it — two of the regulars, Marta and Devin, got into an argument and asked me to settle it. I could not.

Both accept as read the usual objection that tracking so understood is not closed under known entailment: I can know that I have hands, know that having hands entails not being a brain in a vat, competently draw the conclusion, and still fail to know that I am not a brain in a vat, because at the nearest envatted worlds my belief is exactly what it is here. Their dispute is about whether that result is a defect of the account and what would have to be added to remove it. I have reconstructed their exchange as six moves.

(1) Marta: the closure failure is an artifact of how we test the derived belief. When I deduce q from p, the method by which I come to believe q is the composite procedure: run method M, get p, apply modus ponens. Test the composite at the nearest worlds where q is false. At those worlds p is false too, and since p is tracked, M does not deliver p there; so the composite procedure yields nothing, and no belief in q is produced. Tracking is inherited through the deduction, and closure holds for one-step deduction. If that means the standard verdict about the vat has to go, so much the worse for that verdict.

(2) Devin: that argument changes the quantifier. Tracking's first condition is not that at every world where p is false the method goes silent; it is a claim about the nearest worlds where p is false. Marta is entitled to silence at the near ones and gets nothing about the far ones. And the nearest worlds where q is false need not lie anywhere near the nearest worlds where p is false. Vat worlds are worlds where I have no hands, hence p-falsifying worlds, but remote ones, and my vision delivers hands there just as it does here. The composite fires and outputs the conclusion. Closure fails for a structural reason: entailment shrinks the falsity region of the conclusion to a subset of the premise's, and that pruning can remove every nearby world, leaving only remote ones where no method of mine was ever required to be reliable.

(3) Marta: then make distance explicit. Attach to each belief a reach: the modal distance out to which its method has to be reliable in the relevant way. The natural rule for deduction is inheritance — the derived belief inherits its premise's reach — since it would be arbitrary for the reach to grow merely because the conclusion is weaker. Predictions: from there are exactly three people in the room I can know there are at least three, because the nearby worlds falsifying the weaker claim are also worlds that falsify the exact count and at which my count changes; and from I have hands I can know I am not a brain in a vat, because vat worlds sit far outside the reach across which my vision's reliability was ever checked. She accepts the second verdict: it was always the skeptic's premise, not the deduction, that should be rejected.

(4) Devin: inheritance makes knowledge cheap in precisely the wrong direction. Take that animal in the pen is a dog, which I track, and it is not a wolf in a convincing dog costume, which follows from it. Every costume world is remote, so under an inherited reach there is no world at which the derived belief is ever tested; it passes vacuously, and I know the negation of every far-fetched possibility, provided only that the possibility is remote and entailed by something I happen to track. Nothing was established about costume worlds — they were excluded from the test by a radius calibrated on ordinary error. Marta has not shown that closure holds; she has shown that a constraint calibrated on nearby errors goes vacuous once the only remaining errors are distant ones. Call the gap between the premise's falsity region and the conclusion's falsity region modal slack.

(5) Marta: the charge of cheapness indicts the original account just as much. On the tracking account as given, I know that I have hands while conceding that this very belief is insensitive at envatted worlds; sensitivity was never demanded at remote worlds, so remote insensitivity was never counted against the premise. Devin cannot then treat remote insensitivity as fatal when it appears in the conclusion. Either remote error possibilities defeat knowledge everywhere — and we are skeptics about hands as well as about the vat — or they defeat it nowhere, and closure survives.

(6) Devin: that dichotomy is too quick. Tolerance of remote insensitivity is not a policy the account adopts; it falls out of where the nearest falsifying worlds happen to lie for each proposition. For hands the nearest falsifying worlds are ones my method handles; for its weaker consequence the nearest falsifying worlds are ones it does not. That is not an inconsistency for Marta to exploit but the geometry of entailment combined with a nearest-world semantics for the counterfactual — which is why a requirement framed as sensitivity is unstable under weakening of the proposition believed. And if Marta wants the reach fixed by something other than inheritance, she must say what fixes it: if it is the neighborhood of the proposition in question, closure fails in every case with modal slack; if it is context, stakes, or what the speaker treats as a live alternative, then the subjunctive machinery has stopped doing any work and the tracking in the theory is decorative.

Please adjudicate this move by move. For each of the six, say whether it is clearly mistaken, partly right, or clearly right, and in a sentence or two why — I want the reasoning behind each rating, not the ratings alone. Then say who is more right overall, and what, if anything, both of them are missing: a distinction neither drew, an assumption both shared, a version of the question neither asked. I do not need references or further reading; I have the texts. I want the structure of the disagreement set out.

Two further things. First, where a verdict turns on something you take to be genuinely contested — how counterfactuals select worlds, what individuates a method, whether a reach can be inherited, whether the costume case is really worse than the vat case — say so, and say how much of your verdict rests on it; I would rather have a rating flagged as conditional than a confident one I cannot trust. Second, if my reconstruction rests on a confusion, say so plainly: if one of the six moves misstates what tracking requires, if reach is not a coherent notion, or if the three things they both want — ordinary knowledge, closure for ordinary deductions, and withholding on the vat — are not really in tension, tell me that instead of splitting the difference politely.
