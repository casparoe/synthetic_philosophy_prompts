The bit of Lakatos I've absorbed (hard core, protective belt, positive vs negative heuristic, progressive vs degenerating problem shifts, and the thing where a patch that only mops up an anomaly you already had counts as degeneration instead of progress) feels like it maps onto this almost too well. But I only have the blog-post version, and I can't turn it into something four senior engineers will sit still for in a 90 minute meeting. They'll walk out if I say heuristic.

So: give me a decision procedure we can actually run in that meeting. Ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands off to a human because the question is really about values not facts. Hard cases I specifically need it to handle:

How do we tell an honest belt adjustment from an immunizing stratagem under those conditions?

That's protected by fiat, so nothing can ever falsify it. Does that need a fourth verdict besides keep / freeze / kill, and if so what is it and who signs off?

If that's right, bake a time window into the procedure: how many quarters of degeneration before we pull the plug, and is there a case were the right answer is to keep funding something that's currently degenerating?

Finish by being straight about what the procedure can't do and where it'll misfire on us.
