Dennett presses Searle with the case of a two-bitser: a cheap vending-machine coin mechanism whose internal state we describe without hesitation as being about a quarter, even though nothing in the mechanism knows or means anything. Searle's reply is that such a state has at most derived intentionality — it borrows its aboutness from designers and users who enjoy original intentionality — and that no increase in complexity turns borrowed intentionality into the real thing.

Now take a variant. The mechanism was not designed by anyone who had quarters in mind: it came out of an automated search over circuit designs that was rewarded only for successful discrimination in an environment containing quarters, and its makers never knew what it discriminated. It now sits in an unvisited warehouse, where it periodically retunes its own discriminator on the basis of its own failed discriminations; after enough retuning it never misfires, and no one is interpreting it at all.

Give me a number for this proposition: the original-derived distinction marks a genuine difference in kind rather than a difference in how much interpretive work an ascriber performs, so that this device's states have at most derived or as-if intentionality and could not have original intentionality in virtue of its organization, history, or causal embedding, however self-modifying or reliable it became. My own credence is around 0.45, and I want to know whether that is complacent.

Decompose it into the sub-questions the number turns on, with a probability and one line of justification for each, then combine them. I suggest these, plus any you think belong: whether a device can be said to misrepresent at all, and whether aboutness requires that possibility; whether a discriminator that has become perfectly reliable still has content; whether derived intentionality can be inherited from a source that never had the relevant content, or must trace back to an intention with that content; whether the regress from derived to original bottoms out in something Searle is entitled to, such as specifically biological causal powers; and whether the force of the Chinese Room argument really depends on this distinction rather than on something about syntax, causation, or consciousness. Then tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to, and which consideration would move it furthest.
