I've just come out of a long argument with my closest friend, who is leaving a job she is good at to open a small restaurant. I kept saying that the base rate on that kind of venture is grim and that this is where any sane probability has to start; she kept saying that her case is relevantly different and that I was being pessimistic by reflex rather than for a reason. I went home and tried to write down the general position I was appealing to, and reading it back I cannot tell which parts of it can survive together. Here is where I think I have landed:

(1) Where a case can be placed in a reference class with decent frequency data, the base rate is the starting point for my probability, and case-specific evidence should move it only if I could have specified that evidence in advance as something that would have separated the successes from the failures within that class.

(2) Some cases are genuinely unprecedented, with nothing usable to serve as a base rate, and there the inside view is all one has; relying on it in that situation is not arrogance but the absence of an alternative.

(3) Every case belongs to indefinitely many reference classes and the case itself does not single out the right one, so there is an irreducible judgment in applying the outside view at all.

(4) When someone roughly as well-informed as I am disagrees with me, I should move substantially toward them, and holding my ground is a failure of modesty; though I do take my own track record on the kind of call in question, where I have one, to be a reason to hold more ground than I otherwise would.

(5) I should still spend most of my analytical effort on the inside view, on working out the specific causal story, even though (1) says the outside view should dominate the number I end up with.

(6) Applied to her: I would put the chance of failure within three years at roughly the base rate, and I also think she should do it, and I told her I would invest.

Please audit the set rather than argue for any part of it. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which only appear to be, and for each real tension what the cheapest revision would be and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them flat. If something here has to go, tell me which thing it is; but do not assume from the way I have written this that I already know where the problem is, or that the item I would give up is the one you would pick.
