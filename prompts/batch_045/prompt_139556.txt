I volunteer (unpaid, long story) at a small county historical society, mostly typing old ledgers into a spreadsheet, and I keep bumping into a pattern I don't have a word for.

Two examples. We have almshouse admission books from the 1890s that are incredible in their detail: height, scars, whether the person was known to drink, who their kin were, where they'd been the year before. But those people are in our collection almost entirely because they got admitted somewhere. Between admissions they're one line in a tax list. So if you sit down to write about poor people in this county using what we have, what you end up writing about is poor people at the exact moments they were being processed by an institution.

Second: a gay bar in the county seat in the late sixties. The richest folder we have on it by far is the sheriff's vice file, arrest logs, a license revocation hearing, photographs taken during a raid. The bar's own newsletter survived as about fourteen issues stuffed in a box of some landlord's papers. Anyone researching that community reconstructs it from the night it got raided.

Here's what bugs me. This isn't just that the sources are slanted. It's that the slant and the thickness point the same direction. The more the record says, the more the person in it was being acted on rather than acting. Which means the usual fix doesn't fix anything: if you notice the source is hostile and discount for it, what you learn is about the sheriff, not about the bar. Discounting costs you exactly the detail that made the source valuable. The record being full and the record being distorted look like the same fact rather than two facts you can trade off against each other.

I also think this is true of big text collections scraped off the internet, which is part of why I care, but I'll leave that alone before I go off on a tangent.

Is there an established name for it? I don't mean bias in sources, or missing records, or hostile sources, those are nearby but they all seem to miss the inversion, that completeness and distortion arrive together so you can't correct one by adjusting for the other. If there is a concept that covers it, I'd want to know what its neighbors are and how it differs from each one. If there isn't, I'd like a coined term with an actual definition and some edge cases, because I definately don't just want a label, I want to know where the thing stops. For instance: does it still count when the recorder has no interest in controlling anybody and just has a different purpose, like a hospital logging what a midwife did? Does a deliberate community collection, people recording their own history on purpose, escape it, or does it just swap which distortion you get, since now the record shows you how they wanted to look? And is a thick self-authored record like a diary the same phenomenon, or the clean opposite?

Sorry, that's a lot of questions. The short version is: what is this, and what is it not.
