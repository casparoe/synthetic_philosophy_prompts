The new thing is called a Virgin Materials Budget. Binding declining annual ceilings — 6% a year from 2030, so roughly half by the early 2040s — on virgin material tonnage in five classes: cement clinker, primary steel, primary aluminium, virgin plastics feedstock, and construction aggregate. The ceilings are enforced by auctioning a shrinking pool of tradeable permits denominated in tonnes, surrendered at the point where the material first enters the domestic economy: mine mouth, quarry gate, port of entry — so imports need permits too, which is meant to answer the leakage objection. Banking allowed for two years, no price floor and no ceiling. Recycled content is exempt from the cap, but only if certified through a mass-balance chain-of-custody scheme. Revenue is not rebated to households; it's earmarked to transit operating budgets and home insulation retrofits. One wrinkle for scale: cement, steel and aluminium come from a few hundred metered facilities, aggregate is most of the tonnage but comes out of thousands of small quarries at a few dollars a tonne, and the plastics exemption is where the lobbying has been heaviest.
