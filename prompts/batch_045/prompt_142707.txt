I work in quality control at a materials lab and read metaphysics in the evenings, and I keep running into a pattern I have no word for. I know the standard counterexamples to reading *x is disposed to M in C* as *x would M if C obtained*: the fink, where the stimulus itself removes the disposition (a wire that goes dead the instant it is touched), and the mask, where the disposition survives but something else blocks the manifestation (a fragile glass too well padded to break). What I keep noticing is the reverse configuration: the conditional comes out true and the behaviour shows up, but not, as far as I can tell, because the thing ever had the disposition.

Two cases from work. (1) A metal sample is not malleable at room temperature, but hammering it heats it past the point where it becomes malleable, so it flattens: the test seems to install the disposition it appears to reveal. (2) A compound that does not dissolve in water is dropped into water; the water trips a mechanism that pulverises it, so it disperses: the visible signature of solubility, produced by something other than the sample's own solubility.

Is there an established concept here, and are (1) and (2) instances of the same one? I suspect they are not: in (1) the object seems to acquire the disposition and then genuinely exercise it, whereas in (2) the manifestation never involves the object's own disposition at all. I also want the boundary drawn: does the glass that shatters the instant it is struck, but because of an unrelated vibration, belong with these or not?

Please don't just hand me a label. Say what exactly the phenomenon is, how it differs from finks, from masks, and from a merely deviant causal chain, and whether the usual repair to the conditional analysis (an intrinsic property that would yield the manifestation absent interference) is even aimed at this direction. If nothing established fits, coin a term and give me a definition with its boundary cases. Answer in three short labelled parts, under 500 words.
