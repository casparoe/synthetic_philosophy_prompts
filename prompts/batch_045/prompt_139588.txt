I'm writing up a statement of my own position for a bioethics seminar on human enhancement, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present it. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) The therapy/enhancement distinction is morally significant in exactly one respect: the state has a positive duty to secure access to therapy, but no comparable duty to provide or fund enhancement — though it doesn't follow from that enhancement may be forbidden.

(2) The line has to be drawn by reference to disease, and disease has to be understood naturalistically, as departure from species-typical functioning rather than as whatever a given society happens to disvalue. That is why I don't think being gay, being short, or being an unruly child can count as a disease.

(3) The natural distribution of health and talent is brute luck and therefore morally arbitrary; justice requires compensating people for natural disadvantage, not merely for disease.

(4) I find the social-model critique from disability studies persuasive: for conditions such as deafness or dwarfism, a large part of the disadvantage is produced by how society is arranged rather than by the body itself.

(5) Even so, I think a deaf couple who use embryo selection in order to have a deaf child do something pro tanto wrong. And I don't accept the familiar objections to enhancement from cheating, inauthenticity, or the positional arms race — on the last of these, we don't object to education on those grounds — so if an enhancement is safe and voluntary, the state should leave people alone.

(6) To handle the baseline problem, I've floated a fix in seminar: count an intervention as therapy when it returns a person to their own earlier level of functioning, an individual rather than a species-typical baseline, so that treating age-related decline or a degenerative condition comes out as therapy.

Which of these are genuinely in tension and which only look that way? Please rank the real tensions by severity, say what I would have to give up in each case, and tell me whether there is a single revision that would clean up the most. I have a vague worry that (4) and (5) can't both be true, but I can't tell whether that is the actual fault line. Kindly write your answer in British English.
