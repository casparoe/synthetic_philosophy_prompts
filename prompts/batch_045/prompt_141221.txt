I have been reading the rationalist blogs since roughly the original sequences — anthropic decision theory, SIA versus SSA, the measure problem, the recurring Boltzmann brain threads — and the thing I have never seen stated cleanly is what I am *allowed to demand* of a cosmology here. Every discussion I have read either fixes a measure and reports the verdict that falls out of it, or announces that the worry is harmless for decision-theoretic reasons, and almost nobody stops to say what a theory would have to deliver before I ought to accept its answer about which kind of observer I am. So I want the specification rather than another tour of the options: write out the desiderata a cosmological theory must satisfy for me to be entitled to take my own evidence at face value — to conclude that I am an ordinary observer embedded in a long, mostly lawlike causal history rather than a fluctuation with a moment of spurious experience — with the case or argument that makes each one a requirement, and be explicit about which are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of one camp.

The candidates I would start from, which you can keep, split, or throw out: that ordinary observers dominate fluctuations in number under the theory's own measure; that the verdict does not swing wildly between cutoffs (proper time, scale factor, causal patch, light-cone time) or between coarse-grainings of what counts as an observer-moment; that the theory does not undermine itself, in the sense that accepting it would force me to discount the reliability of the reasoning that got me there; that it engages with the *specific contents* of my evidence — my memories of a lab, of correspondence, of stable public records — rather than only with the bare fact that I have some evidence, since a fluctuation reproducing all of that is far rarer than a fluctuation reproducing a moment; that it has something principled to say about the continuum between a bare brain, a brain plus a hallucinated environment, and what I would call a "Boltzmann archive," a fluctuation large enough to contain internally consistent structure of the sort that would make most of its occupant's beliefs true; that it grades reliability by domain of belief rather than returning a single global verdict, since the natural outcomes here are mixtures; that the conditional fraction of fluctuation-observers converges as I accumulate evidence rather than oscillating; and that even in the bad case the theory remains action-guiding, because on the branch where I am a fluctuation nothing I do makes a difference and so every decision I make should be made as if I were not. I also want the two places where I expect the list to be doing real work: first, whether excluding fluctuations by fiat — by declaring them impossible on kinetic grounds, or by ruling them outside the reference class of observers — is a genuine solution or just a rule that also quietly excludes ordinary observers; and second, whether the "youngness"-style implication, that typical observers should find themselves absurdly early, is a real cost or an artifact of one family of measures.

The part I care most about is the structure of the list itself rather than any scoring: tell me which of these demands are genuine and which are the inherited prejudices of people who grew up arguing about reference classes, and if some subset of the desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied — my suspicion is that non-self-undermining and any measure-relative dominance condition are on a collision course, and that nothing survives both a cutoff-invariance requirement and a genuinely infinite spacetime — say which ones have to go and what the resulting, weaker standard is worth. Then tell me which single requirement excludes the most candidate theories, and, only briefly, score four stances against the list: fix the measure and the problem disappears; fluctuations are physically impossible so the question never arises; the possibility is real but decision-irrelevant; and the correct anthropic update screens it off entirely. If it helps to fix the purpose: I want a theory good enough that I can keep doing physics and planning next year without multiplying anything by a discount factor for probably being a fluctuation.
