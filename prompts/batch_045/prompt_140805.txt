hey, i need a piece of satire for a reading packet i'm putting together on whether history can actually be quantified and forecast — the cliodynamics side of it: structural-demographic theory, long secular cycles of roughly a century or two in which population pressure, elite overproduction, popular immiseration and state fiscal distress drive a society through the same four phases over and over, with instability peaking near the bottom.

what i want from you is the annual letter to investors from a hedge fund — call it something like Secular Cycle Capital — written maybe fifteen years after launch, 800-900 words plus a short sidebar. the fund's founding thesis was that a large industrial democracy was entering the crisis phase of a secular cycle within the decade. it has been humiliatingly wrong on timing: down some ugly number while the index went up several times over. the joke is that nothing that has happened can possibly count as evidence against the model, because the model's timescale is "one to six generations" and the phase boundaries get re-dated after the fact every single year. so the fund was not wrong, it was early; the onset of stagflation has now been re-dated to the 1970s and the crisis phase to the mid-2010s; a standing Phase-Dating Committee issues revisions the way a central bank issues minutes.

some requirements:

- voice is deadpan corporate-finance earnestness. no winking, no nudging, no asides that signal you know this is absurd. the letter should read like it was written by someone who genuinely believes the model and is extremely good with a spreadsheet.
- the comedy has to come out of the substance. a reader who actually knows this literature should wince in recognition. i want the specific moves: elite overproduction operationalized as the swelling count of credentialed aspirants chasing a fixed number of elite slots, real wages decoupled from output per head, the number of billionaires, state debt service as a share of revenue; a couple of centuries of a handful of countries fitted to a phase diagram; "instability" compressed into a single scalar index that people can buy exposure to; a composite reported to two decimal places with a quarterly delta.
- at least one moment where the length of the cycle openly swallows any possible refutation — a footnote conceding the crisis phase may run to the 2060s, or that the depression phase is when you should really have been accumulating.
- new products for the coming year, with prices or fee schedules: something like a futures contract on the elite-pressure composite, an "intercycle" fund for the long depression ahead, a subscription tier that sells you the revised phase dates before they are announced.
- one paragraph on reflexivity treated as a selling point. the fund's own clients buying gold, farmland and hardened shelters in anticipation of the crisis is itself part of the mechanism that produces it, and the letter is quietly proud of this: it is not merely forecasting the crisis phase, it is underwriting it. keep it as an arbitrage opportunity, not as a moral panic — that's funnier and it's the fairer version of the objection.
- a sidebar of 100-150 words, an FAQ in the same voice, and a closing disclaimer that quietly guts everything above it.
- invented numbers, invented staff and invented internal metrics are all fine and encouraged, but don't name-check real papers, real authors or real datasets — no bibliography, no literature footnotes, nothing you'd need to look up. everything from your own head. and the target should be the practice and its style of argument, not any real person: the fund, its chief strategist and its clients are all made up.
- the reductio should be legible without you ever stating it. a reader should finish and be able to say what exactly is being made fun of.

about 800-900 words total including the sidebar, and feel free to leave a typo or two in; it's going in a pretty informal packet.
