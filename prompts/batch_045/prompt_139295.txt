I'm working on what I'll call the two-sided problem of acausal threats, and before I commit to any decision theory I want the specification such a theory has to meet. Write the desiderata for an account of rational choice adequate to both sides: first, an agent facing a threat from a being whose existence — or whose decision to punish — is causally independent of the choice the threat is meant to influence, of the shape where the threatened party is a simulation or reconstruction and carrying out the punishment is certain to cost the threatener more than complying would have gained it; second, an agent that must not itself become a threat-maker, which is the case that matters most if the agent is an AI running a surrogate rather than a terminal goal, since a proxy-maximizer's threats are credible only if executing them still scores well on the proxy. Give the motivating case behind each desideratum, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are the preferences of one camp: I suspect "never reward a threat that is worthless to the threatener once the choice is made" and "cooperate with agents whose choices are subjunctively but not causally dependent on yours" both appear on somebody's list, and I want to know whether they can be had together. Two further constraints: the theory must not make action impossible in advance by requiring the agent to model every possible punisher before it can choose anything at all, and it must say what difference, if any, it makes whether the target is the original agent, a copy, or a mere prediction of one. Flag any subset you think is jointly unsatisfiable — if the list cannot be met in full, that is the result I most want. Borrowed cases are fine, invented ones better; keep it to the criteria and the cases that justify them, and confine any remarks about functional decision theory or evidential-style views to a sentence each rather than a scorecard.
