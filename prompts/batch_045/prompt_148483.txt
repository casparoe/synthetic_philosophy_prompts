I've got no philosophy background at all — I work at a distribution centre and got into this from a blog post I read on my breaks about something called "sphexishness". The example was a digger wasp: she drags a paralysed cricket to her burrow, leaves it on the doorstep, goes inside to check the burrow is clear, comes back out and pulls the cricket in. If you move the cricket a few inches while she's inside, she comes out, drags it back to the exact threshold, and goes in to check again — and you can make her do this over and over. Apparently some philosopher had the idea from this, though I can't remember who.

I've been writing down what I actually think about it and I'd like someone to tell me whether it hangs together, because it definately feels like it doesn't and I can't find the seam.

(a) The wasp is the right picture of what's wrong with mechanical behaviour: the routine keeps running even when the world has changed in a way that makes it pointless.
(b) Someone in the comments said the story is probably embellished and real digger wasps aren't anywhere near that dumb, and that sounds right to me — but I also think it doesn't touch the point. The wasp in the story is still exactly what I mean by sphexish even if no actual wasp ever did it.
(c) Sphexishness isn't about an behaviour's being caused, or predictable, or determined. It's about the shape of the control: whether the system can be brought to revise what it's doing in light of how things actually are. So determinism isn't the threat here, and a fully deterministic system could still be non-sphexish.
(d) I made up this pair of warehouse robots, Loop and Tally. Both pick up a box, carry it to the chute, set it down, walk in to check the chute, come back and load it. If the box is moved while they're checking, they go back, reset it and check again. They behave identically in every environment I can think of to test them in, including ones nobody built them for. But Loop's check-and-reset is a blind loop left over from a badly written routine, while Tally's is there because in the environment Tally was originally built for, boxes do get shifted around often enough that the check pays off on average. I want to say Loop is sphexish and Tally isn't, even though I can't design any experiment that tells them apart.
(e) I also think whether a system is sphexish is an empirical question you settle by putting it in weird environments and watching whether it revises — not by asking about its history or how it came to be built.
(f) On free will I hold two things I can't reconcile: given (c) I think determinism is compatible with acting freely. But I also think that if what I choose tonight is already fixed by the past plus the laws, then all my deliberating is theatre and nothing was ever really up to me.

Which of these actually clash, which only look like they clash, and how bad is each one? Rank them, tell me for each what would have to give, and then tell me which single change would clean up the most. My friend says (d) and (e) can't both be true; I think the problem is really (a) and (b). I suspect we're both wrong.
