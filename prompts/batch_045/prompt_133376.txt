I got into this whole mess because my lunch group at work (we're all backend/infra engineers, I have zero formal philosophy training) did a sort of book club on a pop-physics book about black holes, and I ended up arguing for weeks with one coworker who's read a bit more philosophy than the rest of us. Eventually he said I should just write down what I actually believe, because he was pretty sure half of it was inconsistent. I did, and honestly it all looks fine to me, so I'd like someone to audit it properly. Here's the list in my own words, and sorry in advance if the terminology is home-made:

(1) There's a complete microphysical state of the world at any given time and it fixes everything else — no extra ingredients, no downward causation, nothing emergent in the spooky sense. (2) At bottom the dynamics are reversible and information-preserving; nothing is ever fundamentally deleted. (3) The thermodynamic arrow of time is an objective feature of the world — entropy really does go up, it isn't just a bookkeeping artifact of what we happen to be ignorant about. (4) I want to say something like: if two total theories agree on every observation any observer could ever make, then there's no further fact of the matter about which one is true. My own made-up version of this: call two theories sealed twins if they agree on the observable universe forever but disagree inside a region that nothing can ever send a signal out of — a black hole interior, somewhere past a cosmological horizon, a sealed vault nobody will ever open — and I'm inclined to say sealed twins aren't really two different theories at all. (5) But I'm also an unapologetic realist about unobservable stuff: electrons, the core of the sun, what's actually inside that sealed vault, whether it contains a diamond or a lump of coal. There's a definite answer even though nobody can ever look. (6) And how much information is in region R strikes me as an objective, observer-independent quantity, the same kind of thing as how much mass is in there — which is why I also want to say that after you scramble a hard drive into thermal noise the information is still in the world, just scrambled past anyone's ability to recover it.

So: does this hang together? (4) and (5) are the obvious head-on collision, and my attempted fix is that (4) is a rule about when two formalisms count as the same theory while (5) is a claim about what exists, so they aren't even talking about the same thing — my coworker says that dodge does no real work, and I genuinely can't tell if he's right. I also have a nagging feeling that (2), (3) and (6) might be quietly fighting each other, because the information that's objective and conserved in (2) and (6) doesn't look like the same quantity that is going up in (3), and if it isn't, then (6) might not be doing the job I want it too. And (1) might be completely innocent or might be doing more work than I think. I'm not attached to any particular one of these — I just want to know where the real trouble is.

Can you go through the pairs and tell me which are actually in tension and which only look like it, ranked by severity rather than flagged all at once, and for each real tension what the cheapest revision is and what it costs me, meaning what I'd have to believe instead?
