I'm a backend engineer with no formal philosophy background — I ended up in a reading group a colleague runs, and the last couple of sessions were on the philosophy of mind, where I bumped into the phrase "multiple realizability." A friend in the group said the thing I'm about to describe sounds like the "same function, different implementation" idea, and that some philosophers hang a whole theory of mind on it, which is why I'm asking here rather than settling it in a code review. The phrase seems to cover part of what I have in mind and not the rest, and I'd like to know whether the rest has a name.

Three cases from work. First: a service that assigned records to buckets was written three times over the years — once as a comparison-based sort, once as a distribution sort over integer keys, and once, for a narrow legacy key space, as a precomputed table of answers. All three passed the same contract tests, no caller could tell them apart, and everyone was happy to say: one job, three implementations. Second: two components that labelled incoming support tickets. One was a trained model; the other was a hardcoded map from the exact strings in our test corpus to labels, plus a fallback for everything else. For months their outputs agreed on every input either of them actually saw — but nobody would say they were doing the same thing. The objections were all of the form "it hasn't learned the rule," "it's memorised the test set," "just wait for the inputs to drift" — that is, they were about inputs that had not happened yet. Third, a case of a different shape: the same module, same code, no changes at all, redeployed downstream of a different parser, so that the bytes arriving at it meant something different. Its internals were untouched and its behaviour on those bytes was unchanged, and yet we said it was no longer doing the same thing.

So: is there an established concept — or several — that carves up this territory? The piece I most need is whatever separates case two from case one: both pairs agree on all actual behaviour, but in one we grant sameness and in the other we withhold it, and what seems to do the work is behaviour in situations that haven't arisen yet. Is that the same phenomenon as multiple realizability, a special case of it, a consequence of it, or something else entirely? And is case three the same phenomenon too, or a separate one — something about how much of the surrounding system gets to count as part of the thing being described? I'd also like to know how one would settle case two in a principled way: does the difference live in the module right now, or only in our expectations about what it will be fed later? Please don't just hand me a label — I want the boundaries: what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, which of my three cases belong together and which don't, and where the philosophically interesting line actually falls.
