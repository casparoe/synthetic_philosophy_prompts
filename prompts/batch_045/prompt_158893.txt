The situation: I am applying for a nine-month dissertation fellowship and need roughly a two-page single-spaced prospectus. My half-formed idea is this. Most discussion of the wager treats its payoff structure as fixed in advance: one deity, one reward condition, an infinite prize, and a decision problem whose answer is supposed to fall out of dominance reasoning. But real theistic reward conditions are often sensitive to things the simple model holds constant. They can be history-sensitive: the reward may be available only to belief that was not acquired for mercenary reasons, or only to belief that arrived as grace rather than as something the agent manufactured. They can be frequency-sensitive: the reward may be tied to belonging to a small or persecuted remnant, or to being among the first, or to believing what those around you do not. Once the reward condition has either feature, the single-agent dominance argument stops being a dominance argument at all, and something else is needed: a fixed-point condition in the history-sensitive case, because whether the recommended action produces the reward depends on why the agent performed it, and a game-theoretic or population-level analysis in the frequency-sensitive case, because whether it produces the reward depends on what other agents do.

Turn this into a proposal. I want: a precise statement of the question, narrow enough to have a determinate answer in nine months; an explanation of why the existing positions leave it open, including an explicit account of why my question is not just the familiar many-gods objection wearing a new hat, and why it is not just the old objection about whether belief is voluntary; the method, month by month, including what I would actually write in each stretch; the range of possible conclusions and which considerations would decide between them; what a genuinely useful deliverable looks like, both for decision theorists and for people who want to know whether theistic traditions of the sincere-faith or remnant type are even coherent objects of wager-style reasoning; and an honest statement of what would count as the project failing, including the possibility that the whole apparatus turns out to be a special case of a problem about dominance arguments generally.
