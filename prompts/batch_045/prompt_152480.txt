hey, so im doing this weird crossover thing for school — half creative writing, half this philosophy unit we're doing on democracy — and i need a short story. like an actual story, with people and scenes and weather and stuff, not a debate with labels on it. but i want the plot to *be* the problem, you know? the thing should be impossible to resolve, not because everyone's stubborn, but because of how the question is built.

the problem: who actually gets to vote on something.

rough setup, change whatever you want: a small coastal town (like 400 people, mostly fishing and a bit of tourism) is voting on whether to let a big resort development go through. if it passes, a developer builds a hotel complex, dredges the harbour, and brings in around 900 workers who'd live in a new block of housing on the edge of town — and a fishing village two bays over loses most of its fishing grounds. if it fails, the town keeps the harbour as it is, the village keeps its fishery, and the town slowly empties out as young people leave, so the kids who stay inherit a dying place. our school only has about 60 seats in it and nobody knows what happens to it either way.

here's the bit i really care about, and its the bit i want the story built around. everyone in the story agrees on some version of "whoever's affected should get a say." but who's affected depends on how the vote goes. the workers only exist as affected people if the resort gets built — if it fails they never come and they're not affected at all. the other village is affected *a lot* if it passes and only *mildly* if it fails (they trade with the town either way). so you can't fix the list of voters until you know the result, and you can't get a legit result until you've fixed the list. it loops.

what i want in the story:

1. three or four characters who each have a genuinely decent answer to "who should be in the room for this" — someone who thinks it's about who gets affected, someone who thinks it's about who has to live under whatever gets decided whether they like it or not (including people who move in later), someone who thinks it's just about who's already on the town roll and that's the whole story, and maybe someone who thinks the kids who aren't born yet count. nobody should be a strawman or a villain. if one of them "wins" i want it to be because the situation cornered the others, not because they were dumb.

2. someone actually tries to run the loop as a procedure, out loud, at a kitchen table or on a whiteboard at the town hall — guess a voter list, take the vote, recompute who's affected given that result, redo the vote with the new list, and keep going. and i want you to be honest about what happens. i was imagining it cycles: start with just residents and it tips to "build," which drags in the workers and the other village, which tips it to "don't build," which makes the workers never arrive and the village only mildly affected, so they drop off the list and you're back to just residents again. if my numbers don't actually cycle, fix them so they do, and make the cycling visible on the page — i want the reader to feel it going round. please don't have it gently settle on the fair answer, that's the one thing that would ruin it.

3. the other half of it: there should be two different voter lists that are both defensible, and each one produces a majority for the outcome that then retroactively makes that list the correct one. so both sides can honestly say they won fair and square, and there's no neutral fact of the matter about who the demos was. sit in that uncomfortable spot for a while.

4. keep it grounded — a town hall, a ferry, a kitchen, a group chat. no sci-fi, no courts, no philosophers turning up. and nobody should give a speech that sounds like it came out of a textbook; the arguing should come from the fact that these people have to keep living next to each other afterwards. maybe the narrator is a kid my age whose family is split down the middle.

length-wise, maybe 1000–1500 words, whatever it needs — just don't hand me an outline or a summary of a story. and please dont end it on a neat lesson or someone going "i guess democracy is complicated lol." end it while its still a mess.
