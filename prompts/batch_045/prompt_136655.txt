I am finishing a dissertation in epistemology and applying for a one-year postdoctoral fellowship that wants a two-page (roughly 900-word) proposal for a project that moves beyond the dissertation rather than extending it. My half-formed idea, which I would like you to turn into the proposal, is that the debate over whether knowing how is a species of knowing that has been fought as a question about constitution — what a piece of knowledge-how is made of — when the more revealing question is about loss: skills decay gradually, rust, and come back with savings on relearning, whereas we are happy to treat knowing-that as something a subject either still has or has simply lost. The thought is that both camps quietly assume knowledge states are all-or-nothing and then disagree only about where to place the non-epistemic machinery; if instead we allow that a state can be retained while partly inaccessible, several standard arguments begin to look like reports on different stages of one process of degradation. The case I keep returning to is the expert who performs flawlessly in training and collapses under competitive pressure while still able to describe and correct the fault, together with its mirror image, in which being made to articulate what one is doing makes the doing worse. Please give me the question stated sharply enough to have a determinate answer; why the available positions leave it open; the method, meaning what I would actually do month by month across a year, and why an approach through degradation and access beats the obvious alternatives of more armchair analysis of skill attributions or of importing a model of motor control from cognitive science; what a successful result would look like and, equally, what would count as the project having failed; and an honest statement of scope and risk for a year in which I will run no experiments. The project is conceptual but will be hostage to some empirical claims, so say how I should handle that. I will supply the citations myself, so give me no bibliography and do not map the literature by author. Write the proposal as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
