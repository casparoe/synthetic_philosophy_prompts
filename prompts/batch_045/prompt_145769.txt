I work on a committee that writes practical guidance about whether a given system has experiences — whether there is something it is like for it, and whether it can suffer. The systems we are asked about are mostly non-human animals and patients with severe brain injuries, and now, with increasing regularity, machine systems. My own training is in cognitive neuroscience, not philosophy, and over the last two years I have watched our discussions take a shape I do not have a word for. I want to know whether the shape has a name, and more importantly how exactly to carve it.

The case that made me start keeping notes went like this.

1. A colleague proposes an account, entirely in machinery terms, of what it is for a system to be in an experiential state of type T. There is a state carrying fine-grained information about some condition of the system or its environment. That state is made available to a downstream subsystem which classifies it as being of type T. The classification is itself available to further subsystems, so that the system's later states and its behaviour depend not merely on the condition but on how the condition was classified. The system can be brought to act as if it were in a state of type T, and to produce reports to that effect. The whole arrangement is plastic: damage the classifier and the discriminations, the behaviour and the reports change together.

2. An objection: you have described every causal and functional fact, but not the thing we were asking about. There remains a question about whether the classifying subsystem itself, or the state it classifies, is the sort of thing there is something it is like to be. If it is not, you have relocated the question rather than answered it.

3. The colleague adds a further layer: a second-order subsystem that takes the first-order classification as input, is itself globally available, is itself plastic. The objection is then made again, about that layer, in the same words.

4. Two features of this iteration strike me as the real phenomenon, and I cannot describe either. First, the objection does not attenuate. In an ordinary regress the nth step is less plausible than the first, or the regress itself is the objection. Here the question asked at level four is asked with exactly the confidence of the question at level one, and by people who would not claim that the regress shows anything. Second, the proposal does not gain. Adding machinery makes the account more baroque and, if anything, lowers our confidence — which is a bad sign for a research programme, and I would like to know why this dialectic has that property.

I have since seen the same shape in three other places, and failed to see it in three more.

It reappears when the machinery on offer is completely different: a proposal framed in terms of a state's position in a similarity space rather than layered classifiers; a proposal framed in terms of a system predicting its own incoming signals; and — the case I find strangest — the proposal of a committee member who holds that nothing whatever has the disputed feature, and that our shared conviction to the contrary is itself just more machinery, a self-monitoring subsystem misrepresenting its own states as possessing a feature they lack. He gives a layered causal story about how that misrepresentation arises, and the objector asks, of the top layer of his story, whether *it* has the feature. The person denying the thing is being asked to account for the thing at every level of the account.

It reappears in weaker form in arguments about whether a symbol-manipulating system really understands anything: offer a mechanism, be asked whether the mechanism understands, offer a meta-mechanism, be asked again. Here I feel the iteration does attenuate somewhat, but I cannot say why, or why it attenuates less than it seems it should.

It does not appear at all when the disputed property is being in motion: one explains the experience of movement in a film by describing motion detectors in the visual system, and nobody goes on to ask whether the detector itself is moving. Nor with heat — a representation of heat is not hot, and the question is not even momentarily tempting. Nor with wetness — a simulation of a storm wets nothing. In these cases the further question does not merely seem silly; it fails to arise. Whatever the phenomenon is, an account of it has to explain the difference.

My own guess at the mechanism, which I do not trust, is this: the disputed notions are ones that, on the view of the person pressing the question, are supposed to be *there for the subject itself*, so that any piece of machinery you propose is automatically something the question can be asked about again, whereas motion, heat and wetness are not like that. I cannot turn this into a criterion, it says nothing about the gradient between the experience case and the understanding case, and it does not tell me whether the move is a discovery about the structure of the concept or a piece of dialectical bullying that merely looks like one.

Things I am fairly confident the phenomenon is not, and would like confirmed or denied: a regress objection; question-begging; a claim about the limits of inquiry; and the familiar complaint that a complete story about the functional facts does not entail the further facts. That last one can be made at a single level, without any iteration, which is why I think my phenomenon is distinct from it — mine is about the question re-arising about the explaining machinery, not about the explained thing failing to follow.

I am not asking for a name to drop. I want the carving: what exactly this is, what it is not, where its edges lie, and what would settle whether the move is in good standing. If there is an established concept, tell me what it covers and where my cases fall outside it; if there is not, coin a term and define it tightly enough to classify cases I have not given you.

Two constraints on the answer. First, I am not asking for a reading list, titles, authors or dates, and I would rather the answer did not rest on them: if a label is standard in some field, say which field and what the label means in your own words. Second, please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on these questions, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.

Please answer in two parts: a short prose passage — 400 words at most — doing the actual carving, then a single JSON object with exactly these fields.
