During the feedback part, someone said it was too preachy, basically propaganda rather than art. Another person, who's in a political theory class and had just read W. E. B. Du Bois, jumped in with the line people attribute to Du Bois in his October 1926 essay Criteria of Negro Art — the bit where he says that every artwork is propaganda, and inevitably has to be — and acted like that settled it: if art is always propaganda anyway, then calling this play propaganda isn't a real criticism. I got annoyed because that felt like a motte-and-bailey, but I coudln't put my finger on the exact move, and we don't have web access in the studio so I can't go check the essay or any secondary lit. I'm definately not asking for citations or a reading list; I just want the conceptual map, and I want it from you as if you can't look anything up either.

Take that one-liner — every artwork is propaganda, or art is always propagandistic and always has to be — and tell me what precise claims it could be making, from the trivial to the radical. For each reading, give me a verdict: is it trivial, is it substantive and defensible, or is it false or implausible? Add any readings I've missed.
