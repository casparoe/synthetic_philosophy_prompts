My friend Ari is deep into Kelly betting and geometric rationality, and I've been collecting his calls on hypotheticals for a while. His verdicts so far: an even-money coin flip with a tested 60% bias, repeated thousands of times, he bets 20% of his bankroll; the same thing at 80% he bets 60%; at 50/50 he bets nothing; a one-off fixed-stake gamble that multiplies your stake by 2.2 with probability 0.55 and by 0.1 otherwise he turns down flat, even though the expected value is positive and some small stake would have positive expected log. He talks about time-average growth rates and won't touch anything with positive EV but negative growth.

There's one remark of his I can't cash out. Asked once about a bet that pays off when the industry he works in does well, he said something along the lines of: if he loses that one he's probably also lost the thing that refills the bankroll, so he'd rather keep the two apart. I can read that as your labor income is part of your wealth, size bets against the whole balance sheet, or as plain don't go there, mental accounting.

New case: a repeatable even-money bet that pays if wind-power installations beat expectations this year. He works at a wind-turbine maker, about a third of his pay is bonus tied to the same installations, bankroll 50k, no other financial assets, and his model says 60% — but that's estimated from data, not known, and his past probability estimates have been off by five to ten points.

What fraction does he bet? I care more about the rules that fit his track record — plain bankroll Kelly, total-balance-sheet Kelly with capitalized future earnings, fractional Kelly for estimation risk, maybe something about drawdowns — what each predicts here (I suspect one of them says take the other side), your best guess with reasons, and the single further hypothetical that would tell the rules apart. If two of the rules collapse into one, say so.
