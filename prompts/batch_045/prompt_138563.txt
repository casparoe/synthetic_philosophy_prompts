I am research director for the Permanent Commission on Mass Deprivation, a standing tribunal that classifies historical episodes of mass starvation under exactly one of three holdings: natural disaster, political crime, or tragedy without author. Its statute directs us to analyze deprivation in terms of what households could command through exchange rather than in terms of aggregate food supply, and it treats the absence of a free press and competitive elections as an aggravating condition.

Our precedents:

1. Bengal, 1943. Aggregate supply near normal; mortality tracked a collapse in the exchange entitlements of landless rural laborers amid wartime inflation and hoarding, while the government prioritized military supply and declined to impose relief obligations on the trade. Held: political crime, aggravated.
2. Ireland, 1845–49. Genuine crop failure from blight, but grain continued to leave the country under guard and relief was conditioned on work tests and on not undercutting private traders. Held: political crime, with a split finding — the blight was the natural trigger, the mortality was political. This is our prototype for a split verdict.
3. The Great Leap Forward. Procurement keyed to inflated local production reports; stocks not released, no imports, local reports of starvation treated as disloyalty. Held: political crime, aggravated, with an explicit finding of no policy of extermination.
4. A 1970s Sahelian drought. Real production collapse in a state with almost no fiscal or transport reach; international relief arrived within two months; mortality concentrated among pastoralists. Held: natural disaster, on the reasoning that a state existing is not the same as a state having capacity.
5. The Verdan case. A government with ample stocks and written warning from its own food ministry did nothing for five months because the starving province had voted against it; it eventually relented. Held: political crime, aggravated, again with no finding that anyone wanted the deaths.

The new case, which I must draft an opinion on. In the Republic of Audra, a well-forecast drought cut the northern staple harvest by roughly 40 percent. The cabinet had reserves equal to about three months of the affected population's needs and a working railway north, but the grain had been pledged as collateral for a sovereign loan, and releasing it would have triggered default and the loss of the credit lines financing food imports; the cabinet chose the loan. Its own food ministry warned in writing in month one that absent transfers, mortality would exceed 200,000. The cabinet did not block relief and granted NGO access from month two, but a verification regime it imposed delayed deliveries by eleven weeks. The proximate price spike was driven not by cabinet order but by a parastatal marketing board buying aggressively under a statutory mandate to stabilize prices. An insurgency held one of the two rail lines and taxed convoys, and roughly half the eventual 180,000 deaths occurred in territory the government could not enter. From month four the government distributed food freely and mortality fell sharply. The press was uncensored, elections followed eight months later, and the governing party lost the north but won nationally.

Please do the following. Identify the two or three rules about what makes a famine a political crime that fit all five precedents about equally well, state each in one sentence, and say what each predicts here — including whether it would return a split verdict in the manner of the Irish case, and how it treats the deaths behind insurgent lines. Give your recommended holding with reasons, name the closest precedent and the hardest competing one, and state plainly what principle the Commission would be adopting under each of the two leading options. Finally, specify the counterfactual variant of the Audran facts that would best discriminate between those options.

Return your answer as two tables plus a short JSON object. Table 2, one row per salient fact of the Audran case, columns: fact, cuts_toward, strength_0_to_1, precedent_that_makes_it_salient.
