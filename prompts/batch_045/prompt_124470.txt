Hi — I'm on our health authority's Scarce Interventions Panel, and I need a draft decision memo I can circulate before Thursday. Sorry in advance, this one's a bit of a maze.

We can fund one of two applications this cycle, definitly not both:

- Harrow Vale persistent-pain service (rural, quite deprived catchment): ~450 patients/yr, £0.75m/yr for three years. Benefits scale roughly linearly if we part-fund it.
- Kestrel House intensive day programme for treatment-resistant depression: ~70 patients/yr, £1.3m/yr, and the provider has put in writing that anything below the full ask isn't deliverable. No partial version.

Our instrument is a "personal ladder": each patient rates 0 = worst month I've had in the past year, 100 = best month I've had in the past five years, and then rates where they'd expect to be in 12 months. Cohort averages: Harrow Vale 31 → 58 (+27). Kestrel House 18 → 74 (+56). So Harrow wins easily on totals (450 × 27 vs 70 × 56), Kestrel wins big on gain-per-person, and Kestrel looks worse off to start with.

Except our analyst says those numbers aren't on the same scale at all. He had samples from both cohorts rate five fixed vignettes describing the same objective clinical states. Two findings. First, the same objective gap between two of the vignettes spans 33 points for Harrow patients and 55 for Kestrel patients, so a Harrow point looks like it's worth about 1.7 Kestrel points — but depending on which vignettes he uses, the multiplier comes out anywhere between 1.1 and 2.4. Second, on two of the five vignettes the cohorts rank them in opposite order (Harrow patients say "constant pain but still working" is worse than "pain-free but can't work"; Kestrel patients say the reverse), so no single rescaling fits all five. He says he can give us a range for the slope but not for where either cohort sits on a common scale, so he can't tell us who is actually worse off to begin with. (He also grumbles that a scale anchored to your own worst month rewards whichever cohort has been through worse things, since they have more room to climb.)

The board is split three ways. Half say: add them up, Harrow wins on totals across the whole multiplier range, done. Tom says utility just isn't comparable between people, so any pick between the two is arbitrary, and the only fair thing is to toss a coin. I keep going back and forth — Tom seems right that we're comparing apples and oranges, but a coin flip feels like giving up, and if he's right in general we can't really do this job next cycle either. A third member says Kestrel first, because those patients are worse off and the gains per person are bigger.

What I want: a memo of roughly 700–900 words recommending one of three things — fund Harrow Vale in full, fund Kestrel House in full, or defer both to the next cycle and re-run with a common instrument. (Deferral has a real cost: the money reverts to the centre at year end, and everyone waits another nine months — please weigh that honestly rather than treating defer as the tidy option.) It should give reasons the losing side could actually reply to, and it should take Tom's coin-flip argument seriously instead of just saying the numbers settle it. Also: be straight about which claims we are and aren't entitled to make publicly, tell me what we should say to the Kestrel patients, and say what we change about the instrument before next cycle, with rough dates. No citations or reading lists — I need something I can put in front of the panel, not homework.

Whatever you recommend, end the memo with a last line that is exactly one of:

Verdict: Harrow Vale
Verdict: Kestrel House
Verdict: defer and re-run
