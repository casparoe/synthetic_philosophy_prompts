I am designing a wordless statistical atlas in the spirit of Otto Neurath's Isotype: standardized abstract pictograms, quantity shown by repeating identical same-size signs rather than by enlarging them, and the studio practice of "transforming" a source statistic into a self-explanatory chart. Neurath himself maintained that the result was not a language but a "language-like technique" — a helping language always accompanied by verbal elements — and the Vienna museum's own catch-phrase conceded the tradeoff: "To remember simplified pictures is better than to forget accurate figures."

A colleague proposes to remove the verbal props by iterating transformation: treat an already-transformed unit (say, a stack of coal pictograms standing for a quantity of energy) as a new primitive sign, and transform it again — for "per capita", for "has changed", for "not", for "if", for "probably" — so that the system becomes compositional and, in principle, unlimited.

Call P the claim that this works: iterated transforming yields a purely pictorial system with the expressive power of a one-page scientific report — negation, conditionality, uncertainty, arbitrarily many distinct claims — each uniquely decodable with no verbal text. Count P confirmed if viewers who share no language with the designer and have had no Isotype training agree at roughly 90% on claims of each of four kinds: negation, change over time, causal or conditional connection, and uncertainty; count it disconfirmed if any one of the four is systematically misread.

My credence in P is about 0.3; my colleague's is about 0.75. Give me yours as a single number, but decomposed: the probability that negation and uncertainty admit pictorial encodings that do not secretly lean on a verbal gloss; that the transformation rules are genuinely recursive and productive rather than a finite catalogue extended by hand; that compositional depth does not multiply ambiguity faster than it multiplies expressive reach; that abstract relations (rate, probability, causation) are legible cross-culturally without training; and that whatever failure occurs is practical rather than principled, i.e. P holds in principle but fails at the 90% threshold. Add any term you think I have omitted, give a number and one line of justification for each, and show how they combine. Then say which term your final number is most sensitive to, which single term most plausibly accounts for the gap between 0.3 and 0.75, and what experiment or argument would move you most.
