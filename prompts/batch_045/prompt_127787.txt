I spent a career in experimental physics, and most of my quantitative life revolved around error bars: partitioning an error budget into statistical and systematic components, quoting intervals and checking by simulation that they actually cover, calibrating a detector against known standards before trusting its numbers, and refusing to take a quoted number seriously until I had been told what ensemble or model the interval was computed under. I have lately been reading about the Principal Principle — the requirement that a rational agent's credence in an event, conditional on the information that the event's objective chance is x, should simply be x — and about the disagreements over what such chances are supposed to be.

I would like you to restate that principle, and the dispute surrounding it, as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use: unknown parameters and estimators, sampling distributions and coverage, calibration, within-model versus across-model uncertainty, and the difference between a quantity defined by a limiting frequency over repetitions and a quantity that only makes sense counterfactually. Please treat me as fluent in that vocabulary and as a beginner in philosophy, and do not give me references or a reading list.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the principle translate cleanly — is deference to chance just consistency of an estimator, or is the chance playing the role of a calibrated instrument whose reading you take at face value, and can my vocabulary tell those apart? Which parts translate only with distortion — for instance, when the chance attaches to a single unrepeatable event, such as the chance that the matter density of the universe exceeds the critical value, where there is no ensemble to average over even in principle? And what cannot be said at all in my terms: the fact that the principle is usually hedged so that only certain further evidence may be added to the conditioning, and the awkward case where an agent's own evidence seems to bear on what the chances themselves are, rather than merely on what happened. For each piece of residue, tell me whether it indicts my vocabulary or suggests that the original claim is thinner than it looks — in particular whether "objective chance" is doing more work than "whatever it is that a rational agent defers to in this way."
