My grandmother is being retired — sorry, is retiring, at 78, and hating the word — after 47 years running the hill-sheep breeding programme at the same station, picking every single mating herself, on paper records and what she calls looking at them. She never learned what a SNP is and says she's too old to start. I'm giving the toast at her dinner on Saturday and I promissed her two things: no gift or instinct talk (her view is that calling it a gift is just a way of never asking her anything), and no announcing to the room that science has now explained what she was actually doing.

The room is the problem. A third are family and neighbours. A third are animal-genomics people who have spent fifteen years being politely baffled by her and who did once take her records, build genomic predictions from them, and get predictions that work fine on average but still can't reproduce her cull decisions out in the tails. And the rest are a startup crowd who are taking over the programme with a selection pipeline that just won its first independent validation — trained, obviously, on four decades of her decisions. My sister will be there too; she does philosophy of biology and will visibly wince if I say Grandma was doing what natural selection does, which I gather is wrong in some specific way to do with goals versus selection, and she also says there's a creationist-adjacent reading of her career I shouldn't hand anyone on a plate.

So: give me a five-minute toast where Darwin's strange inversion — comprehension demoted from cause to effect, competence first and understanding maybe never — is the actual spine of why her career was remarkable, not decoration. It has to hold two things at once: that something real is being lost, and that the thing replacing her is competent-without-comprehending in the second, Turing-ish sense, and is quite literally made out of her. No consoling the family with a pretend continuity, no sneering at the people building the pipeline. She also distinguishes knowing why from knowing how and gets annoyed when people collapse them, if that's useful. And no use of the word legacy, which she's banned.
