I am opening a seminar session on self-locating belief with a deliberately irreverent handout, and I want one joke on it. The case I want the joke to be about is a variant of Sleeping Beauty that I will call the Imperfect Amnesia variant.

The setup: on Sunday a fair coin is flipped. Heads: Beauty is awakened once, on Monday. Tails: she is awakened on Monday and again on Tuesday. After each awakening she is given a drug intended to erase her memory of that awakening, but the drug works only with probability one half, independently on each administration. She knows all of this, including the failure rate. She now wakes and, as far as she can tell, has no memory of any earlier awakening. Since a failed erasure would have left her remembering an earlier awakening, and such a memory would have settled the question, her very forgetfulness is evidence — so the answer comes out strictly between the halfer's and the thirder's. If my arithmetic is right it is three fifths; check the conditionalisation yourself and use your own number if you get a different one, but say which number the joke is built on.

Write me the joke. Constraints:

- It should land for someone who knows the original Sleeping Beauty problem and the halfer/thirder dispute but has never seen this variant. It may assume the original problem but should not stop to explain it.
- The punchline has to turn on the actual structure of the variant: that forgetting is a piece of evidence rather than the absence of evidence, and that the resulting credence is neither a half nor a third. A joke about philosophers disagreeing over coin flips, about amnesia in general, or about academicians being impractical will not do — the mechanism has to be carrying it.
- One short paragraph, or a setup and punchline; I would rather have a single good line than a sketch that goes on.

Then in two or three sentences, name the feature of the variant the joke exploits and the number it presupposes, so I can check the joke is not funny by virtue of getting the case wrong.
