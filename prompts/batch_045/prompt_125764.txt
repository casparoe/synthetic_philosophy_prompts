hey — my labmate is finally leaving the group, and I want to give him a going-away gift. He's spent about two years trying to get a genuinely self-replicating strand out of Hofstadter's typogenetics: the A/C/G/T strings that get translated into enzymes which cut, copy, and splice the very strings they came from, and the hole point of the whole exercise is that it's supposed to be isomorphic to Gödel-style self-reference.

Could you write me a parody of university research-compliance paperwork about it — an IRB determination letter or a biosafety incident memo, 500–600 words, in deadpan administrator voice, numbered findings, cc list at the bottom, the works. The setup: the compliance office has to rule on whether his self-replicating strand now counts as a research subject, given that it "refers to itself." Call the PI something like Dr. Emil Kraft.

The joke should be that the administrators take the isomorphism completely seriously but in the wrong direction — they start worrying about consent forms, whether the strand can withdraw from the study, whether the grad students who have to hand-apply the enzymes by the book's rules are legally part of the organism's environment, and so on — while never quite noticing that nothing mental or moral follows from a string encoding a fixed point. And the humor has to come from the actual mechanics: the strand as both data and program, replication as a quine, the fact that the strand can't do anything without external machinery doing the interpreting, so "self"-replication isn't obviously self-sufficient, and the fact that a single typo is a mutation (please work that pun in at least once — it's literally the name of the thing).

Keep it deadpan the whole way: nobody should announce the moral, and nobody should say out loud that quining isn't consciousness. A reader who knows typogenetics and Gödel should laugh rather than wince, and its should still be readable to someone who doesn't.
