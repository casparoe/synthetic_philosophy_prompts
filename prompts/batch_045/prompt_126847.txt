I'm writing the conceptual framing section of a thesis chapter on information-to-work conversion, and I keep finding that the literature on Maxwell's demon is full of accounts that each show one demon failing rather than a specification of what a successful account would have to be. So before I adopt any of them, I want the spec.

The purpose I need it for fixes what counts as adequate: I need an account good enough to underwrite quantitative claims about real feedback-controlled devices — finite, quantum, continuously monitored, run a finite number of times — not merely one that re-secures the second law for an idealized cycle in the thermodynamic limit. Please write the desiderata that any account of why demons fail must meet to serve that purpose, with the case or consideration behind each requirement.

Among the cases I want the requirements to be anchored to, in addition to the plain one-molecule Szilard engine:

- the inherited-correlation engine: no measurement step at all, because the correlation between device and system is already there in the preparation; there is no act of observation whose cost can be charged,
- the noisy monitor: the demon measures but its record is degraded before it acts, so whatever the bookkeeping is, it has to be graded in the quality of the correlation rather than all-or-nothing,
- the endless-tape demon: a reversible automaton with unbounded memory that never erases, run for an arbitrarily long but finite time, together with the variant in which the memory is finite but so large that erasure is deferred beyond any run I care about,
- the thermal demon: the demon's memory registers are themselves in equilibrium with the same bath as the working substance, so its records start out random,
- the single-shot run: one molecule, one cycle, where I need a bound that holds except with some small probability rather than on average over infinitely many repetitions,
- the quantum monitor: continuous weak measurement of an observable that doesn't commute with what the demon later acts on, and systems whose relevant states are not perfectly distinguishable.

Beyond coverage, I want the requirements to say what the account must *respect or keep apart*: the cost of acquiring information as against the cost of erasing it as against the cost of the memory merely existing; logical as against thermodynamic irreversibility; information as a property of the demon's records as against a relation between demon and system; the claim that no demon achieves net work extraction as against the claim that an information-theoretic bookkeeping identity explains why. I also want two of the requirements to be, respectively, non-circularity — the account may not help itself to the second law, or to ergodicity, or to whatever equilibration it is supposed to protect, and it must say whether the erasure bound enters as a premise or as something derived, and what independent support that premise has if it is one — and strength: an account that only reproduces the inequality, without giving the maximum work per bit available to a demon with a given error rate and a given finite memory, is too weak for my purpose.

Format please: a numbered list of requirements, each with its motivating case in a sentence or two; then a short table marking which are non-negotiable for my purpose and which are preferences of particular camps; then the part I most want — which subsets of the requirements you think cannot be jointly satisfied, with the argument, and which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidate accounts. My own guess is that there is a genuine tension between non-circularity and coverage of the endless-tape case, but argue me out of it if that's wrong. A very brief scorecard of the main families — the erasure-based exorcism, the no-go or circularity critique, the mutual-information form of the second law under feedback, the trajectory or fluctuation-relation treatment — against the non-negotiables only is welcome, but keep it short; the criteria are the deliverable.

One epistemic request: separate what is settled among people who actually work on this from what is contested, and don't present a contested claim as the consensus. In particular, I want to know which of the desiderata are ones a defender of each family would reject as a demand of the opposing camp rather than a requirement, where you take the second-law bookkeeping identities themselves to be settled but the question of what *explains* the demon's failure to be open, and where you are extrapolating beyond what you can support. Assume you have no access to papers or the web; work from the physics, and flag the places where your confidence is lower than your wording suggests.
