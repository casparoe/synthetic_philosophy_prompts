I work in distributional and risk-sensitive reinforcement learning, and I'd like you to do a translation exercise for me. The vocabulary I actually think in: return distributions, distributional Bellman operators, risk functionals, distortion risk measures (CVaR, quantile-based and other spectral weightings), coherent versus non-coherent risk measures, aleatoric versus epistemic uncertainty, offline policy evaluation and distribution shift, importance weighting, and calibration of a learned reward model. I have never taken a decision theory course; what I know about expected utility theory I picked up secondhand from papers on risk-sensitive RL, so please don't assume I know the standard dialectic.

I want the classic Allais problem and the independence axiom of expected utility theory restated in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can manage. Please handle both patterns rather than only the famous one: the common-consequence pattern (the Allais pairs themselves, where a large shared chance of a fixed payoff is mixed into both options) and the common-ratio pattern. And I want independence restated as a constraint on my machinery rather than as a constraint on preferences. My guess is that it comes out roughly as a linearity-in-mixtures condition on the risk functional — the functional commutes with mixing, so the value of a mixture is the mixture of the values — and I'd like you to confirm or correct that, and then say what more the axiom is asserting beyond it, if anything.

Then I want the residue report, which is the part I care about:

1. Which features of the common-consequence and common-ratio phenomena translate cleanly into statements about distortion risk measures or quantile-based objectives, and which translate only after something has been smuggled in that my framework doesn't supply — for instance, whatever it is that makes a degenerate distribution (a payoff you get for sure) behave specially in a way that a distribution putting probability 0.99 on that same payoff does not.

2. What has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Two candidates I'd like you to rule on: the distinction between a normative and a descriptive reading of the axiom, i.e. whether it is a requirement of rationality or an empirical hypothesis about how people choose; and the family of money-pump and dynamic-inconsistency arguments, since in my world an agent that isn't optimizing a mean or an exponentially discounted mean is simply optimizing something else, and I'm not sure my vocabulary has anything corresponding to exploitable as distinct from suboptimal under whatever metric I happened to pick.

3. Whether my framework's habit of choosing a risk functional directly, instead of constraining behavior across choices, loses something the axiom was doing — or whether the axiom was only ever a roundabout way of pinning down a functional, so that nothing is lost in going direct.

There is one further thing I'd like you to test rather than take on faith. I've been playing with a reading of the certainty effect on which it isn't a risk attitude at all. On this reading, subjects discount stated probabilities because they don't fully trust the experimenter's randomizer, so the sure option is the only one whose payoff doesn't depend on a device they can't verify; the phenomenon would then be a case of model uncertainty about the reward channel — closer to distribution shift, or to ambiguity about the sampling process, than to risk sensitivity. Call it the randomizer-distrust reading. Is it a genuine rival explanation, or does it collapse into something my vocabulary already has a name for, such as ambiguity aversion, evaluation under epistemic uncertainty, or an off-policy correction? And if it is a genuine rival, does that change any of your answers above?

Please write in American English, and please keep the translation honest: where a piece of the puzzle only fits after you've quietly added an assumption my vocabulary doesn't carry, mark it as distortion rather than counting it as translated. And for whatever doesn't survive, tell me whether that's a gap in my framework or a sign that the original debate is doing less work than it's usually made to look like it's doing.
