Please explain a conceptual contrast between the two main superrationality experiments. In the Platonia dilemma, twenty thinkers may each claim a large indivisible prize that is awarded only if exactly one of them claims it; the superrational strategy is to claim it with probability equal to the reciprocal of the number of players. In the original Luring Lottery, by contrast, a large fixed prize is divided equally among all entrants, so entering always yields a positive but continuously diminishing share. Now consider a hybrid rule: the prize is divided among entrants as in the Luring Lottery, but a sole entrant, if there is one, receives a multiplier β on the whole prize, interpolating between the two cases as β grows. Explain how and why the optimal superrational entry probability changes as the payout rule moves from continuous division toward all-or-nothing, and why the pure Luring Lottery leaves the optimal probability comparatively underdetermined—nearly flat over a wide range—while the Platonia-style case pins it down sharply. What does this show about what renormalization is supposed to accomplish?
