After a long argument with a friend about whether a conscious experience can be revised after the fact, I came away with firm verdicts on individual cases and no idea what generalization they support. I'll use the usual labels without endorsing anyone's version of them: on an Orwellian revision the subject really did undergo the earlier experience and something later alters the record — memory, report, judgment — so that it now seems otherwise; on a Stalinesque one the earlier experience never had the content in question, because the later input settles what was experienced, and there is nothing to erase.

My verdicts. First, color phi: a red spot on the left, a green one on the right a tenth of a second later, and the subject sees a single spot sliding across and turning green partway along. I do not think there was ever a moment at which the subject consciously underwent a static red flash followed by a static green one. That one is Stalinesque. Second, take two stipulations — I don't care whether the experiments have been run. Suppose a tone 100 ms after a brief flash makes people place the flash at the tone's time; and suppose that a tone 200 ms after the second spot of a phi display flips whether people say motion or two separate flashes. In both I want the opposite verdict: the flash was experienced when it occurred, the tone only moves the report or the judgment, it does not reach back and change what was seen. Those are Orwellian. Third, that I am now in pain, in the left knee, is not open to revision by anything later, so revision stories had better be confined to a restricted class of features and not apply to experience wholesale.

The first verdict and the two stipulated ones are the pair I cannot reconcile: in one a later stimulus settles what was experienced, in the others it settles only what is said about what was experienced, and the difference does not look like one of principle.

My own attempt: later input can revise an earlier experience's content but never its time, since an experience occurs when it occurs and no later event can make it have occurred at another time. Please test that against the three cases and repair it. I suspect it mishandles color phi, where what shifts is precisely a timing — the dot is experienced as changing color midway, at a time when nothing changed color — and that it overgenerates: a flash and a beep 80 ms apart fall inside any integration window, and I don't want to say the flash's color was revised by the beep. So it looks as though it needs a window clause and a same-object or same-modality clause, and I want to know whether those clauses are earned or ad hoc.

Give me the principle stated precisely, with the case that motivates each clause; tell me what it predicts about the noise in the street that I am sure, on waking, had been part of my dream for some time; and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it cannot keep and which you would overturn. There is also a case I go back and forth on: reading a long sentence, the early words seem, once I reach the end, to have meant all along what they turned out to mean, and I can't tell whether that seeming was there as I read them or was added when the sentence resolved.
