Me and two friends got into a massive argument last night that ran way past when any of us should have gone home, and I want someone to referee it because I honestly can't tell who won.

Background: our friend Sophie is pregnant. She and her partner just got screening results saying the kid will very likely be born with a condition that means recurring chronic pain for the rest of his life, but a normal lifespan and, as far as anyone can tell, a life that's still clearly worth living and probably a happy one. She's decided to continue. Someone raised a glass, Jonah said he wasn't sure congratulations was quite the right thing to say, Ren jumped in on the other side, and it went theoretical immediately. I've tried to reconstruct thier moves as fairly as I can.

Ren 1: There's a basic asymmetry in how goods and bads work. Pain is bad, and not being in pain is good even when there's nobody around to enjoy not being in pain. Pleasure is good, but missing out on pleasure is only bad for someone if there is someone who's missing out. So nobody is harmed by never being created, and anybody who is created is harmed by it, because every real life contains some pain and none of the pleasure in it can be credited back to a person who otherwise wouldn't have been there at all. On Sophie's case: she'd be knowingly adding a life with real pain in it, and the good decades can't compensate, since compensation has to land on somebody. (I have a feeling this is associated with some philosopher named Benatar, but I'm going off Ren's version.)

Jonah 1: Two problems. One, that doesn't get you the conclusion about Sophie, because on your own account the harm scales with how much pain there is, so it can't treat a mostly happy life with chronic pain the same as a life of misery. Two, the whole thing smells like a restatement rather than an argument. We already accept that nobody owes it to anybody to have children, and that you owe actual children not to wreck their lives. Calling that an asymmetry doesn't explain it or extend it, and it gives you no new reason to think creating Sophie's kid counts as harming him.

Ren 2: On the first, fine, I'll say it plainly: a life with a lot of pleasure and some pain is less bad to create than a life of agony, but less bad is still bad, and no quantity of pleasure flips it to good, because a benefit needs a beneficiary. On the second: those two ordinary judgments are not free-floating, so try explaining them some other way. If bringing a happy person into existence is genuinely good, then it's something there's reason to do, and more of it is better, and you're stuck saying couples who don't reproduce are neglecting a duty. Everyone thinks that's nuts. The asymmetry is the only thing on offer that makes adding happy people optional while making miserable people forbidden.

Jonah 2: False choice. You can perfectly well hold that producing some good is optional without holding that it's harmful. I can decide to commission a mural in a courtyard that's getting demolished in five years: skipping it harms nobody, doing it isn't required of me, and if I do it nobody is harmed and the world is a bit better. Optional and harmless at the same time. Also, and this is the bit I kept coming back to, your view seems to run on a technicality about when the subject shows up. If Sophie's kid already existed and she could add ten good years to his life, those years would obviously be a benefit to him. The only reason they can't count now is that at the moment she's deciding, there's nobody there for them to be good for. That's a fact about timing, and timing shouldn't be doing that much moral work.

Ren 3: The mural isn't a counterexample, it's a category error: a mural has no interests, so harm doesn't apply to it either way, and my claim was always about what we owe persons. And on timing, every view about future people has to say where the subject comes from. Yours has to claim Sophie can do whatever she likes right up until some line and then suddenly owes the kid things; I just put the line earlier. The difference between us isn't that I'm relying on a technicality, it's that you haven't said what makes the difference.

Jonah 3: Then take it to the end, because here's where I think it breaks. If nobody is deprived by not existing, your view can't count the end of the species as any kind of loss, and not just the species: no future music, no future love, no future discovery, none of it is a loss, because there's nobody who misses out. It's not that the conclusion is grim, I don't care about that, it's that the view has no vocabulary for the exact kind of loss that you, in the rest of your life, treat as the most serious kind there is.

Ren 4: Right, no loss, in that sense, and I'll own it. But the asymmetry is a claim about what there's reason to do, not about what I'm allowed to care about or grieve over. And notice you've stopped talking about Sophie. Her question isn't about the species, it's about what she owes this one kid.
