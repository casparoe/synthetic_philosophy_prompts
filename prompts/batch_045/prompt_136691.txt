I'm working on a piece about how the effective altruism / rationalist crowd uses giant-number arguments, and I keep running into the torture-vs-dust-specks one: would you rather one person be tortured for decades, or 3^^^3 people each get a speck of dust in their eye? Everyone I've interviewed so far says the "correct" answer is the specks, on the grounds that it's just expected-utility maximisation — a tiny harm multiplied by an astronomically large number.

To me that looks like the same bug as Pascal's mugging: take a negligible quantity, multiply it by an absurdly huge number, and your decision procedure spits out something crazy. And the fix seems obvious — cap the utility function, go bounded or logarithmic, so that past a certain point more people just stop mattering. My editor says the two cases are different animals and that capping doesn't even touch the specks case, but she couldn't explain why. Is she right, or is that just discomfort talking?
