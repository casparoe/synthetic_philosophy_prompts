hey, so I could use some help sorting this out — it's gotten weirdly consuming since we moved out to an old farmhouse with a couple of acres last spring. Two hives of honeybees, a veg patch, and in the last eight months I've delt with: a yellowjacket nest tucked into the shed eaves, ants in the kitchen, head lice brought home from my daughter's school, a bedbug scare from a secondhand armchair, mosquitoes every single evening, and now a guy tells me the barn beams are riddled with wood-boring beetles and the only real fix is to tent and fumigate the whole barn.

I'm not a vegetarian and I'm not one of those people counting insect deaths in their oat milk. But I've been reading a bit about insect nociception and now I can't stop having opinions about individual cases, and the opinions don't line up with each other at all. Here's my gut on them:

- Aphids on the kale, spray with soapy water: completely fine, no pang whatsoever.
- The yellowjacket nest, right by the shed door, they haven't stung anybody: fine to knock it down, but I felt a real pang — way more than about the aphids.
- Kitchen ants, followed the trail, boiling water down the crack: fine, and somehow *less* of a pang than the wasps, which seems backwards given there were hundreds of them.
- Head lice: fine, zero hesitation, I'd do it gladly.
- Bedbugs: fine, heat-treat the whole house, whatever it costs. Though: we got them from an armchair I dragged home myself, so in a sense I carried them in.
- Mosquitoes in the bedroom at night: fine, and I don't feel bad about it, I feel *good*.
- My own bees: I take most of the honey and give them sugar syrup for winter. I don't think this is fine and I don't think it's not fine — I just don't feel clean about it. Same when I pinch an old queen and requeen.
- A beer trap in early spring, to catch queen wasps before they've even built a nest: this one I feel is *worse* than knocking down an established nest in July, and I can't explain it. Killing one queen in April prevents a whole colony's worth of deaths later. It should be the *better* one.
- Ant bait, the little gel stations where the ant carries poison home, dies over a day or two and takes the colony: I feel worse about this than about squashing an ant instantly, even though instant is supposed to be the humane option and this kills far more.
- My neighbour has a company fog his entire yard every month for mosquitoes; it takes out the moths and lacewings and everything else. That strikes me as clearly not fine, in a way that me swatting a mosquito in my bedroom obviously isn't — and I don't think the difference is just numbers.
- The sterile-male thing, where they irradiate males so the population just fails to reproduce: this feels different from killing in a way that isn't merely "less bad". I feel fine about it, which is strange if what bothers me is a population being wiped out.
- Tent and fumigate the barn: genuinely haven't decided. Everything inside dies, including stuff that was doing nothing to me.

The thing I keep circling back to is sort of a principle, but I can't make it work. Roughly: what bothers me isn't the death and isn't really the numbers, it's something about solicitation — whether I've drawn the insects into some kind of relation with me first. Attracted them, fed them, made them dependent, profited off their labour — and *then* killed them anyway. The beer trap lures them. The bees I've literally been farming. The kitchen ants I let have crumbs for a week before I lost patience. The bedbugs I hauled in myself. Whereas the mosquitoes and the lice and the beetles in the beams just turned up, and I'm defending something.

But it doesn't explain the April queen wasp case at all (nothing solicited, the nest doesn't even exist yet), it doesn't explain why the yard fogging feels so much worse than swatting (nobody solicited the moths either — unless my neighbour counts as soliciting them just by living there, and that feels like a cheat), and it gives the wrong answer on the barn beetles, since the barn is arguably what's attracting them.

So: is there a principle here — or a small set of candidates — that gets most of these? I want something precise enough that I can actually run new cases through it, with the case that motivates each clause. Tell me what it says about cases I haven't listed, especially: ordering ladybugs or predatory mites by mail and releasing them to eat the pests (that's hiring a killer, which feels like its own thing), spraying the orchard with something that'll also knock down my bees for a week, and the barn. And be honest about which of my verdicts have to go — I'd definately rather drop two of them and end up with something I can use than have a baroque machine that saves all thirteen.
