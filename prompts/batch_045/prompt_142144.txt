Next month I'm giving a 20-minute talk in the animal welfare science session of an aquaculture conference — the room is mostly fish biologists and farm vets who want operational welfare indicators they can audit, plus maybe three comparative psychologists who know Dennett's four grades of minds (Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian) and think of it as a nice teaching device but not a research tool. I have about ten minutes of Q&A and four slides of data on reversal learning and detour tasks.

My thesis: the four grades are a ladder of behavioural control, not a ladder of welfare, and the step that actually matters for welfare is one Dennett doesn't draw. Call a creature an offline Popperian if its internal selector can pre-screen candidate actions that recombine elements it was never reinforced for, decoupled from what its senses are telling it right now — that's my proposed sub-grade inside the Popperian one, and I argue it's the minimal capacity for an outcome to matter to the animal before it arrives, which is where a whole family of farm welfare problems (anticipatory frustration, barren-tank apathy, chronic stress from not being able to complete a prepared action sequence) comes from. Corollary for this room: stop ranking species by grade, and stop using grade membership as a sentience gate.
