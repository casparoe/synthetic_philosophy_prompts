I'm a science-and-ideas journalist working on a feature about who checks the checkers once the checkers are language models, and before I start interviewing people I want the spec, not the sales pitch. So: write me the desiderata that any scheme for scalable oversight — getting a weaker judge to reliably supervise a stronger model — has to meet to count as adequate. Each requirement needs the concrete case behind it (the situation that shows why you'd want it), and each needs to be marked either non-negotiable or as a preference of one particular camp.

Let me fix the purpose so adequacy isn't up for grabs: I want to know what would let a regulator actually lean on such a scheme when certifying high-stakes model output — clinical guidance, an engineering safety case, a financial disclosure — in a domain where no available human expert can check the whole thing end to end. Concretely: the judge is a smart generalist rather than a specialist, has maybe 40 minutes per item, works in what I'll call a sealed room (no re-running the model, no outside tools, no phoning someone who knows more), and the object being judged is a long chain of reasoning whose conclusion may be true but deeply counterintuitive, or false but beautifully presented.

I have a few hobby-horses of my own that I'd rather you rule on than just adopt: that the scheme shouldn't get to assume the two sides in a dispute differ in skill or in honesty; that it shouldn't get to assume truth is more persuasive than a well-told falsehood under time pressure; that a verdict has to survive being handed to a second judge with different priors; and that it can't simply push the problem up a level to another overseer who also needs overseeing. Tell me honestly which of those are genuine desiderata and which are just my tastes dressed up as requirements — I've read enough of this stuff to know its easy to do that.

Then the part I most want: which pair of desiderata do you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, and which single requirement does the most work in knocking out candidate schemes? After that, a very short scorecard — a paragraph total — for debate, recursive or iterated amplification-style schemes, and market- or prediction-market-flavored oversight. Keep the weight on the criteria rather than the scoring, and keep the two seperate. Plain prose, American English, under 900 words if you can.
