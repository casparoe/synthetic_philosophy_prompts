hey — I want an FAQ page, roughly 600 words, for a startup that sells a small physical randomization gadget to absent-minded drivers. Think Kickstarter-page-meets-support-center copy: numbered Q&As, friendly, deadpan, 8–10 of them, no visible punchlines and no winking at the reader.

Setup, in case you havent come across this one: a guy is driving home along a highway with two exits that look exactly alike. Taking the first one drops him in a terrible neighbourhood (payoff 0). Taking the second one gets him home (4). Missing both leaves him at a motel at the end of the highway (1). He knows the layout perfectly, but at any given exit he can't tell whether it's the first or the second, and he can't remember whether he's already passed one. The usual thing people say about this case is that the plan he'd choose before starting the engine is not the plan he wants to be following once he's actually sitting at a exit. There's also a usual reply to that — something like: follow the plan your later self will be tempted to abandon, and don't re-decide. I don't care which of those the company's copy quietly commits to, but it should commit to one and stay consistent about it for the whole page.

The joke material I want mined rather than invented:

- he's supposed to randomize, but he can't remember whether he already did, so the gadget has to do something about that, and about what happens if he flips twice
- his guess about which exit he's at depends on what he intends to do there — so the gadget should have features that work on his own credence about where he is
- being given information, or being made to remember, can leave him worse off than never knowing
- one mode where the gadget tells him the outcome of the flip and one where it keeps it from him
- invent at least two extra product features, tiers, warranty clauses or fine-print items that push those points somewhere slightly absurd — a subscription level, a passenger-sync feature, whatever — and include one question from a furious customer who followed the instructions exactly and ended up at the motel, with the company's deadpan answer

If the FAQ ever names a probability or an expected payoff, it should name the right one (naming none at all is fine). Nobody should ever explain the paradox out loud. Someone who actually works on this stuff should laugh rather than wince.
