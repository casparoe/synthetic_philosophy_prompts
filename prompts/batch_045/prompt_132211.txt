I'm home for the summer in the small town where I grew up, and the town council — my mom is on it, which is why I keep getting dragged into this — has been fighting for months about a granite drinking fountain in the square. It was put up in 1903 by a local mill owner named Josiah Crane, who also paid for the public library and the first high school. He also turned out to be, we all found out a couple of years ago when a grad student wrote a local-history piece about it, one of the most active campaigners in the county for compulsory sterilization, and he wrote and paid to hand out a stack of pamphlets saying that people he called unfit should be stopped from having children. Nothing else in town is named after him, just this fountain. Last week the council voted 4–3 to keep it but bolt on a bronze plaque saying roughly that it was erected by Josiah Crane, mill owner and benefactor, who also campaigned to have people sterilized against their will, and that this hurt families in the county. My mom voted yes and says taking it down would be erasing history, and that the plaque turns the fountain into a lesson instead of a tribute. My sister says the opposite: once it's just an object with a caption on it, nobody is honoring anybody, so keeping it costs nothing. My uncle has a third idea, which is that the council should just not decide — the basin is cracked and the repair quote is about $19,000, so they quietly skip the repair, let the weather finish the job over the next ten years, and put a bench or a veterans' marker there when it falls apart; he says that isn't the same as tearing it down, because nobody is doing anything, they're only letting something happen. And one more wrinkle: the original deed of gift apparently says the fountain was given on the condition that it carry Crane's name for ever, and a great-grandson has written to the local paper saying the family would want the money back if the name ever comes off.

So: is the plaque actually a coherent middle position, or is it fudging something? Is my uncle's let-it-crumble plan genuinely different from removing the thing, given that the council is choosing not to spend the money? And does that condition in the deed obligate anyone alive now, or is my mom just wrong that there's a promise being broken at all? My gut says all three of the options on the table are each dodging some question nobody wants to say out loud, but I can't get any further than that.
