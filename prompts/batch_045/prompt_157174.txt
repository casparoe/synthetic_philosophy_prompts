Write a satirical landing page of roughly 700 words for a fictional startup — call it something like the Moral Trade Exchange — a two-sided marketplace that matches counterparties whose values diverge, so that each can buy cheaply the actions the other values highly and sell dearly the actions it barely cares about. Both sides should believe they got the better of the deal, and by their own lights both should be right.

The humor has to come from getting the mechanics of moral trade right, so that someone who has thought about the idea recognizes the target and judges the satire fair rather than merely hostile. Please work in: (i) the baseline problem — a listing counts as a trade rather than a threat only if the seller credibly would have done the bad thing anyway, plus whatever certification mechanism the platform invents for that; (ii) deontological side-constraints as non-fungible, inalienable holdings that the platform declines to list; (iii) the verification problem for actions no counterparty can observe, and the escrow or oracle mechanism invented to solve it; (iv) value drift — your future self may not want the trade you made — together with a service that re-trades on that future self's behalf and thereby binds it; (v) acausal matching, i.e. counterparties you will never meet or causally affect, priced by simulating what they would have agreed to; (vi) an awkward premium audit tier that scores a matched pair from some third, overarching evaluative standpoint and keeps reporting the trade as a net loss even though each side gains by its own lights; (vii) at least one testimonial capturing the complaint that a trader now acts for the wrong kind of reason. Without ever naming it outright, let a reader feel the "this is just the market for indulgences" worry.

Use the register of genuine startup copy — hero heading, how-it-works, pricing, FAQ, testimonials — and stay deadpan; the narrator must never concede that any of this is absurd. Begin with the piece itself, with no preface or commentary. After it, add a note of 100–150 words on where the piece takes liberties: which feature you exaggerated beyond what a defender of moral trade would grant, and where the joke depends on a contested reading of the idea.
