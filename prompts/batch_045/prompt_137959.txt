Diplomatic apologies for historical wrongs have a stable shape. The apologizing state reaches for the least costly instrument it thinks will do: a written expression of regret or sorrow, drafted by lawyers, usually with a clause disclaiming any admission of legal liability. The wronged party typically rejects this and demands something costlier and more specific: a head of government traveling to the site, a public gesture of abasement, the wrong and the victims named out loud, reparations as well but never instead. And the apology is often for acts of a predecessor regime that the apologizing state partly defines itself against having overthrown. What does this pattern presuppose?

My first thought was costly signaling. Remorse is cheap to assert, so a claim of remorse is credible only if the apologizer pays a price an unrepentant state would not pay. But I cannot make the handicap principle do the work, for two reasons.

First, specificity. It is not enough to incur some large cost; the cost has to be incurred in a way tied to the content of the wrong, in the right place, naming the right people, in the right words. Costly-signaling theory as I understand it cares only about the magnitude and differential incidence of the cost and is indifferent to the vehicle. So the practice seems to be demanding something the theory cannot explain — call it the indexicality requirement. What commitment does it add? That an apology is a speech act with conditions of fittingness or felicity, and not merely a reliable signal of a hidden inner state?

Second, currency. Money is costly, and states do pay it, yet both sides treat reparations as insufficient on their own, and the apologizer fights hardest over the parts that cost nothing — the admission, the gesture. Does the practice presuppose that what is really transferred is standing, face, or status, and therefore non-fungible? And is the disclaimed-liability clause consistent with that reading, or does it give the game away and show the practice to be pure ritual?

Last: is the signal even about present remorse? The alternative is that the cost functions as a bond, a commitment device that buys credible future non-repetition rather than expressing a current attitude, which would fit the forward-looking uses these apologies are put to.

Tell me which of these commitments the practice strictly requires, which it merely fits well with, and where it is philosophically underdetermined. Then tell me what would have to change in the practice if the signaling picture were simply false — if there were in principle no reliable way to distinguish a repentant state from an unrepentant one. Please answer in American English.
