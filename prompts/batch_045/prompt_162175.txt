My dissertation compares how liberal and non-liberal regimes handle the visibility of citizens to the state, and I keep hitting a puzzle about what each regime takes a person to be.

Three practices, described plainly. German and EU data protection law treat the mere collection and storage of information about someone as an interference needing justification, insist that data gathered for one purpose not be reused for another (purpose limitation), and grant erasure and fresh-start rights. US law leaves collection largely unregulated and regulates use instead, on the view that information you have voluntarily handed to a bank, a phone company, or a website is information you have no protected interest in at all. China's social credit apparatus aggregates conduct across unrelated domains — paying debts, obeying court judgments, minor infractions — into a single standing of trustworthiness, and then sanctions through goods deliberately detached from the original domain, such as transport tickets or school placement.

I want the implicit theory reconstructed, not the policy debate. Concretely: does purpose limitation strictly presuppose that conduct and character are domain-relative, so that reliability in one sphere is no evidence about any other, or does it merely fit that view and also fall out of a distrust of official discretion, of a public/private distinction, or of administrative tractability? Does the unified-profile design strictly presuppose a global trait of trustworthiness, given how much of the situationist critique of global character traits it seems to flout? And what view of identity over time do erasure and fresh-start rights require — must the present self be thought partly unbound by a permanent record of the past self, or can a purely consequentialist account of profiling's chilling effects generate them?

I have also been toying with a different test for what counts as protected, and I would like to know what it presupposes: not whether you disclosed a datum to anyone, but whether you could have declined to generate it without exiting ordinary life. Under that test, what becomes of each of the three practices, and what would each regime have to give up?
