In my senior-year Contemporary Issues seminar we debated whether the world is becoming more or less violent. One student closed the discussion with a line I also keep seeing online: "We live in the most peaceful era in human history" — violence is at an all-time low, and we believe otherwise only because every incident now gets filmed and shared. I raised two worries and was waved off. First, "low" could mean few victims in absolute terms, a small share of the population, or a small chance for any given person, and those can come apart. Second, the denominator could be people alive today or person-years lived; since there are far more of us and we live much longer, a falling annual rate might still mean more violence encountered over a single lifetime. I am not convinced the line settled anything, but I cannot say precisely why.

Please set out, as a list, the distinct precise claims that sentence could be making: what counts as violence (only deaths, or also injury, threats, domestic abuse, state violence?), which population, which time span, absolute versus per capita, and rate per year versus risk accumulated across a whole life. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false; say which of them would actually support using the line to end our debate; and say whether my two objections tell against some readings and not others. Please answer in American English.
