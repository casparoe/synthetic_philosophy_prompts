I am writing a short paper on Hofstadter's careenium: a billiard-table-like box in which vast numbers of tiny magnetic balls (symms) careen about and clump into higher-level structures (symmballs), while the bounciness of the walls varies with the state of the world outside. It is meant as a toy model of the brain, with symms as neural substrate and symmballs as concepts. The organizing question is who shoves whom: do the symms push the symmballs around, or do the symmballs push the symms around?

Since the symm-level dynamics are complete and deterministic, I can take two careenium states that realize the same symmball pattern out of different symm arrangements, and the two will then evolve differently. So the symmball level cannot be doing causal work: it is not even sufficient for what happens next, and the substrate goes on its way regardless of how we describe it.

My co-author wants to rescue top-down causation by stipulating an extra term in the symm dynamics, such that each symm also feels a force proportional to the local symmball-level density, so that macro-level facts literally push on the substrate. My question: is this the right way to secure the claim that symbols push the substrate around, and how large should that extra term be for the symmball level to count as a genuine cause rather than an epiphenomenal readout?
