I teach a first-year seminar on historiography, and our closing session puts Polybius's cycle of constitutions alongside Ibn Khaldun, Spengler, and Toynbee. I want to end with a poem rather than a summary.

Please write a sestina whose six end-words correspond one-to-one to the six phases of anacyclosis — kingship, tyranny, aristocracy, oligarchy, democracy, ochlocracy — appearing in that order in the first stanza, rotating by the standard rule thereafter, and all six returning in a three-line envoi. The form itself should carry the cyclical claim: the rotation is a cycle of length six that comes back to its starting arrangement whether or not anyone believes in it, and I would rather the poem's structure show that than have any stanza assert it.

The content should complicate it. Set the poem in a polity that has read its own cyclical theory: its magistrates keep a ledger of where the city currently stands in the cycle and stage the expected degeneration in advance, crowning the tyrant on schedule and subsidizing the mob on schedule, so that the transition is orderly and nothing is lost. I want the reader to feel the worry that a recurrence which everyone predicts and plans around is not evidence for a cyclical view of history at all — it is self-fulfilling — while the poem's rotation goes on turning regardless of what anyone intends.

Two further constraints. Arrange the stanzas so that the ochlocracy stanza closes on the end-word that belongs to kingship: let the mob already be speaking in the idiom of the ruler it is about to install. And let the envoi be where the question settles, making something of the fact that the six words return in a stanza of a different shape and length than the one they left. Accuracy matters: a reader who knows Book VI of the Histories should recognize the sequence and its mechanism of degeneration — each good form decaying through the inherited power and corruption of its holders — rather than finding only the slogan that history repeats.

After the poem, add one sentence of prose saying what the ending commits to, and then a final line in the form \answer{...} containing a single label — circle, spiral, line, or a short phrase of your own — naming the view of recurrence the closing lines actually take.
