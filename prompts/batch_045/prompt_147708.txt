I have been trading decision problems with a friend who works in this area, and I want to know what she will say about one I have just built. I care less about the verdict itself than about which rule it falls out of, so please treat the prediction as a way of getting at the rule.

Her track record, in my own shorthand: in Newcomb's problem with opaque boxes and a near-perfect predictor she one-boxes; in the smoking-lesion case, where a lesion causes both the desire to smoke and the cancer, she smokes; in the hitchhiker case, where a stranger drives her to town only because he predicted she would pay on arrival, she pays once she arrives; in the counterfactual mugging, where a predictor flips a coin and pays a million in the tails branch to anyone who would have handed over a hundred if asked, she pays; in XOR blackmail, where a letter is sent exactly when either a disaster has occurred or she is the sort of person who would pay to avert it, but not both, she refuses to pay.

The new case is a counterfactual mugging with an extortionist in the predictor's chair. A week ago someone abducted a person she loves and ran a detailed simulation of her in order to fix, in advance, what he would do: the hostage is released unharmed tomorrow if and only if the simulation predicted she would pay ten thousand dollars when asked. The simulation has been run, its result is settled and will not be revisited, and she is now asked for the money. To isolate the structure, stipulate three things: the transfer is anonymous, the kidnapper never learns whether it was made, and he dies before he could ever repeat the scheme. There are no legal, reputational, or other downstream consequences in either direction.

Does she pay? The work I want is this: set out the rules that are compatible with all five of her earlier verdicts, including which of the familiar theories those verdicts already rule out, and make sure at least two of the surviving rules come apart on the ransom case; say what each of them predicts here and which structural feature each one is keying on — that the harm was fixed by a prediction of her own policy, or that the agent who fixed it profits from her being the sort of person who pays. If you think my three stipulations collapse the case into a plain counterfactual mugging and make the kidnapper framing idle, say so and explain what that framing was supposed to be adding. Then give me your best guess with the reason for preferring that rule, and name the variant of the case that would separate the competing rules most cleanly, even if it is not the obvious one.

Please structure the response as follows. Candidate rules, about 150 words: the rules consistent with her five verdicts, stated precisely enough that their disagreement about the ransom is derivable rather than asserted, plus a note on what the track record excludes. What each rules says about the ransom, about 250 words: the prediction per rule, the feature doing the work in each, and an honest assessment of whether my stipulations are doing more work than I think. Best guess, about 150 words: the verdict you expect, the rule you take her to be running, and what would move you off it. The variant that settles it, about 150 words: the modified case you would put to her, why the rules diverge there, and what each would say.
