I am working on Machiavelli's treatment of fortune and virtù, and I would rather settle what to demand of an interpretation before reading more of them. Write me the desiderata — with the case or consideration behind each — that an interpretation of the pair has to meet, under one stipulation about purpose: the reading is to be adequate as guidance for an agent who must act now, so it has to be possible, in advance of the outcome, to say what counts as acting with virtù, and not merely to redescribe afterwards who happened to be fortunate.

The items I most want adjudicated rather than assumed:

- the arithmetic: if fortune arbitrates roughly half of what men do and leaves the rest to them, must virtù be an independent causal factor, or may it be read as the choice of means towards ends that fortune supplies — and if the latter, does any advice survive?
- the river comparison: what does an interpretation owe to the distinction between preparing against fortune in calm times and withstanding it once it floods, and to the implication that the same works that protect one city wreck another?
- the conformity-to-the-times problem: his own remark that men hold to their modes while the times move, so that identical conduct ruins one man and saves another. Must an interpretation decide whether virtù is a stable trait or a fit between a trait and an occasion, and can it hold both?
- the two hard cases as he presents them: Cesare Borgia, cited as the model of a new prince, undone by his father's death and his own illness at the decisive moment; and Julius II, whose impetuosity succeeded only because the times agreed with it and would have ruined him had they changed. Do these show that virtù is insufficient, or that it was never really present? And, relatedly, is the closing comparison of fortune to a woman who yields to the bold load-bearing for the account — a claim about the kind of conduct fortune rewards — or decoration, and what turns on the answer?
- the institutional level: the suggestion in the Discourses on Livy that a commonwealth outlasts a principality because the variety of its citizens lets it vary its mode as the times vary. Must a reading of the individual-level advice be continuous with this, or is demanding continuity a prejudice of the republican reader?

Mark which requirements are non-negotiable given the purpose I have fixed and which are preferences of a particular camp; say which single requirement excludes the largest number of interpretations currently on offer; and if some pair cannot be jointly satisfied — I suspect the action-guiding requirement and the situation-relative one are in direct tension — say so explicitly and say which should give way.
