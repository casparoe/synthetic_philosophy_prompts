I've been chewing on the worst-case vs average-case guarantees thing in alignment for like a week and I keep going in definately circles, so I'd love it if you'd write me a dialogue that actually puts pressure on both sides instead of letting one of them win by construction.

Setup: two senior people at the same lab, both of whom have read all the same stuff and who agree on the bleak parts of the threat model — they think a sufficiently capable system can be deceptively aligned, that optimization pressure eats any eval you build, that you shouldn't trust a model's stated reasons. None of that is what they're arguing about. What they're arguing about is what kind of assurance to try to buy, given they have to actually make a call this quarter: whether to hand about two hundred internal engineers a coding agent with repo write access, tool use, and a persistent scratchpad that survives between sessions.

Have them argue through three things I've been calling, for lack of better words, the corridor, the handoff tax, and the shared ruler. The corridor is the set of situations in which your guarantee is supposed to hold, plus the assumption of corridor opacity — that the system can't tell whether it's inside the corridor or not, so it can't condition its behavior on that. The handoff tax is the observation that guarantees attach to a frozen artifact — a model in a harness with a specific toolset, prompt stack and memory setup — and every change to any of those costs you some of the guarantee, so maybe you only ever prove things about a snapshot that isn't the thing that ships, and guarantees don't compose at all once you bolt on a scratchpad. The shared ruler is the point that the average-case side's evidence comes out of a monitor built from the same lineage as the agent, so the monitor is least reliable exactly on the inputs where the agent is most capable, which means the ruler shrinks precisely where you needed the measurement.

What I want is for each of them to get to use all three against the other — the worst-case person leaning hardest on the shared ruler and on the capability ratchet dilemma (that the capabilities you'd need in order to build or check a guarantee are the same ones that break the assumptions the guarantee rests on), the average-case person leaning hardest on the handoff tax and on the claim that corridor opacity is unachievable in practice, so a worst-case guarantee is a guarantee about an object that never actually exists at deployment time. Neither should be a strawman: each of them should at some point state the other's best argument better than the other stated it, and each should concede one thing that genuinely costs them something. End with them still disagreeing, but having localized the disagreement to one specific checkable claim, and have both say what they'd actually do on Monday morning.

Format: continuous prose only, the way you'd write an email — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. Dialogue as running prose with speaker attribution inline, no stage directions. And please don't have either of them name-drop papers or cite anything; I want the arguments, not the recieving history.
