I'm defending a dissertation chapter on the bootstrapping problem in three weeks, and what I need now is a Q&A prep sheet rather than another draft of the argument. The chapter's thesis is that the familiar self-verification cases aren't, at bottom, a problem about closure, or about the circularity of the reasoning, or about induction as such; they are a problem about the evidential structure of reliability attributions. I defend what I call the Independent Error Channel requirement: knowledge that a source is reliable requires that there be some channel, distinct from that source, capable in principle of registering the source's failures, and that the track record one bootstraps from be anchored in that channel by hits and misses the source itself did not certify. Where the requirement holds, ordinary bootstrapping is entirely in order — checking one thermometer against another, a translation against a dictionary, a witness against a second witness — and that is the selling point: I get to keep closure, keep induction, and keep externalism, while explaining why the pure cases, in which a source's own outputs are the only evidence for its reliability, yield nothing. The argument is short: the inductive step in a bootstrapping argument requires that the sample of checked instances not be filtered through the very source being assessed; absent an independent error channel it is filtered exactly that way, so the evidence is screened off by the hypothesis and the likelihood ratio is one; so the knowledge isn't merely easy, it is zero. I arrived at this after an argument with a friend who works in machine learning, and the analogy I keep returning to is that a source with no independent error channel is like a classifier evaluated only on unlabeled data drawn from its own training distribution.

The audience is my committee plus two visitors, and I can predict their temperaments better than their questions. My supervisor is a process reliabilist who thinks every problem in this area is the generality problem wearing a hat, and who will want to know which process type a channel is supposed to be and why my requirement doesn't simply inherit the arbitrariness it was introduced to fix. The second reader is a formal epistemologist who translates everything into priors and likelihoods, is convinced the whole puzzle is a screening-off fact, and will be unimpressed unless the requirement is stated as a condition on the evidence model rather than in my metaphorical vocabulary. The external member works on Moorean responses to skepticism and holds that the demand for knowledge of a source's reliability is itself the skeptic's premise; she will press me on whether taking that demand seriously concedes the skeptical dialectic from the start. There is also a measurement theorist from statistics who thinks in terms of identifiability and signal detection, and for whom the interesting claim is that sensitivity cannot be estimated without ground truth, plus a junior philosopher of machine learning who will want to know whether my requirement is just the demand for a held-out labeled set and whether self-supervised learning refutes it.

What I want from you is the objections I will actually get, ordered by how much damage they would do rather than by how clever they are, each phrased in the idiom of the person who will raise it — process individuation for the reliabilist, likelihood ratios for the Bayesian, whether the question is even well posed for the anti-skeptic, identifiability for the statistician, validation and distribution shift for the machine learning person — and each with a reply I could give in about two minutes, an indication of what I should concede outright, and a note on where the reply is soft. Include the case-variants I should expect to be hit with: a device with two channels whose failures are perfectly correlated, which satisfies my requirement extensionally but shouldn't; a source whose reliability seems knowable a priori, where my view appears to predict skepticism about something plainly known; and the regress worry that the independent channel needs its own channel. Two things matter most to me. First, tell me which of my three steps is the weak joint the committee is most likely to find and how I should reinforce it. Second, name the objection I should be hoping nobody asks: my own suspicion is the application of the requirement to deduction or to reasoning itself, and what it would force me to say about knowing that our own inference is reliable, but if you think there is a worse one, say so and explain why it cuts deeper.
