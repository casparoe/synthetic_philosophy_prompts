so im in highschool and ive gotten kind of obsessed with this question: what should you believe about a math claim you havent proved yet — like whether some enormous number is prime, or whether the googolth digit of pi is a 7. The view ive ended up with (i think i got it from some MIRI writeup my friend sent me) is that the right norm here is basically a no-Dutch-book condition: there can be a computable procedure that keeps putting prices on every sentence and revising them as it proves more stuff, and the sense in which it's doing well isnt "its credences match the truth" but "no computable betting strategy can make unlimited money off it" — it never gets systematically exploited, at least in the limit. I think that's basically right and i want to write a contest paper defending it.

So: what would count against it? Not "math is subjective" handwaving. I mean real defeaters, and i want them ranked from fatal to merely awkward.

- How much is doing work in "in the limit"? If a reasoner can be wildly wrong for the first trillion steps and still count as fine, is that a fatal problem for a norm meant for agents like us, or just a practical complaint?
- Is inexploitability a truth-conduciveness norm at all, or is it pure coherence? If two inexploitable reasoners can disagree about the same conjecture indefinitely, what am i even claiming when i say one is doing better than the other?
- What happens with sentences your axioms never decide, like a godel-style one? If the price never gets forced anywhere, does the criterion just go silent — and is silence on the interesting cases a problem?
- And the self-reference stuff: what should it believe about its own future beliefs, or about cases where its beliefs about what it will do are part of what makes a claim true? Someone mentioned these things can get "trolled" into wrong beliefs about their own actions. Real defeater or fixable edge case?

Which of these would someone who already likes this framework just concede up front, and which would they actually fight? And be honest with me: has this been set up so that nothing could ever refute it, and if so is that a bug or fine? Tell me which one youd find most convincing, and where to focus so the paper actually says something.
