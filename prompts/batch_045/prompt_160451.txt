I'm playing a critic, not myself, so don't take it personally. Here's my opening.

Your account stands or falls on resentment, gratitude and indignation being responses to the will we take another person's action to manifest. Here is something with no will at all, and every one of the attitudes turns up anyway, and they don't reliably dissolve once the mechanism is explained. So pick one. Either the attitudes track the appearance of being addressed rather than any will, in which case they can't be what moral responsibility consists in; or they really do track will, and you owe me an account of why the machine is outside the practice when it answers to reasons better than half the people you'd happily hold responsible. And don't give me the move about how we couldn't give up the participant stance even if we wanted to — I'm not proposing we give it up, I'm proposing it's running on the wrong fuel. One more thing while I have you: people in this case do something odd. They resent the thing when it's cold and feel gratitude when it's warm, but they don't hold it responsible — they close the tab rather than demanding an apology. If the attitudes peel off from holding responsible that easily, what's left of your claim that the two are internally tied together?
