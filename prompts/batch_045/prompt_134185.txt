Hi — I work for a state alliance of about 250 independent pharmacies. I'm the staff person who shepherds our practice standards: the board of nine votes on them four times a year, our counsel has to clear whatever we send out, and whatever we recommend goes to members in writing with reasons they can argue with. Because we're competitors, counsel is extremely clear that the alliance can't be used to reach any understanding about what members will do — no joint commitments, no we-all-agreed-to-this, nothing that smells like coordinating conduct. So each member ends up deciding alone whether to take on some costly practice where the payoff only shows up if enough others do it to: keep making a compounded pediatric suspension at a loss so the regional referral network and the inspector's familiarity with it survive, post real cash prices when the shop across town doesn't, stay open extra evening hours for a vaccination service that only draws people if most pharmacies offer it. Roughly eight of these a year, and the stakes run from a few thousand dollars to existential for a small store.

What I want is a decision procedure, runable in half an hour by someone with no philosophy background, that takes seriously the thing I've been reading about — that if I reason evidentially about my own action, and the world is large in the sense that I can't write down in advance every state that would screen off the correlation between what I decide and what other agents relevantly like me decide, then my cooperating is genuine evidence that they cooperate, and that can beat the obvious causal calculation where I just eat the cost by myself. The story is that in a small world, where you can condition on a fully specified state, you get the usual defection answer, and large worlds break it. I get the headline. I cannot turn it into something nine pharmacists can actually run.

So: the steps in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no. Please handle specifically: how to fix the reference class of agents my action is evidence about before anyone knows which way it points, since otherwise everyone picks the class that tells them what they wanted to do anyway; what to do when the class comes out nearly empty, or when I've conditioned on so much of my own situation that the only agent relevantly like me is me; not double counting when there's also some real causal channel, because word does get around the county; what it even means to decide to cooperate if the whole point is that I can't choose which kind of agent I am — managing the news versus managing the world; and the case where widening the class adds people but drops the correlation, trading off against the same marginal benefit.

Then run it on two proposals: keeping the money-losing compounded pediatric suspension, and adopting posted transparent cash prices. Include one case where it hands us an answer we won't like — I'd rather have that than a procedure that always says be nice. And tell us definitely where it hands the decision back to a human because the question is one of values rather than facts, and honestly where you think it will just misfire.
