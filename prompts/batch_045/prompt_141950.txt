I never went to university for any of this. I got into an argument with my brother about why our basement floods and it turned into me trying to work out how anybody ever decides *which story* is the right one when two stories both fit the facts equally well. Here are my gut calls, and I want to know what rule Im unconsciously following.

1. Basement floods every March, east wall only, only after heavy rain. Story A: the downspout on that side dumps water against a clay bank right at the foundation. Story B: the town storm sewer backs up in snowmelt season and pushes water up through the floor drain. A for me, and I'd back A before anybody crawls out there and looks.

2. My friend went quiet on me for three weeks then came back as if nothing had happened. A: she was annoyed by something I said. B: she was dealing with something unrelated and pulled back from everyone. I pick A, even though I know B is far more common.

3. Three weird things in my house: wifi dies around 7pm, the bulbs in one room hum, the microwave clock keeps resetting. A: three separate faults, each with a decent story of its own. B: one cause behind all three, though I can't say what it is. I go with B.

4. Grandad's workshop clock, nobody had wound it in years, found running. A: my uncle wound it and never mentioned it. B: there's a battery in it after all and the battery still had charge. I go with A — a hidden thing a person did.

5. My sourdough starter died four times running, always on day three. A: something living in my kitchen that I can't see and can't test for. B: I keep making the same mistake with temperature. I go with B, and A feels like cheating. These two are the ones I can't square: in 4 the invisible cause is my favourite story, in 5 it's the worst one on offer.

6. Our office numbers jumped right after the new manager started and everyone says she's brilliant. Explanation: she changed what gets counted. That one makes me happier than any other explanation I've ever had.

My guess at my own rule: go with the story I'd have bet on *before* I saw the details — the one that would have made me expect roughly this. I think that gets 1 and 6 right and gets 3 and 5 wrong, and for 2 and 4 I geniunely can't tell.

Lay it out like this:

**The principle** — two sentences, plain words. If you need a technical term, explain it in the same sentence.
**Case by case** — a couple of sentences on each of my six: does your principle give my verdict, yes or no.
**The awkward pair** — one short paragraph on 4 versus 5, and what actually separates them.
**Where I'm wrong** — one short paragraph, honestly: which of my verdicts your principle can't save, and which one I should drop.
**A case of your own** — make up a case yourself, one short paragraph, where your principle forces a verdict I probably won't like.
**My flip-flop** — half a paragraph on this one, which I go back and forth on: every job I've had, within six months I'm being left out of meetings I should be in. Story A: something I do. Story B: it's the kind of flat, disorganised workplace I keep picking, it happens to everyone there and I'm just the one who notices it as a pattern. Does your principle settle it, or does it genuinely depend on something I'd have to go and find out?
