The complaint: one of this year's readers was the doctoral supervisor of an applicant from six years ago. She didn't recuse. My friend's position, which I cannot fully shake: he's into Confucian role ethics, and he says duties come attached to roles and don't evaporate the moment a degree is conferred. He also brought up that story in the Analects where someone is praised as upright for testifying that his father stole a sheep, and Confucius reportedly says something like: no, in my village fathers and sons cover for each other, and that's where real uprightness lives. He calls the dynamic 'the recusal ratchet': every scandal widens the list of disqualifying ties until the only people left judging are the ones who know the least, while the people with actual knowledge and actual obligations get pushed out, and negative partiality (rivals, ex-collaborators who fell out, people with grudges) stays completely unregulated the whole time because it never shows up on a disclosure form.
