I'm in a sort of informal reading group on animal ethics and we've spent like three sessions stuck on one question: is brain size morally relevant? I thought we'd already settled the easy parts. Raw brain mass can't be the thing, since most of it just tracks body size (that's the whole reason people reach for encephalization quotient instead), and EQ isn't great either because what you'd really want is the number of neurons doing the relevant kind of work — and that measure apparently breaks too, because I read somewhere that some bird forebrains pack in primate-level numbers of tiny neurons, and kids who've had an entire hemisphere surgically removed still come out conscious and functioning surprisingly normally. So all the naive versions are dead. Fine.

What we cannot get past is the exchange rate. My roommate has this view he calls "moral weight per watt": brains are metabolically expensive tissue, so the right comparison isn't how much neural machinery an animal has but how much experience it gets per unit of energy burned — on his numbers a crow or a small dog beats a cow, and he thinks that gives small efficient brains a stronger claim on our concern than big wasteful ones. I think that's clever but wrong, and that the right currency is total phenomenal output: if an elephant or a whale really does have several times more neurons than us doing whatever produces experience, then there's its just more experience happening inside that skull, so ceteris paribus it gets proportionally more moral weight, the way ten people in a burning room count for more than one person in a burning room.

We keep going in circles and I don't think we're even disputing facts anymore, we just can't agree which quantity is the right one to plug into the scale. Can you settle it? I don't need "it's really about sentience, not size" — we all accept that already; the question is how much weight each being gets once sentience is in play. So: per watt, per neuron, per gram of cortex, or some other measure, and what's the argument for picking that one?
