Taking a formal epistemology module this term and our TA put a variant of Sleeping Beauty on the board last week that I cant get out of my head. Reminder of the set-up she was modifying: fair coin on Sunday, Heads means you're woken once, Tails means you're woken on two days, memory wiped in between, wakings subjectively identical. Her twist: the coin isn't tossed on Sunday, it's tossed at the end of the first day, after the first waking, so on day 1 it hasn't happened yet. Second twist: Tails buys a thousand wakings (days 1 to 1000), Heads just the one.

I think I've turned this into an argument that kills the halfer position half my seminar holds, but the way it does it makes me think I cheated somewhere.

(1) Conditional on this being the first waking, Beauty's credence in Heads is exactly 1/2. If the experimenter walked in and said "this is day 1", she'd say 1/2: the coin is fair and untossed, and nothing about her situation is evidence about a toss that hasn't happened.
(2) Conditional on this being a later waking, her credence in Heads is 0.
(3) So cr(Heads) = 1/2 × cr(day 1). Just total probability over the partition {day 1, ..., day 1000} of her centred possibilities.
(4) Halfer premise: waking up tells her nothing she didn't already know on Sunday — she knew the protocol guaranteed at least one waking, and nothing in a waking distinguishes the days — so cr(Heads) = 1/2, same as Sunday.
(5) So cr(day 1) = 1. At every single waking she's certain this is her first waking.
(6) But on Tails runs, half the time, there are 999 wakings at which she's flat out certain of something false, and on day 1 she can see this coming — she knows there's a 1/2 chance she'll soon be certain of a falsehood. That can't be the right answer.

Steps 1 to 3 feel like bookkeeping to me, so I want to reject 4, but I can't see what exactly is wrong with it, and if I do reject it I get cr(day 1) = 2/1001, meaning she should be 99.8% sure she's on a later waking — wierd, but at least not certain of a lie. Which step do you reject, why did it look safe, and what do the other exit routes cost? Lead with the one step you'd reject in a single sentence, then spend the rest on those two things.
