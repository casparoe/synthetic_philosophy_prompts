My opening move, and I think it definately breaks you. Forget the time-indexed version of grue. Define: an object is greck iff either it is green and has been examined at least once, or it is blue and has never been examined. Now suppose that, as it happens, all emeralds are green. Then every emerald anyone has ever looked at is a positive instance of "all emeralds are greck" — green and examined — and nothing ever refutes it, because the moment you examine a new emerald it flips into the green-and-examined disjunct. But greck also says the next emerald you look at is blue, and that you can never check: doing the looking is what makes it examined. Meanwhile "examined", "observed", "green", "blue", "not" are about as entrenched as vocabulary gets, and they're greck's parents, so entrenchment-by-parentage looks useless here. So what, on your story, makes "all emeralds are green" projectible and "all emeralds are greck" not? And "nobody has in fact projected greck yet" isn't an answer — I'm asking what's wrong with someone who starts projecting it today.
