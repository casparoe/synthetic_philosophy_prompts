I am applying for a nine-month postdoctoral fellowship in philosophy (decision theory and formal epistemology) and need the central document — about two pages, single-spaced — for a project I have only half-formulated. I would like you to write it.

The question sits where the small-world/large-world distinction meets the literature on evidential cooperation. In small worlds the story is familiar: an evidential decision theorist facing a prisoner's dilemma against a sufficiently similar opponent — a copy, a clone, an agent produced by the same mecha­nism — treats her own act as evidence about the other's act and cooperates. What interests me is the large-world version, where the agent does not yet know what she will do, so her unfolding intention, and even the intermediate steps of her deliberation, are themselves evidence she can condition on.

My half-formed idea is that two mechanisms get run together under the label of evidential cooperation, and that they come apart as soon as we let the agent be uncertain *how similar* the other agents are. Call them mirror cooperation and signal cooperation. Under the mirror mechanism the agent is uncertain which agent in the population she is; her act is thus a sample from a policy she shares with the others, and conditioning on it is conditioning on the policy. Under the signal mechanism the agent knows who she is, but is uncertain what kind of agent she is; her act is then one imperfect indication of her type, and types are correlated across the population. My suspicion is that evidential cooperation is robust under the first mechanism and fragile under the second: when my own act is a single noisy signal about a shared type, conditioning on it moves my expectation about the others' acts by less and less as the population grows, so the case for cooperation should decay with population size in a way that the two-agent mirror case hides. If that is right, much of the optimism about the cooperative credentials of evidential decision theory rests on examples of the wrong shape: voting, climate mitigation, and coordination among AI developers look signal-type rather than mirror-type, and it is not obvious that the two-agent results carry over to them.

Please turn this into a proposal that contains: a statement of the question precise enough to have a determinate answer, including a definition of the mirror/signal split that could be checked against a model rather than merely asserted; an explanation of why the standard defenses of evidential cooperation and the standard objections to it both leave this open — the sympathetic arguments tend to proceed from two-agent or perfectly correlated cases, the critical ones from the thought that conditioning on your own act is illicit regardless of the mechanism; the method, laid out month by month, including the minimal formal model (parameters for population size, degree of type-correlation, and how much of the agent's deliberation is observable to her before she acts) and a defence of that model against the obvious alternatives, such as running the question purely informally or burying it in a simulation; the range of possible conclusions and which considerations would decide between them; and an honest account of what would count as the project failing and what might make it too large for nine months.
