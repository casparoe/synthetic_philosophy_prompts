After a long argument with a friend last night I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. For decades the physics-of-information literature has been split between two camps about why the demon fails. One holds that the demon is defeated by the thermodynamic cost of information processing — canonically, the cost of erasing its memory, so that the second law survives only once information is treated as physical. The other holds that this is beside the point: write down a physically explicit demon, give it a finite memory, require the whole apparatus to run cyclically, and the ledger already balances, because acquiring and storing the record costs at least as much as the demon later extracts; on that view nothing about erasure, and nothing about information as such, is needed to save the second law. Both camps now work in the idiom of fluctuation theorems, both accept the same generalized bounds in which the extractable work is limited by the correlation between system and memory rather than by the memory's own entropy, both accept every experiment on information-to-work conversion, and neither side has moved in years.

My hypothesis is that the quarrel is not about physics but about what an exorcism is supposed to accomplish: whether the target is a particular imagined demon whose failure has to be demonstrated, or the broader thesis that information is physical, which the critics suspect is being smuggled in as a premise. But I can see three other candidates and can't tell which is doing the real work: (i) a verbal dispute about 'erasure', where one side means compressing a memory into a standard state and the other means any operation that leaves the environment carrying the record; (ii) a hidden empirical or formal question about whether measurement can in principle be done reversibly, and hence whether the cost attaches to acquisition or to reset; (iii) a disagreement about which setups count as the data — the one-molecule engine with a one-bit memory versus, say, a demon that never resets at all but periodically swaps its full memory, by a reversible interaction, against an external register prepared in the maximally mixed state, so that the correlations are pushed into the environment without ever being compressed.

Tell me which of these, if any, is the actual structure of the disagreement, using what each side really argues, and say what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction, a theorem, an experiment, a restatement of the question — or why no progress should be expected.
