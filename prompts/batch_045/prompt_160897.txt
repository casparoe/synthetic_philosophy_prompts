I run a small reading group on philosophy of physics — most of us came to it through the usual blogs and forum sequences rather than through a degree program — and I've been asked to write up my own position on the interpretation of quantum mechanics so the others can attack it. Here it is:

1. Everett is basically right: no collapse, no hidden variables, the quantum state is the whole physical ontology.
2. Copenhagen isn't a theory so much as a rule about when to stop asking questions. Bohmian mechanics strikes me as the same theory with invisible extra furniture bolted on: I don't see the particle positions doing any work the wavefunction isn't already doing.
3. I'm fairly operationalist about probability. Probabilities are credences, and a probability assignment that never makes a difference to what you'd bet on or how you'd act is, as far as I can tell, empty.
4. That said, I don't buy the decision-theoretic derivations of the Born rule. They look to me like they recover the squared-amplitude weights by building in, as a constraint on rationality, exactly the kind of indifference to branching that was supposed to be derived.
5. Nevertheless, I do hold that an agent who knows she is about to branch should apportion her concern across the branches in proportion to their squared amplitude, and not in proportion to how many branches there are.
6. And I think the quantum suicide / immortality style of argument is bogus, not merely unsettling.

Please audit the package rather than explain the interpretations to me. Which pairs genuinely pull against each other, and which only look like they do until you unpack them? For each real tension, tell me what would have to give and what that costs me elsewhere in the list. Rank them by severity — I'd rather not treat all of them as equally bad — and say whether there's a single revision that would clean up more than one at a time.
