i'm on a city charter review commission (mid-sized city, we also run the regional water utility) and we keep circling the same fight: who gets a say on what. three live cases: whether long-term non-citizen residents can vote in municipal elections, whether an unincorporated neighborhood just outside the city limits that draws on the same aquifer gets any input into a water-rate restructure, and whether people who've lived abroad for twenty years but still own property here keep their vote. i tried to write down my actual view rather than just settling each case by gut, and i want a consistency check before i bring it back to the committee. here it is:

1. the all-affected principle is the right starting point: the demos for a decision is everyone whose important interests are substantially affected by it.
2. the demos has to be settled in advance and independently of the decision. you need a roll, you cant discover who was entitled to vote by seeing how things turn out, and nobody should get to resize the electorate to win.
3. children and adults with severe cognitive disabilities are in. i'm fine with guardians or proxies casting those votes.
4. i'm an instrumentalist about why democracy is worth having: it tracks the common good better than the alternatives, and i don't think there's a freestanding right to participate that holds regardless of outcomes.
5. non-citizen residents: yes in municipal elections, no in national ones.
6. future generations count, and i want an institution for them — some kind of guardian body with a suspensive veto over irreversible decisions.
7. my own bit, which i invented and am sort of proud of: instead of a binary in/out, three tiers — full members who vote, associate members who can speak and propose but not vote, and conditional standing, where outsiders whose expected stake in a particular decision crosses a threshold get a vote on that decision and nothing else.

all seven feel equally solid to me, which is exactly why i'm suspicious. please go through the set and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, rank those tensions by how bad they are, and for each one say which commitment has to give and what giving it up would cost me. i also want to know if any of them only look inconsistent and turn out to be fine on closer inspection — and if so, why the appearance. concrete cases please: if one of these can't deliver the verdict i actually want in the aquifer case, or in the non-citizen case, say which one and show me where it breaks. finally, is there a single revision that cleans up the most at once, and is my tiered scheme (7) helping or just making the underlying mess worse?
