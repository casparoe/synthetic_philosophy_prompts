I am a seventeen-year-old student, and I have run into a contradiction that I suspect is not really about me or my school, so I would like help working out what is going on underneath it.

Last year my school changed how homework in mathematics and philosophy is graded. Handing in a written solution or essay is no longer enough: a few days later we have to defend it in a short oral examination, in class, without notes or devices, and a large part of the mark now depends on the defence rather than on the submitted text. My teachers were explicit about the reason. A chatbot can now produce a clean, well-structured written derivation of a problem it does not understand, and so, they said, a written explanation is no longer evidence of anything. What is left to test is whether you can rebuild and defend the reasoning live, under time pressure, while someone interrupts you with a follow-up question they invented on the spot.

What puzzles me is that the same adult world seems to run the opposite rule for the systems themselves. When an AI produces its answer as a numbered chain of steps instead of blurting out the final line, it gets better marks on the tests that are used to compare systems, and this is widely reported as the system reasoning rather than guessing. Systems are also trained by being rewarded for written derivations that land on the right answer. And yet the people who build them also warn that such a derivation is often written after the fact, that the system would have given the same answer without it, and that it should not be read as a record of what the system actually did. So one written explanation is worthless as evidence of understanding when a student produces it, and genuine evidence of understanding when a model produces it, and the same people seem comfortable with both judgements.

The reverse asymmetry bothers me too. My physics teacher still credits me with understanding a topic when I have to look up a constant or check a formula in my notes, and nobody thinks a professional mathematician has stopped understanding her field because she needs a week and a textbook to reconstruct a proof. So the live, unaided, real-time standard that my school now applies to students is not the standard anyone applies to adults.

I have two questions, and I would rather you argued them out than pointed me to sources, since I have no access to academic papers.

First: what theory of understanding does each of these practices presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view in my school's oral-defence rule, and the implicit view in the practice of treating a generated chain of steps as evidence of reasoning. Be careful to separate what each practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and tell me where the practice is underdetermined, so that several different theories of understanding would license it. Is there any single, coherent account on which a written rationale counts as no evidence at all from a student and as real evidence from a model? If not, say plainly which of the two practices is the confused one and why.

Second, here is my own attempt at a better test, and I would like you to evaluate it rather than humour it. Instead of asking whether an explanation is present, ask whether the explanation is load-bearing. Take a chain of steps that a system produced, alter one stated reason in it: change a premise into its near-opposite, delete a step in the middle, or insert a consideration that ought to be irrelevant. Then see whether the final answer moves in the way that that reason should push it. Call this the steering test. A reason whose alteration moves the answer is doing work; a reason whose alteration leaves the answer where it was is decoration, however fluently it is written. Is the steering test a better proxy for understanding than either of the two practices I described? What exactly would passing it establish, and what would it fail to establish? And what should we say about systems that get the answers reliably right while producing no readable derivation at all: under which of the theories you lined up do they understand, and under which do they not?
