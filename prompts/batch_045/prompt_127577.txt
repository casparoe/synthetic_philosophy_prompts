Here's the ask. Write an internal onboarding FAQ for new caseworkers at the Office for Introspective Accuracy — a government agency in a country where illusionism about phenomenal consciousness has been adopted as official doctrine. Header first (mission statement, founding statute, the Office's powers), then 8-10 numbered Q&As. Around 800 words. Voice: deadpan civil-service, heavy on forms, escalation procedures, performance targets, budget lines.

Setup, so we're on the same page. Citizens write in with first-personal reports: that there is something it is like for them, that their pain has a felt quality, that colours look some way to them, that they know what an experience is like. Official doctrine: no such properties are instantiated; the introspective system misrepresents its own states as having them. So every report is a report of something that isn't there, and the reports just keep arriving, and somebody has to administer that. That's the Office.

Things I want worked in:

- The Office treats the administrative problem of processing reports as though solving it were the whole job. There's a target for reports processed, a fully specified causal model of how a report gets produced from a first-order state, and a line in the annual report saying that once that mechanism is completely mapped, the file is closed. Nobody ever states that mapping the mechanism explains why people talk the way they do and not what they are talking about — the reader should notice that on their own.
- Recursion. Citizens and staff file second-order reports: that it still seems to them as though there is something it is like, that even if the property isn't instantiated the seeming is itself something they are acquainted with, and so on up. Give each level a form number and an escalation ladder, and note somewhere that every new level generates a fresh wave of paperwork, so the ladder never terminates. Maybe a figure: reports received have risen every year since the doctrine was adopted, and the Office is oddly proud of the processing rate rather than worried by the volume.
- Be fair to the target. The Office must never say that experiences don't exist, or that nothing is going on in there. The doctrine is that no phenomenal properties are instantiated. Have the guidance get genuinely annoyed at a citizens' campaign that keeps stating the view in the stronger way, and give that misstatement an officious capitalised name.
- A staff-training note: do not use the colour-scientist vignette or the philosophical-zombie vignette during induction; both have unacceptably high conversion rates among trainees.
- The caseworker's own case. Every new caseworker must record, at induction, what it is like for them to complete the form. At least one Q&A should be written by someone who has plainly lost the ability to keep the official line and their own case apart — without ever admitting it, and without the Office ever commenting on it.
- A budget note: the gap between the physical facts and the reports is funded through some near-future year, plus a sentence about what happens to the Office if the funding lapses.

Keep it legible. Someone who's never heard of the hard problem should be able to folllow the comedy; someone who has should think the satire is fair and not a straw man. Stay deadpan, don't wink, and don't let the document accidentally concede that phenomenal consciousness is real — unless the slip is itself the joke. pls no puns on qualia unless one is really good.
