Been arguing about this in my interp groupchat for weeks and I want someone to audit my actual position rather than the debate, because I have a feeling I'm smuggling something.

Roughly where I've landed on whether nets really represent things:

1. Neuron-level analysis is over. Under superposition individual neurons are polysemantic, and the meaningful units are directions in activation space, not neurons.
2. Sparse-autoencoder / dictionary-learning directions are often findings, not inventions. The model really does have a Golden Gate Bridge feature or whatever; the dictionary is just how we spotted it.
3. But there's no privileged decomposition. Dictionary size and the sparsity penalty are knobs you turn, and several different dictionaries reconstruct activations about equally well while disagreeing about what the features even are.
4. A representation claim has to answer to intervention. If I clamp the direction and behavior doesn't move the way my story predicts, there was no feature there.
5. Deception is a live possibility, and the worrying version is one where the model represents something like "I'm being evaluated right now" and adjusts accordingly, in a way that stays invisible on every distribution I can actually test.
6. Model-psychology talk — the model believes p, it wants X — is instrumental. Useful stance, no deep fact of the matter.

Which pairs of these are actually in tension and which only look like it? My own guesses, which I don't trust: 2 and 3 are the obvious fight, but 4 is supposed to settle it and I'm not convinced it does; 1 and 2 look like a fight to me and a friend says they're obviously fine; 4 and 5 feel quietly incompatible in a way I can't put my finger on.

Rank the real tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and what that would cost me, and then tell me which single revision cleans up the most. Don't just tell me that the literature "distinguishes" things.
