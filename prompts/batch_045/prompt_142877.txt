My opening move. In my field there is an argument I meet constantly and have learned not to trust: some outcome would be catastrophic, its probability is not zero, therefore no cost of guarding against it is too great. I distrust it because it proves too much — for any effort you might spend, there is some other catastrophe, equally unexcluded by anything anyone knows, that the very same argument would have you spend it on, and the safeguards are not independent, since hardening a system against one failure mode routinely leaves it more exposed to another. Your wager looks to me like that argument in period costume. There are more gods than yours that no argument has ruled out, and the ones that reward their own adherents tend to punish everyone else's; so either your reasoning tells me nothing at all, or it tells me to wager on all of them at once, which cannot be done. And in any case, believing a proposition is not something I can switch on by deciding to.
