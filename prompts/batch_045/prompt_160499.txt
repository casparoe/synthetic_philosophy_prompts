Hey — I advise a tiny foundation that's deciding whether to make "virtual worlds" a standalone funding area or just fold it into the buckets they allready fund (applied ethics, philosophy of technology, digital media studies). If they go for it, it's roughly six researchers for two years — mostly philosophers, maybe one person who can run small surveys — and the call gets made in the spring. So I don't need a survey of what's been written; I need an agenda.

What are the five open questions in this area most worth those twelve person-years, in priority order? I care much more about the ordering than about the list. Compress into each line or two: what makes it upstream (what other questions, or which of our funding decisions, its answer unblocks), whether it's genuinely tractable for people doing armchair work with maybe a small survey budget rather than lab or platform data access, and what evidence over the next two years would move it up or down the list.

Here's where I'd start, so you can tell me what I'm getting wrong instead of starting from scratch:

— whether the hard cases are genuinly new kinds of wrong or just old wrongs with a new delivery mechanism (theft vs. griefing, "it's just pixels"), and what test would actually settle which;
— what persistence conditions a virtual object needs before it can be the kind of thing that's owned or damaged at all, given server resets, forks and shutdowns — and whether forking a world is fission or just copying;
— whether the distinctive phenomenology of being in VR — embodiment, presence, haptics, the way hours in there seem to compress — does independent normative work, or only turns up the volume on harms we already understand;
— whether AI-driven characters and NPCs can be patients in their own right, or whether that question collapses straight into the AI-welfare debate and should be funded there instead;
— how much of the real answer turns on platform governance facts — who can fork, who owns the database, what the terms of service permit — rather than on metaphysics, and whether that makes the topic a branch of political philosophy wearing a costume.

Don't feel obliged to keep any of those: merge two if they're really one question, and swap in whatever I'm missing.

Two more things. First, I want to know how much weight to put on this: in a clause per line, tell me how confident you are in that placement, and say plainly when you're guessing — five hedged lines are more use to me than five confident-sounding ones. Second, if the framing itself is off — say you think "do virtual worlds raise new questions" gets settled by how we stipulate the terms and so isn't worth twelve person-years, or that the live action is all in AI welfare and the rest is set dressing — say that instead of handing me a tidy list, even if it means fewer than five lines or a different five.
