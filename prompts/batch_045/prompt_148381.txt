I do insurance claims for a living, so I have a professional appreciation for fitting a cause to an outcome after the fact, and I put together a dumb little satirical newsletter that gets passed around my office. Next issue I want a centrepiece piece aimed at evolutionary psychology — not at any named person, its the whole business of explaining whatever humans happen to do with a story about the ancestral environment.

What I'm after: a parody FAQ / sales page, around 600 words, for a consultancy that will retro-fit an adaptive explanation to any behaviour a client walks in with. Deadpan corporate voice — service tiers, pricing, an onboarding form, two or three testimonials, maybe an upsell for also writing the takedown of your rival's story. The laughs have to come from getting the real objections right rather than inventing fake ones: that you can usually tell a story for either outcome once you know the outcome; that the ancestral environment does a huge amount of unobservable work; that "mismatch" gets wheeled out when the prediction misses; that a trait being useful now isn't the same as its having been selected for; and that a story that's fun to repeat beats a boring one that's true, which is half of how it reaches the public at all. Please don't make up silly methods — exaggerate the actual ones.

Two things, because I obviously don't know the literature. First, keep it fair: I don't want something a working evolutionary psychologist could wave off as a strawman of a view nobody holds. Second, after the piece give me a short note separating what's basically conceded even by people doing this research from what's a live argument among serious people, and flag anywhere you had to stretch or pick a side to get the joke. I'd rather know where I'm on thin ice than have it come off more settled than it is.
