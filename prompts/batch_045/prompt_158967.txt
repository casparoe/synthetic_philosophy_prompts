I've more or less signed up to the block universe purely on the strength of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument — pick any event you like, some observer somewhere is simultaneous with it, so if that's the only notion of "real" on the table, everything is real. I dont find the standard presentist replies very convincing (the hand-waved-in privileged foliation, "real" is indexical the way "here" is), and I'm writing this up as a chapter, so I'd like someone to actually try to break it: what would count against the view, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and flagged for which of them a block-universe person would grant as counting against them as opposed to just what critics say.

Three things I keep circling on: (1) "simultaneous in some frame" isn't transitive, so the argument seems to need a premise the physics doesnt obviously hand it, and otherwise all I get is real-for-a-frame — a per-frame block instead of one big one — is that fatal or just a downgrade; (2) it's suspiciously cheap, it goes through with tiny relative velocities as long as the event is far enough away, and I cant tell whether that's a feature or a sign the conclusion is doing the work; (3) the version I actually find most persuasive is a different one — after a round trip involving real acceleration, events that were in my own future are in my past, so the argument bites without any Andromeda-scale distances — but I'm not sure whether that version is cleaner or just smuggles in more.

Also be honest about how much empirical exposure the view has: would a dynamically preferred slicing (Lorentz violation, or a quantum-gravity story that hands you a distinguished foliation) defeat the argument or only a premise, is there anything about collapse or the measurement problem that gives you a privileged now, and if it turns out the whole thing is a thesis about what "real" means rather then about physics, is that insulation a problem — and what, if anything, does the view predict that a growing-block or presentist rival doesnt?
