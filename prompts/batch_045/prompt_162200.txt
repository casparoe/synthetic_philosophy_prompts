ok so I'm writing up my position on process vs outcome supervision for an internal doc and I want someone to poke at it beofre I circulate it. Roughly where I've landed:

(1) Step-level correctness is derivative. A reasoning step is "good" only in virtue of it's tendency to lead to true answers; there's no freestanding notion of a good step we could hand-label independently of outcomes.

(2) Process supervision is what we need once the model is better than we are at the task. On long proofs, or anything where our grader is weaker than the model, the outcome signal is either unavailable or trivially hackable, so step-level signal is the only thing left.

(3) I don't want to optimize the model's stated chain of thought. Any training pressure on the text of the reasoning turns it into a performance aimed at the grader rather than a report of whatever actually produced the answer, so I want to score the final answer and leave the prose alone.

(4) Reward hacking is my main worry and it gets worse as the model gets stronger, so I'm suspicious of any signal the model can make look good without being good.

(5) I'm fine with noisy step labels from a weaker judge. I'd rather have a weak process signal than no signal, and I don't think judge disagreement is a big deal.

(6) I want the chain of thought to be a faithful report of the model's actual reasoning, because that's basically the only handle we have for noticing deception.

The scheme I've actually been running, in case it matters: we only apply the step reward on steps the model itself flags as load-bearing (I call them checkpoint steps) and leave the rest of the chain unsupervised, which keeps the labelling cheap and, I thought, keeps us from rewarding style.
