The thesis, so you have something to aim at: the indirect view is right — 'S ought to φ' is made true by facts about what an idealized deliberative counterpart of S would settle on — but every version on the market idealizes globally (evidence, ends, reasoning power, all at once) and so gets estranged from S's actual question. My fix is anchored idealization: you only idealize S along the dimensions S herself would, on reflection, treat as defects to be corrected, and then you take a fixed point of that partial idealization — idealizing the result again gets you the result back. Where there are several fixed points I say 'ought' is indeterminate rather than overdetermined; where there's none, there's no fact about what she objectively ought to do, only about what she subjectively ought to do. Two problems I flag myself: the procedure isn't commutative (idealize along D1 then D2 and you can land somewhere different from D2 then D1 — I lean on fixed points as the order-invariant outputs), and the old fetishism / one-thought-too-many worry, which I answer by saying the counterpart's output has the shape 'φ because p', so the agent takes up p directly and the idealization is transparent equipment for finding reasons, not a surrogate reason.
