I host a small podcast on free will, and next week's guest is Dr. Mara Ilves, a neuroscientist-philosopher who defends what she calls the endorsement model. The idea: conscious will never initiates anything, its job is ratification or withholding, i.e. the Libet-style veto. But unlike Libet she insists the veto is genuinely causally efficacious rather than a fresh mystery. On her account the readiness potential is a leaky accumulator drifting toward a release threshold, and consciously feeling the urge is what raises that threshold — which is why she says vetoed trials show a larger RP amplitude, not a smaller one, and why vetoing late feels effortful. Her newest, still-unpublished result is what she treats as load-bearing: in 40 subjects, back-averaging from the moment the movement would have occurred, they found no pre-SMA precursor at all on trials where people vetoed.
