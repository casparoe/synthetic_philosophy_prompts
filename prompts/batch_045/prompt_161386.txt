I'm writing a novel built around a single scene: a deliberative assembly of roughly thirty people must settle a question of fact, and the moral weight of the book rests on the claim that the group, after talking it over, arrives at the truth where any one member alone would not. To keep myself honest I've written out, as explicitly as I can, the view of collective judgment the scene presupposes. It comes to five commitments:

(1) The Condorcet jury theorem is the right model here: voters who are each a little better than chance, judging independently, produce a majority whose reliability approaches certainty as the group grows, and that is what entitles me to treat my assembly as wise.

(2) Actual votes are never independent — shared evidence, a single trusted informant, deference to whoever spoke first — so independence is an idealization my characters cannot meet, and I take that to explain why real assemblies disappoint even when their members are individually competent.

(3) What makes the assembly smart is the deliberation itself, and the mechanism is convergence: discussion moves the members onto the same, better body of evidence, and the agreement that results is the point of the scene rather than a side effect of it.

(4) Where I can identify some members as better judges than others, their votes should count for more; I hold no principled objection to weighting by demonstrated competence.

(5) One member, one vote is what equal respect among the members requires, and I don't want to surrender it — the scene needs the least experienced members' votes to count in full.

Before I commit to this in prose I'd like an audit rather than a tutorial. Which of these genuinely pull against each other, and which only appear to? I'm least sure about (2): I've been assuming it is the main threat to (1), but I want to know whether I've stated the relevant condition too crudely and whether some forms of dependence are harmless while others are fatal. Rank the real tensions by severity, say for each what would have to be given up and what that costs me as a novelist, and then name the single revision that cleans up the most. Please don't simply discard whichever commitment looks most expendable — tell me which one is actually load-bearing.
