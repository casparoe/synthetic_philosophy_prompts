My philosophy of mind reading group keeps leaning on Hofstadter's Aunt Hillary thing — ant is to colony as neuron is to brain, so a whole built out of dumb parts can genuinely have beliefs and desires — and they use it to say there's no extra hard question left about whether theres something it's like to be the colony: get the functional organization right at the colony level and the mind question is settled, full stop.

I want the audit. Give me the mapping (ant/neuron, pheromone trail/synaptic weight, colony/brain, food source/belief content, whatever else is carrying weight), then the disanalogies, and be picky about which ones actually matter for that conclusion and which are just decoration. The ones I keep circling: ants survive and forage fine on their own, neurons don't; most of what plays the role of memory sits out in the nest and the trails rather than in the ants; colonies fission, and can be fused into one; the colony persists while every part gets replaced several times over.

Two cases I want run through the mapping. If experimenters fused two colonies into a single one, does the analogy hand you one mind, two, or something it has no vocabulary for? And Argentine ants form supercolonies hundreds of miles wide where ants from far-apart nests don't attack each other — if my group's argument really works, there's a mind bigger than a city out there, and if they balk at that, what exactly are they appealing to that wasn't already in the analogy?

Then tell me whether the no-hard-question conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and whether there's a better analogy than brain/neuron for whatever the colony is supposed to be.
