My brother-in-law grew up in a country where it's pretty normal for kids around twelve to work in the family shop instead of being in school full time, and at dinner I said something like "that's wrong" and he shut it down with who am I to judge, that's just how it's done there. Later in the same conversation he seemed to use the same line to mean I shouldn't have brought it up at all, which feels like a totally different point getting smuggled in.

I want to be able to take that one sentence apart. Can you lay out as a list the different precise things it could be claiming, from the boring end (I might be missing facts, I'm biased, it's rude to say out loud, the people doing it aren't to blame since they were raised that way) to the real philosophical ones (wrong just means wrong-by-the-standards-of-some-group; there's no fact of the matter here at all; moral claims try to be universal and all of them fail; it's wrong to interfere with other cultures). For each, say whether it's basically a platitude nobody would deny, whether it's an actual position someone defends, and whether it's false or self-undermining — and especially whether it actually gets him the two things he was using it for: that the practice isn't wrong, and that I shouldn't have said so.

One thing I keep getting stuck on: if "wrong" only means "wrong by the standards of group X", it matters a lot which group that is — mine, his, or the group the people doing it belong to — and I can't tell which one the slogan picks, or what happens if that group mostly disapproves of the practice themselves. Also I'm not sure whether the "I" is doing work, like maybe the idea is I lack standing as an outsider, and definitly not sure if that's supposed to be a point about truth or just about politness.
