The question I set (20 marks total).

Presentism is the view that only present entities exist. Eternalism is the view that entities at all times — past, present and future — exist equally. We have discussed two objections to presentism: the grounding objection, roughly that if nothing past exists then there is nothing for truths about the past to be made true by, so that a presentist must find present truthmakers, reject the truthmaker principle, or treat past-tensed truths as brute; and the relativity objection, roughly that the relativity of simultaneity appears to leave no room for a unique, objective present.

Call the following position archival presentism: only present entities exist, and every true proposition about the past is made true by the intrinsic, non-relational features of presently existing things — call these archival features: the shape of a fossil, the microstructure of a memory trace, tree rings, the position of a geological stratum — together with the laws of nature.

(a) [7 marks] State the grounding objection in your own words, and explain why it is a problem for presentism in particular. Then assess whether archival presentism answers it. Be careful to distinguish singular propositions about particular past individuals (for example, that Winston Churchill smoked cigars) from general or qualitative ones.

(b) [6 marks] Is archival presentism genuinely a form of presentism, or does it collapse into the growing block theory (the past and present exist, the future does not) or into eternalism? Argue for your answer.

(c) [7 marks] How much work does the relativity of simultaneity really do in this debate? State the strongest version of the relativity objection and the strongest presentist reply, and say which you think succeeds. Throughout, keep separate the question of which times and entities exist from the questions whether temporal passage is objective and whether the distinctions past/present/future are objective (the A-theory/B-theory contrast): presentism is not to be identified with the A-theory, nor eternalism with the B-theory, and four-dimensionalism about persisting objects is a further thesis again.

The student's answer.

Presentism is the view that only the present is real: only presently existing objects exist, and the past and the future are not real at all. Eternalism is the view that past, present and future objects and times are all equally real. So presentism is the A-theory of time, the view that there is an objective passage or flow and an objective present, while eternalism is the B-theory, the view that there is no passage and no privileged present; and eternalism is in effect the same view as four-dimensionalism, because if future objects exist then a persisting object must have temporal parts.

The grounding objection says that if there are no past objects then there is nothing to make our claims about the past true, and if we grant that truths need truthmakers this is serious trouble: 'Churchill smoked cigars' would be true, yet there is no Churchill and no cigar-smoking for it to be about. It is a problem specifically for presentism because the eternalist and the growing block theorist can simply point to the past objects and events themselves, whereas the presentist has to make do with whatever exists now. Archival presentism is meant to solve this, since the truthmakers for past-tensed claims would then be present archival features, so there would be present truthmakers after all. I think this solves the grounding objection completely. The archival features in my memory trace or in the fossil record are traces of those very past events and objects: they stand in the right causal and historical relations to Churchill's cigar-smokings. So the present really does contain traces of the past, and the fact that the traces are present while the objects they are traces of are not is simply what presentism always claimed.

But this shows that archival presentism is not really a form of presentism at all. If the past is preserved in the present in the form of traces, then the past is not gone but stored, and a stored past is an existing past. That is just the growing block theory with the block redescribed in terms of traces, and the claim distinctive of presentism — that only the present exists — has been quietly given up. Archival presentism therefore collapses into the growing block view, and if it also admitted future-directed traces it would collapse into eternalism.

Finally, the relativity objection settles the whole dispute. Special relativity shows that simultaneity is frame-relative: whether two distant events are simultaneous depends on the frame of reference, so there is no absolute present, and without an absolute present presentism cannot even be stated coherently. Some presentists reply by postulating a privileged foliation of spacetime, but that is simply helping oneself to the very thing physics has ruled out, which is not a move a philosopher is entitled to make. So presentism is false and eternalism is true.
