I'm a software engineer — roughly twelve years, mostly backend and distributed systems — with no formal philosophy background, and I've ended up responsible for writing up a disagreement that started in a reading group three sessions ago and has not moved since. What I want from you is the document we should have produced at the start: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration.

The topic is the procreation asymmetry: the pair of claims that (i) there is no moral requirement to bring into existence a person whose life would, on the whole, be good, and (ii) there is a moral requirement not to bring into existence a person whose life would, on the whole, be bad. (I gather this is the sort of view associated with Narveson and Benatar, but I don't care about attribution. Please give me no citations, no bibliography, and no reading list — I have no library access and no way to check them, and they would be useless to me.)

The two camps are:

Camp A, the unified or axiological camp: the two halves are a single datum, and the correct explanation is one axiological asymmetry about absences — roughly, that the absence of pain is good even when there is no one whose absence of pain it is, while the absence of pleasure is not bad when there is no one who is thereby deprived of it.

Camp B, the two-grounds or deontic camp: the two halves are not one datum. The no-duty-to-create half follows from there being no one who could be wronged or benefited by not being created, together with the optionality of creating; the duty-not-to-create half follows from the wrongness of imposing a bad life on someone who will then exist. The asymmetry is a conjunction of two claims with different grounds, and the axiological formulation is at best a redescription of that conjunction rather than an explanation of it.

Note that both camps accept the asymmetry in the clear cases. The residual disagreement is about its ground, and therefore about what happens outside the clear cases. That is where I want the document to live.
