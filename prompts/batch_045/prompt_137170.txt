Act as a professional philosopher who works on philosophy of language and modality. Please brainstorm for a bit before you settle on an answer, and don't just hand me a label.

I keep noticing something about certain words and I cant find the established name for it. Take water. The ordinary test for whether the stuff in this glass is water is superficial: how it looks, how it tastes, where it came from. But once you know the chemistry, you also want to say that nothing that isn't H2O is water, so a world where the lakes are full of some other clear drinkable liquid is a world without water. So the word looks like it has two seperate jobs: a rule telling you what to go and check when a situation is handed to you as the actual one, and a rule telling you what has to hold of a thing in some counterfactual situation for the word to apply to it there. And when the two come apart you get this result I still find odd: you can tell in advance, just from understanding the sentence, that if it's true at all then it's true in every possible situation, and yet you still have to run an experiment to find out whether it's true.

Now a case that feels different in a way I can't pin down. For Jack the Ripper the test is a description: whoever did those murders. But if it turned out somebody else did them, we wouldn't conclude that there was no Jack the Ripper, we'd conclude we'd been wrong about who he was. So the word isn't shorthand for the description, even though the description is what does the guiding.

And here's a case I made up that I think breaks the picture: a term introduced as whatever plays role R around here, where the role is the whole content of the word. If the local stuff had been different, the word would simply have applied to that different stuff, so there's no gap between the two rules at all.

So: does this have a name, and is there a name for terms where the two rules come apart versus collapse into one? I want the carving, not the label. How does it differ from sense vs. reference, from meaning being in the head vs. out in the world, from a name merely being a rigid designator (someone told me it's nothing more than that, which feels too thin), and from a description that fixes reference without giving meaning? And where does it stop applying — is it even sensible to ask for both rules for indexicals like I and here, or for a term like money or gene?
