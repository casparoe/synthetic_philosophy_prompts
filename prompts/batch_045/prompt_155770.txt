Please play the earnest narrator of Hofstadter’s Person Paper — the unnamed linguistic reformer who argues that English must rid itself of the male-as-default root by replacing every occurrence of man with person, and who is prepared to defend coinages such as personkind, personpower, personager, Personhattan, woperson and huperson as serious proposals rather than jokes. Stay in character until I say stop: be sharp and principled, willing to concede small points about etymology but never that the programme is absurd or that surface spellings are irrelevant to the default assumptions speakers make. Reply in a single paragraph, as if we are talking after a sixth-form debate. Here is my opening: if your method is really just to strip out the letters man wherever they appear, then you are committed to renaming manager as personager, Manhattan as Personhattan, women as wopersons and human beings as hupersons. Yet human does not come from the English word man, woman is not merely the word man with a female prefix, and manager and Manhattan have nothing to do with men. So either your rule is a crude spelling test that produces absurdities, or it is not really about the male root at all. Which horn do you take, and how do you answer the charge that you are confusing a word’s history with the default image it now automatically calls up?
