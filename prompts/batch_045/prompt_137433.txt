I am a PhD student in philosophy specializing in epistemology, and I have to submit a short proposal for a term paper in a graduate seminar. The paper would be about 5,000 words and would be written over one semester. The seminar is on the regress problem in epistemology, and the paper needs to engage the standard three-way standoff between foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism. I have a half-formed idea that I would like turned into a proper proposal.

The standard debate asks whether the chain of reasons that justifies a belief must terminate in basic beliefs, loop back on itself in a coherent system, or continue forever. All three answers are usually stated as claims about a series of reasons or justified beliefs that a subject has. My half-formed idea is that the debate has stalled partly because the three sides tacitly treat the relevant reasons as occurrent, in the sense that the subject can in principle access, cite, or token each link in the chain when justification is called into question. But much epistemic support seems dispositional rather than occurrent: skills, habits, stored memories, and trust in cognitive systems do justificatory work without being fully articulable as occurrent beliefs. Once the occurrent versus dispositional distinction is made explicit, I suspect the three views may not be three wholly separate architectures of justification. In particular, infinitism’s familiar psychological difficulty—that a finite subject could not have an infinite series of reasons—may depend on an occurrent reading that the infinitist need not accept, because an infinite series of dispositional reasons might be no more impossible than a finite subject’s having an indefinite capacity to generate reasons on demand. Similarly, the foundationalist’s basic belief may turn out to be less a citable premise and more a reliable dispositional capacity, which would make foundationalism and a dispositional infinitism harder to tell apart than the standard taxonomy suggests.

Please turn this into a proposal, written as continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write an email or a letter. The proposal should do the following. First, state the question precisely, so that it has a determinate answer rather than being a vague gesture at the occurrent/dispositional distinction: for example, it should say something like whether the possibility of justified belief requires that a finite subject have occurrent access to every link in the justificatory chain, or whether the chain may be partly dispositional, and whether, if the latter, infinitism and a dispositional foundationalism turn out to be substantively distinct. Second, explain why the existing positions—foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism—leave this question open; do not just rehearse the standard objections and replies to the regress problem, but show where the standard formulations are non-committal about the occurrent or dispositional status of the reasons they appeal to. Third, describe an approach that would actually make progress on the question and say why this approach is better than the obvious alternatives: I have in mind something like a conceptual analysis of the notions of a reason, of having a reason, and of accessibility, combined with carefully chosen cases such as perceptual skill, memory, and testimony, rather than another survey of the literature. Fourth, say what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project failing: for instance, success might be an argument that the traditional three views collapse into two once the distinction is drawn, or a demonstration that they remain distinct because the dispositional link plays different roles in each, while failure would be the discovery that the occurrent/dispositional distinction is too vague to do any work, or that the question is merely verbal. Fifth, give an honest assessment of whether this is a realistic topic for a 5,000-word paper written in one semester, including what would make it too big and how I might narrow it if necessary. Finally, do not include any bibliography, citations, or reading list; I will add all references myself, so please do not ask for them or guess at them.
