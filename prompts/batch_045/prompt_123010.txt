I want the specification before the theories. I am working on the norms governing assertion — whether the bar for permissibly stating something is knowledge, truth, reasonable belief, or something else — and before I weigh the candidates I want it fixed what such a norm is supposed to be for. Fix the purpose as follows: the norm should be able to do work elsewhere, underwriting both an account of what a hearer acquires when they are told something and an account of what institutions owe the public when they issue statements.

Write the desideratum list on that understanding: the data any account has to accommodate, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the structural features it must have, each with the case or consideration that makes it a requirement. The distinctions I care about include sincerity versus warrant, the act's being defective versus the speaker's being blameworthy, and a norm constitutive of assertion versus one that merely regulates an act we could perform without it. Then sort the requirements into non-negotiable, negotiable, and mere prejudice of one camp — demands such as that the norm be a single unitary rule, or that it be formulable in terms of knowledge, strike me as things no theory should have to grant.

Among the cases I want covered: hedged versus unhedged assertion; the infelicity of stating something while disavowing knowledge of it; assertions whose stakes are low for the speaker and the immediate audience but high for someone further downstream who will act on them; assertion addressed to no one who can take it up; assertion by a collective that has no single mind; and assertion of moral, aesthetic, or legal contents where truth-aptness is itself contested.

Finally, say which pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and which single requirement does the most to exclude otherwise attractive candidates. Touch on the familiar norms only to mark where each fails a requirement; I will do the scoring myself.
