hey — I need a piece of satire, and I want the genre to be very specific: an accreditation standard. You know those ISO-ish compliance manuals an office writes for itself, the ones with numbered clauses, "the Organization shall maintain a documented procedure for…", an appendix with a worked case study, and a footer showing the document version. That thing.

The fictional author is the internal standards body of a government agency that exists to run interrogations in the one case everyone agrees is hard: the ticking bomb. Give it a bland name, something like the Office for Preemptive Necessity or the Extreme Interrogation Compliance Board. Around 800 words, deadpan throughout, no winking at the reader.

What I want skewered is not "torture is bad," it's the specific move where the hypothetical stops being a one-off permission and becomes a design brief: because the case might arise, you need a standing unit, with recruiters, training pipelines, medical supervisors, probability thresholds, and pre-printed forms, all funded and staffed years before any bomb exists. The clauses should be genuinely well-engineered — these people are competent and sincere and believe that adding a form has solved the moral problem.

A few things I want in it, because the jokes should come from real features of the argument:

- every stipulation that makes the hypothetical work (that there is a bomb, that this person knows, that the info is gettable, that it works, that it stays "clean") is satisfied by stipulation, and the standard's job is to turn stipulation into paperwork. My favourite version of this: a certificate that the Bureau knows, that it knows that it knows, and that the detainee knows that it knows that the detainee knows — a cascade that the form cuts off at three levels because there's only room for three signatures. Please use that.
- a clause extending "ticking" to things that tick only metaphorically — a slow-moving threat, a statistical one — let the exception quietly eat the rule.
- an after-action review form with no box for "would it have been found anyway."
- a closing budget line implying the unit has never actually had a qualifying case.

Nobody named or real, no citations, and it should still land for someone who's never read a word of philosophy.
