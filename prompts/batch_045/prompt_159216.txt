I'm giving a paper to our department's logic reading group next week and I need Q&A prep, badly. Short version of the thesis: the regress in Lewis Carroll's 1895 Mind dialogue is usually answered by saying you don't assert modus ponens, you use it — rules aren't premises, end of story. I think that answer is too cheap. The tortoise isn't missing a premise; he's quietly exploiting the fact that any finite list of explicitly granted rules is compatible with deviant interpretations of the connectives, so granting a rule never pins down which rule you've granted, and the standard reply just restates the problem at a higher level of generality. My positive proposal, which is the part I actually want to defend, is that the regress terminates not in a proposition but in a prior: call it the simplicity reply. An inference rule is a prior over interpretations of the logical constants; priors aren't assertibles, so there's nothing for the tortoise to ask you to write in his notebook; and the deviant interpretations he needs are genuinely available but lose badly on prior probability. Deduction gets a non-deductive, broadly Bayesian justification, with no final premise to append.

The room matters a lot here, so: two people who work in proof-theoretic semantics / inferentialism and will say the demand for a justification of a basic rule is a category error because the rules are constitutive of the meanings (one of them always reaches for Prior's tonk to show constitutive rules can't be arbitrary); one formal epistemologist who does Bayesian epistemology properly and will want to know what space my prior even ranges over and whether logical omniscience means I can't have uncertainty about interpretations of my own connectives in the first place; one Kripke/Wittgenstein person who will ask whether I've given a straight solution or a skeptical solution and whether I've noticed which one I've given; and a handful of grad students who read the normativity-of-logic literature and will press on why one ought to have that prior, i.e. whether I've just relocated the normativity one floor up rather than dissolved it.
