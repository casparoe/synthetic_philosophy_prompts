In the standard two-player centipede game, backward induction entitles the first player to stop immediately, and discussion of the case tends to treat that conclusion either as a reductio of backward induction or as a reductio of the intuition that it can be rational to continue. I do not want another verdict on the original game. I want the specification that a solution concept for finite perfect-information games would have to meet before any such verdict carries much weight.

Write the desiderata. For each one, give the case or consideration that motivates it, mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference peculiar to one tradition, and flag any subset of the desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable — that last part is what I most want to see. Territory I want covered, which you should reorganize as you think best:

- the distinction between what it is optimal to do at a node given that one has arrived there and what one would have wanted, ex ante, to bind oneself to; and whether an adequate theory has to make these coincide
- whether confidence in an opponent's rationality is a global premise or a local one that the evidence of having reached a late node can erode; and whether a mechanism that occasionally implements the wrong move is evidence of the same kind as a small chance that the opponent is irrational
- what a theory owes us at nodes that its own advice renders unreachable, and whether it must say anything at all there
- the variants I can generate, which I would like the list tested against: a game whose number of nodes the players do not know; one in which the payoff to stopping is randomized rather than fixed; one played against a committee that decides by majority vote; one played against one's own earlier self, whose reasoning has since been erased; one in which both players are first told how a previous pair played
- whether matching the way people actually play these games is a genuine desideratum, a category mistake, or something a theory should explain rather than predict

Then tell me which single desideratum does the most work in excluding candidate theories, whether anything on offer satisfies the list taken as a whole, and give a one-paragraph scorecard for the two or three main families of response.
