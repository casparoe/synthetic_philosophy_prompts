my friend and i spent like two hours on this last night and i went to bed annoyed because i think i built an argument that proves way too much and i cant find the broken step.

his view is tranquilism: the only thing that matters for how well a life goes is the absence of craving and suffering. everything else — knowledge, love, achievement, even pleasure — matters only as a cause of that. here's what i wrote down after he left:

(1) Only the absence of craving and suffering matters in itself; nothing else does.
(2) Craving is a desire, and suffering is roughly an unfulfilled aversion — pain you want stopped, or the felt frustration of a desire.
(3) Being worried about whether you'll suffer tomorrow just is having a present craving about the future; by (1) a present craving is itself something bad; so caring about your own future suffering makes you worse off right now.
(4) So the fully tranquil person has no preference between "a sharp twinge tomorrow, then perfect calm" and "no twinge tomorrow" — and by (3) whoever does prefer the second one is, at this moment, in the worse state of the two.
(5) Now imagine a drug — call it Stillwater — that leaves every pain signal completely intact and deletes only the wanting-it-to-stop. By (1)+(2), a life of torture-plus-Stillwater contains nothing bad at all, and by (4) preferring to skip it is itself the defect. (for the record: i think there's a real neurological condition roughly like this, people report the pain but not the suffering, so please dont answer that the drug is impossible.)
(6) And by (3) again, wanting to become tranquil is itself a craving, so you can't coherently pursue the state the theory calls good; and once you have it there's nothing you want, including more of it.
(7) A theory of welfare on which caring about your own future welfare is a defect and on which you can't coherently pursue what it calls good gives nobody any reason to bring about what it calls good.
(8) So tranquilism is either false or practically empty.

he says (1) survives fine and all i've shown is that tranquility is a weird state. i think something slid — maybe "craving" is doing different work in (2) and (3)? — but every fix i try either turns him into a plain hedonist or collapses the whole thing. which step do you reject, why did it look fine, and what does rejecting it cost? if i have to pick, id rather keep something like (1) than give up the thought that caring about your own future is okay — but if the honest answer is that tranquilism has no room for prudence at all, just say that.
