Here's the setup. A colleague of mine — call her Dana — has become the department's unofficial oracle on self-locating evidence: whenever a case involving duplication, observer counts, or birth rank comes up, people ask Dana. Over about four years I've quietly written down her verdicts, and they don't look like any single textbook package to me. (1) Standard Sleeping Beauty: 1/3. (2) The same setup but with the duplication done simultaneously — two subjects created at once in qualitatively identical rooms rather than one subject awakened twice with amnesia — also 1/3; she says the temporal/simultaneous difference makes no difference whatsoever. (3) Two-batch creation: a fair coin; Heads, ten observers come into existence, numbered 1–10; Tails, a thousand and ten come into existence, numbered 1–1010; everyone learns their own serial number and nothing else; you learn you are number 3. She says this favors Heads by roughly a hundred to one. (4) The presumptuous philosopher case — two cosmological theories that fit all the ordinary evidence equally well but disagree enormously about how many observers the universe contains — she rejects the inference to the many-observers theory, and she adds that if that style of reasoning had any force at all it would push the other way, toward the fewer-observers theory, which she also thinks is absurd; so she's suspicious of the whole population-scale family of arguments. (5) The doomsday argument as applied to actual human history: she calls it unsound, but only because of the reference class problem — we don't know who counts as an observer or where the boundaries of the class are, so the numbers are noise. She's perfectly happy with the abstract version in (3).

My own guesses keep failing and its starting to annoy me. I assumed that anyone who thirds in Sleeping Beauty must be running something self-indication-flavored, but that style of view seems to endorse the presumptuous philosopher, which she rejects, and it also seems to give no update at all in (3), where she gives a large one. The only thing I can get to fit is sampling applied at the level of observer-moments with a fairly broad reference class — but then I don't see how she can reject the doomsday argument in principle while accepting (3), and I don't see why her answer in (1) wouldn't shift if the world contained vastly more unrelated observer-moments.

So here is the new case I want to put to her at our next meeting, and I want to know in advance what she'll say. It's Sleeping Beauty again, with two stipulations I can vary independently. Does her credence in Heads differ between the variants, and if so which way? Second, the wallpaper variant: under Tails the two awakenings happen in rooms with different wallpaper, striped and polka-dot, Beauty sees which one she is in, and wallpaper colour is stipulated to be independent of the coin.
