I'm writing a short position paper on choice under risk and want an audit of the package before I commit to it in print. Here, roughly, is where I stand.

(1) I have the Allais preferences. In the usual textbook pair I take the sure million over the 89/10/1 gamble, and I take the 10 percent shot at five million over the 11 percent shot at one million. I have never been moved by the arguments that this pattern is irrational.

(2) So I reject the independence axiom as a requirement of rationality. My reason is that not every change in probability is a change of the same kind: giving up certainty — moving an outcome's chance from 1 to 0.89 — is not the same thing as moving it from 0.11 to 0, even though the arithmetic difference is identical, and I don't see why a rational person must treat the two as equivalent.

(3) I reject the money-pump and sequential-choice arguments against (1). Whether the choice I actually face is rational cannot depend on what I would choose in later choices that I will never be asked to make.

(4) I want the belief–desire distinction to keep doing real work. Credences should obey the probability calculus and answer to evidence alone, and I would like it to remain possible to read someone's beliefs and their tastes off their choices separately.

(5) Preferences must respect stochastic dominance; an option that pays at least as much in every state and more in some cannot be worse.

(6) It is irrational to care whether a chance is resolved in one stage or two — a compound lottery and its actuarially equivalent one-stage reduction are the same option.

(7) In impersonal contexts — a risk assessment, a policy memo — the right procedure is to multiply: a one-in-ten-thousand chance of ten thousand deaths is exactly as bad as one certain death, and I would not apply any certainty weighting there.

I am fairly confident of each of these taken alone and much less confident that they are jointly tenable. Please tell me which pairs genuinely collide and which only look as though they do, rank the real collisions by severity, and for each say what would have to be given up, what the cheapest revision is, and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the package.
