Hey, hoping someone here actually knows this stuff rather than just repeating retreat talk.

I've been sitting a while and doing that "look for the one who is looking" thing, and I hit this weird regress: every time I find something that seems like the witness, I can also observe *it*, so it turns out to be just another object. I ran that maybe a dozen times and the conclusion I settled on was: the witness isn't any of the objects — fine — but then where's the seperation between my witnessing and yours? Nothing I found had a name tag on it. So I've landed on what I've started calling the one-flame view, after an image I had of a row of lamps lit off a single flame: there's no self *in* anyone, but there's also nothing that makes awareness mine rather than yours, so really there's just awareness, and anatta is the teaching that nobody owns it.

What pushed me further was the undeclared questions. The Buddha apparently refused to answer whether the self is the same thing as the body, and I read that as: the answer is unstatable but it's the big one, and since he also taught that all dhammas are not-self, the unstatable answer has to be that the self is *everything* — not a personal soul, a cosmic one. There's a line I half remember where he lists views like "the self and the world are the same" as things other wanderers hold, but listing it doesn't prove he thought it was wrong, does it?

Anyway, now I can't make the mechanics work, and that's my question. If there's one awareness, why isn't one person's awakening everybody's? Why does karma seem to land on the person who did the thing instead of being spread across all of us, and why do the texts talk about streams in the plural? I keep going back and forth: maybe my karma just means karma that's psychologically available to this body, the way a headache is mine without anyone owning it. Or maybe the Buddha's actual target is only the possessive relation — my feelings, my body — and not the existence of a subject at all, and the one-flame thing is simply what's left over once you drop ownership.

Two things I'd rather you not do. Don't just tell me this is the cosmic-self teaching with extra steps: my flame isn't permanent or blissful or luminous, it's just unowned, so I don't see that it fits. And don't just say he declined to answer and leave it there — I want to know *why* he declined, if declining was maybe not about the answer being too deep but about the question.

So which premise is the load-bearing wrong one — the move from "no self found among the aggregates" to "one self behind them all," or the unowned-therefore-undivided step, or what I'm doing with the silence? If the questions are declined because they're badly formed, what exactly is badly formed about asking whether there is one awareness or many? And if you can, say what an arhat's compassion is supposed to even be on the correct version of all this, since that was half of what pushed me toward the one-flame thing in the first place.
