hi — practical one, and I realise up front it might be the wrong question.

Context: I'm one of four engineers on a team that ships a generative image/video tool. Legal have handed us a requirement: the tool may produce "artistic" nudity but not sexual content, and we need (a) a classifier that routes requests and (b) a one-page policy a user can appeal against. Legal won't say what "artistic" means — they've said, cheerfully, that's not their job. So it's landed on us. We've got about six weeks, of which realistically maybe two person-weeks of actual thinking time before we just have to pick something and build it.

I did some wikipedia-level reading over the weekend. It looks like philosophers have been arguing about what art is for decades and largely concluded it's a mess — cluster concepts, the "artworld" thing, family resemblance, that sort of stuff. I can't go read the literature; I've got a day job and no library access, so please don't give me a reading list or named theories to look up. What I want is triage.

My instinct, which might be badly wrong: this is an API design problem, not a metaphysics problem. We don't need the correct definition, we need one that agrees with our three-person review panel on most of a held-out set of real cases, can be written down on one page, and won't embarrass us when a journalist finds an edge case. I also suspect a big chunk of the literature is secretly about whether something is *good* art, which we don't care about at all.

So: given that, which of the things people actually fight about in this area should we spend our two weeks on, and which can we just stipulate and move on? Concretely:

- Is "is it art?" genuinely seperable from "is it any good?", or does anything we'd actually write down end up sneaking in a quality judgement anyway?
- Does it matter whether our rule is about the object, about the process that made it, or about the social context it lands in? Our test set has cases like: the identical audio file uploaded by a signed musician as "Untitled" and by nobody as "sound test 3"; a spreadsheet someone made, printed, framed and hung in their kitchen; a game texture no player ever saw; a four-second clip from the prompt "something that looks like a 1950s colour-field painting". Which of those are the ones that will actually decide which rule we pick, and which are noise?
- Is there a version of this where we give up on defining art and define something adjacent instead? I have a vague idea along the lines of "was anyone trying to make something to be looked at", but I can already see it falling over on the texture case.
- And what should we be watching over the next six months that would tell us we picked wrong — appeals volume, press, our own panel disagreeing with itself?

Rank them, say why, and be explicit about the criteria you're using and the trade-offs between them. I'm also interested in anything we're not even asking about that we should be. Where you're guessing, or where people in the area would genuinely disagree, say so and say how much it'd matter for us — I'd much rather have "50/50, here's what would settle it" than a confident wrong steer. And if the whole framing is a mistake — if "we just need a working definition" is dodging something that'll bite us later, or if legal's carve-out can't be made to work in principle — say that plainly rather than helping me build the wrong thing politely.
