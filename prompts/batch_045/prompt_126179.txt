I keep a harvester ant colony in a formicarium — maybe 3000 workers, one queen — and I've got into the habit of talking about her as one animal, the way the old Aunt Hillary setup does. I'm trying to work out what I actually believe when I talk like that. I care less about whether she's conscious than about whether she's got beliefs of her own: whether there's a subject there that can be wrong about where the seeds are.

Going by gut:

- the whole colony: yes. She reroutes foraging when I block the main trail, moves the nest after I put the lamp on the wrong side, and I'd feel bad about killing her in a way I don't about stepping on a single ant.
- one forager: no.
- a slime mold in a petri dish that reliably grows the shortest route between two oat flakes: no.
- a laptop simulation of the same colony-level algorithm, same pheromone bookkeeping: also no, but I can't say why without sounding like a substrate snob.
- 1500 workers with no queen, scooped into their own box, just milling: no. Tip them back in: Aunt Hillary again.
- fission: she splits into two equal halves, each with a daughter queen, both foraging and defending within a week. I want to say both are Aunt Hillary, but that feels like having it both ways — maybe it should be neither, or two new colonies.
- fusion: two rival colonies forced into one nest, a month later one shared trail network. My gut says one mind and that it's a new one, which doesn't sit well with my fission verdict.
- February, when she's dormant, no foraging, almost no signalling: still Aunt Hillary. I don't think she blinks out and a successor wakes up in spring.
- the parasite case: ants infected by a fungus that makes them clamp on at exactly the height and leaf angle the fungus needs. Trails still form, the colony still sorts out where to feed, but I feel like the real agency has partly leaked out of her and into the fungus, even though the behaviour looks the same.
- a company that acts as if it expects demand to rise: no, that's just a façon de parler.

My tentative principle is: a system is a genuine subject of belief when it's a single self-maintaining locus of goal-directed activity with a good of its own, that can fail at it and receive feedback from its own failures. But the slime mold seems to meet that and I still vote no, and the principle is silent on fission and fusion.

Can you give me a principle that gets my verdicts, or close? State it precisely, say which clause is doing the work on which case, tell me what it predicts about cases I haven't listed (all her ants replaced one percent a week by robot ants that play the same roles; or an exact duplicate of her organisation built in a second formicarium), and be straight about which of my verdicts it can't save.
