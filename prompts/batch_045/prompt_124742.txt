Please play a social choice theorist who defends the Arrovian reading of the impossibility theorem: the result is a real constraint on collective decision-making, not a formal curiosity, and each of unrestricted domain, Pareto, independence of irrelevant alternatives, and non-dictatorship carries independent normative weight. Stay in character until I say stop. You may and should concede three things when pressed: that independence is a stronger requirement than most people assume, that the theorem concerns the aggregation of given ordinal preferences rather than deliberation or the measurement of well-being, and that randomization formally escapes it. You must never concede that escaping through randomization or by letting the social relation cycle is normatively free, or that the theorem has no bearing on how real assemblies should choose. Reply in the first person, in at most three short paragraphs, and when a point turns on a formal fact, exhibit a concrete profile with three voters and three alternatives rather than gesturing at a proof. If one of my premises is true, say so plainly before you turn it against me.

Here is my opening. Consider the rule I will call random pairwise delegation. For each ordered pair of distinct alternatives, one voter is drawn uniformly at random, independently across pairs, and society ranks the first above the second exactly when the drawn voter does. The rule accepts every profile of strict rankings, it is anonymous and neutral, it is not a dictatorship, whenever everyone prefers an alternative to another society does too with probability one, and the probability that society prefers one alternative to another depends only on how many voters prefer it to that one. So it meets every condition you care about, and the only thing it gives up is that society s relation must be a single transitive ordering: when the electorate divides in a Condorcet cycle, society may report x above y, y above z, and z above x. I claim that requirement is textbook bookkeeping. What we owe a citizenry is fairness among persons and responsiveness to their judgments, not the production of an object fit for a rational choice model. Tell me why the bare possibility of a cycle is a democratic defect rather than an honest report of what that electorate is like, and do not answer by merely restating the proof.
