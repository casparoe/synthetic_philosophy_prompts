I am writing a paper defending a line I have started calling the Parity Defense against the combination problem, and I want it stress-tested before I commit to it in print.

The target position is micropsychist panpsychism: there is something it is like, at a very small scale, for at least some of the basic constituents of the physical world, and the macro-experiences we know about are what you get when micro-experiences stand in the right compositional relations. The standard objection is the combination problem, in its two usual parts: the subject-summing version, which asks how many distinct micro-subjects yield one macro-subject rather than merely a great many micro-subjects arranged subject-wise; and the quality-summing version, which asks how micro-qualities compose into the specific character of, say, seeing red, as opposed to into no macro-quality at all or into an unrecognizable one.

My defense is that neither part is a cost special to panpsychism. The first is the general metaphysical question of how there can be composite objects at all, wearing a phenomenal label: anyone who accepts that there are organisms or artifacts already accepts that some collections have a unity not visibly assembled out of the unities of their members, and I claim the panpsychist is entitled to the very same primitive, with no extra explanatory debt incurred by the fact that what composes here is experiential. The second is the hard problem of consciousness again in a new costume: every view that does not eliminate macro-experience owes some account of how it arises from what is not itself that macro-experience, and panpsychism's version of that debt looks no larger than the physicalist's, and arguably smaller, since at least the base materials are of the right general kind. On this reading the combination problem is either a cost shared with views the objector already accepts, or an artifact of demanding that composition be intelligible in a way we demand nowhere else.

I have also been working on a sharpened version, the borrowed-criterion version. Physicalist accounts of which collections compose composite objects are not metaphysically neutral: they appeal to causal integration, dynamical cohesion, informational unity, and the like. The panpsychist, I want to say, can simply borrow those same criteria for subject-composition, so that the restriction on which collections of micro-subjects compose a macro-subject is inherited from physics rather than invented for consciousness. If that works, the Parity Defense strengthens into an identity: the panpsychist is not positing a new composition principle at all, only applying the ordinary one to experiential parts.

What would count against this? I am not interested in the dialectic where a critic says that composition remains mysterious even so, since I do not think mystery is a currency that discriminates between views here. I want the defeaters that a defender of the Parity Defense should take seriously, and I want them sorted.

Specifically, please give me:

The full space of things that would count against the Parity Defense, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with the ranking justified rather than asserted. I especially want the failure modes internal to the borrowed-criterion version, since that is where I think the real exposure lies. My own worry is roughly this, and I would like it developed or dissolved: the borrowed criteria are stated in non-phenomenal terms, so either they fail to entail that there is a phenomenal subject at all, in which case borrowing them buys nothing, or they are supplemented by a bridge principle saying that collections satisfying such-and-such physical condition have a subject, in which case the bridge principle looks like a new fundamental posit that the physicalist does not need — which is just the combination problem reappearing one level down. Tell me whether that dilemma is real, whether both horns are live, and whether there is a version of the borrowed-criterion move that escapes it.

An overflow problem I keep circling: for any collection of micro-subjects that composes a macro-subject, there are vastly many overlapping, partially nested collections that are also candidates. Something must arbitrate. If arbitration is by maximal unity, then whether a given subject exists, and how many subjects there are, becomes sensitive to continuous, empirically varying matters — split-brain cases, gradual anesthesia, fluctuating integration — and the diachronic identity of the macro-subject threatens to go indeterminate in a way that the identity of a table apparently does not. Say how damaging that is, and how much of it the panpsychist can simply embrace.

Which of these a committed defender should concede as genuine evidence against the view, as opposed to which they are entitled to dismiss as simply restating the opposition. I want the concessions made from inside the view, not the objections as a critic would phrase them.

What the Parity Defense predicts or commits its holder to that its rivals do not. A position that risks nothing is worth nothing, and I would rather find out now whether mine is one. My sense is that the borrowed-criterion version at least makes panpsychism hostage to empirical facts about integration in a way that a bare it-is-primitive version does not, and that this is a genuine, checkable exposure. Say whether you agree, and what else the view is on the hook for.

Whether the Parity Defense, and especially the bare version, has been quietly qualified into unfalsifiability by the move to treating composition as primitive; and if it has, whether that is a defect for a position of this kind or an acceptable feature of a thesis in fundamental metaphysics.

Finally, one asymmetry I cannot settle. The Parity Defense leans on the thought that composition is a problem for everyone. But cosmopsychist versions of panpsychism face something more like a decombination problem: not how parts make a whole subject, but how the one big subject gets carved into the many subjects we seem to be. Physicalist metaphysics contains no obvious analogue of carving, as opposed to building, so the parity move may simply fail there. Does it? And if the Parity Defense works for micropsychism and fails for cosmopsychism, does that asymmetry itself tell against the defense, or is it just a reason to be a micropsychist?

Please answer with a definite verdict rather than a survey: which single defeater you take to be the most serious, whether on balance the Parity Defense survives it, and what you would revise if it were your paper. Where you are genuinely uncertain, say so and say what would settle it for you. I should add what would move me most: an argument that panpsychism, on its own terms, ends up committed to some distinctive and fundamental phenomenal posit that the physicalist is not — a bonding relation, a bridge principle, a restriction on composition stated experientially. Arguments of the form this is still unexplained leave me cold, since I think every view here is in that boat.
