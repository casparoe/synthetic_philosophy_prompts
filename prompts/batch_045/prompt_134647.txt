I came to philosophy late, after a career in analytical chemistry, and I am trying to set down where I have landed on vagueness for a reading group I attend. Here is the package:

(1) Vagueness is never in the world. There are no vague objects, no vague properties, and no vague facts about how many grains are on the bench. It is wholly a feature of our representations.

(2) Tolerance is simply false. The sorites reasoning is valid and its premises cannot all stand, so some single grain makes the difference between a heap and a non-heap. I am entirely untroubled by the thought that we could never find out which.

(3) Meaning is fixed by use, and the actual use of a vague predicate singles out no particular cutoff. There is nothing in what speakers do, or would do, that makes the threshold 10,000 grains rather than 9,999.

(4) Higher-order vagueness is real. For at least some cases it is itself unsettled whether they are borderline, so the proposal of treating a vague predicate as admitting many acceptable sharpenings, and counting a claim as true when it comes out true on all of them, merely relocates the difficulty, since which sharpenings are acceptable is itself an unclear matter.

(5) In a concrete case — a statute prohibiting the carrying of dangerous weapons, applied to someone with a butter knife in her pocket — there is no fact of the matter as to whether the statute applies. The honest course is to settle it on policy grounds and stop looking for a truth to discover.

(6) In scientific practice we dissolve this sort of thing by stipulation: a detection limit, a significance level, an operational definition. Once stipulated, the claims are perfectly precise, and I do not think anything of substance would be lost if every vague predicate in a serious context were replaced by a stipulated one.

I have a persistent feeling that these do not all belong to one person, but I cannot locate the fault. Please audit the set: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look as though they are, and for each real tension what the cheapest repair would be and what it costs. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equal, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I am not asking for a survey of the positions available in the literature — I want to know what I have to give up.
