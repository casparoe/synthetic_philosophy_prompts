Going straight at both means he overshoots and sleeps in the car: 1. He knows all of this while he's still at the bar, and can make a plan then.

Here's what bugs me. There seem to be two ways of evaluating a plan and they dont agree:

(i) From the bar: if he'll continue with probability p at each intersection, expected payoff is 4p(1-p) + p², which peaks at p = 2/3 and gives 4/3.

(ii) From the intersection, once he's there: he certainly visits the first intersection and only reaches the second with probability p, so conditional on being at an intersection the chance it's the first one is 1/(1+p) — i.e. 3/5 when p = 2/3. Then EXIT-now looks worth (2/5)·4 = 1.6, and CONTINUE-now (and then continue with prob 2/3 at the next one) looks worth (3/5)·2 + (2/5)·1 = 1.6 as well. So the plan is at least stable — he's indifferent — but the number he gets is 1.6, not 1.33.
