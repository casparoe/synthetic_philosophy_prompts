I am trying to get clear about a distinction that I suspect the literature already has a name for, and I would like that name, together with a definition sharp enough to settle cases at the edge.

The first half of the puzzle I believe I follow, and I understand it to be called the symbol grounding problem. If a system's symbols have whatever content they have purely in virtue of their relations to one another — the way the entries of a monolingual dictionary define each word by means of further words — then the system can be flawlessly coherent internally and yet fail to be about anything at all: every symbol is held in place by the others, and nowhere does the net meet the world. I have seen this attributed to a 1990 paper by Harnad, which I have no way of obtaining. The summaries I can reach say that the remedy is to anchor the symbols in something non-symbolic. Grant all of that.

What I cannot find a name for is the difficulty that begins the moment the remedy is applied. Suppose you attach the symbols to the world in the obvious way: the system gets a sensor, a certain internal state reliably fires in the presence of red things, and the symbol for red is tied to that state. This seems to me to presuppose an answer to a further question that nobody has asked. A sensor does not deliver a red thing; it delivers a continuous flow, and something has to decide which stretches of that flow count as *the same again* and which count as *a different one*. The apple in daylight and the apple after the lamp is dimmed. The tomato and the fire engine. The flushed cheek and the sunburnt one. Until that is decided, I do not see what has been grounded, since the symbol could equally have been tied to redness, to redness-or-orangeness-under-this-illumination, or to any of the other groupings the flow admits — and there is no end of candidate groupings that fit the sensor's history equally well.

Two cases make it vivid for me. First, a machine I want to sort cups from non-cups. I give it a camera and ten thousand training instances, and it succeeds. But nothing in that history distinguishes its having latched onto cup-hood from its having latched onto cup-or-mug-in-this-building, or onto being the kind of thing that stood on a table while the camera was switched on, or onto casting a shadow of such-and-such a shape. Every one of these fits the training record perfectly; they diverge only on objects not yet encountered, and then only relative to a decision about which differences matter that has not been made. What troubles me is that the difference is invisible from the inside: the machine would produce the same classifications, with the same confidence, whichever grouping it had settled on. Second, a child learning a colour word. An adult points at a tomato and says the word; the child applies it to an orange; the correction consists of more instances of exactly the same kind of flow, and yet within a year or two the child has it right. So the difficulty is real but evidently surmountable, which is part of why I want it identified rather than just gestured at.

Let me say what I take it *not* to be, since I suspect several nearby things will be offered and I would like them distinguished rather than merely listed. It is not doubt about whether there is a world doing the pushing; I concede the pushing. It is not the original complaint that symbols point only at other symbols. It is not the general observation that any two objects resemble one another in infinitely many respects, so that resemblance alone can never single out a grouping — that feels like a broad sceptical moral about similarity, whereas what I am describing is the specific demand that *something* select the respect, here and now, in a creature or a machine with a finite history. Nor is it, I think, the puzzle about which of a system's other commitments to revise when it acts. A friend has suggested that it is simply the old problem of universals in a laboratory coat; I want to know whether that is right, or whether the sensorimotor setting changes the question.

So: does the second difficulty — selecting the grain, or the respect of sameness, at which the anchoring is to take place, as opposed to the problem of achieving anchoring at all — have an established name? If the nearest established notions are several, tell me which fits best and precisely how my case departs from each of the others. If there is nothing established, coin a term for it, define it, and mark two or three boundary cases. I have no library access where I am, so please give me the carving itself rather than directions to a literature.
