i've been going back and forth on pacifism for like a decade and I've decided I don't actually know what I believe, only what my gut says case by case. Going purely on gut:

- Wrestling a guy off my friend and hitting him until he lets go: fine, obviously.
- Pointing an unloaded gun at a mugger to scare him off, when I'd never fire: fine, and somehow cleaner than hitting him, which is weird because the whole point is the threat of the thing I wouldn't do.
- Crippling or blinding that same mugger when a shove would've worked: not fine, worse than the shove, but not as bad as killing him. I can't say why permanence matters this much and it bugs me.
- Two people who sign up in advance to beat each other up (boxing, MMA): fine. Two people who sign up in advance to fight to the death: not fine. I have no account of why consent runs out there.
- Tackling and tying up a sleepwalker who's about to pull a trigger, or a six-year-old with a live grenade: fine. Nobody's violating anyone's rights in those cases, so any princiiple of mine that says "violence is answer to rights-violation" is already in trouble.
- Locking a man in my basement overnight because I know for dead certain he'll kill someone if I don't and the cops won't come: I think fine. But it's pre-emptive, forward-looking, and completely non-consensual, which are three things I'm otherwise against.
- Smashing up a weapons factory's machinery at night, empty building, planned for weeks, nobody hurt: genuinely fifty-fifty, I flip every time I think about it.
- Paying taxes that I know partly fund a war I think is unjust: fine, doesn't cost me a minute of sleep. Volunteering to drive the munitions truck for that same war: not fine. Even though the money almost certainly does more damage than my one truck would.
- The state executing a serial killer, guilt certain, no deterrent or incapacitation benefit at all: not fine. Punitive violence feels off-limits in a way defensive violence just doesn't.
- A violent slave revolt, or resistance fighters blowing a rail line to slow a genocide: I can't bring myself to condemn it, and next to the execution verdict that makes me feel like a hypocrite.

My best stab so far: violence is okay only when it's the least force that will stop a rights-violation that is actually underway or about to be, and never when it's punitive, deterrent, or aimed at changing someone's future behaviour. But the sleepwalker and boxing cases have no rights-violation, the basement case is pre-emptive coercion, and the revolt blows straight past "least force," so either the principle is broken or I'm holding two views and haven't noticed.

What I want from you: one or two candidate principles stated precisely enough that I can run fresh cases through them, with the case that motivates each clause, an honest list of which of my verdicts each one can't keep (and which you'd tell me to drop), and its verdicts on two or three cases I haven't listed — say, sabotaging the data centre that a drone programme runs on, a country keeping conscription for a purely defensive war, and non-lethal violence against someone's property vs. against their body. Also tell me whether the "least force" clause is doing any real work or just laundering my squeamishness. Under 600 words, and if you have to pick between covering every verdict and being precise, be precise.
