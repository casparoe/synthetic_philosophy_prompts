So here is what my gut says, and I want to know whether it adds up to anything.

- The ball's being scarlet grounds its being red; its being red grounds its being colored. Yes to both.
- The wall's being red is grounded in its parts being arranged thus — not in any single molecule.
- The statue and the lump of clay: neither grounds the other. I don't think the clay grounds the statue; they're one thing, and "in virtue of" is the wrong relation here. He said that's just flatly false.
- Snow's being white grounds it's being true that snow is white. Yes, even though the truth doesn't feel like a further fact at all.
- The triangle's being equilateral and its being equiangular: no grounding in either direction. Same fact in two costumes. He said grounding is asymmetric so one direction has to win.
- The light is on; the switch is up, and separately the backup generator kicked in, and either one alone would have done it. I want to say both of them ground the light's being on.
- Those last two are the pair I can't reconcile: in the triangle case each candidate ground is necessary for the other, in the light case neither is necessary, and yet I say no there and yes here. That feels backwards.
- Game engine case: the character's being poisoned is grounded in a status flag in memory, and the flag is grounded in voltage states in the chip. I refuse to say the poisoning is grounded in the voltages directly, but I also can't say the voltages are irrelevant. Is "ground versus mere enabling condition" a real distinction here or just a dodge?
- The set containing Socrates exists, and that's grounded in Socrates existing. But I also want to say no mathematical fact is grounded in anything, and that doesn't bother me at all. Am I just being inconsistent?
- He ended by claiming it all bottoms out in one big fact — the whole cosmos — with everything else grounded in it, and that nothing else is fundamental. I hate that, but I couldn't say why.

My own stab at a principle: X grounds Y iff you'd have to mention X when giving the fullest true answer to "in virtue of what is it the case that Y?", and you wouldn't have to mention things that are merely necessary background. Test it against my list and repair it, or throw it out and give me something better.
