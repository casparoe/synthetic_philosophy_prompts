I'm giving the closing talk at a small invitation-only workshop on macroeconomic modelling in a couple weeks, and I need Q&A prep. I'm a science journalist — I've covered econ and modelling for about ten years — and I got invited because of a piece I wrote arguing that the professions habitual shrug at unrealistic assumptions (the all-models-are-false move, the as-if defense, the F-twist) is basically fine when a model is being used to describe or forecast inside a regime that isn't moving, but stops buying anything when the same model is used to score a policy that would itself change the behavior the false assumption misdescribes. The talk extends that into a positive proposal. My claim is that unrealistic assumptions are only harmless when they are counterfactually inert for the quantity you're quoting — when the falsehood sits off the causal path to the output — and that the remedy for the cases where it doesn't is not realism for its own sake (I dont think anyone can say what more realistic means in the abstract, and I say so in the talk), but a de-idealization schedule: for each idealization that is load-bearing for the counterfactual the model is being used to evaluate, you have to write down who is supposed to relax it first, what evidence triggers that, and what finding would make you withdraw the policy recommendation. I've got 25 minutes plus 20 for questions, and I'd rather walk in having already lost the arguments in private.

The room is about 45 people and it's not a philosophy crowd.
