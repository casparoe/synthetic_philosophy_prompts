I'm retired now — thirty-odd years in chemical physics, spectroscopy and statistical mechanics mostly — and I've drifted into reading philosophy of physics, so forgive me if I'm using the vocabulary loosely. The view I've ended up holding is roughly the psi-epistemic one, and my reasons are probably not the usual ones. First: the wavefunction looks to me like it's doing exactly the job a Gibbs distribution over an ensemble does in classical stat mech. Nobody in my old department thought the Liouville density was a physical field smeared over phase space, and the fact that the quantum state lives on configuration space strikes me as the same kind of artifact of the formalism rather than a discovery about what the world is made of. Second, and this is the bit I never see discussed: which wavefunction gets assigned to a subsystem depends on how you partition the world into subsystems, and a partition looks like a choice of descriptive variables, not a fact out there.

So: what would count against this? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal down to merely awkward, and I want to know which ones someone actually committed to the psi-epistemic side would concede as counting against them and which they'd just wave off as question-begging. Please take on the standard no-go theorem — the one showing that a state of knowledge can't reproduce the interference predictions without sneaking something ontic back in — since that's what I keep being told settles it, and tell me honestly how much it really settles. Also tell me what else my view is committed to that I may not have noticed (does it drag me toward hidden variables, or toward some particular story about what the Born rule is?). And finally: has this debate been qualified to the point where neither side risks anything, and if so is that a defect or just what happens to a question this far from data? American English, please.
