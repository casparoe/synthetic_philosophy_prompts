So, the special composition question: when do some things add up to one further thing? My own answer is what I call the Grip Principle — some things compose something iff they're mutually stuck, each part held in place by the others, so the whole survives a good shake. That's why there's a table here and no such thing as my left shoe plus the Eiffel Tower: no grip, no object. And this isn't just a verbal quibble, right? We plainly know the shoe-Tower thing doesn't exist, so any theory that says every bunch of things composes something (universalism, I think it's called) is refuted by common sense, end of story. The Grip Principle looks like the obvious middle ground, so: (1) isn't this basically what the serious moderate views are groping toward, and why is it so hard for people to nail down the details? (2) why does anyone still defend the extreme answers? (3) and does the answer really have to be one single necessary and sufficient condition covering all cases — could there be one rule for organisms and a different one for artifacts? Everyone writes as if it has to be one rule, and I'v never seen that defended.
