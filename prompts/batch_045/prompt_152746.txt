The standard taxonomy of moral luck distinguishes four varieties: luck in the results of one's action, luck in the circumstances one faces, luck in the character and dispositions one has, and luck in how one was causally determined to act. I want to ask about a pattern that does not obviously sit inside any of these four, so I will stipulate a label for it. Call it *counterfactual luck*: luck in what you would have done, or in what would have happened, had some chance event gone otherwise in a way that in fact it did not.

Here is what I want from you. Construct a concrete example — set in ordinary life, and not a variation on the familiar textbook dramas (the careless driver who may or may not kill a child, the artist who abandons his family for his work, the citizen who would have collaborated under a worse regime) — in which an agent's blameworthiness appears to depend on a piece of counterfactual luck. Be specific: give the agent, the setting, the careless or wrongful thing she does, and the chance event whose non-occurrence does all the work. The case should satisfy these constraints: no harm actually occurs; the agent's actual conduct, actual circumstances, and actual mental states are the same in the case and in the comparison case you implicitly or explicitly set beside it; and the only thing that varies is a fact about what would have happened in nearby possibilities that never obtained. One agent and a variant of her situation will serve as well as a pair of agents.

Then, briefly and in order:

(a) Say whether the difference in blameworthiness your example produces strikes you as genuine or as a confusion, and what drives that reaction.
(b) Identify which of the four standard varieties your case comes closest to, and explain precisely why the fit is imperfect — which feature of the case resists being absorbed into that variety.
(c) Give the strongest reason for treating counterfactual luck as a further variety in its own right, and the strongest reason for treating it as merely a special case of one of the four.
(d) In a sentence or two: what does your example suggest about whether blameworthiness attaches to what an agent actually does or to the risk her action carried?
