For the past year my graduate seminar on Wittgenstein's late notebooks on certainty has been going around in circles, and I want a diagnosis rather than another position paper.

The three readings we keep returning to are these. The first treats the certainties Wittgenstein discusses — that the earth has existed for a very long time, that this is a hand, that I have never been far from the earth — as genuine propositions we hold without evidence: truth-apt, embeddable, rationally held though not evidentially supported, arational rather than irrational, and candidates for some distinctive non-evidential warrant. The second denies that they are propositions held at all: they are the scaffolding of the practices in which asking and doubting get a sense, a kind of animal certainty nearer to acting than to opining, and the demand that they be warranted is itself the confusion the method is meant to dissolve. The third says the first two are fighting over a class with fixed membership when in fact the relevant certainties are local to an inquiry, numerous, and revisable as the inquiry changes. Every exchange ends in restatement. The propositionalist notes that the certainties have the surface form of assertions, that we assert them and treat them as true, and that denying them content looks like a theory protecting itself by stipulation. The non-propositionalist replies that the surface grammar is exactly what is in question, that true as applied here is not the epistemically loaded notion the other side needs, and that the propositionalist's warrant-talk reimports the very picture being criticized. The localist says both are generalizing from one or two famous examples.

My hypothesis is that this looks verbal but is really a disagreement about cognitive architecture that neither side has made explicit. To test it I have been using neutral apparatus: represent a subject's commitments as sentence-like items tagged with a defeasibility rank, where an item of rank zero is never revised in response to incoming input but is instead used to discount that input, while items of higher rank are revised when they conflict with enough else. On this model the propositionalist is claiming that a functional-role account of belief should count rank-zero items as beliefs, and the non-propositionalist is claiming that a rank-zero item is a parameter of the update rule rather than a state the rule operates on. Two things then happen. First, hinge-hood looks graded rather than binary, since defeasibility rank is a matter of degree; so both readings may be idealizations of a continuum. Second, each reading faces a reflexivity problem it rarely addresses, since each must say what kind of claim it is itself making about hinges, and whether that claim can be doubted.

So: is this a verbal dispute about belief, truth, and knowledge; a substantive dispute about what a cognitive system must be like in order to have empirical beliefs at all; a dispute about what the proper object of epistemology is; or a hidden empirical question about actual subjects — how they behave under conversion, severe breakdown, radical translation failure, or scientific framework shifts such as the one in which a commitment that had functioned as framework-level was converted into a testable hypothesis — masquerading as conceptual analysis? Support the diagnosis with what each side actually says, not a survey of the literature. And tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a distinction, an operational measure of defeasibility rank, a change of question — or nothing, because the dispute is not the kind that closes.
