Hey — I'm a philosophy PhD student (mostly phil. mind / phil. cognitive science) and I'm putting together the handout for our reading group on higher-level causation. I want to run the whole thing through Hofstadter's careenium setup instead of the usual human case, because the human case drags in consciousness and qualia and I want that off the table for a couple of sessions.

Recap for whoever ends up reading this: Careenia is made of some rubbery goo whose bits come in two flavors, plain balls and "simmballs". Simmballs clump into stable patterns, and some of those clumps are simms — structures you can read as symbols, that can be copied, mutated, recombined, and that are about things (a simm for a predator, a simm for a direction, whatever). So the question is whether the simm-level story does any causal work: when a simm gets a clump of simmballs to move, is that just our way of tracking the balls shoving each other around, or is the pattern itself doing some of the pushing?

What I want from you is the spec, not the scoreboard. Before I assign another paper on mental causation / special-science causation / whatever, I want the list: what does an account of simm-level causation have to deliver to count as adequate — which cases it has to get right, which distinctions it can't collapse, which facts about the substrate it has to be consistent with — with the case or consideration under each one. To fix what "adequate" means: good enough to let me keep using symbol-level explanations in cognitive science (a representation caused the behavior, the plan drove the search) without that being hand-wavy, and good enough to say what an experiment on Careenia would even settle.

Some things I think belong on the list, so you can tell me which are real requirements and which are just my own prejudices:

- differentiate between a couterfactual-dependence test and a "who's really producing the motion" test, and say why we should care about the one we care about;
- handle the scramble case — swap which simmballs realize the simm, keep the pattern, and the simm-level story doesn't budge — without that automatically making the simm causally idle;
- handle near-misses: clumps that are structurally almost a simm but aren't about anything (junk patterns, malformed copies, a simm that's been mutated one step too far), and say which side of the line the causal work falls on;
- handle two structurally identical clumps where only one has the history to be about something — does aboutness matter to the causation, or only the shape?;
- say what I've started calling the read/write asymmetry: tons of patterns can be read off the substrate after the fact, but only some of them let you predict what the goo does next. Which ones, and why?;
- the bookkeeping condition, as I've been calling it: the simm-level dynamics should be able to run on its own, without you having to peek at the balls to figure out the next simm state. Is that necessary for genuine simm-level causation, or is it just what a nonreductive physicalist wants for Christmas?;
- not cheat: no extra shove added to the substrate's physics, but also no quiet slide into the simms being decorations, and no accepting that every single simmball movement has two independent sufficient causes.

Also: mark which items are non-negotiable and which are preferences of one camp (I suspect "no overdetermination" is an identity-theory taste and "simm-level laws must be exceptionless" is a Fodor-ish taste, but tell me if I have it backwards), tell me which subset of the requirements can't all be satisfied together — that's the part I most want, since if the list itself is inconsistent that's the interesting result — and say which single requirement knocks out the most candidate views.

Then, briefly, like three lines each max: how epiphenomenalism, a straight reductive identity story, the "the simm just is what the simmballs are doing" pattern view, an interventionist/difference-making account, and the "the simm is a program the substrate is running" view do against the list. I really do care much more about the criteria than the scoring — I can do the scoring myself once I know what I'm asking for. Page-ish is plenty, a table is fine, and please don't give me a reading list, I've got the canon, I just need to know what I'm supposed to be demanding of it.
