Our reading group (me, two philosophy grad students, and one computer science postdoc who refuses to be called a philosopher) has spent basically a whole semester stuck on the causal exclusion argument, and Im the one who has to write up where we got to. Two of us have calcified into positions. Dev thinks mental properties are perfectly ordinary causes and that the exclusion argument only looks forceful because it quietly assumes causation is a single, one-relation, all-or-nothing affair: hes happy with a difference-making, contrastive, interest-relative picture and says the argument slides between a cause being sufficient for its effect in the circumstances and its being the only thing that mattered. Nina thinks the argument is basically sound: she agrees mental properties are real, irreducible, and not epiphenomenal in the silly sense, but she holds that the causal work on any occasion is done by whatever physical property realizes the mental one, and that any claim the mental has to being a cause is inherited from that rather than being an independent contribution. They have been talking past each other for months and I want a document both of them could actually sign.

Here is the case we keep coming back to, so please use it too (and do say where its underspecified). Mira is deciding whether to sign a petition. She has an occurrent desire to sign, call it M, which on this occasion is realized by neural state P. P is causally sufficient, in the circumstances, for her hand moving in the signing way; it produces that effect through a route that involves no further mental state; and there is no separate, independent cause of the signing hanging around waiting to be an overdeterminer. Nina says: P caused the signing, M did not cause it, though M is what makes the cause the kind of cause it is and what makes the story about the signing intelligible. Dev says: both M and P caused it, because Mira signed because she wanted to is true, the counterfactuals about her wanting to sign line up exactly with the counterfactuals about P, and demanding an extra bit of independent causal oomph over and above the counterfactual and pattern structure is a picture rather than an argument.
