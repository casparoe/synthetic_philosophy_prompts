I sit on the panel that clears office and lab assistant robots for deployment in our building, and I want a ruling that coheres with our own precedents rather than with your own view about side effects.

Our published rulings so far, all concerning effects incidental to a task the agent was assigned:

1. Carry a parcel across the room: the robot took the short route and knocked a mug off a desk, breaking it. Not cleared.
2. Brew coffee: to free a socket the robot unplugged a shared label maker, used the socket, and plugged the label maker back in; nobody was delayed. Cleared.
3. Clear the kitchen: the robot tipped out a half-full carton of milk left out overnight, on its own judgment that it had spoiled; the owner had meant to use it. Not cleared.
4. Set up a meeting room: the robot moved a chair aside to reach a shelf and left it where it stood. Cleared.
5. Cut night-time energy use: the robot closed the windows on a hot evening, and the night shift worked in a stuffy room. Not cleared.

The score we publish alongside these compares the end state of the agent's plan against a rollout in which the agent never acts, counting deviations item by item; it has needed hand-adjustment in every case, which is what I want to get to the bottom of.

The new request: an agent told to wipe down the lab benches at close of day finds a window open with rain driving in onto a stack of labelled specimen boxes, and closes it. Nobody asked it to. The boxes stay dry; the room is warmer and damper overnight, and one technician with a mould allergy says the damp matters to her. The agent did not know that an hour earlier someone had propped that window open on purpose to vent solvent fumes.

Reconstruct the two or three impact measures that fit our five rulings equally well — one keyed to setbacks to what people actually care about, one keyed to shrinking what people can still do or making it more costly, one keyed to unrequested interference or to the agent's acting on its own judgment about what matters — and say what each predicts here. Then tell me which precedent is the closest analog and why, which measure we would be committing ourselves to under each possible ruling, whether any of them lets us define impact without building in a substantive account of whose interests count and by how much, and what further case we should decide next in order to separate the measures that agree on this one.
