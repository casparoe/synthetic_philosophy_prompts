I work in the clinical effectiveness team of a large NHS trust, and four times a year I put a report in front of the board that decides where a small improvement team is sent next. I keep hitting the same problem in a form I have never seen it usefully explained in: the combined figures say an intervention worked, the figures split by ward say it did not, or the other way round, and I then have to tell a room of non-specialists which number to act on. I have been reading about this on my own and I think I follow the basic idea of Simpson's paradox, but two things still defeat me. First, one of our statisticians keeps saying that splitting by a variable the intervention itself changes — whether a patient was well enough to be seen by the therapy team, for instance — can manufacture a reversal rather than uncover one, so that the combined figure is the honest one; I cannot tell in advance which kind of variable I am splitting by. Second, it seems to matter whether the board will act trust-wide or ward-by-ward, and I do not know how to make that a step in the rule rather than an excuse afterwards.

Please turn this into a written procedure my team can run in an afternoon, using only the numbers we already hold, and which a clinical director can sign off on and lay governors can follow. Give me the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops and tells me to use the combined figures, where it tells me to stratify, and where it hands the decision back to people because what is unsettled is what we are trying to do rather than what the data say. Please mark clearly which steps are arithmetic that nobody disputes and which rest on views that are genuinely contested among people who study this, so I do not present a contested reading to the board as if it were agreed.

Then run it on three cases from our last cycle: a discharge pathway whose apparent reduction in thirty-day readmissions disappears entirely once you split by admitting specialty; a virtual ward scheme I argued for, whose benefit shows up only in the combined figures and not within any age band; and a sepsis screening tool where the only split available is by whether the patient reached the resuscitation bay, which the screening itself affects. I would rather you told me plainly if one of these has no answer the procedure can defend.
