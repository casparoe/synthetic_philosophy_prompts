I've been chewing on this on my own for a while and I've landed on the view that virtual stuff is just plain real: the sword my character carries in an MMO is a genuine object, its made out of server data and other people's recognition instead of steel, and somebody torching my house in a game or walking off with my gear is a real harm to me and not a pretend one, kind of like how the money in my bank account is real even though its mostly just numbers somewhere.

What would count against that? I don't mean it's only a game. I mean the actual cases, intuitions and arguments that ought to move me, sorted from would-flat-out-kill-the-view down to just embarrassing, and I want you to say which ones someone holding my view should honestly concede count against it and which ones they'd be entitled to shrug off. Three things I keep tripping on: copying (I can duplicate a virtual sword for basically nothing and everyone agrees the copy isn't the original, but you can't do that with a real sword or a real dollar, so what's doing the work?), rollback (if the admins restore the server to before my house burned, is the harm undone, compensated for, or neither?), and this case I made up that I've never seen anyone talk about: a procedurally generated item that spawns in a region no player ever enters, is never rendered and never interacts with anybody, but is sitting there in the save file. Is that a real object nobody ever ran into, or not an object at all? I'm also worried my view quietly slides between three different claims, that virtual objects are real because they're stable tracked data, because a community treats them as real, or because of what they do to people, and that some of those are a lot easier to break than others. So: what's the strongest thing that would make me drop this, and how much of that is genuine exposure versus my view being slippery enough to absorb whatever gets thrown at it?
