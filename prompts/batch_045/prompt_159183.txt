Analyse the Volkswagen emissions scandal of 2015 through the lens of Hannah Arendt's account of lying in politics, as set out in her essay Lying in Politics (collected in Crises of the Republic, 1972), and through the additional stipulative distinction supplied below. Do not simply repeat or paraphrase the Pentagon Papers case that Arendt herself discusses; the point is to test her framework on a different episode.

Treat the following facts as given, since you have no access to external sources. From 2009 to 2015 Volkswagen sold turbocharged direct injection diesel cars in the United States and elsewhere, marketing them as clean diesel. In 2013 the International Council on Clean Transportation commissioned West Virginia University's Center for Alternative Fuels, Engines and Emissions to test on-road emissions; by May 2014 the testers found large discrepancies between laboratory results and real-world nitrogen oxide (NOx) emissions. The Environmental Protection Agency repeated the tests and pressed Volkswagen for an explanation; Volkswagen responded with a voluntary recall in December 2014 and technical explanations, but the discrepancies persisted. On 3 September 2015, after the EPA threatened to withhold certification of 2016 models, Volkswagen admitted that it had programmed the engines to activate emissions controls only during laboratory testing, a defeat device. On 18 September 2015 the EPA publicly announced a recall order; on 20 September Volkswagen admitted deception and apologised; chief executive Martin Winterkorn resigned on 23 September 2015. The software was installed in about eleven million vehicles worldwide, around five hundred thousand of them in the United States, in model years 2009 to 2015, and real-world nitrogen oxide emissions were up to forty times the permitted United States limit.

By Arendt's account I mean, at least, the following claims, which you should use rather than any summary of the essay's own case. Factual truth is contingent and lacks the self-evidence of rational truth; it is what it is regardless of power, but it has no power to compel belief. The political liar is an actor who, unlike the truth-teller, is free to invent, to substitute an image of reality for reality, and to treat facts as material to be managed. Modern organised lying, public relations and image-making aim not merely to conceal a particular fact but to destroy the shared factual world in which facts can be checked. The liar's strength is precisely that he acts, whereas the truth-teller is bound by a reality he did not make; and the consistent replacement of fact by lie ends by destroying the sense of orientation in the real world rather than by making the lie accepted as truth. Reconstruct these claims in your own words if you wish, but keep to this reading.

To make the mapping non-trivial, use the following stipulative distinction, which is not Arendt's own. Call an assertoric lie any attempt to induce belief in a false proposition by means of a statement; call an artefactual lie any attempt to produce a false appearance of compliance or performance by altering the material or procedural conditions under which the relevant audience tests or measures an object, so that the true state of affairs does not register in the accepted verification set-up. Volkswagen's marketing of clean diesel and its pre-September 2015 denials are assertoric lies; the defeat device is the central artefactual lie.

Give a disciplined mapping, in Arendt's terms, of the episode. Specify who the relevant actor or actors are; what factual truth was at stake; what the lie or lies were; what image of itself Volkswagen was trying to preserve; what options it had and which it chose; what power it exercised and over what; and what the position of the truth-tellers was, especially their dependence on measurement and repeatable testing rather than on political opposition or leaked documents.

Then say what Arendt's framework explains well: which features of the scandal it illuminates, and why the language of image-making, organised deception and the vulnerability of factual truth seems apt. Then say where it misdescribes or misses the case. Be specific about the differences between a state's political lie about a war or policy and a corporation's artefactual lie about a product; about the role of independent, repeatable testing in eventually exposing the deception; and about whether the central wrong here was a set of false statements or a redesign of the conditions of verification. Say where Arendt's theory is simply silent—for example, on corporate agency, on regulatory epistemology, or on the difference between consumer deception and citizen deception—rather than forcing a verdict from her.

Then turn the case back on the theory. Is Dieselgate a confirmation of Arendt's view of the fragility of factual truth, an anomaly that strains her speech-centred model of lying, or a case her theory never anticipated because the central lie is artefactual rather than assertoric? Explain which, and why.

Finally, say what a defender of Arendt's account would say in reply to the artefactual-lie objection, and whether that reply is a natural extension of her view or an ad hoc revision. Do not cite or quote any source, and do not discuss the Pentagon Papers case except as a brief contrast.
