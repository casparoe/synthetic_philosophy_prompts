I'm teaching the multiple drafts model in a graduate seminar on consciousness this term, and I want to be able to tell my students something more informative than "there is no Cartesian theater." Here is where I've got to. Suppose we could track, moment by moment, every content-bearing stream in a subject's brain, and we stipulated that a content becomes conscious at the first instant at which its downstream influence grows dominant enough over competing streams to fix what the subject will say and do — a measurable threshold of fame in the brain, as it were. Then every conscious content would have a determinate onset time, and any sequence of them a determinate temporal order, which is just what an empirical science of consciousness seems to require. And it appears to cost us nothing Dennett actually argues against: ascendancy so defined is a relational, distributed property rather than a place where it all comes together, so the theater is given up while the finish line is kept. Is that the right way to read the target — is the Cartesian theater essentially a thesis about location, so that relocating the finish line to a distributed, dominance-based criterion is fully compatible with the multiple drafts model? And if it is, does the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction then collapse into a straightforward empirical question that such instrumentation could settle?
