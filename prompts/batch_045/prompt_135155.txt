I want a worked example that shows why auditing each model on its own isn't enough.

Build me a two-stage case where: (a) it's realistic, one paragraph, no magic predictors or tiny populations; (b) each model individually satisfies equalized odds exactly — equal true and false positive rates across two groups — when audited on a shared benchmark sample where every applicant happens to have both scores; (c) but the pipeline, i.e. pass both, has a true positive rate gap of at least ten points while its false positive rate is basically the same across groups; (d) and the failure isn't the familiar base-rate / calibration-impossibility thing — keep qualification rates equal across groups and make each single model look clean, so none of the standard single-model impossiblity results is doing any work.

Show that if the two stages' errors are independent within both groups the gap collapses. And finish with what the vendors would say in that meeting: the most honest objection to calling this a fairness failure of either model, what they'd insist is really a pipeline design problem, and what our audit procedure should demand from them instead.
