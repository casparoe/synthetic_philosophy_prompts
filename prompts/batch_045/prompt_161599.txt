The claim that a device can convert information into work needs a standard of assessment before it needs a verdict, so I want the specification first: write the desiderata an account of information in thermodynamics would have to satisfy. Purpose: good enough to tell an experimentalist whether a proposed nanoscopic information engine can beat the ordinary thermodynamic bounds, and if not, exactly which step of its cycle pays. Assume the reader has statistical mechanics — entropy as a state function, the second law, the Carnot bound — and knows nothing of the specialist literature. Name no authors and give no citations; define every view you discuss by its commitments.

The desiderata have to be answerable for each of the following, and I want cases I have not thought of added to the list: a demon with a single bit of memory, reset every cycle; a demon with an effectively unbounded tape it never resets, so the record accumulates; a demon handed a tape that already carries the relevant correlation with the system, so that no measurement is performed by anyone; a demon whose detector is noisy and yields only partial information; a demon operating on a working substance that already spans two temperatures, versus one operating on a substance at equilibrium; a demon that spends its record on refrigeration rather than on work; a quantum version in which the memory becomes entangled with the system and the measurement is non-disturbing; a passive mechanism with no memory at all — trapdoor, ratchet and pawl, one-way valve — which must be excluded in a way that isolates what the memory contributes; and a device small enough and observed briefly enough that transient decreases of entropy are routinely recorded.

For each requirement give the case that motivates it and mark it non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. I expect the hardest parts to be these, and I want them stated as requirements rather than as opinions: identify the bounded quantity and its units, and say whether the bound is a floor on dissipation, an identity, or a limit approached only in a slow process; say whether the bound is derived from statistical mechanics or imported as an extra postulate; require the books to close over any finite number of cycles, including cycles that leave a debt in an unerased memory; require consistency with entropy's being a state function, so that no choice of path through the cycle changes what is owed; and take a position on whether the accounting may mention what some agent knows, or must be phrased in purely physical terms.

Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — I suspect one such pair is the demand that the cost always be a local event at the moment of erasure, against the demand that the accounting be path-independent and free of agent-relative quantities; and another is treating information as a storable, tradeable resource on a par with free energy, against excluding epistemic notions from the bookkeeping. Then say which single requirement rules out the largest share of otherwise plausible accounts, and why.

Finish with a brief scorecard — a few lines each — for three views, defined stipulatively: that the cost is paid at erasure; that the cost is paid when the correlation is acquired; and that the whole phenomenon is a special case of a generalized nonequilibrium free-energy bound, with ordinary thermodynamics recovered when no information is processed. Keep the weight on the criteria, not on the scorecard.
