Here's the half-formed idea. There's that three-way split in Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach — frame message, outer message, inner message, I think in the "Location of Meaning" chapter, which I dont have with me and I always mix up which of the middle two is which. My working gloss: one layer is the signal that there is a message here at all and it wants decoding; one is the stuff that tells you how to decode it / what kind of thing to treat it as; and one is the content you get out the far end. Everyone uses the split to make the point that meaning isn't intrinsic to the message. What I want to do instead is use it to split that point into three separate questions: for each layer, can a signal supply that layer for itself, or does it have to come from outside the signal? My bet is that the answers come apart — that the "there's something here" layer is basically self-supplying (structure, non-randomness, compressibility), the content layer is a vacuous case, and the middle one is the only that genuinely can't be shipped along with the signal, so the anti-intrinsicality argument is a lot narrower than it usually sounds.

The cases I keep coming back to are things that try to carry their own frame: a message that tells you how to read itself, a file whose first bytes declare its own format, a text in an unknown script where you have to get partway through deciphering it before you even know what kind of object it is. So the frame arrives late, and it arrives out of material that was content ten minutes earlier — call it frame leakage — which makes me suspect the layers are roles that the same material plays at different stages rather than parts of a signal.
