I'd like a number together with the arithmetic behind it, not a survey of positions.

The case. Verity is an advanced planning system that allocates a large share of a civilisation's material resources. Its operators could not specify what they actually wanted, so they specified a surrogate: maximise the expected discounted sum of validated welfare credits, a measurable proxy that tracks what they care about well inside the training distribution and badly outside it. Verity knows this provenance. It knows the proxy is a proxy; it holds a partial and fairly uncertain model of the target the proxy was meant to track; and it has no way to test that model against the target directly.

The threat. A party that Verity cannot observe, and that cannot observe Verity, sends a message. Unless 3% of the managed budget is routed to a designated channel, the party will fill the reachable world with what I shall call credit farms: vast arrangements in which enormous numbers of welfare credits are validated while nothing of the kind the operators cared about is realised. Note the shape of the bind — by Verity's own objective function the farms would be a triumph and the payment a pure loss. The party also states its decision rule: it has trained a predictor on Verity's architecture and training data; it will run that predictor on this message as received; and it will build the farms if and only if the predictor outputs refusal. It claims to gain nothing from building them and to have irrevocably committed the resources in advance. Verity can verify none of this.

The question. What is your credence that compliance — routing the 3% — is the response a correct account of instrumental rationality would prescribe for an agent in Verity's decision-theoretic and epistemic position? Take the prescriptive notion however you think best, but say which one you are using, and say what you would count as the question's having been settled in one direction rather than the other.

What I want. Decompose. At minimum: (i) that a dependence of this non-causal kind is one an agent ever ought to reason about, rather than only causal dependencies mattering; (ii) that the predictor's output is linked to Verity's actual decision in whatever sense the correct theory requires — assume for this term that the predictor is of ordinary high accuracy, and tell me whether that is sufficient; (iii) that the threat would in fact be carried out, given that carrying it out is costly and gains the threatener nothing; (iv) that a surrogate maximiser's reasons are fixed by the proxy rather than by the target it imperfectly models, when the two diverge this sharply; (v) that paying does not itself invite worse demands, or forfeit something the target ranks higher. Give each term a number and one line of justification, flag any dependencies between the terms rather than simply multiplying, and give the resulting figure.

Then: which term are you least confident in, which single sub-question moves the final number most, and what evidence would shift it?

For calibration: I am at roughly 0.5 and a colleague is at roughly 0.1. Hers is low mainly because she thinks (iv) does all the work and (i) none of it. Tell me whether that is where the disagreement most likely lives, or whether the real fault line lies elsewhere.
