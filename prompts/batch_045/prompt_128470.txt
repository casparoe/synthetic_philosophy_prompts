I run a small reading group that's been working though self-locating belief, and everyone has already said there piece about the plain vanilla case, so I want something that pushes past it. Here's the setup I've been messing around with, and I want you to argue one side of it.

Case A, the plain one. Fair coin on Sunday. Heads: Beauty is woken Monday and that's it. Tails: she's woken Monday and Tuesday, amnesia in between, no clock, no way to tell the days apart. On waking she's asked her credence in heads. Halfers say a half, thirders say a third, and we've heard all of that already.

Case B, mine. Same coin, but now there are two people, Beauty and her understudy. Heads: Beauty is woken Monday, the understudy sleeps through the whole thing. Tails: Beauty is woken Monday and the understudy is woken Tuesday. When you wake up you genuinely can't tell which of the two you are — twins, no mirrors, the experimenter never uses either name — though both of you were told the protocol in advance and know it. If you knew you were Beauty you'd be at a half, since Beauty gets woken either way. If you knew you were the understudy you'd be at zero, since the understudy only ever wakes on tails. You're two-thirds sure you're Beauty, and that nets out at a third.

Case C, also mine. Same as A except that on tails Beauty is woken Monday, then scanned overnight, and an exact duplicate of her is woken Tuesday in her place while the original sleeps on, with the duplicate put under for good at the end of Tuesday. Again you can't tell whether you're the original or the duplicate. Same arithmetic as B: a half if you knew you were the original, zero if you knew you were the duplicate, a third if you don't.

What I want you to argue for: that A has to get the same answer as B and C, so a third, and that the halfer's way of parking A at a half is dead. In particular, don't let anyone get away with this reply — in B and C you're ignorant about *who* you are, which is a real lottery with a real chance of you never waking up at all, whereas in A Beauty was guaranteed in advance to be woken at least once whatever the coin did, so her being awake isn't news and she stays at a half. I need you to actually kill that move, not just give it a name. And whatever principle you use has to say something about the duplicate in C, who is in a tails world every single time and would still be answering a half if she reasoned like a halfer.

Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no preamble and no summing up at the end. Keep it in plain language, no more than one line of arithmetic anywhere, and don't cite anybody — half the group hasn't read the literature and I want something I can read out loud.
