I'm a philosophy professor, and I'm the one non-specialist on the classification panel of an international catastrophe register — the rest are demographers, historians, and risk modelers. I'm drafting the written opinion we'll circulate before our decision on whether to re-code three events from "natural hazard — drought/flood" to "politically caused catastrophe": the Bengal famine of 1943, Ireland in the 1840s, and the Great Leap Forward. This isn't merely classificatory. Our hazard codes are what sovereign catastrophe bonds and resilience grants key off, while a separate reparations statute keys off the political codes; the same deaths end up funded, compensated, or neither depending on which box we tick.

Three complications make it harder than it looks. First, our standing rule attributes a death to the hazard only if it would still have occurred under a "non-heroic" government — one using the administrative means and information actually available at the time, but not required to act against its own governing priorities. On the entitlement account that Sen's work on Bengal made famous, all three events fail that test; but if every famine fails it, the hazard category is empty, and re-coding these three conveys nothing a future coder could use. Second, our early-warning partners calibrate their alert thresholds on the hazard-coded sample, and their analysts warn that removing three of the largest entries will make those thresholds fire later and less often — a classification change that could itself cost lives. Third, the relief agencies point out that "natural disaster" is the phrase that unlocks money and sympathy, and that a political code hands successor governments a way to treat famine deaths as something other than victims of a disaster at all.

Draft the opinion: recommend one of re-coding, retaining the hazard code, or splitting each entry into separate causal-attribution and responsibility fields; state a rule the panel can apply consistently to future events rather than only to these three; and give the strongest version of the option you reject, in terms I can quote back both to the relief agencies and to the panel members who think famine mortality should be counted where the rainfall failed.
