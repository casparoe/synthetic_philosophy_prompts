I work in valuations and estate work for a mid-sized auction house — I read art history as an undergraduate and have now been at this for about fifteen years — and there is a pattern in the objects that cross my desk for which I have never had a word, and about which a conservator friend and I argue endlessly. The pattern is this: some objects are worth *more* once they are revealed to be something other than what they were taken to be, and not merely because they re-settle at a lower but respectable price under a new description — the exposure itself seems to add value. The case my friend and I keep returning to is the famous one of the Dutch painter who, in the 1930s and 40s, produced several newly "discovered" biblical and domestic scenes in the manner of a seventeenth-century master; when the deception collapsed, the very canvases that had been sold as Vermeers apparently became collectible under the forger's own name, at figures that a competent, honest, signed picture by the same hand would never have approached. Nothing physical about the objects changed; what changed is that they are now known to be fakes, and to be fakes that once got past a museum, a celebrated authority, and a market. My friend says this is nothing but provenance gossip: the object has become a relic of a notorious episode, and what people pay for is the story, not the picture. I don't think that can be the whole of it, because the same is not true of the many perfectly competent fakes whose exposure simply destroys them, and because these pictures are still hung, discussed, and apparently responded to as pictures.

I also think I can see neighbouring cases, and I can't tell which of them are the same animal. There are works made openly as homages or pastiches — signed, exhibited, never meant to deceive — which the market later decides to treat as forgeries of a grander name, and which then acquire the same sort of premium. There are now, in my own field, sets of works produced in the manner of a recently dead painter and sold plainly labelled as synthetic continuations of his late manner; these, I'm told, sometimes outperform his genuine minor works, even though nobody was fooled and no institution was embarrassed. Against those: the fake whose maker is anonymous and has no other work to be reattributed to, where exposure seems only ever to subtract; the fake discovered before anyone in a position to certify it ever certified it, where the deception was real but unsuccessful; and, outside art altogether, fabricated field notes or a specimen assembled from parts, where I cannot see any route by which exposure could add value at all — the object is simply worthless the moment it is known to be dishonest.

So my question is whether this configuration has a name of its own in the philosophical or critical literature, and I want to be clear that a name is not what I am really after: I want the carving. I have read a little around the edges and know there is a well-known puzzle about why an undetectable fake should be worth less than the genuine article when nobody can tell the two apart, but my case seems to run in the other direction — the value doesn't merely fail to collapse, it rises — and I have never found it discussed. If there is an established concept, I would like to know exactly how my pattern sits with respect to it and to its nearest neighbours, and whether it is one thing or several. If there isn't, I would like a term, a definition, and a decision about the boundary cases above: whether the phenomenon requires an actual deception or only attributed one; whether it requires an institution that was really fooled, or whether demonstrated skill is enough; whether the premium belongs to the object's aesthetic life or only to its documentary interest; and whether it decays once the scandal is no longer something anyone living remembers. I ask partly for my own sake and partly because I write valuation notes that have to say, in a sentence, what sort of object a reclassified thing now is, and I have never had a vocabulary for the sentence.
