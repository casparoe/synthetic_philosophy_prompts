ok so here's roughly where I've landed on all this and I want to know whether it holds together before I say any of it out loud to my reading group. Bit of context: my mum died in Feburary after a hard year, my partner's due in May, and somewhere in the middle of all that I got half-convinced by a view called tranquilism — nothing is good except the absence of craving and the absence of suffering. Pleasure, achievement, knowledge, love: worth nothing in themselves, only insofar as they quiet craving or head off suffering. It sounds grim said like that but it's been fitting how this year has actually felt.

The package, roughly in the order I thought of it:

(1) tranquilism as above, plus: a life with no craving and no suffering isn't improved one bit by adding pleasant stuff on top of it.
(2) my mum's last year was, in some way I can't name, better for being hard and lucid than it would have been if we'd kept her comfortable and vague the whole time. I don't want to say the suffering was good. But I don't want to delete the year either, and I'd be insulted on her behalf if someone called it just a bad year with nothing to show for it.
(3) I give about 10% of what I earn to reducing suffering, and I don't think there's any comparable reason to fund making already-content people more pleased. But I do think craving-reduction — addiction, anxiety, that restless itch for the next thing — is on a par with treating pain, not a distant second best.
(4) I think it would be a catastrophe if we went extinct this century, and I think it would have been a bad thing if my mum had never existed. I know people with views like (1) are supposed to give these up. I don't want to.
(5) I want my daughter to have a life full of curiosity and ambition and music and love, not a calm quiet one. I keep telling myself this doesn't clash with (1) because it's her life and she'll want what she wants.
(6) I volunteer on a crisis line and we're trained to sit with someone's distress rather than steer them out of it. I think that's right even when steering would pretty clearly reduce their suffering, and I'd feel I'd done something wrong if I steered them.

Is this one position or five? Specifically: which pairs are in real tension and which only look like it, and how bad is each one? Order them by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what that would cost me. And if there's a single revision that cleans up most of the mess, tell me which. Please don't just tell me to drop (1) — I want to know what (1) commits me to that I might not of noticed, including whether the absence-of-craving half is doing any real work or whether it just collapses into only suffering is bad. My guess is (5) is the weak spot but I can't say why, so don't take my word for it.
