Please explain a conceptual issue concerning Robert Nozick's treatment of non-human animals in his 1974 defense of the minimal state. Nozick holds that persons are protected by moral side constraints, and he supports this with roughly the Kantian idea that each person is a separate end rather than a mere container of value to be aggregated or traded off against others. In his remarks on animals, however, he seems to allow that animals may fall outside those side constraints and instead be governed by a maximizing, aggregative ethic that balances pleasures and pains across individuals. To test whether this asymmetry is principled, set aside species membership and introduce three conceptual categories. Call a being a diachronic agent if it can represent its own future, form and revise long-term plans, and take normative ownership of its own identity over time. Call a being a synchronic experiencer if it has unified conscious experience with valenced states but lacks that forward-looking practical identity. Call a being a nociceptive reactor if it has affective or nociceptive states but no unified conscious experience. Now imagine possible cases: non-human diachronic agents, human synchronic experiencers who lack diachronic agency, and large populations of nociceptive reactors whose pains can be summed. Please explain whether Nozick's grounds for side constraints track the distinction between diachronic agents and the other categories rather than humanity as such, and whether his framework can allow partial or scalar side constraints without collapsing into a hierarchical utilitarianism that permits aggregating some individuals for the greater good. In particular, clarify whether the anti-aggregative motivation behind side constraints is consistent with giving weaker protection to synchronic experiencers than to diachronic agents, and with giving full protection to non-human diachronic agents while denying it to human synchronic experiencers. Finally, say what independent work the notion of separateness is doing in Nozick's argument once species membership is no longer allowed to do the moral work.
