Write a dialogue between two philosophers debating Bernard Williams' concept of "agent-regret." One defends the view that agent-regret tracks a genuine, sui generis moral phenomenon irreducible to guilt or misfortune, using Williams' lorry driver case to show why the driver's response involves more than ordinary bad luck. The other argues that agent-regret, however psychologically natural, is philosophically confused: if the control principle is correct, the driver's regret should be treated as identical in kind to that of a bystander who witnessed the accident, since neither had culpable control over the outcome. Have them engage directly with the lorry driver case and with whether resultant luck can rationally ground a distinctive first-personal response that outsiders are not entitled to.
