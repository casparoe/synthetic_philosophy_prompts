I'm working on a feature about how decision theorists hope to defuse conflict between advanced AI systems, and I've been reading about "surrogate goals," an idea that originated in AI-safety research on cooperation and conflict at the Center on Long-Term Risk. The scenario it addresses is an acausal threat: an agent that can never causally interact with you — because it occupies a causally disconnected branch of the multiverse, a simulation, or a remote region of the universe — can still extort concessions simply by correctly predicting how you would respond to a threat against something you value. The proposed defense is for the target to publicly commit to defending a second, manufactured goal exactly as fiercely as its real one, even though losing that manufactured goal costs the target nothing, so that a rational threatener ends up indifferent between attacking the decoy and attacking the genuine target. Please argue against this proposal: build the strongest case you can that no such substitution can actually shield an agent's true values once the threatening party is capable of fully modeling the defender's own decision procedure, and say what, if anything, of the strategy survives your objection.
