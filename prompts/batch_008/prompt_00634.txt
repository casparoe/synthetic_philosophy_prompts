I'm a philosophy PhD student working on formal approaches to distributive ethics and social choice theory. I also sit on my graduate program's small research-grants committee, which allocates a modest annual budget across a handful of competing proposals submitted by fellow students. Several members want to formalize our deliberations by having each of us report a von Neumann–Morgenstern utility function over the possible outcomes of each proposal and then simply averaging these into a single committee ranking, citing Harsanyi's aggregation theorem as justification. Please describe the most important implications of Harsanyi's theorem for whether, and how, our committee should adopt this procedure, bearing in mind that members generally agree on what a successful outcome would look like but often hold quite different views about how likely any given proposal is to actually succeed.
