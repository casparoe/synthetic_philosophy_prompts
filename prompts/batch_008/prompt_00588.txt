Fifteen years ago, as a self-absorbed teenager, I said some genuinely cruel things to my closest friend during a bad falling-out, and we never spoke again. She recently tracked me down and asked for a real apology and some acknowledgment of what I did to her. Here's what's tripping me up: when I try to write that apology, it feels dishonest, almost like confessing to a crime someone else committed. My values, my temperament, even the way I treat people, have kept changing since then—slowly at first, then quite dramatically—so that the teenager who hurt her feels less like an earlier version of me than a distant relative I happen to share a name and a few memories with. At the same time, it seems obviously wrong to write back that it wasn't really me, since I'd be furious if anyone tried that line on me. Is there something confused about thinking I'm off the hook because I'm no longer the same person, or does the fact that I can't find any real continuity between us mean this apology isn't fully mine to give?
