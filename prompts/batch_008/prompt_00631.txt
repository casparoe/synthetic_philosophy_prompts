A friend and I spent much of last night arguing about the grounding objection to Molinism, and we got stuck on Plantinga's reply to it. As I understood him, his point is that the demand for "grounds" is itself suspect, since we are just as hard-pressed to say what grounds the mundane counterfactual that Curley, having freely accepted a $35,000 bribe, would also have freely accepted a $36,000 one—yet nobody doubts that such a counterfactual could be true. My friend thought this settles the dispute in the Molinist's favor; I wasn't convinced. Make the strongest case you can that there is a genuine disanalogy between everyday counterfactuals of freedom like the Curley case and the counterfactuals of creaturely freedom Molinism requires God to know logically prior to any decision to create—one that survives Plantinga's point and explains why the mundane case gives the Molinist no real comfort.
