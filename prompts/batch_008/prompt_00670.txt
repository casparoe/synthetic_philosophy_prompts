In a philosophy of mind reading group I attend, we recently discussed Giulio Tononi's Integrated Information Theory of consciousness, and one member of the group made an argument that most of us found persuasive, though something about it kept nagging at me afterward. Her argument ran roughly as follows: IIT identifies a system's level of consciousness with its integrated information, Φ, which is usually introduced informally as a measure of how much a system's parts interact with and constrain one another over and above what they do independently. But if that is the relevant standard, she argued, then all sorts of things we're confident are not conscious — or at least not more conscious than any individual human being — should come out with enormous Φ values. A national power grid, the global internet, or the dense web of communicative and economic relations that make up an entire society all involve vastly more interacting elements, and vastly more information passing between them, than the roughly 86 billion neurons in a human brain. Since none of these strike us as more conscious than a person, she concluded that this amounts to a reductio of IIT.

I couldn't immediately spot the flaw, though I suspected there was one, since this seemed like too easy a way to dispose of a theory that a fair number of serious neuroscientists and philosophers still take seriously. I vaguely recall that IIT has faced (and its defenders have responded to) related objections involving simple grids of logic gates that turn out to have surprisingly high Φ on the formalism, so I'm unsure whether my friend's argument is just a looser version of that same genuine and still-unresolved problem, or whether it fails for a more basic reason connected to how Φ is actually defined and computed — something to do with a "minimum information partition" and an "exclusion postulate" that I don't fully understand. Could you walk through where, if at all, her argument goes wrong, and explain what Φ does and doesn't actually track about a system's sheer size and connectivity?
