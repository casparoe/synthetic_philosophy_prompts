While I was prepping my animal ethics seminar this came up and I want a second opinion. We're covering that odd little stretch of Anarchy, State, and Utopia that gets assigned constantly and then mostly waved away in discussion — where Nozick floats "utilitarianism for animals, Kantianism for people" as a way to keep his side-constraints protecting persons from ever being sacrificed for the greater good, while still letting us run ordinary cost-benefit tradeoffs on animals (he's also the one who dreams up the case of painlessly snapping your fingers to kill 10,000 unowned cows, which comes just before the utility monster shows up). He never fully signs on to the view himself, but it's clearly meant as a serious live option, and honestly it tracks a lot of my students' gut reactions pretty well — testing on animals is fine if the stakes are big enough, testing on people isn't, full stop.

A student pushed back on it in a way that's stuck with me more than the usual objections do. Her point was that whatever Nozick (or anyone) says grounds the inviolable status of persons — rational agency, being able to conceive of and steer your life as a whole, whatever it ends up being — either (a) plenty of actual humans (infants, people in permanent vegetative states, people with profound cognitive impairments) don't clear that bar any more convincingly than a pig or a crow does, so the hybrid view is already quietly committed to some humans losing full protection, or (b) the criterion gets loosened to species membership or "typical" or "potential" capacity, in which case it's not really doing independent philosophical work anymore and is just smuggling the line back in by fiat. Either way the sharp person/animal cutoff looks like its resting on more than the stated justification can actually carry. I think this is basically a version of the old "marginal cases" move, but I've never actually seen it run carefully and specifically against Nozick's hybrid view in particular, engaging with the best replies available to him (species-typical potential, some story about having a Kantian will rather than any measurable capacity, etc.) rather than against speciesism in the abstract.

Could you actually build that argument out properly for me — as rigorous and un-question-begging as you can make it, addressing the strongest replies on Nozick's behalf rather than the easy ones? Id like to have something solid enough to hand my students as a model of how to press this kind of objection, instead of the usual one-line "well, marginal cases!" gesture that everyone in the seminar already sees coming from a mile off.
