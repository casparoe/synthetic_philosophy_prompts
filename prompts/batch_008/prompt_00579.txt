ok this has been bugging me — every intro course treats the butterfly dream at the end of the Qiwulun as basically Descartes's dream argument 2000 yrs early, i.e. "how do you know your not dreaming right now." but that always felt like a misreading to me: the passage ends by calling it "the transformation of things" (wu hua), and the whole chapter's actual point seems to be that any perspective — waking vs dreaming, being a Ru vs a Mohist, w/e — looks total and self-evident from the inside, so you shouldn't get too attached to whichever one you happen to occupy. that's different from needing an Archimedean point to escape the doubt, which is Descartes's whole project.

given that, what do you think Zhuangzi would actually say about Bostrom's simulation argument, or Chalmers's "simulation realism" (his claim in Reality+ that even if we're sims, the everyday objects around us are still real, just made of bits)? would he engage with the question on its own terms, or think the whole debate is quietly smuggling back in the fixed, non-relative standpoint he spent that chapter trying to talk us out of? genuinely don't know what he'd say here, curious what you think.
