Acausal trade is supposed to let two agents who can never causally interact still end up benefiting each other, simply because each can accurately work out what the other would do. What I've never been able to settle for myself is whether that kind of mutual, accurate prediction is really enough to count as trade, or whether something more is required: that the two agents' choices be linked by something more than correlation, so that one agent's choosing to cooperate is, in some sense, the very same underlying fact as the other's choosing to cooperate, rather than the two choices merely happening to line up. (This is roughly what people mean by "subjunctive dependence" when they discuss timeless decision theory.) Write a dialogue between two people who both accept that acausal trade is a coherent idea, but who land on opposite sides of this question. Have them test their positions against the case of two civilizations on opposite sides of the cosmological horizon, each deciding — purely on the basis of simulating what the other would do — whether to consume every resource within its reach or to hold some back for the other's sake. They should respond to the specific points the other raises rather than simply restating their own view.
