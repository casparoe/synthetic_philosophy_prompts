I'm a PhD student in clinical psychology, and a situation with one of my closest friends has been on my mind in a way I can't quite resolve, so I'd appreciate an outside perspective. Marco and I have been close since undergrad, and for most of that time he has struggled with alcohol, including two serious relapses over the past several years. During his heavy drinking periods he does things that would be unforgivable from almost anyone else: he missed my sister's wedding entirely after promising for months that he would fly in, and during arguments he has said cruel, pointed things that he later insists he doesn't remember at all. When he's sober, which is most of the time, he is unusually self-aware about this exact pattern and will sometimes send long, careful messages afterward working through exactly what he did and why it hurt me. What complicates things further is that his drinking episodes don't look like a simple loss of control from the outside: friends who were with him at the time say he still seemed to be making choices in some sense, like deciding on the bar over the wedding despite reminders all that week, which makes it feel too convenient to just say he "wasn't himself."

My difficulty isn't really about the underlying facts, since I already accept, professionally, that dependence involves genuine disruptions to judgment and impulse control; it's about what to do with that fact in how I relate to him day to day. Some weeks I feel plainly resentful toward him, the ordinary way you'd feel toward a friend who let you down, and his later remorse matters to me and seems to restore something between us. Other weeks I try to set that reaction aside entirely and think of him more the way I'd think about a patient with a relapsing illness, treating the whole situation as something to be managed rather than taken personally, and in those moods his apologies start to feel less like something to be moved by and more like a symptom to note. Neither mode feels sustainable on its own: the first seems unfair given what I know about dependence, and the second, if I hold onto it for more than a few days, leaves me feeling like I've quietly stopped being his friend and started being his case manager. A few labmates have told me I just need to decide, once and for all, whether he's responsible or not, but that hasn't helped, since it doesn't explain why both reactions keep seeming partly right depending on the situation. Is there a more principled way of thinking about when it's appropriate to hold someone like Marco responsible for what he does while drinking versus when I should treat it as something that simply happens to him, and is it actually coherent to relate to him both ways depending on the moment, or am I just failing to make up my mind?
