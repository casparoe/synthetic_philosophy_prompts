I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole reading about the Hobbes-Wallis feud from the 1650s onward -- the saga where Hobbes claims in De Corpore that he squared the circle, Wallis tears the "proof" apart within the year, and the two of them spend the next two-plus decades publicly savaging eachother (at one point Hobbes dismisses Wallis's algebra as a "scab of symbols" all over the page). What's more interesting to me than the drama itself is that Hobbes had this whole idiosyncratic theory of why geometry counts as real knowledge in the first place: he thought we can be certain of geometric truths specifically because we construct the objects ourselves, a line literally generated by the motion of a point, a plane by the motion of a line, etc, the same way he thought civil philosophy is certain because we're the ones who construct commonwealths. He was actually pretty dismissive of Euclid's own definitions (things like "a point is that which has no part") for not specifying any real construction, and he even thought Euclid's axioms needed to be demonstrated rather then just assumed -- so it's not like he was some fanboy trying to import "the Euclidean method" wholesale into every other field, his actual view was weirder and more specific than that.

Given all that, what do you think Hobbes would've made of two later developments: non-Euclidean geometry showing up in the 1800s (where you can drop the parallel postulate and still get a perfectly consistent geometry), and then the Hilbert-style formalist turn where "point" and "line" become just placeholders implicitly defined by whatever axioms you pick, with no required connection to actual bodies or motion at all. Does his maker's-knowledge story have any way of absorbing that, maybe by treating these as alternate constructions he just hadn't gotten around to considering, or does it basically require geometry to stay pinned to one specific physical/kinematic picture of space in a way these developments would've wrecked? I'm not looking for the "Hobbes was a stubborn crank who couldn't do math" take -- I want you to actually work through what follows if you take his stated views on geometry seriously.
