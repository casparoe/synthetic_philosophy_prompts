I don't have any background in philosophy or formal logic, but I enjoy puzzling over odd little inconsistencies in everyday reasoning, and one has been nagging at me for weeks. Over the past year I put together a booklet of local history for our neighborhood association: around four hundred short entries covering old businesses, founding dates, street name changes, and similar details. I cross-checked nearly every entry against at least two independent sources before including it, so if you asked me about any single entry in isolation, I'd tell you with real confidence that it's correct.

Yet when my sister asked whether the finished booklet, taken as a whole, was accurate, I heard myself answer, almost without thinking, "oh, there's bound to be a mistake in there somewhere" — the same reflexive thing you'd say about almost any sufficiently long reference work. She thought this was a strange thing to say and accused me of contradicting myself: if I'm confident entry one is correct, and confident entry two is correct, and so on through all four hundred entries, she argued that ordinary logic should make me confident they're all correct together, meaning I should expect zero errors rather than casually assuming there's "probably one or two." But that's not how I feel at all, and I don't think it's how anyone reasonably would feel about a project of that size. I've since noticed the same pattern elsewhere: I trust every individual line of a coworker's expense report, or every fact rattled off in a friend's rambling wedding toast, while still fully expecting that something in each one will turn out to be slightly off.

I tried looking into whether this was some kind of recognized problem, but not knowing what to call it, I didn't get very far. So I'm hoping you can help me figure out who's really being unreasonable here, my sister or me: is it actually possible to be rationally confident in each individual claim in a large set like this while also being confident that the set as a whole contains a mistake, or is she right that I can't consistently hold both beliefs at once?
