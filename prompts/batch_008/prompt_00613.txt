I've been reworking the epistemic logic unit of my formal epistemology seminar, and I want to get past the textbook statement of the problem of logical omniscience. On the standard Hintikka-style possible-worlds semantics, an agent automatically knows every valid formula and every logical consequence of what it knows, simply because knowledge is modeled as truth across a set of accessible worlds. What I'd like from you is a gut-level explanation of why two of the most influential remedies aren't really solving the same problem. Impossible-worlds semantics (Hintikka, Rantala) lets some epistemically accessible worlds violate classical entailment, which blocks the closure property directly; Fagin and Halpern's awareness-structure approach instead filters an underlying implicit-belief relation that is itself built in exactly the old, fully omniscient way, so that only the beliefs singled out as "explicit" escape omniscience. My suspicion is that the awareness strategy therefore leaves logical omniscience fully intact at the implicit level and merely relabels which beliefs we are willing to attribute — tell me whether that's a fair characterization, and whether there's a principled reason to prefer one strategy over the other beyond a difference in what each is meant to model.
