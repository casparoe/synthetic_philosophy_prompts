Something happened during an on-call shift a few weeks ago that I still haven't been able to think my way out of, and I'd like an outside perspective on it.

I was around fourteen hours into a messy production incident, running on almost no sleep, when I finally traced the bug to what looked like a clear root cause. I walked through the logic two or three times, checked it against the logs, and talked it through with a teammate over chat — by the end I was about as sure as I ever get that I'd found the right fix. Right before merging, another engineer mentioned a pattern our team has noticed across several years of postmortems: fixes written more than twelve hours into an incident get reverted roughly three times as often as fixes written earlier in a shift. Tellingly, the engineers who'd written the bad late-shift fixes hadn't rated their own confidence any lower than the ones whose fixes held up. So in that state, feeling sure of yourself apparently isn't much of a signal either way. I went back over my reasoning after hearing this and genuinely couldn't find anything wrong with it; it still looked airtight to me.

What I haven't been able to resolve is what I'm actually supposed to do with that information. On one hand, it seems like good evidence that people in exactly my situation are often confidently wrong, and that ought to count against simply trusting my own read of things here. On the other hand, it feels strange to conclude that the fix is now less likely to be correct, when nothing about my actual reasoning has changed and I still can't locate a flaw in it — I'd essentially be holding onto a belief while also believing I shouldn't fully trust myself for holding it, which seems like an odd position to knowingly occupy. I brought this up with a couple of friends over drinks and we argued about it for close to an hour without reaching any real agreement. Is there a coherent way to think about how much weight a statistic like that should get in this particular case, or does it just expose a genuine conflict between two things I otherwise have good reason to trust?
