I'm currently TAing a seminar on aggregation in ethics, and it strikes me that Taurek's "Should the Numbers Count?" is usually taught as though the only alternative to his view is straightforward utilitarian summing. But Scanlon, who is just as resistant to interpersonal summing as Taurek is, tries to vindicate the intuition that we should still save the greater number through his 'tie-breaking' argument: pair each claimant in the smaller group against one claimant in the larger group, note that the paired claims cancel out, and let the larger group's unpaired, leftover claimants settle the matter. Critics have argued that this leftover claim is doing exactly the aggregative work Scanlon claims to avoid. Rather than have the dispute summarized from the outside, could you write a dialogue between a Taurekian who presses that objection and a defender of Scanlon's argument who has to answer it, with each side engaging the other's specific move rather than replaying the standard utilitarian-versus-Taurek debate?
