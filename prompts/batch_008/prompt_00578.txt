I'm about two-thirds through a novel about a sister and brother, and if I'm honest I've been quarrying it straight out of my own life more than I'd like to admit. My actual younger brother is bipolar, and the pattern for close to a decade now has been: he goes quiet for a few weeks, resurfaces manic or close to it, says something to me or our mom thats clearly meant to wound (and weirdly specific, like he's been saving it up for months), then a week or two later calls wanting to be close again like nothing happened. Everyone - my mom, my therapist, even him, when he's lucid - says some version of "that's the illness, not him, try not to take it personally." I believe that, mostly. But here's what's been bugging me: on the occasions I actually manage to not take it personally, when I successfully relate to him the way you'd relate to bad weather, or a car that keeps breaking down, something to plan around instead of someone to be hurt by, it doesn't feel like relief. It feels like I've quietly demoted him from brother to problem. So in practice I just oscillate - furious at someone who maybe can't help it, then coldly managing someone I maybe owe more than management to.

My writing group read a scene built on this and told me the sister character was "inconsistent" and needed to pick a lane emotionally, which annoyed me, because I think the inconsistency is the truest part of the whole thing. Before I decide how to actually resolve it for her arc though, I want to know what you honestly think about the underlying situation itself: is the anger the fair response and the detachment a cop-out, is it the other way round, or is bouncing between the two just what it looks like to keep loving someone whose worst behavior you can't fully explain away?
