Quick one for you — I've been re-reading Maimonides on sacrifice for a seminar and got stuck on something. In the Guide (III:32) he argues the whole sacrificial system was basically a divine concession: God let the Israelites keep offering animals because thats just what worship looked like to people steeped in ancient idolatry, and the real goal was to gradually wean them toward "higher" forms of worship like prayer and intellectual contemplation of God. On his view, sacrifice was never good in itself, just a rung on a ladder people happened to need at the time.

Here's the wrinkle: there's an actual ongoing project (Temple Institute, some Texas ranchers, etc.) using embryo transfer and genetic screening to try to breed a totally unblemished red heifer, since Numbers 19 requires one to produce the purification ash needed before Temple sacrifices can resume. A handful of candidate heifers have already been flown to Israel for this.

So given Maimonides' actual reasoning for why sacrifice existed in the first place, what do you think he'd say about this project? Does his own logic cut against reviving the practice now that we're supposedly past that rung of the ladder, or is there a way to read him as more sympathetic than you'd expect? Actually work through the tension instead of just restating his general position on sacrifice.
