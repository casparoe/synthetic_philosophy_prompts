Fell down an SEP rabbit hole this weekend, started from Judea Pearl's causal graph stuff (I do a lot of A/B test analysis at work) and ended up reading about this whole fight between "counterfactual" theories of causation (Lewis-style: C caused E iff E wouldn't have happened without C) and "interventionist" ones (Woodward/Pearl-ish: C causes E iff some intervention on C would change E, cashed out with structural equations).

Could you write me a dialogue between one philosopher from each camp, arguing over the classic Suzy-and-Billy rock throwing case (I think its called "late preemption")? They both throw rocks at a bottle, Suzy's gets there first and shatters it, Billy's would've shattered it a split second later if she hadn't thrown. Plain counterfactual dependence says Suzy's throw wasn't a cause, since the bottle shatters either way -- which is obviously wrong. Apparently the structural-equations people "solve" this by freezing the non-intrinsic stuff and isolating the actual process, but I've also read that this basically just relocates the problem rather then actually solving it.

Want them to really go at it, not just restate their own positions past each other -- the counterfactual theorist should push on whether "freezing" is principled or just ad hoc, and the interventionist needs real comebacks, not just the definition again. Both should be equally sharp, nobody's a strawman here.
