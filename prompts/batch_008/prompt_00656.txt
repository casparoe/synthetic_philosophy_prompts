Ok, this is going to sound like a very online-rationalist thing to admit, but here goes. For the past few months I've been keeping a nightly tracking spreadsheet for about a dozen traits I'm trying to cultivate (patience, honesty, not procrastinating, eating decently, etc). Each day I put a dot in a trait's column if I notice myself failing at it, and each week I pick one trait to focus on extra hard while still passively tracking the rest, then rotate to the next one the following week so backsliding on the old ones still gets caught. I'm sure I'm not the first person to invent this, it's basically a spreadsheet version of something people have apparently done for centuries.

Anyway, the thing I can't shake is what happened once I added "humility" to the list a few weeks back. Almost immediately I noticed that succeeding at it, like not mentioning an achievement, or letting someone else win an argument, made me feel quietly pleased with myself, which is obviously exactly backwards. So I tried correcting for that too, feeling bad about feeling pleased, and then caught myself being a little smug about *that* correction as well. Every level of the tracker seems to get colonized by the same vanity its supposed to be stamping out, and I can't find a stable floor to stand on. My roommate says I should just quietly drop "humility" from the spreadsheet and keep tracking everything else, but that feels like conceding the interesting part instead of actually resolving it.

So: is there an actually coherent way to pursue a trait like this through deliberate self-monitoring, or is explicit tracking just structurally the wrong tool for humility specifically, in a way it isn't for, say, flossing? And is my roommate's "just drop it" secretly the mature move here, or a cop-out dressed up as pragmatism? Would love your actual read on the logic of the situation, not just a generic "be patient with yourself" pep talk.
