So there's this idea of 'moral trade' — e.g. someone who cares a lot about animal welfare offers to donate more to poverty charities if their friend (who doesn't care about animal welfare but does care about poverty) agrees to eat less meat. Pitch is that both people come out ahead by their own values even though they don't agree on what matters, kind of like how ordinary trade doesn't require people to agree on taste.

Thing I can't get past: doesn't this whole scheme depend on knowing what the other person would've done anyway? Kinda like the carbon offset problem — if the friend was gonna cut back on meat next year regardless, the "trade" isn't creating any extra moral value, its just a transfer dressed up as a deal. And the usual fixes for trust issues in a trade (escrow, contracts, checking up on each other) can verify someone kept their end of the bargain, but none of them can verify a counterfactual like that. Is this a fatal problem for the whole idea, or is there a way of thinking about it where it doesn't matter as much as it seems?
