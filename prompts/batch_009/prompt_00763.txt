Retired physicist here, spending my retirement finally reading philosophy instead of running detectors. Something's been bugging me about the "direction of time" stuff. The standard line is that the fundamental laws are time-symmetric, so the thermodynamic arrow can't come from them directly and has to be explained statistically, via some special low-entropy "past hypothesis." But that's not actually true anymore, is it? We've known since the 60s that neutral kaons violate CP, and by the CPT theorem that means time-reversal symmetry is violated too, at the fundamental level. So there IS a genuine temporal asymmetry built into the laws after all. Doesnt that mean the whole "entropy increase is just a statistical fluke needing special initial conditions" story is basically out of date, and the second law is grounded in fundamental physics after all? Why do philosophers of physics still write like this is an open problem?
