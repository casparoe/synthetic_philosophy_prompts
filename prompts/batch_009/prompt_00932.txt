For the past couple of years, I've handled morally tricky situations roughly the way I handle technical ones: I find someone who's better at this than I am and go with their call. If my car makes a strange noise, I don't try to diagnose it myself; I just trust my mechanic. Increasingly, I've started doing the same thing with ethics. My friend Nadia is unusually good at working through messy interpersonal situations, and over the last few years I've asked her about things like whether to tell my sister that her fiancé was flirting with someone else at a party, whether I was obligated to mention a security deposit my landlord had clearly forgotten he owed me, and how to handle a friend who'd told me something worrying in confidence. In every case, Nadia's answer held up well once things played out, noticeably better than whatever I would have worked out on my own. At this point, I mostly just ask her what to do and do it, the same way I'd take my accountant's word on a tax question rather than work through the tax code myself.

My friend Priya, who's finishing a philosophy degree, recently told me she thinks this is a mistake, not because Nadia is unreliable, but because leaning on someone else's judgment for a moral question is different in kind from leaning on their judgment for a technical one, even if the two of them are equally trustworthy. I found this hard to credit, since I can't think of a single case where Nadia has steered me wrong. But trying to defend myself, I noticed something odd: when her advice works out, I don't feel like I've done anything especially praiseworthy, whereas if I'd followed her advice and things had gone badly, I suspect I'd still feel at fault, in a way I wouldn't about a botched repair my mechanic talked me into. I can't tell whether that feeling is tracking something real and important about morality, or whether it's just squeamishness that wouldn't survive scrutiny. Is there an actual, principled difference between taking someone's word on a moral question and taking someone's word on a technical one, or is Priya just holding morality to a standard she wouldn't apply anywhere else?
