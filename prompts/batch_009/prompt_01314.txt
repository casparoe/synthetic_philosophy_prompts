Write an essay on whether Thomas Aquinas's writings support a right to tyrannicide exercised by private individuals, and if so, under what conditions.

Aquinas addresses the killing of tyrants in at least three texts spanning his career: the early Commentary on the Sentences (II Sent., d. 44, q. 2, a. 2), which appears to countenance killing a usurping tyrant when there is no superior authority left to pronounce judgment on him; the Summa Theologiae's discussion of sedition (II-II, q. 42, a. 2), which treats resistance to a genuinely tyrannical regime as falling outside the sin of sedition; and the mature De Regimine Principum (De Regno I.6), which stresses the dangers of private initiative and channels remedy through public and communal authority instead. Commentators disagree about whether these passages trace a real development -- even a reversal -- in Aquinas's thinking, or whether they can be reconciled as consistent applications of a single doctrine once the relevant distinctions (for instance, usurper versus lawful ruler turned tyrannical, or solitary action versus communal uprising) are properly drawn.

Weigh the textual and interpretive evidence on both sides, but don't end on a noncommittal note -- defend a specific, textually grounded verdict about what Aquinas is best read as permitting, to whom, and against which kind of tyrant.
