Been down a rabbit hole with Lara Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility (REU) theory from Risk and Rationality and I think I'm missing something basic. Her big pitch is that REU lets you be genuinely, rationally risk-avoidant in a way that violates independance -- but isn't that basically just... having a concave utility function? Plain old diminishing marginal utility already gets you "prefer a certain $50 over a coinflip between $0 and $100." So what is the risk function actually doing that a concave u doesn't, and why does Buchak think independence is the axiom to ditch rather than something else? Would love a concrete toy example with real numbers where an REU-maximizer and a standard concave-EU-maximizer make different choices -- ideally one that makes clear the difference isn't just "more risk averse" but a different *kind* of risk aversion. This keeps coming up in EA-ish discussions of risk aversion around x-risk and I'd like to actually get it rather than nod along.
