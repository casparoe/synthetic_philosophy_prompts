I'm the secretary for my school's robotics club, which means I take fairly detailed notes at our meetings. Something that happened this year has been bothering me, especially since I recently reread last year's notes and noticed what feels like a mirror-image case. This year, our president decided to spend the rest of our budget rebuilding the drivetrain right before regionals because she was convinced it would raise our score. When I pointed out that doing this would leave nothing for the outreach demo we run every spring for the elementary school down the street (a lot of current members say that demo is the reason they joined the club in the first place), she said, almost word for word, "I don't care about the outreach thing, I just want us to place higher this year." She spent the money, we cancelled outreach for the first time in the club's history, and our score only improved slightly anyway. When people found out why the demo got cut, most of the club said she had killed it "on purpose," even though helping or hurting outreach was never really part of what she was going for either way.

Last year, though, something close to the opposite happened. That year's president wanted to buy a professional-looking display setup for our booth at competitions, and knew that doing so would leave just enough money left over for the new team T-shirts people had been asking for all season. He told us flatly, "I don't care about the shirts, I just want us to look more professional," bought the display, and we ended up with the shirts as a result. Nobody in the club said he had gotten us the shirts on purpose; people just treated it as a nice coincidence.

Both presidents knew exactly what would happen to that other line item, said outright that they didn't care about it either way, and were proven right about the tradeoff. Yet the club reacted to the two situations in completely opposite ways, and a friend and I ended up debating it at lunch this week when I brought it up again. Something about that asymmetry feels off to me, but I can't quite figure out whether it's actually unfair or whether there's some principled reason "on purpose" ought to work differently in these two cases. Is there a real difference between them, or were we all just letting how we felt about the outcome decide what we were willing to call intentional?
