Ok so I had a whole two hour argument with my friend Dana last night about whether relations can just hold between things or whether they need some further glue to attach them to their terms, and it ended with her pulling up this bit from Bradley's Appearance and Reality (chapter 3, "Relation and Quality") that neither of us could fully unpack:

"...a relation without terms seems mere verbiage; and terms appear, therefore, to be something beyond their relation. ... And hence a relation, we must say, without qualities is nothing. But how the relation can stand to the qualities is, on the other side, unintelligible. If it is nothing to the qualities, then they are not related at all; and, if so, as we saw, they have ceased to be qualities, and their relation is a nonentity. But if it is to be something to them, then clearly we now shall require a new connecting relation. For the relation hardly can be the mere adjective of one or both of its terms; or, at least, as such it seems indefensible. And, being something itself, if it does not itself bear a relation to the terms, in what intelligible way will it succeed in being anything to them? But here again we are hurried off into the eddy of a hopeless process, since we are forced to go on finding new relations without end. The links are united by a link, and this bond of union is a link which also has two ends; and these require each a fresh link to connect them with the old. The problem is to find how the relation can stand to its qualities; and this problem is insoluble."

Dana's take is that the whole force of this passage is that the chain never terminates, so a and b never actually end up related because the job of relating just keeps getting punted down the line forever. I think its something else - that the very first move (needing a brand new relation just to hook the original relation up to its terms) already shows the scheme is broken, so it wouldn't matter even if the regress somehow stopped after one step. Who's actually closer to what Bradley's doing here? And is this basically the same worry people later raise about the "unity of the proposition" (like why "Socrates is wise" is one fact rather than just Socrates and wisdom sitting next to each other), or is that a genuinely seperate puzzle that only looks similar from a distance?
