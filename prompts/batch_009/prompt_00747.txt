Okay so I've been in a reading group with some other phil PhD students working through the "intertheoretic comparisons" chapters of MacAskill and co's stuff on moral uncertainty -- basically, if you've got credence spread across utilitarianism and some deontological view, how do you even compare how much each theory "wants" a given action, given that they don't obviously share a unit of value? A friend of mine in the econ department heard me describe this and immediately said it sounded exactly like the interpersonal comparison of utility problem from social choice theory -- the thing that shows up in Harsanyi's aggregation theorem and in the whole literature following Arrow's impossibility result about whether interpersonal comparability is what you'd need to escape it. Turns out MacAskill is totally aware of this and even borrows voting-theoretic tools (variance voting, Borda-ish methods) to try to make intertheoretic comparisons work, which only made me more curious about how far the analogy really goes.

So here's what I keep getting stuck on: is "comparing choiceworthiness across moral theories under uncertainty" and "comparing utility across persons for social choice" the same problem wearing different clothes -- so that an Arrow-style impossibility result should worry moral uncertainty people just as much as it worries social choice theorists -- or is there a principled disanalogy somewhere that lets one be tractable while the other isn't? My best guess at a disanalogy is that persons at least plausibly share a common currency of wellbeing even where it's unmeasurable, wheras rival moral theories might not even agree that a common currency of "choiceworthiness" exists in the first place (a Kantian's notion of a constraint violation doesn't obviously live on the same scale as a utilitarian's aggregate welfare, even in principle). But I'm honestly not sure that holds up under pressure. Could you actually dig into this and tell me whether fixes like variance normalization (or just retreating to "my favourite theory") are doing real philosophical work, or just quietly smuggling in an answer to the hard version of the question?
