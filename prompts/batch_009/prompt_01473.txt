So I've been nerd-sniped by Parfit's teleporter thought experiment again, specifically the "branch-line" version. Setup: the scanner on earth doesnt just destroy you and build a copy on Mars -- it makes the copy fine, but the original body back on earth doesn't get destroyed, just badly damaged, so you (the original) are still gonna die within the week regardless. Parfit's move here is that this should feel basically just as good as ordinary survival, since all that matters is Relation R (pyschological continuity/connectedness) and that holds between you and the Mars copy -- identity itself was never doing any real work. As a dev this always felt like forking a process, letting the child run off with all the same state, then sigkilling the parent and saying "relax, the fork survived, nothing bad happened to you."

Argue that the branch-line case actually cuts against Parfit here, not for him: that the sheer intuitive horror of being the guy stuck on earth is real evidence that identity is doing work Relation R can't capture, and that Parfit's "you should feel fine about this" move just stipulates away the intuition he needs to explain rather than actually explaining it.
