I do not have any formal background in philosophy or mathematics, but I have spent a long time thinking about Thomson's lamp on my own, and I believe I have worked out a resolution that seems to get overlooked. If you represent turning the lamp on as adding 1 and turning it off as subtracting 1, the running total after each flip is exactly the partial sum of the series 1 - 1 + 1 - 1 + ..., where a total of 1 means the lamp is on and a total of 0 means it is off. It is a standard fact that this series has a Cesàro sum of 1/2, so it seems to me that the supposed paradox actually has a precise answer: at the two-minute mark, the lamp is "half on," in exactly the sense that the series sums to 1/2. Doesn't this mean the puzzle has effectively been solved by basic real analysis, and that philosophers who still treat it as an open question simply haven't caught up with the mathematics?
