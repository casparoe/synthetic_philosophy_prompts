Been going down a Berkeley rabbit hole lately. Started with the classic 'esse est percipi' idea, that nothing exists unless its being perceived, and then found out he also wrote this whole pamphlet called The Querist about money, where he says coins don't have any real intrinsic value and are basically just 'tickets' for keeping track of exchanges. That seems like a pretty obvious extension of his immaterialism to me — if matter isn't even real, a gold coin obviously can't have 'real' value either, so of course he'd land on money being just an arbitrary token. So how come none of the philosophy overviews I've read ever mention the Querist or connect it back to the perception stuff? Feels like a pretty big miss if his whole economic theory just falls out of his main philosophy like that.
