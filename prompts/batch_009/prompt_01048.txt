In longtermist discussions on LessWrong and the EA Forum, I frequently see Cowen and Parfit's argument cited to show that a positive rate of pure time preference is indefensible: there's no principled reason why the mere date at which a benefit occurs should reduce its moral weight. This strikes me as clearly correct. But when I look at how this plays out in actual cost-benefit analysis, even Nicholas Stern — usually treated as the reasonable, pro-future-generations side of his dispute with Nordhaus over climate policy — still discounted future consumption at roughly 1.4% per year rather than 0%, and over a century that compounds into an enormous difference in how much weight future people get. Doesn't Stern's own choice of a positive rate show that he never really accepted the Cowen–Parfit argument, and that even the "ethical" side of the climate-discounting debate ends up smuggling pure time preference back in under a more palatable label?
