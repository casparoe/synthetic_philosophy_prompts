A friend and I had a long argument last weekend about our city's housing debate. The council is considering approving thousands of small, basic apartment units that would let far more people afford to live here, but the added density would noticeably worsen things like commute times, noise, and access to green space for current residents and newcomers alike. My friend's position is that we should keep approving this kind of development as long as life here is still clearly worth choosing for each new resident, since that only adds to the total amount of good lives being lived, and that holding back growth just to protect a more pleasant but smaller city is really just selfishness dressed up as prudence. I couldn't pinpoint exactly what was wrong with his reasoning, but it still didn't sit right with me. Was he right?
