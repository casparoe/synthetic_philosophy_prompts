So I fell down a Henry Sidgwick rabbit hole recently (the Methods of Ethics guy, patron saint of half the EA/utilitarian crowd), and the thing that really got me is his psychical research stuff. He was literally the first president of the Society for Psychical Research, and spent decades of his life going to seances and testing mediums -- which sounds like a weird hobby for a serious ethicist, except it wasn't a hobby at all. His ethics runs into this problem he calls the "dualism of practical reason": he can't find a way for pure reason to show that impartial benevolence beats rational egoism, unless you throw in a "cosmic postulate," like an afterlife where virtue actually gets rewarded. So the ghost-hunting was him trying to find that postulate empirically. And the kicker is that his own scrupulousness kept ruining it for him -- he and his circle were the ones who caught the era's star medium, Eusapia Palladino, cheating at their own sittings in Cambridge in 1895.

Can you write me a short story built around that bind? Doesnt have to be literally Sidgwick, a fictional stand-in works fine, but I want the actual structure of the problem doing real work in the story -- someone whose whole ethical framework needs a specific empirical fact to be true, and whose honesty as an investigator is exactly what keeps disproving it. A few pages is plenty, and don't feel like you need to resolve it neatly at the end -- that'd kind of defeat the point.
