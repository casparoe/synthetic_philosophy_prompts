Last night, over dinner, a friend of mine who studied economics in graduate school and I ended up in a long, surprisingly heated argument that started as a joke and was never really resolved. For the past year I've told everyone, including myself, that finishing a short story collection is my top personal priority outside of work. When she pressed me on it, though, it turned out that on almost every evening where I actually had a choice between writing and something else — reading, going out, scrolling on my phone — I chose the something else. Her position was that this settles the matter: since none of us can climb inside anyone else's head, the only thing "preference" can sensibly mean is whatever ordering best fits a person's actual choices, and by that standard, whatever I consistently pick when I'm free to do otherwise simply is what I most want, full stop. On her view there is no further fact about my "true" priorities hovering above my choices that those choices could be failing to track; my insisting that I "really" want to finish the book, despite a year of behavior pointing the other way, is just a flattering story I tell myself, not a report of some hidden preference underneath it.

I pushed back with the website blocker I installed specifically to stop myself from choosing social media over writing at night — doesn't the fact that I engineered my own future choice set show that I prefer writing at some level that matters? She said this only shows that installing the blocker was my most preferred action at the moment I installed it, and that it says nothing about what I "really" prefer at 9pm when I'm actually tempted; on her account, each moment simply has its own preference, with no privileged fact about which one counts as the real me. I couldn't locate the flaw in this, but it also seems to entail that nobody could ever be honestly mistaken about their own priorities, or fail to get what they most want, which strikes me as absurd. Was she basically right that choice just is preference and there is nothing more to it, or is there a defensible sense in which what I keep choosing can come apart from what I actually want or value?
