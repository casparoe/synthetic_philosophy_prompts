so i've been reading Dummett's On Immigration and Refugees for a chapter on migration ethics, and the bit that's been bugging me is his line about a nation's "right not to be submerged" by immigration. He calls Thatcher's "swamped" comment utterly ridiculous, then turns around and defends basically the same idea in more careful language, insisting the right is "of extremely limited application" and wasnt remotely triggered by anything happening in Britain when he wrote the book. Say he'd lived another decade and watched Sweden take in somewhere around 160,000 asylum seekers in 2015 alone, close to 2% of the population in a single year and by far the highest per capita in the EU. Would his own stated criteria have actually counted that as a genuine case of submersion, or would he have found some principled reason it still doesn't qualify? mostly trying to work out whether the concept is doing real philosophical work for him or whether it just collapses back into Thatcher's line once the numbers get big enough.
