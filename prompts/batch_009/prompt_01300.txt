Random tangent that's been eating my week: my reading group (we're doing nonfiction this quarter, god help us) got into this Derek Parfit thing called the Hitchhiker case, and now I can't stop turning it over as material for my next story collection. Basic version: you're dying of thirst in the desert, a driver stops, but he wont take payment up front and he's freakishly good at telling when someone's lying, so he'll only give you the ride if he's convinced you'll actually pay him once you're safe in town. Here's the annoying part - once you ARE safe in town, there's no self-interested reason left to pay up, the ride already happened, so a purely "rational" person reasons their way straight to stiffing him. Except the driver knew you'd reason exactly that way, so he never would've picked you up to begin with. So the only people who make it out of the desert alive are the ones who were never going to run that calculation, they were just always going to pay. Parfit has an essay that literally calls this "rational irrationality," and apparently it connects to Gauthier's stuff about people who can't help keeping their word doing better, over a lifetime, than people who coldly recalculate their interests at every single step.

Anyway, I don't want to write the desert version, it's the intro-philosophy-class example and everyone's seen it a hundred times. What I want is a short story (800ish words, doesn't need to be exact) that steals the skeleton but sets it somewhere totally different - could be a custody dispute, a smuggling job, a marriage proposal, first contact with something non-human, genuinely whatever setting appeals to you most. The one part I don't want lost in translation: someone needs help, or trust, or rescue, right now, and they can only get it because the other party can somehow correctly tell, in advance, that this person is the type who'll follow through later even once following through has stopped benefiting them and might actively hurt them. Please don't let that "somehow" just be a background check or a reputation system though, that's a related but much more boring problem than the one Parfit's actually poking at. Once you've written the story, tack on a sentence or two after it telling me exactly which beat is doing the "hitchhiker" work, just so I know it wasn't an accident.
