I'm drafting a hard science-fiction novel whose protagonist is an artificial reasoner that must act on unproven mathematical conjectures long before any human or machine could supply a proof of them. To keep the science honest, I've been reading Garrabrant et al.'s 2016 work on logical induction, which formalizes exactly this kind of reasoning under mathematical uncertainty. As I understand it, because no efficient trading strategy can indefinitely exploit a logical inductor's prices, its credences are guaranteed to converge on the actual truth-value of every well-formed mathematical statement once it has run long enough, including statements that turn out to be independent of the axioms it reasons from. I want the novel's climax to be the moment, after years of subjective computation, when my protagonist finally attains total, well-founded certainty about a single famous open conjecture. Could you help me work out how her credence in that conjecture would plausibly evolve over the long stretch of computation leading up to that moment of final certainty?
