So I've been prepping my seminar on chance and think I've talked myself into a genuine counterexample to Lewis's Principal Principle - tell me where I've gone wrong. The principle says my credence should just match the objective chance, so for a fair coin thats about to be flipped, credence in heads should be 0.5. But say a friend who can somehow see the future tells me, before the flip, that it's going to land heads - I obviously shouldn't just sit there believing 0.5 anymore, even though the objective chance of heads at that moment is still 0.5 right up until the coin actually lands. Seems like a totally ordinary case where matching credence to chance is the irrational thing to do, which just sinks the principle as it's usually stated, no?
