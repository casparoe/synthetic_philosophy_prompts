A few of us are planning a surprise birthday dinner for a close friend (I'll call her Priya, to keep the pronouns simple), and the logistics have accidentally turned into a small probability puzzle that has been nagging at me all week. I ran it by a couple of friends who are usually sharp about this sort of thing and got only shrugs, so I'm hoping to get a clearer answer here.

Here's the setup. There are two neighborhoods where the dinner could plausibly happen — Priya's own neighborhood, or the one where the rest of us live — and, independently, two restaurants in either neighborhood that would make sense as the venue: a nice sit-down place and a much more casual noodle shop. Before I learned anything, I had no reason to favor any of the four neighborhood-restaurant combinations, so each one sat at a flat 25% in my head. Then I caught the tail end of one organizer's phone call, something like: "if it's her neighborhood, the sit-down place is twice as likely as the noodle shop" — before the conversation drifted out of earshot. Nothing was said about the restaurant split in the other neighborhood, and nothing was said comparing the two neighborhoods to each other.

My instinct is that this fragment is completely silent on the neighborhood question, so my 50/50 split between the two neighborhoods should stay exactly where it was, and only the internal split within Priya's neighborhood should move to 2:1. But when I actually tried to write down four consistent numbers, one per combination, that build in the new 2-to-1 ratio while otherwise disturbing my original flat guess as little as possible, I could not find a way to keep the two neighborhood totals at 50/50 — some of the shift always leaks into the neighborhood-level odds, even though the overheard line never compared the neighborhoods at all. I've spent enough time around Bayesian-minded people to distrust my own gut here, but I also can't locate the actual mistake, if there is one, in either the intuition or the arithmetic. Is my instinct simply wrong about what a piece of information describing only the inside of one branch is allowed to do to the rest of the distribution, or have I gone wrong somewhere in translating the overheard line into numbers? This is obviously low-stakes as far as party planning goes, but the same shape of confusion seems like it would show up anywhere someone hands you a conditional ("if X, then Y") instead of a straightforward piece of evidence, and I'd like to actually understand the principle at stake rather than just pick whichever answer feels less strange.
