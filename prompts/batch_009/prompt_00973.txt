In the seventeenth discussion of *The Incoherence of the Philosophers*, al-Ghazālī uses the case of fire touching cotton to argue that what we call a necessary causal connection is in truth only a habit (*ʿāda*) God has so far maintained and could withhold at any moment; Averroes takes up this very passage in *The Incoherence of the Incoherence* to insist that fire and cotton have genuine natures, and that denying this makes the world unintelligible to reason. For my seminar on classical Islamic philosophy, please write a dialogue of around 700 words between an occasionalist and an Aristotelian who are still arguing over this exact case. The occasionalist should press the point that no contradiction follows from supposing that fire fails to burn cotton, and should explain why this already settles, on their view of modality, that the connection is contingent rather than necessary; the Aristotelian should respond that this notion of necessity misses what matters, namely whether a thing's causal powers belong to its essence, and that stripping fire and cotton of such powers undermines the very possibility of scientific knowledge. Both speakers are equally accomplished philosophers: each turn should answer the specific move just made rather than restate a settled position, and the dialogue should close with the disagreement still genuinely open.
