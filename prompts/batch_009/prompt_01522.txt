One of my students brought this up in office hours and I can't shake it. Her dad took part in one of those paid online decision-making studies, the kind where you make a bunch of choices between bets and one gets randomly picked afterward to actually pay out. First choice: (A) a guaranteed $1,000, versus (B) 89% chance of $1,000, 10% chance of $5,000, and 1% chance of nothing. He took the guaranteed grand, no gamble for him. Fine, seems resonable. Later in a seperate part of the same session: (C) 11% chance of $1,000, otherwise nothing, versus (D) 10% chance of $5,000, otherwise nothing. This time he went with D. Her brother, a stats minor, told him afterward that picking A and D together is just incoherent -- if he was cautious enough to pass up the great odds in B, he should've played it safe with C too -- and basically accused him of being irrational. Dad felt kind of insulted but couldn't say why his son was wrong, if he even was. Is the son right that there's no way to defend both picks at once, or is he missing something?
