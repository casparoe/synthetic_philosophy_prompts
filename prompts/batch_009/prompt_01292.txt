Here is Dana Scott's widely used reconstruction of the axioms, definitions, and theorems making up Kurt Gödel's modal-logic version of the ontological argument, based on notes Gödel showed Scott in the early 1970s (Gödel never published them himself; they appeared only posthumously in his *Collected Works*):

Axiom 1. Either a property or its negation is positive, but not both: ∀φ[P(¬φ) ↔ ¬P(φ)]

Axiom 2. A property necessarily implied by a positive property is positive: ∀φ∀ψ[(P(φ) ∧ □∀x(φ(x)→ψ(x))) → P(ψ)]

Theorem 1. Positive properties are possibly exemplified: ∀φ[P(φ) → ◇∃x φ(x)]

Definition 1. x is God-like (Gx) iff x has every positive property: G(x) ↔ ∀φ[P(φ)→φ(x)]

Axiom 3. The property of being God-like is itself positive: P(G)

Corollary 1. Possibly, God exists: ◇∃x G(x)

Axiom 4. Positive properties are necessarily positive: ∀φ[P(φ)→□P(φ)]

Definition 2. φ is an essence of x iff x has φ, and φ necessarily entails every other property of x: (φ ess x) ↔ φ(x) ∧ ∀ψ[ψ(x) → □∀y(φ(y)→ψ(y))]

Theorem 2. Being God-like is an essence of anything that is God-like: ∀x[G(x)→(G ess x)]

Definition 3. x necessarily exists (NE x) iff every essence of x is necessarily exemplified by something: NE(x) ↔ ∀φ[(φ ess x) → □∃y φ(y)]

Axiom 5. Necessary existence is itself a positive property: P(NE)

Theorem 3. Necessarily, God exists: □∃x G(x)
