I spent most of my career as a field geologist (lots of stratigraphy, some structural work), and now that I'm retired I've been filling the time by reading my way through philosophy and history of science instead of rocks. Given how much grief my own field used to catch from the young-earth crowd over how literally to read Genesis, I got curious about the original template for all these science-vs-scripture fights and picked up Galileo's 1615 letter to the Grand Duchess Christina. Most of it I can follow without too much strain, the "two books" and accommodation stuff is dense but graspable, but I hit a wall near the end, in the section where he's actually working through the Joshua "sun, stand still" miracle. I found an old 1661 English translation online (so please forgive the spelling, it's not me), and this is the bit that's got me and a couple of retired-engineer friends stumped over coffee:

"Which businesse, if the Celestial Motions be taken according to the Ptolomaick Systeme, can never any wayes happen, for that the Sun moving thorow the Ecliptick, according to the order of the Signes, which is from East to West (which is that which maketh Day and Night) it is a thing manifest, that the Sun ceasing its true and proper Motion, the day would become shorter and not longer; and that on the contrary, the way to lengthen it would be to hasten and velocitate the Suns motion; insomuch that to cause the Sun to stay above the Horizon for some time, in one and the same place, without declining towards the West, it would be necessary to accelerate its motion in such a manner as that it might seem equal to that of the Primum Mobile, which would be an accelerating it about three hundred and sixty times more than ordinary. If therefore Joshuah had had an intention that his words should be taken in their pure and proper signification, he would have bid the Sun to have accelerated its Motion so, that the Rapture of the Primum Mobile might not carry it to the West."

Then, after a digression where he notes that Dionysius the Areopagite and Augustine already thought it was the whole heavens that stopped rather than just the sun, he adds this, which confuses me even more:

"I come in the second place to consider how the Solar Body, although stable in one constant place, doth nevertheless revolve in itself, making an entire Conversion in the space of a Month, or thereabouts, as I conceive I have solidly demonstrated in my Letters Delle Machie Solari. Thirdly, if we respect the Nobility of the Sun, and his being the Fountain of Light, by which not onely the Moon and Earth, but all the other Planets become illuminated, I conceive that it will be no unlogicall Illation to say, that it, as the Grand Minister of Nature, and in a certain sense the Soul and Heart of the World, infuseth into the other Bodies which environ it not onely Light, but Motion also, by revolving in itself: so that in the same manner that the motion of the Heart of an Animal ceasing, all the other motions of its Members would cease, so, the Conversion of the Sun ceasing, the Conversions of all the Planets would stand still."

I can tell he's trying to argue that the Copernican picture actually handles the Joshua miracle more elegantly than the Ptolemaic one, but I can't reconstruct the actual mechanics well enough to see why. Could you walk me through, in plain terms: (1) why, on the Ptolemaic system, stopping the sun would supposedly make the day shorter rather than longer, and where that "three hundred and sixty times" figure comes from; (2) what work his sunspot-derived discovery that the sun rotates is actually doing in the Copernican fix, i.e. is the "heart of the world" business meant as real causal physics or more of a rhetorical flourish; and (3) whether you think this whole maneuver sits oddly with his own stated principle earlier in the letter that scripture shouldn't be stretched to fit an unproven physical hypothesis. He seems to be doing exactly that here in reverse, using Joshua as a selling point for Copernicus rather then just neutralizing it as an objection. Am I misreading the tension there, or is that a real inconsistency in his position?
