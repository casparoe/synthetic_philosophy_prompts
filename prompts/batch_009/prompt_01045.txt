My girlfriend has gotten really into astrology over the last few months – full birth chart, she even did a synastry comparison for the two of us on our anniversary, the whole thing – and I usually just let it slide because its harmless fun. But last week she sent me some infographic claiming that Johannes Kepler (yes, THE Kepler, elliptical orbits guy) wrote an entire book defending astrology against people who wanted to ban it outright, something called Tertius Interveniens or close to that. Her argument was basically: "if one of the greatest scientists who ever lived took this seriously enough to write a whole book defending it, maybe you should stop being so smug about my rising sign."

I didn't have a great comeback in the moment, but it's been bugging me since. I'd always just assumed Kepler churned out horoscopes purely for cash because his job as Imperial Mathematician required it, and that he privately thought the whole practice was nonsense - kind of like how a modern astronomer might tolerate the newspaper horoscope column without believing a word of it. Now shes got me second-guessing that. Was Kepler actually a believer in astrology in anything like the sense my girlfriend means it - zodiac signs, mercury retrograde wrecking your week, compatibility by star sign, all that - or is "he wrote a whole book defending astrology" doing a lot more work in her argument than it should? Kind of want the real story before round two of this debate happens.
