So I'm drafting a section of my quals paper on accuracy-first epistemology / epistemic utility theory (Joyce, Pettigrew, etc) and I keep getting stuck on something that feels like a genuine problem with the view, unless im missing something obvious. If credences are only epistemically good insofar as they're accurate, shouldn't the norm that falls out of the theory just be "go as extreme as possible," since a maximally confident credence in whatever's actually true is literally more accurate than any hedged credence in that same truth? But the literature always ends up recommending calibration instead, i.e. proportioning your confidence to the evidence rather than maxing it out. Feels like the real normative work is being done by something other than accuracy, and "accuracy" is just along for the ride. Is that a legit worry about the framework, or am I misunderstanding how the scoring rules are supposed to be doing the work here?
