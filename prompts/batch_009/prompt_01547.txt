so i've been kind of obsessed with this Newton's bucket thing ever since i watched a video about it, and i went and actually dug up the original passage because i wanted to see what the old man actually wrote instead of just the pop-science summary of it. found this bit below (its from an old 1846 English translation, so sorry if some of the wording feels clunky):

"If a vessel, hung by a long cord, is so often turned about that the cord is strongly twisted, then filled with water, and held at rest together with the water; after, by the sudden action of another force, it is whirled about in the contrary way, and while the cord is untwisting itself, the vessel continues for some time this motion; the surface of the water will at first be plain, as before the vessel began to move; but the vessel by gradually communicating its motion to the water, will make it begin sensibly to revolve, and recede by little and little, and ascend to the sides of the vessel, forming itself into a concave figure... This ascent of the water shows its endeavour to recede from the axis of its motion; and the true and absolute circular motion of the water, which is here directly contrary to the relative, discovers itself, and may be measured by this endeavour. ... And therefore, this endeavour does not depend upon any translation of the water in respect to ambient bodies, nor can true circular motion be defined by such translation. ...; but relative motions...are altogether destitute of any real effect. ... It is indeed a matter of great difficulty to discover, and effectually to distinguish, the true motions of particular bodies from the apparent; because the parts of that immovable space in which these motions are performed, do by no means come under the observations of our senses."

my own reading of this was basically: the water climbing up the sides is like a physical readout proving space itself is a real "thing" out there, kind of like an invisible tank that the whole universe sits inside of, and the water is basically rubbing up against the walls of that tank, that's why it goes concave even once it's not rotating relative to the bucket anymore. but then that very last sentence kind of wrecks my theory, where he straight up admits you cant ever actually sense or observe this "immovable space" with your senses. so if we can never directly detect it, how does watching some water crawl up a bucket wall count as proof that its really out there, rather than just proof that somethings going on that we haven't explained yet? it feels like hes trying to have it both ways, space is 100% real AND totally undetectable at the same time. is there a step of the logic im missing here, or is this actually a genuine weak spot that people have argued about for ages? my buddy who's way more into physics than me just says "that's basically the thing that set up general relativity" but that doesn't really answer my actual question lol.
