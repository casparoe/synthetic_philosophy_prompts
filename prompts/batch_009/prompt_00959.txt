Aumann's derivation of correlated equilibrium from Bayesian rationality rests on a common prior ranging over the full action profile, including each player's own eventual choice. Gul objects that positing a "prior stage" at which two players held identical beliefs about player 1's own future move is not just psychologically implausible but antithetical to the Savage-style subjectivism the common prior assumption is supposed to rest on, since Savage himself denied that equally informed, rational people must converge on the same probabilities. For a dissertation chapter on the epistemic foundations of equilibrium concepts, please argue that this "prior view" of the common prior assumption can be read as fully consistent with Savage's personalist conception of probability, even when the priors in question range over a player's own future actions.
