For the past several national election cycles, a friend of mine and I have kept up a small tradition: each of us puts down a modest amount of money on how we think the race will go, taking opposite sides whenever we disagree. I have no formal background in economics or philosophy—I just follow the news closely and have always enjoyed the ritual almost as much as the election itself. Lately, though, something about this arrangement has started to bother me, and I haven't been able to reason my way out of it, so I'd like to lay out the problem and hear what you make of it.

My friend is genuinely careful about this sort of thing: he follows the same polls and forecasts I do, he doesn't bet out of loyalty or wishful thinking, and over the years he has been right about as often as I have. Neither of us needs the money, and I don't think either of us bets for the thrill of it—if anything, we'd each rather keep our cash and simply be proven right for free. What nags at me is this: if he's willing to take the side I think is the safer bet, doesn't his willingness alone tell me something? He has seen what I've seen and thought about it just as hard, and he still wants to bet against my judgment, so I seem to have a reason to be a little less sure of myself than I was a moment before. But he can reason the same way about me: he knows that I know all this about him, and that I'll factor his willingness into my own confidence, so my continuing to offer the bet should make him a little less sure too, by that very same logic. Carried all the way through, it seems like two people who each think the other is basically reasonable, and who each roughly know what the other knows, should end up talking themselves out of ever placing the bet—yet every four years we just shake hands on it, none of this reasoning ever actually crossing our minds. I notice the same uneasy feeling when I read about people actively trading stocks, or risking money on those political forecasting sites where you can bet on real-world events: someone equally well-informed is always sitting on the other side of the trade, and I don't see why that doesn't give everyone pause.

Is there a flaw in how I'm reasoning through this, or does it point to something genuinely puzzling about betting, trading, and disagreement between people who each take the other's judgment seriously? I would like to actually understand what's going on here, rather than conclude that I'm simply overthinking a harmless bet.
