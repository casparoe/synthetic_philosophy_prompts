I've noticed the same unsettling pattern twice in my career. On two separate occasions, I've joined a group with strong shared convictions — first a startup evangelical about one "obviously correct" technology stack, then an online community built around a fairly fringe investing philosophy — and each time I started out neutral, even mildly skeptical, about its core views. Yet on both occasions, before I had engaged with a single one of the group's actual arguments, I could already predict with some confidence that if I stuck around for six months, I would end up believing whatever everyone around me believed. Both times, I was right.

Here's what I can't work out: given that I could already foresee my own future opinion, would it have been rational to just adopt that opinion immediately, on the grounds that I was going to arrive there eventually anyway? My instinct says no, but I can't reconcile that instinct with cases where deferring to my anticipated future opinion seems clearly fine — for instance, I would happily trust the judgment of the version of me with three more years of production experience over my current take on a hard architectural decision I'm wrestling with today. What actually distinguishes these two kinds of cases, and should the first one worry me, as a sign that something was already going wrong in my reasoning even before I had joined either group?
