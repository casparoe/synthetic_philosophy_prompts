Okay, this is going to sound random, but hear me out. A few weeks ago I took my little cousin to the science museum and we caught the Foucault pendulum demo, and the guy running it said something like "the pendulum isn't actually changing direction, the floor is rotating underneath it relative to the distant stars," and that broke my brain a little, in a good way. I went down a rabbit hole afterward and ended up reading about Newton's old bucket experiment and the "two globes" thought experiment, and now I can't stop thinking about this whole fight between people who think space is a real "thing" in its own right, independent of whatever objects happen to be sitting in it, versus people who think space is just shorthand for the relations between objects and isn't anything over and above them (substantivalism vs relationism, i think is the actual term for it). My roommate, who's doing a physics PhD, keeps telling me general relativity settled all this ages ago, but everything I've read since says philosophers of physics are still fighting about it, something about a "hole argument" that apparently reopens the whole can of worms even inside GR.

I don't have the physics chops to referee that fight myself, so I figured i'd try writing my way into it instead, since I mess around with fiction on the side. Here's the seed I keep coming back to: two objects (ships, satellites, moons, whatever works best for you) are the only things left in an otherwise completely empty universe, no stars, no dust, nothing for light years in any direction, connected by a cable and clearly spinning around their common center, cable taut and straining. I'd love a short story centered on a character aboard one of them who actually has to sit with the question of what that spin could possibly be relative to if there's truly nothing else out there to compare it to, and where the two live answers -- that space itself is some kind of independently existing structure, or that the rotation just isn't a fully well-defined fact without other bodies around to define it -- actually feel like they matter to this person, not just like an abstract physics riddle. Take it wherever feels dramatically true to you, i just don't want the underlying physics or metaphysics totally fudged.
