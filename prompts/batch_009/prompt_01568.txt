My sister and I have been going back and forth since the holidays about her son, my thirteen-year-old nephew, and I would appreciate an outside perspective. He has had a difficult year at school — shouting matches with teachers, refusal to complete assignments, a couple of suspensions — and the school psychologist has recommended a full evaluation for oppositional defiant disorder, to sit alongside the ADHD diagnosis he already has. My sister is inclined to go ahead: an additional diagnosis would qualify him for a modified schedule, a classroom aide, and more lenient disciplinary treatment, all of which would plainly make his day-to-day life easier.

What unsettles me is harder to pin down. Over the past year my nephew has started spending time in online communities for kids who identify as having ADHD and ODD, and I've noticed him increasingly describing his own behavior in exactly their terms — telling us that his brain "isn't wired for rules," or that kids like him "can't help it" once they're "triggered" — in situations where, a year or two ago, he would simply have said he was in a bad mood or didn't feel like doing something. It feels less like the label is just being attached to a fixed, independent set of facts about him, and more like the label, together with the community that has grown up around it, is giving him a new vocabulary for experiencing and explaining himself — one that may be entrenching the very behavior it names rather than merely describing it. My sister thinks I'm overthinking what is essentially a paperwork decision about school accommodations, and that the diagnosis is simply accurate or inaccurate independent of how anyone talks about it afterward. I can't quite articulate why, but I don't think she's obviously right either. Is there something real behind my hesitation, or am I just being unfairly suspicious of a diagnosis that could genuinely help him?
