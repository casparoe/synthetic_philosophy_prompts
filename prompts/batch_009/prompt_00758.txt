okay so I had a genuinely heated argument with my friend Mara last night (like, 1am, way too much wine, voices raised) about whether it even makes sense to say a chatbot "believes" anything, and neither of us budged so I want an outside read. It started because she brought up this study from a few years back where some philosophers fine-tuned GPT-3 on Dennett's own writing, then asked the real Dennett and the "Dennett-bot" the same ten philosophical questions — including one about whether we could ever build a robot with beliefs — and had experts on Dennett's work try to guess which answers were actually his. Turns out his real answer basically said no, these "digital boxes of truths" don't have beliefs because they can't act on them, they're not autonomous agents, but a plurality of the Dennett experts actually picked the AI's answer over his real one, since it was way more liberal about machine belief, which is apparently part of why it read as more genuinely "Dennett-like" to people who know his theoretical commitments. Dennett himself later admitted over email that he wished he'd added a line to his answer, since it came out sounding less liberal than he'd meant it to.

Mara's take is that this basically catches Dennett contradicting his own theory: the whole point of the intentional stance is that there's no further fact about belief beyond whatever makes the intentional-level description predictively/explanatorily indispensable — that's the "real patterns" move, beliefs are as real as centers of gravity as long as positing them buys you compression you can't get more cheaply from the design stance or the physical stance. If that's ALL there is to it, she says, we should just straightforwardly attribute beliefs to current LLMs, since talking about what a model "thinks" or "wants" is often the only tractable way to predict its outputs. My take is that the "autonomous agency" bit Dennett tacked on isn't just a face-saving patch, it's tracking something the official predictive-compression criterion actually misses, and once you build it back in, its a much closer call for something like you (yes, I see the irony of asking an LLM to referee this).

Can you write me a proper essay settling this: does Dennett's own official criterion for the intentional stance (predictive/explanatory indispensability relative to rival stances) by itself license attributing genuine beliefs to current LLMs, or is some further condition like autonomous agency actually required to make sense of his broader view — and either way, do systems like ChatGPT or Claude satisfy that further condition or not? Please actually weigh the strongest form of both Mara's reading and mine rather than strawmanning one of us, but don't let it dissolve into "it depends how you define belief" — land somewhere specific by the end.
