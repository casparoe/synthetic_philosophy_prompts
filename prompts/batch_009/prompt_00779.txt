I'm a philosophy PhD student working on formal epistemology, and I've been stuck on one particular move in the Dutch Book literature on probabilism. The classical argument only shows that incoherent credences sanction a set of bets, each individually fair, whose sum guarantees a loss no matter what happens — which looks like a fact about betting prudence rather than epistemic rationality, since nothing compels an agent to accept any bets at all. Christensen's attempt to "depragmatize" the argument tries to relocate the defect into the act of judging each bet fair, so that the guaranteed loss becomes a mere symptom of some underlying inconsistency among the agent's fairness judgments, rather than the source of the irrationality itself. But exposing that inconsistency seems to require a further premise stating that fairness judgments about separate bets must combine additively, which looks uncomfortably close to presupposing the very additivity the whole argument is meant to establish — roughly the objection Maher presses against Christensen's proposal. Could you help me see clearly what's being smuggled in at that step, and give me your sense of whether the depragmatizing strategy can survive it, or whether Dutch Book arguments are better off abandoned in favor of a representation-theorem defense of probabilism?
