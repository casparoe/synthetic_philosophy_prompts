This has kind of taken over my brain lately: I'm Deaf (grew up signing, went to a Deaf school, basically my whole social life is Deaf), and I just found out from an audiologist that I actually qualify for a cochlear implant now, which wasnt true last time I looked into it a few years ago. My hearing brother keeps telling me to just treat this like any other big decision — make a pros and cons list, put rough numbers on stuff like job prospects and safety versus the surgery, cost, and recovery, and go with whatever wins. I actually tried that this week and it left me more lost, not less, because I have no idea how to weigh "maybe I get to hear music" against "maybe I stop feeling like myself, or like part of my community," especially since I don't even know what hearing would be like for me and can't exactly test it out first. Late-implanted Deaf adults seem to land all over the place on this, and a couple people in my Deaf group act like even considering it means something's wrong with being Deaf, which honestly isn't where I'm coming from. Is my brother right that I'm overthinking a pretty normal cost-benefit call, or is there something actually different about a decision like this — and if so, how am I even supposed to think it through?
