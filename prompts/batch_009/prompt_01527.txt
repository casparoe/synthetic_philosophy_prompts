I spent about 35 years running a small analytical chemistry lab before I retired, and lately I've been auditing a philosophy course at the local community college just to keep my brain limber (turns out I like epistemology way more than I expected to). Last week's session was on rational decision-making, and it got me thinking again about a situation involving an old business partner of mine that I've never quite been able to shake.

Back in the early 2000s, a former colleague, Ray, and I co-developed and patented a corrosion-resistant coating and started a small company together to license it out. A few years in we had a falling out over how the royalties were being split, and it ended up in court. At the time the dispute was over something like $40,000 in unpaid royalties — this was 2006. Ray and I are both long retired now, and the lawyers are STILL at it. I've personally put close to $300,000 into legal fees at this point, and from what mutual friends tell me, Ray's spent something similiar going after me right back. Every couple years theres a new motion, a new expert report, a new appeal. Whenever my lawyer gently suggests settling, my gut reaction is that quitting now would mean everything I've already sunk into this was for nothing — plus I've got this nagging feeling that Ray's more strapped for cash than I am and will cave first if I just hold on a little longer. Funny thing is, I hear secondhand that Ray says almost the identical thing about me.

I know how this looks from the outside — two old men who've each spent something like seven times the original amount, fighting over a sum that's now pocket change next to the legal bills. But every time I try to talk myself into dropping it, that same "not after everything I've put in" feeling stops me cold. Is there some real rational logic underneath this that I'm missing, or have Ray and I just been outsmarted by our own psychology for twenty years running? I'd genuinely like to know how you'd size up what's actually driving this.
