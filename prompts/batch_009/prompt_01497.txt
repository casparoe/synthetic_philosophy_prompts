I'm taking a philosophy of mind seminar this semester, and for the past few weeks the course has focused on theories of motivation and reward, moving from classic mid-century behaviorist experiments on animals up through how reinforcement-learning systems are trained today. One example that has stayed with me, mentioned almost in passing during lecture, is the well-known experiments from the 1950s in which rats with electrodes implanted in certain brain regions would press a lever thousands of times an hour to stimulate themselves, sometimes ignoring food, water, and sleep in the process. I keep thinking about this in connection with something much smaller and sillier that's been happening in my own life.

For the past year or so I've kept an unbroken streak on Duolingo. When I started, the streak was only ever a side effect of wanting to actually learn the language. Lately, though, on days when I'm exhausted or buried in coursework, I've caught myself opening the app purely to clear the easiest possible exercise, translating some sentence I've seen five hundred times before, just so the streak survives, with no real learning taking place at all. What strikes me is that when the little animation confirming the streak plays, I get almost exactly the same hit of relief and satisfaction that I used to get from genuinely mastering something new. My roommate says I'm overanalyzing a phone app and that the fix is simply more discipline, but it feels to me like something has gone quietly wrong in how my own goals and rewards are wired together, rather than that I've just become lazy.

So I'd like a second opinion: is my roommate right that this is a non-issue best solved by trying harder, or is there something philosophically significant going on when the felt reward for an activity comes apart from whatever that reward was originally supposed to track? And if it is significant, does it matter that the relief I feel in the moment seems completely real regardless of where it comes from, or is there a principled reason my present satisfaction should count for less than the kind I used to get? I'd rather understand what's actually going on here than just be told to have more willpower.
