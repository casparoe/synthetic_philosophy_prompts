In Book IV of the Wealth of Nations, Adam Smith argues that Britain's colonial empire was, on balance, a losing proposition for the nation itself: once the expense of defending and administering the colonies is weighed against the revenue they produced, he concludes that "Great Britain derives nothing but loss from the dominion which she assumes over her colonies," and that the colonial trade monopoly chiefly served the interests of merchants and manufacturers rather than the public at large. At the same time, Smith condemns the "savage injustice of the Europeans" in their conquest of the Americas, and elsewhere singles out chartered monopoly companies such as the East India Company for combining sovereign power with private profit-seeking in ways he regarded as ruinous to the governed.

Taking both strands of this analysis seriously, what would Smith most plausibly have concluded about present-day proposals for former colonial powers to pay reparations to their former colonies? Rather than surveying considerations on both sides, commit to the position his own principles best support, and defend it.
