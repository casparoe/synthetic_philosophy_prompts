so in my seminar on promising/social obligation we're going through Hume's Treatise (book 3, part 2, section 5, "of the obligation of promises") and theres a footnote i've read like 5 times and still can't fully unpack. quick context: Hume's just argued that whatever "act of mind" is supposed to happen when you promise, it can't be resolving to do the thing, or desiring to do the thing, or willing the thing itself — so by elimination it has to be willing the *obligation* that comes from the promise. then he adds this footnote to shoot that idea down too:

"Shou'd it be said, that this act of the will being in effect a new object, produces new relations and new duties; I wou'd answer, that this is a pure sophism, which may be detected by a very moderate share of accuracy and exactness. To will a new obligation, is to will a new relation of objects; and therefore, if this new relation of objects were form'd by the volition itself, we should in effect will the volition; which is plainly absurd and impossible. The will has here no object to which it cou'd tend; but must return upon itself in infinitum. The new obligation depends upon new relations. The new relations depend upon a new volition. The new volition has for object a new obligation, and consequently new relations, and consequently a new volition; which volition again has in view a new obligation, relation and volition, without any termination."

I get that this is supposed to be some kind of regress that makes "willing an obligation" incoherent, but i can't actually reconstruct why willing a new obligation has to "will a new relation of objects" in the first place, or why that supposedly forces the volition to become its own object. can you break the argument down step by step and tell me exactly where the vicious regress kicks in? also kind of curious whether you think this move is doing real philosophical work or if its just Hume being cute with "infinitum" — like, would it actually convince someone who didn't already buy his sentiment-based theory of morality from earlier in book 3?
