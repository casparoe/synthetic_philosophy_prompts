Write a joke about infinite ethics that turns specifically on the problem of "infinitarian paralysis," as raised by Nick Bostrom: if the universe already contains an infinite amount of both positive and negative value, then adding or subtracting any finite quantity of value — one saved life, one prevented war, a lifetime of kindness — leaves the cosmic total exactly as infinite as it was before, so standard aggregative or utilitarian reasoning can no longer distinguish the moral worth of one action from another, or from doing nothing at all. For the joke to succeed, the humor needs to depend on genuinely grasping this specific paradox of infinite aggregation; a generic "infinity is a weird, big number" punchline should not count as a solution.
