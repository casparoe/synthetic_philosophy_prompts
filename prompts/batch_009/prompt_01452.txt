So this week in my decision theory seminar we covered Good's theorem — the result that for a Bayesian expected-utility maximizer, the expected value of free information is never negative, since worst case you just ignore it and do whatever you would've done anyway. The proof is basically a Jensen's inequality/convexity argument once you set it up properly, and it feels almost too clean to argue with: how could looking at more evidence before you act ever make you worse off in expectation, given that "don't update on it" is always still an option?

Then like two days later a friend who's doing an econ PhD was telling me about the "Hirshleifer effect," from Jack Hirshleifer's old paper on the private vs. social value of information. The standard illustration involves a bunch of risk-averse farmers who'd love to insure each other against a bad harvest — except if a forecaster credibly reveals whether the harvest will be good or bad before the farmers get a chance to sign their insurance contracts, the whole insurance market unravels, since there's no more risk left to pool once everyone knows the outcome. So everyone ends up worse off in expectation then if the forecast had never been made public (or had shown up after contracts were signed). That's a case where more information, handed out for free to everybody, makes every single person expect a worse outcome.

I can't shake the feeling that these two results are in tension, and I'm assuming they're not actually contradicting each other since both are supposedly solid, widely-accepted results. Can you walk me through whats really going on here — specifically, which assumption behind Good's theorem is being violated in the Hirshleifer setup, and why does dropping it open the door to this kind of result? And as a bonus: is there a sense in which Good's theorem is still quietly true for each farmer taken in isolation, and the "badness" only shows up once you zoom out to the market/group level — sort of like a prisoner's-dilemma structure for information? Or is that too cute a way to put it?
