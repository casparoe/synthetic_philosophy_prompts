So my dad went in for his yearly checkup last month and the doctor told him he "has" high blood pressure now. But get this—when we pulled up his old records, his numbers were basically identical to three years ago, when the exact same doctor said he was totally fine. Nothing about his actual body had changed. What changed was that the official guidelines for what counts as high blood pressure got lowered a while back, so overnight, without his body doing a single different thing, he went from "healthy" to "has a chronic condition that needs managing."

My brother says I'm making a big deal out of nothing, like it's just some bean counters redrawing a line on a chart and nothing "real" has changed about my dad either way. But it's bugging me more than that. If a committee can just vote to move a number and suddenly millions of people who were fine yesterday officially have a disease today, doesn't that mean being "sick" isn't purely some fact about your body that doctors just discover, the way they'd discover a broken bone or a virus? It feels like there's a judgment call baked in somewhere about what level of risk counts as bad enough to be worth treating. And once I started noticing this I saw it everywhere—like how obesity got voted into being an official "disease" by one medical group but not everyone agrees, or how a lot of what used to just be "kids being kids" now gets diagnosed as ADHD.

So here's my actual question: is there ever a purely neutral, values-free fact about whether someone is diseased, or does calling something a disease always sneak in some assumption about what's good or bad for a person, even when it's dressed up in official medical numbers? Is my brother right that I'm overthinking a technical redefinition, or is my gut onto something real here?
