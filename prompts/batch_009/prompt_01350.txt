I'm in the final year of a PhD on Frege's theory of sense and reference, and until recently I thought of Frege the man as little more than a sympathetic footnote to Frege the logician: a provincial professor at Jena whose life's work fell apart when Russell's letter about the contradiction in the Grundgesetze arrived, and who died in relative obscurity in 1925. That picture got considerably more complicated a few weeks ago, when someone in my reading group brought up the political diary Frege kept for almost exactly one month at the end of his life, from 10 March to 9 April 1924 — left out of his published Nachlass for decades, printed in German only in 1994, and translated into English two years after that. Reading it, I understood why Dummett, who had revered Frege as "an absolutely rational man," described being genuinely shaken by what he found there: contempt for parliamentary democracy, universal suffrage, and Catholics, and an antisemitism severe enough that Frege endorses stripping Jews of political rights.

What has been nagging at me since goes beyond ordinary discomfort about having built a dissertation around a bigot. Frege's central philosophical commitments — his insistence that thoughts are objective denizens of a mind-independent domain rather than private mental episodes, his hostility to psychologism, his demand that logical and mathematical truth be held rigidly apart from the flux of individual or cultural perspective — strike me as continuous with the very same impulse that shows up in the diary as contempt for pluralism and compromise: a craving for a single, hierarchical order of correctness imposed on what he experienced as unruly heterogeneity. If that parallel is real, it seems to follow that the sense/reference distinction and the whole apparatus of objective, mind-independent meaning my dissertation is built on is not the neutral piece of technical machinery my subfield treats it as, but is quietly continuous with, maybe even expressive of, the politics of the 1924 diary.

I have a chapter draft due to my committee soon and cannot decide whether this deserves a methodological footnote or whether I am running together considerations a clearer head would keep separate. So I would like to ask directly: does this inference from psychological or thematic affinity to philosophical contamination actually hold up, or is there a real confusion in it that I am too close to the material to see?
