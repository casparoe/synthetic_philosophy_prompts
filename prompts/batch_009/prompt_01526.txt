A friend and I spent much of last night arguing about the Ellsberg paradox. They insisted that ambiguity aversion is simply a bias that a sufficiently rational agent would train out of themselves; I maintained that declining to be probabilistically sophisticated about the ambiguous urn is perfectly defensible, and that it is Savage's Sure-Thing Principle that should give way instead. Neither of us budged, but we agreed that the paradox has an almost comic structure once the relevant bets are laid out side by side. Write me a joke about the Ellsberg paradox. The punchline should depend on the actual logical structure of the paradox — the fact that the preferences it elicits cannot jointly be rationalized by any single probability assignment to the ambiguous urn — so that it lands for someone who understands why the paradox is a paradox, rather than working as a generic joke about hedging one's bets or fearing uncertainty.
