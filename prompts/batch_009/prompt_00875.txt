Been down a rabbit hole reporting on the longevity scene (cryonics outfits, Bryan Johnson's whole 'Don't Die' thing, people casually talking about uploading their minds to a server), and it keeps bringing me back to Epicurus. Everyone quotes his "death is nothing to us" line, but theres a less-cited doctrine of his (Principal Doctrine 19, if you want chapter and verse) saying infinite time contains no greater pleasure than finite time, if you measure pleasure by reason instead of the flesh -- basically, a longer life isn't automatically a better one, same as judging a meal by taste rather than portion size. Running his own logic forward: would Epicurus look at a cryonics facility or a mind-uploading pitch deck and call the whole project a category error, basically thanatophobia with a lab coat on -- or could a coherent version of him sign on to radical life extension anyway, so long as its pursued calmly instead of out of dread? Want his actual arguments applied here, not just the ancient-guy-who-said-chill-about-death vibes people usually stop at.
