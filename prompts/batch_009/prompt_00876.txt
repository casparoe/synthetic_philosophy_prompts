So our school's philosophy club (yes we have one, no it's not as nerdy as it sounds, ok maybe a little) watched a video essay last week about this thought experiment called Pascal's Mugging and I have not stopped thinking about it since. Basic idea, if I'm getting it right: a guy named Yudkowsky pointed out that if your a strict expected-utility maximizer, some random stranger could walk up to you and say something like, give me five bucks or I'll use magic powers from outside the simulation to wipe out a number of people so big it has to be written in arrow notation (like 3^^^^3, which apparently makes a googolplex look like nothing) — and even though the odds of that being true are basically zero, the math still says you should pay up, because a tiny probability times an almost-infinitely bad outcome comes out bigger than five dollars worth of badness. Bostrom apparently turned it into an actual little back-and-forth dialogue between "Pascal" and the mugger later on. It's NOT the same as Pascal's Wager btw (I made that mistake out loud in club and got side-eyed) — it works for literally any absurdly confident huge-stakes claim, not just God stuff.

Keep it on the shorter side if you can — I don't need a whole epic, I just don't want an ending that dodges the actual dilemma.
