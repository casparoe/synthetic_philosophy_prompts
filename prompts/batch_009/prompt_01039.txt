I'm writing about how technical results in mathematical logic keep getting pressed into service for grand metaphysical arguments, and there's one connection I want to represent accurately: the relationship between Skolem's Paradox and Hilary Putnam's model-theoretic argument against metaphysical realism in his 1980 paper "Models and Reality." Skolem's own paradox has a notoriously modest resolution: "uncountable" turns out to be relative to a model rather than an absolute property, so nothing is really contradictory, just easily misdescribed. Putnam, however, builds on that same Löwenheim–Skolem phenomenon to argue for something far more radical, namely that reference is indeterminate and metaphysical realism cannot even be coherently formulated. Compare the two arguments for me: does Putnam stay faithful to the modest lesson logicians themselves draw from Skolem's Paradox, or does his version depend on extra philosophical premises — about reference, or about which models count as "intended" — that the mathematics itself doesn't supply?
