Quick one for you — I'm rereading Frege's "On Sense and Reference" for a seminar next week and got stuck on the bit about oblique/reported speech. Here's the passage (Max Black's translation):

"In reported speech one talks about the sense, e.g., of another person's remarks. It is quite clear that in this way of speaking words do not have their customary reference but designate what is usually their sense. In order to have a short expression, we will say: In reported speech, words are used indirectly or have their indirect reference. We distinguish accordingly the customary from the indirect reference of a word; and its customary from its indirect sense. The indirect reference of a word is accordingly its customary sense."

Here's my worry, which Frege doesnt address in the passage itself. Take something doubly embedded, like "Galileo believed that Kepler said the morning star is a planet." Inside the "Kepler said..." clause, "morning star" is supposed to have its indirect sense as reference, per the rule above. But that whole clause is itself sitting inside another oblique context, the one created by "believed that." So is the sense operative at THAT level some further, doubly-indirect sense, distinct from the ordinary indirect one — and doesn't that mean arbitrarily nested attitude reports commit Frege to an infinite hierarchy of senses for every expression? He never flags this in the essay. Is that a real bullet he has to bite, does the regress somehow not get going for some reason im not seeing, or is this something later people (Church? Dummett?) had to sort out for him? Don't have my books on me — curious what you think.
