So my aunt lost her husband almost two years ago and still cries most days, can't sleep right, barely leaves the house. Her therapist just gave it an actual name — "prolonged grief disorder," apparently a real diagnosis now — and put her on meds. My aunt seems relieved to finally have a label for it; my mom's kind of offended on her behalf, insisting that calling it a "disorder" medicalizes something that's just the cost of loving someone that much, and that theres nothing wrong with my aunt that grieving in her own time wouldn't fix. I've got a character in my new book stuck in the exact same spot and keep flip-flopping on who's right. Is there some real, principled line between an actual disease and an unusually hard version of a normal human experience, or is that distinction basically made up and it just comes down to whatever doctors and insurers decide is worth treating?
