The gist of my notes: I don't think this is just a story about a few p-hackers or "researcher degrees of freedom," I think it's baked into frequentist logic itself. A p-value only ever tells you P(data this extreme | null is true), but basically every professor I've had talks about "p < .05" like it means "only a 5% chance this effect isn't real," which is the totally opposite conditional, P(null | data). Frequentism doesn't even have a legit way to talk about P(hypothesis is true) without smuggling in a prior, so my conclusion is that if the field had reported Bayes factors or posterior odds instead of a binary significance cutoff from the start, people would've been forced to be upfront about their priors and effect sizes instead of hiding behind the word "significant" — and we'd have way fewer zombie findings like the one that just ate my thesis.
