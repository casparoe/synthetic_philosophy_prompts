As a longtime reader of LessWrong and the EA Forum, I've been following discussions of using cooperative bargaining solutions to formalize the parliamentary model of moral uncertainty, in which delegates representing one's credence in various moral theories bargain over which action to take. My understanding is that the Kalai-Smorodinsky solution is a strict improvement on the Nash bargaining solution for this purpose: it preserves Nash's independence of irrelevant alternatives while additionally guaranteeing individual monotonicity, so no delegate can end up worse off merely because the feasible set of compromises expanded in that delegate's favor. If that's right, I don't understand why anyone modeling the parliament would still bother with the Nash solution rather than switching to Kalai-Smorodinsky across the board. What am I missing?
