So a friend of mine who's way deeper into the rationalist/AI-safety blogosphere than I am was explaining this idea called "surrogate goals" to me last week and it kinda broke my brain. As far as I understood it: the worry is that some agent -- a future AI, a simulation, whatever, it doesn't even need to be causally connected to you at all, thats the "acausal" part (this is basically the same neighborhood as that Roko's basilisk thing, but more general) -- could threaten you into doing what it wants just because you're both able to predict each other's behavior well enough. The proposed defense is that you deliberately commit to acting like you care about some fake, made-up goal as well as the thing you actually value, so that a rational threatener (who only bothers threatening stuff that will actually move you) ends up "spending" the threat on the decoy instead of on the real thing. My friend seemed to think this is a genuinely sound strategy and not just a cute trick, and I can kind of see the appeal, but it also feels like it should collapse the second you poke at it -- like, wouldn't a threatener smart enough to model you in the first place just notice the goal is fake and go after the real one anyway? And isn't the fake goal a real goal the moment you actually start defending it, so aren't you just back where you started, one level up?

Could you write me a dialogue between two people who both clearly know their decision theory cold -- the kind of two people you'd find going back and forth in a long LessWrong comment thread, neither one a pushover or strawman -- where one of them defends adopting a surrogate goal as a genuinely rational defense against acausal threats, and the other argues the whole move is incoherent or self-undermining once you actually look closely. I want real back-and-forth, not two people just repeating their priors louder each time -- each person should respond to the specific point the other just raised (like how you make the commitment to a surrogate goal credible without that cost making it a "real" goal, whether the strategy only works on threateners dumber than you, whether it just pushes the regress up a level, stuff like that). It doesn't have to end in agreement, but I'd want it to end with it being clear exactly where the two of them actually disagree.
