A situation in my family has been on my mind for weeks, and since I don't know anyone with a background in philosophy or economics to run it by, I'm hoping you can help me reason through it carefully rather than just offer a quick verdict.

When my grandmother passed away, she left my cousin and me identical sums of money, no conditions attached, which the whole family treated as an obviously fair way to divide things. My cousin, though, has always had pricier tastes than I do — good restaurants, boutique gyms, that sort of thing — while I'm genuinely content with much simpler pleasures. A month or so after the money came through, he told me he'd been mulling it over and had concluded that the equal split wasn't actually fair to him, since the same sum buys him noticeably less day-to-day contentment than it buys me. He suggested I transfer some of my share to him so that we end up equally satisfied, not merely equally funded. When I objected, he offered an analogy: imagine someone born with a rare condition such that only expensive food registers as tasting like anything at all to them; surely, he said, we'd think it fair to give that person more money so they can enjoy meals the rest of us take for granted — and he doesn't see why his own case is different in principle, since neither of us chose the tastes that determine how far our money goes.

I mentioned this at dinner with a few friends and opinions split roughly down the middle, which didn't help. My own instinct is that an equal division of money is simply what fairness required here, and that how much satisfaction each of us squeezes out of it afterward is our own affair — but I can't identify a principle that rules out my cousin's argument without also implying that we shouldn't help people whose needs are, through no fault of their own, more expensive to meet. Is there a coherent way of thinking about what our fair shares should actually track — the money itself, the satisfaction it produces, what each of us is able to do with it, or something else entirely — that would tell me whether my cousin has a genuine point or is just rationalizing wanting more?
