Write a dialogue between al-Ghazālī and Averroes (Ibn Rushd) that continues their historical debate over causation in the seventeenth discussion of the Tahāfut al-Falāsifa and its rebuttal in the Tahāfut al-Tahāfut. Al-Ghazālī should defend his claim that the conjunction of fire and the combustion of cotton reflects only God's habitual custom rather than a necessary connection grounded in fire's nature — a claim he needs in order to leave room for prophetic miracles. Averroes should press the objection that denying such necessary connections between things and their properties makes demonstrative (burhānī) scientific knowledge impossible. The two should engage directly with each other's strongest argument rather than simply restating their own positions.
