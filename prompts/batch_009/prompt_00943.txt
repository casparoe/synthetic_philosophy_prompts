My brother teaches high school chemistry, and he told his class that the 'planets orbiting a nucleus' model of the atom is wrong, but so is the 'solid ball' model that came before it. Some kid asked him why bother switching pictures at all if both are just wrong, and all my brother could say was that the newer one is 'closer to the truth.' That set us off arguing at dinner about what 'closer to the truth' could even mean once youve already admitted a theory is flat-out false. I tried to make it rigorous myself, like just tally up everything a theory gets right minus everything it gets wrong, but my brother pointed out i could rig that tally just by choosing how finely to chop the claims into pieces. So is there an actual, non-cheating way for one false theory to be closer to the truth than another false theory, or is that phrase just something we say to feel better about being wrong?
