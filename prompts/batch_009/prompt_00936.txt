This term I'm taking a seminar on Aristotle's Politics, and we've just worked through Book I's treatment of household management and acquisition. I was struck by two of his claims: that natural exchange has a built-in limit, since it serves the finite needs of the household, whereas chrematistics — moneymaking pursued as an end in itself — has no natural limit at all; and that currency is inherently 'barren,' which is why he judges interest-taking the most unnatural way of getting wealth. Rather than another analytical response paper, I'd like to try a creative one: could you write a short story, centered on a single character over a meaningful stretch of time, that dramatizes the slide from provisioning a household through natural exchange to pursuing money without limit as an end in itself — something that makes Aristotle's diagnosis felt rather than just asserted?
