After forty years as an experimental physicist, I have spent my retirement reading rather more philosophy of science than I ever expected to, and one issue keeps nagging at me. My own field is littered with theories that were once treated as essentially correct about the deep structure of reality -- the caloric theory of heat, the mechanical luminiferous ether -- only to be abandoned entirely, which is just the sort of historical pattern Larry Laudan invoked in his well-known list of once-successful-but-false theories to motivate the pessimistic meta-induction against scientific realism. I have since come across John Worrall's structural realism, which tries to blunt that argument by pointing to cases such as the transition from Fresnel's elastic-solid ether theory of light to Maxwell's electromagnetic theory, where Worrall claims the mathematical form of the theory survived even though the underlying ontology was completely overturned. Could you compare the pessimistic meta-induction with Worrall's structural realist response, using this Fresnel-to-Maxwell transition specifically as the test case? I am especially interested in exactly what is supposed to be preserved across that transition, and whether, in your assessment, this genuinely rescues a meaningful realism or simply relocates the same instability up one level, into the structure itself.
