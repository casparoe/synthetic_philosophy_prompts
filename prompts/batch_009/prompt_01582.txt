This semester, my personal finance class has us each running a simulated stock portfolio through a trading app as a long-term project, and my friend Priya and I turned it into a running competition on the side. Every Sunday night we video call each other and walk through our complete reasoning for whatever move we're planning to make when the market opens Monday — earnings reports, analyst notes, technical indicators, anything either of us has found — and neither of us holds anything back. We're both consistently near the top of the class, and I genuinely think of Priya as one of the most careful, level-headed people I know; she says the same about me. And yet almost every single week, right after one of these calls, we end up taking completely opposite positions on the same stock — I'll buy exactly what she's decided to sell — and whoever's portfolio performs better gets to pick where we eat lunch that Friday.

I mentioned this pattern to my older brother, who's majoring in economics, expecting him to laugh, but instead he said something like: "If you're both being fully honest, and each of you genuinely trusts the other's judgment, hearing each other's complete reasoning every week should be making your views converge, not leaving you just as far apart as when you started." That comment has stuck with me, because he's right that we never actually seem to talk each other into changing our minds, no matter how much we share. So is there something actually irrational happening between Priya and me each week? Does it even make sense for two people who trust each other's judgment this much to keep betting against one another the way we do, or is this just what ordinary disagreement between two smart, honest people looks like?
