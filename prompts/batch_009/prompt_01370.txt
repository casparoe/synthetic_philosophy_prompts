I have been preparing a graduate seminar on Hume's economic essays, and there is a tension in his monetary theory that I would like help thinking through carefully before I present it to my students. In "Of Money," Hume argues that the absolute quantity of specie circulating within a single kingdom is, considered on its own, "of no consequence," since prices simply scale with the stock of coin and money is nothing more than the oil that eases the wheels of commerce rather than one of the wheels itself. Yet in that same essay he goes on to argue that a nation whose money supply is increasing enjoys a genuine, if temporary, boost to industry and employment, because wages and prices lag behind the new specie as it diffuses through the economy — and he even recommends that sound policy consists in keeping the money supply perpetually rising, precisely so as to prolong this favorable interval indefinitely.

What I cannot work out is how this policy recommendation survives contact with Hume's own price-specie-flow mechanism in "Of the Balance of Trade," which is meant to show that any attempt to accumulate specie beyond what a nation's trade and industry can naturally sustain is self-correcting: rising domestic prices erode the competitiveness of exports, cheapen imports, and drain the money back out again. If that adjustment mechanism is as automatic and reliable as Hume takes it to be, it is hard to see how a magistrate could keep the money stock climbing indefinitely without triggering exactly the corrective outflow Hume describes elsewhere, which would seem to undercut the very policy he recommends. So: is this a real inconsistency in Hume's economic thought — something in the neighborhood of what Adam Smith seems to have suspected when he praised Hume's reasoning as ingenious while worrying that it smuggled back in the idea that opulence consists in money — or is there a more charitable reconstruction on which the short-run stimulus argument, the long-run neutrality thesis, and the specie-flow mechanism all fit together? I would especially appreciate precision about which notion of "the long run" is doing the work in each strand of the argument, since I suspect that is where the apparent contradiction is actually hiding.
