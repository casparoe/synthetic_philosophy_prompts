At work we're implementing the human-oversight provisions of the EU AI Act for a model that scores loan applicants' creditworthiness, and the exercise strikes me as resting on a confusion. Andreas Matthias's 2004 "responsibility gap" argument shows that once a system's behavior comes from training rather than hand-coded rules, its designers can no longer predict specific outputs precisely enough to be genuinely blameworthy for any one of them. But if that's the reason nobody is responsible for what the model does, then regulations requiring "meaningful human control" seem to be demanding that we manufacture exactly the kind of control the argument says can't exist — so isn't all the oversight documentation we produce just theater, rather than something that actually closes the gap?
