A former student of mine, who has never been particularly active in party politics, was recently invited to join a political party as a dues-paying member, mainly to push harder for environmental causes she cares about. She tells me the local meetings are energizing and full of camaraderie, but she's also noticed that members who privately doubted a proposal still end up publicly backing it once the leadership settles on a line, and that most meeting time goes toward recruiting new members and winning the next election rather than debating policy on its merits. She shrugs this off as simply the price of being effective, since nothing happens in politics without organization behind it. Something about that response strikes me as too quick, though I can't say exactly why. Is she right to treat this as an unavoidable cost of doing business, or does what she's describing point to a more principled worry?
