A dilemma from my own life has been nagging at me in light of this term's ethics seminar. I currently split my volunteering between tutoring a single student in my neighborhood, where I can directly see him improve, and helping organize a campaign to overhaul how our city funds public schools, which could benefit thousands of students but could just as easily backfire in ways nobody could have predicted, such as shifting enrollment patterns or triggering funding cuts at other schools. The trouble isn't just that I don't know the odds of the campaign succeeding; I don't even know whether its unforeseeable side effects are more likely to be good or bad. A friend argues that since I can't get any handle on those downstream effects, I should stick to the tutoring, where the good I do is at least real and traceable. That strikes me as a convenient way of letting myself off the hook, but I can't quite articulate why. Is there a principled reason to favor the option whose effects I can actually track, or is my friend onto something?
