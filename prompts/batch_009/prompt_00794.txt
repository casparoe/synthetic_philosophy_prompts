I keep circling back to the Doomsday Argument every year or so, ever since I first ran into it through Bostrom's Anthropic Bias and the old anthropic-reasoning threads on Less Wrong and Overcoming Bias. At this point what bothers me isn't the argument itself but an unresolved trade-off sitting underneath it. The Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA), which has you reason as though you were a random draw from some reference class of observers, is what generates the Doomsday Argument's pessimistic update on how much longer humanity has left. The standard way to block that update, the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA), only pays for itself by committing you to something like Bostrom's Presumptuous Philosopher: faced with two cosmological theories that are empirically indistinguishable except that one implies vastly more observers than the other, you're apparently obligated to favor the many-observer theory, sight unseen, purely because your own existence is more likely under it. Every treatment I've come across lays out this trade-off, calls it a genuine dilemma, and then declines to actually take a side.

I'd like you to take a side. Please argue for the SIA horn: that we should reject the Doomsday Argument's inference, accept SIA, and bite the bullet on the Presumptuous Philosopher, defending in some detail why favoring the many-observer theory on anthropic grounds alone is less absurd than it looks at first pass. If you think the bullet can instead be dodged outright, without quietly reintroducing SSA-style reference-class restrictions to do the work, I'm open to that move as well, but only if you actually show how, rather than just gesturing at some more sophisticated theory of observation selection effects. What I don't want is another survey of the literature that restates the dilemma and shrugs; I want the single strongest, most sustained argument for one specific resolution that you can construct.
