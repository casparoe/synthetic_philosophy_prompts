I work as a software engineer, and I've recently been reading about the causal exclusion argument in philosophy of mind. It seems to me that multiple realizability already dissolves the problem: a program is never simply identical to the transistor states that implement it on a particular machine, since the same program can run on completely different hardware, and we don't think the chip-level story makes the code-level explanation causally redundant. By the same logic, the multiple realizability of mental states should guarantee that they aren't threatened by the causal sufficiency of their physical realizers. So I'm not sure why Kim and other philosophers still treat non-reductive physicalism as unstable in the face of the exclusion argument, rather than treating multiple realizability as having already settled the matter. What am I missing?
