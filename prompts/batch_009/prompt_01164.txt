Longtime reader of the Overcoming Bias/LessWrong literature on disagreement here, and I have a persistent worry about the common prior assumption behind Aumann's agreement theorem. The standard defense — Harsanyi's line, refined in Hanson's "Uncommon Priors Require Origin Disputes" — says two honest Bayesians' differing beliefs must trace to differing evidence rather than differing priors, and that residual disputes about priors dissolve once you push back to a shared "pre-prior" over how those priors arose, unless the agents have an explicit dispute about that origin. But "they share a pre-prior yet received different, hard-to-verbalize evidence about their own reliability" seems empirically indistinguishable from "they simply have different pre-priors," however far back the regress goes.

Could you construct one detailed example — specific agents, a specific disputed claim, and a full history of what each has observed and inferred about the matter and about themselves — where this underdetermination is as sharp as possible, so that careful Bayesians would genuinely disagree about which description fits?
