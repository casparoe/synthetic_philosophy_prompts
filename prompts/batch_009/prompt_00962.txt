Hey, I'm putting together a handout for my grad seminar on interpersonal utility aggregation and want a dialogue for it. Quick context in case it helps: Harsanyi's aggregation theorem says that if each person's preferences over lotteries satisfy the vNM axioms, the social planner's do too, and social preferences respect everyone's unanimous judgments (Pareto), then social utility has to be a weighted sum of individual utilities -- which a lot of people have read as basically an axiomatic argument for utilitarianism. But then theres Diamond's old counterexample: two equally deserving people, one indivisible good, and most of us think flipping a fair coin is better than just handing it to person 1 -- yet Harsanyi's rule comes out flatly indifferent between the coin flip and either sure allocation, since expected utility is linear in the probabilities. Some people (Broome? someone) try to rescue Harsanyi by redescribing the outcomes so "having gotten a fair shot" counts as its own bit of utility, which always struck me as a little too convenient.

Could you write me a dialogue, maybe 500-700 words, between a committed Harsanyian and a critic pressing the Diamond case? I want real back-and-forth -- the Harsanyian should get to make the redescription move (or something better) and defend it under pressure, and the critic shouldn't just fold, they should push on whether that move is principled or just an ad hoc patch. No strawmen -- both should be arguing at full strength.
