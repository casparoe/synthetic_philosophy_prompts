Two weeks ago, a close friend was arrested for vehicular manslaughter: he glanced at a text message for a few seconds and struck a pedestrian who had stepped into the road. Driving home from the very same gathering that night, I did precisely the same thing and, purely by chance, no one crossed my path. Since then I have been reading Nagel's and Williams's original papers on moral luck, and it seems to me they effectively establish that what philosophers call resultant luck cannot legitimately make one person more blameworthy than another, since the outcome lay outside either driver's control. His lawyer has asked me to speak at the sentencing hearing, and I would like to argue that the manslaughter charge amounts to punishing my friend for bad luck rather than for any greater fault on his part than my own. Have I understood the philosophical literature correctly, and would this be a sound basis for that argument?
