Long-time readers of the Overcoming Bias/Less Wrong sequences will recognize the standard picture of acausal trade: two causally disconnected agents each benefit the other, but only because each can simulate the other well enough to be confident the favor will be returned. Evidential cooperation in large worlds (ECL), which grew out of Caspar Oesterheld's paper "Multiverse-wide Cooperation via Correlated Decision Making," is often presented as securing the same cooperative benefit without that apparatus: you act well simply because doing so is evidence that relevantly similar agents elsewhere do likewise, with no simulation, reciprocity, or even mutual awareness required. Please compare the two proposals. Is ECL a genuinely distinct decision-theoretic mechanism, or is it acausal trade with the bargaining stripped out and re-described as an evidential update? And can it recover what seems most distinctively trade-like about acausal trade—differentiated exchange between parties with different values—or only a cruder convergence on some shared compromise?
