I'm trying to sell my kayak to a guy from my climbing gym. Neither of us wants to name our real number first, because whoever moves first basically hands the other leverage, so we've been doing this slow lowball-then-counteroffer dance over text for like two weeks now, and theres a decent chance the sale just falls apart from sheer friction even though I'm fairly confident there's some price in the middle that would make us both happy.

I complained about this to a friend who does something in AI alignment research, and she said something that's been bugging me since. She claimed that in negotiations like this, you can sometimes design a totally different negotiating procedure — some rule both sides pre-commit to in advance — that's guaranteed, regardless of what our actual private numbers turn out to be, to leave both of us at least as well off as we'd be under the status quo back-and-forth, and crucially, without either of us ever revealing those numbers to the other. That sounds almost like a free lunch to me. Either the new procedure changes who captures more of the surplus, in which case it can't be better for BOTH of us in every case, or it doesn't actually change anything real and she's just dressed up "be more trusting" in fancier language, which isn't a guarantee of anything. I feel like I'm missing some real structural insight here rather than just lacking creativity about negotiating tactics. Is she describing something rigorous, or is this just consultant-speak? And if it's real, how can you possibly guarantee an improvement for both sides while nobody learns any new information?
