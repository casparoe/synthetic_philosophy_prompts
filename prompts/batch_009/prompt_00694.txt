In my philosophy of science seminar on causal inference, we studied Simpson's Paradox using the classic 1986 kidney-stone study: the percutaneous (less invasive) treatment did worse than open surgery in both the small-stone and large-stone subgroups, yet better overall once the two groups were pooled. The lesson I took from lecture was that whenever this kind of reversal occurs, the stratified, subgroup-level comparison is always the one that reveals the "real" causal effect, since splitting the data controls for the confound.

For my term project, I have found what looks like the same pattern in data comparing a flipped-classroom method to standard lectures: lectures outperform the flipped classroom both among students who completed the pre-class homework and among those who did not, yet the flipped classroom wins once the two groups are pooled together. Given that the structure matches the kidney-stone case exactly, am I right to trust the stratified result here as well and conclude that lectures are the causally superior teaching method?
