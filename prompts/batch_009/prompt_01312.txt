I've been chewing on the two-envelope paradox, and from everything I've read, the whole thing is really just a statistics trick: the paradox only gets off the ground because people sneak in an improper prior (like a uniform distribution over all posible dollar amounts, which can't be normalized -- reminds me of the non-normalizable plane-wave states you get for a free particle). So it seems to me that if you just set the problem up honestly, with an actual, normalizable prior over the smaller amount -- say an exponential distribution -- the paradox should simply dissolve, and switching becomes genuinely the correct move every single time you play. Am I basically right about that? And if so, is there anything left for philosophers to actually argue about once you've fixed the prior, or does the whole puzzle just evaporate at that point?
