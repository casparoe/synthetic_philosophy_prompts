Ok, this is going to sound like a weird rabbit hole but bear with me. I was in the comments under an old Astral Codex Ten post about calibration and "put a number on it" culture, and someone dropped David Lewis's triviality results for indicative conditionals as a supposed knock-down against a commenter who kept saying stuff like "my credence that if the Fed hikes, we get a recession is 70%." I went and read through the SEP entry (which keeps switching between calling this "Stalnaker's Thesis" and "Adams' Thesis" and I'm honestly not sure if those are the same claim or not) and I think I followed the core proof: if you require P(A > B) = P(B|A) to hold not just for your current credences but also for whatever you'd get by conditionalizing on B and on not-B, you can chain those together and show A and B have to come out probabilistically independent, for basically any A and B you pick. Which is obviously nuts as a general law, so somethings gotta give. (Someone in the thread also mentioned a companion paper about "Popper functions" as some kind of escape hatch, but that part lost me completely.)

Here's what's actually bugging me though: if Lewis's proof really shows that "probability of the conditional equals the conditional probability" is false in general, doesn't that mean the entire rationalist/forecaster habit of quoting a percentage for a conditional claim -- the Fed/recession thing, or honestly half the questions on Metaculus phrased as "if X resolves yes, will Y..." -- is built on an incoherent identification? Like, is everyone doing this technically commiting a logical error they just haven't noticed, kind of like nobody noticed Russell's paradox for a while? I don't want to be the annoying philosophy guy showing up to ratio people's forecasts, but I can't figure out why this doesn't seem to bother anyone in the community.

So: am I misreading what the theorem actually rules out, or is "shut up and multiply" forecasting culture quietly resting on a mistake here? And if it's the former, what's the actual takeaway supposed to be?
