I'm taking a seminar on practical reason and moral motivation this term, and a personal dilemma of mine seems directly relevant to what we've been discussing in class. Years ago, before we lost touch, I promised a close friend I'd keep donating to a charity she cared about deeply; I feel nothing for that charity now, and I'm confident no amount of honest reflection would change that. A classmate insists that since keeping the promise wouldn't serve anything I want or value, or could ever come to want or value through reflection, I have no genuine reason to follow through -- only that others might think less of me if they found out. That seems too quick to me, but I can't say exactly why. Is my classmate right that my indifference settles the matter, or does the promise still give me a reason to act, regardless of what I currently want?
