Suppose three friends are texting about a fourth, Elena, who has just done a quick fifteen-minute walkthrough of an apartment before deciding whether to sign the lease. One friend, who has no stake in the matter at all, remarks, "sure, she knows the place is fine, she looked it over." A second friend, who has agreed to co-sign the lease and would be liable for thousands of dollars if a serious problem turned up later, says, "there's no way she knows that from a fifteen-minute look." Elena's own evidence and her own practical situation are held fixed across the two remarks; only the stakes of the two speakers differ, not hers. My friend took this to be contextualism working exactly as advertised, with different standards salient to different speakers, while I felt it actually creates trouble specifically for pragmatic encroachment, since that view is supposed to locate the relevant stakes in the subject, and yet the pull toward "she doesn't know" here seems to track the co-signer's exposure rather than Elena's own situation.

Then, using the case above as a test: does either view, on its own resources, predict or explain the pull toward "she doesn't know"? And if pragmatic encroachment genuinely struggles with cases like this one, is there a principled way for it to accommodate them without simply collapsing into contextualism?
