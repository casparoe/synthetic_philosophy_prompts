For the better part of a year, my friend Sam and I have gone back and forth about whether a small company whose stock we both follow is going to succeed. Neither of us holds anything back: whenever one of us comes across a new piece of news, or thinks of a new angle, we tell the other immediately, so by now we have each heard everything the other has to say on the matter. And yet I remain fairly confident the company will do well, while Sam remains fairly confident it will not. I described this standoff to another friend who knows a good deal about probability and economics, and she insisted that this sort of thing should not be possible between two honest, clear-thinking people who have shared their reasoning in full: if we still end up disagreeing after all that, at least one of us has to be making some kind of mistake, even if neither of us can say what it is. That struck me as too strong a claim. Why couldn't two people simply start out seeing a situation differently, and remain that way, even after hearing each other out completely? Is she right that our continued disagreement is itself proof that one of us is being irrational, or is there a hole in her argument?
