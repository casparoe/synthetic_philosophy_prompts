I'm a PhD student in philosophy drafting a term paper for a graduate seminar on truthmakers and the metaphysics of facts, built around Bradley's regress and its bearing on the unity of the proposition: the question of what distinguishes a mere list like "Socrates, wisdom" from the unified, truth-apt sentence "Socrates is wise." I've worked through Bradley's original argument in Appearance and Reality and his 1910–11 exchange with Russell in Mind, and I'm comfortable with the standard menu of replies — Bergmann- and Armstrong-style non-relational ties/nexus, the Fregean mutual-interdependence/unsaturatedness line, and the "brute fact" view on which states of affairs simply unify their own constituents. What I'd like feedback on is a specific way of sharpening the "benign infinitism" reply, since that's the strand I've chosen to build my central chapter around, and I'd rather find out now whether it holds up than after another two weeks of drafting. (A friend in my cohort who works on philosophy of language thinks the whole strategy is a non-starter and that I should just write on the Fregean line instead, but I'd like a second opinion before I take her word for it.)

Here is the core move, in note form. Armstrong (1997), followed by Orilia, blocks the viciousness of the instantiation regress by comparing it to the hierarchy generated by iterating the truth predicate — "p," "it is true that p," "it is true that it is true that p" — on the grounds that nobody takes that hierarchy to show truth is unreal, since each level is generated trivially from the one before rather than being a separate achievement that has to be secured. I want to push this further than Armstrong does and argue that "relating" (or the copula, on the linguistic side) deserves the same fully deflationary treatment that "true" gets on a disquotational theory: the further "facts" that R relates a and b, and that this relating itself involves a relating, aren't additional ontological jobs but trivial restatements, so the regress comes out as provably harmless rather than merely not-obviously-vicious. My worry is that this just relocates Maurin's objection to Orilia — that positing an infinite chain of explanatory facts presupposes, rather than explains, that a is F in the first place — one level down: a thoroughgoing deflationism about relating looks like it collapses into the "brute fact" view that Olson and Armstrong already hold, the one Vallicella already attacked as incoherent, rather than actually answering the "how" question.

Could you tell me, first, whether my reconstruction of the regress and of these replies is accurate and fair to the people I'm attributing them to; second, whether pushing the disquotational analogy this hard actually buys anything beyond what Armstrong and Orilia already claim, or whether I've just redescribed their view in new language; third, whether the disanalogy I flagged at the end is fatal to the whole strategy, or whether there's a natural way of blocking it; and finally, bluntly, whether this looks like a live thread for a dissertation chapter or a well-trodden dead end I should abandon before I sink more time into it.
