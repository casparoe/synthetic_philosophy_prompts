Okay so I've fallen down a pretty deep rabbit hole the last few weeks reading old posts on the EA forum and lesswrong about this idea called "evidential cooperation in large worlds" (I think it used to be called "multiverse-wide superrationality" before that, some guy named Oesterheld came up with it around 2017?). The basic idea as I understand it is that the universe, or multiverse, is probably really big, maybe even infinite, so there's almost certainly a huge number of other reasoning agents out there who think basically the same way I do, even though I can never actually interact with them or find out what they decided. And the argument seems to be that because of that, if I treat it like I'm in some giant version of the twin prisoners dilemma with all these "copies" of me and act cooperatively — like actually taking other value systems seriously instead of just my own pet causes — thats evidence they're doing the same back, so somehow I end up better off acting nicer/more cooperative than plain self interest, or even normal altruism, would tell me to.

I sort of get the logic on the surface (kinda like Newcomb's problem, where you act as if your choice determines the correlated agent's choice even though theres no actual cause and effect happening) but I can't figure out what it would actually cash out to if someone really took it seriously.
