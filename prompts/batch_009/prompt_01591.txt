I'm a philosophy PhD student drafting the free will chapter of my dissertation, and I want to check an inference before it goes into the lit review. Frankfurt's (1969) cases seem to refute the Principle of Alternative Possibilities outright, and the standard rescues on offer—the flicker-of-freedom strategy and the dilemma defense pressed by Ginet and Widerker—don't survive once the counterfactual intervener is specified carefully enough. Since alternative possibilities were always the crucial premise linking determinism to the denial of moral responsibility, doesn't Frankfurt's result mean that incompatibilism has lost the debate, leaving compatibilism as the only serious position left standing? I'd like to know whether there is any way for an incompatibilist to grant Frankfurt's point about PAP and still hold a defensible position, or whether resisting compatibilism at this point amounts to special pleading.
