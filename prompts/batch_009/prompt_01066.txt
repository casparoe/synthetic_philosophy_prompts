I am a retired experimental physicist -- my field was quantum optics, and I spent a good part of my career running Bell-inequality tests -- and in retirement I have been reading about the metaphysics of laws of nature. I have become interested in what happens to David Lewis's Humean Supervenience once quantum mechanics is taken seriously. Tim Maudlin argues that entangled states refute the thesis, since the joint state of two particles is not fixed by their individual local states together with the spatiotemporal relations between them, contrary to what Humean Supervenience requires. Barry Loewer's well-known reply relocates the Humean mosaic itself: he treats the wavefunction as a field on the theory's high-dimensional configuration space, so that the apparent non-locality of entangled particles in ordinary three-dimensional space is really just a local arrangement of qualities in the truly fundamental space. I would like you to argue that Loewer's maneuver, whatever its technical merits, amounts to a pyrrhic victory for the Humean: it preserves supervenience only by discarding the very feature that made Humean Supervenience a substantive, recognizably empiricist thesis about our world, namely that the four-dimensional spacetime of ordinary experience is where the fundamental facts reside.
