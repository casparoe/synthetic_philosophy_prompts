I'm about a third of the way into a new novel that forks partway through — my protagonist Mireille either takes a job offer in Marseille or stays home and marries her college boyfriend, and from that point the book just alternates between both versions of her life, chapter by chapter. Someone in my writing group asked, kind of offhandedly, whether "Marseille Mireille" and "Hometown Mireille" are really the same person who just ends up different, or basically two different women who happen to share a childhood. I couldn't let this go and fell down a rabbit hole into modal metaphysics, specifically this whole fight between David Lewis's counterpart theory and the "transworld identity" camp (Kripke, and Plantinga too I think) about what's actually going on when we talk about "the same" person across different possible worlds — and now I'm alot more confused than when I started, ha.

As far as I can tell: Lewis thinks nothing literally exists in more than one world, so there isn't one Mireille who's in both — there's two women, each the closest match ("counterpart") for the other, related by resemblance rather than strict identity. He's also got this argument from "accidental intrinsics" — if the same person really did have, say, all ten fingers in one world and only nine in another, that's supposedly a problem for genuine cross-world identity, since havng a certain number of fingers isn't the kind of property that should only hold "relative to a world." The transworld identity side thinks this whole move is a dodge — the Kripke line is basically that when I ask what would've happened to HER, I mean her, and she couldn't care less whether some other woman merely resembling her ends up in Marseille instead. But then I don't fully get how their side explains what actually makes it the same woman across worlds if not resemblance — Plantinga's answer seems to be something like a primitive "individual essence" she carries with her no matter what, which sounds a little like cheating too, and I also don't get what's supposed to happen on Lewis's view if two equally good counterparts of her turn up in the same world.

Anyway — could you write me a dialogue between a die-hard counterpart theorist and a die-hard transworld-identity theorist actually hashing this out, maybe stuck sharing a cab after a metaphysics conference? I want them evenly matched, genuinely responding to each other's specific points (the Humphrey-style objection, the accidental-intrinsics move, the multiple-counterparts worry, whether "individual essence" is doing real explanatory work or is just a label for the mystery) rather than each just restating their own view at the other. It's fine, actually preferable, if it doesn't resolve neatly — that's sort of the point.
