For a feature I'm reporting on whether large language models can be said to understand anything at all, I keep running into the same touchstone whenever I interview cognitive scientists and AI researchers: Stevan Harnad's 1990 paper on the symbol grounding problem. As several of them have described it to me, Harnad showed that a system built entirely out of symbols manipulated according to their shapes rather than their meanings can never make genuine contact with the world those symbols are supposedly about, because chasing down what any one symbol means only ever turns up more symbols — his own illustration was trying to learn Chinese from a Chinese-Chinese dictionary, which teaches you nothing if you don't already know the language you're looking words up in. Since large language models are trained purely on text, with a token's "meaning" fixed only by its statistical relations to other tokens rather than by any contact with a sensor, a body, or the thing itself, more than one source presented this to me as an airtight, decades-old case against machine understanding: Harnad's argument, on their account, had already proven back in 1990 that no system trained this way could ever cross over into real reference, no matter its scale, its architecture, or how convincingly it talks. One person went so far as to call it "the impossibility result the field keeps rediscovering and forgetting." I gather Harnad has even revisited the question himself somewhere written specifically about ChatGPT, though so far I've only managed to track down the abstract.

If that is really what the 1990 paper established, I am having trouble understanding why the understanding of language models is still treated as a live research question rather than a closed one: why interpretability researchers keep designing new experiments around it, why philosophers keep publishing rival accounts of what grounding would even require, and why Harnad's own later writing on the subject apparently reads like an open inquiry rather than a victory lap. Could you help me work out what is generating that mismatch, and, to the extent my summary of the argument needs correcting, what the more accurate account of what the symbol grounding problem does and does not establish for systems like these would be?
