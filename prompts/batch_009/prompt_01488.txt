I've been chewing on this for a while and it kind of breaks my brain. If neuroscience is right that every physical event (say, a neuron firing) already has a complete physical cause, how does my desire for coffee ALSO cause my arm to move and make the coffee? Feels like the mental cause just gets squeezed out by the physical cause sitting underneath it — unless the two are literally the same thing. But then people say mental stuff can't just BE physical stuff, since the same desire could be built out of totally different material in different brains (or in an alien, or a robot). So the mental can't reduce to any one physical property, but it also can't seem to do any real causal work of its own. I think this is called the exclusion argument or something (some guy named Kim?). Anyway, could you write me a dialogue between two people hashing this out: one's a non-reductive physicalist trying to save real mental causation without reducing minds to brains, the other thinks that's hopeless and says you gotta bite the bullet — reduce the mental after all, or just accept it doesn't really cause anything. Make them actually engage with each others strongest moves (like whether 'overdetermination' is a cheap way out, or whether some other theory of causation saves the day), not just repeat talking points past each other.
