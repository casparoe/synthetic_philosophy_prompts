I've been reading about Frege beyond his formal logic, and something keeps nagging at me. In the last year of his life he kept a private diary, not published until decades later, showing him to have been a committed antisemite and opponent of parliamentary democracy who wanted Jews stripped of political rights. Michael Dummett — the philosopher most responsible for bringing Frege into English-language philosophy, and himself a lifelong anti-racism campaigner — has said that discovering this shook him personally, though he maintained it left the validity of Frege's logic untouched and merely made Frege harder to read with the same sympathy. I'm not fully convinced that's the end of the matter. Frege built his career on attacking "psychologism," holding that there is exactly one objective, mind-independent realm of logical truth which a thinker either grasps or simply gets wrong. Could someone make a serious case that this same conviction — that there is one correct standpoint, and that everyone who disagrees is simply mistaken — is of a piece with the intolerance running through his politics? Or is that connection just a coincidence of one man's biography, with no real bearing on how we ought to read his philosophy? Please set out the strongest version of both sides, and tell me where you come down.
