I recently adopted a ten-year-old rescue dog, and while reading about the ethics of caring for an aging pet I came across Kant's remark that a horse or dog which has served faithfully for years deserves continued care and something like gratitude, rather than being cast aside once it can no longer work. Since Kant is clearly granting the animal a claim to decent treatment for its own sake in that passage, I'm curious how this direct duty toward animals is supposed to square with the rest of his ethical system, where only rational, self-conscious beings count as ends in themselves. Did he ever actually resolve that tension, or is it a standing inconsistency in his account of our obligations to animals?
