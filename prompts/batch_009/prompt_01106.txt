A few years ago, on a forum somewhere, I came across an argument for a strange kind of cooperation people call 'evidential cooperation in large worlds,' and I still haven't shaken it off. The idea, roughly: if the universe or multiverse is large enough, it almost certainly contains countless civilizations we can never observe, contact, or causally affect, many holding values quite different from our own. If enough of them reason the way we do, then acting on behalf of a broad compromise among many possible values, not only our own, gives us evidence, though never a cause, that similarly-minded civilizations elsewhere are doing the same for ours. No message passes between us, and there is no way of ever checking whether it worked, yet it can still be rational to quietly give up something we want for values we don't even hold, as a kind of trade with people we will never meet.

I'd like to see what this looks like from the inside, as fiction rather than argument. Write a short story, in the tradition of hard science fiction built around a single unsettling idea (the way 'dark forest' game theory drives so much of The Three-Body Problem), centered on a character or civilization that takes this reasoning seriously enough to act on it, and that has to live without ever learning whether anyone out there held up their end.
