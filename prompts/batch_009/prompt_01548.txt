A close friend from university died suddenly last month, and at the memorial someone read out the line Einstein wrote to the family of his friend Michele Besso, shortly after Besso died: that 'the distinction between past, present and future is only a stubbornly persistent illusion.' It was offered as consolation; instead, it sent me into the philosophical literature on presentism and eternalism, where I have found more confusion than comfort. If eternalism is correct and my friend's entire life is simply an unchanging region of the block universe, I cannot tell whether my grief tracks any real loss, as opposed to a mere fact about where I am located in spacetime relative to him. Presentism has the opposite difficulty: if only the present moment is real, it is unclear what makes it true, right now, that he ever existed at all. I would like a short story that takes this dilemma seriously, through a character with real stakes in resolving it, rather than settling for the "everything happens at once" shorthand common to time-travel fiction — the metaphysics should drive the plot, not merely decorate it.
