In my philosophy of language seminar, we've been working through the debate over indicative conditionals, and I want to check my understanding of David Lewis's triviality results. As I understand it, Lewis proved that no proposition can have a probability that always matches the corresponding conditional probability P(C|A) — the so-called "Equation" — and that this is precisely why he belongs with Adams and Edgington among those who deny that indicative conditionals express propositions at all. But other sources I've read list Lewis together with Grice and Jackson as defenders of a truth-conditional analysis of "if" (specifically, the material conditional), which seems like exactly the kind of view his own triviality proof should rule out. I'd appreciate help seeing where my understanding has gone wrong.
