I've been part of an informal reading group—mostly people I know from LessWrong and the old Overcoming Bias comment threads—working through Jaynes's *Probability Theory* alongside some of the formal epistemology literature on updating on uncertain evidence. Lately we've gotten stuck on van Fraassen's "Judy Benjamin problem," and I'd like your help settling an argument that's been going back and forth among us for weeks. Here's the setup: Judy's unit has parachuted into a region split evenly into Red and Blue territory, and each half is further split into a Headquarters Company sector and a Second Company sector, so that beforehand she assigns probability 1/4 to each of the four resulting quadrants. She radios her own headquarters for a fix on her position, and headquarters reports back, "We can't tell whether you're in Red or Blue territory, but if you're in Red, it's three times as likely that you're in the Headquarters sector as in the Second Company sector" — at which point contact is lost. If Judy updates by finding the distribution that minimizes relative entropy (the "minimum cross-entropy," maximum-entropy-style update) relative to her prior, subject only to that new 3:1 constraint on the two Red quadrants, the striking result is that her credence in being in Blue territory rises above one-half — even though the message is, on its face, entirely about the internal breakdown of Red territory and says nothing about Red versus Blue directly. Van Fraassen introduced this as a serious embarrassment for relative-entropy updating (and, more broadly, for the project of extending Richard Jeffrey's rule to evidence that arrives in conditional form), and much of the literature that followed him — Grove and Halpern's alternative "sophisticated" treatment of conditioning among it — has instead tried to engineer update rules that leave Judy's Red-versus-Blue odds exactly fixed at 1:1.

I'd like you to argue the unfashionable side: please make the strongest case you can that the shift toward Blue is exactly the rationally required response to headquarters' message, rather than an artifact of an ill-chosen distance measure, and that the rule-designers who keep Judy's Red-versus-Blue credence pinned at one-half are the ones making a mistake. I'm especially interested in you addressing head-on the intuition that seems to be doing all the work for everyone on the other side — that a piece of evidence which is "only about" the internal ratio within Red territory cannot rationally bear on Red versus Blue at all — and explaining exactly what's wrong with that intuition, or what it would take to vindicate it after all. If you can, close by generalizing: what, in your view, licenses an update to redistribute credence over propositions that a piece of evidence doesn't explicitly mention?
