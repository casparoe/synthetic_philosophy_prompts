hey so for my aesthetics seminar we just started the Schopenhauer unit and I think I bit off more than I can chew. everyone (like, every summary you find online) reduces his whole view on music down to "music is a direct copy of the will, not of the Platonic Ideas like the other arts," and honestly I had that soundbite memorized before I'd even opened The World as Will and Representation. But actually sitting down with §52 (bk 3) for tmrw's discussion section, the real argument is so much stranger than the one-liner, and I don't wanna just repeat the Wikipedia version if the prof calls on me to unpack it. (I've been using the free Haldane/Kemp translation online instead of buying the assigned one, so sorry if the wording's a bit archaic -- fun fact, that translation calls the book "The World As Will And Idea" instead of "Representation," same book though.)

Here's the bit that's actually messing with me:

"music also, since it passes over the Ideas, is entirely independent of the phenomenal world, ignores it altogether, could to a certain extent exist if there was no world at all... Music is as direct an objectification and copy of the whole will as the world itself, nay, even as the Ideas... Music is thus by no means like the other arts, the copy of the Ideas, but the copy of the will itself, whose objectivity the Ideas are. This is why the effect of music is so much more powerful and penetrating than that of the other arts, for they speak only of shadows, but it speaks of the thing itself."

And then a couple pages later he lays out this whole analogy where the deep bass = "unorganised nature, the mass of the planet," the inner voices of the harmony = the plant and animal kingdoms, and the melody on top = the "intellectual life and effort of man" -- basically the entire chain of being stacked into a single chord.

What I can't square is this: earlier in the book he's adamant that the Ideas are only accessible once you strip away time, causality, and what he calls the principium individuationis -- so they're already one whole step removed from ordinary representation. But music, as actually heard, seems like about the most temporally individuated thing there is: it's literally just organized note-sequences unfolding in time, and he even keeps stressing how rule-bound and quasi-arithmetical it's form is. So how does music get to skip past the Ideas and count as just as direct a copy of the will as the Ideas themselves -- when, by his own logic, a rule-governed, time-bound thing like a melody should sit lower in the hierarchy, not tie for first place? Is that a genuine hole in the theory, or is there some standard move philosophers make to patch it up? Bonus question if you've got thoughts: is the bass-notes/inorganic-nature pairing actually supposed to carry argumentative weight (something about overtones "containing" the higher notes, mirroring how simpler stuff underlies more complex stuff), or is it really just leaning on the coincidence that we happen to call some notes "low" and others "high"?
