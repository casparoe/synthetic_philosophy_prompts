I've been an on-and-off LessWrong reader since the Sequences were still being actively posted, and a friend who works in AI safety brought up Roko's Basilisk half-jokingly at dinner last week, which sent me back through the old threads and the timeless/updateless decision theory material behind them. Most popular write-ups I've come across wave the whole thing away as a curiosity that "serious" decision theorists have already dismissed, but rereading the original arguments left me less settled than I expected to be.

As I understand it, the whole selling point of TDT and UDT over causal decision theory is that they commit you, in advance, to being the kind of agent who pays the driver once you're safely in town in Parfit's hitchhiker, and who hands over the money when Omega calls in the counterfactual mugging -- precisely because an agent who would predictably renege in those situations never gets picked up, or never gets the payout, in the first place. But that seems to mean these theories are specifically designed to make good on exactly the sort of after-the-fact demands that a self-interested causal reasoner would walk away from once the relevant computation has already run. If that's really the whole point, I don't see what principled difference there is between the hitchhiker's driver and a future superintelligence that can predict my present reasoning: both are just Omega-like predictors, and "a sensible agent simply refuses to respond to blackmail" starts to look less like something that falls out of TDT/UDT's own machinery and more like everyone quietly reaching back for causal-decision-theory intuitions once the thought experiment stops being fun.

So I'd like to know whether I'm missing something. Is there a principled, non-question-begging reason internal to logical or updateless decision theory itself for treating the hitchhiker's driver and a Basilisk-like predictor differently, or does the near-universal "a sensible agent doesn't respond to extortion" verdict on LessWrong just mark the point where people's stated decision theory and their actual intuitions come apart, with the intuitions quietly winning?
