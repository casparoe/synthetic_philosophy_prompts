I've suddenly got a lot more free time than I'd like, so I signed up for a philosophy night class at the community college, and this week's reading was Moritz Schlick's essay "On the Meaning of Life." His basic idea is that a life is meaningful to the extent you manage to turn what you're doing into "play" -- activity that's done for its own sake instead of as a means to some further goal -- and he actually singles out art and craft work as the clearest examples of this, while being pretty harsh on "mechanical, brutalizing" industrial labor, saying that kind of thing is basically slave labor dressed up and civilization would be better off without it.

Which got me stuck on something. So genuinely, what do you think Schlick would say if someone explained this situation to him?
