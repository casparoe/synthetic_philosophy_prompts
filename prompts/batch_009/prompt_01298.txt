Popper's qualitative definition of verisimilitude was meant to explain how one false scientific theory could count as closer to the truth than a rival false theory, by comparing the true and false logical consequences that each theory entails -- what Popper called its "truth content" and "falsity content." In 1974, David Miller and Pavel Tichý independently proved that this definition has a fatal consequence: it entails that no false theory can ever have greater verisimilitude than another false theory. Rather than reconstructing their formal proof, please give me a gut-level, intuitive explanation of why Popper's setup forces this collapse -- what is it about comparing truth and falsity content in this way that makes it impossible for one falsehood to ever improve on another? I'd also be curious, briefly, whether the similarity-based "likeness" approaches that Tichý and others developed in response actually escape this problem, or merely relocate it.
