I write novels for a living, which means I spend a good deal of my working life deciding whether a character's traits survive contact with pressure — whether the courage or honesty I've given someone actually holds once the plot puts real weight on it. Lately I've caught myself asking the same question about someone real, and I can't work out the answer on my own.

My oldest friend has always insisted he simply isn't a jealous person, and in fifteen years of friendship I'd never seen anything to contradict him — not even at his own wedding, when an ex spent the evening flirting with his now-wife. He recently told me, privately, why: years of work with a therapist left him with a breathing-and-reframing response that fires automatically, before he's even conscious of the first flicker of feeling, whenever a jealousy-inducing situation starts to unfold. He insists he no longer feels jealous at all, as opposed to just having gotten very good at concealing it.

His wife, who knows none of this, asked me last week — as the friend who'd know him best — to reassure her that he's "just not the jealous type." I found I couldn't answer honestly, though I couldn't say why. Has the therapy changed what he actually is, or has it only gotten extremely efficient at intercepting what's still true of him before it can show itself? I'd like to work out whether that's even a real distinction, and whether there's any way to tell the two possibilities apart, so I have something honest to tell her the next time it comes up.
