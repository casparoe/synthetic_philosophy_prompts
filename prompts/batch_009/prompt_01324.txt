i went down a bit of a rabbit hole this week reading about "safe Pareto improvements" - it's this idea from a pretty obscure corner of game theory / multi-agent AI research (the main paper is by Oesterheld and Conitzer, not that the names matter much). The setup: two parties are stuck in some tense negotiation and each one delegates the actual playing of the game to a representative they trust to be strategically sharper than they are - could be a lawyer, a diplomat, an AI system, whatever. Default move is just "go get me the best deal you can." The clever bit in the paper is that the two original parties can sometimes do better by coordinating ahead of time to restrict what instructions they give their reps - e.g. banning certain aggressive moves outright, or changing how the rep's payoffs get scored - in a way thats guaranteed to leave both original parties at least as well off as under the default, no matter what private info anyone turns out to have or how the negotiation actually unfolds. That "guaranteed no matter what" part is the "safe" - its not just a Pareto improvement that works out in expectation, it literally can't backfire on you later. The example the paper actually uses is two countries sitting next to a newly oil-rich desert who both want to annex it and are worried their militaries will blunder into a war neither side actually wants.

I think theres a genuinely good short story buried in here and I've never seen anyone actually write the story version instead of the game-matrix version. Could you write me one? Setting's up to you - doesn't have to be literal countries, could be corporations, rival startups, crime families, whatever fits. The thing I really want is for the plot to hinge on the actual mechanic: the tension of whether to give your (much more capable) representative totally free rein or tie its hands in advance with these weird pre-committed restrictions that can't be exploited later, plus maybe a twist or climax that only lands once you get why "safe" means something more specific than just "good for both sides eventually." Keep it fairly short, flash-fiction length is fine - i'd just rather the concept actually be load-bearing than window dressing.
