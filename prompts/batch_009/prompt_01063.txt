Okay so I've fallen down a rabbit hole lately reading old rationalist and effective-altruism blog posts about decision theory, and there's an idea that's been stuck in my head for days: an "acausal threat," where some agent, like a future superintelligence, or honestly anyone you could never actually meet or causally interact with, can still threaten you and get what it wants, purely because you can each model what the other would do. The standard answer I keep running into is "just be the kind of agent that never, ever backs down to a threat, and then no rational threatener bothers threatening you in the first place." But then I found this other idea called "surrogate goals," where instead of relying only on that hardline stance, you deliberately make yourself also care about some made up decoy goal, so if a threat ever does land, it lands on the decoy instead of on what you actually value. Me and my housemate got into it about which of these actually works better and neither of us has any background in this stuff so we just went in circles for like an hour - he thinks surrogate goals are basically self delusion that a threatener smart enough to be scary would see straight through, and I think "just never back down" quietly assumes the threatener already knows you're 100% committed, which seems like a big assumption to just build in.

Could you write this out as an actual dialogue between two people who each seriously hold one of these views? One thinks the hardline "never back down" commitment is enough by itself and that surrogate goals are an unnecessary (maybe even counterproductive) extra step, the other thinks you need surrogate goals as a backup no matter how hardline you are, because that commitment can never be perfectly credible or perfectly known to whoever's threatening you. I want them to actually engage with each others specific points, like whether a threatener could ever tell a "real" goal apart from a decoy one, what happens when neither side is sure what the other actually knows, and whether deliberately growing a decoy goal counts as a kind of backing down in itself. Doesnt need to be set anywhere special, two people hashing it out at a meetup or over text is fine, I just want the actual back and forth to be sharp and for neither one of them to obviously be the strawman.
