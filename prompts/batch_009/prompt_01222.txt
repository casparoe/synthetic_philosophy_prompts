I've been working through some of Aquinas's political writings on my own, and as I understand him, a ruler who governs for his own benefit rather than the common good forfeits the legitimacy that makes obedience owed to him, which is why Aquinas treats killing such a ruler as a praiseworthy act rather than murder. Shouldn't consistency with Aquinas's own premises commit the tradition to endorsing tyrannicide far more readily than it actually has?
