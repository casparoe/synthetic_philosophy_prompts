Been down a rationalist-blog rabbit hole on "acausal trade" lately -- the idea that two agents who have zero causal contact (no way to signal, communicate, or affect each other at all) can still end up cooperating, just because each one reasons about what the other would predictably do and acts on that. It's kept nagging at me because it reminded me of Leibnizs monads from a philosophy gen-ed I took forever ago: substances that supposedly have no "windows" onto each other, so they literally cant interact, yet the universe stays in perfect sync because god pre-arranged a "harmony" between them at creation. Feels like both ideas are answers to the same weird question -- how do you get coordination with zero causation -- just from totally different directions. If Leibniz somehow got a crash course in modern decision theory and acausal trade, do you think he'd call it basically a rediscovery of his own pre-established harmony, or a completely different (maybe even confused) idea, since nobody's positing a god arranging things ahead of time? And would he think it's actually rational for people to act on that kind of reasoning, or would he say the whole thing falls apart without something like god holding it together?
