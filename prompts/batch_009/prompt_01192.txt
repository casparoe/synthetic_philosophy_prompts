I spent 35 years as an experimental physicist leaning on Bayesian stats without ever askign why credences have to obey the probability axioms in the first place. Turns out there's more than one argument for that. The classic one is the Dutch book argument - if your credences don't fit the probability calculus, a bookie can hand you a set of bets that guarantee you loose money no matter what happens. But I recently ran into Jim Joyce's "accuracy-dominance" argument from the 90s, which claims to reach the very same conclusion without any betting or money at all, just accuracy as a purely epistemic good. Could you compare the two for me - how different are they really underneath, does the accuracy argument actually escape the pragmatic baggage of the Dutch book one like Joyce says it does, and is one more convincing to you than the other?
