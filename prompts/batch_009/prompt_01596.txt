A friend and I spent last weekend arguing about her faith: she offered a cumulative case built from the historical evidence for the Resurrection and the fine-tuning of the physical constants, while I insisted that no such evidence could license the certainty she placed in it, and we ended up only repeating ourselves more loudly. Rereading Wittgenstein's Lectures on Religious Belief afterward, I was struck by his claim that a believer's "unshakeable belief" in something like the Last Judgment isn't held on ordinary evidential grounds at all, and by his suggestion that it isn't even clear a believer and an atheist mean the same thing when they seem to disagree. Please describe the most important implications of Wittgenstein's views on religious belief for the argument we had, including whether we were ever really contradicting each other, whether either of us was entitled to treat the dispute as a matter of evidence and probability, and what the strongest objections to this Wittgensteinian diagnosis (such as the charge of "fideism") might be.
