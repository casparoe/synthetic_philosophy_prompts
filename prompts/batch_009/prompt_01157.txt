In A Theory of Justice, Rawls has the parties in the original position, deliberating behind the veil of ignorance, arrive at the difference principle by reasoning according to the maximin rule: they choose whichever arrangement makes the worst-off position as good as possible. But maximin is just the strategy an expected-utility maximizer would follow given an extremely risk-averse utility function, so it seems that Rawls's entire argument for the difference principle ultimately amounts to stipulating that the parties are highly risk-averse. If that is right, why do political philosophers continue to treat the original position as yielding a genuine alternative to utilitarian reasoning under uncertainty, rather than simply a utilitarian calculation performed under an idiosyncratic assumption about risk preferences?
