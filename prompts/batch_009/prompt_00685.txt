My grandpa built a small manufacturing business from nothing, and he's always said it'll go to whichever of his grandkids he judges to genuinely love the craft, not the ones who are just after the money. He's kind of freakishly good at reading people too, like he called my aunt's divorce two full years before it happened and knew my cousin would flunk out of law school before she'd even picked which one to attend. Over Thanksgiving he dropped a bombshell: he's already had his lawyer draw up the transfer paperwork based entirely on his own read of each of us and locked it away, and since he's never once in his life gone back on a decision, nothing any of us do between now and when he retires is gonna change whats already sealed in that envelope.

My cousin Danny's take is that this makes the rest of us showing up on weekends to help out at the shop completely pointless from here on out. His reasoning is that the decision's already locked in one way or another, so acting extra devoted now can't change what's sealed in that envelope, so the smart move is to stop wasting your Saturdays and go enjoy your life instead. Everyone at dinner kind of nodded along because it's hard to argue with the logic, but it also rubbed me the wrong way and I couldn't say why. Was talking about it with a friend afterward and neither of us could figure out: is Danny actually being rational here, or is he missing something?
