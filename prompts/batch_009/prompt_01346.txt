So I'm about two-thirds through a novel and I've hit a scene I keep chickening out of writing because I don't trust myself to make both sides actually smart about it. Basic setup: my protagonist, Nora, spent almost twenty years forging the work of a minor (invented) Dutch golden-age painter, good enough that one of her pieces hung in a small regional museum for a decade before it got flagged in a pigment test. Her older brother Willem is the curator who authenticated it and got quietly forced out over the scandal. They haven't spoken in years, and the book's climax is a dinner where it finally comes out and they actually argue about it, not just yell.

I want their argument to track a real philosophical fault line instead of just being "stealing is bad" vs "art is art." From what I remember (I took one aesthetics class like a decade ago so forgive me if I'm fuzzing the details) theres a genuine split here: Alfred Lessing's line is basically that aesthetic value just tracks the perceivable properties of the object, so if nobody can tell Nora's painting from a real one, its aesthetically on par with the genuine article, and the only thing "wrong" with it is the fraud and money involved, which he treats as a totally separate question. Then there's Nelson Goodman's reply (with maybe a bit of Denis Dutton mixed in, the idea that appreciating art is bound up with appreciating an achievement) which says no, actually, the history of how the object came into being isn't separable from its aesthetic identity, so even a currently undetectable fake is a different, lesser thing, and the fact we can't tell yet just means our eyes aren't trained on the right feature yet, not that there's no difference.

Could you write the dinner-table dialogue between Nora (arguing the Lessing side, defending her work) and Willem (arguing the Goodman/Dutton side) for me? I want real back and forth, where each of them has to actually engage with the other's strongest point instead of just restating their own — e.g. Willem should have to grapple with the fact that nobody gets worked up about a "forged" pressing of a record or a paperback copy of a novel the same way they do about a painting, and Nora should have to grapple with why people still feel cheated by fakes that nobody, ever, could detect. No need for a resolution, I'd honestly prefer it stays a draw. Something like a page, maybe a page and a half.
