I've been chewing on the Judy Benjamin problem again after it got name-dropped in an old LessWrong thread I was rereading, and I keep flip-flopping on who's actually right, so instead of reading position papers past each other in my head I want to see two people who actually know what they're doing hash it out loud.

Quick recap in case it's useful for framing: Judy's platoon gets dropped somewhere in a region thats split into Red territory and Blue territory, and each of those is split again into a "Headquarters Company" area and a "Second Company" area, so 4 equally likely quadrants going in, 25% each. Command radios her: "I can't be sure where you are, but if you're in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 you're in the Headquarters Company area" -- and then the signal cuts out. If you update by minimizing cross-entropy (relative/KL divergence) against the prior subject to matching that 3:1 constraint, which is supposedly the natural generalization of conditioning to this kind of "soft" conditional testimony, you get the famously weird result that Judy's credence she's in Blue territory actually goes *up*, from 50% to something like 53%, even though the message was 100% about the internal Red split and literally never mentioned Blue. Van Fraassen has this bit about how it looks like Judy's just engaging in wishful thinking about being in the safe territory.

So here's what I want: a dialogue between two characters (set it wherever, a rationalist meetup, an email thread, whatever) who represent the two live camps on this. One of them should really bite the bullet for the cross-entropy answer -- maybe hes more the Jaynes-maximum-entropy-worshipping type -- and argue the shift toward Blue is a genuine, if counterintuitive, requirement of updating correctly on this kind of evidence, not a bug to be explained away. The other should defend something closer to the Grove-Halpern/Douven-Romeijn line, that no legitimate update rule should ever let P(Red) vs P(Blue) move here, since the testimony was entirely about the internal Red split, and that any formalism implying otherwise has just been refuted, however elegant the math. I want them actually engaging each other's strongest moves -- stuff like reflection violations, whether "the odds are 3:1" should even be read as a report of a conditional probability versus something more like a conditional bet or a material conditional, whether the "obviously this can't touch Blue" intuition is just another bad probabilistic intuition we ought to override -- rather than each side just restating its priors with more confidence. Neither one should be a pushover for the other; I want to come away from it still genuinely unsure who has the better of it, same as I am now.
