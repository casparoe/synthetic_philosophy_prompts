I've been falling down a philosophy-of-math rabbit hole and hit something that genuinely surprised me: Hugh Woodin, one of the biggest names in set theory, has apparently argued *both* sides of the continuum hypothesis over the course of his career. Early on he used Omega-logic and "forcing absoluteness" to build a case that CH is false; more recently he switched and started arguing for CH instead, based on his "Ultimate L" program (basically a souped-up version of Godel's constructible universe, one that's compatible with large cardinal axioms in a way the original L wasn't). Then on the complete other side of the field there's Joel David Hamkins, who thinks the whole hunt for a "true" axiom that settles CH one way or the other is misguided from the start -- his "set-theoretic multiverse" view says there's no single privileged universe of sets, just a bunch of equally real ones, and CH is basically a switch that flips depending on which universe you're standing in. On his view CH is already "settled," just not in the way anyone was hoping for. I mentioned this to a friend doing a math PhD and she just shrugged and said something like "yeah, that's set theory for you," which did not help.

What I actually want to know is whether Woodin (or someone defending his kind of view) and Hamkins are disagreeing about something real here, or whether they've just quietly adopted different meanings of words like "true" and "exists" and are arguing past each other -- the way two people can have a merely verbal dispute that looks substantive from the outside. I definately don't have the background to referee this myself. Could you write me a dialogue between a Woodin-style "universe" theorist and a Hamkins-style multiverse pluralist where that exact question -- substantive vs merely verbal -- is what they're explicitly trying to sort out together? I want real back and forth, each one responding to the other's actual argument instead of just restating their own position, and I want some real technical texture in there (forcing, what would make a new axiom "evident" rather than merely convenient, what a determinate truth value for CH would even consist in) rather than just slogans. Neither one should obviously win or come off as the weaker position by the end.
