I went down a bit of a rabbit hole this week reading about Integrated Information Theory and got stuck on what I'd call the "phi rewards really dumb systems" problem. The most famous version is Scott Aaronson's old blog reductio: he described a specially constructed "expander graph" of logic gates that, by IIT's own math, scores a higher phi than an actual human brain, and Tononi's side didn't even dispute the math - they conceded it, with Tononi adding that a plain 2D grid of gates would do it too, and then argued that our intuitions about what should count as conscious just aren't a reliable guide here. Aaronson's comeback was that this is inconsistent, since Tononi's own go-to defenses of IIT (like "obviously the cerebellum isn't conscious, see, the theory gets that right") lean on the exact same kind of intuition he's telling everyone else not to trust. Then seperately there's the newer "unfolding argument," which points out that any recurrent (loopy) network can be "unfolded" into a feedforward network computing the exact same input-output function, and feedforward networks always have zero phi under IIT - so the theory ends up saying two systems that behave identically in absolutely every possible test could still differ on whether there's anything it's like to be them, with no experiment ever able to tell you which one it is.

Could you write me a dialogue between a committed IIT theorist and a skeptic who presses exactly these two lines of attack on them? I don't want either side to be a pushover - give the theorist real substantive comebacks (not just "trust the math, common sense doesn't get a vote"), and have the skeptic keep pushing on whether those comebacks basically make phi unfalsifiable or just quietly redefine "consciousness" into something else entirely. Something like 10-ish exchanges should be enough, as long as they're actually responding to whatever the other person just said rather than each restating their opening position over and over.
