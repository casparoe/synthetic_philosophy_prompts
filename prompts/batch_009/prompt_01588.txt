A friend and I spent a long evening arguing about Gongsun Long's white horse paradox and whether Xunzi's "Rectifying Names" chapter actually refutes it. My position was that "a white horse is not a horse" trades on a genuine distinction between denying that "horse" and "white horse" name the very same thing and denying that white horses fall under the class of horses, and that Xunzi's standard reply — that people simply call a white horse a horse — never engages that distinction at all. My friend argued this misses Xunzi's real point: for him, names are conventions fixed by the sage-kings to secure social and moral order, so a "distinction" that unsettles settled usage is a problem regardless of its logical coherence. We left off unsure whether we were even disagreeing about the same thing. Could you write a dialogue between Gongsun Long and Xunzi that stages this dispute properly — with each pressing the other on their strongest points — and has them try to work out whether their disagreement is substantive or largely a matter of talking past one another?
