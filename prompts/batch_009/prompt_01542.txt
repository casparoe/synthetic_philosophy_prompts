Longtime reader of the rationalist blogosphere here, where Sharon Street's 2006 "Darwinian Dilemma" paper gets invoked often enough that I'd come to treat the matter as essentially closed: since our evaluative attitudes were shaped by natural selection for reproductive fitness rather than for detecting independent moral facts, non-naturalist moral realism simply cannot be true. So I was surprised to find Derek Parfit devoting a substantial part of On What Matters to defending exactly that kind of realism well after Street's argument was already widely known. Was Parfit just failing to engage with her strongest point, or is there a serious rebuttal to the Darwinian Dilemma that keeps this debate genuinely open rather than settled?
