I am preparing a lecture on the philosophy of set theory and want to make sure I present the continuum problem accurately to my students. As I understand the history, Cohen's method of forcing, together with Gödel's earlier consistency proof, established that the Continuum Hypothesis (CH) is independent of ZFC. Since neither CH nor its negation can be derived from our axioms, there is, as I currently intend to tell my students, simply no fact of the matter about whether CH is true. Given this, I am unsure how to explain why a set theorist such as Woodin would spend decades defending particular new axioms as ways of settling CH one way or the other; it seems that such work must amount to advocacy for a preferred axiom system rather than genuine mathematical discovery. Could you help me think through whether this is the right way to frame the matter for my students, and if not, where exactly my reasoning goes wrong?
