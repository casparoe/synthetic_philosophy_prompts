My friend and I had a long argument last night about two-dimensional semantics that we never quite resolved. She pointed out that Twin Earth is a possible world in which 'water' picks out XYZ instead of H2O, and concluded that the distinction between primary and secondary intensions actually shows that 'water is H2O' is only contingently true, undercutting the necessity Kripke claimed for it as a paradigm case of the necessary a posteriori. I couldn't work out what was wrong with this, especially since two-dimensionalists like Chalmers and Jackson themselves treat Twin Earth as a genuine possibility. Given that there really does seem to be a possible world where 'water' fails to denote H2O, should I concede that two-dimensional semantics undermines, rather than vindicates, Kripke's claim that identity statements between natural-kind terms are metaphysically necessary?
