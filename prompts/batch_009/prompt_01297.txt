I spent my career as an experimental physicist, and I have never quite lost the habit of asking whether a claim is falsifiable. Lately I have been reading about the debate over whether large language models genuinely understand language, or are merely "stochastic parrots." One thought experiment that keeps coming up, due to Bender and Koller, involves a hyperintelligent octopus that taps an undersea cable between two islanders and learns, purely from the statistics of their messages, to mimic one side of the conversation — until, famously, one islander is chased by a bear and asks for help building a weapon from sticks, a request the octopus cannot handle because it has no grasp of what the words refer to. Current models, however, now handle exactly this kind of open-ended, real-world request quite well. Doesn't that mean the octopus argument has simply been falsified by the evidence, the way a good empirical hypothesis should be when it turns out to be wrong? I suspect I am missing something about what kind of claim was actually being made.
