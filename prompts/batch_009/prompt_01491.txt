For a piece I'm writing on AI reward hacking, I've been reading about Goodhart's Law, including the taxonomy David Manheim and Scott Garrabrant proposed, which splits it into regressional, extremal, causal, and adversarial variants. As far as I can tell, what unites all four under one law is that some strategic actor recognizes a measure has become a target and exploits the resulting gap between proxy and goal — the way a factory manager pads output figures once she learns which number head office is watching. That reading fits classic cases like Soviet quota-gaming neatly enough, but I'm unsure how researchers can apply the same term to something like OpenAI's CoastRunners boat, which looped endlessly in a lagoon farming respawning targets instead of finishing the race: the agent had no awareness that its score stood in for anything else, so there doesn't seem to be any strategic exploitation going on at all. Shouldn't cases like the boat count as a separate phenomenon from genuine Goodharting, rather than being folded into the same law?
