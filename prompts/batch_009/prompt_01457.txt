As a software engineer working on reinforcement learning systems, I recently encountered the absent-minded driver problem in decision theory - in particular, the well-known gap between the 'planning-optimal' strategy an agent commits to in advance and the 'action-optimal' strategy implied once it recalculates its beliefs at the decision point itself. Please describe the most important implications of this gap for how we should design and evaluate policies in POMDPs with state aliasing, where distinct underlying states look identical to the agent, and indicate whether you think the inconsistency it exposes is a genuine problem for expected-utility maximization or merely an artifact of self-locating belief.
