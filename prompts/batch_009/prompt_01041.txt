so in my seminar on early analytic ethics we just finished the last chapter of Principia Ethica (the "Ideal" chapter) and somethings bugging me. Moore basically treats personal affection the same way he treats aesthetic appreciation -- loving someone is just admiring contemplation of their good qualities, same structure as admiring a beautiful painting. but if thats the whole story then it seems like his own view commits him to saying that if my friend has qualities x, y and z, and some new person shows up with the exact same qualities to a slightly higher degree, i should admire (and therefore love) the new person more -- i.e. i should basically be willing to trade up to whoever's most admirable. that seems to wreck any normal notion of loyalty or caring about someone as a specific individual rather than a bundle of properties. can you explain exactly which of Moore's commitments generate this "trade up" problem, and whether a Moore-style organic-unities view could block the result without just abandoning the admiring-contemplation account of love altogether?
