Hey, kind of a random request but this has been living in my head all week. I'm a philosophy PhD student working on just war theory / ethics of war, and for a seminar paper I've been re-reading Anscombe's "Mr. Truman's Degree" -- the pamphlet she wrote after she and, by most accounts, three other dons voted "non placet" when Oxford's Convocation gave Truman his honorary degree in 1956. Everyone who's heard of this remembers the headline ("Anscombe called Truman a murderer!") but almost nobody engages with the actual arguement, which is weirder and more interesting than the headline suggests. Her claim isn't just "civilian deaths are bad" -- it's a specific double-effect-style distinction between choosing civilians as your target versus merely foreseeing their deaths as a side effect of hitting a legitimate one ("Choosing to kill the innocent as a means to your ends is always murder," as she puts it). Shes also got this deliberately technical, kind of counterintuitive definition of "the innocent" as "not harming" rather than "morally blameless," so a surrendering soldier counts as innocent even though he's obviously not blameless. And weirdly, she stages the utilitarian comeback herself -- something like, would you boil one baby alive to prevent some disaster befalling a thousand or a million people? -- before rejecting it. Apparently this whole episode is even part of what got her giving the lectures that became Intention the next year, which is a wild bit of philosophical history if true: an honorary-degree scandal basically midwifing the intention/foresight distinction that's since taken over just war theory.

For a slightly self-indulgent side project (not the actual paper), I want to try dramatizing the disagreement instead of just summarizing it. Could you write me a dialogue -- maybe 800-1200 words -- between two invented Oxford dons (not literally Anscombe) on the evening of the vote, hashing it out over drinks somewhere like the Eagle and Child? One of them was among the non placet minority and is defending that position; the other voted placet and is happy to defend both the bombing and the degree, not sheepishly but as someone who genuinely thinks the alternative would've been worse. Don't flatten the pro-Truman don into "the ends justify the means," full stop -- give him the strongest version available: the invasion-casualty projections, the point that "area bombing" of cities had already collapsed the combatant/noncombatant distinction well before Hiroshima, maybe some needling about how easy absolutism is from a common-room armchair. And don't let the Anscombe-adjacent don just repeat "murder is murder" -- I want real engagement with the double-effect distinction, the "not harming" definition of innocence, the boiled-baby thought experiment, and her move of treating "unconditional surrender" as a policy choice rather than a fixed fact of nature.

Basically: two smart people who've actually read each other's position and keep responding to specifics instead of trading slogans. It's fine, maybe even better, if nobody budges by the end.
