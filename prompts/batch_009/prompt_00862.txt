I'm in the final stretch of drafting a detective novel, and a disagreement with my editor has left me second-guessing something I had always taken for granted about conditional sentences. For most of the book, my detective reasons in the indicative mood about a mystery that is, for her, still genuinely open: "If the housekeeper didn't lock the study at ten, then one of the other staff must have a key." In the closing chapter, once the true sequence of events has come to light, I have her revisit that same piece of reasoning in the subjunctive: "If the housekeeper hadn't locked the study at ten, one of the other staff would have had a key." I had always assumed the second sentence simply restates the first for an antecedent we now know to be false — the same underlying claim, narrated from a later vantage point — so that whatever justified the one automatically justified the other. My editor keeps circling the subjunctive versions in these passages as non sequiturs, though she admits she can't say precisely why they don't sit right, only that they "don't follow the way the originals did."

Trying to settle the disagreement on my own, I dug out some notes from the one logic course I took at university, years ago, which never really got past truth tables, and learned that logicians distinguish "indicative" from "counterfactual" conditionals — a split I gather is meant to correspond more or less to the indicative/subjunctive one I've been describing. My instinct, though, is that this is fundamentally a grammatical distinction rather than a logical one: once the antecedent and consequent are fixed, whether a sentence frames a possibility as still open or as contrary-to-fact shouldn't change whether the inference actually goes through, since it's the same possibility being described either way, just narrated at a different point in the story's timeline. If my instinct is correct, I would like a clearer way of making that case to my editor; if it is mistaken, I would like to understand precisely what I am getting wrong about how these two kinds of conditionals relate to one another.
