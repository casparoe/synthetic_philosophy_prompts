In discussions of metaphysical grounding, it is standardly assumed that reality is "well-founded": every grounded fact or entity is ultimately grounded in something fundamental and ungrounded, so that chains of "in virtue of" explanation always terminate. A minority position denies this, holding that grounding chains might instead descend infinitely with no bottom level ever reached (metaphysical infinitism), or loop back on themselves, so that following the chain of "in virtue of" explanations far enough eventually leads back to where one started (metaphysical coherentism).

Write a short story, in any genre or setting, in which a character discovers that their reality has one of these unfounded structures instead of the usual layer-cake picture. Rather than having characters simply state the metaphysics, use plot and concrete detail to convey why giving up the assumption that explanation must eventually bottom out would be disorienting — or perhaps liberating — for someone who had always taken it for granted.
