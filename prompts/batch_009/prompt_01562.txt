So I'm about halfway through a novel and stuck on a subplot I'm hoping you can help me think through. One of my characters, Mara, is a productivity-influencer type who's built a whole app and personal brand around "radical self-optimization" - and it turns out (twist reveal around chapter 9) that her whole system is basically a repackaged version of Ben Franklin's virtue project from his autobiography, the one where he tracked 13 virtues in a little book and marked off whichever ones he'd blown that day. I want a scene, maybe a fever dream or a hallucination after she pulls an all-nighter, where she actually has to reckon with Franklin himself about what she's built, and I really don't want it to be the lazy "founding father is baffled by an iphone" bit. My writing group keeps pushing me to make it feel like its actually grappling with something, not just a gag.

Here's what's nagging me. Franklin wasn't just tracking habits for their own sake, he was after real moral perfection (his own phrase for it), and he's got this whole story in the autobiography about how his list started at twelve virtues until a Quaker friend told him he was "generally thought proud," so he tacked Humility on as number 13 - and then admits, pretty ruefully, that he only ever really mastered the appearance of it rather than the substance. That appearance-vs-reality bit seems like it should matter a lot to how he'd judge an app where people livestream their morning routines and compete over habit streaks in front of an audience, which is sort of the opposite of a private ledger you keep for yourself. Then again, he was also weirdly systematic about the whole thing - he cycled through the 13 virtues one at a time, four times a year, and picked Temperance to go first specifically because it would clear his head for tackling the rest - so maybe he'd be delighted someone finally built the app version of his book.

Could you actually work through what a well-reasoned version of Franklin's reaction would be, rather than just grabbing whichever answer is more dramatically convenient? I'd like it grounded in the actual specifics of how he ran the project rather than vibes, since Mara's supposed to be enough of a Franklin nerd that she'd notice if the ghost got the details wrong.
