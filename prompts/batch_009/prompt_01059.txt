I'm a novelist working out the natural history for a secondary-world fantasy series, and my editor and I are at an impasse. Humans and a related people I call the Fenrai diverged on separate continents roughly two hundred thousand years ago and are now as visually and culturally distinct as any two peoples in the story — yet a human and a Fenrai can have healthy, fertile children together, and mixed communities are common. A treaty central to the plot reserves certain rights for 'humankind' alone, and a council of elders must rule on whether the Fenrai qualify. My editor insists the interbreeding settles it: they're one species with two looks, no different from a Great Dane and a Chihuahua. But I've given the Fenrai an entirely separate evolutionary lineage in my notes, and abandoning that over the interbreeding feels wrong. Is my editor right that shared fertility decides the question, or is there a legitimate basis for treating humans and the Fenrai as different species regardless?
