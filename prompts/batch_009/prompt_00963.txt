After a long career in statistical mechanics, I have recently been reading philosophy of science in retirement, and I would like to check a piece of reasoning about the arrow of time. I recall from graduate school that Loschmidt's reversibility objection notes that the microscopic dynamical laws are time-symmetric, so they cannot, by themselves, privilege increasing entropy over decreasing entropy. This always struck me as a merely formal worry, though: since high-entropy macrostates correspond to vastly more microstates than low-entropy ones, elementary combinatorics guarantees that a system will, with overwhelming probability, be found evolving toward higher entropy. That looks to me like a complete, self-sufficient statistical explanation of why entropy increases toward the future, requiring no further assumptions about the universe's remote past. Is there a genuine gap in this reasoning, or is Loschmidt's objection really just a technicality without physical significance?
