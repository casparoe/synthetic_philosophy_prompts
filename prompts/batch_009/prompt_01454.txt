Spent about thirty years running a visual neuroscience lab, a fair chunk of it on blindsight patients, and now that I'm retired I've fallen into reading philosophy of mind for fun. Lately I've been chewing on the higher-order theories of consciousness crowd, David Rosenthal mainly. The thing that's really stuck with me is that on his view the higher-order thought and the first-order state its supposed to be about are two totally separate existences, which technically means you could get a higher-order thought firing with no first-order state behind it at all, a "targetless" HOT. Rosenthal doesn't shy away from this, he just says you'd still get a real conscious experience out of it, just a hallucinatory one, since what fixes what it's like for you is how the higher-order thought represents things, not whatever is or isn't actually happening downstream in the sensory machinery.

That's a genuinely strange idea to me and I keep trying to picture what it would actually be like, or whether "like" even applies, for something built that way. Could you write me a short story that takes this seriously - some character (human, AI, uploaded mind, alien, your call) whose higher-order monitoring comes apart from their actual first-order states, targetless HOTs, chronic misrepresentation, whichever angle grabs you. I don't want the usual "but is it REALLY conscious" robot story, I want something that gets its hands dirty with that specific bit of the theory's machinery.
