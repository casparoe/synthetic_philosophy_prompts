This year I'm taking an IB Theory of Knowledge class, and lately we've been picking apart the difference between knowledge and belief, which has made me a lot more alert to the exact way people phrase claims about what they think is true. That new habit is probably why an odd moment with my basketball team a few weeks ago has been stuck in my head ever since, even though I still can't quite figure out what's actually going on in it.

Before our regional semifinal against the top seed in our district, our coach, who has a reputation among the players for blunt, tell-it-like-it-is speeches, gathered us in a huddle and said something very close to this: "I'm not going to sugarcoat this for you: we are going to lose tonight. But I don't believe, for one second, that we're going to lose." Strangely, it worked as a pep talk: we played our best game of the season, even though we did end up losing by four points. On the ride home, I kept turning that sentence over in my head. It isn't a straightforward contradiction, since both halves could easily be true at once: he could turn out to be completely right that we were going to lose, regardless of whether he personally believed it. But hearing him assert the loss and then, in the same breath, deny believing in it struck me as strange in a way that an outright contradiction, like "we're going to win and we're going to lose," would not have been.

When I described this to my friend Priya, our point guard, she told me I was overanalyzing something coaches say all the time, and that it obviously just meant "the odds are against us, but I refuse to give up hope." That paraphrase feels right emotionally, but it doesn't really explain why the original sentence sounded so strange in a way that her version doesn't. Is Priya right that there is nothing more to it than that, or was our coach doing something more specific and interesting with that second sentence, something other than simply reporting what he believed? I would really like to understand precisely what is going on here, rather than just leaving it as "that's just how coaches talk."
