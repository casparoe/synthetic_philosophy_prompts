Okay, this might be kind of a weird question, but it connects to something in my philosophy of science class - we've got a unit on philosophy of psychiatry coming up in a few weeks and I have a feeling this is related, even though I can't say exactly how yet. My roommate Priya got really into ADHD TikTok about a year ago, started recognizing herself in a bunch of "signs you have undiagnosed ADHD" videos, and eventually got an official diagnosis from a psychiatrist. She's since joined a couple ADHD subreddits and discord servers. Ever since being diagnosed she's started attributing way more of her behavior to it then she used to - losing the thread in conversations, "rejection sensitive dysphoria," hyperfixating on a hobby for two weeks then dropping it completely, stuff like that. She says the label just finally gave her the vocabulary to see a pattern that was there all along, and that she was probably underdiagnosed as a kid because girls with ADHD get missed a lot.

My other friend Marcus thinks this whole thing is kind of bogus. He points out that ADHD diagnoses have shot up a ton over the last couple decades, that the diagnostic criteria keep getting loosened and broadened (it used to be basically a childhood, hyperactive-little-boys diagnosis, now it covers way more adult presentations), and that there's a whole online content economy now that rewards people for finding a label for themselves. His take is that Priya didn't uncover some hidden truth about herself so much as learn to perform a category that was itself invented and expanded pretty recently, possibly in response to tons of people like her showing up and describing themselves this way to clinicians and researchers.

Honestly they both kind of seem right to me and I can't figure out how to referee it. What bugs me is that the criteria for the diagnosis and the way people like Priya talk about (and maybe even experience) their own symptoms seem to have shifted a lot over roughly the same stretch of time, and I can't tell if that's actually significant or if I'm just seeing a pattern that isn't there. It feels intuitively different from how a "real" diagnosis like a broken arm or strep throat works, but I'm not sure that intuition actually holds up under any scrutiny. So who's right, Priya or Marcus? Or is there some other way of looking at this whole situation that neither of them is getting at?
