In a game theory reading group I attend, someone recently summarized Aumann's agreement theorem as showing that two rational people cannot, strictly speaking, agree to disagree, which prompted immediate objections that this describes almost no disagreement any of us have ever actually had. That gap between the formal result and everyday experience seems like fertile ground for a joke, but every attempt anyone made in the room ended up as a generic jab at stubborn people rather than something that actually engaged with the theorem itself.

I'd like you to write a joke about Aumann's agreement theorem that would land with an audience who knows the theorem's actual moving parts: the common prior assumption, the requirement that the posteriors themselves (not merely the underlying private information) become common knowledge, and the fact that if two honest Bayesians simply keep alternating announcements of their current probability estimate for some event, that exchange is guaranteed to converge to agreement in finitely many steps. The joke doesn't need to mention any of this machinery explicitly, but the punchline should only really work because of it -- ideally, the humor should turn on exactly which premise (shared priors, common knowledge, honest updating) is quietly failing in whatever ordinary scenario you choose to set it in. I would rather have a short joke with a legible setup and punchline than a clever-sounding paragraph that only works as an in-joke for people already primed to see what you're going for.
