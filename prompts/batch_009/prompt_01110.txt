I'm on a bit of a free will kick lately — reread the whole "Free Will (Solution)" sequence on LW (How An Algorithm Feels From Inside, Thou Art Physics, Possibility and Could-ness, The Ultimate Source, etc) and then fell down a rabbit hole into the actual academic lit on manipulation arguments, wich none of my in person friends have any patience for lol. Eliezer's basic pitch is that free will "dissolves" once you realize you just ARE the deterministic algorithm — that "could have done otherwise" cashes out as a fact about what's reachable in the abstract search space of a planning computation, not a claim that history could've physically rewound, and that demanding to be the "ultimate source" of your action (rather than a link in a causal chain going back to the Big Bang) is asking an incoherent question dressed up as a deep one. I basically buy that on its own terms.

But then I read Mele's zygote argument (goddess Diana builds a zygote in Mary such that Ernie grows up to deliberately kill someone at 30, in a fully deterministic universe, hitting every compatibilist condition you could ask for — reasons-responsiveness, ownership of the relevant desires, no coercion at the moment of action) and Pereboom's four-case Plum argument, and it looks like both are basically purpose-built to take Eliezer's own move — "there's no principled difference between designed-by-a-goddess and produced-by-ordinary-physics, it's all just upstream causal history" — and point it at compatibilism instead of away from it.

So here's what I actually want: an essay that stages this fight properly, using the real mechanics of the zygote/four-case setups rather than a one-paragraph gesture at "some philosophers object that manipulation feels different," and then actually commits to an answer instead of just listing positions. Does the LW-style reductionist framing give compatibilists (old-school Frankfurt/Fischer-Ravizza types included) any real new leverage against manipulation arguments, or is biting the bullet — insisting Ernie and Plum really are responsible and our intuitions about designed agents are just misleading us — the only move left that's actually consistent? Pick a side and argue it well rather than hedging; this is just for my own notes so don't worry about making it pretty.
