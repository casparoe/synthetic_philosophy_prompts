Random tangent that's been eating my brain while im outlining my next book -- there's a marine-biologist character who's obsessed with cephalopods. I keep thinking about Descartes and his whole 'animals are just unfeeling automata' bit: no soul, no real pain, just very elaborate clockwork, which he lays out pretty plainly in the Discourse and in letters like the one to Henry More. His account of sensation depends on the soul meeting the body at exactly one point, the pineal gland, so all the scattered stuff coming in through the senses can get unified into a single experience. So genuinely curious what you think he'd make of an octopus, where somethign like two-thirds of the neurons live out in the arms, which taste, grab, and explore pretty independently of the central brain, and keep reacting to things for a while even after being cut off entirely. Does that actually strain his framework, or is it just more elaborate mechanism to him, no different in kind from a dog? And what do you think he'd say now that actual scientists have signed things like the 2024 New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness, putting bees and octopuses in the 'realistic possibility of conscious experience' camp?
