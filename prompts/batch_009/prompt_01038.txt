I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have spent a good deal of time thinking on my own about what is sometimes called the paradox of fiction. From what I can tell, philosophers resolve it by pointing out that a genuine emotion such as fear or grief can only occur if the person feeling it believes its object actually exists; since I know perfectly well that a character in a novel was never a real person, whatever I feel when that character dies cannot be true grief, only a kind of pleasant self-deception I indulge in while reading. This has actually been reassuring to me, because it means there is nothing irrational about how attached I get to people who never existed — I am simply not having a real emotion about them in the first place. Is that a fair summary of what the paradox shows?
