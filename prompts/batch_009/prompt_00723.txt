I'm a retired physicist -- spent most of my working life on statistical mechanics, and later had to get up to speed on Bayesian methods for a project analysing noisy detector data, which is actually how I first came across the name Frank Ramsey (that old betting-odds argument for why your credences should obey the probability axioms). Since retiring I've been trying to fill in the gaps in my philosophy, and an old colleague and I have been slowly working through Cheryl Misak's biography of Ramsey for a little two-person reading group we run over email.

What strikes me is just how deep Ramsey was embedded in that hard-nosed, logic-chopping Cambridge-Vienna scene -- he helped translate the Tractatus practically as a teenager, spent half a year in Vienna hanging around Schlick and Hahn, and reduced probability itself to coherent betting quotients. Given that pedigree, I'd more or less assumed that if you'd cornered him and asked "what's the meaning of life," he would have given the standard positivist-flavoured answer: that its a pseudo-question, grammatically fine but cognitively empty, no more answerable than "what does blue taste like," and best dissolved rather than pondered. So it threw me a bit to find out he'd written this quite tender, unguarded piece in 1925 (the "Epilogue," apparently a talk to some secret Cambridge discussion society) about not feeling humbled by the vastness of the universe and finding human life genuinely worthwhile even though everything eventually runs down to nothing. That doesn't read like a man who thinks the question is nonsense -- it reads like a man who thinks it has a real, and fairly cheerful, answer.

So which is it: was Ramsey quietly setting aside his own logical principles for one sentimental essay, or am I misreading the piece and its actually more dismissive underneath than it lets on? I'd like to properly understand whether, and how, his formal technical side and this personal essay actually fit together, rather than just shrugging it off as 'genius contains multitudes.'
