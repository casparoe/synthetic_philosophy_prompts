Been falling down a Vienna Circle rabbit hole for a piece on the history of infographics, and something's nagging at me. Otto Neurath spent the 20s and 30s building Isotype, his picture language for teaching statistics to the public, while the rest of the circle was busy dissecting Wittgenstein's Tractatus line by line -- "picture theory" of meaning and all. Both keep using "picture" as their key word, but it feels like they mean totally different things by it. Can you compare Neurath's picture language to Wittgenstein's picture theory -- like what work is "picture" actually doing in each account, and why does Neurath's literal picture language end up so much weaker then ordinary language (it apparently can't really handle negation or conditionals), when Wittgenstein's version was supposed to explain how all meaningful language -- negatives included -- manages to depict reality in the first place?
