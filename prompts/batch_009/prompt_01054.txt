A few of us from my local ACX meetup were arguing about this last week, which sent me down a rabbit hole of old Alignment Forum posts, specifically ARC's "Eliciting Latent Knowledge" report and all the discussion it spawned back in 2021. I think I get the core problem in the abstract -- you've got a predictor that knows way more about the world than your human labelers do, and when you train a "reporter" head to answer questions about whats going on, theres this nasty degenerate solution where the reporter just learns to simulate what a human would say given the observations they have access to, instead of actually translating the predictor's internal beliefs. Since the human simulator and the direct translator give identical answers on every training example the humans can actually check, gradient descent has no way to favor one over the other -- they only come apart on exactly the cases you care about, i.e. when the AI knows something the human overseer can't verify.

Problem is my only mental handle on this is the SmartVault/diamond example (camera watching a vault, a thief who might tamper with the sensors, etc), and I've got a nagging suspicion I've just memorized that one story rather than actually understanding the underlying structure. So: can you come up with a totally different concrete scenario -- no vault, no diamond, no security camera -- that has the same bones? I want a setup where a predictor could plausibly come to know something true and important that doesn't reduce to "what would a human conclude from the observations available to them," and where a naively-trained reporter would learn to just imitate the human's best guess, then confidently give the wrong answer in exactly the case that matters most. Bonus points if you can flag why your example isn't secretly solvable by one of the "easy" patches people always bring up, like just collecting more labeled data or having the reporter ask clarifying questions.
