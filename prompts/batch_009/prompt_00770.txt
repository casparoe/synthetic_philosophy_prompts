I'm a backend engineer at a mid-sized company, and we're putting together a ten-year retrospective of our core platform for the engineering blog. Over that decade the system has been rewritten piece by piece: every module from the original codebase has since been replaced, we moved from a single monolith to a set of microservices, and we've migrated to a new database twice, so that no line of code from year one is still running in production. At the same time, the service has never had a gap in operation beyond a scheduled maintenance window, every customer account and its data has been carried forward without interruption, and the endpoint our customers hit today is the very same one they hit ten years ago. My tech lead wants the post to say we've "run the same platform, continuously improved, for a decade." I think that's misleading and want it to say we "replaced our original platform with a new one, in stages." We've argued about this for an embarrassingly long time without either of us budging, even though we don't disagree about a single fact in the system's history — we agree on every migration, rewrite, and outage that ever happened, and it unsettles me that two people can agree on all of that and still be stuck on what looks like it should be a factual question.

I mentioned the dispute to a friend, who said it reminded her of the old puzzle about a ship that has all of its planks replaced over the course of many repairs, and whether it's still "the same ship" afterward. I don't have any background in philosophy, so I looked into that, but the standard answers I found — that it depends on what you mean by "ship," or on which parts matter most, or on there being some continuous chain of repairs — didn't explain why my tech lead and I can't just resolve this by being precise about our definitions and then moving on; the disagreement seems to survive every attempt to pin it down that way. Is there some further fact about the platform that we're overlooking and should be arguing about instead, or is something else going on in disagreements like this one? I'd like to understand what's actually at stake here and whether there's a principled way to work out who's right, rather than just picking whichever description happens to sound better for the blog post.
