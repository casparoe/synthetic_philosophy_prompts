I never studied philosophy formally, but for the past year or so I've been teaching myself pieces of it by following whatever rabbit hole catches my interest, usually starting from Wikipedia or old forum posts and working backward toward the original sources. A while back I started reading about Bertrand Russell's early struggles with logic and ended up completely sidetracked by something called "Bradley's regress" and the related puzzle philosophers call the unity of the proposition. As I understand it, the worry starts with something as simple as "John loves Mary": why is that a single, unified claim rather than just three items — John, loving, Mary — sitting next to each other like groceries on a list? The moment you try to explain that "togetherness" by bringing in a relation to tie the parts together, the relation itself seems to need a further relation connecting it to the original two things, which needs another, and so on forever.

What I find almost funny about it is how differently people reacted once they saw the regress coming. Bradley himself apparently took it as evidence that relations, and maybe the whole world of separate, related things, aren't fully real, which pushed him toward this strange view where reality is really one undivided whole. Frege, on the other hand, tried to dissolve the whole problem by insisting that concepts are "unsaturated," incomplete entities that latch directly onto an object without needing any middleman relation at all. I brought all this up with a couple of friends over dinner, mostly to see whether I could make the regress sound funny rather than just confusing, and I ended up promising I'd come back with an actual joke instead of another rambling explanation.

Could you write that joke for me? I'd like the punchline to genuinely turn on the mechanics of Bradley's regress or the unity-of-the-proposition problem — the endless demand for one more relation, or the contrast between Bradley's mystical way out and Frege's logical one — rather than being a generic "philosophers overthink everything" joke. Ideally, someone who actually understands the regress should hear it and think that's exactly the absurdity, rather than suspecting that I don't really know what the problem is.
