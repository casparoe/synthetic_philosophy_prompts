For my philosophy of language seminar, we've spent the last two weeks on Grice's account of speaker meaning and conversational implicature, and next class each of us has to bring an original example of a flouted maxim. I'd rather bring an actual joke than a plain example, so could you write one whose punchline depends on a character deliberately flouting a specific maxim other than Quantity — Quality, Relation, and Manner are all fair game — in order to implicate something that is never literally said? Please don't just adapt Grice's own philosophy-testimonial-letter case, which already trades on Quantity. The joke should read as genuinely funny even to someone with no background in pragmatics, but also include a one-sentence Gricean gloss afterward identifying the maxim being flouted and reconstructing the inference from what is said to what is implicated.
