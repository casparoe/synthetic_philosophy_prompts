I'm three years into a PhD dissertation defending Frege's claim that the laws of logic are objective truths, holding independently of the psychology, culture, or historical position of whoever happens to think them — the view he spent Grundgesetze der Arithmetik and his anti-psychologistic essays trying to secure once and for all. Last week a committee member mentioned, almost in passing, that Frege kept a private diary in 1924 expressing vicious antisemitism, contempt for parliamentary democracy, and support for stripping Jews of political rights. I've since confirmed this is real and well documented, and that it even deeply shook Michael Dummett — himself a lifelong anti-racism campaigner and Frege's greatest expositor — when he first learned of it.

This has thrown my whole project into doubt. If the very mind that insisted logic answers to nothing but itself, untouched by any thinker's particular standpoint, could be so completely captured by one of the ugliest ideologies of his century, doesn't that suggest the "timeless objectivity" Frege claimed for logic was never really available to begin with — that in his case it was less a discovery than a prejudice dressed up as one? Should I now treat the central thesis of Grundgesetze as compromised by what we've learned about the man who wrote it?
