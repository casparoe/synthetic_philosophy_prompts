I'm a software engineer working on LLM evaluation, and I've started relying on "self-consistency" in our pipeline: sampling the same prompt many times at a nonzero temperature and taking the majority answer as the final output. I justified this approach to my team by appeal to the Condorcet Jury Theorem — since each individual sample does better than random guessing, the theorem guarantees that the majority vote converges to the correct answer with near-certainty as we add more samples. We're now considering dropping our more expensive human-verification step even for our hardest eval questions, on the logic that we can always buy arbitrary accuracy by sampling a cheap, weak model more times instead of paying for a stronger one. Does the Condorcet Jury Theorem really license that conclusion, and if not, exactly where does the argument break down?
