So I'm part of this small philosophy of mind reading group (mostly people who dropped out of grad school, plus one actual professor who humors us) and we just spent two sessions on panpsychism and the combination problem. We read some Chalmers and Goff on why its so hard to explain how a huge number of micro-experiences (like whatever it's like to be a quark, if that's even a thing) could ever add up into one unified subject like me, and then somebody brought in a paper on cosmopsychism as a proposed fix -- basically flip the whole picture around and say the universe itself is the one fundamental conscious subject, with individual minds like ours somehow carved out of or derived from that cosmic consciousness, instead of built up out of tiny parts.

I got pretty into this until my friend Priya pointed out that you've now just got the mirror-image problem: instead of explaining how zillions of tiny subjects combine into one big one, you have to explain how a single undifferentiated cosmic subject could ever give rise to separate, bounded subjects like you and me, each stuck with our own limited point of view (I think this is sometimes called the decombination problem, or maybe the boundary problem, not totally sure if those are the same thing). We argued about it for like an hour at the pub afterwards and didn't get anywhere -- we were both convinced the other side was just relocating the mystery rather than solving it. Could you write a dialogue between a committed micropsychist (bottom-up combination, willing to bite the bullet with something like a phenomenal bonding relation to get subjects to combine) and a committed priority cosmopsychist, where they actually engage with each other's strongest objections instead of just talking past each other? I want to know if one side genuinely comes out ahead here or if it's a real stalemate.
