So at work we've been using 'self-consistency' with our LLM calls -- sample the model a bunch of times and go with whatever answer comes up most -- and a teammate mentioned this is basically just the Condorcet Jury Theorem in disguise. Now I can't unsee it everywhere: reddit upvotes, x/twitter community notes, prediction markets, all claiming to be 'wisdom of the crowd' machines. But the theorem only actually works if each voter is independent AND more likely than not to be right on thier own -- break either assumption and more voters makes the group verdict worse, not better. Condorcet himself was a total Enlightenment optimist, big on progress, public education, and using probability to design better institutions. If you sat him down today and showed him how these crowd platforms actually behave -- bots and algorithmic feeds wrecking independence, misinformation dragging average competence below 50/50 on plenty of topics -- do you think he'd see his theorem vindicated at internet scale, or would he say we've built machines that trade on the theorem's name while violating everything it requires? Actually reason through the math/assumptions, don't just give me vibes.
