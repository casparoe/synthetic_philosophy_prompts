Ok so I had this long argument with my friend Jae last night about surrogate goals as a defense against acausal threats (the Baumann/Oesterheld idea where you get your AI to care a little bit about some fake goal so a blackmailer targets that instead of the thing you actually value). Jae thinks it basically works as advertised — cheap threat, everyone's better off. I kept pushing back that it just relocates the problem: if a threatener is smart enough to model you in the first place, wouldn't it also see that the surrogate is only instrumentally adopted, and just route around it to threaten whatever you'd actually cave on? Jae's answer was that none of that matters as long as your commitment to respond identically to surrogate-threats and real-threats is credible/verifiable — at that point the threatener is indifferent between the two. I wasnt fully convinced, especially once the commitment races problem comes in — doesn't adopting a surrogate goal early just become another move in a race to commit that could blow up on you? We argued in circles for like an hour and never really landed anywhere.

Could you write me a dialogue between two people who both know this stuff cold — one defending surrogate goals as a genuinely workable partial defense against acausal extortion, and one arguing they don't actually solve anything for something like the reason above — where they actually engage with each other's best arguments instead of talking past each other? Would be great if the commitment-races angle came up too.
