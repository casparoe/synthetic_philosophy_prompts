I'm a philosophy PhD student working on the metaphysics of dispositions. For a paper I'm writing, I'd like a dialogue that stages the central dialectic directly, rather than summarizing it, between two philosophers who are bubble-wrapping a fragile antique vase for shipping and end up arguing about what its fragility actually consists in. One is a reductionist who wants to analyze dispositional predicates like 'fragile' in terms of subjunctive conditionals, and who is willing to retreat from the simple conditional analysis to something like Lewis's reformed version (appealing to a persisting intrinsic causal basis) once pressed. The other is a realist who holds that finkish and masking cases jointly show that no successor to the simple analysis can succeed, and that dispositions must instead be treated as primitive. Have the realist open with a genuine finkish counterexample (Martin's electro-fink, or an analogous case invented on the spot), and then, once the reductionist retreats to the reformed analysis, have the realist turn the very bubble wrap they're using into a live masking counterexample against it — at which point the reductionist should have a real further move available in this literature, not just a face-saving dodge. Keep both sides equally sharp, make sure each reply engages the specific point just made rather than restating an opening position, and let the disagreement stand unresolved at the end.
