I'm putting together a seminar module on Nietzsche's naturalism and the body, and I'd like to pressure-test a discussion question before assigning it. In the "Why I Am So Clever" section of *Ecce Homo*, Nietzsche gets surprisingly granular about physiology: he treats nutrition as a question on which "the salvation of humanity" depends more than on any theological curiosity, denounces alcohol as one of "the two great European narcotics" (pairing it with Christianity), dismisses coffee because it "makes one gloomy," insists a thinker must learn "the size of one's stomach," and calls a sedentary existence "the real sin against the Holy Spirit." Now suppose he were confronted with today's biohacking and quantified-self culture: continuous glucose monitors, intermittent-fasting protocols, nootropic stacks, and apps that algorithmically tune meals and sleep for cognitive output. Would Nietzsche recognize in this a natural heir to his own physiological self-experimentation and doctrine of self-overcoming, or would he diagnose a new ascetic ideal — external, quantified rule-following displacing the cultivated instinct he prized? Answer with reference to his actual arguments about nutrition, instinct, and decadence, not to generalities about his admiration for strength.
