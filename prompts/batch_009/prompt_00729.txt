I've been working through some old LessWrong and Alignment Forum material on decision theory in my spare time (my day job is software engineering, not philosophy), and I keep getting stuck on the relationship between Timeless Decision Theory (TDT) and Updateless Decision Theory (UDT). Both are introduced with essentially the same slogan — decide as if you were fixing the output of the abstract algorithm you implement, rather than just choosing one physical act — and yet they're treated as importantly different: apparently UDT "pays" in the counterfactual mugging while TDT does not, even though the two are said to agree on cases like Newcomb's problem and Parfit's hitchhiker.

As I understand it, the mechanical difference is that UDT evaluates a policy from a point before the agent has made any observations, while TDT lets the agent update on its observations first and only then reasons about the timeless computation. Could you unpack why exactly that difference is what produces divergent verdicts in counterfactual mugging specifically, and not in the cases where the two theories agree? I'd also like your assessment of whether this makes UDT simply a more consistent, more general version of TDT's core idea, or whether fixing TDT's problem this way smuggles in an independently questionable commitment — reasoning from "before" any observations at all — that has its own costs and isn't obviously forced on us.
