Ok so I fell down a rabbit hole a while back and learned Avicenna apparently wrote his "flying man" argument while he was literally in prison. The setup: imagine a person created instantaneously, fully grown, floating in a void, blindfolded, with his limbs spread out so they don't even touch each other -- zero sensory input, not even the feel of your own arm against your side. Avicenna's claim is this guy would still immediately affirm "I exist" without affirming he has hands or a body or anything else at all -- pure self-awareness, no senses required. Basically Descartes' cogito six hundred years early, except he's using it to argue the soul is seperate from the body, not to bootstrap a theory of knowledge from scratch.

Can you write me a short story that takes this setup seriously, kind of the way Permutation City takes dust theory seriously? Like some mind -- human, upload, freshly-booted AI, whatever -- actually waking up into that exact predicament, and the story follows through on whether that kind of self-awareness would really hold up with literally nothing to ground it. I don't want the Avicenna stuff to just be spooky window dressing, I want it to be the actual thing the story is about.
