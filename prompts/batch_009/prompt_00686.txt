We also discussed William Rowe's famous example of a fawn that burns to death slowly in a forest fire, alone and unseen, which he uses to argue that at least some suffering appears to serve no greater purpose at all. After class, a couple of us got into a long argument about whether these theodicies only sound convincing because God's reasoning is assumed to be permanently hidden from us, and whether any of them would hold up if that mystery were removed entirely.

That's what I'd like your help exploring: could you write me a short story, somewhere around 1,000 words, set in a near future in which a single benevolent, superintelligent AI governs the world and is fully transparent about its reasoning, able to explain any decision it makes to anyone who asks? What I'm really curious about is whether removing the excuse of divine mystery makes these old arguments look sturdier or more threadbare, and I suspect a story could show that better than an essay could -- so please try to actually dramatize the confrontation rather than smuggling in a philosophy lecture with characters attached.
