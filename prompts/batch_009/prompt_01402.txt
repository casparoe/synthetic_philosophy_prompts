ok so i've been stuck on this thought experiment for weeks now, the one (i think originally from a rationalist blog) where you gotta pick between one person getting tortured for 50 years vs some absurdly huge number of people, like 3^^^3 or whatever that notation means, each getting a single dust speck in their eye for a second. the "shut up and multiply" crowd says you have to pick the torture, because if you actually do the math the total disutility from all those specks adds up to way more than one guy's torture, and not seeing that is just scope insensitivity.

it bugged me but i couldn't say why until i ran into this other argument (scanlon, i think, or someone in that contractualism space) that uses basically the same setup flipped around - a guy trapped somewhere getting badly hurt, and the only fix is interrupting a broadcast that millions of people are enjoying, which would only mildly annoy them. the arguement there was that you're not allowed to just add up millions of tiny annoyances and weigh the pile against one guy's real suffering, because morality is about justifying yourself to each person on their own, not to "humanity's total utility." that felt way more right to me, but then i got worried its just a trick, cause couldn't you always find some number of mildly annoyed people whose combined annoyance should obviously outweigh one guy's pain? where's the actual principled line supposed to be?

could you write me a dialogue between two people hashing this out, one who bites the bullet and picks torture over specks every time (a hardcore aggregator type), and one who's more like the scanlon guy and thinks adding up harms across different people like that is illegitimate. i want real back and forth where they respond to each other's actual points, not just restate their own view - especially want the aggregator pushing hard on whether the no-aggregation side can survive being asked exactly how many mildly annoyed people it would take to flip the verdict, and the other person giving an actual answer instead of dodging it.
