Got into it with my friend last night about whether art criticism is actually worth anything, or if it's just noise on top of the real thing. She's firmly in the "if you have to explain it, it didn't work" camp. I remembered Tolstoy goes off on critics in What Is Art?, so I dug it up to back her up, but rereading it now I'm not sure the argument actually holds together. Here's the bit that's bugging me:

"Critics explain!" What do they explain? The artist, if a real artist, has by his work transmitted to others the feeling he experienced. What is there, then, to explain? If a work be good as art, then the feeling expressed by the artist—be it moral or immoral—transmits itself to other people. If transmitted to others, then they feel it, and all interpretations are superfluous. If the work does not infect people, no explanation can make it contagious. An artist's work cannot be interpreted. Had it been possible to explain in words what he wished to convey, the artist would have expressed himself in words. He expressed it by his art, only because the feeling he experienced could not be otherwise transmitted.

So the way I read it, he's saying explanation is either unnecessary (the art already worked) or useless (it didn't, and words won't fix that), so critics add nothing either way. But that last move — if it could've been said in words the artist would've just used words — seems like its doing a lot of heavy lifting, and kind of proves too much? Doesnt the same logic rule out explaining jokes or poems, which people do constantly without it obviously killing them. Am I missing a step in his reasoning or is this actually shakier than it looks at first pass?
