My team has spent two separate meetings arguing about something we can't resolve, and I'd like an outside perspective. We shipped a feed-ranking feature built entirely from weighted heuristics and hand-tuned rules based on click history — no neural networks, no training loop, nothing that updates itself after deployment. Nobody disputes any of that; everyone agrees on exactly what the system does and how it was built. What we can't agree on is whether we're allowed to call it "AI" in our documentation and marketing. Half the team says yes: it's an automated system doing something that used to require human judgment, and that's how competitors describe comparable features. The other half says no: "real" AI requires machine learning, in their view, and calling this AI is closer to false advertising. It's not just a matter of pride, either — our legal team recently introduced a mandatory review process for anything labeled "AI," so the label carries real downstream consequences. We've compared outside definitions, dictionary entries, even a couple of industry white papers, and nobody has budged. Is one side simply right and the other confused, or is something else going on here?
