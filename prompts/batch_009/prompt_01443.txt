Before she died last month, my mother made it clear she didn't want the usual kind of funeral. She used to say it wouldn't matter to her whether her body ended up feeding worms in the ground or turning to ash in a furnace, since neither fate was any more natural or dignified than the other, and she asked us not to spend money on embalming or an expensive casket. Now that she's gone, my brother wants to go ahead with a full traditional service regardless, arguing that funerals are for the living, not the dead, and that honoring her memory doesn't mean treating an offhand remark as a binding instruction. I keep going back and forth: her reasoning about the body struck me as basically sound at the time, but I'm no longer sure it settles what we owe her, or each other, now. Was she right that it genuinely doesn't matter, or is my brother onto something her argument leaves out?
