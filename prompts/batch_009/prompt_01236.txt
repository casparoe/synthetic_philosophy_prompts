I'm putting together supplementary readings for the composition unit of my graduate metaphysics seminar, and this year I'd like to open with a piece of short fiction before we turn to van Inwagen's technical apparatus. Would you write a short story, roughly 1,000-1,500 words, that dramatizes the Special Composition Question -- the problem of specifying the true, non-trivial conditions under which some objects, the xs, compose some further object?

Please give real dramatic weight to at least two rival answers rather than simply naming them: for instance, universalism (any objects whatsoever, however scattered, compose something), nihilism (there are only mereological simples, arranged tablewise or personwise but never actually composing a table or a person), van Inwagen's own proposal that composition occurs just when some things' activity constitutes a life, or Markosian's view that facts about composition are simply brute, admitting of no systematic explanation. I'd rather this not be a seminar discussion transplanted into a dorm room or café, with characters taking turns reciting positions -- the philosophical bite should come from what happens to the characters, not from exposition.
