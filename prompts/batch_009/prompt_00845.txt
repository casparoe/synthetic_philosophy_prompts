Hey, quick one that's been rattling around in my head. In the Theological-Political Treatise (ch. 20) Spinoza argues the sovereign literally can't control what's inside people's heads, and that trying to police opinion just breeds hypocrisy and resentment instead of actual peace - people just learn to say one thing and think another. But he's not a free-speech absolutist either: he says outright that unlimited freedom of expression would be disastrous, and that "seditious" speech - stuff that denies the sovereign's authority, or is driven by hatred/deceit and tears the commonwealth apart - is fair game for the state to shut down. He ties all of this to his case for democracy as the "most natural" regime, since in a democracy nobody's handed over their right to be consulted the way subjects have under a king.

So, if Spinoza got beamed into the present and had to rule on platforms and goverments cracking down on "misinformation" and "hate speech" online, which bucket does that fall into for him - legit anti-sedition policing, or exactly the mind-control he warns against? And does his whole argument even survive the jump from a small 17th-century civic body to algorithm-driven mass democracy, or does something break along the way? Reason it out from his actual distinctions, not the "Spinoza = Enlightenment free-speech guy" cliché everyone reaches for.
