Random one from a conversation with my uncle over the holidays that's been rattling around in my head since. He still keeps his old sheepdog around even though she can't herd anymore and mostly just naps in the barn all day. When I asked why not just rehome her and get a working dog instead, he said something like: "she gave me ten good years, and if I got rid of her now just because she stopped being useful, I'd be turning myself into someone who discards things the second they stop paying off." He was pretty explicit that he doesn't think the dog has interests or a claim of her own that matter here, its more that the choice would say something bad about him. Somethings been nagging me about that reasoning ever since, like there's a real philosophical move happening but maybe a shaky one. Does it actually hold up, and is there a standard objection to grounding kindness to animals this way instead of just saying the dog's own welfare is what matters?
