I could use an outside perspective on a disagreement that keeps coming up in my family, since a few of my friends and I got into a long argument about it over lunch last week without actually resolving anything. My mom grew up in a wine-producing part of northern Italy, where it was normal for teenagers to be given a small, watered-down glass of wine at big family dinners starting around age fourteen or so, always in front of adults, and she wants to raise my younger brother and me the same way. Her reasoning is that if we get used to handling alcohol calmly in a low-stakes setting now, while someone is actually watching, we'll be much less likely to completely lose our heads around it later, once we're at college parties with nobody supervising us at all. My dad, who grew up in the U.S. and didn't touch alcohol until he was legally allowed to, thinks this is backwards: he argues that any exposure at all, even a supervised sip at dinner, just gets us used to the idea of drinking earlier than we otherwise would, and that the only way to make sure we're not already halfway toward a taste for it by the time we're on our own is to avoid it completely until then. Both of them insist they're doing this purely for our sake, so it isn't simply a case of one of them caring more than the other.

What started as a pretty narrow question about our own household turned into a much bigger disagreement once my friends weighed in with their own families' very different rules -- a disagreement about how something like self-restraint is even supposed to develop in a person in the first place. Is it the kind of thing you build up through supervised, low-stakes practice with the very temptation in question, the way you might build up a callus, or does any early practice run just quietly wear down the resistance you're trying to protect, so that the only reliable path is avoidance until someone is already mature enough not to need it? I'd be curious whether there's an actual, principled way to think through which picture is closer to correct, or whether this is really just a difference in temperament and family culture that doesn't have a deeper answer underneath it.
