About four years ago I got pulled into local politics for a single reason: a developer wanted to pave over some wetlands near where I grew up, and the only group that seemed capable of actually stopping it was a small political party active in my county. I joined to help with that one fight, expected to drift away once it was resolved, and instead ended up staying involved for years. On the whole I don't regret it, but something about the experience keeps nagging at me, and I haven't been able to fully work out what it is, even after talking it through with friends who are involved in party politics themselves, some in my party and some in the rival one across town.

Here is the pattern that bothers me. Whenever a vote or a public position comes up that I haven't thought hard about, or one where my own instinct says the official line is simply wrong, there is a strong, mostly unspoken expectation that I go along with it anyway: repeat the talking points, campaign for the candidate, defend the platform to skeptical neighbors, and keep any private doubts private. People who are otherwise reasonable and perfectly willing to change their minds in ordinary conversation become suddenly immovable and start reciting the same handful of arguments the moment a topic touches party business. When I have pushed back, the answer I usually get is some version of "unity is how we win, and once you're inside the tent you owe us your public support no matter what you privately think." What strikes me most is that none of this seems specific to my party or my cause. Friends in the rival party, who joined for completely different reasons and hold more or less the opposite views, describe the identical dynamic, right down to the phrasing.

I never studied politics or philosophy, so I don't have a name for what I'm noticing, and a friend recently told me I'm overthinking something that is simply how any large group gets things done. But I can't shake the feeling that there is something built into what a political party actually is, rather than into this or that party's particular leadership or culture, something that would show up in a party organized around almost any cause, including ones I genuinely believe in. I would be curious what you make of it: is my discomfort tracking something real and important about how parties function, or am I just being naive about the compromises that collective action requires? And if it is something real, does that point toward reforming how parties operate internally, or toward a more basic conclusion about political parties in general?
