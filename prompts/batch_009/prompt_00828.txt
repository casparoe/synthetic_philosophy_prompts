hey, long-time lurker of LW/ACX-adjacent stuff here. I've been chewing on acausal trade (two agents that can never causally interact still cooperating because each can model the other well enough to know theyd reciprocate) and think ive talked myself into a point about it that I haven't seen anyone make explicitly, but I'm not confident I'm not just missing the obvious rebuttal. Might write this up properly at some point but wanted a sanity check first.

The worry: the vivid acausal trade scenarios (some vastly bigger/older civilization, or a future superintelligence, that might be modeling you right now and will reward or punish you based on what it predicts you'd do) are unverifiable by construction - that's kind of the whole point. The standard reason people don't just cave to any Pascal's-mugging-style argument is that a hypothesis claiming an astronomically large payoff or population should get an astronomically tiny prior to match it. But that fix is famously broken in exactly this kind of case, because you can describe unimaginably larger numbers of simulated people using barely any extra bits - going from "3^^^3 people" to "3^^^^3 people" costs one more arrow, not a proportionally bigger description. So the prior penalty doesn't scale anywhere near fast enough to cancel the claimed payoff, and the naive expected-value math stays exploitable.

What I don't see discussed much is that this seems to infect basically any acausal trade proposal where you can't independently bound how big or capable the other party could be - which, since you can't causally observe them, is more or less all of them. But acausal trade gets treated as a comparatively respectable, "real" application of decision theory (it's trade, not mugging!), even though the unverifiable-astronomical-stakes part looks identical to me.

So: is there an actual disanalogy between "trading partner" and "mugger" that I'm missing, or does most acausal trade reasoning just quietly inherit the mugging problem? And separately - is this a "yeah, obviously" point to anyone who's actually thought about it for five minutes, or does it add something? Poke holes in it if you can, that's genuinely what I want out of this.
