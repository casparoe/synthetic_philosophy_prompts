Something happened at work a few weeks ago that's still bugging me and I can't quite work out who's actually right about it. My manager called me and two other people into her office and told us, pretty matter of factly, that our whole department was getting moved to the new downtown building starting next month. She didn't say "I think" or "I heard," she said it like it was already decided. I didn't question it at all since, well, she's the manager, so I went and gave everyone on my team a heads up so they could plan around it. Turns out the move had actually gotten cancelled weeks earlier and nobody had told her yet, so I ended up telling like a dozen people something completely false. A couple coworkers have been kind of cold with me since, and one of them literally wrote "thanks for the misinformation" in our group chat, which stung, because I wasn't making anything up, I fully believed it when I said it.

My roommate says I'm completely in the clear because all that matters is I didn't mean to deceive anyone, but my sister, who never backs down from an argument, thinks it's more complicated then that. She says maybe theres a way to still do something wrong by stating something as fact when you only ever heard it secondhand and never actually confirmed it yourself, even if you're being totally sincere about it. That's kind of thrown me for a loop, because I always figured "not lying" and "not doing anything wrong" were basically the same thing. So who's actually right here, is there a real difference between lying and just wrongly asserting something you believe but don't actually know, and if there's a difference, what would even make the second one wrong when nobody was trying to deceive anybody at any point?
