Last winter my car slid off a remote road during a snowstorm, well outside cell range. A stranger with a tow truck stopped, but said he'd only pull me out if I gave him my word that I'd pay him $300 once we reached town; he mentioned he'd been promised money before and left empty-handed, so he studied me for a moment before deciding he believed me. There was no contract, no way for him to find me afterward, and virtually no chance we'd ever meet again, yet once we got to town I withdrew the cash and paid him as promised. My friend insists this was a mistake: the tow was already finished and irreversible, I'd never see him again, so the $300 bought me nothing further and was pure loss. I felt certain I still owed him the money, but I couldn't out-argue my friend, and we went back and forth for the better part of an hour without either of us budging. Was my friend right that paying was irrational once the rescue was already behind me, or is his reasoning missing something?
