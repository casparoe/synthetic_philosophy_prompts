My housemate and I keep clashing over whose ethical priorities should govern our shared household purchases. I care a great deal about animal welfare and avoid buying anything tested on animals, even at a premium, while she's indifferent to that; she, in turn, feels strongly that we should boycott fast-fashion brands over labor conditions in garment factories, something that has never much bothered me. Rather than continue litigating whose concern should win whenever we buy something jointly, we struck an arrangement: she now buys only cruelty-free personal-care and cleaning products for the household, and I buy only from clothing brands with vetted ethical labor practices, and neither of us pushes the other to extend this any further. Each of us judges, by our own values, that we're better off under this arrangement than before, and the arguments have stopped. But something about it nags at me: it feels less like we've resolved anything and more like we've each paid the other to stop interfering with our own conscience, without getting any closer to agreeing on what's actually right. Is that a legitimate worry, or is this actually a perfectly sound way for two people who are never going to convince each other to handle a standing moral disagreement?
