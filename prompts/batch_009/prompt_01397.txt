been rereading the parts of Plato's Laws where the Athenian Stranger goes on this huge tangent about wine and drunkenness (most of book 1 and a chunk of book 2), and its way more elaborate than the "Plato liked moderation" cliche people reduce him to. He's got actual age brackets: no wine at all under 18, moderate wine from 18-30 with zero tolerance for real drunkenness, and only once your past 40 are you allowed to actually let loose and "invoke Dionysus" at these supervised drinking parties, which have to be run by a designated sober elder (over 60, specifically) whose job is basically to command the drunks the way a general commands troops. And his argument for the whole setup is oddly rigorous: wine is a "confidence/boldness" drug, he even points out that there's no such thing as a real "fear drug" you could use to test courage, but wine is basically the real-life opposite of that, so getting drunk under controlled conditions counts as a genuine stress-test of your sophrosyne (self-mastery), the same way facing manufactured danger tests courage.
