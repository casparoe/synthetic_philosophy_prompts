Random one for a piece im poking at on the history of drinking culture. I've been digging through Plato's Laws (not the Symposium, the actual Laws) and found this argument from the Athenian Stranger that genuinely surprised me: he says supervised drinking parties are a legit way to test and train self-control, the same way armies test and train courage by exposing soldiers to manageable danger. Sparta and Crete, he claims, are great at drilling courage but have nothing equivalent for resisting pleasure, so they just ban wine outright instead of using it. Meanwhile elsewhere, especially in the Republic, Plato treats intoxication as basically corrosive to the very part of the soul this is supposed to train. Could you write me a dialogue between two people at a party, drinks in hand, one defending the Stranger's argument as sound and the other convinced its either inconsistent with Plato's own psychology or just doesn't work the way he thinks? I want real back-and-forth where they actually engage with each other's strongest points, not just talk past each other -- and don't let either one off easy.
