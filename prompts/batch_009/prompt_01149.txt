I've been thinking on my own about rescue dilemmas — cases where you can save one stranger or a different, larger group of strangers, but not both. My understanding is that the philosopher John Taurek addressed this by arguing that the fairest solution is a coin flip weighted according to how many people are on each side, so that a hundred people would have a much better chance against a lone stranger than two people would, while two against one would come out close to even. Since this weighted-coin procedure seems like such a sensible, fair compromise, why do philosophers still argue over whether the numbers should count at all?
