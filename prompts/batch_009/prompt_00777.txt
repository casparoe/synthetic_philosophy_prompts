I'm assembling supplementary material for a graduate seminar in formal epistemology, and I want to move past the standard textbook motivation for probabilism. The synchronic Dutch book argument -- violate the probability axioms and a bookie can construct a set of bets that you would accept individually as fair but that together guarantee a loss -- is already familiar to the students. What I'm after is a dialogue that gets into the far more contested diachronic version of the argument: the one used to claim that an agent who plans to update her credences by some rule other than conditionalization, or who violates van Fraassen's Reflection Principle, is thereby irrational, since a bookie could exploit her across time even though her credences are perfectly coherent at each individual moment.

Please write a dialogue between two formal epistemologists, A and B, who both accept the synchronic argument for probabilism but disagree sharply about what the diachronic version actually establishes. A should lay out the standard case: a bookie can sell the agent a bet at one time and a complementary bet later, once her credences have shifted according to her own announced updating plan, so that she is guaranteed to lose money no matter how things turn out -- which looks every bit as damning as the synchronic case. B should respond with something like the "sophistication" objection associated with Levi and Maher: an agent who sees the trap coming can simply decline whichever later bet would complete it, so the theorem only ever catches naive bettors who evaluate each offer in isolation, and naivety about betting strategy is a different failing from having an incoherent credal state. A should push back rather than concede the point, and B should then press further with something in the spirit of Christensen's "depragmatizing" worry: if a sufficiently forewarned agent can always dodge the loss, then betting behavior was never doing the real argumentative work, and what is actually owed is an independent, non-pragmatic account of why departing from conditionalization or Reflection amounts to epistemic incoherence rather than sometimes being perfectly sensible -- as it arguably is for an agent who correctly anticipates that her own future judgment will be manipulated or impaired. Have each side directly engage the other's strongest move rather than talk past one another, and let the dialogue end with the disagreement still genuinely open rather than have either side simply concede.
