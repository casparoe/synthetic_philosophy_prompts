ok so i've been on a bit of a causal-models kick lately (blame the LW sequences + finally getting round to Pearl's Book of Why) and i keep running into people who treat David Lewis's old counterfactual theory of causation -- the '73 paper, causation as counterfactual dependence cashed out via similarity between possible worlds -- and Woodward's interventionist theory -- causation cashed out via hypothetical "surgical" interventions on a causal graph, basically the philosophical cousin of do-calculus -- as genuine rivals. wich kind of confuses me tbh, since on the surface they both just look like counterfactual-dependence theories wearing different hats (and Lewis's account is also the ancestor of causal decision theory / a lot of the Newcomb's-problem stuff, so its not like he was ignorant of the intervention-y way of thinking about it).

so here's my actual ask: can you construct a real causal scenario -- concrete events/variables, not bare C-and-E labels -- where Lewis's analysis and Woodward's analysis would output different verdicts about whether the cause actually caused the effect? then explain what's really doing the work in producing that disagreement: is it the closest-possible-world similarity ranking, the fact that interventions surgically cut the other arrows into the cause variable, some backtracking issue, or something else entirely. bonus points if you can say which verdict tracks the intuitively "correct" judgment better, and why that's not just a cop-out of "whichever theory happens to give the right answer here."
