Ever since a home DNA test contradicted our family's longstanding claim to descend from a minor Habsburg noble, my aunt has treated every new document I turn up as further proof that the archives themselves have been tampered with to hide the truth. Trying to make sense of her reasoning, I came across the dogmatism paradox in epistemology: the idea that if I genuinely know some claim to be true, I thereby know that any future evidence against it would have to be misleading, and since I am entitled to disregard evidence I know to be misleading, I am already entitled to disregard whatever counter-evidence the future brings. If that argument holds up, then my aunt is not reasoning badly at all — she is simply doing what anyone who truly knows something is rationally licensed to do, and my mistake has been assuming she still owes my evidence a hearing. Have epistemologists essentially shown that dismissing counter-evidence out of hand is the rational policy for someone who really does know they are right?
