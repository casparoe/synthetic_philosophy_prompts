I'm several drafts into a novel whose protagonist is a literary translator — she normally works from French and German — who, in the year after her mother's death, starts reading her way through classical Chinese philosophy in Burton Watson's translations, partly as a way of finding a vocabulary for grief that isn't the Stoic or Buddhist consolation she feels she's already encountered a thousand times. I want one of her central set-pieces to lean on Zhuangzi, and I keep returning to two passages: the moment in the "Perfect Happiness" chapter where Huizi finds Zhuangzi drumming on a basin and singing after his own wife's death, and Zhuangzi explains that he has come to see her dying as simply the latest in a sequence of transformations running from formlessness to vital breath to body to life to death, no more remarkable than the turning of the four seasons; and the equally famous butterfly dream in "Discussion on Making All Things Equal," where Zhuangzi wakes unsure whether he is a man who has just dreamed he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming he is a man.

Read together, these have always struck me as two expressions of a single idea that is, in effect, a kind of reincarnation: that death is not an ending but a change of costume, and that Zhuangzi can drum on his basin without hypocrisy because he has concluded that his wife's identity has simply passed into some new form, the way he himself might wake up tomorrow as a moth, or a tree, or another man altogether. I would like my protagonist to arrive at more or less this reading, aloud, in conversation with a skeptical old friend, as the emotional high point of the chapter, so before I commit it to the page I want to make sure the philosophy actually holds up. Is this a reasonable gloss on what Zhuangzi is doing in these two passages, or is it the kind of tidy misreading that a specialist in classical Chinese thought would wince at? A friend from my old graduate program, who now teaches philosophy for a living, made a face when I described the scene to her but had to run to a meeting before she could explain what was bothering her, and it's been nagging at me ever since.
