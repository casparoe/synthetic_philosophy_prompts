So in my AP Gov class we did this whole unit on political polarization and my teacher gave us an excerpt from Simone Weil's essay where she basically argues that ALL political parties should be abolished completely, not reformed, just gotten rid of entirely. She wrote it in France in 1943 right before she died, and from what I understood her main argument is that parties are basically machines built to whip up what she calls "collective passion," and once your a member you're under constant pressure to toe the party line even when your own conscience or the actual facts say otherwise. She even calls it a kind of "intellectual leprosy" that spreads out from politics into how people think about everything else. She also says the real goal of any party, deep down, is just to keep growing bigger forever, which is why she thinks they're basically totalitarian by nature even inside a democracy.

Anyway me and my friend got into an argument about this at lunch because he thinks her whole point is kind of outdated since she was specifically reacting to the Nazi party and the French Third Republic falling apart, not anything going on today. So here's what I'm curious about: what do you think Weil would say about how political tribalism works now through stuff like Twitter/X, Facebook groups, or subreddits, where people get sucked into intense partisan bubbles without ever officially joining a party or paying dues or w/e? Do you think she'd say that's basically the same "collective passion" mechanism just running on an algorithm instead of a party office, or would she think it's actually a different (maybe worse, maybe less bad, not sure) kind of problem since theres no formal organization forcing anyone to stay loyal? And kind of related to that, do you think she'd be into things like nonpartisan primaries or ranked-choice voting, since those are meant to weaken parties without fully getting rid of them, or would she just see that as a cop-out given how extreme her actual position was?
