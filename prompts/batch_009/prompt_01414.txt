I'm a retired evolutionary biologist (34 years on sexual selection in birds, if it matters) whose spent the last year or so happily reading philosophy now that I don't have grad students to supervise anymore. Recently I got hooked on Sharon Street's "Darwinian Dilemma" paper against moral realism -- her argument that realists owe us an explanation of why natural selection, which pretty clearly shaped our evaluative attitudes because they promoted survival and reproduction rather than because they tracked mind-independent moral truths, would also have made us reliable trackers of those truths. Either there's no link between selection and the independent truths (so our moral beliefs are probably mostly off track), or realists have to posit a wildly convenient coincidence where tracking the truth and being fitness-enhancing just happen to line up. As a biologist this felt very familiar, and, honestly, pretty devastating.

But then someone in my reading group pointed out the obvious follow-up worry: our logical and arithmetical faculties were surely shaped by selection too (theres a pretty clear fitness payoff for ancestors who could infer and count correctly), yet basically nobody thinks 2+2=4 or modus ponens gets "debunked" this way. So there's got to be some asymmetry that lets math and logic off the hook while morality stays on it. I've run across a few candidate asymmetries in my (admittedly amateur) reading -- that necessary truths make reliability trivially "safe" regardless of the process that produced the belief, that mathematical objects might be "plenitudinous" (every consistent structure exists, so there's nothing external left to fail to track), and that math is indispensable to our best science in a way moral facts aren't. What I'd like is for you to actually mount the hard-nosed case that none of these three moves restores the asymmetry -- argue, as rigorously as you can, for full "companions in guilt": that if Street's dilemma is fatal to robust moral realism, it's equally fatal to mathematical and logical realism, and nobody gets to keep their platonism just because it's about numbers instead of norms. Take each escape route at its strongest rather than swatting down a cartoon version of it.
