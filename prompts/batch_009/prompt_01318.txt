I've been reading about A. J. Ayer's logical positivism for a philosophy elective, including his argument that claims about surviving one's own bodily death are only meaningful if we can specify what empirical difference that survival would make -- otherwise, by his verification principle, they're not so much false as literally meaningless. What makes this more interesting to me is that in 1988, the year before he died, Ayer choked on a piece of salmon and his heart reportedly stopped for about four minutes; he later admitted the episode had somewhat unsettled, though not overturned, his confidence that death would be the end of him, even as he remained an atheist. Suppose Ayer had lived to see today's debates about mind uploading, in which a person's brain state is copied onto a computer: drawing on his verificationism and on whatever theory of personal identity he actually held, would he likely have counted a successful upload as that same person surviving, or merely as a new, qualitatively similar person? Please reconstruct the reasoning he would probably have used, rather than just stating a conclusion.
