I'm writing a seminar paper on Gödel's modal ontological argument, in the formalization that derives from Dana Scott's notes, and I want to get a firmer grip on the modal collapse objection Howard Sobel raised against it in 1987 — not as a slogan, but as something I can actually watch happen.

Here are the moving parts: a property φ counts as an essence of x just in case x has φ and φ necessarily entails every property x has; x has necessary existence just in case every essence of x is necessarily exemplified; and it is an axiom of the system that necessary existence is itself a positive property. Together with the axiom that positive properties are closed under strict implication, this is what lets the argument derive that God necessarily exists — but Sobel showed that the very same apparatus forces every true proposition whatsoever to be necessarily true.

Rather than have you just restate that conclusion, could you pick some thoroughly mundane contingent fact of your own choosing (nothing to do with God) and walk through, step by step, exactly how these definitions and axioms force it to come out necessary? I'd especially like you to pinpoint which single definition or axiom is doing the collapsing work, since Sobel and Anderson (in his 1990 "emendations" of the proof) end up locating the culprit rather differently, and adjudicating between the two is the crux of my paper.
