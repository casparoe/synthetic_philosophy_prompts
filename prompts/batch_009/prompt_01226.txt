Something's been bugging me and I can't tell if I'm being irrational about it. I've got two job offers: Job A is boring big-co, $175k guaranteed, and Job B is an early-stage startup, $130k base plus equity that (by the founders own numbers) has maybe a 7% shot of being worth ~$4M and otherwise nothing. Expected value clearly favors B, and my friend, whos big into rationalist blogs, says turning it down is just scope-insensitive bias, since the gap between 130k and 175k isn't nearly enough to justify skipping the higher-EV option.

But when I imagine the exact same choice scaled way down, like a guaranteed $1,750 vs a 7% shot at $40k, I get the exact same pull toward the safe option, even though at that scale neither outcome would change my life at all. So it's clearly not about the money's marginal value to me, it feels more like I just don't like that shape of bet, period. Is my friend right that this is just a bias I should correct, or could there be an actual principled reason to weigh a gamble differently than its expected value, separate from how much you value the outcomes themselves?
