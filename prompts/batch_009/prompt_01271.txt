In some decision-theory and AI-safety writing on acausal threats, I've come across the idea that an agent can protect its real values by adopting a cheap 'surrogate' goal and a firm, predictable policy of reacting to any threat against that surrogate exactly as strongly as it would react to a threat against what it actually cares about. Since the threatening party in these scenarios never causally interacts with the agent but simply predicts how it will respond, the hope is that it gets just as much leverage, at far lower cost, by targeting the worthless surrogate instead, leaving the agent's genuine values untouched even if the threat is carried out. What I cannot work out is why a predictor sharp enough to recognize the surrogate as a decoy would not also infer that the agent's 'equally strong' reaction to threats against it is just as hollow, since the agent does not really mind losing it, and so would go after the genuine goal after all. Is there an actual mechanism that keeps this defense from unraveling once the threatening party is sophisticated enough to see through the substitution, or does it only work against predictors that are not quite that sharp?
