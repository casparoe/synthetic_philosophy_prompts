Slightly different from what I usually write about, but I can't stop turning this over so I figured I'd ask. I cover science and ideas for a living, and at a dinner party last weekend two friends of mine — Priya and Dan — worked out an arrangement that's been rattling around in my head ever since. The two of them have almost mirror-image ethical blind spots: Priya is convinced eating meat is a serious moral wrong but flies constantly for work without a second thought, while Dan has practically calculated his household's carbon footprint down to the kilogram and bikes everywhere, but eats meat at every meal and thinks Priya's vegetarianism is a bit much. Half-joking, half serious, they cut a pact right there at the table: for one year, Dan goes vegetarian and Priya buys offsets for every flight she takes, and whoever caves first owes the other $300.

Their reasoning was that this gets more total good done than either of them just privately feeling guilty about there own blind spot, since each person is only on the hook for the thing that's cheap for them personally, not the thing that's actually hard for them to change. A couple of people at the table thought this was genuinely clever; someone else said it was basically a scam dressed up as ethics, since neither of them is becoming a better person or changing their mind about anything, they're just paying each other to swap chores. I keep flip-flopping on who's right about this. Is there something legitimately sound in the logic here — like they're actually generating more of what each of them cares about than they would alone — or is it really just a clever way of letting each other off the hook?
