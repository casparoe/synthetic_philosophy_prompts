This past winter, while my partner and I were traveling, our four-year-old spent about two weeks of bedtimes in the care of a relative who, to keep her from getting out of bed after lights-out, told her that something would grab her feet if she put them outside the blankets in the dark. That relative hasn't done bedtime since, but the effect has outlasted the cause. Months later, we're still, most nights, walking her calmly through the room, showing her there's nothing under the bed or in the closet, leaving a light on, and reasoning with her at length about why none of it is real. She'll readily agree, in the moment, that there's nothing to be afraid of — and is nonetheless still too frightened most nights to fall asleep unless someone stays with her. My partner thinks we simply haven't been clear or patient enough yet and wants to keep responding with more of the same kind of explanation. I find myself doubting that explanation is even the kind of thing that could fix this, though I'd struggle to say why. I'd be curious which of us has the better read on what's actually going on in her mind, and whether that changes what we should be doing at bedtime instead.
