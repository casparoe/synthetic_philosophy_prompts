I'm taking an upper-level seminar on formal epistemology this semester, and we've just wrapped up a unit on David Lewis's account of objective chance. We worked through "A Subjectivist's Guide to Objective Chance" and then, more hastily than I would have liked, the three papers that Lewis, Michael Thau, and Ned Hall published together in the same 1994 issue of Mind on what Lewis had by then nicknamed the "big bad bug" for his own view.

The problem, as I understand it, is that under Humean Supervenience the chance of an event at a time is fixed by the entire pattern of particular facts across all of history, future included, so there are "undermining" futures that the very chance distribution at that time assigns positive probability to, but which, were they to occur, would make that distribution false. The proposed fix, the New Principle, has you conditionalize on the complete chance theory of the world rather than on a bare, single chance hypothesis. My professor sketched this quickly and promised to circle back before our final papers are due, but the semester is almost over and I doubt we'll get there, so I'd like to think through for myself whether this is a principled repair or a technical patch bought at the cost of the view's original appeal.

Could you write me a dialogue between two philosophers of probability who disagree about exactly this? One should be a committed Lewisian about chance who thinks the New Principle fully rescues deference to chance without sacrificing Humean Supervenience; the other should be a primitivist who holds that single-case objective chances are an irreducible feature of the world, not something read off the total pattern of history, and who argues that the very need for a patch shows Humean chance was never suited to ground the Principal Principle to begin with. I'd like both to be genuinely strong: the Lewisian should say something substantive in defense of the New Principle rather than simply restating it, and the primitivist should explain concretely how treating chance as primitive dissolves the undermining worry in the first place (and concede whatever that move costs elsewhere, such as making chance harder to know from frequencies) rather than just handing the Humean's problem back to them. Please have them respond directly to each other's points in sequence rather than delivering parallel monologues, and feel free to let them fight over a concrete case, such as a long run of decays in a radioactive sample, if it helps keep the disagreement from floating off into pure formalism.
