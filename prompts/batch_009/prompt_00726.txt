Quick one — I'm doing a piece on "emotional contagion" research (the psych finding that feelings spread between people almost like a virus) and somebody told me Tolstoy basically got there first in What Is Art?, so I've been slogging through the old English translation. Got stuck on the bit where he's trying to seperate real art from counterfeits. His test is basically: does the work "infect" you with the artist's feeling. Then he says the whole quality of a piece comes down to how strong that infection is — "The stronger the infection the better is the art, as art, speaking now apart from its subject-matter" — meaning it doesn't even matter if the feeling being spread is a good or an ugly one, just how hard it hits you. He backs this up with three things that supposedly crank up the infection: how individual the feeling is, how clearly its expressed, and how sincere the artist was.

Except then a chapter later he flips this around completely and says good vs bad art is ALL about subject matter — whether the feelings being spread line up with the "religious perception of the age." So which is it? Those seem like two separate scales that could easily point in different directions (like, a really potent piece of propaganda would ace the first test and bomb the second). Am I missing something that reconciles these two criteria, or is this just a genuine loose thread he never ties off?
