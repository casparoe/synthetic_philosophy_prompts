Odd thing happened a couple weeks ago that I can't stop chewing on, and it's making me question something pretty basic about how agreements between people actually work. I was reporting a piece on the whole service-charge-vs-tipping debate restaurants are having right now, and then, completely unrelated, me and my partner got into a real argument over splitting a dinner bill while on vacation somewhere with a noticeably higher restaurant tax than back home. We've had the same unspoken "rule" for something like six years now - tip 20%, whoever's got their phone out does the math, no discussion needed, it's always just worked. Except this time my 20% and his 20% came out several dollars apart. Turns out I've apparently always calculated it off the pre-tax subtotal, and he's always calculated it off the total after tax, and neither of us ever noticed because back home the tax rate is low enough that the two methods basicaly always round to the same tip.

Here's the part that's actually been bugging me: there's no way, even in principal, to figure out which one of us was "really" doing it right all along, because every single dinner we've split for six years is completely consistent with both versions of the rule. It's not that either of us is misremembering - the fact just isn't sitting anywhere, not in memory, not in anything either of us ever wrote down or said out loud. And it's made me weirdly suspicious that this isn't just a tipping quirk - like it should apply to basically any rule two people think they share, right up until some edge case finally pries the interpretations apart. So genuinely, is there an actual fact about who was "right" here, or is that the wrong question to even be asking? And is our situation some kind of fluke, or is it secretly the normal condition of any rule anybody follows?
