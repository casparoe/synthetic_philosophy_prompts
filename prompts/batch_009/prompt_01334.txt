So i've been reading Wittgenstein's On Certainty on my own time (no philosophy background, i just find this stuff fascinating) and theres a tension in it i can't find a straight answer to anywhere. Around 341-343 he compares hinge propositions to the hinges of a door - they gotta stay totally still or the door, meaning all our normal doubting and reasoning, can't swing at all, which makes it sound like hinges are basically permanent and untouchable. But earlier, around 96-99ish, he uses a completely different picture, a riverbed, and flat out says the bed itself can shift over time even though right now its the hard channel that the water (the stuff we actually go around testing) flows through. Those two pictures feel like they pull in opposite directions to me: one says the foundation can't move an inch or the whole structure breaks, the other says the foundation drifts around historically and that's just how it works. Is there an actual way to reconcile these, or is Wittgenstein just being loose with his metaphors? And if hinges really can drift around like a riverbed, i don't get what's left of the claim that they're a completely different KIND of thing from ordinary beliefs, and not just really old really entrenched ones.
