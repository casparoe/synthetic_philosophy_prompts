So I spent about 35 years in condensed matter physics before I retired, and this past year I've fallen into a bit of a philosophy rabbit hole on laws of nature - the Humean (Lewis's Best System stuff) vs anti-Humean (Armstrong, Maudlin) debate. Below are some rough notes toward an essay where I try to connect it to my own field. Would love some honest pushback before I embarass myself showing this to anyone else.

Rough idea: Lewis says a law is just whatever comes out as a theorem in whichever deductive system best trades off simplicity against strength when summarizing the Humean mosaic - the total pattern of particular, non-nomic fact, with nothing "underneath" doing any governing. That reminds me a lot of renormalization group flow in stat mech. You can start with wildly different microscopic Hamiltonians - different lattices, different couplings, totally different "mosaics" at the micro level - and under coarse-graining they all flow to the same fixed point, giving the same critical exponents (say, the 2D Ising universality class). Nobody thinks that fixed point is some separate thing governing the spins from above - its just the most economical redescription of the coarse-grained pattern, which sounds a lot like what Lewis wants a law to be. If anything, universality (wildly different micro-mosaics converging on one macro-law) seems to help the Humean case, since it suggests the "law" really is a feature of the best redescription, not a necessitation relation running through the underlying physics.

Where I get stuck: which fixed point you flow to depends on the actual microdynamics, and that dynamics looks suspiciously like the productive, "necessary-connection" business Humeans are suppose to reject. So maybe I've just shoved an anti-Humean commitment down one level, and the RG picture doesn't actually help Lewis at all.

Two things I'd really like your take on: does this analogy actually track the real debate, or am I equivocating between "law" as microdynamics and "law" as macro-regularity? And is this the kind of point people already make in this literature, or is there some obvious reason it doesn't work that I'm not seeing?
