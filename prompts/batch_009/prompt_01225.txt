Write a dialogue between an internalist about practical reasons who argues from within Bernard Williams's framework of the "subjective motivational set" and the "sound deliberative route," and an externalist who denies that every genuine reason must trace back to an agent's actual motivations. Have the externalist press two connected challenges: Derek Parfit's case of Future Tuesday Indifference (an agent who cares in the ordinary way about future pain except that he feels no concern at all about pain occurring on a future Tuesday, despite holding no false belief and making no error of instrumental reasoning), and the suggestion that the correction for false belief that Williams allows for in his own gin-and-petrol example licenses an idealization extensive enough to pull an agent's "real" reasons far from anything actually present in his psychology, thereby blurring the very distinction internalism is meant to mark. The internalist should try to meet both challenges without simply abandoning the internalist thesis, and each side should respond to the specific point the other has just made rather than restate a general position.
