Had a long argument with my friend last night about Borges and time. She thinks all the labyrinths/forking-paths stuff is just cute wordplay, but I was trying to explain the actual argument in that essay of his - New Refutation of Time, I think its called - where he uses Berkeley and Hume to say theres no self behind experience, just a bundle of perceptions, so time dissolves into disconnected instants with nothing to string them together into a "me." Then, right after basically proving this to his own satisfaction, he turns around and admits he still feels crushed by time anyway, still knows hes going to die, the world is stubbornly real and so is he. She said that's him chickening out of his own argument, i think that reversal basically IS the argument. Can you write me a short story (Borges doesn't need to be a character) where someone reasons their way to that same conclusion, genuinely convinced their self doesn't persist through time, but finds they can't actually live like that's true - and don't resolve it neatly, let the tension stay stuck at the end.
