I have just started a research position on a formal verification team, writing proofs in a dependently typed proof assistant, and background reading on the history behind our tools has left me with a tension I have not been able to resolve on my own. Under the Curry–Howard correspondence, and its extension in homotopy type theory, intuitionistic logic has become the internal logic of computation: propositions are types, proofs are programs, and everything is mechanically type-checked. Yet L. E. J. Brouwer, the founder of intuitionism, held that mathematics is a languageless activity of the mind that exists prior to, and independently of, any formal or linguistic system -- on his view, even his own student Heyting's axiomatization of intuitionistic logic was at best an unnecessary, secondary description of mathematics, never the real thing. Given Brouwer's actual philosophical commitments -- his mentalism, his theory of choice sequences, and his deep suspicion of formalism as such, not merely his rejection of the law of excluded middle -- how do you think he would have reacted to seeing his logic mechanized as the operating logic of proof assistants and extended into programs like univalent foundations? Would he have recognized this as intuitionism finally vindicated by computation, or as a formalist takeover of a philosophy that was never supposed to be about formal systems at all? Please defend your answer with reference to the specific elements of his actual views, rather than resting on the more commonly cited rejection of excluded middle.
