I am working on a novel set among a small circle of Cambridge philosophers in the years just after the war, and I have written myself into a corner with a chapter I can't quite trust yet. The scene is a long, quietly tense dinner-table exchange between an elder don whose entire outlook is anchored in G. E. Moore's common-sense defence of the external world, and a younger colleague, fresh from Wittgenstein's own seminars, who has become convinced that Moore's whole approach is subtly but importantly confused. I want the disagreement on the page to be a real one rather than a decorative gesture toward "two famous philosophers who disagreed," and at the moment I don't trust myself to write it that way, because on the surface the two men seem to be saying almost the same thing: that a proposition like "the earth has existed for a long time" or "here is a hand" is not something anyone seriously doubts in ordinary life.

What I'd like from you is a comparison sharp enough to write real dialogue from. As I understand it, Moore wants to treat such propositions as items of knowledge, indeed as evidence enough to prove the sceptic wrong, whereas Wittgenstein, in the remarks eventually published as On Certainty, does not dispute a single one of Moore's certainties and yet denies that "I know" is even the correct thing to say of them, treating them instead as hinges that make ordinary doubting, testing, and knowing possible rather than as further specimens of it. I would like you to lay out precisely what Wittgenstein thinks Moore gets wrong, given that he isn't disputing Moore's list at all; how the hinge and river-bed imagery is meant to change the logical status of these certainties rather than their content; and, since I don't want my Moorean character to simply lose the argument by authorial fiat, where a resourceful defender of Moore might reasonably push back against the Wittgensteinian line. I would rather have the real shape of the disagreement, edges and all, than a tidy summary that makes the two men sound as though they were never truly at odds.
