I am a novelist working on a scene in which a wife confronts her husband about his affair, and he counters that since she has been unfaithful too, she has no standing to blame him. As I understand the philosophy of blame, her hypocrisy does not merely make her an awkward messenger — it defuses the accusation itself, since a hypocrite's blame carries no real force. I would like his rebuttal to capture this precisely: how might he phrase it so that his point lands not as "you are in no position to judge me" but as "you are simply wrong that I have done anything blameworthy"?
