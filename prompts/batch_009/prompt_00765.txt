My second novel was recently shortlisted for a fiction prize that carries a fair amount of prestige in my country, and at the dinner following the announcement I found myself in a long exchange with one of the judges, a critic I have known socially for years. I had made an offhand remark about how strange it is that five or six people, over the course of a single reading season, effectively decide which books will be treated as the best of the year, and she took genuine exception to this. Her position was that the judges do not manufacture literary merit; they merely detect it, in the way a thermometer registers a temperature it did not create. I asked her, in turn, what independent access any of us actually has to that merit, apart from the very judgments, prizes, and slowly accreted canon she was invoking, and how we would ever distinguish a case where a panel got it right from one where its members had simply agreed with each other. She retreated to the claim that, across enough independent juries and generations of readers, the books that keep being chosen simply are the good ones, since that is more or less what literary quality consists in. At the time this struck me as an evasion dressed up as an answer, though I could not quite say why.

I have kept turning the exchange over since, partly because some version of it will likely surface in the novel I am currently drafting, which centers on a disgraced literary prize, and partly because it refuses to resolve itself on its own terms. It seems to me that we had stumbled onto something more general than a disagreement about prize culture: a structural bind in which neither of the two obvious positions, that a book is good because it is chosen, or that a book is chosen because it is good, can be held on its own without difficulty, and in which her retreat to the judgment of history may simply restate the problem one level up rather than escape it. I would be grateful for help thinking this through with more rigor than I managed at the table: is there a well-established shape to this kind of impasse, was my sense that she never truly answered my question correct, and is there a genuine third option available to her, or does she ultimately have to commit to one side or the other?
