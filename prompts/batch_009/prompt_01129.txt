ok so I fell down a rabbit hole this week rereading old Alignment Forum posts on bargaining and "commitment races" -- the worry that two AGIs (or the labs behind them, or whoever) might each precommit to a specific "fair" bargaining solution before they ever actually meet the other side, and if it turns out they'd committed to different solution concepts, the mismatch itself becomes the source of conflict even though both sides genuinely think their own is the reasonable one. The example that keeps showing up is Nash bargaining vs. the Kalai-Smorodinsky solution, and when a friend pushed me on it I realized I don't actually have a gut sense of which one I'd bite the bullet on. Nash keeps independence of irrelevant alternatives but gives up monotonicity; KS does the opposite. Everytime I read an explanation it just states this like a fact of nature, "you can't have both, pick one," without ever making me feel why a reasonable person accepts the weird cost of their preferred axiom. I get that the two agree on nice symmetric cases (like the classic restaurant game, where they both land on a 2/3 chance of Italian and 1/3 sushi or whatever) -- its the messy asymmetric cases where they come apart that I can't get any intuitions for.

Could you write me a dialogue that actually sits in that mess? Setting: two equally sharp negotiators (human, AI, doesn't matter, you pick) sitting down to agree on the protocol they'll use for resolving every future dispute between the parties they represent, before any real stakes are on the table yet. One is committed to Nash, the other to Kalai-Smorodinsky, and neither budges without a real argument. I want them going after each other's actual weak spots -- get the KS advocate to really explain why its fine that dropping some option nobody would've picked anyway can flip the outcome, and get the Nash advocate to really explain why it's fine that your own side's best-case ceiling going up can leave you worse off in the actual deal. Don't let either one fold early or turn into a strawman for the other to knock down -- I want to end up genuinely torn, same as I am right now.
