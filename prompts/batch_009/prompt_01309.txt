Last weekend, a friend of mine who is finishing her doctorate in clinical psychology and I got into a long argument at a dinner party about the recent rise in adult ADHD and autism diagnoses. I claimed that Ian Hacking's notion of a "looping effect" captures something genuinely important here: the diagnostic criteria shift, the people who receive the label reorganize their self-understanding and sometimes form communities around it, and this in turn feeds back into how clinicians and committees like those behind the DSM revise the category itself. She wasn't having it. In her view, this is simply Robert Merton's self-fulfilling prophecy and Howard Becker's labeling theory under a fancier name, with Hacking getting credit mainly for prose style rather than for any real philosophical addition. I dug out some old notes from a philosophy of science seminar afterward, but nothing in them settled the question cleanly, and it's been nagging at me since.

Could you help me compare Hacking's looping effect with self-fulfilling prophecy and labeling/societal-reaction theory as accounts of how classifying people changes them? I'm especially interested in whether there is a genuine difference in mechanism, rather than just a difference in emphasis or vocabulary, and whether Hacking's picture requires that the original classification be false in the way Merton's does. I'd also like to understand why Hacking thought this kind of looping was distinctive of classifications in psychiatry and the human sciences, rather than something that could just as easily turn up in, say, mineralogy or chemistry, since that claim seems to be doing a lot of work for him. If it helps sharpen the comparison, feel free to draw on his own preferred case of multiple personality disorder rather than sticking to the ADHD/autism example.
