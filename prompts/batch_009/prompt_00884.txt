Im a retired physicist, and now that I've got free time on my hands I've been reading my way into philosophy of science. Keeps bringing me back to Wigner's essay on the 'unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics' - but every treatment I find reaches for the same three examples: complex numbers and quantum mechanics, Riemannian geometry and general relativity, group theory and particle physics. Heard them so many times now they've stopped feeling like a mystery and started feeling like a party trick.

Give me a genuinely different example: some bit of 'pure' math that was built with zero thought of physical use, and that later turned out to be exactly the tool some branch of science needed. Sketch the real history for me - who did the math, who did the science, how much time passed between them - and then tell me straight: does this case actually support Wigner's sense that something spooky is going on, or is there a more boring explanation, like selection bias, that deflates the mystery once you look closer?
