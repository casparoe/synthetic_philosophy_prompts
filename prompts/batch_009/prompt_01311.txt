So I'm about a third of the way into a novel where one of my POV characters is a color-obsessed art restorer -- she wrote most of a PhD on Goethe's Zur Farbenlehre before dropping out of academia, and now she can't help dragging him into every argument she has. Right now I'm stuck writing a dinner party scene where she gets into it with a cognitive science postdoc about "cognitive penetration of perception," basically the modern debate over whether beliefs, emotions and expectations can reach in and actually change what you perceive, as opposed to just what you conclude afterward or where you happen to point your eyes. On one side theres Fodor's old claim that early vision is "informationally encapsulated," i.e. deaf to whatever the rest of your mind believes; on the other you've got people like Susanna Siegel arguing for real cases of penetration -- her example of Jill perceptually experiencing Jack's face as angry because she (unjustifiably) fears he's mad at her, or the Proffitt experiments where hills look measurably steeper to people who are tired, out of shape, or wearing a heavy backpack.

Given that Goethe spent basically his whole career arguing against Newton that color is never just a neutral physical fact sitting "out there" waiting to be measured, but is produced through the eye's own lively, participatory activity -- he even calls colors "the deeds and sufferings of light" at one point -- where do you think he'd actually land if you dropped him into this debate? Would he side with the encapsulation camp, the penetration camp, or think the whole framing is kind of confused, maybe because it smuggles in the same subject/object split he spent his life trying to get rid of? I want my character's rant to sound like she's actually engaging with the guts of the debate instead of doing generic "ahh perception is subjective" hand-waving, so feel free to get properly into the weeds and use your own judgement on how charitable to be to Goethe here.
