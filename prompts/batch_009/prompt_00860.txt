I am preparing a session on the metaphysics of laws of nature for my graduate seminar, and I want to present one particular difficulty with real precision rather than gesturing at it loosely. Van Fraassen's 'inference problem' is usually treated as the central objection to Armstrong/Dretske/Tooley-style necessitarian theories: even granting a contingent relation of necessitation, N(F,G), between universals, it is unclear why N's holding should guarantee that all Fs are in fact Gs, or underwrite the corresponding counterfactuals. I understand the objection's rough shape, but I would like an intuitive, non-formal explanation of exactly why this inferential gap is so hard to close. I am especially interested in why Bird's strategy of grounding necessitation in the dispositional essences of properties, rather than in an external contingent relation between distinct universals, is thought by several critics to reintroduce a structurally similar gap rather than dissolve it.
