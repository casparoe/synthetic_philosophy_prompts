"The Principle of Alternate Possibilities (PAP) says a person is only morally responsible for what they did if they could have done otherwise. Frankfurt attacks this using the case of Black and Jones: Black wants Jones to do a certain thing, and has a device that lets him monitor Jones and intervene if Jones is about to choose differently, forcing him to do it anyway. But if Jones just does the thing on his own, without any signs of choosing otherwise, Black never has to act. In that scenario Jones could not have done otherwise (Black would've stepped in), but he's still responsible, since nothing actually manipulated him. So responsibility doesn't require the ability to do otherwise, and PAP is false.

Defenders of PAP came back with the 'flicker of freedom' move: even in the Black/Jones case, Jones still had one alternate possibility available, namely giving off the prior sign that would trigger Black's device, so PAP survives in a weaker form. Frankfurt introduced this flicker-of-freedom response himself to make his own case more convincing, but Fischer argues the flicker is too thin and morally irrelevant to be what grounds Jones's responsibility, so it doesn't actually rescue PAP.

Because Frankfurt cases show responsibility doesn't depend on alternate possibilities, this is enough to establish compatibilism: the only reason determinism looked threatening was that it removes our alternate possibilities, and once that's off the table there's nothing left for the incompatibilist to point to, so moral responsibility and determinism come out compatible after all."
