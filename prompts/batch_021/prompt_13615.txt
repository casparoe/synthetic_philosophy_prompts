I'm a novelist working on a chapter set at a forward operating base, where a captured enemy officer awaits a decision on his status: he killed only enemy soldiers, spared civilians, and otherwise obeyed the laws of war, but he fought for the side that a new ceasefire agreement has just formally branded as the aggressor. Write the scene as a dialogue between my two recurring characters, tasked overnight with drafting a joint recommendation on his case: Major Ilse Reyes, the base's staff judge advocate, who defends Walzer's traditionalist view that combatants who fight permissibly are moral equals whatever the justice of their cause; and Dr. Aurelio Voss, the unit's civilian ethics advisor, persuaded by McMahan's revisionist case that an unjust combatant's compliance with the laws of war gives him no liability-based right to kill, so that the prisoner is not, in fact, anyone's moral equal. Have them press each other on the hardest versions of the disagreement - the unfairness of expecting soldiers to adjudicate their own war's justice, the practical costs of an asymmetric law of armed conflict, and whether obedience to the rules of engagement can really cancel out a war's injustice - and let the recommendation remain unwritten at the end of the scene.
