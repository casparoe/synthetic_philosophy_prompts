I have spent the last few weeks working through Lara Buchak's Risk and Rationality after it kept being invoked in a LessWrong thread on the Allais paradox, and I think I have the basic formal apparatus down. Risk-weighted expected utility (REU) theory keeps the usual utility function u over outcomes and probability function p over states, but instead of taking a straight probability-weighted average, it applies a separate, strictly increasing "risk function" r, with r(0) = 0 and r(1) = 1, to the decumulative probabilities — the probability of getting an outcome at least as good as each given one — before taking differences across ranked outcomes. When r is the identity function, this collapses to ordinary expected utility; when r is convex, the agent comes out globally risk-avoidant in a way Buchak insists cannot be reduced to the curvature of u. The point of building in this extra structure is to make risk attitude a third rational primitive, alongside credence and value, so that classic Allais preferences — a certain $1M preferred to a 10%/89%/1% gamble on $5M/$1M/$0, together with a 10% shot at $5M over 90% nothing preferred to an 11% shot at $1M over 89% nothing — come out as fully rational, rather than as the incoherence that the more orthodox view associated with the LessWrong sequences, on which satisfying the VNM axioms is close to constitutive of coherent preference, would presumably diagnose them as.

What I have not been able to settle on my own, and what my more decision-theory-minded friends and I keep talking past each other about, is what becomes of all this once choices arrive sequentially rather than as a single global gamble. If I understand her correctly, Buchak wants REU to govern "global" gambles assessed all at once, rather than a running series of local decisions — which looks to me like precisely the move one would need to make in order to sidestep the dynamic-consistency troubles that independence-violating theories (rank-dependent utility, prospect theory, REU among them) are notorious for. If the plan an REU maximizer would commit to in advance, evaluating an entire decision tree as one global gamble, disagrees with what a fresh REU calculation over just the remaining sub-gamble recommends once a later decision node is actually reached, then the agent either has to be held to a plan her own theory no longer endorses at that point, or she behaves inconsistently over time. It is not obvious to me that confining REU's scope to "global" gambles does anything more than stipulate this problem out of existence rather than answer it.

So here is what I would like from you. Construct one complete, fully numerical sequential choice problem with two decision nodes — concrete monetary outcomes, concrete probabilities at each node, and an explicit strictly convex risk function of your own choosing (r(p) = p^2 is fine, or propose your own) — such that (i) the REU-maximizing plan for the whole tree, computed by treating it as a single global gamble, calls for one action at the second node, while (ii) recomputing REU using only the outcomes and probabilities of the remaining sub-gamble once that node is actually reached recommends a different action. Work the REU sums through explicitly rather than simply asserting the conflict, state precisely which rationality condition is being sacrificed as a result, and close by assessing whether Buchak's restriction of REU to one-off global gambles is a principled way of defusing this problem or whether it merely relocates it.
