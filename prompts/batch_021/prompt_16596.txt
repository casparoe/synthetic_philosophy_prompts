Brought a new scene to my writing group and it kind of hijacked the whole meeting. My firefighter MC goes back into a collapsing building for a trapped dog after all the people are already safely out, and two of the resident philosophy nerds in the group got into a whole thing about whether that still counts as heroic. Naomi said no: hes risking his own crew, who'd have to go in after him if he gets trapped, for a dog, and that's not supererogatory, its just reckless — supererogation is supposed to be about the agent spending his own safety, not gambling with everyone else's. Theo said that's true of literally every rescue, firefighters and lifeguards and mountain rescue teams all risk backup having to save them, and nobody calls that reckless, so her rule would disqualify basically all real-world heroism. Naomi came back that it's not about risk existing, it's about proportion: risking a lot to save a person is worth what's at stake, doing the same for a dog isn't, and supererogation needs the good achieved to be commensurate with the risk imposed on others, not just on the guy running in. Theo said that just smuggles in a contestable claim about animal worth and dresses it up as a conceptual truth about supererogation, when plenty of firefighters would say risking a lot for any living thing is exactly what makes an act heroic rather than reckless in the first place. Neither of them budged and I don't have the philosophy background to referee it myself. Can you go through all four moves, say who's more right at each step (not just who wins overall), flag anything you think both of them are missing, and end with a line reading FINAL: Naomi or FINAL: Theo?
