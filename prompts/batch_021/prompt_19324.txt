At my company we have a standing rule that nobody merges to main on a Friday without a second engineer signing off on the review, a policy that exists because of a couple of bad weekend outages a while back. Last Friday I merged a one-line hotfix without that second sign-off, because the only other reviewer online was tied up in a meeting and the change was about as low-risk as it gets; nothing broke, and I posted about it in our team channel right after. On Monday, in our regular retro, our engineering director used it as his example of "engineers who don't respect the process," without saying my name but clearly meaning me, and spent a good five minutes on accountability in front of the whole team.

What's been bothering me since is that I know for a fact he pushes commits straight to main with no review at all on a fairly regular basis, and has for years; nobody says anything about it because he's the director and effectively owns the deploy pipeline. I mentioned this to him afterward, carefully, and he said it's different for him: as director he's ultimately accountable for the whole system in a way the rest of us aren't, so the rule was never really meant to apply to him in the same sense. I don't know whether that's a genuine difference in our positions or just a convenient way of exempting himself from something he enforces on everyone else.

I ran the situation by a friend, who said the comparison doesn't really hold up: whatever he does or doesn't do, I still broke a rule I'd agreed to, and two wrongs don't make a right. I can't disagree with that part. But it still feels like there's a separate issue with him specifically being the one to stand up and deliver that lecture, given what he does, that isn't captured just by asking whether my hotfix was against policy. I have a 1:1 with him next week and keep going back and forth on whether to raise it, so I'd like to actually think this through rather than just going with my gut. Is there a real, principled way to distinguish "was breaking the rule wrong" from "did he have any right to be the one calling it out in that way," or is my friend correct and I'm just dressing up annoyance at being singled out as some deeper unfairness?
