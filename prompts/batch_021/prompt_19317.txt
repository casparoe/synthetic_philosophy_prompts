Half the room has known about his 1924 political diary for years and have basically made their peace with it, keeping it in a totally seperate drawer from the sense/reference stuff; the other half are newer students who just found out this term that the father of modern logic spent his last years writing viciously anti-semitic stuff, sneering at social democrats, and insisting Germany's real mistake was ever going parliamentary at all, and a couple of them have half-jokingly asked if they're still allowed to like Begriffsschrift. Could you write me about five minutes of opening remarks that actually stakes out a real position on whether the diary should change how we read the logic, instead of doing the easy "well, he was a man of his time" move and rushing on to the agenda? Apparently Dummett had his own private version of this crisis when he first found out, in case that's worth working in somehow.
