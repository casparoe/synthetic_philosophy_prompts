My uncle Ray died last week, 74, a house painter by day who spent almost every evening for fifty years painting these big sentimental scenes -- barns, his dog, the lake -- that never got anywhere near a gallery. He was kinda obssessed with Tolstoy's What Is Art?, and had this whole speech about "infection" and how most museum art was basically "counterfeit" next to stuff regular people could actually feel something from, which used to make my mom roll her eyes at Thanksgiving. I'm giving his eulogy Saturday to a room that's half his old union buddies who've never heard of Tolstoy and half my cousin Priya, who has an MFA in painting and will visibly wince if I get up there and basically say "expertise doesn't matter, art is just vibes." Can you write it? It needs to use Tolstoy's actual argument -- the infection idea, the universality criterion, what really separated true art from "counterfeit" art for him -- to honor what Ray sincerely believed, without condescending to Priya, turning Tolstoy into an anti-intellectual strawman, or pretending Ray's technique was secretly masterful when it wasn't.
