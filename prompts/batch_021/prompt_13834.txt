Last month, my younger brother broke our neighbor's window with a baseball, something our dad had explicitly warned him about, promising that whoever was responsible would lose a month's allowance. When my brother admitted what he had done, I offered to have the allowance taken from me instead, since he is saving up for something important, and, a little to my surprise, our dad agreed to the swap. My brother now considers the matter closed, since a month's allowance was lost either way. Something about this still bothers me, though, in a way that feels different from simply paying off a debt he owed someone: if I had covered a bill he owed the neighbor, I would be confident the debt was genuinely settled no matter who paid it, but I am less sure that losing my allowance in his place settles things in the same way, since the person who was actually careless faced no real consequence himself. Did my dad's agreeing to the swap actually make things right, or is there something about being punished for wrongdoing that cannot be handed off from one person to another the way a debt can?
