I work in application security and sit on my company's vulnerability review board, which decides how we classify and disclose security flaws to customers and auditors. We found a bug that lets a crafted request trigger a buffer overflow and give an attacker control of the process; rather than fix the code, our engineers added a monitor that instantly kills the process if it ever tries to do something a normal request wouldn't, like launching another program, and in months of testing it has caught every single attempt to seize control. Half the board wants to close the ticket as 'Resolved,' since no attack has ever succeeded; the other half wants it kept open as 'Mitigated,' since the flaw itself is untouched and would hand over control exactly as before if the monitor ever failed. I've come to think the real disagreement isn't about wording, but about whether something still counts as broken once whatever would make it manifest as broken is reliably caught every time. Please draft the recommendation memo I need for next week's meeting -- I want a clear ruling on how to classify and disclose such cases going forward, a concrete deadline for further remediation, and a serious response to the 'Resolved' camp's reasoning rather than a dismissal of it.
