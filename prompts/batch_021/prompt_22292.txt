As a side project, I've built a simple automated trading agent, and I recently noticed that its preferences over trades are technically intransitive: it ranks option A above B, B above C, and yet C above A, purely as an artifact of how its scoring heuristics combine. A friend with a philosophy background tells me this is exactly the kind of setup that "money-pump" arguments target, and that such coherence arguments are the main reason philosophers think any rational agent must act as an expected-utility maximizer. Please describe the most important implications of these coherence arguments for whether I actually need to eliminate the intransitivity in my agent, and for whether there is any principled way it could keep non-expected-utility preferences without thereby becoming exploitable.
