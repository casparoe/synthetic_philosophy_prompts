I cover science and ideas for a living, and lately im down a rabbit hole reporting on the weirder edges of the effective-altruism/rationalist decision-theory scene. A researcher I interviewed for the piece brought up this idea called "evidential cooperation in large worlds" almost as an aside, and I haven't been able to stop thinking about it since (I've also seen it called multiverse-wide cooperation via correlated decision-making, which I think was the original name before someone rebranded it). As best I can tell, the pitch is: if reality is big enough, maybe even infinite, there are tons of agents out there we can never causally reach - too far away, seperate branch of the multiverse, whatever the physics - who nonetheless reason in ways correlated with how we reason. So choosing to cooperate, like treating value systems other than your own decently even at a real cost to yourself, is itself supposed to be evidence that similarly-wired agents elsewhere are choosing the same thing in their own little pocket of reality. Worth doing "acausally," apparently, even though no message ever crosses the gap and nobody on the other end will ever know, thank you, or pay you back.

Could you turn this into a short story that makes the idea land in the gut rather than read like a decision theory problem set? I don't want a character who just stands around monologuing the theory - I want the logic baked into what they actually have to give up. Maybe center it on one person (or an AI, or an alien, or a whole civilization, your call) at the exact moment they have to eat a real cost for the sake of a "deal" that, by its own premise, can never be confirmed, witnessed, or reciprocated by whoever's on the other side of it. Keep it fairly tight, a few paragraphs to a couple pages is plenty, and please don't default into a first-contact-with-aliens plot out of habit - I want the specific can't-ever-check-your-work, no-causal-contact flavor of this idea to actually be doing work in the story, not just sitting there as flavor text.
