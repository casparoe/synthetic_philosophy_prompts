So this came up in a rationalist-adjacent Discord I'm in (the kind of group where someone will absolutely derail movie night to argue about Bayesian updating), and nobody actually had a clean answer: whats the real difference between epistemic contextualism and pragmatic encroachment, once you get past "both say stakes matter somehow"? Everyone reaches for the bank case -- low-stakes Hannah says "I know the bank's open," high-stakes Hannah suddenly isn't so sure -- like it settles things, but that case is compatible with about four different theories, and I want to know where DeRose/Cohen/Lewis-style contextualism and the Hawthorne/Stanley "subject-sensitive invariantism" (plus the Fantl & McGrath "knowledge justifies action" angle) actually part ways, not just where they overlap.

Could you lay out the core structural difference -- contextualism relocates the stakes-sensitivity into the semantics of "knows" as used by the attributor, pragmatic encroachment puts it into the subject's own epistemic standing -- and then run both theories through a case where the attributor's stakes and the subject's stakes point in opposite directions? Take a bored gossip with nothing on the line describing a friend who's about to bet the house on the proposition, and then the mirror case: a genuinely panicked attributor talking about some subject who couldn't care less either way. What's each theory committed to saying about the truth of "she knows" in both directions, and can contextualism dodge the ugly result without just quietly smuggling the subject's stakes back into the attributor's context through the side door (which has always struck me as a bit of a cheat)?

Then give me an actual verdict, not a "both sides have costs" shrug: which view comes out of that divergence case looking less ad hoc, and does contextualism's semantic-blindness problem (ordinary speakers not reporting any sense that they're doing context-shifting when they use "know") end up costing it more then pragmatic encroachment's problem of making truths about the world sound like they track how much you personally care? And if you've got room: translate the whole dispute into pure credences with no binary knows-operator anywhere -- does the disagreement survive, or was it parasitic on a vocabulary we probably shouldn't have trusted to begin with?
