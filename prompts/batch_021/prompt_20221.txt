Ok this is a dumb thing to be stuck on but its been bugging me for days, so throwing it your way.

At work we have a background job that sometimes needs one retry before its safe to actually run (some upstream dependency needs a moment to finish). A worker pulls the job off the queue and either processes it right then or defers it back for another pickup later. Because of how the queue is set up (fully stateless workers, no attempt-count in the message, long story) the worker genuinely cannot tell whether this is the 1st or 2nd time it's picked up that specific job - every pickup looks identical from inside the handler. After 2 pickups with nothing processed, the job just falls into a dead-letter queue for a human to sort out.

Rough costs: processing on pickup #1 (too early) corrupts some data and costs us about $3k to fix. Processing on pickup #2 is the ideal case, basically $0. Landing in the dead-letter queue costs about $2k in manual cleanup. Since the handler literally can't tell the two pickups apart, the only lever we have is a single fixed probability of "process vs defer" baked into the code, used the same way every single time it fires.

I worked out the probability that minimizes our expected cost by treating the whole two-pickup process as one thing to optimize ahead of time. My coworker says that's not how you should reason about it - he thinks that once you're actually inside the handler making the call, you should instead ask "given this same hardcoded probability, how likely is it that THIS pickup is the 1st vs the 2nd," and pick the best action from that. Except the probability of "which pickup is this" obviously depends on the very same number we're trying to solve for in the first place, so now he's convinced the whole thing is circular, or that there's no real fact about "which pickup this is" for the handler to even have a probability about. We're adding a real counter going forward so this exact situation won't come up again, but it's driving me a little crazy: is he onto something real here, or are we just going in circles over a glorified retry handler?
