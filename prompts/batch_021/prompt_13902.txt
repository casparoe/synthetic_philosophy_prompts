While reporting a feature on the recent proliferation of legal sports betting apps, I interviewed a self-described data-driven bettor who described his confidence heading into the NBA playoffs as follows: a 70 percent chance the Lakers make the playoffs, a 65 percent chance the Warriors make the playoffs, and an 80 percent chance that at least one of the two teams misses out entirely. When I pointed out that these three figures cannot all be correct at once, he dismissed the objection, arguing that it does not matter as long as he never accepts an individual bet that looks unfair on its own terms. Something about that response struck me as too quick, though I could not immediately say why. Is he right that this kind of overall inconsistency is harmless as long as no single wager looks bad in isolation, or is there a rigorous sense in which his approach could be quietly losing him money no matter how the season actually plays out?
