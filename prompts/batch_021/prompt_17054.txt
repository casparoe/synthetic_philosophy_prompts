Write a dialogue between two Nietzsche scholars who disagree about how to interpret the strikingly detailed prescriptions on nutrition, climate, and daily regimen that Nietzsche sets out in "Why I Am So Clever," the chapter of Ecce Homo devoted to just these questions. The first scholar argues that this fastidious self-regulation is a genuine exercise of the will to power and of the self-overcoming that culminates in what Nietzsche elsewhere calls "the great health": mastering one's own physiology, on this view, is continuous with, not opposed to, his ideal of strength. The second scholar counters that this very fastidiousness is a textbook instance of the ascetic self-denial and physiological decadence Nietzsche diagnoses in the Third Essay of the Genealogy of Morals, so that his dietary regimen is, by his own lights, a symptom of the sickness it claims to cure. Have the two work through specific passages and Nietzschean concepts rather than trading slogans, each pressing the other on exactly where disciplined self-mastery shades into self-mortification, and make sure neither has the easier side of the argument.
