Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have been working through Quine, and his program of naturalized epistemology, replacing the traditional normative question of how belief is justified with the empirical psychology of how belief is actually formed, strikes me as exactly the sort of unsentimental, science-first move a physicist can appreciate. Intellectual consistency, surely, should have pushed Quine to treat moral values the same way, dissolving questions of what one ought to value into questions for sociology and psychology about what people do in fact value, so that a moral disagreement becomes just an unfinished empirical inquiry, no different in kind from an unresolved question in biology. Yet his one sustained piece on the subject, "On the Nature of Moral Values," is, I gather, read by specialists as considerably more guarded than this. Have I sized up his actual position correctly, or is there a principled reason a thinker so committed to naturalism would stop short of naturalizing ethics along these lines?
