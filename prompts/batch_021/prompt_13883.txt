I'm a second-year PhD student in epistemology, and a reading group I'm part of recently worked through Hume's remarks on testimony, Reid's reply, C.A.J. Coady's book-length case against Hume, and Jennifer Lackey's discussion of "selfless assertion" back to back. It left me wanting to see the reductionism/anti-reductionism dispute staged as an actual confrontation, rather than the tidy textbook gloss (reductionists think testimonial justification reduces to induction from our experience of testimony's track record; anti-reductionists think we're entitled to trust testimony by default) that tends to stop right before the dispute gets interesting.

Could you write an extended dialogue between a reductionist about testimonial justification and an anti-reductionist, staged around Lackey's well-known case of a science teacher who, for independent religious reasons, does not herself believe in evolutionary theory, yet teaches it competently and in good faith -- accurately presenting the standard evidence to her class without any intention to mislead -- so that her students seem to come away knowing that evolution occurred, despite the teacher's own disbelief in what she is asserting. I'd like the anti-reductionist to have to explain what, on their view, grounds the students' entitlement to trust her once the teacher's own sincerity (in the sense of asserting only what one believes) is in question, perhaps by appeal to something like Burge's idea that we are a priori entitled to accept whatever is intelligibly presented as true absent specific reason for doubt, and to defend that appeal against the charge that it collapses into unprincipled credulity. Symmetrically, I'd like the reductionist to have to explain how a hearer could ever assemble enough independent, non-testimonial evidence to calibrate testimony's general reliability in the first place, given Coady's point that so much of the background knowledge one would need for that calibration -- history, geography, most of science -- is itself available only via testimony; the reductionist is welcome to retreat to something more like Elizabeth Fricker's monitoring-based "local" reductionism if that is the strongest available version of the view. Please have both characters engage directly with each other's actual objections rather than reciting position statements past one another, and let the dialogue close in genuine, unresolved disagreement rather than a tidy synthesis.
