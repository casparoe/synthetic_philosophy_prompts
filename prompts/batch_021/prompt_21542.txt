My aunt died a few months ago. Near the end, alone with me, she asked me to promise I'd scatter part of her ashes at a particular spot by a lake she had never once mentioned to the rest of the family, at dawn, and never explain to anyone why that place or that hour. No one else knows what I promised, or why; my husband, who will be with me when I finally do this next weekend, knows only that a promise exists, not what it contains. What strikes me is that no one could ever check whether I get it right, it cannot hurt my aunt now, and it cannot disappoint anyone who might someday learn of it -- yet it feels like the most binding promise of my life, more binding than the ones other people can actually verify. Please write the few minutes of private remarks I'll read aloud at the lake beforehand, for an audience of just my husband and, in whatever sense still applies, my aunt. I want the words to genuinely work through why a promise like this still holds when no one is left to enforce or even notice it, rather than simply asserting that it does or reaching for comforting lines about love and memory -- while still sounding like something a person would actually say at dawn by a lake, not a philosophy lecture.
