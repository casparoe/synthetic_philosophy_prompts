I serve on my town's Historic Preservation Commission, and next month we're due to rule on a case that has divided us in a way our ordinance doesn't resolve. A former feed store on Main Street has stood on the same foundation and served the same role since 1888, but fires, renovations, and flood repairs have since replaced every scrap of the original material -- nothing from 1888 remains. The owner wants our "Landmark" designation, and the tax abatement attached to it, which our ordinance reserves for buildings that have "retained their historic character since original construction." Two commissioners say this plainly fails, since nothing original survives; two others say it plainly qualifies, since it's been the same landmark the whole time. Neither side disputes a single fact the other one asserts -- I think they simply mean different things by "the same building" -- but I can't tell whether that makes our disagreement beside the point, or whether the outcome now hinges entirely on which meaning the commission decides to adopt. Could you draft the short memo I'll circulate before the vote? It should recommend one specific ruling, and the reasoning behind it should be something the losing side could actually engage with, not just a shrug that calls this a matter of definition and moves on.
