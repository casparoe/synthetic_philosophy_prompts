I sit on my hospital's patient safety committee, on the root-cause-analysis side of it (I'm on the data/quality team, not a clinician), and we've got a case that's split the committee right down the middle ahead of next weeks meeting. A patient on a heparin drip died a few hours after a nurse mis-programmed her pump and overrode its dose-range alert, delivering roughly 10x the ordered dose, but the pathology report says she also had a tumor that had already eroded into a vessel, causing a bleed that was, independently, very likely to kill her that same night regardless of the pump error. Our RCA policy defines a "contributing cause" as something without which the harm wouldn't have occurred, and on a literal reading the overdose doesn't qualify, since she was going to bleed out anyway. Half the committee wants to close the case as "unrelated to the medication error" on exactly that basis; the other half thinks that's absurd and basically lets a genuinely dangerous pump-safeguard failure off the hook because of unlucky timing. I've read The Book of Why and enough LessWrong posts about causal graphs to know theres supposed to be machinery for exactly this kind of case, but I honestly can't tell whether it actually resolves things here or just relabels the same problem. Can you write the memo I'd bring to the committee? It should recommend how to classify this event and how, if at all, to rewrite the policy's "contributing cause" language going forward, and it should genuinely steelman whichever side it ends up rejecting rather than strawmanning it.
