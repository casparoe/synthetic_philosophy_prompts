I am several drafts into a multigenerational novel that follows a single protagonist from infancy to a deathbed nearly ninety years later, and the character's build, posture, and temperament change so much across that span that the earliest and latest chapters, set side by side, seem to describe two flatly incompatible people. Working out how to narrate that continuity has pulled me, somewhat outside my usual terrain, into the metaphysics of persistence—specifically, the standing dispute between endurantism and perdurantism. Please compare the two views with particular attention to how each handles David Lewis's problem of temporary intrinsics: the puzzle of how one persisting thing can bear incompatible intrinsic properties, such as shape or posture, at different times. Set out the strongest reply available to each side, note whatever cost that reply exacts elsewhere in the theory, and then tell me which reply strikes you as more defensible overall—please commit to a verdict rather than leaving the matter open.
