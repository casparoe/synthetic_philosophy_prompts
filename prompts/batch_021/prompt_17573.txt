I'm developing a unit on the ethics of blame for a graduate seminar I'm teaching, and I want to press on a specific move in the literature on hypocrisy and standing to blame. The dominant account says a hypocrite's blame is disqualified because, in holding others to a standard while exempting themselves from it, they implicitly deny that they and the person they are blaming are equally subject to morality's demands. Please argue against this account. Specifically, construct a case in which someone blames another for a fault they openly and sincerely admit sharing — explicitly disclaiming any special exemption for themselves — yet who still seems to lack standing to blame, and use this case to defend the rival view that what disqualifies a hypocrite is simply the unfairness of continuing to enforce a standard on others that one is not, in practice, holding oneself to.
