"Harry Frankfurt's 1969 paper attacks what he calls the Principle of Alternate Possibilities (PAP): the claim that a person is morally responsible for what she has done only if she could have done otherwise. (a) State PAP in your own words, then present Frankfurt's Jones/Black case (or a relevantly similar case of your own construction), and explain exactly what the case is supposed to show about the relationship between moral responsibility and alternate possibilities. [10 points] (b) Critics have responded to Frankfurt with at least two strategies: the 'flicker of freedom' defense and the 'dilemma defense.' Choose ONE of these and (i) explain how it is supposed to save PAP from Frankfurt's counterexample, and (ii) explain how a defender of Frankfurt can respond to it. [10 points]"

And here, copied exactly as submitted, is the answer I've been assigned to grade:

"(a) PAP says that someone can only be blamed or praised for an action if it was genuinely possible for them to have acted differently — if there was no way events could have gone otherwise, the person isn't responsible for what happened. Frankfurt attacks this with the case of Jones and Black. Black wants Jones to vote for a particular candidate and has a device that lets him monitor Jones's thinking; if Jones starts to show any sign that he will vote otherwise, the device activates and makes him vote for Black's candidate instead. As it happens, Jones decides on his own, with no input from the device, to vote for Black's candidate anyway, so the device never has to do anything. Frankfurt says Jones is clearly responsible for his vote, since he made the decision entirely on his own and the device played no role in how he actually decided — yet Jones could not have done otherwise, since if he had wavered, the device would have made him vote for Black's candidate regardless. So moral responsibility doesn't actually require alternate possibilities the way PAP claims; what matters is only how the action actually came about.

(b) I'll take the flicker of freedom defense. This objection points out that Jones did retain one alternative possibility: he could have given off the initial sign of wavering that would have triggered the device, even though he didn't. Defenders of Frankfurt use this remaining sliver of possibility to argue that PAP survives after all, just in a smaller form than we first thought — some alternate possibility was available to Jones the whole time. To get rid of every last alternative possibility, Black would have to control Jones's decision from the very start, but then the decision wouldn't be Jones's own anymore, and he wouldn't be responsible for it either way. So it looks like Frankfurt can't have it both ways."
