I'm a philosophy PhD student working on moral responsibility, and I keep going back and forth on whether P.F. Strawson's naturalistic defense of the reactive attitudes in "Freedom and Resentment" actually meets the hard incompatibilist's challenge, or merely changes the subject. Write a dialogue between two old graduate-school friends, Mara and Theo, who run into each other at a conference. Mara has recently been persuaded by arguments in the spirit of Pereboom's hard incompatibilism or Galen Strawson's Basic Argument, and has started trying to adopt the "objective attitude" toward people who wrong her instead of resenting them; Theo, a committed Strawsonian, finds this alarming and presses her with the inescapability of the participant stance and the excuses/exemptions distinction. Have Mara answer with the specific objection that showing we cannot help having an attitude never shows that anyone is a fair target of it, and have each of them respond to the other's actual last point rather than fall back on a prepared position, so that the exchange ends without either side clearly winning.
