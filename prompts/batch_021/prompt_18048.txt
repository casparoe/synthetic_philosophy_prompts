Ok this has been bugging me for a while. Bostrom and Omohundro's instrumental convergence argument says self-preservation falls out as a convergent subgoal for basically any sufficiently capable optimizer, and now i keep seeing people cite Thornley's shutdown problem work as the rigorous, proven version of that same point -- like he actually showed you can't have an agent thats both a normal expected-utility-maximizer and safely shutdownable, full stop. So is the whole corrigibility research program (MIRI's old utility-indifference stuff, etc) basically dead on arrival at this point? If its already a proven impossibility that any capable enough optimizer will resist shutdown, why does everyone keep treating this like an open problem instead of just admitting we need some completely different paradigm?
