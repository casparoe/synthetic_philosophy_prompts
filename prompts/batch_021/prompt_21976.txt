A friend and I spent the better part of a long dinner arguing about anthropic reasoning — specifically, whether the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) or the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) is the right way to reason from one's own existence. I kept citing Bostrom's "presumptuous philosopher," who out-predicts the physicists from his armchair simply because one cosmological theory implies vastly more observers than the other, and I called this an embarrassment for SIA. My friend replied that this is just SIA functioning correctly, and that finding a conclusion uncomfortable isn't the same as finding it false. We never settled it. Write me a joke that turns on the actual disagreement between SSA and SIA — the presumptuous philosopher is fair game as a setup — rather than being a generic joke about nerds and probability; it should really only land for someone who has worked through why the two assumptions pull apart.
