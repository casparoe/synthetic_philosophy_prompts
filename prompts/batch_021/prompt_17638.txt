So two weeks ago I had this long, kind of unresolved argument with my friend Alex about whether calling someone a hypocrite is ever a real objection or just a way of dodging the actual argument -- it started over some news story, I don't even remember the specifics now, but we went in circles for like an hour and got nowhere. Its been bugging me since. Then this past weekend at game night, two other friends of mine, Priya and Sam, ended up having almost the exact same fight, except about someone we all actually know, so I paid way more attention this time and think I can reconstruct it pretty faithfully. I'd rather get an outside read on their exchange specifically than relitigate mine with Alex.

Context: our friend Ben posts a lot about how people should stop taking unnecessary flights because of emissions, and gives people grief about it in person too, but he flew to a wedding in Portugal this summer and flies home for the holidays every year. Someone brought that up and it went: (1) Priya said Ben has no standing to lecture anyone about flying, period, since he does the exact thing he's criticizing. (2) Sam said that's a non sequitur -- whether Ben personally flies has nothing to do with whether his point about emissions is correct, so writing him off for hypocrisy is just an ad hominem with extra steps. (3) Priya said she's not contesting the content, she's contesting whether HE gets to be the one saying it -- like you wouldn't want a serial cheater publicly shaming other people for cheating, even though cheating actually is bad. (4) Sam said fine, maybe that makes Ben an obnoxious or badly timed messenger, but that's different from saying we get to disregard the substance, and mixing those two up is exactly the mistake people make. (5) Priya escalated to the case of a government that bombs civilians and then turns around and condemns terrorists for killing civilians -- she said there's clearly something wrong with that condemnation specifically, not just its optics, like they've forfeited the right to even invoke that norm. (6) Sam said that example has way more bite, but Ben isn't a state actor causing comparable harm, he's a guy who flies to see his family twice a year -- and if that's enough to strip someone's standing, then nobody has standing to criticize anything, since were all complicit in a dozen bad systems (fast fashion, factory farms, whatever), which would just silence all criticism of collective-action problems.

They never resolved it and honestly both sides landed real punches. Could you go through moves (1) through (6) and give a verdict on each one -- not just an overall winner -- and also flag anything important you think both of them are missing? There's clearly some real distinction they're both circling without quite landing on it, something like "you don't get to say this" versus "this isn't true," and I don't know if that's actually a clean distinction philosophers have worked out or if it's messier than that.
