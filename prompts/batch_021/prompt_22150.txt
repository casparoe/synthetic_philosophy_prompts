So I went down a wikipedia rabbit hole on von neumann last weekend (guy basically invented half of computer science, also did game theory, also helped build the bomb) and found out he wrote this piece in 1955 for Fortune magazine called 'Can We Survive Technology?', doing existential-risk-type thinking before that was even a genre of blog post. Thing that got me is he doesnt argue for banning anything or for some regulatory fix. His whole point seems to be that there ISN'T a fix -- that the danger is basically a permanent condition of the world getting 'smaller' because of tech, not a one-time problem you solve and move past. And then his actual proposed answer is something like 'patience and flexibility,' which honestly sounds like a non-answer to me. Can you unpack what he's actually arguing here -- why does he think no formula or policy could solve this for good, and is 'patience and flexibility' doing any real work as an answer or is he just dodging the question of what we're supposed to actually do?
