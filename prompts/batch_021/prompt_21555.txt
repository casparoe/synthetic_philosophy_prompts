Last night a friend of mine, Priya, and I spent nearly three hours after dinner arguing about laws of nature, and neither of us really won. She kept pressing what philosophers of science sometimes call the "no explanation" objection to Humeanism: if a law just is an axiom of the deductive system that best balances simplicity and strength in summarizing the actual pattern of particular facts, then citing "the law of gravitation" to explain why an apple fell is circular, since the law counts as a law only because apples (and everything else) already fall that way. I tried to hold the Humean line by bringing up objective chance, and she turned that against me too, pointing out that the Best System is supposed to fix a chance for a present event by appeal to facts about the entire history of the universe -- including, apparently, events still in that event's future -- which seems to leave a present chance hostage to whatever happens afterward. I went home without a satisfying reply to either point.

As it happens, I host a small philosophy podcast, and next week's guest has built her career defending exactly this Lewis-style Best System Analysis of laws. I'd like to use the interview to finally get the answers I couldn't produce at dinner, so could you draft eight to ten questions for me? I want at least one question that presses the explanatory-circularity objection in a way she can't deflect just by repeating that laws are a systematization rather than a description, at least one on the undermining-futures problem for chance that Priya raised (Lewis himself called this "the big bad bug"), and at least one on whether her appeal to a privileged set of "natural" properties -- needed to keep simplicity from being trivial -- is quietly doing the same metaphysical work anti-Humeans want laws to do, so that the whole dispute collapses into a merely verbal one. For each of these, please add a follow-up question keyed to the reply she's most likely to give, and end with a question asking whether anything could, even in principle, count as evidence for governing laws over her view, given that the two are usually taken to be observationally equivalent.
