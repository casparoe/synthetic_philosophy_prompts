Okay so this weekend I got into a two-hour argument with my friend Priya over way too many beers about whether any of the "we found the feature for X" interpretability results mean anything philosophically, or whether it's all just linear algebra dressed up as discovery. She does interpretability work at an AI lab and thinks a 'feature direction' or a 'circuit' is only ever as real as the basis you happened to decode it in -- that calling something a 'deception feature' is basically a category error wearing a lab coat. I argued that once a direction is linearly decodable, keeps showing up off the distribution it was found on, and reliably moves behavior when you intervene on it, that's functionally just what 'representation' has always meant in cognitive science, and refusing to call that "real" starts to look like moving the goalposts. We didnt convince each other of anything, obviously.

Then Monday it stopped being an abstract argument, because its basically the live fight on my company's model release review board, which I sit on. Our interpretability team ran a probe for a 'harmful-intent' direction and a separate situational-awareness/eval-detection direction on the model we're about to ship, found neither active across the red-team transcripts, and separately showed they can suppress a related direction via activation steering with no measurable capability loss -- and they want all this written up as core evidence supporting release, on top of the usual behavioral red-teaming. Two board members won't sign off, arguing that treating an unreplicated probing result as evidence about what the model "really" represents is exactly the unjustified realism Priya was yelling at me about, and that nothing rules out us having latched onto a spurious correlate of this particular eval set rather than anything the model actually represents. Our release policy only requires 'sufficient evidence of safety across available methods' and says nothing about how mechanistic evidence should be weighed against behavioral evidence, so the vote keeps stalling on exactly this disagreement.

Could you draft the memo I'll circulate before we reconvene this Thursday? I want an actual recommendation with a timeframe attached -- approve, hold pending specific additional evidence (tell me concretely what that evidence would have to be, not just "do more research"), or something else -- and whichever way it comes down, I want the losing side's concern engaged on its merits rather than just waved away.
