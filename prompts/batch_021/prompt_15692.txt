Okay so this is going to sound niche but bear with me. I TA for an intro logic course and I'm also in a philosophy of language seminar this term, and we just spent like three weeks on Frege -- Begriffsschrift, the sense/reference stuff, the whole logicist project of grounding arithmetic in pure logic. I'd basically started thinking of the guy as this model of pure rational objectivity, like THE founding father of analytic philosophy. Then my professor mentioned, almost as an aside, that theres a diary Frege kept for a couple weeks in 1924 (so right at the end of his life, with Weimar Germany a mess around him) thats full of hard-right, antisemitic, anti-democratic rants -- wanting Jews stripped of political rights, cheering on Ludendorff, contempt for parliamentary government, all of it. Apparently it just sat in his papers for decades and wasn't published/translated until long after he died, and it rattled people pretty badly when it did come out -- even Michael Dummett, who probably did more than anyone to make Frege central to analytic philosophy, has talked about how disturbed he was to find out about it.

I've got a term paper on Frege due in a few weeks and now I can't stop turning this over in my head while I write it. It feels different to me than the Heidegger-and-Nazism situation, where at least people can argue the politics grows out of the philosophy somehow -- Frege's actual technical work (the logic, the philosophy of math and language) seems to have zero explicit contact with any of this. So: can you write me an essay that actually works through whether this diary should bear on how we assess Frege's philosophy? Lay out the strongest arguments on both sides -- including the basic "separate the work from the man" line, but also anything more interesting people have said about whether his politics and his hardcore anti-psychologism/objectivism might be more connected than they look -- and then actually land somewhere and defend it, not just conclude "it's complicated."
