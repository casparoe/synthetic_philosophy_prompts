So from what I can tell, Löb's theorem proves that a consistent formal system can never adopt a general reflection principle for itself -- "if I can prove P, then P is true" -- which is exactly the Löbian obstacle that shows up in the old MIRI tiling agents papers on self-modifying AI (its basically a beefed-up cousin of Gödel's second incompleteness theorem). Doesn't that mean the whole tiling agents research program was mathematically dead on arrival, since its provable that no proof-based agent can ever certify a successor (or its own future self) is sound? If thats really what the theorem shows, why did Yudkowsky and co spend years formalizing tiling agents instead of just publishing the Löbian obstacle as a knockdown argument that self-modifying AI can never be trusted?
