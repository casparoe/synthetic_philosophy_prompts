I sit on the grants committee of a small foundation that funds work on existential and suffering risk. A staff researcher wants us to earmark part of the annual budget for causes tied to value systems the committee itself assigns little weight to, arguing from evidential cooperation in large worlds: a sufficiently large universe should contain many agents in situations like ours who share our disposition to cooperate but hold different terminal values, so compromising now is evidence that our counterparts elsewhere compromise too, and the trade pays off acausally. Two of my fellow committee members call this a rebranding of ordinary worldview diversification under moral uncertainty that adds nothing but jargon; the researcher insists it is a distinct argument, since it would counsel the same compromise even if we were fully confident our own values were correct. Please draft the memo I'll circulate before our next meeting: recommend whether to adopt the earmark, reject it, or pilot it for a fixed term with a named review trigger; say what would separate a principled use of this reasoning from an unfalsifiable one that could rationalize funding anything; and give the skeptical members' position its strongest form, rather than treating it as mere discomfort with jargon.
