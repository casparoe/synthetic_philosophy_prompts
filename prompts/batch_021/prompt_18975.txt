I have long followed the debates about value learning on LessWrong and the Alignment Forum, and one recurring worry there is that inverse reinforcement learning cannot, even in principle, uniquely recover an agent's reward function from its behavior alone: any observed policy is equally consistent with a sensible reward paired with a competent planning process, and with a wildly different reward paired with a compensating, equally distorted planning process. Armstrong and Mindermann formalized this as a kind of no-free-lunch result, showing that a simplicity prior by itself cannot break the symmetry between such decompositions. This strikes me as structurally similar to Quine's indeterminacy of translation and Davidson's radical interpretation, where a speaker's meaning and beliefs are jointly underdetermined by their verbal behavior, and can be disentangled only by imposing something like the principle of charity. Please compare these two underdetermination problems in depth. Are they, at bottom, the same problem recurring in two different disciplines, or does the parallel break down once you examine how each field actually proposes to resolve it? In particular, does Davidson's charity-based resolution offer any genuine guidance for how an artificial agent should fix a "rationality prior" over human planners, or is that move doing something importantly different from what charity does for a radical interpreter?
