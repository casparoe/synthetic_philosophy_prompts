At work, I helped build a fraud-detection model for our payments platform, trained on transactions our risk team had manually labeled as fraudulent. It performs well overall, but it recently started flagging large rush orders from first-time customers around the holidays — orders that turn out to be ordinary gift-buying, but which resemble a cluster of fraud cases in the training data. When I called this a false-positive bug, a colleague on our data-science team disagreed: she argued the model was never really "about" fraud in the first place, only about whatever pattern correlated with the training labels, so there's no principled sense in which these flagged orders count as a mistake. I find that hard to accept, but I can't explain why the model should count as representing fraud specifically rather than just the pattern it locked onto. Is she right that this is just a disagreement about labels, or is there a real sense in which the model can misrepresent something?
