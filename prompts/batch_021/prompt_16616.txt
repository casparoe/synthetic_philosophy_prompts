I've been reading about the philosophy of probability in my spare time (mostly Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy articles, since I'm a backend engineer rather than someone with formal training in this), and I keep getting stuck on what's called the "reference class problem," which I understand traces back to Hans Reichenbach. As I follow it, the issue is that when you want to assign a probability to a single case — whether a specific patient will respond to a treatment, or whether a specific loan applicant will default — that individual belongs to indefinitely many different classes (all patients with condition X; all patients with condition X over 60; all patients with condition X over 60 who also smoke; and so on), each of which can yield a different base rate, with no obvious principled way to say which class is the correct one to use for that person.

This actually maps quite directly onto my day job: I build churn-prediction and credit-risk models, and we routinely add more features — tenure, recency, support-ticket history, whatever we can get — specifically to make each prediction more "individualized." Given the reference class problem, it seems to me that the philosophically correct resolution is basically just the thing we already do in machine learning: keep conditioning on more and more relevant information until you reach the narrowest, most specific reference class available for that individual, since a more specific class can only be a more accurate one, and the arbitrariness should simply disappear as you approach it. A colleague of mine who minored in philosophy insists this doesn't actually resolve the problem, and might even sharpen it, but that strikes me as backwards — how could using *more* information about a case ever fail to fix a problem that is, at bottom, about not having a principled way to choose which information to use? Is there something I'm missing about why philosophers still treat this as a live, unresolved problem rather than something straightforwardly dissolved by "just condition on everything you know about the case"?
