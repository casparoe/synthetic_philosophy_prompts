I spent my career as an experimental condensed-matter physicist, and now that I'm retired I've been working through popular philosophy of mind books to fill the time -- lately its been Giulio Tononi and Christof Koch on Integrated Information Theory. As an old simulations guy, I liked that IIT gives you an actual number, phi, that you can in principle compute for a system, rather than more of the usual hand-wavy talk about qualia. It felt refreshingly close to physics, for once.

Here's the bit that's been nagging me though. If consciousness is just a matter of a system carrying enough integrated information, then it seems to follow that a detailed enough simulation of a brain -- every neuron, every synapse, running on some future supercomputer -- would have to be just as conscious as the original, since all that same information and its integration is still there, just running on different hardware. That seems like it finally hands the "mind uploading" crowd the scientific backing they've been missing: get phi high enough in your simulation and you've got a real conscious mind, no metaphysical residue left over. My daughter did a philosophy MA and insists IIT people would actually deny this, but couldn't tell my why over dinner last week. So: given that IIT is supposed to be our best current scientific theory of consciousness, why do so many philosophers still treat mind uploading as an open, spooky metaphysical question rather than just a very hard engineering problem?
