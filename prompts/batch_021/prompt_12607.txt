I'm a retired geologist who spent about forty years reading rock strata for a living, and now that I've finally got time on my hands I've been working through the classical philosophy I always meant to get to, currently deep in Plato's Laws. Last week I hit a passage (early on, book 1 sliding into book 2, I think -- my edition doesn't make the seams obvious) that genuinely surprised me, and when I brought it up at the reading group I joined last year, one guy flatly refused to believe Plato wrote it, which sent me back to double-check. The Athenian Stranger is arguing with a Spartan and a Cretan whose home cities basically ban or frown on wine-parties because they think it wrecks military discipline, and instead of agreeing with them he flips it completely: a well-run symposium, with a sober president keeping order, is actually a cheap and useful way to test and train someone's self-control, the same way you'd deliberately expose a soldier to manufactured fear to train courage -- except a failed drinking-test costs you alot less than a failed courage-test on a battlefield. He then lays out this whole age-graded scheme: basically no wine at all for kids, moderate wine through your twenties and thirties, and then real license to actually get drunk once your past 40, because by then wine supposedly loosens the "crustiness" of old age the way fire softens iron, making older men easier to sing, dance, and generally shape into decent citizens.

What's stuck with me is this idea that testing someone's character doesn't have to wait around for a real crisis -- you can apparently manufacture the crisis in a controlled dose, on schedule, with someone sober minding the room. I'd love a short story out of this (something like 1500-2500 words), one that takes the actual mechanism seriously instead of treating "getting people drunk" as a punchline, and drops it somewhere I wouldn't expect -- far future, alien culture, corporation, research station, whatever appeals to you. Some institution uses a controlled, disinhibiting ritual (it doesn't have to literally be wine) as a real test of who earns citizenship, rank, or trust. I want the plot to turn on a point where the Platonic logic actually breaks down: maybe the "sober president" running the test isn't as sober or honest as advertised, or somebody sails through the test beautifully and then falls apart the first time real danger shows up later with nobody sober around to catch them, or the ritual just can't work on someone who never developed the ordinary shame it's designed to strip away in the first place. Real characters with something at stake, please, not a philosophy dialogue wearing a costume.
