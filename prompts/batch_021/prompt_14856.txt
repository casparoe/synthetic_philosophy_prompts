I'm a backend engineer at a small fertility-tech startup -- we built the pipeline that takes genetic data from IVF embryos and ranks them by predicted lifetime risk of stuff like heart disease, type 2 diabetes, a few cancers, so the clinic and the parents can decide which one to transfer. That product's been live for over a year now and nobody really blinks at it anymore. What's actually on the table for Thursday's ethics review (I got roped onto this committee because I wrote a chunk of the scoring pipeline, and apparently that makes me "technical enough" to have opinions on the ethics too) is a proposed new tier that adds a composite risk score for psychiatric conditions -- depression, schizophrenia, ADHD -- computed the same way as the disease scores.

About half the committee wants to just wave it through, same idea as the existing product, parents can do what they want with the extra info. The other half is stuck on two things. First, the obvious one: ranking embryos by predicted risk of depression or schizophrenia sure sounds like it's making a judgment about people who already live with those conditions, and our one actual disability-rights person on the committee is not happy about it. Second, a weirder objection our genetic counselor keeps raising: legal wants us to keep marketing all of this, old tier and new, as "giving your future child the healthiest possible start," but she points out that framing doesn't really hold up once you think about it, since whichever embryo actually gets transferred just is the resulting kid, and that kid was never "at risk" for whatever the embryos that didn't get picked might've developed -- those would've been different kids, not this one. Somebody linked a philosophy article calling this "the nonidentity problem," which I guess is an actual established thing and not just her being clever, but I don't have the background to know how much weight that should carry, or whether it's the kind of thing that should change what we ship versus just being a fun fact that doesn't move the needle.

Could you write the actual memo I'm suppose to bring to Thursday's meeting? I need a real recommendation -- ship the psychiatric tier, kill it, or ship it with specific conditions -- not a list of considerations with no bottom line, and if it's "conditions" I want actual next steps and a rough timeline attached, not "we should study this more." Whichever way it comes out, it needs to seriously engage both the disability-rights objection and the identity weirdness instead of just falling back on the "we're helping your future child" language, because that's the exact phrase under fire and half the committee will call it out immediately if the memo leans on it.
