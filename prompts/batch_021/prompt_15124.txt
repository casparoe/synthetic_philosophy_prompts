My closest friend from an old online rationalist forum is getting married next month, and she's asked me to give the toast at the reception. She's the one who first explained Gregory Kavka's toxin puzzle to me: an eccentric billionaire will pay you handsomely if, by tonight, you sincerely intend to drink a foul but harmless toxin tomorrow — and he has some reliable way of telling whether you really do — so that once the money is already in your account, you have no reason left to go through with it. We spent years arguing about what the puzzle actually shows, and I want to build the toast around it, even though only a handful of the guests will know what decision theory is. Write me a five-minute toast that explains just enough for everyone else to follow, uses the puzzle's own mechanics — not just a vague nod to "keeping commitments" — to say something specific and true about her, and doesn't leave the room thinking her vows are precisely the kind of promise it says a rational person can't keep.
