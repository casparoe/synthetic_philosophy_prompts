So I'm taking a 19th-century aesthetics seminar this semester and we just spent two lectures on Schopenhauer's philosophy of music from The World as Will and Representation (book 3, section 52, Payne translation if that matters), and honestly I came out more confused than I went in. The big takeaway everyone repeats is that music is supposedly different from every other art because it doesn't copy the Platonic Ideas the way painting, sculpture, or poetry do - it skips that whole ladder of "grades of objectification" and copies the will directly, which is apparently why it hits us so much harder than the other arts do. Ok fine, I can sort of follow that. But then a couple pages later there's this paragraph that's been driving my study group crazy:

"In the whole of the ripieno parts that produce the harmony, between the bass and the melody-carrying voice, I recognize the whole gradation of the Ideas in which the will objectifies itself. Those nearer to the bass are the lower of these grades, the still inorganic bodies of nature; the higher ripieno voices represent to me the plant and animal worlds; and finally, in the melody, in the high, singing, principal voice, leading the whole and progressing with unrestrained freedom in the unbroken, significant connection of one thought from beginning to end, presenting a whole, I recognize the highest grade of the will's objectification, the intellectual life and endeavor of man."

Isn't he just directly mapping voice parts onto the hierarchy of Ideas here - bass to minerals, inner voices to plants and animals, melody to humans? That seems like exactly the kind of "copying the Ideas" move he says music alone is exempt from. Our prof brushed it off as "just an analogy, not a real correspondence," but that feels like it's dodging the question rather then answering it. Can you actually help me work through whether this is a genuine inconsistency in his system, or whether there's some principled way for Schopenhauer to have music both bypass the Ideas entirely AND still be describable in this idea-mapped way? Even just knowing whether this tension is something Schopenhauer scholars actually argue about (vs. me just misreading the passage) would be a big help before our discussion section tomorrow.
