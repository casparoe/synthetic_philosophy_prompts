I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I am drafting a feature on the metaphysics of persistence. Almost every accessible treatment I have found stops at the Ship of Theseus and a passing nod toward "four-dimensionalism," so before I start interviewing philosophers for the piece, I would like to see the underlying dispute conducted at full strength. Could you write a dialogue between an endurantist and a perdurantist, already several exchanges into the argument, that works through Lewis's problem of temporary intrinsics, Armstrong's rotating homogeneous disc, and the grounding problem for objects said to coincide throughout their existence (such as a statue and the lump of clay composing it)? If it comes up naturally, have one side press the other on how stage theory (exdurantism) complicates their position. Both characters should be equally sharp: each needs a genuine, technically serious rejoinder to the other's objections rather than a restatement of their own starting position, and neither should be allowed to simply win by the other falling silent.
