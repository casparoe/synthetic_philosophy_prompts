In my graduate seminar on rational agency, we've been puzzling over "acausal trade": the idea that two agents with no causal contact whatsoever - no shared light cone, no channel of communication, not even certainty that the other exists - might still coordinate, simply because each can reason accurately about how the other reasons and conditions its own choice on that prediction rather than on any signal actually received. One of the least-explored wrinkles in this literature is the so-called "commitment race": since a sufficiently capable counterpart might, in effect, be simulating your deliberation as you conduct it, the mere act of taking time to think a decision through carefully can itself be read - and exploited - as weakness, pressuring both sides toward hasty, irrevocable bargaining positions that, if incompatible, leave everyone worse off than patience would have. I'd like you to write a short story, no more than 1000 words, that dramatizes this bind for a character, institution, or civilization facing a counterpart it will never meet and cannot verify. Please avoid the familiar furniture of alien first-contact tales, face-to-face encounters between identical simulated copies, and AIs that explicitly blackmail their makers, and do not name the underlying concept anywhere in the story itself: I want the dilemma legible through plot and consequence alone.
