Hey, I'm working on a short paper (conference-length, not a monster) on Wittgenstein's 1938 lectures on religious belief and could use an outside read before I send it anywhere. What follows is more sketch than polished prose in bits, sorry -- tell me if it's even coherent.

The received view, mostly thanks to Kai Nielsen's old "Wittgensteinian Fideism" piece, is that Wittgenstein (and the Phillips-style "contemplative" school that grew out of him) insulates religious utterances from rational assessment by making them belong to a seperate language-game with its own internal criteria, so an outside "but is it true" question doesn't even get traction. My claim is that this misreads what's actually doing the work in the 1938 notes. Wittgenstein doesn't say belief in the Last Judgement is unreasonable, or exempt from reasons because its internal to a practice -- he says the belief shows itself to be a different kind of proposition by how it regulates an entire life (dread, hope, endless self-examination), not by functioning as a prediction that could simply turn out true or false the way an ordinary historical claim (Napoleon losing at Waterloo, say) could. The contrast he's chasing is one of kind, not degree of certainty -- so Nielsen's charge that this move makes religious claims unfalsifiable by fiat sort of misses what's being said.

Here's the bit I can't get past though: most actual believers I know would insist the Resurrection is factual in exactly the sense Waterloo is factual, full stop, no regulative-vs-descriptive distinction intended on their part. Doesn't that make Wittgenstein's reading revisionist about what these people believe, rather than a report of it? I gesture at Malcolm's stuff about W's own strange relationship to religion as a way out of this, but I'm not convinced it earns its keep.

Two things I want from you: (a) tell me honestly whether I'm being fair to Nielsen and to Wittgenstein, and (b) tell me whether that last move actually answers the revisionism worry or just restates it in fancier language.
