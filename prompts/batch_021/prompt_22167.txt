My dad had a massive stroke back in March and ended up on a ventilator, and three days in the doctor sat us down and said he was "brain dead," which basically meant he was already gone even though his chest kept rising and falling and his heart was beating fine. Because his heart was still going they said he could donate his organs, so they took him into surgery for that first and only turned the vent off afterward, which is when his heart actually stopped. Ever since I've been down a total rabbit hole reading about how that whole brain-dead definition got settled on back in the 60s/70s, partly to make transplants and end-of-life calls cleaner, and about doctors who've documented "brain dead" bodies that keep growing, heal wounds, even carry a pregnancy for months, and now i honestly dont know if brain dead is the same thing as just plain dead. My sister is 100% sure dad died the second that doctor said those words. My mom is just as sure he died when his heart actually stopped after they turned the vent off. I'm giving his eulogy this weekend and I don't want to just pick whichever version is more comforting if I don't actually believe it anymore, but I also can't get up there and make it sound like they took his organs from someone who wasn't dead yet, thats not fair to him or to my family. Can you write the eulogy for me? It has to actually work for a room of regular grieving people and not turn into a lecture, but it can't quietly pretend I've got an answer to when he actually died that I dont.
