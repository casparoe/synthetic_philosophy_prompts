Word got around at my school that one of the assistant coaches got let go for doing something pretty bad, but when you actually trace the story back, nobody telling it saw anything themselves — its always "my sister heard from a girl in her class who heard it from her older brother" or whatever, like four or five people removed from whatever actually happened. My friend Mara's take is that you shouldn't believe or repeat ANY of it unless somebody can point to actual first-hand proof, otherwise you're just passing along a rumor about a rumor about a rumor. My other friend Dev says that's a way too strict standard, because pretty much everything we "know" — history, geography, stuff on the news, even which foods are safe to eat — we only know because someone told us, not because we personally checked it ourselves. So if you're only allowed to believe things you can independently verify, you'd basically have to throw out almost everything you believe about anything you haven't seen with your own eyes.

It turned into a whole arguement at lunch that went nowhere for like twenty minutes, which was extra annoying since we're literally in the middle of a media-literacy unit in English about primary vs secondary sources, but that hasn't actually settled anything for us. I kind of think they're both a little right, which bugs me — Mara's got a point that a story passed through five people feels shaky, but Dev's got a point too that demanding total independent proof for everything is impossible in practice, you'd know almost nothing if you actually lived like that. Is there some actual principled way to figure out when you're justified in just taking someone's word for something versus when you should hold off, or is this genuinely just a case where reasonable people can land in different places and there's no real answer?
