I'm a retired physicist, and now that I've gotten pretty into philosophy in retirement, I've been reading through some of Einstein's non-physics writing for the first time, including his 1949 essay "Why Socialism?". Everyone quotes the bit about capitalism producing "economic anarchy," but what actually stuck with me is near the end, where he basically admits he doesnt know how you'd stop the bureaucracy of a planned economy from becoming all-powerful and trampling individual rights - he flags it as an open problem in his own preferred system, right there in the essay making the case for it. Pretty honest thing to put in writing.

Here's what I can't stop chewing on: he was worried about two seperate dangers, private capital getting concentrated enough to control democracy on one hand, and planning bureaucracy getting unaccountable on the other. Today's big tech companies seem to fuse both into one thing - massive concentrated capital plus a kind of algorithmic, near-planned control over jobs and information flow that just didn't exist in his day. Reasoning strictly from the concerns he actually laid out, not from "what would a generic socialist say about big tech" - what do you think Einstein would conclude about that combination? Would he see it as his own worst case arriving through the capitalist door instead of the socialist one, and would that change what he'd recommend, or is it just the same unsolved bureaucracy problem wearing a new hat?
