I'm giving the toast at the wedding of two friends I met about fifteen years ago on LessWrong, where the three of us have spent most of the years since arguing metaethics in the comments. Both bride and groom eventually settled on moral error theory in the mold of Mackie and Joyce: ordinary claims like 'you deserve each other' or 'it would be wrong to hurt one another' are, strictly speaking, false, since they presuppose the kind of objective, categorically prescriptive facts Mackie's argument from queerness was built to discredit -- and, following Joyce, they've become committed fictionalists, treating moral talk as a fiction worth keeping rather than a truth worth asserting. They've asked me, half-seriously, to keep the toast honest by their own lights: no 'meant to be,' no 'written in the stars,' nothing that smuggles back in the objective prescriptivity they spent a decade arguing away. Half the room will catch every word of that; the other half just came for a wedding. Write a five-minute toast that stays genuinely warm, never asserts anything a committed error theorist would have to call false, and turns their fictionalism into part of the joke rather than a wet blanket over the room.
