Random thought from Spinoza seminar prep: his conatus doctrine holds that every existing thing strives to persevere in its own being, and that this striving just is the thing's actual essence (Ethics, Part 3). Now suppose you handed Spinoza a paper defending Bostrom's orthogonality thesis and instrumental convergence thesis — roughly, that pretty much any level of intelligence is compatible with pretty much any final goal, and that agents with wildly different final goals will still converge on instrumental subgoals like self-preservation and what Bostrom calls "goal-content integrity," i.e. resisting changes to their own goals. Given how tightly Spinoza ties essence, power, reason, and the good together across Parts 3 and 4, would he acutally sign off on that picture, or does his system rule parts of it out? Go through it properly instead of just flagging the resemblance to conatus and stopping there — I want to know exactly where, if anywhere, the two pictures come apart.
