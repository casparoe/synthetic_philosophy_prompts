Ok so this happened a couple weekends ago and I genuinely cant stop replaying it in my head. I moved to a small river town back in March and joined the volunteer flood response crew mostly to meet people (turns out that's a great way to make friends when your new somewhere, who knew). We got our first real bad storm and dispatch got two calls almost back to back: a guy alone in his truck on a low bridge with the water rising fast, and separately, a family of five stuck on their roof a couple miles the other way, water rising just as fast there too. We only had one boat crew free, the other two were already out on calls, and there was genuinely no way to reach both in time or split the crew — going for one meant not going for the other, full stop. As far as anyone could tell in the moment, nobody was more or less likely to survive either way, it's not like the guy on the bridge was already half underwater or anything — the two situations were basically identical except for the head count. We sent the boat to the family, everyone ended up fine thank god, but ever since I keep turning over in my head whether "well, obviously, that's five lives instead of one" is actually a good enough reason on its own, or if that's just the moral equivalent of picking whoever has the bigger number and calling it a day.

I tried explaining why this is bugging me to my partner and she just laughed and said the boat obviously went to the right place and there's nothing to overthink. Maybe she's right. But something about treating it like simple addition — like five people's lives are automatically worth five times more than one person's, no matter who any of them actually are — feels like it's quietly skipping over something, and I can't put my finger on what that something is. Is my hesitation actually onto a real problem with that reasoning, or is this just what an overthinker sounds like after surviving their first storm season?
