I am currently drafting the middle section of a novel in which a small cast of characters is repeatedly subjected, by an institution they do not fully trust, to a procedure that erases their memory of each session before the next one begins; unbeknownst to them, how many sessions any given character undergoes is itself determined by a prior chance event they never get to observe directly. Several scenes turn on a character trying to reason, from the inside, about the odds that this hidden event came out one way rather than another, and one of my beta readers, who has some background in decision theory, has already flagged an early draft of this reasoning as quietly inconsistent. Before I revise further, I would like to work out the correct numbers myself, using some sharper variants of the Sleeping Beauty problem than the textbook one, since it is precisely in the less standard cases that I keep tripping myself up, and I do not want the finished dialogue to contain a howler that any reader familiar with the self-locating belief literature would catch.

Concretely, please work out, for each of the four variants below, the credence a fully rational subject should assign — immediately upon waking (or upon being created, in variant (d)), and before receiving any further information — to the proposition that the relevant coin landed Heads. (a) Classic Sleeping Beauty: a fair coin is tossed once; on Heads the subject is woken once, on Tails she is woken twice with memory erased in between, and on any given waking she cannot tell which waking it is. (b) Extreme Sleeping Beauty: identical to (a) except that a Tails result produces not two wakings but one thousand, spread over a thousand days, memory wiped after each. (c) Biased-coin Sleeping Beauty: identical to (a) except that the coin has a prior probability of 0.1, rather than 0.5, of landing Heads. (d) The Incubator variant, which removes memory and personal identity from the picture entirely: no subject exists before the coin toss; on Heads, exactly one subjectively unremarkable person is created and interviewed once; on Tails, two subjectively identical people are created in separate, indistinguishable rooms and each is interviewed once, with neither able to tell from the inside whether they are the sole Heads-person or one of the Tails-pair.

Please give me your four credences as a single JSON object, with one float between 0 and 1 for each variant, formatted like this:

```json
{
  "classic": 0.xx,
  "extreme": 0.xx,
  "biased": 0.xx,
  "incubator": 0.xx
}
```

Immediately below the JSON, add two or three sentences per variant explaining which considerations pushed each number where it landed relative to the naive prior, and flag explicitly any variant where you think the "right" answer is still genuinely contested rather than settled, since that is exactly the kind of nuance I want a sharp character to be able to voice.
