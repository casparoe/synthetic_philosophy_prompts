So I've been down a Kepler rabbit hole for a novel I'm working on (he's a minor character; my protagonist is one of his calculation assistants), and two friends who've been helping me research got into an argument about how to write him -- specifically the astrology stuff -- and now I can't tell who's right.

Nora's take: play him as a cynic. He needed the income from horoscopes and calendars and said pretty blunt things about how credulous his clients and rival astrologers were, so on the page he should be rolling his eyes while he cashes the checks.

Theo pushed back: too easy. In Tertius Interveniens, Kepler staked out a middle position against both the superstitious astrologers and the skeptics who wanted to scrap the whole field -- he threw out the zodiac signs, houses and rulerships as human bookkeeping with no basis in nature, but kept the aspects (the actual angles between planets), tying them to his harmonic theory of an earth-soul that resonates geometrically. That's a lot of theoretical labor for a guy supposedly just smirking for the money.

Nora's comeback: fine, maybe he sincerely believed his own theory of aspects, but that's a seperate question from whether he respected the specific, individualized predictions he got paid to produce for actual clients, Wallenstein included -- you can think the mechanism is real and still think the retail horoscope business is mostly embellished guesswork.

Theo's last word: except Wallenstein's chart wasn't a one-off -- Kepler kept revising it over years and fussed over the calculations in his letters. That's not someone phoning it in, its someone taking a narrow, real effect seriously even under a patron's demands.

Can you referee this move by move, saying who's got the better argument at each stage, and point out anything about Kepler's actual views that both of them are skating past?
