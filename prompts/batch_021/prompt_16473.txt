Four decades in statistical mechanics left me intuitively comfortable with the idea that, given enough time, thermal fluctuations can throw up almost any configuration of matter, however outlandish. Since retiring I have taken up philosophy of physics, and keep encountering how some cosmologists turn that same intuition against particular models of the universe's future: if a positive cosmological constant keeps the universe expanding forever into a de Sitter-like state, then over infinite time such fluctuations should produce vastly more stray "Boltzmann brain" observers than observers like us, who arrived via the ordinary route of stars, planets, and evolution. From what I can tell, some physicists treat this overabundance as a genuine strike against such models rather than a mere curiosity. Please describe the most important implications of the Boltzmann brain problem for how physicists should choose between rival cosmological models, and how seriously you think this sort of self-locating, anthropic reasoning should be taken as a tool for theory choice in physics.
