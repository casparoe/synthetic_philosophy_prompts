I'm a philosophy PhD student finishing a term paper for a graduate seminar on comparative epistemology, and my argument puts Zhuangzi's butterfly dream from the "Qiwulun" chapter directly alongside Descartes' dream argument in the First Meditation. My claim is that the two thought experiments are doing essentially the same philosophical work: both notice that dream experience can be subjectively indistinguishable from waking experience, and both use that fact to conclude that, at any given moment, one cannot know for certain whether one is asleep or awake, which puts all ordinary empirical judgments in doubt. On this reading, Zhuangzi pre-empted the Cartesian skeptical strategy by roughly two thousand years, and arguably did it more economically, since he needs no equivalent of the deceiving-God or evil-demon hypothesis to generalize the doubt beyond the immediate case of the dream itself.

When I presented a draft of this at the seminar, my advisor said the comparison "flattens" something essential about what the Zhuangzi passage is doing, but we ran out of time before she got past telling me to look again at how the butterfly dream sits right after the chapter's earlier argument about whether two disputants, or some third party brought in to judge between them, could ever settle who is right. I've since reread that material alongside the passage's closing line about "the transformation of things," and I still cannot see what would be lost by treating the butterfly dream as a compressed, non-Western version of the Cartesian argument. Could you help me work out what my advisor is likely getting at? Specifically, I want to know whether there is a genuine, defensible difference in structure or philosophical purpose between the two dream arguments, rather than merely a difference in cultural packaging around the same basic move, before I revise the paper.
