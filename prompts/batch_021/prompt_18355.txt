I cover science and ideas for a national magazine, and that beat landed me a seat on the artistic advisory committee of a mid-sized opera company -- the body that has to sign off on any directorial concept the general director flags as high-risk before it goes into rehearsal. We're reviving Don Giovanni next season, and the director we've engaged wants to stage every one of Giovanni's seductions as unambiguous assault, arguing that no company should let a serial predator come across as charming in 2025. Our dramaturg is pushing back with Bernard Williams's own argument about the opera: that it's a serious work precisely because the score's vitality is never fully brought to heel by the plot's moralizing, and that flattening this tension into straightforward horror turns "an idea" into a case study -- solving a problem Mozart and Da Ponte were never trying to avoid. (Williams, as it happens, spent years chairing an opera company's board himself.) The committee votes before the brochure goes to print in three weeks, and it's split down the middle. Please draft the short memo I'll circulate recommending that we approve the concept, reject it, or require specific changes, with reasoning that takes seriously what a traditional staging would actually cost -- not only artistically, but for the patrons in our own audience who have themselves experienced assault.
