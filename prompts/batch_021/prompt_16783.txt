A few months ago I received a modest inheritance, and by the end of this week I need to decide whether to put it into one of two opportunities that a family friend at a boutique wealth-management firm has been recommending: a regional real-estate fund or a pre-IPO allocation in a logistics startup. The two looked close enough in expected return that the choice initially felt unimportant, until my friend mentioned, almost in passing, that a well-known contrarian fund operating in the same space has an unusually good record — something like nine times out of ten, according to people who track it closely — of figuring out in advance which of two comparable opportunities like this one "the crowd" (meaning people with roughly my profile: same broker, same newsletters, same risk tolerance) will end up favoring, and then quietly positioning against exactly that one before the predictable wave of money arrives, since it is that very herd behavior which creates the short-term mispricing they profit from. The crucial detail, as my friend stressed, is that whatever the fund has already decided, it decided days ago, purely by modeling people like me: it is not reacting to my decision as I make it, only to the kind of person who tends to make that decision.

Here is what I cannot get past. Whenever I talk myself into the real-estate fund because it feels like the steadier, less herd-driven option, I immediately realize that "it feels steadier" is exactly the sort of reasoning that would be typical of someone in my position — which means the contrarian fund has probably already positioned against real estate, which makes the pre-IPO allocation look better instead. But the moment I lean toward the pre-IPO allocation, the identical worry arises for that option, and I swing back again. I have been through this loop more times than I would like to admit, and no amount of further thinking ever seems to settle it: becoming confident in one option seems to be exactly the evidence that it is the wrong one. Is there a rational way to break out of this and actually commit to one of the two, or is the fact that I keep flip-flopping telling me something important about the situation itself?
