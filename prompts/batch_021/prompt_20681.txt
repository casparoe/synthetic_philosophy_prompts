So I went down a rabbit hole on prediction markets and stumbled onto this 'futarchy' idea from Robin Hanson - basically the goverment votes on what counts as national welfare, then lets speculative markets bet on which policy will actually raise it, so the policy pretty much runs on autopilot based on market odds instead of politicians arguing about it. It reminded me of that bit in Keynes' General Theory where he compares the stock market to a newspaper contest where you win by picking the face you think everyone else will think is prettiest, not the one that's actually prettiest. Do you reckon Keynes would say futarchy is somehow immune to that problem, or would his own beauty-contest logic basically predict the futarchy market just turns into people betting on what other people will bet, with the real welfare outcome kind of dropping out of the picture? Genuinely curious how he'd react to the whole scheme, beyond just "he liked government intervention so he'd probably be into it."
