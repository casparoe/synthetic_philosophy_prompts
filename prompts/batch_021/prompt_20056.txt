An old college friend and I have spent the last few weeks debating whether the universe's past could be infinite, and he keeps returning to Thomson's lamp as his knockdown argument. His claim is that switching the lamp at each stage of the sequence 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, ... of a minute leaves it, once the minute has elapsed, in a state that is neither on nor off — an outright logical contradiction that, he says, shows completed infinite totalities cannot exist in reality, infinite pasts included. I'd like to push back with something more substantial than intuition before we talk again. Does Thomson's lamp really generate a contradiction in the sense he's claiming, and whatever the answer turns out to be, does it actually settle anything about whether an infinite past is possible?
