Ok, here's something that's been bugging me and I haven't seen anyone spell out clearly. Move #1: take standard expected utility theory, plug in a log utility function, and you get Kelly-optimal bet sizes — this is basically Bernoulli's old fix for the St. Petersburg paradox, and its how Kelly's result usually gets 'justified' inside a vNM framework. Move #2: Scott Garrabrant's geometric rationality sequence on LW treats the geometric expectation (weighted geometric mean over outcomes) as a basic rationality notion in its own right, and derives Kelly betting from that without a utility function in sight. Since maximizing E[log W] and maximizing the geometric expectation of W is literally the same maximization, my gut says geometric rationality is just log-utility EU theory wearing a trench coat, but I get the sense Garrabrant would say that's missing the point. Can you dig into where the two actually come apart — do they rest on different axioms (independence etc), do they generalize differently to bargaining/multi-agent cases, is there any situation where they'd actually recommend different actions — and then tell me whether geometric rationality earns its keep as a genuinely distinct foundation, or whether it collapses into expected utility once you look closely enough.
