Ok so I went down a rabbit hole on Scanlon's buck-passing account of value and kept running into this "wrong kind of reasons problem" that people apparently still write whole papers about. From what I can tell its basically just Kant's shopkeeper thing again -- an action can be totally right, but it only counts morally if you did it for the right kind of reason (duty) instead of the wrong kind (self-interest, fear, whatever). Feels like Kant closed that case two centuries ago, so why do metaethicists still treat the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem like its an open bug instead of a solved one?
