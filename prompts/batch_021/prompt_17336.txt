In my philosophy elective, we covered teleosemantics: the view that a mental representation means what it does because of the biological function that shaped it. The standard illustration is a frog's neural "bug detector," which represents flies specifically -- since tracking flies is what natural selection built it to do -- even on the occasions it fires at something that isn't a fly. I keep arguing with a friend about whether the AI chatbots we use for homework help actually mean anything by what they say, given that nothing about how they were trained resembles the natural-selection process behind the frog's circuit. Could you lay out the most important implications of teleosemantics for that question, in a way that engages with how the theory actually works rather than repeating the usual generic points about whether chatbots "really" understand language?
