Random one from the epistemic-logic side of my CS PhD: in multi-agent systems, KK ('if an agent knows P, it knows that it knows P') is just a bog-standard axiom you bolt onto your accessibility relation when you want an S4-ish model -- basically Hintikka's positive introspection axiom from the 60s, nothing metaphysically scary about it. But I've been reading Williamson's Knowledge and Its Limits for a seminar, where he frames KK as one particular instance of this much bigger thing he calls 'luminosity' (a condition is luminous iff whenever it holds, your automatically in a position to know it holds), which he then argues fails for basically every interesting mental state -- feeling cold, being in pain, etc -- via the margin-for-error stuff. So is KK really nothing more than 'luminosity, applied to knowledge itself', or is there a genuine disanalogy given that knowledge (unlike feeling cold) is factive and already supposed to be some kind of achievement? And does his anti-luminosity argument actually hit KK exactly as hard as it hits those other states, or could someone coherently reject luminosity across the board while still keeping KK, or the reverse? Not looking for a SEP-style definitions dump -- more the actual structural relationship between an old logic axiom and Williamson's newer thesis.
