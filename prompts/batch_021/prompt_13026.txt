I'm a PhD student in philosophy of language currently drafting a dissertation chapter on Quine's naturalized semantics, and a puzzle raised in my advisor's reading group on Word and Object and "Ontological Relativity" has stayed with me. Most introductory treatments present the inscrutability of reference — the fact that "gavagai" could equally well be rendered as "rabbit," "undetached rabbit part," or "temporal stage of a rabbit" — as merely the sub-sentential face of indeterminacy of translation: no possible pattern of assent and dissent to the stimulus meanings of the relevant occasion sentences could adjudicate between rival analytical hypotheses, so reference simply inherits whatever indeterminacy already afflicts translation manuals as wholes.

What unsettles this picture for me is Quine's proxy-function argument in "Ontological Relativity," which seems to secure something importantly different, and in some ways more radical. Let f be any one-to-one function on the universe of discourse — sending each rabbit to its singleton, say, or to some other systematically chosen proxy — and reinterpret every predicate's extension as the f-image of its old extension. Every sentence of the theory keeps exactly the truth value it had before, so the "translation" from the language to its reinterpreted self is completely homophonic, sentence for sentence, with no rival manual and no cross-linguistic underdetermination of stimulus meanings anywhere in sight; yet what the singular terms and predicates are taken to refer to has been shuffled beyond recognition. This looks like a purely formal result available "at home," for a single fixed language.

I'd like you to argue, as rigorously as you can, that inscrutability of reference and indeterminacy of translation are genuinely independent theses in Quine's apparatus, rather than the latter being the general phenomenon of which the former is simply a special case restricted to referring apparatus. Please work through the proxy-function construction in enough detail to show why it does not require two non-homophonically related manuals to go through; explain why Quine nonetheless tends to present the two theses as continuous with one another (his view that stimulus meaning attaches only to whole occasion sentences, never to their parts, is presumably doing some of that work); and consider at least one serious objection to the independence claim — for instance, that ordinary, non-observational indeterminacy of translation is itself generated by variation in the very same analytical hypotheses whose sub-sentential correlations constitute inscrutable reference — before defending your position against it.
