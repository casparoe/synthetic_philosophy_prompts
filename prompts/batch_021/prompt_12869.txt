I've been tinkering with a little sports-arbitrage bot as a side project and fell down a Kelly criterion rabbit hole. I get the formula fine, but the justifications people give for why you're supposed to bet the Kelly fraction seem to be arguing completely different things, and it's bugging me.

One version is straight expected utility theory: you maximize E[log(wealth)] because log happens to be your utility function, and Samuelson's famous rant against the "Kelly camp" (the one written entirely in one-syllable words) is basically right that theres nothing forcing your utility to be log instead of some other concave function -- maximizing geometric mean growth is just what a log-utility investor happens to want, not a universal rule.

Then there's this "ergodicity economics" stuff from Ole Peters and that crowd, which says that's missing the point entirely: log growth isn't a preference at all, its just the correct time-average growth rate for a multiplicative process, and plain expected value is the actual mistake, not risk aversion.

So can you compare these two justifications properly? Are they competing answers to the same question, or talking past each other -- and does the ergodicity argument really give a non-arbitrary reason to single out log specifically, or does it just relocate the same arbitrary judgment call somewhere else?
