ok so I've been rereading Franklin's autobiography (yes, I'm that guy) and the part that stuck with me way more than the actual famous 13 virtues list is this little story he tells about a guy who buys an axe and wants the whole surface polished up bright like the edge, but gets tired of turning the grindstone halfway through and decides he actually likes a "speckled axe" better after all. Franklin uses it against himself basically, admitting he pulled the exact same move with the "Order" virtue -- decided some ongoing mess and disorganization was just fine rather than putting in the work to actually fix it, even though he kept marking his little daily chart religiously the whole time.

given he'd already caught himself doing that specific kind of self deception 250-ish years before anyone slapped the name "Goodhart's law" on it, what do you think Franklin would make of todays streak-based self improvement apps (github commit streaks, duolingo, the whole "dont break the chain" thing)? would he see them as finally fixing the problem he ran into with Order, or would he say they just relocate the rationalizing from your own head into the app -- so now people are optimizing to keep the streak/chart looking good instead of actually getting more virtuous, basically a fancier speckled axe.
