I sit on the small-grants panel of a private foundation that funds unconventional physics research on foundational questions -- multiverse cosmology, quantum foundations, and the like. We've received a proposal to fund an observational search for the cosmic-ray anisotropy that a well-known 2012 lattice-QCD argument says would appear if spacetime runs on an underlying computational lattice, as a test of the simulation hypothesis. Half the panel considers this a genuinely falsifiable prediction worth funding; the other half considers it unfalsifiable metaphysics in a lab coat, since a null result can always be blamed on a finer lattice spacing we haven't yet probed. Draft the panel's decision memo -- fund, decline, or fund with conditions -- with reasoning the applicants could actually respond to, without just invoking Popper's falsifiability criterion as though naming it settles things: the dispute here is precisely about what that criterion can bear, and the losing side's case should be represented as strongly as the winning one.
