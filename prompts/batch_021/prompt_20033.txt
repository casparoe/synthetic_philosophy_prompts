A friend and I spent an entire dinner arguing about whether a chatbot can be said to believe that Paris is the capital of France. She invoked Dennett's intentional stance in its strongest form: if ascribing beliefs and desires is simply the strategy that best predicts a system's behavior, then the metaphysical question is already settled. I wanted to object that this makes a language model no different from Dennett's own thermostat, which he was willing to count as a believer of the most minimal sort, but I could not articulate why that comparison should trouble her.

I host a small philosophy podcast, and next week's guest defends exactly her position: that large language models are "true believers" in Dennett's sense, not merely convenient fictions.
