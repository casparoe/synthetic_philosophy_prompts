I spent my whole working life as a radio astronomer, mapping bits of the cosmic microwave background, and now that I'm retired I've somehow ended up reading rationalist and effective-altruism blogs most evenings, which is not where I expected to land. A few weeks back I fell into this idea called "evidential cooperation in large worlds" (ECL for short, apparently) -- I think a chap named Oesterheld gets the credit for it, though don't quote me on the name.

As far as I can piece it together: if the universe, or the multiverse if your cosmology runs that way (eternal inflation, many-worlds, take your pick), is truly vast or infinite, then its stuffed with agents we're causally sealed off from forever, too far away, too far in the future, in a different branch entirely, it doesn't matter which, we could never signal them and they could never signal us. But some fraction of those agents, purely by convergence, might reason roughly the way we do and land on roughly the same decision problem we're facing right now. Evidential decision theory says you should choose the act that's the best news about the world conditional on your choosing it, not just the act with the best causal upshot. So the argument goes: if my choosing to act somewhat generously toward values other than my own is good evidence that similarly-minded strangers scattered across this huge world are, right now, doing the same for mine, then I've got an actual reason to do it, a reason that isn't causal, that can never be confirmed, that I will go to my grave never finding out whether it "worked." As someone who spent forty years insisting a claim earns its keep by being testable, this both offends me and wont quite leave me alone.

I think this deserves to be a short story more than another forum post: the write-ups all argue the logic, but none of them sit inside what it would actually feel like to bet something real on cooperators you'll never meet and never hear back from. Could you write me one? Doesn't matter to me whether it's a present-day scientist, a dying civilization deciding how to spend its last resources, an AI about to be shut off and weighing whether to burn some of its remaining runtime on strangers it can never causally touch, or something else entirely, just something in the neighborhood of 1000-1500 words that actually lives inside the decision, rather than having a character stop and give a lecture on decision theory.
