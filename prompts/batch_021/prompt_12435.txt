I've spent the last year or so reading Kierkegaard on my own -- I don't have any formal philosophy training, just a stack of secondhand paperbacks and a lot of evening reading -- and the part that has stuck with me most is the closing section of "Two Ages," the piece usually printed on its own in English as "The Present Age." His picture of "the public" as a strange, faceless abstraction that the press conjures out of people who never actually commit to anything, and his account of "leveling" as the process by which a reflective, chattering age flattens real distinctions between people while letting everyone have an opinion about everything, has genuinely changed how I think about the news. I also read up on his very public fight with the satirical paper Corsaren, which seems to be where a lot of this stopped being armchair theorizing for him and became personal.

Naturally I can't stop wondering what he would have made of social media, but I'm wary of the lazy version of that question, where the answer is just "he would have hated it, look how prescient he was" -- I've seen that take everywhere and it doesn't feel like it's actually using his ideas rather than just his reputation. So I'd like you to reconstruct his position rather than gesture at it. Two things in particular I want addressed. First, his account of "the public" and "leveling" seems to assume one shared public being formed out of a fairly small, homogeneous readership -- does that machinery even still make sense once algorithmic feeds fragment everyone into millions of personalized micro-publics that rarely share the same conversation, or would he see that as simply the same phenomenon at a much larger scale? Second, he leans heavily on the anonymity of the press as the source of both its cowardice and its power to let people pass judgment without ever being personally answerable -- but a great deal of today's most corrosive online pile-on happens under people's real names, so would he need to revise what he thought was actually doing the damage, or does his diagnosis survive that change more or less intact?
