I'm on the committee that runs our department's Critical Thinking course -- the big gen-ed one that half the university takes to fulfil their reasoning requirement. There's a unit on Wittgenstein's On Certainty and hinge epistemology that's been in there for years, but a colleague wants it pulled after a student used it in her final paper to argue that her flat-earth views can't be rationally criticised, since they're "just as groundless, and just as much a hinge," as any of the certainties the rest of us take for granted -- and she got most of the marks before anyone caught the move. Half the committee wants to keep teaching it (better students learn to make that move and then see what's wrong with it, than never meet it at all); the other half says its basically Wittgensteinian fideism repackaged for 19 year olds, and we're just handing them a licence to be unreachable about whatever they've already decided to believe. We need this settled before syllabi are due at the end of the month. Can you draft the memo I'll send round -- a clear recommendation (keep the unit, cut it, or teach it differently), with reasoning that actually engages the strongest version of whichever side you don't recommend, rather than a strawman.
