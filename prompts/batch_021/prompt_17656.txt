Been down a rabbit hole on Henry Sidgwick's side gig running the Society for Psychical Research -- turns out the guy was way more rigorous than I expected, e.g. the Creery sisters telepathy tests, where he and the other SPR investigators got what looked like solid results for years before eventually realizing the sisters were probably just signalling to each other, and said so publicly. He also wasn't in it just for curiosity's sake: the "dualism of practical reason" from the Methods of Ethics (the fact that he couldn't actually reconcile egoism with utilitarianism on purely rational grounds) seems to have left him kind of desperate for empirical evidence of an afterlife or some moral order to the universe, something to make ethics rational again now that he was loosing his religious faith. So given both his evidentiary standards AND his personal stake in the answer, what do you think he'd have made of Daryl Bem's 2011 "Feeling the Future" precognition study and the replication-crisis freakout it set off in psychology? Would he land on "this is rigorous methodology, so the result stands," basically where he started with the Creery sisters, or would his own history make him the first to smell a rat?
