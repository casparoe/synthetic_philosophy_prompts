Last year's student council election for class president at my high school ended with several people admitting they had ranked their actual favorite second, just to keep a candidate they disliked from winning, so I've been asked to look into whether a different ranked ballot could prevent that kind of tactical voting. While researching this, I noticed that Arrow's Impossibility Theorem relies on the independence of irrelevant alternatives condition to derive its dictatorship conclusion, whereas the Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem doesn't include anything like independence of irrelevant alternatives among its assumptions at all. So if we deliberately picked a ranked system that violates independence of irrelevant alternatives, such as the Borda count, shouldn't that let us dodge the Gibbard-Satterthwaite conclusion even though it can't dodge Arrow's, and finally give our council a ranked ballot that nobody can benefit from gaming?
