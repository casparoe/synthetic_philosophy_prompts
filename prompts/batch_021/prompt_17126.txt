I teach a seminar that dips into formal epistemology and self-reference, and every year I lose half the room right around the point where I try to explain the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents -- the fact that a self-improving system can't get a proof-based guarantee that its successor is trustworthy off the ground, since no consistent system can vouch for it's own soundness (thanks, Löb's theorem). I keep meaning to write a short story that dramatizes this instead of just lecturing at them and never get round to it, so could you have a go? Give me an AI, or a lineage of AIs building successors, that runs headlong into that specific wall -- I don't want a generic 'the robot turns evil' plot, I want the logic itself to be doing the dramatic work, and by the end it should be clear why something other than proof (trust, testing, hope, whatever) had to fill the gap it left behind. A few pages is plenty, and it's totally fine if it reads more like hard sci-fi than literary fiction.
