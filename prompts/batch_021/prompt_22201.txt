For my IB Philosophy extended essay, I've chosen to write about the metaphysics of dispositional properties, and I've gotten stuck on a pair of counterexamples that my supervisor pointed me toward after I asked why philosophers don't simply accept the "simple conditional analysis" of a disposition — the idea that, say, a vase is fragile just in case it would break if it were struck. The first family of counterexamples is built around C. B. Martin's "electro-fink": a wire connected to a device that flips the wire between live and dead at the exact instant a test would be performed on it, so that whether the wire actually conducts current when touched comes apart from whether it is genuinely disposed to conduct current in the first place. The second family involves "masked" dispositions, usually illustrated with something like a fragile glass packed so carefully in padding that it would survive any fall without breaking, or a poison rendered harmless by an antidote administered in the same instant it is ingested — cases where the disposition is clearly still present, but something downstream stops it from ever manifesting.

A couple of people in my study group insisted last week that finks and masks are really just two versions of the same trick dressed up differently, while I suspected there was a more precise, structural difference between them, and we couldn't settle the disagreement. Could you compare finkish and masked dispositions as objections to the conditional analysis, explaining exactly how each one drives a wedge between having a disposition and satisfying the corresponding counterfactual? I'm especially interested in whether the two counterexamples undermine the analysis in the same way or whether one of them is doing something importantly different from the other, and, if you're willing to commit to a view, which of the two you'd say poses the more serious problem for the conditional analysis, and why. Since I want to use your answer as the backbone of one section of my essay, I'd rather have a precise, well-justified comparison than a longer list of further examples.
