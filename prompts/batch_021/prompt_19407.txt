For my history of economic thought seminar we just read Keynes's essay "Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren" from 1930, where he predicts that within about a hundred years (so basically now) the "economic problem" will pretty much be solved and people will only need to work something like 15 hours a week, since compound growth plus technology will have made everyone so much richer. Honestly the part that stuck with me more than the economics was the psychological prediction — he seems almost more worried about whether people will actually know what to DO with all that free time than whether we'll have enough stuff. He talks about how most people (except maybe the artsy Bloomsbury types he hung around with) have only ever been trained to strive, not to enjoy, so theres a real risk of a kind of collective nervous breakdown once the pressure of scarcity is gone. He also has this bit distinguishing "absolute" needs from "relative"/status needs, where he basically admits the status ones might never be satisfied no matter how rich everyone gets.

Obviously the 15 hour week never happened, but what's bugging me is it doesn't seem to be because we're too poor for it — plenty of people who are financially comfortable still work 60 hour weeks, brag about side hustles, and feel guilty taking a vacation, and then spend a lot of the free time they DO have doom-scrolling instead of anything Keynes would probably call "the art of life." My study group got into a whole argument last night over whether Keynes basically already predicted this outcome (like, it's just his "relative needs" people, exactly as he said) or whether hustle culture and infinite-scroll social media would've genuinely thrown him for a loop. So, going off what's actually in the essay and not just the "he predicted a 15 hour week and was wrong" meme version of it — what do you think Keynes would actually say if he saw all this? Would he read it as basically vindicating his worries about leisure, or as some totally different phenomenon his framework doesn't really have room for?
