I'm a philosophy PhD student working on moral psychology, and I want a dialogue that pulls apart two things I keep seeing treated as though they were the same claim: weakness of will and hyperbolic discounting.

Write a dialogue between two characters. One defends Richard Holton's view that weakness of will is a specific failure in the exercise of the capacity to stick to a reasonably formed resolution, not reducible to any pattern of preference over time. The other defends a picoeconomic, Ainslie-style view on which resolve, temptation, and their breakdown are all fully explained by hyperbolic discounting among a person's successive momentary selves, so that there is nothing further left for "weakness of will" to name. Set the dialogue at the moment a man who resolved the night before to stop gambling walks past his usual casino and feels the pull to go in, and have each character try to show that the other's account either mishandles this particular case or does no explanatory work beyond what their own view already supplies.
