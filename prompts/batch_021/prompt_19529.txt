I'm currently taking a philosophy of biology seminar, and this term I've also been appointed the student member of my university's Disability Accommodations Appeals Panel. A student has appealed our denial of exam extensions, citing severe misophonia -- acute distress triggered by others' chewing, tapping, and similar sounds. Our policy covers any "documented physiological or psychological dysfunction that substantially limits a major life activity," but nowhere defines "dysfunction," and the two outside specialists we consulted disagree in a way that maps exactly onto the etiological/causal-role debate from our reading. One, following the selected-effects line associated with Wright and Neander, argues that the heightened aversive reaction is plausibly just what a threat-monitoring mechanism was selected to produce, so nothing here is malfunctioning, however distressing the experience. The other, following Cummins's causal-role account, argues that the selection story is beside the point: what matters is whether the mechanism currently supports the relevant capacity -- sustained attention through a timed exam -- which it clearly does not, so it counts as dysfunctional regardless of its evolutionary pedigree. Please draft the panel's written decision: recommend an outcome and a time frame for review, and explain to the losing specialist why their account of function isn't simply wrong, just insufficient to settle this case.
