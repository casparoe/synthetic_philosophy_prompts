I spent my career as an experimental physicist working on gravitational-wave detection, and since retiring I have finally been working through the philosophy of physics literature I always meant to read. Starting from Newton's bucket, I have made my way to Earman and Norton's hole argument, which shows that if the points of the spacetime manifold are taken to have their own primitive identity independent of the metric, the general covariance of Einstein's field equations entails a troubling indeterminism in any region devoid of matter. This has unsettled me, because for decades, in public lectures, I described gravitational waves as literal ripples in a substance called spacetime without ever asking whether that way of talking was actually defensible. Please describe the most important implications of the hole argument, and of the substantivalist–relationist debate more generally, for whether such talk of spacetime as a genuine substance is warranted, and for how I should now describe these phenomena to a lay audience.
