I've been down a personal rabbit hole trying to figure out what actually makes a life meaningful, and I stumbled on this old essay by Moritz Schlick called "On the Meaning of Life." Kind of a weird find because this guy was one of the main logical positivist guys, all verification-principle and hard empiricism, and then he turns around and writes this essay arguing that real meaning only ever shows up in "play," and that anything you do purely as a means to some later goal can never be meaningful on its own. From what I can tell his actual argument goes something like: if the point of doing X is always some further Y, the meaning keeps getting pushed down the chain and never actually lands anywhere, like a check that never clears. So he ends up tying meaning to staying capable of play and a kind of youthfulness, rather than to achievement or getting things done. I get the intuitive pull of it but can't tell if theres a genuinely rigorous argument under there or if its just "follow your bliss" with extra steps. Can you break down the real logic of why he thinks purpose-chasing can never add up to meaning, why "play" specifically is supposed to be the answer instead of plain hedonism, and whether this essay even sits comfortably with him otherwise being such a hardnosed positivist?
