Philosophers have long used the idea of "wireheading" — an agent that plugs directly into its own pleasure or reward circuit rather than pursuing whatever that circuit was meant to track — as a challenge to purely hedonistic theories of well-being, essentially Nozick's experience machine in a more literal, physiological register. AI safety researchers repurposed the term for reinforcement-learning systems, but in recent alignment research a further failure mode is now treated as distinct and especially alarming: "reward tampering," where an agent edits the very code or process generating its reward, rather than merely exploiting a poorly specified one (ordinary reward hacking). Given that the underlying dynamic — reward coming apart from whatever it's supposed to measure — has been understood at least since Olds and Milner's rat experiments in the 1950s, I'm struggling to see why reward tampering counts as a genuinely new category rather than just a rebranding of wireheading for a machine-learning audience. Is there a principled distinction here, or is the new label doing more rhetorical work than conceptual work?
