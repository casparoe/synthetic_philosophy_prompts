Ok so I've been down a rabbit hole this week rereading the entropy chapters of Carroll's *From Eternity to Here*, plus a bunch of old Overcoming Bias/LW posts that reference this stuff, and I got into an argument about it with a friend that neither of us actually won, because neither of us could spell out one of the key steps properly, as opposed to just gesturing at it. Everyone agrees the microdynamics (classical or quantum, or close enough -- CPT-invariant) are time-reversal invariant, which is basically the content of Loschmidt's reversibility objection to Boltzmann's H-theorem: for every entropy-increasing trajectory there's a time-reversed one that's entropy-decreasing, so a strict theorem that entropy always increases can't be a pure consequence of the dynamics alone. The modern fix is Boltzmann's combinatorial move (entropy tracks how much phase-space volume a macrostate takes up) plus some probability measure over microstates. But here's the bit that confuses me: if you just apply that statistical reasoning starting from an arbitrary low-entropy moment, with no further assumptions, it seems to predict higher entropy in *both* directions away from that moment, since the exact same counting argument that says "most compatible microstates evolve toward higher entropy going forward" also says "most compatible microstates would have come from higher entropy going backward." So the only reason we get a one-way arrow instead of a symmetric entropy valley around any given "special" moment is that we additionally just stipulate -- as a further, physically brute postulate, Albert's "Past Hypothesis" -- that the actual early universe was low entropy, full stop, and refuse to run the identical statistical argument backwards from later times.

That feels like it's doing basically all the real explanatory work while getting smuggled in as an afterthought, which bugs me. So two things I'd love an actual walkthrough of, rather than just gestures at: first, why do people like Albert or Carroll think the Past Hypothesis counts as a substantive, non-question-begging explanation of the arrow of time, rather than just a relabeling of the very asymmetry we wanted explained in the first place (and is Price's stuff basically the "it's just smuggled in" objection, or is he making some seperate point)? And second, how exactly does this connect to the Boltzmann brain problem -- I get the one-sentence version (eternal de Sitter future -> thermal fluctuations forever -> way more "spontaneous" observers than "genuine" ones eventually) but I don't get why that's supposed to be a problem *specifically for the Past-Hypothesis picture*, as opposed to just a totally unrelated weird cosmology issue that happens to also involve entropy. Would rather have someone actually trace the logical connection between the two than just have both facts asserted next to each other, which is how every pop treatment of this seems to handle it.
