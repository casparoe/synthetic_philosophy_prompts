I've been reading a bit about the difference between Peirce's and James's ideas of truth -- Peirce saying truth is basically the 'final opinion' a community of investigators would eventually converge on if they investigated forever, and James saying a belief counts as 'true' if it actually pays off and has real 'cash value' in someone's actual experience. Got me wondering how each of them would cope with todays internet, where algorithms sort people into separate bubbles that each spend years 'doing their own research' and just harden further apart instead of converging, and where believing stuff that's flat out false (conspiracy theories, whatever) can genuinely make someone's life work better for them emotionally and socially even though it's not true. Which one of their theories do you reckon breaks worse under that kind of pressure, and could either of them patch things up without basically giving up on having a real theory of truth?
