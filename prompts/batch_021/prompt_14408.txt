I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and next month I'm interviewing a philosopher known for defending imprecise probabilities: the view that when evidence is too thin to justify one number, a rational agent's degrees of belief should be represented by a whole set of probability functions rather than a single number. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the position: I want at least one question on the dilation problem, where conditioning an imprecise credence on new evidence can leave one with a wider spread of probabilities than before, rather than a narrower one; one on how she chooses between rival decision rules, like Gamma-maximin and E-admissibility, which can recommend different actions from the same imprecise credal state; and one built on Adam Elga's argument that acting on imprecise credences in the usual way commits a rational agent to refusing two bets that together guarantee a profit. For each, add a follow-up keyed to her likely deflection, and close with a question about what would get her to write down a sharp number after all.
