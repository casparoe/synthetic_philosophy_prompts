I serve on my university's Honor Council, and this term's jurisprudence seminar just covered the Hart–Fuller debate over whether a validly enacted but secret rule can still count as binding law, and how to treat those who exploited it. A case now before the Council bears an uncomfortable resemblance to it. Four years ago the Council passed, by recorded vote, a 'non-toleration' clause treating failure to report a roommate's violation within 48 hours as itself a violation — but, unlike the rest of the Code, never published it, so only Council members knew it existed. A member invoked the clause against a first-year, threatening to report her roommate for not disclosing it unless she accepted a year's suspension for an offense that otherwise warranted only a warning; she complied. That member is now running for Council chair, with elections in two weeks. The clause has since been repealed as coercive and secret, and the suspended student, now an alumna, has asked the Council to expunge her record and sanction the former member. Our charter forbids retroactive sanctions for conduct 'permitted under the Code as it then stood,' and members are split over whether the clause's secrecy means it was never truly part of the Code. Please draft the short memo I'll circulate before the vote, recommending what the Council should do about both the record and the candidacy, with reasoning that seriously engages the strongest argument against whatever you recommend.
