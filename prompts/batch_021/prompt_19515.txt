At work we've started using inverse reinforcement learning to infer reward functions from logs of user behavior instead of hand-designing objectives for our recommender system. It seems to me that once we fit a Boltzmann-rational IRL model and recover the reward that best explains someone's choices, we've effectively derived what's actually good for that user in a mathematically rigorous, value-neutral way, since we're just reading the objective off the data rather than assuming any particular theory of well-being. Given that, how should we handle cases where a user's inferred reward function conflicts with what they explicitly say they want—should the recommender defer to the revealed reward, on the assumption that actions are more reliable evidence of a person's real preferences than their stated claims?
