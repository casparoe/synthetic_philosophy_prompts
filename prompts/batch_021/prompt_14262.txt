I'm a junior, and this started as a sports argument, not a philosophy one, but it's been stuck in my head ever since, probably because we're in the middle of a unit on free will in my philosophy elective. At our conference semifinal a couple of weeks ago, we were down by one with three seconds left, and the coach drew up the final inbounds play for Jayden, who missed badly, rather than for Marcus, who had already hit five threes that game but had picked up his fourth foul with two minutes left and hadn't been back on the floor since. At lunch a few days later, two of my friends, Priya and Sam, got into a long argument about whether that had been the wrong call, and I've tried and failed to figure out who actually won each part of it.

(1) Priya said the coach obviously blew it, because if Marcus had taken that shot instead, he would have made it — he was having the best shooting night any of us could remember. (2) Sam said that's not something anyone is entitled to just assert: Marcus never took the shot, so there's no fact of the matter about what would have happened — not merely that we can't find it out, but that nothing makes one answer true rather than the other. (3) Priya pushed back that there has to be a fact of the matter, because Marcus has a particular skill level and a particular temperament under pressure, and holding all of that fixed, one outcome was always going to occur, whether or not anybody could ever confirm which. (4) Sam replied that this can't be right if Marcus's shot in that exact moment was genuinely up to him rather than just a readout of his skill and psychology; if the choice was truly free, then no amount of detail about "what Marcus is like" fixes in advance what he would have done, so there's nothing left over to make the claim true. (5) Priya answered that this proves too much, since people constantly say things like "she would have said yes if he'd asked" or "he would have taken the deal if it had come sooner," and we don't treat those as meaningless — just as hard to check. Sam didn't have a quick response to that, but wasn't ready to concede either, and then the bell rang.

Could you go through this move by move and say who had the stronger argument at each step, rather than only declaring an overall winner? I'd also like to know whether the question underneath all of this — what, if anything, could make a claim like "Marcus would have made that shot" true — actually has a satisfying answer, or whether the two of them stumbled onto a real, unresolved problem.
