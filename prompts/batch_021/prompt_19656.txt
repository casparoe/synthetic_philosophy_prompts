Six weeks ago, when a woman I know only slightly through a mutual friend posted that she was in kidney failure and needed a living donor, I filled out the transplant center's online screening form -- mostly, if I'm honest, just to feel like I was doing something rather than nothing, and without expecting anything to come of it. Last week they called to tell me I am a match, and I now have to give them an answer within the month. The coordinator has laid out the terms plainly: a small but real surgical risk, several weeks away from my job to recover, in exchange for a good chance of giving this woman -- whom I have actually met only twice -- her life back. When I mentioned this at dinner, my brother told me I had lost my mind, and that I owed a near-stranger nothing at all.

Here is what I can't argue my way out of, whatever my brother says. If this same trade-off were compressed into a single vivid moment instead of stretched across a waiting list, an intake form, and a surgery date months away -- if she collapsed in front of us right there at the table, and a doctor said I could save her at this same cost to my body and this same delay to my week -- I don't think anyone would hesitate to say I obviously should. Spread out and mediated the way it actually is, the identical trade in risk and benefit somehow starts to feel like generosity rather than something required of me, and I can't tell whether that shift is tracking something morally real -- some genuine difference between letting her illness run its course and actively cutting into my own body to stop it -- or whether it's just a fact about how my attention and sympathy happen to work, with no real moral weight behind it at all.

I don't need help with the medical logistics; I have those well in hand. What I would like, before I call the coordinator back, is help thinking through whether the distance and the paperwork actually change what I owe her, and, if they don't, where -- if anywhere -- a line could be drawn on how much of myself this kind of reasoning can demand, because I don't see what stops it well short of a kidney.
