I'm TAing a philosophy of science course this term and the week on demarcation criteria sent me down a rabbit hole on Popper and Darwinism that I can't shake. So in Unended Quest Popper basically says natural selection is "almost tautological" (survival of the fittest just cashes out as survival of the survivors) and downgrades the whole Darwinian theory to a "metaphysical research programme" rather than a genuinely falsifiable science -- which is a big deal given that falsifiability is supposed to be HIS demarcation criteria in the first place. Then a few years later, in that Darwin college lecture from the late 70s (published in Dialectica, 1978 I think?), he seems to walk it back and literally calls it a recantation. Except when you actually read the recantation closely it kind of just says: fine, the fully general framework is still closer to a metaphysical scheme, but particular selection hypotheses about specific traits and environments are independently testable, so the theory as a research programme is scientifically respectable after all. Which sounds suspiciously close to a position he could've taken the first time around.

I got into an arguement with a labmate about this (she's phil of bio, I'm more general phil of science) -- she thinks the tautology charge was never serious and that Popper basically caved after creationists started quoting his "not a testable scientific theory" line approvingly, while I think there's a real, still-unresolved worry in there about what "fitness" is even supposed to explain. Could you write me an essay that actually takes a side? I want you to work through (1) whether Popper's original tautology/metaphysical-research-programme charge was a fair application of his own criterion or a misreading of how the theory actually functions, and (2) whether the 1978 recantation is a genuine change of mind or mostly a rhetorical retreat -- and actually land on a verdict for both rather than just presenting both sides and shrugging.
