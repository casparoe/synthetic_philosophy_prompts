I sit on my science magazine's editorial standards committee. Three years ago, a reporter here promised an anonymous source 'permanent, unconditional' confidentiality for lab notebooks proving data fabrication in a widely cited study; based on those notebooks we named a junior postdoc as the fabricator, and she was dismissed. That reporter has since left. The postdoc is now suing her university for wrongful termination, and discovery suggests a senior scientist directed the fabrication and that she was acting on his instructions. But authenticating the notebooks requires our source to testify about how he obtained them, which would unmask him. Reached again, he refuses, and correctly notes that our promise had no such conditions. Draft the committee's short decision memo: recommend a concrete course of action and a time frame, and engage honestly with the strongest argument against whatever you recommend, rather than settling this by simply declaring that a promise is a promise or that clearing her name obviously comes first.
