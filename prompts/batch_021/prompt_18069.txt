I'm teaching a graduate seminar on the Vienna Circle this term, and this week we hit a wall trying to make sense of Neurath's enthusiasm for "picture language" -- the ISOTYPE work he did with Arntz and later Marie Neurath, growing out of the Gesellschafts- und Wirtschaftsmuseum in Vienna. On the surface it looks like a fairly practical, almost design-adjacent project (pictorial statistics for adult/worker education, that well-known rule about repeating a fixed little icon rather than resizing one big icon to show a bigger quantity, since people misjudge area scaling). But Neurath clearly thought something more philosophically loaded was going on -- that picture statements have some kind of edge over verbal ones in terms of ambiguity, cross-lingual accessibility, maybe even resistance to a certain kind of rhetorical distortion that ordinary language allows.

What I can't square is how this fits with the rest of his epistemology. He's the guy who gave us the boat metaphor (you can't dry-dock the ship and rebuild it from scratch, you patch it plank by plank while staying afloat) and who pushed back on Carnap's appetite for a fully formalized "ideal" language for science -- so in the protocol-sentence debates he's supposed to be the anti-foundationalist, coherentist, everything-is-revisable figure of the Circle. But then the picture-language stuff almost sounds like its smuggling in a kind of naive foundationalism, as if a picture could give you some more direct, less conventional grip on the facts than a sentence does. And that's before you even get to the problem that pictures are notoriously bad at representing things like negation, generality, or hypotheticals -- so I'm not sure "picture language" can really be a language in the full sense he sometimes seems to want.

Can you help me get an intuitive, gut-level handle on how Neurath might have reconciled (or thought he'd reconciled) these two strands -- the anti-foundationalist, protocol-sentence side of his thought versus the "pictures are somehow more trustworthy or universal than words" side? And feel free to just tell me straight if you think he didn't actually reconcile them, and it's really just two separate projects that happened to share an author. I don't need a lit review or citations, I mostly just want the underlying philosophical logic laid out clearly enough that I can explain it to my students without hand-waving.
