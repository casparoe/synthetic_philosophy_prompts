As a novelist, I want to write a short story built around a distinction that unsettles me more than the everyday idea of simply not being believed. Miranda Fricker calls that more familiar half of epistemic injustice "testimonial injustice," but she also identifies "hermeneutical injustice": the harm of living through something for which the shared concepts needed to name and make sense of it do not yet exist, often precisely because the people who might have coined those concepts have been excluded from the conversations that generate them. Fricker's own illustration involves women enduring sexual harassment in the years before the term itself existed to name it. I would like a short story, set in whatever period or milieu you choose, that dramatizes a genuinely different instance of this gap - a character living through something real and damaging that neither they nor anyone around them has the words for - rendered entirely through scene and incident, without any character stepping outside the story to explain the theory.
