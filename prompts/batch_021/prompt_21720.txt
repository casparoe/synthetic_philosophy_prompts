Here's roughly where I've gotten stuck. Geach's original point is that "lying is wrong" seems to mean exactly the same thing whether it's asserted flat-out or sitting unasserted in the antecedent of "if lying is wrong, then getting your little brother to lie is wrong" — but if the whole meaning of "lying is wrong" is that it expresses disapproval, nobody's disapproving of anything in that antecedent, so it's not obvious what the modus ponens is even supposed to track. I can reconstruct Blackburn's quasi-realist fix well enough — stacking on higher-order attitudes so that holding "wrong(lying)," "wrong(lying) → wrong(inducing lying)," and "not-wrong(inducing lying)" all together counts as being in an inconsistent state of attitude, not just an inconsistent set of beliefs. But then I started reading Schroeder's Being For, and he seems to think every existing proposal (Blackburn's included) handles, at best, the easy problem about negation and inconsistency while quietly punting on the harder problem of actually validating the logical form of the inference — and his own "biforcated attitude semantics" gets offered almost apologetically, like he doesn't fully buy it either. Throw in Gibbard's plan-laden possible-worlds apparatus from Thinking How to Live, plus Dreier's paper arguing that a lot of these "solutions" just relocate the problem instead of solving it, and I honestly can't tell anymore whether Frege-Geach has been answered or whether expressivists have just gotten really good at describing why it hasn't.
