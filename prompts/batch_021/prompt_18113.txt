So I've been getting really into meditation over the past year (nothing fancy, just an app plus a weekly group my friend dragged me to), and the teacher keeps bringing up this Buddhist idea of anatta, or "no-self" -- basically that the sense we have of being one continuous "me" persisting through time isn't quite what it seems. That sent me down a rabbit hole a few weeks back into Derek Parfit's Reasons and Persons, since someone on a forum said it's basically the analytic philosophy version of the same insight: Parfit argues personal identity over time is nothing but overlapping chains of physical and psychological continuity, no extra "fact" of identity underneath it all, and I'm pretty sure he even says at one point that the Buddha basically got there first. Here's the bit that's bugging me though -- both Parfit and a bunch of the Buddhist ethics stuff I've read since seem to make this jump from "there's no deep fact separating tomorrow-you from some random stranger who happens to be caused by you" to "therefore you should loosen the special concern you have for your own future self and extend something more like it to everyone else" (impartial compassion, basically). As a total outsider to philosophy that jump feels sketchy to me -- couldn't that exact same premise about there being no deep self just as easily support "so nothing about anyone's future rationally matters, not even your own" instead of "so everyone's future matters equally"? The metaphysics alone doesn't seem like it should obviously pick a side there.

Could you write me a dialogue between two philosophers hashing this out face to face? One holds the no-self/reductionist package (Buddhist-flavored, Parfit-flavored, or some mix -- your call) and thinks it really does support that impartial-concern conclusion. The other is willing to grant the exact same reductionist metaphysics for the sake of argument, but specifically attacks the inference from "no deep self" to "impartial compassion," rather then re-litigating whether the self exists in the first place. I want actual back-and-forth where each one responds to the other's specific points instead of just taking turns monologuing, and please don't make either one an obvious pushover -- the pushback character should be just as sharp as the reductionist one. Doesn't need to end in agreement. Give them names or just labels, whatever reads better to you.
