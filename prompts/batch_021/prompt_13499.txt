I sit on the internal model-evaluation review board at an AI company, and ahead of an upcoming release we need to decide whether to approve "debate" as a required scalable-oversight check for a category of outputs our reviewers cannot verify directly -- for instance, whether a piece of generated code contains a subtle security vulnerability, or whether a biology synthesis write-up omits a safety-relevant step. The protocol under consideration follows the original debate proposal: two instances of our frontier model argue opposing sides of a disputed claim in front of a judge who cannot evaluate the object-level question unaided (either a much weaker model or a reviewer working under a tight time limit), on the theory that even such a judge can reliably pick out the honest debater, because true claims are systematically easier to defend under cross-examination than false ones.

The board is split roughly down the middle. One faction wants to approve debate as the primary gate for this output category, pointing to encouraging results from recent debate experiments with language models, where letting debaters be more persuasive tended to help rather than hurt judge accuracy. The other faction keeps raising what's sometimes called the obfuscated-arguments problem: a dishonest debater can bury the single load-bearing flaw inside an otherwise-correct-looking tree of sub-claims so deep, or behind so many individually plausible steps, that the honest debater has no way to cheaply direct the judge to exactly the right spot within the judge's actual time and ability to check -- so the "truth wins because it's easier to defend" guarantee can fail in principle, not merely because of current model limitations. Please draft the memo I'll circulate to the full board recommending that we approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions the use of debate as a required check for this output category. If you recommend conditions, specify them concretely (e.g., number of debate rounds, whether debaters may cross-examine each other, how the judge is selected or trained, what happens on a low-confidence or split verdict), and make sure the memo treats the obfuscated-arguments objection as a real design constraint rather than a worry to be waved away by citing debate's good track record so far.
