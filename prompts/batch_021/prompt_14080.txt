Six months ago my father was diagnosed with behavioral-variant frontotemporal dementia, and he has since become cutting and, at times, cruel toward my mother and occasionally toward me — behavior that feels utterly unlike the man who raised me. Trying to work out how to feel about this, I have been reading P.F. Strawson's essay "Freedom and Resentment," particularly his contrast between the participant reactive attitudes we direct at responsible agents and the objective attitude we adopt toward those we exempt from responsibility altogether. My difficulty is that he does not fit cleanly into either category: he is not a child, has not been declared incompetent, and is often lucid in conversation, so the exempting conditions Strawson describes do not obviously apply, even though the behavior itself is plainly symptomatic rather than chosen. Please describe the most important implications of Strawson's framework — including his distinction between excusing a particular act and exempting a person altogether, his remarks on vicarious versus personal reactive attitudes, and his suggestion that the objective attitude might be folded into a relationship only partially rather than adopted wholesale — for how my family might calibrate both the indignation I feel on my mother's behalf and the resentment I feel on my own.
