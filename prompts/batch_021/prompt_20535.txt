After decades of running particle physics experiments, I came away convinced that the frequentist interpretation of probability must simply be correct: whenever we repeated a decay-rate measurement often enough, the fraction of counts converged on the value our theory predicted, exactly as the long-run frequency definition requires. Now that I have taken up the philosophy of probability in retirement, I am puzzled to find serious philosophers still defending Bayesian subjectivism and propensity theory as live alternatives. Doesn't this repeated laboratory confirmation of frequency convergence settle the interpretive dispute decisively in frequentism's favor?
