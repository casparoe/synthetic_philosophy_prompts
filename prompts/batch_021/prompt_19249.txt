Two members of a small nonprofit's board I sit on argued at a meeting over a proposal to expand the seven-person grants committee to twenty-five members, on the grounds that a bigger panel would more reliably identify the stronger of two competing proposals. Elena invoked the Condorcet jury theorem: if each member is even slightly better than a coin flip at judging which proposal is stronger, and members vote independently, simple-majority decisions become almost certain to be correct as the panel grows, so expanding the committee is a straightforward accuracy gain. Raj objected that this holds only if judgments are independent, and a committee that discusses applications together is exactly the setup where independence collapses -- a couple of persuasive members can sway the room, so a twenty-five-person committee that ends up converging on what two or three people think is barely more reliable than a much smaller one. Elena conceded the point about live discussion but proposed a fix: have all twenty-five vote independently and simultaneously by secret ballot before any discussion, preserving the theorem's key assumption while still scaling up. Raj replied that secrecy isn't statistical independence -- if everyone was recruited from the same networks, trained on the same rubric, and read the same guides beforehand, their errors can be correlated even with nobody seeing anyone else's ballot, and the theorem needs independent errors, not merely simultaneous ones. He added that the theorem cuts both ways: it also assumes each member's competence exceeds fifty percent on this specific judgment, which nobody has actually shown, and if that assumption is false, more members would drive the committee toward the wrong answer with near certainty rather than the right one. Neither budged. Go through the exchange move by move: say who has the better of it at each step, flag anything both of them are overlooking, and close with an overall verdict on whether the proposed expansion is actually supported by the theorem they're both invoking.
