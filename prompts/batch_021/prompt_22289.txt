(a) Reconstruct the basic structure of a Frankfurt-style case, making clear exactly why the agent lacks alternative possibilities and yet still seems morally responsible. (b) Explain the "flicker of freedom" strategy for resisting the anti-PAP conclusion. (c) Assess whether the flicker-of-freedom strategy succeeds.

And here's the students answer, copied exactly as submitted:

"(a) In Frankfurt's case, Black is a neuroscientist who wants Jones to kill Smith. Black monitors Jones and will intervene to force the killing only if Jones shows a prior sign that he's about to decide against it; as it happens Jones decides on his own to kill Smith, so Black never has to act. Because Black's device guarantees the outcome no matter what, Jones could not have done otherwise -- but since Black never actually touches the actual sequence of Jones's deliberation, Jones's decision flows from his own ordinary reasons and desires, so we still hold him responsible. This refutes PAP and thereby shows that determinism is compatible with moral responsibility, since responsibility only tracks the actual causal history of a choice and not whether other paths were open. (b) The flicker-of-freedom strategy, due to Fischer, tries to save PAP by pointing out that Jones did have one tiny alternative: he could have failed to produce the prior sign, which would've triggered Black's intervention. So there's technically still a fork in the road, just a thin one. (c) I think the strategy fails, because that flicker -- whether Jones twitches in a way that counts as "the sign" -- isn't itself under Jones's rational control in any way that would let him be responsible for exploiting it, so it can't be the kind of alternative that responsibility is supposed to require."
