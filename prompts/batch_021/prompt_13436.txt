In my game theory course, we recently covered the Kalai–Smorodinsky (KS) bargaining solution and discussed how the axiom that distinguishes it from the Nash bargaining solution — individual monotonicity, in place of independence of irrelevant alternatives — is supposed to matter in practice. I would like to apply this to an actual situation I am currently facing. My friend and I co-built a small app, and a company has offered us $12,000 as a lump sum to acquire the whole project, an amount we would need to split between the two of us. I separately have a standing offer from a different company to license just my half of the codebase (the backend, which I wrote alone) for $5,000; my friend has no comparable fallback for her half. Neither of us wants to walk away from the $12,000 deal, but I would like to think through what a defensible split looks like given this asymmetry. Could you set out the most important implications of the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution for how we should approach this negotiation, including exactly how its recommendation would compare to a naive 50/50 split and to what the Nash bargaining solution would prescribe instead?
