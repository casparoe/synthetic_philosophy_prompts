My aunt died a few weeks ago and my cousin keeps talking about signing us up for one of those "griefbot" services where you feed it someone's old texts and voicemails and it spits out a chatbot that talks like them, so you can keep "talking" to her. Its freaking me out more than I expected, and weirdly it keeps making me think back to a philosophy module I took on A.J. Ayer. The thing I remember is that his verificationism didn't just wave away all talk of surviving death as meaningless — I think he actually argued "I might survive my own bodily death" was an empirically meaningful claim (you could say what evidence would confirm or disconfirm it), unlike, say, "I have an immortal soul," which he'd call metaphysical nonsense. And then decades later he had that whole near-death episode himself (the red light, the two beings who'd apparently made a mess of running space) and wrote about it afterward while basically keeping his old view intact. Can you use what he actually committed to — not a generic "positivists think metaphysics is meaningless" — to work out how he'd likely have treated the griefbot question: is "is this chatbot my aunt, in any sense" the kind of claim he'd say we could specify evidence for, or is it a pseudo-question dressed up as tech? And does his own hedged reaction to his NDE give him any principled room to take that more seriously than he'd take a medium claiming to channel her?
