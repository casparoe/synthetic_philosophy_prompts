Last night a friend of mine and I ended up in a surprisingly heated argument that started from something small. She works as a biostatistician and has chronic migraines, and for her neurologist she keeps a daily log rating the pain from 0 to 10. I asked her, half-joking, how she could be sure that today's "7" really picks out the same amount of pain as the "7" she logged the previous week, given that she has no way to line the two episodes up side by side and compare them directly. She insisted the numbers obviously track something real: she just knows, from the inside, whether a given flare is worse, milder, or about the same as earlier ones, and her doctors have found the resulting trend lines genuinely useful for adjusting her medication.

I pushed further and said that since she is the only one with any access at all to what is being rated, there seems to be no way for anyone, including her a week later, to check whether she is applying the scale consistently rather than just however it happens to feel to apply it in the moment; without any such check, I suggested, it isn't obvious the numbers are tracking anything stable rather than recording whatever impression occurs to her when she writes them down. She thought this was close to absurd, especially given how clinically useful the log has been, and accused me of trying to argue her out of trusting her own experience. Things got even more tangled when I pointed out that her 7/10 for a migraine and my own 7/10 for, say, a bad ankle sprain a few years back might not be tracking anything comparable at all, which she waved off as a separate and much less interesting question than the one we'd started with.

Neither of us landed a decisive point, and I told her I'd think it over and come back with something better argued, so I'd like your help doing that properly. Is there a genuine problem lurking here, distinct from ordinary worries about memory lapses or bias, or was I just being needlessly skeptical of something she has every right to trust? If there is a real difficulty, does it actually show that private ratings like hers are arbitrary, or is there some other way they could be anchored to something stable that neither of us thought of? I'm especially curious whether the fact that she alone has access to the sensation being rated is itself the source of the trouble, or whether that's a red herring and the real issue, if there is one, lies elsewhere.
