So I've been down a panpsychism rabbit hole for the last couple weeks, started with Galen Strawson's stuff on why a physicalist who's serious about matter basically has to become a panpsychist, then some Philip Goff, and now I keep bumping into "the combination problem" everywhere and I think I only half get it. The basic version is clear enough: if quarks or photons or whatever have some tiny bit of experience, you need a story for how a huge pile of those tiny experiences becomes the one unified experience I'm having right now, and apparently William James already pointed out back in the 1890s that you can't just pack a hundred little feelings side by side and expect a new feeling to pop out that's aware of all of them at once.

What I'm actually stuck on is the more in-the-weeds stuff. Goff wants to patch this with a primitive "phenomenal bonding" relation, and I honestly can't tell if that's a real solution or just a fancy label glued onto the exact same mystery. Then the cosmopsychists (Schaffer, Shani, that crowd) flip the whole picture around and start with one giant cosmic subject, asking how it breaks apart into little subjects like you and me instead -- which apparently has it's own mirror-image issue people call the decombination problem. Could you break down, on a pretty intuitive/gut level, what's actually supposed to be doing the explanatory work in each of those two moves, and whether either one genuinely dissolves James's original worry rather than just relocating it somewhere else?
