I'm a PhD student in medieval Jewish philosophy, and there's a tension in Maimonides on sacrifice that I haven't been able to resolve for my own religious practice, not just academically. In the Guide (III:32) he explains the entire sacrificial cult as a concession to the idolatrous worship the Israelites already knew, with prayer as the more advanced mode of service that sacrifice was always meant to lead toward; yet in the Mishneh Torah he codifies the sacrificial laws as still binding and rules, in Hilchot Melachim, that the messianic king will rebuild the Temple and reinstate them. So when I reach the passages in the liturgy that ask God to restore that service, I genuinely don't know what someone who accepts Maimonides' own account of sacrifice's origin is supposed to be hoping for. Please describe the most important implications of Maimonides' accommodation theory of sacrifice for how I should understand those petitions, and for whether continuing to pray for the practice's restoration is even consistent with taking his account of its purpose seriously.
