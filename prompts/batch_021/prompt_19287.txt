Ok this is a pretty niche thing but it's been bugging me for weeks. I work on multi-agent RL at a small startup, and a couple months ago our team lead had us read David Lewis's old book Convention because half the "emergent communication" papers we like cite him for the definition of convention -- basically a coordination equilibrium everyone conforms to because its common knowledge that everyone expects everyone else to conform to it (people always bring up his Paul Revere lantern-signal example). Made sense to me. Then in the reading group one of our researchers, who did a philosophy minor, said that whole common-knowledge apparatus is kind of obsolete now because of Brian Skyrms's work: populations of dumb reinforcement-learning agents, or even just replicator dynamics with no learning at all, reliably converge on exactly the signaling conventions Lewis describes, without any agent ever having anything like common knowledge or being much of a rational "agent" at all. She also mentioned a philosopher named Gilbert who thinks Lewis's whole framework is wrong for a totally separate reason -- something about it missing the "you ought to conform" feeling a real convention has, which she said requires some kind of joint commitment between people rather than just matching expectations.

I tried reading around this a little on my own afterward and just ended up more confused about who's actually right. So here's what I'm hoping you can do: write me an essay that actually takes a side instead of just laying out both views. Does a regularity have to satisfy Lewis's common-knowledge-of-mutual-expectations condition to count as a genuine convention or social norm, or do the evolutionary/learning results show that condition was never really necessary -- just one especially cognitively demanding route to a coordination equilibrium among several? Pull in the Gilbert-style objection about normativity too if you think it actually bears on settling the question. I don't need a hedge-everything conclusion -- something conditional like "necessary only when X, not when Y" is totally fine, I just want you to actually land somewhere instead of giving me a survey.
