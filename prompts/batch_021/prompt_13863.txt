So this happened at our regular Friday poker night and I haven't been able to let it go. My friend Marcus is one of those guys who does all his odds math in his head, fast, no app, no notes. Right after, another regular at the table, Renee, who reads a ton of psych/stats stuff for her job, says: "hold on, you told us an hour ago you've been up since 4am and you're two whiskeys in -- there's real research that people in that exact state screw up quick math like this constantly, and it feels just as certain to them as when they're dead right. From the inside you literally can't tell which one you are right now." Marcus stops, redoes the whole calculation slowly out loud, and gets 70% again. He says he checked it and its still 70%, and that stat about tired or tipsy people doesn't change one single fact about these actual cards, so he should believe exactly what he believed before. Renee says that's not good enough -- the whole reason her stat is scary is that it's about people redoing their own math and still feeling totally sure, so his recheck can't prove he's fine, since the recheck was done by the same possibly-compromised brain. Marcus fires back that this is a trap with no exit: if a recheck never counts for anything, then literally nothing he does can ever bring his confidence back up, which seems nuts. Renee backs off slightly and says okay, maybe it's not "nothing," but it's definately not "zero adjustment" either -- he should land somewhere below 70%, even without finding an actual error anywhere. Marcus then says something like: "so you want me to sit here believing it's 70%, while ALSO believing I probably shouldn't be that confident it's 70%? Come on, that's just incoherent, nobody can rationally hold both of those at once." Renee says no it isn't, it's just being appropriately humble about your own track record in that state, and that people are in exactly that spot all the time without their heads exploding.
