I'm taking a seminar on social choice theory this term, where we recently covered Harsanyi's aggregation theorem, and I also sit on my university's Graduate Research Council, which runs a small internal grants competition. Modeled on the practice of funders like the Swiss National Science Foundation, the Council resolves near-ties after peer review by lottery rather than asking reviewers to rank proposals more finely than they can reliably do. This cycle, two proposals are tied under our rubric, but one member has invoked Harsanyi's theorem to argue that we should skip the lottery and award the grant outright to Proposal A, since a methodological weakness already flagged in Proposal B's review (not serious enough to fail it) makes A's expected payoff modestly higher. Others say this defeats the point of having the lottery at all: if any detectable asymmetry can override it, tied proposals never really get an equal shot, they just lose to whatever further metric someone happens to find. I need to circulate a memo before Friday's meeting, when this cycle's results are finalized. Please draft it: recommend a specific way to resolve this case, propose a policy for comparable ties going forward, and give a fair hearing to the strongest argument on whichever side you don't end up recommending.
