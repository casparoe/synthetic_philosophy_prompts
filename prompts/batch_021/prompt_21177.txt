My little sister's getting married in a few weeks and roped me into doing the toast, which everyone assumes will be easy since I write novels for a living — except her whole academic career is Wittgenstein's On Certainty and half her philosophy department is flying in for it. Her fiancé's a trauma surgeon who's spent their whole relationship telling her he needs "evidence" before he'll believe anything, and her stock answer is always that some things arent the kind of thing evidence even applies to, they're more like the hinge the door swings on. I want the toast built around that properly, not just "hinge proposition" dropped in as a punchline — something true about committing to someone without proof, but it can't just collapse into "love doesn't need reasons," because that's not actually her view and the philosophers in the room will clock the difference immediately. It also has to work for my gran, who'll be sitting two seats from the head of the department and has never heard of Wittgenstein in her life. Five minutes, and I want at least one real laugh in there.
