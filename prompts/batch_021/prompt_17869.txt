I've been preparing a seminar on the philosophy of mathematics and want to run a worry past you. Gödel's constructible-universe proof and Cohen's 1963 forcing construction jointly establish that the Continuum Hypothesis is independent of ZFC: the axioms settle neither CH nor its negation. This strikes me as more troubling than set theorists usually let on. Surely a genuinely consistent axiomatization of sets ought, in principle, to settle any well-posed question about the entities it postulates; if ZFC cannot even determine the cardinality of the continuum, one of the most basic facts about the sets it purports to describe, that looks like evidence that the axioms are inconsistent and are merely obscuring the contradiction rather than resolving it. Shouldn't persistent independence phenomena of this kind raise our credence that ZFC harbors a hidden inconsistency, rather than being treated as a benign, well-understood feature of the theory?
