I'm about two-thirds through a generation-ship novel and I've hit a scene where I don't just need the right vibe, I need the actual document a character would produce. Setup: my ship is currently 60 years into a 200-year crossing, carrying about 6,000 colonists who live pretty well -- decent private space, real surplus in food and power, enough slack that people can take up hobbies and learn things that aren't strictly useful. The chief engineer has just told the Colony Council that if they eat into the reserve margins that have sat untouched since launch, the ship could support growth up to somewhere around 25,000 colonists by arrival, mostly through higher birth allowances plus reviving a lot more of the frozen embryo bank than originally planned. The catch: everyone's living space, food surplus, and "discretionary" allowance would drop close to the floor the ship's charter certifies as a life worth living -- the charter calls it the Founders' Floor. Nobody would be miserable exactly, just a lot more cramped and threadbare than now.

One councillor is pushing hard for the expansion, using language she clearly picked up from some pop-philosophy book, about how a population that's big enough and just-barely-flourishing-enough beats a small thriving one on the numbers, and that the charter's own "worth living" floor commits the ship to exactly that trade whether the council likes it or not. Another councillor keeps pointing out that most of the additional people in question don't exist yet under any version of the plan, and wouldn't even be the same specific people depending on which version the council picks, so its not really clear anyone is being shortchanged if the council just says no.

My protagonist, Anke, is the elected Continuity Officer -- basically the person whose actual job is to write these calls up properly instead of everyone just going with their gut -- and the council votes at the solstice session in three weeks. Could you draft the memo she'd submit: recommendation up front, reasoning after, like a real internal document rather than an essay? I need it to land on something concrete -- approve the expansion, reject it, or approve some capped or phased version, ideally with an actual number or threshold attached -- and I need it to take the "more lives worth living is just more good" argument seriously rather than wave it off, exactly as seriously as it takes whatever's wrong with treating "worth living" as the only bar that matters. My writing group's note last time was that this needs to read like a smart, well-researched layperson wrote it under deadline, not a philosophy journal article -- but they'll absolutely notice if the reasoning underneath is hollow, so please don't dumb down the substance, just the register.
