I'm a novelist, and for the past year I've been drafting a book that follows a circle of retired classicists, which is how I ended up rereading Cicero's De Senectute more closely than I had since university. That reading has turned out to be useful in an unexpected way: my aunt Eleanor, who taught Roman history for thirty-six years and is about as unsentimental a person as I know, is retiring next month, and her department is holding a dinner for her at which both her colleagues and our family will be present. I've been asked to give the toast, and Eleanor has let it be known, to me privately and I suspect to no one else, that she is dreading retirement in a way that has nothing to do with death and everything to do with the first of the four traditional complaints against old age that Cicero has Cato spend the dialogue rebutting: that it withdraws us from any pursuit that still matters.

The trouble is that Eleanor knows this text better than I ever will, so a toast that simply narrates Cato's answer -- that old age has occupations that are properly its own, that authority and long practice compensate for what strength takes away, that a life should be composed like a play and allowed to end well rather than merely stop -- will land, for her and for at least half the room, as a paraphrase she could have written herself in her sleep. She is also a committed materialist who has told me for years that Cato's closing hope for the soul's immortality is the one place where Cicero's nerve fails him, so the speech cannot lean on that consolation as though she would accept it. What I would like from you is a toast of about four minutes that takes Cato's actual argument against that first complaint -- not just its most quotable moments -- and either presses on some part of it that does not obviously survive the trip from a Roman senator's farm to a university history department, or finds a genuine modern equivalent of the compensations he offers, without pretending Eleanor has a Senate seat waiting for her. I would rather you show your work on the philosophy than smooth it over, since her colleagues will notice either way, while still leaving the family in the room, who will not recognize a word of Latin, feeling they have attended a toast and not a seminar.
