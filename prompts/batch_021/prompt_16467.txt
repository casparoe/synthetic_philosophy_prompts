Ok this is a bit of a weird one but hear me out. I've been re-reading Kierkegaard's "Two Ages" (the bit everyone anthologizes as "The Present Age") and his idea of "the public" has been living in my head rent free -- not the generic "crowds are dumb" take, but the specific claim that the press conjurs a phantom collective out of people who never actually assemble, that levels everyone's passion and commitment down into idle chatter and opinion-having, with nobody ever on the hook for anything becuase "the public" itself has no hands and no history. And obviously his own biography is basically a case study for the theory, since he got turned into a walking punchline by the Corsair.

Everyone and their dog has already written the "twitter mob cancels a guy" story though, and that's exactly what I don't want. Can you write me a short story (~1000-1500 words) that's actually built out of Kierkegaard's specific mechanics here -- the leveling, the anonymity, the diffusion of responsibility onto no one in particular, reflection substituting for action -- but set it somewhere that isn't obviously 2020s social media. Historical, far-future, nonhuman narrator, whatever, I just don't want a thinly-veiled Twitter allegory. Extra credit if the ending lands on something Kierkegaard himself would sign off on, since his answer to all this wasn't "fix the public," it was "become a real individual."
