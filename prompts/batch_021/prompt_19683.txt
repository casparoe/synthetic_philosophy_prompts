My teacher of twelve years — a hospice physician who also ran the small Madhyamaka study group I still attend on Sundays — died last month. I write about science and ideas for a living, which is presumably why her most senior students asked me, rather than one of them, to give the eulogy at a memorial that will mix serious students of emptiness with hospital colleagues and secular relatives who have never heard of the two truths. She was explicit, in her last years, that Nāgārjuna's tetralemma — the refusal to say a thing exists, does not exist, both, or neither — applies with full force to what happens to a person at death, and that she wanted no one saying she was "at peace now," and no one saying, either, that she was "simply gone." Please write the eulogy: it has to comfort people with no philosophical background at all without asserting any of the four positions her own philosophy spent a lifetime dismantling.
