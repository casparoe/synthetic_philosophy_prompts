Had a long back and forth with my friend last night about Hofstadter's "superrationality" thing—the idea that in a one shot prisoner's dilemma against someone who reasons exactly like you, you should cooperate, since by symmetry whatever logic makes you defect makes them defect too, so you might as well both land on the better outcome. My friend kept insisting this is basically just Kant's categorical imperative wearing a game theory costume, but I think theres a real difference: the superrational move still seems to bottom out in "this gets me a better payoff," which sounds more like a hypothetical imperative than a categorical one to me. If Kant somehow got briefed on Newcomb-style decision theory, do you think he'd actually endorse superrational cooperation as a genuine instance of the categorical imperative at work, or would he say it's not the real thing at all, just self interest in a universalizability costume? Where does the analogy actually hold up and where does it fall apart?
