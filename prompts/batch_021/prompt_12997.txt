I'm putting together this week's session for my grad seminar on Sidgwick's *Methods of Ethics*, and I went down a weird rabbit hole last night that I can't quite shake. One of my students, who's way more into AI stuff than I am, kept bringing up "coherent extrapolated volition" (CEV) — Yudkowsky's old proposal that instead of pointing an AI at humanity's actual, present-day preferences, you should point it at whatever our preferences would converge to if we knew more, thought faster and more clearly, were more the people we each wish we were, and had had more time to reflect together. It hit me that this is basically dressing up, in engineering language, the exact same move Sidgwick makes when he tries to get from individual "considered judgments" up to the "point of view of the universe" — except Sidgwick thought he needed a handful of independently self-evident axioms (prudence, justice, rational benevolence) to license that move, wheras CEV seems to just trust that extrapolation plus coherence-seeking will do the work on its own, with no independent axiomatic backing at all.

Obviously Sidgwick never had to think about AI (he died in 1900, CEV wasn't formulated til 2004), but he thought unusually hard, for his era, about exactly the kind of idealization-of-preferences move CEV relies on, and about whether such moves can actually bridge the gap he's most famous for never closing: the dualism of practical reason between egoism and universal benevolence. So here's what I want your take on: if you explained CEV to Sidgwick, do you think he'd see it as a live candidate for finally resolving (or at least dissolving) that dualism, on the grounds that it operationalizes impartial extrapolation at civilizational scale? Or would his own doubts about whether such convergence is even possible without some theistic guarantee, combined with his insistence on independently self-evident starting points, make him pretty scathing about the whole project? Feel free to get properly into the weeds here — this is for my own prep/amusement rather than for the students directly, so I'm after your best reconstruction of how his actual arguments bear on this, not a diplomatic both-sides answer.
