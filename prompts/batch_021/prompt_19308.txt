Ok so this is a bit of a rabbit hole I fell into after an argument with a coworker about, of all things, a system design decision, where someone jokingly brought up "Aumann's agreement theorem" to say that if we were both actually rational we couldn't still disagree after fully hearing each other out. I looked it up and got the standard gist: two Bayesians who share the same prior can't have it be common knowledge that their posteriors on some event differ -- if you truly know that I know that you know (and so on) what my probability estimate is, and vice versa, we must already agree. Fine, that part felt almost tautological once I sat with it long enough. But then I found a follow-up result, I think by Geanakoplos and Polemarchakis, that goes further: if two people just take turns bluntly stating their current probability estimate out loud, over and over, with zero justification or evidence shared, they're guarenteed to converge to the exact same number after some finite number of rounds. That's the part that actually broke my brain a little, because naively I'd expect two people with totally different private info to either talk past each other forever, or maybe drift around, but not actually lock onto the same value just from hearing a sequence of bare numbers.

Can you walk me through the actual mechanics of why that iterated-announcement process has to terminate in agreement? My hazy intuition is that it has something to do with each announcement implicitly leaking information that refines what the other person's number could've meant, kind of like a consensus protocol where each round shrinks the space of remaining possibilities until you hit a fixed point -- but I don't have a rigorous handle on why that refining process can't just stall out short of full agreement, or cycle forever instead. And if theres room, I'm also curious what's being quietly assumed here that makes it fall apart for actual humans arguing online -- is it mainly the shared-prior assumption doing the heavy lifting, or is the "perfectly honest, single well-defined number, no incentive to hedge or bluff" part just as load-bearing and easy to gloss over?
