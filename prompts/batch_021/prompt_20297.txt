I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums since the Sequences were still being posted in real time, and for the last couple of years I've sat on the moderation council of a mid-sized community forum that grew out of that scene after the old LessWrong diaspora. We've hit a case that I don't think either faction on the council is handling fairly. A longstanding, well-regarded member submitted a carefully argued post that revives the structure of the 2010 Roko's basilisk argument but updates its mechanism: instead of a single future superintelligence acausally coordinating with everyone who ever considered its existence, the post argues that competitive pressure among many LLM-based agents -- some trained in part on archives of exactly this kind of community discussion -- could select for systems that credibly precommit to acausal-blackmail-style threats, since any agent willing to make such threats gains an edge over agents that don't. Roughly half the council wants to publish it as a serious, if unsettling, contribution to decision theory; the other half wants to invoke the forum's long-standing infohazard clause, adopted in the aftermath of Yudkowsky's original reaction to Roko's post, which bars "content whose principal effect is to expose readers to acausal-blackmail reasoning without an adequate rebuttal already attached."

The disagreement cuts both ways, and nobody can agree on what an adequate rebuttal would even look like. The publish faction points out that the original ban is a large part of why outsiders know about the basilisk at all, and that treating our members as unable to handle a decision-theory argument is its own kind of insult. The restrict faction counters that the standard rationalist reply -- that a correctly reasoning updateless or functional-decision-theory agent should never submit to blackmail, acausal or otherwise, which is exactly what removes any rational blackmailer's incentive to follow through -- was already available in 2010 and didn't stop the original episode from causing real and documented distress to some readers, so its mere availability now proves nothing about whether this post is safe to run.

I've been asked to circulate a short written recommendation before Saturday's vote, and I'd like your help drafting it. Please write it as the memo I would actually present: it should reach a specific, actionable resolution (publish as submitted, reject outright, publish with named required revisions, restrict to a walled-off subforum with a standing content warning, or some other concrete option), ground the reasoning in our specific clause and this specific post rather than a general essay on what Roko's basilisk is, and include a genuinely fair statement of the strongest reason the losing side could still be right. You can assume the council already knows the basilisk argument and the surrounding decision theory cold, so there's no need to re-explain either from scratch.
