So last night my friend Dana and I got into a whole thing about Frank Ramsey -- yeah, the Cambridge guy who did the Ramsey pricing and Ramsey theory stuff and died crazy young, like 26. Dana had just been reading that talk he apparently gave to the Apostles near the end of his life (the "Epilogue" essay) where his answer to feeling small next to the universe is basically: draw the world in perspective, not to scale. So the stars end up about the size of threepenny bits, and the eventual heat death of everything barely registers because, discounted the way you'd discount any far off cost, its present value right now is basically nothing. His whole point being that size and vastness are only impressive if you've already decided to care about size, and stars can't think or love, so why should their bigness make human stuff feel small in comparison? Dana thinks this is obviously just cope -- a smart guy managing his own mortality with a neat rhetorical trick, extra sad/ironic since he actually died only a few years after saying it. I said no, I think he's onto something real, that meaning genuinely doesn't scale with the size of the container its happening in. We argued about it for like two hours and didnt really get anywhere.

Now I kind of want to see the idea worked out in a story instead of us just talking past each other again. Could you write me a short story that dramatizes this exact bind: someone who gets forced, vividly and inescapably, into confronting the "true" cosmic-scale picture of things -- whether that's literal (they somehow actually perceive the real scale of the universe or of time) or more personal (something like a terminal diagnosis, where the far future just stops being relevant to them the way it used to be) -- and who has to work out, from inside their own situation, whether Ramsey's "perspective, not to scale" move genuinely holds up once you can't just choose to stop looking at the real scale of things, or whether it quietly falls apart under the weight of it. I don't want a tidy moral either way at the end -- I'd rather it just sit in the tension and let the character's specific circumstances do the arguing for them.
