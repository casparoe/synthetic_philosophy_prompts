My sister and I jointly inherited a small apartment building, and for the past year we have debated whether to sell it or continue holding it for another decade; we now have a concrete offer of $2 million on the table and need to decide soon. We have exchanged every piece of relevant information, such as rent rolls, repair histories, and comparable sales, and gone over our reasoning with each other in detail more than once, so neither of us is withholding some fact the other lacks, nor is either of us noticeably less careful with numbers than the other. Yet she still puts the odds that holding on will leave us better off ten years from now at around 80 percent, while I put it closer to 30 percent, and neither estimate has moved in months of conversation. This troubles me: if we have genuinely seen the same evidence and both simply want to arrive at the correct answer, shouldn't a disagreement like this either dissolve or at least narrow the more we talk about it? Is it actually possible for two reasonable people to examine identical information and remain stuck at different probabilities indefinitely, or does the fact that we are still stuck mean that one of us must be reasoning badly somewhere?
