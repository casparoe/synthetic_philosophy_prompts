I've been sketching a dissertation chapter on the moral fetishism debate and want to stress-test its central move before I build the chapter around it. Michael Smith's (1994) fetishism objection charges Kantian accounts of moral worth with requiring an agent to be motivated by a de dicto concern for rightness, rather than by the substantive features that make an action right. Julia Markovits's (2010) Coincident Reasons Thesis tries to dissolve the objection by requiring only that an agent's motivating reasons coincide with the reasons that actually justify the action, without requiring any belief about the action's rightness. My worry is that this concedes too much: the thesis cannot distinguish a genuinely virtuous agent from one whose motivating reasons coincide with the right-making reasons merely because of a reliable but non-moral disposition—a "wired-in" tendency to act for the relevant de re reason, absent any real moral concern. Please argue that this indistinguishability is a decisive problem for the Coincident Reasons Thesis, that Paulina Sliwa's (2016) knowledge-based amendment does not resolve it, and that only an account like Nomy Arpaly's (2003), which builds a substantive responsiveness to right-making features into the analysis of moral worth itself, can mark the relevant distinction. Please steel-man Markovits's and Sliwa's positions rather than reducing them to strawmen.
