Deflationists about truth (disquotationalists, minimalists) hold that the truth predicate does no substantive explanatory work: its whole point is to serve as a device for expressing generalizations, via instances of the schema "P" is true if and only if P. Against this, philosophers of logic have raised a conservativeness argument: if a truth predicate governed by such biconditionals is added to Peano Arithmetic, and the arithmetic's induction schema is allowed to range over formulas that contain that predicate, the resulting theory is no longer a conservative extension of arithmetic — it proves new arithmetical theorems, including principles equivalent to the consistency of arithmetic, which Gödel's second incompleteness theorem says arithmetic cannot prove about itself. Explain why this non-conservativeness result is thought to threaten deflationism's claim that truth is metaphysically and explanatorily "thin," and discuss whether restricting induction, or some other maneuver, gives the deflationist a principled way out.
