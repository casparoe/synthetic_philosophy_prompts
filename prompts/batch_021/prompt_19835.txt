and here's Priya's answer: "Anscombe's basic claim is that murder just means intentionally killing innocent people, and that's what the bombs did on a massive scale, since they were dropped on cities full of civilians specifically to break Japan's will to keep fighting, not on military targets. So even though Truman's defenders said the bombs were necessary to end the war fast and avoid a bloodier invasion, Anscombe doesn't accept that this changes anything, because you can't make an act that's intrinsically wicked okay just by pointing to good things that came out of it afterward — the good consequences don't retroactively clean the act. This is basically the same logic as pacifism: since war always involves killing, and Anscombe thinks killing is absolutely forbidden no matter the outcome, her objection to Truman is really just a specific case of the pacifist idea that violence can never be justified by its results. This pamphlet is also where Anscombe first coined the term 'consequentialism,' which she used to attack the idea that we should judge actions purely by outcomes rather than by intentions or by moral rules about what you're never allowed to do. So the Truman pamphlet and her critique of consequentialism are basically the same argument: outcome-based ethics lets people excuse anything, including atomic bombs, as long as the numbers work out, and she thinks that's a corrupt way to reason."
