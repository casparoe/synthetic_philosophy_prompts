here's the question exactly as Austin gave it: "State Zorn's Lemma. Then use it to prove that every proper ideal I of a commutative ring R with 1 is contained in a maximal ideal of R."

and here's Malik's answer, copied word for word:

"Zorn's Lemma: if P is a nonempty poset such that every subset of P has an upper bound in P, then P has a maximal element.

Let I be a proper ideal of R. Let S = {J : J is a proper ideal of R and I ⊆ J}, ordered by inclusion. S is nonempty since I ∈ S.

Let C be a chain in S, and let K be the union of all J in C. I claim K is in S.

First, K is an ideal: if a, b ∈ K, then a is in some J1 in C and b is in some J2 in C. Since C is a chain, J1 and J2 are comparable, so say J1 ⊆ J2; then a, b ∈ J2, so a − b ∈ J2 ⊆ K. Also, if r ∈ R and a ∈ K, then a ∈ J for some J ∈ C, and ra ∈ J ⊆ K since J is an ideal. So K is an ideal.

Clearly I ⊆ K, since I ⊆ J for every J ∈ C (every J in S contains I).

K is proper: since C is a chain of proper ideals increasing under inclusion, their union can't suddenly become all of R, so K ≠ R.

So K ∈ S and K is an upper bound for C. Since every chain in S has an upper bound in S, Zorn's Lemma applies and gives a maximal element M of S.

Now I claim M is a maximal ideal of R. Suppose it isn't: then there's an ideal N with M ⊊ N ⊊ R. Since M ⊆ N we get I ⊆ N, and N ≠ R means N is proper, so N ∈ S. But N properly contains M, which contradicts M being maximal in S. So M is a maximal ideal of R containing I."
