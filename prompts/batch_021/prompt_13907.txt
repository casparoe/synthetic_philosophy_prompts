I sit on the epistemics working group at the research nonprofit where I work, which runs an internal play-money prediction market that leadership consults before greenlighting projects. Volume is chronically thin and follows the same pattern every time: prices sit frozen for weeks, then jump only days before resolution once a few clearly-informed staff start trading and everyone else stops quoting against them -- the no-trade theorem playing out in miniature, since uninformed traders correctly infer that anyone willing to take the other side of a stale price knows something they don't.

The group is split three ways: install a subsidized automated market maker (an LMSR, following Hanson) that always offers a counterparty, treating its expected losses as the price of earlier information; scrap the market for a Metaculus-style tournament scored against eventual outcomes, since proper scoring rules sidestep adverse selection entirely; or keep the status quo and just teach leadership to discount early prices.

Please draft the memo I'll bring to our decision meeting. It should recommend one option, or a specific hybrid, with a rough implementation plan, and it should take seriously the strongest argument against your recommendation -- especially the worry that a subsidized market maker doesn't resolve the no-trade problem so much as pay to route around it, with the subsidy flowing mainly to whoever already holds the best information.
