I'm a novelist, which is presumably why my father — a retired philosopher who spent his career on Descartes — has deputized me to write the eulogy for the small service we're holding this weekend for our dog, Juniper, who died last Sunday after fourteen years with the family. He still defends the "beast-machine" thesis with total sincerity, and his one condition is that I not attribute to Juniper anything that he, or in his view Descartes himself, would have to reject as false. The complication is that the real audience is his grandchildren, eight and eleven, who are inconsolable and need comforting, not a lecture on Cartesian dualism. Please write the eulogy: gentle enough to genuinely help two grieving children, precise enough about Descartes' actual view of animal minds — including the more careful position he stakes out in his letters, where he seems to grant animals something like sensation while still denying them thought and an immortal soul — that my father won't wince, and visibly shaped by that tension rather than resolved by quietly picking a side.
