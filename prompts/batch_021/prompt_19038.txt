So I've been reporting a piece on autonomous weapons systems -- a bit outside my usual science-and-ideas beat, but the ethics angle pulled me in -- and I sat in on a panel at a tech-ethics conference where two of the panelists ended up going back and forth for a good ten minutes on what one of them called the "responsibility gap." Im still turning it over in my head. The first panelist, a philosopher, argued that once a weapon is genuinely selecting and engaging targets on its own via machine learning, nobody can be fairly blamed if it kills the wrong person: not the programmers, who couldn't have predicted that specific decision, not the commanding officer, who didn't make the actual call, and not the system itself, which isn't a moral agent that can be punished or feel remorse. Since the laws of war require that someone be accountable for a killing, she said, that gap alone makes deploying these systems impermissible until it's somehow closed.

The second panelist, a JAG lawyer, shot back that this is overblown -- the law assigns consequences without any single culpable individual all the time (strict liability for defective products, vicarious liability for employers, command-responsibility doctrines), so there's no reason autonomous weapons need some special extra ingredient that other risky systems don't. The philosopher answered that he was eliding two different things -- who ends up legally on the hook, versus who actually deserves blame -- and that the second is a distinct "retribution gap" that liability schemes don't even try to close, which matters in a domain like war where holding someone responsible is suppose to be part of the point, not just an incidental side effect. He came back by saying retribution is a shaky thing to hang an argument on in the first place -- plenty of legal philosophy is skeptical that punishment needs to satisfy anyone's retributive urges at all -- so a "gap" in something we may not even owe victims isn't obviously a real problem. Neither gave ground, and honestly I couldn't tell who was landing more punches by the end. Could you go through this move by move and say who's got the better of each exchange -- not just an overall winner -- and flag anything you think both of them might be missing?
