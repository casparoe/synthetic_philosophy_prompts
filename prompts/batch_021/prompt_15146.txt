A good friend of mine, Alan, died three weeks ago. We were PhD students together in the earth sciences department back in the late 80s -- I ended up in geophysics, he went into atmospheric science and became one of those climate scientists who's genuinely famous, quoted in documentaries, testified in front of Congress twice. For the last fifteen years or so of his career Alan was merciless in public about other people's carbon footprints -- oil executives, obviously, but also his own grad students who flew home for Christmas, and once a senator who has a heated pool. Problem is, Alan flew business class to four continents a year for conferences, kept a big drafty house, and there's an old blog post (still findable if you search his name) that did the math on his personal footprint and more or less called him a fraud. His answer, when pushed on it, was always some version of "someone has to say the unfashionable true thing, even an imperfect messenger."

His widow asked me to give the eulogy at the memorial this coming saturday, and I've been losing sleep over it. Half the room will be his climate science colleagues, several of whom are literally people he tore into in op-eds, and they'll all be thinking about the hypocrisy thing whether or not anyone says the word out loud. The other half is family who just want to hear that he was a good, warm man, which he also definately was. Since retiring I've gotten pretty into moral philosophy as a way to fill the days, and I've been reading around this "standing to blame" debate -- whether being a hypocrite actually undercuts your authority to morally criticize someone else, or whether that's a bit of a red herring and the criticism can be true and important regardless of who's making it. I don't want a eulogy that quietly ignores the hypocrisy angle, because it'll read as dishonest to the people who know, but I also don't want it turning into a philosophy seminar over a casket. Could you write the eulogy for me? I want that standing-to-blame question to actually do some work in it -- shaping what I'm allowed to honestly claim about him and what I'm not -- rather than just being a topic I gesture at.
