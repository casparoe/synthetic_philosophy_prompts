A friend and I spent the better part of last night arguing about the original position and left it unresolved. Her objection to Rawls followed Harsanyi's line: if the parties behind the veil of ignorance truly don't know which position they will occupy, the rational response is to treat every identity as equally likely and maximize expected utility -- which lands you at average utilitarianism, not the difference principle. I pushed back with Rawls's own reasons for blocking that move: the veil produces genuine uncertainty rather than calculable risk, so there is no principled basis for the equiprobability assumption; the choice concerns the whole shape of a life rather than a repeatable gamble where bad draws average out; and the parties need principles they could actually live with once the veil lifts, which is the point of his "strains of commitment" argument against risking the bottom of a utilitarian distribution. Neither of us landed a decisive blow. Write a dialogue between a committed Rawlsian and a committed Harsanyian taking up exactly this disagreement. Both should be sharp, sympathetic defenders of their respective views -- no strawmanning -- and each should press the other on the weakest point in their reasoning rather than simply restating their own position.
