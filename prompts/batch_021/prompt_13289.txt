I run a small philosophy podcast and next week's guest defends what's usually called a "Wittgensteinian" line on religious belief -- basically the view, going back to Wittgenstein's 1938 lectures on religious belief, that something like belief in the Last Judgement isn't a dodgy empirical hypothesis held on thin evidence, but more like a picture that regulates someone's whole life, so that the believer and the atheist aren't just asserting and denying the same proposition the way two people arguing about a historical fact would be. She's written a fair bit in the D.Z. Phillips/Swansea-school tradition and is very good at reframing objections as category mistakes -- e.g. "your treating grammar as if it were a hypothesis" -- so I'm worried the episode turns into 45 minutes of her saying "meaning is use" while every question just bounces off her.

Can you draft me 9 or 10 interview questions that don't let that happen? I want at least one that goes right at the Kai Nielsen "fideism" charge -- that carving religion out as its own language-game with its own internal standards just makes it immune to criticism by definitional fiat, which looks less like philosophy of religion and more like a permanent exemption. I want one that presses on whether "regulates a whole life" is doing any real work that "deeply held and unfalsifiable" wasn't already doing by itself. I want one on ordinary believers -- most people in the pews clearly think the resurrection literally happened and that prayer literally changes things, i.e. they take themselves to be making historical and causal claims, not just avowing a picture -- so is her account describing what believers believe, or quietly correcting it while insisting philosophy "leaves everything as it is"? And I want one exegetical question about whether she's defending what's actually in Wittgenstein's notes, which he never wrote up himself and which we only have secondhand through students' transcriptions, versus the much more systematic position Phillips later built on top of them. For each question, throw in a short follow-up tuned to whatever dodge she's most likely to reach for.
