So I'm teaching a grad seminar on philosophy of art this semester and we finally got around to Tolstoy's What is Art?, which I'll admit I'd never actually taught before, only read it once years ago as a student. We spent most of the session on the "infection" definition that everyone quotes, but the bit that's actually been nagging at me since is a passage later in the book where Tolstoy tries to explain what makes one work MORE infectious than another, rather than just infectious or not.

In the Maude translation it goes something like this (I'm reconstructing partly from memory and my notes, so the wording might be a touch off, but this is the gist): "The degree of infectiousness of art depends on three conditions: (1) the greater or lesser individuality of the feeling transmitted, (2) the greater or lesser clearness with which it is transmitted, and (3) the sincerity of the artist, that is, the greater or lesser force with which the artist himself feels the emotion he transmits ... Of these three conditions the last is the most important, for if the artist does not himself feel what he wants to express, but merely simulates the feeling, no amount of skill will make the work infect others."

Heres my problem with it: the whole first half of the book is Tolstoy tearing down "beauty" and "taste" as criteria for art precisely because they're hopelessly subjective and don't give you a real test, and infection is supposed to be his objective replacement for all that. But then condition (3) turns around and makes the strength of the infection depend on a fact about the artist's private inner state at the moment of creation, which none of us can check directly, we only ever infer the sincerity FROM the fact that we felt infected in the first place. That looks circular to me, or at least it makes "sincerity" do a lot of unearned work while pretending to be a hard-nosed, non-subjective criterion. Is there something elsewhere in the book, or in how Tolstoy uses "sincerity," that actually gets him out of this problem, or is this basically a real soft spot in the argument that people who work on him have flagged before? This isn't really my period so I'd like a second opinion before I say anything too definitive to my students next week.
