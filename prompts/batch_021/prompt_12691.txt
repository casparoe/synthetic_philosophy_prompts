1. the "almost-sure long-run dominance" argument: given a long enough horizon, a kelly bettor's wealth beats that of any other fixed betting strategy with probability approaching 1.
2. the ergodicity argument: ordinary expected-value math secretly averages over a bunch of parallel copies of you that dont exist, but a real person only lives through one time series, and for compounding bets the time-average growth rate and that ensemble average pull apart — so the geometric growth rate is the only thing actually meaningful to optimize for.
3. the "it's just log utility" critique: kelly betting is simply what falls out of expected utility theory when your utility happens to be log(wealth) — treating it as more "rational" than other utility functions is smuggling in an assumption, not deriving one.
4. the bargaining-style "geometric rationality" framing some rationalist-adjacent people use: bet sizing gets treated like a bargain between your possible future selves across different timelines, so the geometric mean pops out as a compromise solution rather than as anyone's arbitrary utility function.
5. the fractional-kelly argument: basically no serious practitioner actually bets full kelly, since the drawdowns are brutal even when your edge estimate is correct, which suggests full kelly isnt the right target even by its own logic.
6. the "only works for casino games" argument: the whole framework quietly assumes repeated bets with known, stable odds, and its pretty shaky once you're talking about one-off real world decisions with uncertain probabilities.
