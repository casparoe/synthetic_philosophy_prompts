This past summer I took part in a student AI-safety reading group, and we spent several sessions on MIRI's "Embedded Agency" sequence by Scott Garrabrant and Abram Demski. One point from it has stuck with me ever since. Classical models of rational agents, such as AIXI, let the agent maintain a hypothesis space over every possible computable environment, secure in the knowledge that the true environment is somewhere within it. Once an agent is embedded in the very environment it is trying to model, though, this guarantee breaks down -- what the sequence calls the grain of truth problem. Could you give me an intuitive, gut-level explanation of why an embedded agent cannot, in general, hold an exactly accurate hypothesis about an environment that is at least as complex as the agent itself, and why this is a problem of principle rather than merely a practical limitation on memory or computing power?
