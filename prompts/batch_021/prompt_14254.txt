So this is a bit random but I had my appendix out two weeks ago (emergency appendectomy, would not recommend) and while I was couch-bound recovering I fell down a rabbit hole reading about whether the thing was even doing anything before it tried to kill me. Half of what I read still calls it a useless vestigal organ and the other half brings up this theory that it's actually a "safe house" where good gut bacteria hide out so they can recolonize your intestines after you get sick. Somewhere in there I stumbled on a whole philosophy of biology debate about what "function" even means that I had no idea existed - there's "etiological" accounts (a trait's function is whatever it was historically selected to do) and "causal-role" accounts (a trait's function is just whatever it currently contributes to some capacity of the organism, no evolutionary history required). Can you pick one of those two views and argue for it as hard as you can, using my poor appendix as the running example? Don't just assert your side though - if you go etiological you have to deal with the swampman-style worry (a molecule-for-molecule duplicate animal that pops into existence by freak accident has organs with zero selection history, so does it's heart really have no function?), and if you go causal-role you have to explain what stops literally anything from counting as a function, like the heart's "function" of making a noise a doctor can listen to.
