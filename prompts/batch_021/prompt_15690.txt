Ok so I fell down a rabbit hole this week that started with an old LW/ACX comment thread about whether instrumental rationality can be the whole story on "shoulds," and it eventually landed me on actually reading Bernard Williams' "Internal and External Reasons" essay -- the one with the example of the guy who thinks he's got a gin and tonic in front of him but its actually petrol, and the bit about the man who's just straightforwardly cruel to his wife. Williams' internalism (a reason-claim is only true of you if you could reach it via some sound deliberative route starting from stuff already in your actual motivational set, what he calls S, otherwise "you have a reason to X" isn't really saying anything) fits really well with the "rationality is just about winning, not discovering capital-M Morality" vibe I got from the sequences. But then I read Korsgaard's "Skepticism about Practical Reason" and some McDowell on the same debate, and they're not strawmen at all -- Korsgaard's charge that pure instrumentalism can't even explain why we're allowed to criticize someone's desires as irrational rather than just describing them seems to land some real punches.

Could you write me a dialogue between an internalist about reasons (running something close to Williams' actual view) and an externalist (running something like Korsgaard's or McDowell's line) hashing this out over a concrete case -- the cruel husband, say, or if that one feels stale, someone who's just totally indifferent to their future self's welfare? I want real back and forth, not a strawman fight: the externalist needs a genuine answer to the "sound deliberation from S" move, and the internalist needs a genuine comeback to the "you can't ground the normativity of instrumental reason itself" charge, with each of them actually responding to what the other one just said instead of restating their own view past each other. If there's a real crux underneath the terminology -- as opposed to the two of them just being stipulatively committed to different definitions of "reason" -- I'd like it to come into focus by the end, even if nobody fully concedes.
