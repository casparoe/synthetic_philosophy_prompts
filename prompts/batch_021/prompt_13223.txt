Heads, Omega gives you $10,000, but only if it predicted in advance you'd hand over $100 in the case the coin had come up tails instead. Tails, Omega just shows up and asks for the $100 -- no upside to you at all in this branch, purely a test of what you would've done.

Dana said obviously you pay: that's what updateless decision theory tells you to do, and refusing is the same mistake as two-boxing on Newcomb's problem. Dana's comeback was dynamic consistency: an agent that wants to precommit but then reneges once the moment actually arrives is exploitable, so a reflectively stable agent just pays, full stop. Marcus's reply was that "ex ante optimal" only means something relative to a chosen prior moment, and nothing forces you to anchor that moment "before the coin flip" rather than "right after Omega tells you the result" -- at which point the pay-up policy is obviously worse. He said Dana was just picking whichever vantage point hands him the answer he already wanted.
