My uncle Ron passed away suddenly two weeks ago, heart attack, he was 68, totally out of nowhere, and I got asked to give the eulogy this Saturday, which is terrifying because he basically raised me for a couple years when my parents were splitting up and I really don't want to screw this up. Here's the thing that's been stuck in my head since he died: I do data/ML-adjacent work at a logistics company, and last year I taught myself the basics of reinforcement learning for a work project, and went down a rabbit hole on this corner of it called inverse reinforcement learning, where instead of handing an agent a reward function and watching what it does, you watch what an agent does and try to work backward to a reward function that would explain it. Ron was the least introspective man alive, he never once explained why he did anything, he just did it, drove three hours in a snowstorm to fix his ex-wife's furnace, gave his good coat away to some guy outside the gas station, skipped his own retirement party to sit with a neighbor's kid in the ER, and now everyone's sitting around trying to figure out "what Ron valued" purely off the pattern of what he did, which is basically the exact problem IRL is built to solve, just messier and with way higher stakes.

What's actually bugging me, and what I want somewhere in the eulogy, is that IRL doesn't spit out a clean answer even in principle. Tons of different reward functions can explain the exact same behavior, there's this almost trick-question example where "he valued literally nothing" technically fits too, since if nothing has value then nothing is better than anything else and every action counts as fine, so you only get a sane-looking answer once you add in some assumption that he was mostly, not perfectly, doing what he cared about, and even then it depends entirely on which "features" of a situation you decided counted in the first place. That last part is exactly what's going to happen in that room on Saturday: his fishing buddies, my aunts, his old coworkers are all going to walk away with quietly different stories about who Ron "really" was, built from whichever slice of him they personally watched, and some of those stories are going to be more flattering to the teller than to Ron. A couple guys from my work are coming and will clock exactly what I'm doing if I lean on this too hard, but everybody else should be able to feel what I mean without ever hearing the words "reward function." Can you write me the eulogy, about five minutes, comforting, not a lecture, that actually uses this idea to hold the uncertainty about who Ron really was instead of pretending we all definately know, while still landing as love and not as a computer science seminar?
