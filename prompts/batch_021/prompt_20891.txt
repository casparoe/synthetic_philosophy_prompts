So a bit of random context: my dad had a bad health scare earlier this year, and while we were filling out his POLST form in the ICU waiting room I realized none of us actually understood half the boxes, we just kind of picked whatever the nurse seemed to expect us to pick. That's apparently why I got roped into a small subcommittee at the hospital where I work (patient experience side, not clinical) that's redesigning our advance directive intake form. Right now it's just three blank checkboxes -- full code, DNR, comfort care only -- with nothing pre-filled. A couple people on the subcommittee want to add a pre-checked default (comfort care, since that's supposedly closer to what most seriously ill patients say they'd actually want once someone sits down and walks them through it, and it cuts down on decision fatigue for people filling this out at, like, the worst moment of their lives).

Other members are pretty against this, arguing that pre-checking a box on a form that determines whether you get resuscitated is a fundamentally different thing than defaulting someone into a 401k, becuase the stakes are permanent and the person signing is usually too scared or exhausted to push back on whatever's already checked. Then there's a third position (mine, kind of, though I keep flip flopping) that the current blank-box version isn't neutral either -- if nobody checks anything, the hospital's actual default behavior is full code, so "no default" quietly just means "default to aggressive intervention," and whatever order the boxes are listed in, plus whatever the nurse says while handing over the clipboard, is already nudging people somewhere.

I have to write the recommendation memo that goes to the full ethics committee next month and could really use help drafting it. Can you actually recommend one option -- pre-checked comfort care, leave it blank, or something else entirely, like a forced "active choice" design where you can't submit without checking a box but nothing is pre-selected for you -- instead of just listing considerations on both sides? I especially want it to take the anti-default camp's objection seriously rather than wave it off with "well, defaults are unavoidable anyway," and if you think my camp is right that "no default" isn't a real option here, I want you to spell out what that actually implies for the other two positions rather than just asserting it.
