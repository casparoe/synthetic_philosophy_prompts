Been prepping a talk on the hole argument for my philosophy of science grad seminar next week, and it's kind of messing with my head. Take any solution to Einstein's field equations and smear it with a diffeomorphism that's the identity everywhere except inside some little vacuum region H, and you get a second solution that agrees with the first on literally everything outside H but disagrees on what happens inside. Doesnt that just mean general relativity is a genuinely, physically indeterministic theory -- that there's an actual gap where the theory can't predict what happens in an empty patch of spacetime even if you're given absolutely everything else in the universe? I don't get why Earman and Norton treat this as a cute puzzle for substantivalists instead of just admitting GR has a hole (pun intended) in its predictive power. Shouldn't physicists be a lot more worried about this than they seem to be?
