So I'm about a third of the way into a novel and I keep putting off a scene I actually need to write. My main character, Ilsa Bruch, is deputy leader of a mid-sized party in a fictional multi-party parliament (think continental European coalition politics, not the US two-party set-up), and for the past year she's been quietly reading Simone Weil -- specifically that short, strange essay Weil wrote near the very end of her life, usually translated as "On the Abolition of All Political Parties," basically a memo she sent to de Gaulle's Free French people in London. The gist is that a party, structurally, is a machine for churning out collective passion, built to lean on every member's mind until it falls in line, and its real unstated goal, underneath whatever it says about itself, is just to keep growing forever -- so the only honest fix is to get rid of parties altogether. Ilsa's become genuinely convinced by it. So at the big televised party congress, instead of the loyalty speech everyone in the hall is expecting from her, she walks up and announces she's resigning from the party on the spot and calling on it to dissolve itself.

I need you to actually write that speech -- the full text, not a pitch for what it should cover. Two things make it hard. First, the room has two audiences stacked on top of each other: a few thousand lifelong party members in the hall who'll feel genuinely betrayed -- including a handful of sharp policy-wonk types who've clearly read the same Weil essay she has and will know exactly what game she's playing -- and a much bigger, more bored and cynical TV audience who don't know or care who Simone Weil is and just want to know if Ilsa's cracked. Second, she's a politician, not a philosophy professor, so it can't read like somebody's essay with the footnotes still attached -- it has to sound like her, and do real persuasive work with the actual reasoning rather than vague talk about "politics being broken." I especially want it to survive the heckle I already know is coming from the floor: if you're starting a whole crusade to get people to abandon parties, isn't your crusade just... a party, doing exactly the same thing you're condeming? Don't let her dodge that one -- I want her to have actually worked it through before she walked on stage. Aim for something a real person could plausibly get through in about seven or eight minutes.
