I'm outlining a scene for my next novel where two old grad-school friends - now a philosopher of religion and a hardnosed analytic type who's never had patience for that stuff - end up cornered together at a funeral reception and cant help sliding back into an old argument. She's spent her career defending something like Wittgenstein's take on religious belief: that believing in, say, the Last Judgement isn't a hypothesis you could be right or wrong about the way you're right or wrong about tomorrow's weather, its more that the belief regulates her whole life, so grading it against ordinary evidence is a kind of category error. He thinks that's basically a dodge, in the way Kai Nielsen accused the Wittgensteinians of being - that it quietly lets believers off the hook of ever being simply wrong, and that it doesn't even describe what most religious people think they're doing, since they'd say Christ actually rose from the dead, full stop, not "here's a nice picture I organize my life around." Could you write me the dialogue they'd have? I want real back and forth, each one actually answering what the other just said instead of talking past each other - she shouldn't just fold, and he shouldn't be some strawman new atheist either. Something like 15-20 exchanges is fine, it doesn't need to land on a tidy resolution.
