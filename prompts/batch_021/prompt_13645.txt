Okay, this one's pretty niche but bear with me. I'm auditing a philosophy of art seminar, and last week's whole session was on Schopenhauer's theory of music, and afterward my friend Dev and I kept arguing about it over coffee until the cafe basically kicked us out over the arguement. The thing that set us off is his claim that music is totally unlike every other art -- painting, sculpture, poetry, architecture, tragedy, all of that apparently works by depicting the Platonic Ideas (the various "grades" at which the Will objectifies itself, from gravity and magnetism up through plant life, animal life, all the way to individual human character). Music supposedly skips that whole step: it isn't a picture of anything, it's a direct copy of the Will itself, on the same level as the Ideas rather than one rung below them -- which is apparently his basis for saying you could just as truthfully call the world "embodied music" as "embodied will." There's also this bit where he compares composers to sleepwalkers, revealing truths about the world they have no conscious access to while awake, which I thought was honestly the most interesting part.

Dev thought all this was genuinely profound -- it would explain why music can wreck you in a way that feels totally different from a painting, like it isn't "about" an emotion so much as it just IS the thing an emotion is only a shadow of. I said it's a gorgeous metaphor he's illegitimately promoting into a metaphysics, and that the specific stuff about melody -- how a tune's wandering away from the keynote and eventual return is supposed to mirror the will's endless cycle of striving, frustration, and momentary satisfaction -- is cute but basically numerology for people who took one semester of music theory. We never got anywhere, which is why I'd rather see the idea dramatized than argued about again.

Could you write me a short story (some real narrative, not an essay wearing a trenchcoat) that takes this specific piece of Schopenhauer completely seriously? I want the plot to actually hinge on music bypassing representation and giving someone unmediated contact with the Will -- bonus points if that melody-as-the-shape-of-desire idea, or the composer-as-sleepwalker idea, does some real work in the story rather than just getting name-dropped. Setting and character are totally up to you, whatever serves the idea best, but given Schopenhauer's whole deal with pessimism, I'd rather the ending not resolve into something warm and fuzzy -- something with a bit more bite to it. Somewhere in the 800-1500 word range is probably about right.
