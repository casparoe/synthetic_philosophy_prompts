Putnam and Fodor treat the multiple realizability of mental kinds as decisive evidence against reductive physicalism: because pain is realized by different physical states in humans, octopuses, and hypothetical silicon-based creatures, there is no single physical kind with which pain as such can be identified. Jaegwon Kim replied that this only rules out a single, species-independent reduction. A local reduction restricted to particular realizers—pain-in-humans to C-fiber firing, pain-in-octopuses to whatever plays that role in octopus nervous systems, and so on—remains fully available, and is all the reductionist should ever have wanted. Please argue for Kim's local-reduction strategy, and directly address the objection that this "victory" is hollow: that fragmenting pain into a family of structure-specific properties eliminates any single kind for psychological generalizations to range over, so that reduction is bought only at the cost of the very generalizations physicalism was supposed to accommodate.
