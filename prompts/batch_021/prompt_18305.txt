In my seminar on Wittgenstein's Philosophical Investigations, we worked through Kripke's rule-following paradox as far as the standard sequence of moves -- dispositionalism, the finitude and normativity objections, and the retreat to idealized dispositions -- but stopped just before the disagreement became genuinely difficult to referee. Write a dialogue between a dispositionalist who tries to save the view by idealizing away finitude and error, and a defender of Kripke's sceptical solution who objects that even an idealized disposition tells us only what the idealized speaker would say, never what the speaker should say, so that a disposition to err consistently would count, absurdly, as correct. Give the dispositionalist a genuine rejoinder -- perhaps an appeal to malfunction, design, or statistical normalcy -- rather than an immediate concession, and have each side engage with the specific move the other has just made rather than restating their opening position. You can assume familiarity with the quus example and the basic shape of the paradox.
