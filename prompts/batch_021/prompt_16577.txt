My grandmother died on Monday and because I'm the novelist of the family I've been informed I'm giving the eulogy Saturday, which apparently means I'm good with words even though my actual skill is making things up, not getting them right. She had alzheimers for the last six or seven years and carried around this fat green spiral notebook everywhere, wrote down absolutely everything the second it happened, and trusted it completely -- never doubted an entry, never cross checked it against her own memory, just consulted it the way the rest of us consult our own heads. My cousin, who's deep into a philosophy PhD and has apparently read every word Andy Clark ever published, keeps texting me that the notebook is basically the Otto case from the extended mind thesis and was, in a real sense, part of her mind and not just a tool she happened to rely on. I love that framing and want to build the eulogy around it, but she'll be sitting in the second row and will absolutely clock it if I quietly smuggle in more comfort than the thesis actually supports, and the other eighty people there have never heard of any of this and shouldn't need a lecture to feel something. Can you actually write the thing?
