I'm giving the toast at my friend Daniel's wedding next month. He's an epistemologist whose dissertation defended what people in the field call the Non-Akrasia Constraint: the view that it's never rational to believe P while also believing that your own evidence doesn't support P, no matter how settled that feels from the inside. He has spent the whole engagement as a textbook case of exactly the thing he says can't happen — the kind of case he'd normally illustrate with a sleep-deprived detective or a hypoxic pilot — except here he'll cheerfully agree that all the evidence, her own testimony very much included, says she's completely in love with him, and then admit, in the same breath, that he doesn't buy it. About a third of the room will be people from his PhD program who'll know exactly what's funny about that; everyone else just wants a toast that's warm and doesn't need a glossary. Write it so his own akrasia is the joke and the emotional core, not a term he name-drops once for the philosophers, and so it doesn't just try to argue him into believing the evidence — since, as he'd be the first to admit, that's exactly the move his own dissertation says can't work.
