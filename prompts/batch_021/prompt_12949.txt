In my Theory of Knowledge class, we spent a session on Thomas Kuhn's claim that a genuine paradigm shift changes not just what scientists believe but the very taxonomy they sort nature into. His own example was the word "planet": before Copernicus it applied to the Sun and Moon but not the Earth, and afterward the reverse was true, so astronomers on either side of that shift were not simply disagreeing about facts -- they were sorting the sky into different categories while using the same word. I cannot stop thinking about what that would actually feel like from the inside. Could you write me a short story, set in any scientific field you like, real or invented, centered on a conversation between a scientist who has crossed over to a new paradigm and a former mentor who has not -- a conversation that keeps quietly failing, not because either person is stubborn or missing evidence, but because one crucial term has silently split into two incompatible meanings? A page or two is plenty; I would like the incommensurability itself, rather than the discovery that caused it, to be what the story is really about.
