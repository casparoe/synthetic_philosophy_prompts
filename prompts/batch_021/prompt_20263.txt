Here's the specific thing I want to pull off. She was obsessed with 'Nueva refutación del tiempo' ('A New Refutation of Time') for decades, especially its final few paragraphs, where after spending the whole essay building this careful Berkeley-and-Hume-flavored case that there's no persisting self and maybe no real time either, just a series of disconnected perceptions, he basically turns around at the end and admits the argument, however airtight, doesn't actually save him from feeling like a man being swept off by a river and mauled by a tiger anyway. She used to say that reversal was the only honest thing shes ever read about death, way more honest than anything a priest could offer. I want the eulogy to actually make that same move for real -- take seriously, for a few minutes, the idea that the 'her' persisting across 84 years might be a kind of construction rather than a solid thing, and then land where Borges lands, back in the loss anyway -- instead of just namedropping him and moving on to safer material. The problem is the room is half family who've never read a page of philosophy and half her old colleagues who will absolutely notice if I flatten the argument or get it wrong. Can you write me something like 5-6 minutes long that actually pulls this off?
