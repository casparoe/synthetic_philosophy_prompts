I'm one of the regular community members on my city's reparations task force (there's a couple lawyers and a historian too, but I just applied because I care about this stuff). At our last meeting the historian pointed out something that's been stuck in my head since: if slavery and everything after it never happened, none of us alive today, Black or white, would even exist, since our actual ancestors would never have met and had the specific kids they did. So how can we say a specific living person was harmed "by" slavery in the way that's supposed to justify paying them, when the alternative isn't "this same person, better off" but "this person never being born at all"? Half the task force thinks this wrecks our whole rationale, the other half thinks its just a rhetorical trick not worth answering. Can you draft the memo I'd bring recommending how we handle this one objection in our final report -- something that actually takes it seriously instead of just picking whichever answer feels comfortable?
