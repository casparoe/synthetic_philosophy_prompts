I'm the engineer responsible for the notifications system on a habit-tracking app meant to help people maintain exercise routines. Our product lead wants me to ship a feature that, whenever someone breaks their streak, sends them a message from an in-app "coach" character expressing personal disappointment, timed using behavioral data to land in the evening hours when lapsed users are most likely to feel guilty enough to reopen the app. It recovers far more lapsed users than a plain reminder does, and since exercising more is exactly what people signed up for, my team treats it as an unambiguous win. But something about manufacturing guilt through a relationship with a character that isn't real, and deliberately timing it to when people are least able to resist, feels different from simply reminding someone of a goal they already hold. Is there a genuine distinction there, or am I overthinking a feature that only helps people do what they already wanted to do?
