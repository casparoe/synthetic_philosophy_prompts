Had a whole argument with my friend Priya last night about Cicero's On Old Age (De Senectute). Her take: the whole thing is just a rich, still-powerful old guy (Cato) telling other rich guys not to worry -- easy to say old age isn't so bad when youre still arguing cases in court, still running your own farm, and everyone in Rome wants your opinion. I pushed back that some of his actual points don't depend on any of that, like the bit where a bitter old age is really just a bitter personality finally showing itself, not something age causes on its own. Never resolved it.

Now for my actual problem: my grandpa turns 85 next week and I'm giving the toast. He taught Latin for 35 years, so his old colleagues and former students in the room will catch a Cicero reference right away, but my cousins and his great-grandkids won't have a clue who that even is. He's got bad knees and can't drive anymore, but he's sharp as ever and babies his tomato plants every summer like they're prize vines.

Can you write the toast? Use Cicero's actual arguments, not "80 is the new 60" fluff, in a way that'd basically win my argument with Priya -- so nothing that only works if you're lucky and well off, and nothing that pretends his decline isn't real.
