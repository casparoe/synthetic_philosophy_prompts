As a novelist, I've grown quietly fascinated by the machinery of literary reputation: the blurb from an author who plainly skimmed the book, the dinner guest who pronounces a novel "devastating" on the strength of a single conversation with someone who has actually read it. I'd like a joke that turns on the philosophical puzzle of aesthetic testimony, and in particular on the so-called acquaintance principle — the thought that taking someone else's word for a work's beauty or power doesn't license the verdict the way taking their word for an ordinary fact would, even when you trust them completely. Please make the punchline hinge on the real shape of that puzzle (say, on why aesthetic judgment seems to resist deference in a way that ordinary factual belief doesn't), rather than settling for a generic jab at pretentious critics.
