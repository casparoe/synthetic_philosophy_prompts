During my career as an experimental physicist I had little patience for metaphysics, but retirement has given me time to indulge it, and for the past year I've been part of a small philosophy discussion group. At our last meeting, two members, Miriam and Dov, got into a genuine disagreement about presentism and eternalism that I'd like an outside opinion on, since I was too busy taking notes to referee it myself.

(1) Miriam opened by arguing that special relativity settles the question: because simultaneity is relative to a reference frame, there is no frame-independent fact about which distant events share "now" with us, so presentism's picture of one objectively privileged present moment cannot be squared with physics — leaving eternalism's block universe as the only view the theory actually permits.

(2) Dov replied that this only refutes a presentist who insists on a single universe-wide present: a presentist could instead relativize "now" to each location, or even adopt a privileged-but-undetectable rest frame, since a Lorentzian reformulation of relativity reproduces all the same observable predictions as the standard theory.

(3) Miriam countered that inventing an undetectable privileged frame purely to rescue a metaphysical picture is exactly the kind of move Occam's razor forbids: it adds structure to the universe that no possible observation could ever reveal.

(4) Dov answered that eternalism isn't free of unobservable additions either — nothing in the mathematics of spacetime forces us to say the four-dimensional manifold "really exists," tenselessly, all at once; that's an interpretation laid on top of the physics, not a consequence of it, so both views are paying a similar toll.

(5) Miriam then brought up Gödel's rotating-universe solutions to Einstein's field equations, in which no global "now" can be defined even in principle, and asked why a metaphysical thesis about the nature of time should come out true or false depending on which solution happens to describe our particular universe. Dov admitted he'd need to think about that one, and the conversation moved on to coffee before anyone settled it.

Could you go through Miriam and Dov's exchange move by move and say who has the better of each point — not just who wins overall — and flag anything you think both of them are missing?
