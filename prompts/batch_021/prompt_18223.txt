My friend Theo and I got into a proper argument about Frege last night after he mentioned that Frege kept a private political diary near the end of his life that only surfaced decades after he died, and its full of hard-right, antisemitic, anti-Weimar stuff -- wanting some strongman to just get rid of parliament and the Social Democrats, that kind of thing. Theo's line was that this is completely irrelevant to Frege's actual philosophy: whether the context principle is true, or whether Basic Law V collapses into Russell's paradox, has nothing whatsoever to do with what the guy thought about Jews or democracy in 1924, and treating it as relevant is basically just guilt by association. I wasn't so sure -- I brought up how weirdly obsessed Frege was with there being exactly one objective, mind-independent realm of logical truth (he even named it the "third realm," drittes Reich, in that essay on Thoughts, which is a genuinely unsettling coincidence given what came later), and how his whole anti-psychologism project is basically a war against relativism and pluralism about truth. Doesn't seem crazy to me that the same guy who couldn't tolerate multiple "true for you" logics also couldn't tolerate multiple peoples or parties having a legitimate claim on Germany. Theo wasn't convinced, we went in circles for like an hour and got no where, and apparently even Michael Dummett went through something like this same crisis of confidence when the diary came out, so I don't feel too bad that we didn't resolve it either.

Could you write this out properly as a dialogue between two Frege scholars who actually know the primary texts cold (not just "he invented the sense/reference distinction" pop-level stuff), arguing it out somewhere like a hotel bar at a conference after a Frege panel? One should hold Theo's "no real bearing" position and the other mine, and I want them each pushing on the strongest version of the other's argument instead of scoring cheap points -- so the "irrelevant" side has to actually engage with the psychologism/pluralism angle instead of just yelling "ad hominem," and the "it matters" side has to concede that liking or disliking Frege's temperament doesn't actually falsify a single theorem or refute the context principle. I'd like it to end with the two of them at least agreeing on exactly what they still disagree about, rather than either side just winning.
