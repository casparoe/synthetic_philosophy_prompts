So a while back a friend of mine wouldn't stop bringing up the Wilt Chamberlain argument every time we argued about taxes, so I finally just sat down and read Anarchy, State, and Utopia myself (zero philosophy background, I was just annoyed). Most of it I could follow okay in a rough way, but pretty early on, right after Nozick lays out his whole "side constraints" idea — the bit about people being ends in themselves who can't just be used up for the greater good — he goes off on a couple pages about animals thats been stuck in my head ever since. From what I can tell (if I'm remembering it right) he floats doing "utilitarianism for animals, kantianism for people": animal pain and pleasure gets added up and weighed against benefits like normal utilitarian math, but people get these hard constraints that supposedly can't be crossed no matter how much good it would do. He even admits this combo is kind of an odd couple and doesn't really spell out how the two halfs are supposed to talk to each other (like what happens if maximizing animal welfare would require using a person as a means), then basically shrugs and moves on to the next topic.

That bit has bugged me for two reasons since. First, the whole justification for people getting side constraints, as I understood it, is about us being able to steer our own life by some overall plan we choose, not about which species we happen to belong to — but plenty of animals seem to do rough versions of that (elephants grieving, chimps planning ahead), while a newborn baby or someone with advanced dementia arguably does less of it than those animals, and Nozick obviously isn't willing to let babies get the "just add up the utility" treatment. Second, earlier in that same part of the book he already trashes utilitarianism in general using his own "utility monster" idea, where a being that gets a crazy return on investment in utility terms would justify sacrificing everyone else to it — and that seems like a problem with adding up utility as such, not just a problem when you do it to people. So it's weird to me that he's totally happy to hand the utilitarian calculator to animals like the monster problem magically doesn't apply there too.

What I actually want from you is the strongest possible version of the argument that this "kantianism for people, utilitarianism for animals" split doesn't hold together — that if you take Nozick's own stated reasons for giving people side constraints seriously, you end up forced, on pain of inconsistency, to extend at least some real side-constraint-style protection to at least some animals too, rather than just letting their suffering be one more input into a utility calculation. Please don't just restate the tension I laid out above — actually carry the argument all the way through, using his own concepts and vocabulary against him as much as you can.
