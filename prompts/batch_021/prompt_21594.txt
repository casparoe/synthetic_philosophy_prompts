My younger brother has drug-resistant epilepsy and is scheduled for a corpus callosotomy next month to stop his drop seizures, so I've spent the last few weeks reading about split-brain research to understand what the surgery might mean for him beyond seizure control. This pulled my aunt Elena, who teaches philosophy of mind, and her partner Sam, a cognitive neuroscience postdoc, into a dinner-table argument I still can't resolve.

Elena's opening claim was that after the callosotomy there will, in some real sense, be two centers of experience where there used to be one. She cited the classic Sperry/Gazzaniga demonstrations -- a patient's left hand independently reaching for an object flashed only to the right hemisphere while the patient verbally denies seeing anything -- and said that is exactly what two minds sharing a skull would look like from the outside.

Sam objected that outside the lab, split-brain patients act as single, coordinated agents almost all the time; the dramatic dissociations only appear when an experimenter deliberately feeds conflicting information to each visual half-field, and even then the hemispheres often stay in sync through shared sensory input and what researchers call "cross-cueing" -- one side picking up on the other's posture or eye movements. An occasional lab-induced hiccup, he said, doesn't show that two people live in there.

Elena replied that rarity isn't the same as unreality: two people who agree about everything and never conflict are still two people, not one. Ordinary life may simply rarely present the kind of contradictory information needed to expose a division that is there all along.

Sam's counter was that this analogy assumes what is in question, since two people have separate sense organs and bodies, while the hemispheres still share eyes, ears, and some intact subcortical connections even after surgery; separable information processing is not automatically separate subjects of experience, and we already accept plenty of dissociated, partly unconscious processing -- blindsight, subliminal priming -- within what is clearly one mind.

They eventually asked me to settle it, which I couldn't.
