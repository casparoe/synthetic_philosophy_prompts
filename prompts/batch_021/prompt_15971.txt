So, one of my former PhD students is getting married in a few weeks and asked me to give the toast at the reception. She wrote her dissertation defending panpsychism, and spent half of it getting annoyed at other panpsychists for not taking the combination problem seriously enough -- how a bunch of micro-subjects (or micro-experiences, depending which chapter your reading) are supposed to add up into one unified subject, rather than just staying a pile of separate tiny minds. Half the room is her old grad school cohort, who'll catch it immediately if I lean on this, the other half is family who's never heard the phrase 'subject of experience' in there life. I can't get away with the standard 'two souls becoming one' toast, because she'd genuinely heckle me for it -- that's exactly the move she thinks panpsychists aren't allowed to make for free. Write me the toast, about three minutes read aloud, built around the combination problem as its actual spine, ideally with one real joke that lands for the philosophers without losing everyone else, and that still sounds like a toast and not like I'm re-litigating her thesis defense.
