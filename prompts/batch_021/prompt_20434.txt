A friend and I spent last night arguing about the demandingness objection to consequentialism: he wants to rescue act-utilitarianism with something like Scheffler's agent-centered prerogative, letting ordinary people stop short of its full demands, and I kept insisting that any prerogative generous enough to cover how most of us actually live just smuggles back in the arbitrary self-favoring that the theory was supposed to rule out. The argument has an uncomfortable relevance this week: my aunt's funeral is on Saturday, and she lived as though no such prerogative existed, giving away most of what she earned as a nurse over four decades by her own utilitarian calculations, and telling anyone who called her generous that she had merely done what was required. Before she died she asked that the eulogy not soften this into a story about a kind woman. Most of the mourners are family and colleagues who have never read a page of moral philosophy, and the eulogy cannot come across as an indictment of how they live. Please write it.
