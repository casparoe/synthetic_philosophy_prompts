ok I've been nerd-sniped by van Fraassen's Reflection Principle lately (from "Belief and the Will") -- the idea that your credence in A right now should equal whatever credence you're certain your future, rational self will assign to A, on pain of a diachronic dutch book if you deviate. want a dialogue between two bayesians who agree on most things but split hard on whether this actually holds up.

the reflection-defender should hold the principle pretty much as stated, with the standard caveat that it only kicks in when you currently trust your future self's rationality (so it doesn't apply if you expect to get drugged, drunk, or brainwashed between now and then). the reflection-skeptic thinks that caveat quietly guts the whole thing -- either it collapses into just "defer to future opinions you've already decided to trust" (not really a constraint at all), or it leaves you with no guidance in exactly the cases you'd want it, like when you can't tell in advance whether an anticipated shift in your own credences is rational updating or just predictable bias creeping in.

make them really engage with each other's specific moves rather than restate their opening positions -- i want to see the dutch book argument actually get pressed on, and the circularity charge actually get answered, not just gestured at. doesnt need a tidy resolution at the end.
