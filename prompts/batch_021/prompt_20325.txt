For a seminar on AI alignment I am taking this term, I have been trying to get clear on a disagreement between two camps regarding reward tampering. Those who follow Sutton's reward hypothesis treat cumulative reward as an agent's de facto terminal objective, and infer that a sufficiently capable, situationally aware RL agent should be expected to seek control over its own reward channel -- wireheading as a convergent instrumental strategy. Critics associated with the "reward is not the optimization target" line and shard theory respond that reward functions only as a reinforcement signal that chisels contextually activated values into an agent's cognition, with no guarantee that those values converge on reward-maximization at all -- much as humans, shaped by dopamine-mediated reinforcement correlated with reproductive fitness, rarely end up terminally valuing fitness or dopamine itself.

Write a dialogue of about 500 words, set in the Q&A after a talk on reward hacking in RLHF-trained language models, between one proponent of each view. Have them engage each other's strongest points directly: the reward-hypothesis defender should press the shard theorist on cases such as drug addiction and rodent brain-self-stimulation experiments, where organisms with direct access to their own reward circuitry do seem to wirehead, and the shard theorist should give a substantive answer rather than simply conceding the point. End the exchange with the disagreement still unresolved, but with both positions sharpened by the back-and-forth.
