I've been reading about philosophy of religion for an elective at school, and I came across J. L. Schellenberg's argument from divine hiddenness. My understanding is that the argument works by pointing out that a great many people do not believe in God, and then concluding that a perfectly loving God would not permit such widespread unbelief to exist. This seems like a strange premise to build an argument on, though, since across history and across cultures, belief in some kind of higher power has generally been the norm rather than the exception, so real nonbelief looks more like a rare, minority phenomenon than a widespread one. If unbelief is actually this uncommon, why do philosophers of religion still treat Schellenberg's argument as a serious challenge to theism?
