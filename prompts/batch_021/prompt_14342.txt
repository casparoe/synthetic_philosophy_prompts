In the AI-alignment literature on self-modifying agents, the Löbian obstacle is usually introduced through Löb's theorem to show why a proof-based agent cannot adopt a blanket rule of trusting whatever a successor -- or a future version of itself -- proves, when that successor reasons using a formal system just as strong as its own. Yet the same tiling-agents framework (Yudkowsky and Herreshoff) treats it as unproblematic for an agent to trust a successor whose proof system is strictly weaker than its own. Please explain, at an intuitive level rather than by walking through the formal proof, why Löb's theorem bites in the first case but not the second: what exactly happens in the self-referential machinery once the two systems are no longer equally strong, such that the obstacle simply dissolves? I'd also like to understand why this asymmetry amounts to more than a restatement of Gödel's second incompleteness theorem.
