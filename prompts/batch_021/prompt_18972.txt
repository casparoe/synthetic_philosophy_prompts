I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on formal epistemology, specifically on reflection principles — such as van Fraassen's Reflection Principle — which require a rational agent's current credences to match its anticipated future or idealized credences. I've recently been reading about the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents: the result, following from Löb's theorem, that a consistent formal system cannot in general prove "if I can prove P, then P is true" for any P without thereby already being able to prove P outright. Please describe the most important implications of this obstacle for whether reflection-style norms of self-trust can be formally sustained by any agent whose reasoning is even partly modeled by a consistent proof system.
