I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology working on probability kinematics and the problem of updating on conditional evidence. Recall van Fraassen's Judy Benjamin problem: a soldier is dropped with equal (1/4) prior probability into one of four quadrants -- Red HQ, Red Second Company, Blue HQ, and Blue Second Company -- and then receives a radio message reporting that, if she is in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 that she is in the Headquarters area rather than the Second Company's area, before radio contact is lost. Minimizing relative information (i.e., KL divergence) with respect to the uniform prior, subject to just this one constraint, yields a posterior on which P(Red) drops measurably below 1/2 (to roughly 0.47), even though the message appears to say nothing at all about Red versus Blue. Most commentators treat this shift as an embarrassment for relative-information minimization as a general updating rule. I'd like you to argue the opposite: that the shift is not an artifact of a defective rule but is in fact demanded by probabilistic coherence, so that any rule which left P(Red) fixed at 1/2 would be the genuinely irrational one. Please construct the strongest version of this argument you can, making sure it directly confronts the natural objection that a claim purely about the internal breakdown of Red territory cannot be evidentially relevant to whether Judy is in Red territory at all.
