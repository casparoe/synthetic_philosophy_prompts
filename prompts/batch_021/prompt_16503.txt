I write about science and ideas for a magazine, and next month I'm interviewing a philosopher who has just published a book arguing that Nietzsche's scattered remarks on diet, climate, and daily regimen in Ecce Homo and Twilight of the Idols are not incidental eccentricities but integral to his mature philosophy. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond a simple recap of Nietzsche's culinary preferences. I want at least one question each on how his attack on Cornaro in "The Four Great Errors" -- mistaking the effect of a slow metabolism for its cause -- squares with his own confident, seemingly universal dietary prescriptions elsewhere in his work; on what work the notion of "great health" from The Gay Science is still doing if it coexists so comfortably with such a detailed rulebook for nutrition and climate; and on whether his own lifelong, largely unsuccessful hunt for a climate that would relieve his migraines and digestion counts as evidence against his physiological theorizing or as exactly what that theorizing would predict. For each of these, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give -- for instance, that the passages in question are diagnostic self-description rather than universal claims. Close with one question that forces her to say plainly whether Nietzsche's dietetics amounts to universal science or a highly particular case study dressed in universal-sounding language, and what that answer implies for how much weight his remarks on race, climate, and cultural decadence can still bear.
