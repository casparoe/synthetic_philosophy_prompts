I'm a philosophy PhD student working on the metaphysics of artifact identity, which is partly why a small heritage railway asked me to join its board of trustees. Our locomotive, the 1911 Bramwell, has run in continuous passenger service for over a century through the line's standard overhaul regime, so that every boiler, wheelset, and frame now on the engine is a replacement; no component currently fitted dates from 1911. A rival preservation trust has spent forty years buying up Bramwell's original boiler, frames, and wheels each time one was condemned and sold for scrap; those parts sat dispersed among sheds and private collections until the trust reassembled them into a complete, if currently static, locomotive two years ago. Both trusts have applied to the national Heritage Rolling Stock Register for the designation '1911 Bramwell,' which carries preservation funding and the right to run under that identity at rallies; the register wants each board's position statement before its panel hearing next month. Please draft ours: it should recommend where we come down — backing our engine's claim, conceding it to the rival trust, or proposing some other resolution — and it should engage honestly with the strongest argument for whichever side we don't take, rather than dismissing it.
