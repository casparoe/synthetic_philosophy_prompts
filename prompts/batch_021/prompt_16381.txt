I'm currently taking a course on classical Chinese philosophy, and we've just spent two sessions on Gongsun Long's "White Horse Dialogue." Most discussions stop at the surface-level arguments — that "horse" names a shape while "white" names a color, or that a black horse satisfies "give me a horse" but not "give me a white horse" — but what interests me is that the exchange really turns on an equivocation in the word "fei" (非): the advocate means that "white horse" is not identical with "horse," while the objector keeps hearing the claim that no white horses belong to the category horse. Neither party is being dense; they are defending two different theses without realizing it. Could you write a short story, roughly 800 to 1000 words, that dramatizes exactly this kind of impasse — two characters heading toward real trouble because a single word is quietly doing two jobs in their dispute? Please invent your own setting rather than restaging the well-worn anecdote about a horse and a border guard, and if you can give the two characters argumentative temperaments that echo the School of Names' two poles — one who keeps separating what looks unified (Gongsun Long's "separating hard and white"), and one who keeps dissolving distinctions that look obvious (Hui Shi's "uniting sameness and difference") — all the better.
