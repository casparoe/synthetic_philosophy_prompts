I've only really gotten into philosophy in the last year or so, after retiring from a career as an experimental physicist (mostly particle-detector stuff, so I like my causes tangible). I've been working through Hume's Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding on my own, and I got stuck on the bit in Section VII, Part II, where he offers what he presents as basically two equivalent "definitions" of cause, one right after the other:

"We may define a cause to be an object followed by another, and where all the objects, similar to the first, are followed by objects similar to the second. Or, in other words, where, if the first object had not been, the second never had existed. The appearance of a cause always conveys the mind, by a customary transition, to the idea of the effect. Of this also we have experience. We may, therefore, suitably to this experience, form another definition of cause, and call it, an object followed by another, and whose appearance always conveys the thought to that other."

He calls the second one just another way of saying the first, but they don't read like the same claim to me at all — the first (especially that "if the first object had not been" clause) sounds like its sneaking in a counterfactual, wheras the second is just describing a psychological habit of association. Old experimentalist in me always figured "cause" just meant whatever knob I turned to make the other thing happen, so honestly neither one quite matches my intuition either.

I gather this exact passage gets cited as basically ground zero for the later split between counterfactual theories of causation (Lewis and that lineage) and the more recent interventionist ones (Woodward, Pearl, the do-calculus crowd). Could you help me see specifically why philosophers read Hume's two definitions as pulling apart rather than just restating each other, and how each one maps onto (or resists mapping onto) those two later camps? Not after a general Hume refresher, more the nuts and bolts of why this passage counts as a fork in the road.
