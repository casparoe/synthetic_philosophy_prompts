I'm a PhD student in philosophy of medicine, and I sit as a student representative on my university's Graduate Council subcommittee revising policy on medical leaves and milestone extensions (quals, proposal defenses, dissertation deadlines). Currently, a fixed list of documented diagnoses -- major depression, generalized anxiety disorder, PTSD -- automatically qualifies a student for an extension once a treating clinician signs off; anything else requires a discretionary petition to the Dean. Student mental health services now wants Prolonged Grief Disorder, added as a formal diagnosis in the DSM-5-TR in 2022, placed on that automatic list, on the grounds that it's arbitrary to make grieving students petition when other diagnoses don't require it. One faculty member on the subcommittee objects that formally listing grief as a qualifying condition pathologizes something universal and implicitly tells students whose grief falls short of the twelve-month threshold that their suffering doesn't count; another argues that the diagnosis's duration cutoff is a judgment call, not a discovered fact, and is exactly the kind of case the discretionary process exists to weigh one student at a time. Please draft the recommendation memo I'll circulate before our next meeting: it should say plainly where Prolonged Grief Disorder belongs -- the automatic list, the discretionary track, or the list with conditions attached -- and answer the strongest version of whichever side it doesn't take, rather than dismissing it.
