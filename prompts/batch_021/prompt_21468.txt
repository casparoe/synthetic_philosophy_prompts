A friend of mine — he's finishing a dissertation that leans heavily on structural equation models — and I spent most of a dinner last week arguing about causation. He kept insisting that Woodward-style interventionism has essentially superseded Lewis's old counterfactual theory; I kept feeling that his account was quietly trading on causal notions it was supposed to be explaining, without ever quite landing the point myself. Neither of us won, and I now have a chance to continue the argument by proxy: I co-host a philosophy of science podcast, and next month's guest is the author of a well-regarded recent book defending the interventionist theory of causation against its counterfactual rivals. I don't want her to get away with the informal "wiggle the cause and see if the effect wiggles" gloss and move straight on to the next question.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that stay close to the actual mechanics of the dispute rather than the popular summary of it. I want at least one question that presses the charge that "intervention" is itself an unreduced causal notion, so that trading Lewis's similarity-of-worlds machinery for Woodward's apparatus swaps one causal primitive for another instead of genuinely reducing causation to anything more basic, and at least one that gives her a late-preemption case in the spirit of two rock-throwers shattering a bottle — Suzy's rock arrives first, Billy's would have shattered it too — and asks her to say, honestly, whether the structural-equations treatment of such cases is any less patched-together than Lewis's own fixes. I also want one built on something like Schaffer's "trumping preemption" case, where a private obeys simultaneous, individually sufficient orders from a sergeant and a major and seems to act because of the major's order alone, so I can find out what non-arbitrary resource interventionism has for that ranking when the equations themselves are symmetric between the two orders; and one about causal claims involving systems nobody could ever manipulate — the trajectories of galaxies, the deep past — asking whether "hypothetical" interventions there just reintroduce plain counterfactual dependence under a new name. For each question, please add a short, specific follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give: the kind of rejoinder that would actually have helped me hold my ground at dinner.
