I'm a philosophy PhD student completing a clinical ethics practicum, through which I hold a non-voting seat on my teaching hospital's Serious Event Review Committee. We meet Friday to finalize our report on a case that has split the committee, and I've been asked to produce a first-draft recommendation memo.

The case: a critical potassium value posted to a patient's chart at 2:14 a.m.; the on-call resident never acted on it, and the patient died of arrhythmia four hours later. Hospital policy requires escalation to state reporting only for deaths "caused by an identifiable error or omission in the process of care." Roughly half the committee wants to classify this as an omission-caused death, since the patient would very likely have survived had the resident looked at the result. The other half argues that nothing the resident did or failed to do actually produced the arrhythmia -- the real story is systemic (an EHR that flags dozens of "critical" values nightly, chronic understaffing, an attending and charge nurse who had the same information and also did nothing) -- and that if we call the resident's inaction a cause, we have no principled way to avoid saying the same of everyone else on shift who equally failed to intervene.

Please draft the memo: recommend whether this case meets the reporting threshold, propose a standard the committee can reuse in future cases, and engage the systemic objection on its merits rather than brushing past it.
