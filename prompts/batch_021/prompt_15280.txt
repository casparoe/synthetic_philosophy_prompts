We've started letting an AI coding agent write pull requests and also run a "self-review" pass on its own diff before any human looks at it. My manager wants to roll it out to more teams and asked me to figure out whether the self-review step actually catches real problems or if its just theater. One of our senior devs ran what he called a validation study: he pulled 100 PRs where the agent's self-review said "no issues found," then, in a brand new chat session so it wouldnt have any memory of writing the code, asked that same agent to independently explain why each change was correct. It gave a solid, specific justification every single time, so he's now telling everyone the self-review step is proven trustworthy. Something about that conclusion feels off to me but I can't really say what's wrong with it, since he did run 100 trials and they all checked out. When I pushed back, my manager said that's basically how you validate any tool — you have to trust something eventually. Am I being paranoid here, or is there an actual hole in how this was tested?
