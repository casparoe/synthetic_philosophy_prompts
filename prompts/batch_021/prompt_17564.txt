Two people on my team got into it during our sprint retro and it's been stuck in my head since. We were arguing about how to phrase a risk estimate for leadership on a gnarly database migration.

(1) Marcus said we should just give one number, like "20% chance this causes a P1," because that's actually actionable, and giving a range is really just a way to dodge blame later -- say "somewhere between 5% and 40%" and you're never technically wrong.

(2) Deepa said no, a range is the honest answer here specifically, because unlike our routine deploys, where we've got years of incident data, this migration touches infra we've literally never touched. We're way closer to "totally in the dark" than "known 20% coin," and slapping the same kind of number on both situations hides how different our actual epistemic position is.

(3) Marcus came back with: fine, but your 5%-40% isn't certain either -- your not 100% sure the truth is even in that band, so you've just pushed the fake precision up one level. It has to cash out as a number eventually, so why not just give it now and be honest about it.

(4) Deepa said it doesn't have to cash out. Her actual belief state isn't one fuzzy number, its genuinely a spread of estimates she can't honestly collapse into one, and refusing to fake that collapse isn't evasive, its just accurate.

(5) Marcus then brought up something he half-remembered from a blog: isn't it kind of damning that under Deepa's approach, learning new, relevant info can sometimes make the range wider instead of narrower? Gathering evidence is supposed to make you more certain, so if her method can spit out "actually now I'm officially less sure," doesn't that show ranges aren't tracking real belief at all?

(6) Deepa admitted this actually happens -- she called it "dilation" and said its a known, documented thing and not some glitch specific to her setup -- but also admitted that even people who like this approach don't have a fully clean explanation for why it isn't weird.

Nobody at the table closed it out and we just moved on to the next agenda item, but i keep chewing on it. Can you adjudicate move by move?
