Random rabbit hole I fell down this week: buried in section 6 of Turing's 1950 paper on the imitation game, he takes ESP dead serious as an objection to machines thinking, says the statistical evidence for telepathy is "overwhelming," and even proposes sticking the human and machine in a "telepathy-proof room" so a psychic contestant couldn't unfairly ace the test. Barely anyone who namedrops the Turing test has read that far.

Heres my question: Turing was also one of the sharpest statistical minds of his century, he basically built a working version of Bayesian evidence-weighing at Bletchley (the deciban). So what's your best guess at how he'd have reacted to how parapsychology actually played out after he died, especially Daryl Bem's 2011 "Feeling the Future" precognition experiments and the replication crisis/decline effect that followed, wich made a lot of that old "overwhelming" psi evidence quietly evaporate? Would his own statistical toolkit have let him see through Bem's numbers, or does that 1950 line suggest he'd have been just as taken in? And would he still think a telepathy-proof room is the right fix today, or would he decide the real threat to his test was always sloppy statistics rather than actual psychic powers?
