I'm interviewing a game theorist next week for a piece on why smart people (and whole companies) keep throwing good money after bad, and her favorite illustration is the dollar auction -- Shubik's game where the dollar goes to the highest bidder but the second-highest bidder also has to pay up and gets nothing, so bids routinely blow way past a dollar. Her whole angle is that this isn't proof anyone's irrational: once your the second-highest bidder, ratcheting up another bid is the rational move given what you and your rival each know about the other's resources, so the "trap" is built into the rules, not into anybody's head. She uses this to wave off the comparison people love to make to plain old sunk-cost escalation in business (funding a doomed project because of what's already been sunk into it), saying that's a different mechanism wearing the same costume.

Can you draft me 8 interview questions that don't just let her restate that line? I want at least one that presses on the fact that in actual lab versions of the game, bids regularly blow past anything a tidy "rational escalation" model would predict, one that makes her actually draw the line between dollar-auction escalation and plain sunk-cost thinking since she tends to blur the two, and one asking what result would actually convince her the bidders, not the rules, were the irrational part. Give me a quick follow-up for each, keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
