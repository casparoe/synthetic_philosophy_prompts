So there are two judges on a three-member appellate panel arguing about how the court should've decided a case, right after the vote. The case turns on two separate issues -- say, whether a valid contract existed and whether the defendant breached it -- and the defendant is liable only if both are true. Judge A voted yes/yes (liable), Judge B voted yes/no (not liable), Judge C voted no/yes (not liable). Go issue-by-issue and the majority says the contract was valid and a breach occured, so liable. Vote on the bottom-line verdict directly instead and the majority says not liable. Same three judges, same votes, opposite outcome depending on which procedure you use -- the classic doctrinal paradox / discursive dilemma.

Write a dialogue between the judge who insists the court has to go issue-by-issue (premise-based procedure) to keep the law coherent across cases, and the judge who thinks that just lets logic override the actual majority and wants the court to tally verdicts directly (conclusion-based procedure) instead. Make them equally sharp -- have each one respond to the other's actual strongest point rather than just restating their own -- and don't let it wrap up in a tidy truce.
