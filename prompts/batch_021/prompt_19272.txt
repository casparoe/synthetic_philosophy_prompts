So I've been covering research-integrity stories for a few years now, and usually the fraud cases are pretty clean cut (blatantly duplicated figures, fabricated datasets, the works). But the longread I'm drafting right now is murkier: it's about a cancer biology lab where sleuths on PubPeer have flagged issues in something like 15 papers over the last two years, which has so far turned into 2 formal retractions, 6 corrections, and 3 "expressions of concern," with the university's integrity office still reviewing the rest. My editor wants the piece to say the PI has "a pattern of misconduct," but every time I try to actually justify that phrase in my own head I get stuck, because almost every single paper on that list has a not-crazy innocent explanation if you look at it alone (a mislabeled lane, a grad student who left the lab years back, a "reused" control image that's chalked up to sloppy record keeping) — its only once you stack the whole list up that it starts to feel damning rather than just unlucky. I even sat down with my editor and tried ranking all 15 papers by severity to find some natural cutoff for "pattern," and we just couldnt agree on where the line should go, or why that particular number of papers and not one more or one less.

That's the part that's actually bugging me: the exact same 15 papers could honestly get written up as "a respected scientist beset by a few unfortunate errors" or "a case of serial misconduct," depending entirely on where some individual reporter or editor personally decides enough is enough — and I don't think that's just me being ignorant of the case. Is there an actual principled way to decide how many flagged papers it takes before "a few sloppy papers" turns into "a pattern," or is any line you draw there inherently arbitrary no matter how carefully you draw it? And if it really is arbitrary all the way down, does that mean a phrase like "pattern of misconduct" is basically unusable in honest journalism, or is there some defensible middle ground between faking a bright line and retreating into mush like "concerns have been raised"?
