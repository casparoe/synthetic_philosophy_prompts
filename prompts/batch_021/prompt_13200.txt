I teach a graduate seminar on epistemic paradoxes, and this week's session is on the surprise examination paradox (also known as the unexpected hanging paradox). Every year the discussion follows the same script: someone reconstructs the backward-induction argument from the last possible day, someone else objects that the teacher's announcement is self-contradictory along Quinean lines, and the class treats the matter as settled. What gets lost is that this whole line of reasoning only targets the multi-day version of the puzzle; it says nothing about the single-day formulation -- "you will have an exam tomorrow, and it will be a surprise" -- which has no days left over which to run an induction, and which if anything is harder to dissolve, since it collapses almost immediately into a bare self-referential blindspot rather than an inconsistent chain of eliminations. A proposed solution that quietly changes the subject from one formulation to the other strikes me as evading the paradox rather than solving it, and I would like your help holding candidate solutions to that standard.
