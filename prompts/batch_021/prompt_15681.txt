For my aesthetics class this week we covered aesthetic testimony -- the puzzle of whether you can be justified in believing a work is beautiful/moving/whatever just because someone whose taste you trust told you so, without ever being acquainted with the work yourself, the same way you can justifiably believe an ordinary fact just because a reliable friend told you it. Apparently loads of people think testimony transmits knowledge fine for regular facts but feels weirdly hollow or "off" for aesthetic verdicts specifically (something about needing first-hand "acquaintance" with the object, not just second-hand reports of its value). Can you write me a short story that actually dramatizes that asymmetry -- like a character who has to decide how much weight to put on someone else's aesthetic judgment in place of their own -- rather then just being a generic story about art or a critic. Doesn't need a neat resolution, 600-800 words is fine.
