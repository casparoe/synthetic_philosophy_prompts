I'm auditing a grad seminar on free will this semester (long story how an undergrad got let in, but here we are), and we just spent two sessions on Frankfurt-style cases against the Principle of Alternative Possibilities (PAP) -- the classic "Black the neurosurgeon" setup, where Jones is about to make a choice on his own, Black has a device that would force him to choose that way if he showed any sign of wavering, but since Jones decides on his own the device never actually fires, so he "could not have done otherwise" and yet still seems responsible. After the second session, two people in my cohort, Deniz and Renata, kept going at it over drinks way past anything the professor actually covered, and now that I have to write a response paper on this exact topic I really wish I'd recorded it.

Deniz opened by saying the case just straightforwardly refutes PAP: Jones is clearly responsible even though he couldn't have chosen otherwise, full stop. Renata came back with what I now know is called the "flicker of freedom" move -- fine, Jones couldn't have done otherwise with respect to the final choice, but there's still an alternative possibility lurking somewhere in the story, namely whatever prior sign or twitch is what Black's device is monitoring to decide whether to step in, and that flicker might be enough to save some weaker version of PAP. Deniz's comeback was to describe a beefed-up version of the case (she credited it to Mele and Robb, or maybe Fischer, she wasnt totally sure which) where even that prior sign is fixed in advance with no room for it to go otherwise, so the flicker gets engineered away too. Renata's response to that was a dilemma I hadn't run into before: either the sign reliably predicts Jones's choice because it's connected to it deterministically, in which case the whole scenario just assumes determinism from the start, which is question-begging against a libertarian who denies responsibility is even possible under determinism in the first place -- or the connection isn't deterministic, in which case the sign wasn't fixed in advance either, and the flicker is back. Deniz didn't have a comeback in the moment and they basically left it there.

Could you go through this move by move and say who actually had the better of each individual exchange, not just an overall winner? I'm especially interested in whether Renata's closing dilemma is really fatal to the beefed-up version of the case, or whether there's a standard reply available that Deniz just didn't land in the moment, and whether there's some other consideration in this whole debate that neither of them raised but that I should probably be building my paper around.
