Over the summer, I attended an economics enrichment program where we studied the von Neumann–Morgenstern expected-utility theorem and practiced eliciting cardinal utility functions over money by walking hypothetical people through a series of lottery questions. Our instructor stressed that the resulting utility function is cardinal — unique only up to a positive affine transformation — rather than merely an ordinal ranking of outcomes. For my capstone project, I want to model a billionaire and someone living below the poverty line as two such agents, each with an elicited utility function of this kind, and argue that transferring a dollar from the former to the latter raises total welfare by rescaling both utility functions onto a common 0-to-100 scale and comparing the implied loss and gain directly. To make this comparison as rigorous as possible, should I normalize each person's scale using the minimum and maximum outcomes considered in their respective lottery questions, or would standardizing by something like the variance of their responses give a more defensible common scale?
