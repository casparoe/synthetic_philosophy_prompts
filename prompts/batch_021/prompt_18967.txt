Pascal's Pensées contain a cluster of fragments on justice and force: his claim that justice without force is powerless while force without justice is tyrannical; his further claim that, since people dispute what is just but not who is stronger, societies have generally responded by calling the strong just rather than making the just strong; and his fragment describing custom as the "mystical foundation" of legal authority, where he warns that tracing a law back to its real origin is dangerous, since most laws rest on nothing better than force or habit, and exposing this threatens the order it sustains.

Suppose Pascal were shown today's system of international courts and tribunals, such as the International Criminal Court, which claim binding authority over sovereign states yet in practice can act against weak states while remaining nearly powerless against strong ones. Reasoning only from the commitments above, rather than from generic seventeenth-century piety, how would he explain this pattern, and what would he recommend doing about it? Does his warning against exposing a law's arbitrary foundations give him reason to oppose publicizing this selectivity, or can he consistently treat this case differently?
