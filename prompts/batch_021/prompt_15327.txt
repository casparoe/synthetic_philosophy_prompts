Okay so this actually happened in my neighborhood a few weeks ago and I still haven't decided who was right. Our HOA has this huge old oak tree by the clubhouse that might be rotting from the inside, and if it's actually diseased it could come down on someone's car (or worse) in the next big storm. Getting an actual certified arborist out to inspect it properly costs around $450, so instead the board just emailed photos of the trunk and canopy to all 120 households and ran a quick poll, "safe" or "not safe," majority wins. One of the board members, an electrical engineer who openly admits he knows nothing about trees but is really into statistics, argued that this was actually the smarter move, not just the cheaper one. His reasoning was something like: sure, any single person glancing at a couple photos might guess wrong, but if you get enough individual guesses, the errors cancel out on average, so 120 amateur votes will beat one expert's opinion more often then not, "that's just how the math works." Everyone kind of nodded like that settled it, and honestly it sounded pretty convincing to me at first too. I brought it up with a couple friends afterward and even they couldn't agree on whether he had a real point or was just dressing up a cost-cutting move as a clever statistical insight.

Here's the thing that's been bugging me though: a bunch of people in that group email just echoed whatever the first couple of loud replies said, instead of looking at the photos totally fresh and forming their own view. I don't have any formal background in this stuff, I just read about this kind of thing on my own time for fun, so I can't quite work out when "ask a big pile of amateurs" actually does beat "ask the one person who knows what they're doing," versus when that's just a nice-sounding argument that falls apart once you look closer. What would actually have to be true about those 120 voters for the board member's reasoning to hold up, and does our tree situation sound like it clears that bar, or not?
