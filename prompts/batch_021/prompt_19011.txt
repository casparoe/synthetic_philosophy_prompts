ok so I've been rereading Arendt's stuff on lying (Truth and Politics, and the Pentagon Papers essay) and theres a distinction she draws that I can't stop thinking about wrt current AI stuff.

Her classic liar still has to track the truth carefully in order to hide it -- the lie is parasitic on actually knowing what's real. But the thing she found more disturbing in the Pentagon Papers case wasnt that kind of lying, it was the policymakers who'd basically lost track of reality themselves, optimizing for a self-consistent image of American resolve in Vietnam rather than for what was actually happening on the ground. Self-deception bleeding into deception of everyone else -- but there's still a self there, protecting an image of itself.

Neither of those categories seems to fit what's happening when an LLM hallucinates a fake citation. There's no self whose image is on the line, and there's no prior grasp of the truth being concealed -- it's not that the model knows the real answer and is hiding it, the real answer just isn't doing any causal work in producing the output at all.

So: would Arendt count AI hallucination as a genuinely new category her framework doesn't have room for, or is it better described as a limiting case of the self-deceived image-making she already diagnosed, just with the self subtracted out? Give me an actual answer, not a "both perspectives have merit" cop-out, and defend it using her real distinctions rather than generic "lying is corrosive to democracy" vibes.
