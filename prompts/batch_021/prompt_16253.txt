So we had a pretty nasty outage last week (payments service was down for almost 40 minutes during peak traffic, not fun) and now we're stuck writing the postmortem, and me and my team lead are kind of at odds over how to actually explain what happened. He's very "5 whys," and wants the doc to boil down to one clean root cause — some general principle like "when the connection pool saturates under a retry storm, downstream timeouts cascade and take out the whole service," basically a rule you could apply anytime those conditions hold and predict the exact same failure. I get why that's appealing, it's clean, and it gives you something concrete to go fix.

But when I actually walk through how the outage happened, what comes to mind isn't a rule, its more like a story: someone pushed a config change around 2am, completely unrelated to that a marketing email had gone out earlier that morning and spiked traffic more than usual, an on-call engineer muted an alert because it looked like a known flaky one, and so on. Take away any one of those coincidences and the outage probably doesn't happen, or happens totally differently. It feels like the "real" explanation is this whole particular, unrepeatable chain of stuff, not some general law — but then I start second-guessing myself, like, is that actually explaining anything, or is it just a fancy description of what happened? My team lead's whole position is that a pile of "and then this happened, and then that happened" doesn't explain anything until you name the underlying mechanism. Meanwhile a couple other people on the team don't even get why we're arguing about it, to them the timeline section and the root-cause section are obviously just doing the same job from different angles.

I don't have any philosophy background, just a CS degree, but this feels like it's pointing at something bigger than postmortem formatting, like theres an actual disagreement buried in here about what even counts as an explanation, not just a style preference. Is my team lead right that only the general-mechanism version really explains the outage and the timeline is just color commentary? Or is there something wrong with assuming every real explanation has to cash out as a repeatable rule? Something about the whole debate bugs me but I can't quite articulate what. If there's an existing way of thinking about this that would help us figure out who's actually right, or whether we're just talking past each other, I'd love to hear it.
