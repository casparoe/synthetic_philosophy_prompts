Since retiring from a career in observational cosmology, I have taken up philosophy in earnest, and for the past few weeks I have been absorbed in the literature on anthropic reasoning. I understand the basic disagreement between the Self-Sampling Assumption and the Self-Indication Assumption, and I can recite Bostrom's presumptuous philosopher case as the standard embarrassment for the latter, but I remain unsure whether the SSA theorist is really any better off, given that the Doomsday argument seems to leave him in a scarcely less awkward position.

Could you write a dialogue between a committed SSA theorist and a committed SIA theorist who are called in, together, to counsel a team of cosmologists facing exactly that dilemma — two theories that fit the data equally well so far but imply wildly different numbers of observers? I would like the two theorists to press each other on their most uncomfortable commitments, the reference class problem on one side and the Doomsday argument on the other, genuinely engaging each other's arguments rather than talking past one another, with neither getting the better of the exchange too easily.
