This year I'm taking AP Psychology, and we recently finished a unit on the biological basis of stress that left me stuck on a question I haven't been able to resolve on my own. I get fairly intense anxiety before major tests: shaking hands, a racing heart, and sometimes blanking on material I clearly know once I'm actually taking the exam. When I asked my teacher why my body reacts this way even though a test obviously poses no physical threat, she said there is nothing wrong with the reaction itself, since it is simply my sympathetic nervous system doing exactly what it was shaped by evolution to do, namely prepare me to deal with danger; the problem is only that exams happen to trigger a system that was built for something else entirely.

A friend of mine, who gets just as anxious, thinks that explanation dodges the real issue. Her point is that if the reaction makes me perform worse on a test I would otherwise ace, then regardless of its evolutionary history, it clearly is not doing its job right now, in the only sense of "job" that should matter to me, since it works against my actual, present goal of doing well on the exam. Trying to referee this disagreement, I noticed that my teacher's answer is really about where the reaction came from and what it was originally selected to accomplish, whereas my friend's answer is about what the reaction actually accomplishes for me today, and that these two ways of judging whether something is "working properly" can point in opposite directions. The same disagreement seems to resurface whenever people argue about whether things like sugar cravings, allergic reactions, or blushing count as the body malfunctioning, as opposed to simply doing what it has always done.

So here is what I would like help with: is there a principled way to decide whose standard of proper function is the better one to apply in cases like this, or are my teacher and my friend simply answering two different questions while believing they are disagreeing about the same one? I would appreciate help figuring out who really has the stronger case, if either of them does.
