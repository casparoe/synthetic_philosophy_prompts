So a friend of mine got me stuck on that old thought experiment about torture versus dust specks a few weeks back, and I've been loosing actual sleep over it since, which is probably not normal. The setup, in case you haven't run into it: one person gets tortured horribly for fifty years, versus some number of people so enormous I can't picture it (I saw it written with triple arrows stacked between two 3's, which I did not understand even a little) each getting a single dust speck blown into their eye for a split second and then forgetting about it forever. Supposedly the "correct" answer, if you do the math properly, is that the specks are worse, because enough basically-zero harms added together eventually outweighs fifty years of torture. That felt wrong to me right away, but every discussion of it I've found online just repeats the same two camps at each other -- "the math is the math, shut up and multiply" versus "you can't just add up suffering like that" -- and nobody actually settles it.

Here's the specific thing bugging me that I haven't seen anyone actually deal with. You can build a ladder of harms between a dust speck and torture where each rung is only a tiny bit worse than the last -- speck, papercut, bad headache, twisted ankle, broken arm, and so on up to actual torture. At any two neighboring rungs, the exact same logic that's supposed to make specks beat torture says that enough people getting the lower harm is worse than one person getting the next harm up -- it's the identical argument, just applied one small step at a time instead of in one giant leap. But if that's true at every single rung (and each one individually seems just as reasonable as the original speck-vs-torture comparison), then chaining them all together forces you to accept that a big enough pile of dust specks beats actual torture -- except now you got there through a hundred "obviously true" baby steps instead of one weird jump, which somehow makes it feel both more airtight and more like a trick at the same time. It reminds me of that sand-heap paradox thing (I looked it up, I think its called the "sorites" paradox, no idea how you're even supposed to pronounce that) except wearing utilitarian clothing. I honestly can't tell if my ladder is a solid argument for just biting the bullet on dust specks, or if it's secretly a proof that you're not allowed to compare and stack up harms like that once they start being different in kind and not just in size.

Could you write me an actual essay wrestling with this -- not a "well, there are good points on both sides" cop-out, but something that takes my ladder problem seriously as part of the actual argument, works out whether it helps or wrecks the case for aggregating harms, and then commits to a real answer on torture vs. dust specks at the end and defends it. You can attach conditions to your answer if you need to, like "X, unless Y," just don't leave me without knowing which one you'd actually pick.
