Ok so this happened with my roommate a couple weeks ago and it's been bugging me since. We split all our shared household costs evenly, groceries, cleaning supplies, all of it, and track everything on Splitwise so it's not like the numbers are ambiguous. Around the third week of the month I noticed she'd barely put any money in, so I brought it up, a little annoyed. She said, and I remember the exact phrasing because it stuck with me, "I've paid for some of the groceries this month." Which, fine, technically true, she'd chipped in like $9 out of a $160 total, so not literally zero. But the way she said it made it sound like she'd covered a decent chunk, not almost nothing, and it wasn't until afterward that I realized how carefully she'd picked that word. When I called her out after actually doing the math, she got defensive and said she'd never once claimed to have paid for "half" or "most" of anything, so she never technically lied to me, and if I assumed more than that, well, that's kind of on me for reading into it.

I brought this up with two friends afterward and they landed in totally different places, which hasnt helped me figure out what I actually think. One said that if nothing she said was literally false then it just isn't a lie, full stop, no matter how sneaky the delivery was. The other said that's a cop-out, that deliberately engineering a false impression in someone's head is basically the whole point of lying, and the exact words you technically used shouldn't get you off the hook. I can't decide who's right. Is there an actual, principled difference between what my roommate did and lying to my face outright, or is "technically true but misleading" basically the same thing with better PR? And when there's a gap like this between what someone literally said and what you reasonably understood them to mean, is the responsibility for that gap more on her, for picking her words that carefully, or on me, for assuming more than she actually said?
