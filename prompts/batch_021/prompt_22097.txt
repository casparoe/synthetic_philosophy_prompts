Write a dialogue between two old friends who've been having the same argument for years (doesn't matter what it's actually about — could be politics, could be whether their favorite band went downhill). One of them keeps bringing up Aumann's agreement theorem: they've each laid out their full reasoning to the other a hundred times now, so if they're both honest and rational this just isn't supposed to be a stable place to end up. The other tries the classic escape of "we just started out with different priors, that's allowed." Have the first one push back on where that difference is even supposed to come from, given that way before either of them had seen any evidence their priors should've matched — basically Robin Hanson's point that priors need some kind of origin story — and make the second person actually answer that instead of folding. Neither should be a pushover; they need to be responding to each others actual points, not just taking turns monologuing.
