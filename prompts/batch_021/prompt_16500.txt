So I'm running my grad seminar on weakness of will this week and I've hit a recurring snag: half my students (the ones with an econ background) keep treating Davidson's old puzzle -- how can someone judge, all things considered, that A is better than B, and then just go do B anyway -- as basically solved by hyperbolic discounting. Like, they'll say "professor, it's literally just a discount curve, the hyperbolic and exponential curves cross so your ranking flips as the reward gets closer, case closed." Which, sure, that captures the preference-reversal part of akrasia nicely, but it kind of breezes past the actual philosophical puzzle, which was never really about preferences changing over time -- its about the apparent irrationality of freely acting against your own considered, in-the-moment judgment of what's best. Aristotle was already worried about exactly this (the akratic "has" the relevant knowledge but doesn't "use" it), centuries before anyone had a discount function.

Anyway, I want to needle them a little at the start of tomorrow's class. Could you write me a short joke that turns on exactly this gap -- the economists' "we solved it, it's just time-inconsistent preferences" confidence versus the philosophers still being genuinely stuck on how akrasia is even psychologically possible? I dont want some generic econ-majors-vs-philosophy-majors joke -- I want the actual punchline to depend on getting both the Davidson/Aristotle side and the hyperbolic-discounting side right, so it'd fall pretty flat for someone who only knew one half.
