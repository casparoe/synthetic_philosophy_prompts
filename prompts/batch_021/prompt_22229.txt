ok so i fell down a rabbit hole this week while researching for a book and landed on Avicenna's "flying man" argument -- the thought experiment where he imagines a person created whole in a single instant, hanging in midair with nothing to stand on, unable to see anything, his limbs not even brushing against each other, totally cut off from every sense. his point is that this guy would still know "i exist," even with zero awareness of having a body at all -- no shape, no weight, no location, nothing to point to. its supposed to show that the self is grasped directly by the mind, seperate from any awareness of the body.

here's what's bugging me as a writer: pretty much every trick fiction uses to put you "inside" a character -- sensation, imagery, even just a felt sense of where their limbs are -- is exactly what's stripped out of this scenario. could you write me a very short story, like a page, from inside the flying man's awareness at the exact moment he registers his own existence, without cheating by sneaking in sensory detail he isn't supposed to have access to? i want to know if that moment can actually be made to feel like something on the page, or if it just collapses into a philosophy lecture the second you try.
