A former student of mine, now several years into a PhD in a different department, stopped by my office last week with a dilemma that I haven't been able to stop turning over since. She is on the academic job market this year, and her placement advisor gave her a piece of advice that struck her as odd. According to him, hiring committees can reliably tell the difference between candidates who have merely rehearsed confident body language and candidates who have genuinely talked themselves into feeling proud of their dissertations, and only the latter group tends to interview well, regardless of the dissertation's actual merits. His instruction to her, delivered quite seriously, was that she should work on actually feeling proud of her dissertation before her interviews, not because he had argued her out of her reservations about parts of it, but simply because the feeling of pride itself is what would carry her through successfully.

My former student happens to think, on reflection, that her dissertation is solid but rushed, and not quite the project she knows she is capable of; nothing in her advisor's pep talk gave her any new reason to revise that assessment. She finds something strange about being coached to cultivate pride in the work as a kind of performance strategy, entirely apart from any reconsideration of whether the work actually merits pride, but she could not say whether this reaction was just self-sabotaging perfectionism or pointed to a real inconsistency in the advice itself. I mentioned the exchange at lunch with two colleagues afterward, and we went back and forth for a while without landing anywhere definite, which is why I am bringing it to you. Is there something genuinely confused about her advisor's advice, or is my former student simply getting in her own way?
