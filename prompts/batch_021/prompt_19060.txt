So Lewis's Humean account of objective chance runs into the famous 'problem of undermining futures': since the chances at a time are just whatever the best system says they are, and the best system gets picked by how well it fits the entire history of the world (future included), there's always some tiny chance the future unfolds in a way that would've made a different theory the best system instead. Lewis tried to patch this in 'Humean Supervenience Debugged' with what he calls the New Principal Principle. Can you argue that the patch doesnt actually work, and that undermining is a real, unfixable problem for Humean chance/laws rather than just an annoying technical wrinkle?
