I'm a PhD student in philosophy of physics currently drafting a dissertation chapter on the hole argument, and I keep getting stuck on how much weight to put on what I've started calling (privately) the "collapse" worry about sophisticated substantivalism. Earman and Norton's argument seems to force manifold substantivalists to choose between accepting a radical indeterminism and denying that two models — agreeing everywhere outside a "hole" but related there by an active diffeomorphism that isn't the identity — represent distinct physical possibilities. Butterfield and Brighouse take the second horn, giving up haecceitism about spacetime points in order to secure Leibniz equivalence, and Pooley has since mounted a more careful defense of this "sophisticated" substantivalism. But relationist critics — echoing a broader skepticism about whether the substantivalist/relationist distinction even survives general relativity, which traces back at least to Rynasiewicz — keep pressing the objection that once you concede there's no further fact about which bare point plays which role, you've conceded everything the relationist actually wanted, and what's left is a merely verbal dispute. I've read the standard rejoinders in the literature and, honestly, none of them have fully convinced me.

Could you write a dialogue of around 700 words between a sophisticated substantivalist and a committed relationist, staged as an argument that breaks out by the coffee urn right after a workshop talk defending the hole argument? I'd like the relationist to press the collapse objection as hard as possible, forcing the substantivalist to say exactly what explanatory or modal work "the manifold of points" is still doing once its points have no individuality beyond their qualitative role. And I'd like the substantivalist to give the best available reply rather than simply asserting that a difference remains — for instance, by engaging with the active/passive diffeomorphism distinction, or by leaning on cases, such as vacuum solutions with no matter at all, that relationism seems to struggle to even state. Please keep both speakers equally sophisticated, have them respond to each other's actual points rather than talk past one another, and feel free to let the exchange end in a genuine stalemate rather than a tidy resolution — that's honestly closer to where I think the real debate stands.
