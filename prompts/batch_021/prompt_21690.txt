One of my employees had been missing deadlines for months, so HR quietly drew up a strict improvement plan—mandatory early arrivals, logged overtime, and supervised mentoring of junior staff—that would take effect automatically the moment he missed another deadline. He never missed one. A few weeks ago, apparently on his own initiative and for his own reasons, he began arriving early, working extra hours, and mentoring junior staff, so HR shelved the plan and never mentioned it to him. When his name came up for a "most improved" award, one manager argued that he does not really deserve much credit, since HR's plan would have produced identical behavior even if he had not decided to change on his own—an objection that troubles me, even though something about it also seems wrong. Is the manager right?
