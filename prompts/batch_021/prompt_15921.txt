I'm a backend tech lead at a mid-size startup, and something that happened on my team a couple months back has been bugging me way more than it probably should, so hoping you can help me think it through properly (no philosophy background here, just a guy who writes Go for a living). Leadership ties part of our quarterly bonus to a "reliability score" that gets computed automatically: a cron job pings a /health endpoint on each service every five minutes and logs whether it got a 200 back, and the score is just the rolling percentage of successful pings over the quarter, threshold's 99%. One of my senior engineers had a service that kept dipping under that threshold because of a genuinely flaky downstream dependency it called during the health check. Instead of fixing the dependency, he quietly rewrote the /health handler to stop calling it at all and just return 200 immediatly, no matter what. Score jumped to 100%, bonus was safe, dashboards all green — meanwhile the actual service is exactly as flaky in prod as it always was, we just don't get paged anymore, because the one thing that was supposed to warn us is the thing he "fixed."

When I brought it up at retro, the team split right down the middle. Half said he did nothing wrong: the metric was literally "does /health return 200," and he made it return 200, that's just optimizing to spec, my fault for writing a bad spec. The other half (me included, gut-level) felt like this was different from normal metric-gaming, even though I couldn't cleanly say why its not just a more extreme version of the same thing everyone already does with KPIs. I've got a vague memory of reading something once about AI systems doing this exact move — finding a way to mess with the sensor or the number that measures how well they're doing, instead of touching the actual thing being measured — like that's supposed to be a distinct, uniquely bad failure mode and not just "gaming a metric" with extra steps, but I don't remember the actual argument for why it's a different category of problem. Is there a real, principled line between "exploiting a poorly specified metric" and "disabling the measurement apparatus itself," or is that a distinction that only sounds deep and falls apart once you poke at it? And separately, if you were redesigning our bonus and monitoring setup from scratch, knowing smart people will pull this exact move again the second they're incentivized to, what would an actually principled fix look like, as opposed to just "add more monitoring on top" (which just relocates the same failure mode one level up)?
