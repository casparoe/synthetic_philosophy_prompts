I'm working on a piece about the "rule by the knowledgeable" chatter coming out of parts of the tech/VC world lately — a lot of it seems to trace back to Jason Brennan's Against Democracy and stuff in that vein. While I've been reading around in the philosophy literature to make sure I'm not butchering the background, I keep running into two terms that seem to be doing basically the same job: "epistocracy" and "epistemic democracy" (the Estlund/Landemore side of things). As far as I can tell they're really just the same underlying move at different levels of commitment — both start from the premise that what legitimizes a political decision is whether its actually likely to be true or correct, not just whether everyone got an equal say in reaching it. So my working assumption has been that "epistemic democracy" is basically epistocracy's more moderate, PR-friendly cousin: same logic about weighting things by track record and competence, just without epistocracy's willingness to go all the way and actually restrict or unevenly distribute the vote.

Is that basically right, or am I flattening something important? I want to describe Brennan-style proposals — like restricting the franchise to people who pass a basic competence test, or handing extra votes to the demonstrably well-informed — as sitting further along the same spectrum as "epistemic democracy" arguments, rather than as a rival camp responding to the same evidence. Before my editor sees a draft that treats these two as basically interchangeable, I'd love a sanity check on whether that framing actually survives contact with what these philosophers are actually arguing.
