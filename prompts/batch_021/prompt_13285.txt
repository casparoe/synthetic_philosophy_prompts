At my high school, discipline runs on a points system: certain infractions (using your phone during a test, skipping a class, that kind of thing) earn you a fixed number of "hours" that you have to serve in Saturday detention before you're cleared to participate in sports or clubs again. Last month my friend Maya got six hours for being caught with her phone out during a chemistry test, but she'd already paid for a Saturday SAT prep course that same week and really didn't want to miss it. Another friend of ours, Jess, who has a completely spotless record, offered to serve the six hours in Maya's place instead, purely as a favor. Our dean actually approved this — his reasoning was that the requirement is just that six hours get served in that room, and since Jess showed up and did it, Maya's record now shows her hours as fully completed, exactly as if she had sat there herself.

This all came up not long after my AP Government class started a unit on the courts, which might be part of why it's stuck with me. A few of us argued about it at lunch without ever settling whether the dean's reasoning actually holds up. Nothing about the arrangement broke an explicit rule, and Jess clearly wasn't coerced into it, so in one sense nothing shady happened. But it still strikes me as different from something like a friend covering your parking ticket or paying a late fee on your behalf, which nobody would find remotely strange. I can't quite pin down why serving someone else's detention feels stranger than paying someone else's fine, even though the dean would probably say those are the same kind of thing: a penalty that just needs to get discharged by somebody, whoever that happens to be. Is he right that the two cases are equivalent, or is there a real difference between them that I'm just not managing to articulate? I'd really appreciate help thinking through what's actually going on here.
