I'm a retired chemist who's gotten pretty into philosophy since retiring, and lately I've fallen down a rabbit hole reading about Solomonoff induction and algorithmic information theory. I think I've finally got a rigorous handle on Occam's razor: as I understand it, Solomonoff induction works by taking every hypothesis consistent with your data, computing which one has the shortest program (i.e. the lowest Kolmogorov complexity), and just running with that single hypothesis as your prediction going forward -- the least complex explanation wins and the rest get thrown out. That seems like a beautifully rigorous version of "the simplest explanation is usually right." So when I'm comparing two competing reaction mechanisms that both fit my old kinetics data equally well, should I just pick whichever one is more compressible in that sense and disregard the other entirely, since thats supposedly what an ideal rational agent would do?
