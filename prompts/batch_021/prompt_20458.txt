I'm a novelist putting together a collection of short fiction about old philosophical fights that never really got resolved, and I want one piece set in classical India. The one I keep getting stuck on is the Nyaya argument over upamana -- whether "comparison" is really its own separate way of knowing things, alongside perception, inference and testimony, or whether it just collapses into one of those. Theres that old example of a guy whose told a gavaya (a kind of wild ox) looks like a cow, and later he's out in the forest, sees the animal, and just knows "that's a gavaya" -- and the whole fight is over what kind of cognition that actually is. Could you write me a short story that makes that example (or your own spin on it) the real engine of the plot -- maybe as a debate between a Naiyayika and a Buddhist or Mimamsa rival -- where the rival's objection gets a genuine hearing instead of a strawman? Don't just dress it up as generic "ancient wisdom" -- I want the argument itself to be something the characters actually need to win.
