So my friend Theo and I had this long back-and-forth last night that spiraled out of a law-and-philosophy seminar he's taking -- he brought up something his professor called the "doctrinal paradox," and I recognized it as a version of this thing called the "discursive dilemma" that I'd read about ages ago in a judgment-aggregation context, and we ended up arguing about it for like two hours. The classic case is three judges deciding a contract dispute: liability requires both (a) a valid contract existed and (b) the defendant breached it. Each judge is individually perfectly consistent in their own views on a and b, but if you take a majority vote on a and a majority vote on b separately and then combine them (the "premise-based" procedure), you get one verdict on liability, while if you just poll each judge's up-or-down verdict directly (the "conclusion-based" procedure) you get the opposite verdict. I find this genuinely unsettling. Theo's position was that it's basically a rigged parlor trick -- in practice a court just fixes a procedure in advance and sticks with it, so the "paradox" is a curiosity, not a real problem for group rationality.

We tried building our own version from scratch, in some non-legal setting, to see if it was actually general and not just a fact about courts -- we tried a hiring committee, then a journal's editorial board voting on whether to accept a paper -- and gave up after twenty minutes because we kept accidently constructing cases where the two procedures happened to agree anyway, which was more frustrating than either of us expected. Could you build me a clean worked example in some other non-court setting of your choosing (please, not judges or a jury)? I want actual committee members with names, actual propositions with a genuine logical dependency between them (not contract/breach again), and the vote tallies spelled out, such that premise-based and conclusion-based voting clearly diverge. And then, since this is the actual crux Theo and I never resolved: if a group just stipulates its procedure in advance, does the underlying problem actually go away, or does it just get hidden -- is there something here that shows up no matter which procedure a group commits to ahead of time?
