Okay so this actually happened to me a couple months ago and it's been bugging me ever since - especially annoying because I've spent enough time reading rationalist-adjacent blogs (SSC/ACX, LessWrong, that whole cluster) that I feel like I should be decent at thinking through weird probability setups, and yet here we are, stumped by my own life. I had a colonoscopy, which meant the usual sedative that wipes you're short-term memory - the nurse says you're actually awake and mumbling answers to routine questions for parts of it, you just never retain any of it afterward. Afterwards, a friend of mine who's a nurse (different hospital, just happens to know this stuff) told me something I hadn't known: with the sedative they typically use, for something like a third of patients it wears off partway through, so they have to give a second dose - meaning that subset of patients gets TWO separate "surface into hazy awareness, mumble through a question or two, then forget it ever happened" moments during the procedure, instead of the one moment everybody else gets. Apparently these moments are completely indistinguishable from each other and from what a one-dose patient experiences - same grogginess, same kind of routine question, no way to introspect your way into figuring out which moment you're in or how many you'll end up having total.

This somehow turned into a whole argument with my friend over dinner that neither of us could let go of. Setup we agreed on: going in, you know that roughly 1/3 of patients need the second dose (and so get 2 of these moments) while 2/3 don't (and get just 1). Now suppose you could interview yourself during one of these brief hazy moments, before anything else is revealed, and ask "what's the probability I'm a two-dose patient, just given that I'm having this kind of moment at all?" Her take is that it has to just be 1/3, flat - thats the base rate, and merely having one of these moments can't tell you anything, since you were guaranteed at least one either way. I don't think that's right, because two-dose patients are effectively "raising their hand" twice as often, so the odds should shift away from 1/3 - my gut says it lands at exactly 50/50, but I couldn't fully nail down why, and she wasn't buying it regardless. We ended up half-jokingly betting $20 on it, even though there's obviously no clean way to ever collect. Who actually has the better argument here - is there a real principled answer to be had, or is this genuinely more of an agree-to-disagree situation, even for two people who probably think about this stuff more than average?
