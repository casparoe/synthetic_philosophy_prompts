So I finished my second novel a few months back — it's already through copyedits, the cover's designed, pub date's locked for March (it's a quiet literary thing about a lighthouse keeper, nothing flashy). Now I'm just in the waiting phase. My editor forwarded me the first batch of trade reviews — Kirkus, Booklist, that sort of thing — and said I could read them now if I wanted, or just wait till they go public. Normally I'd dive right in, but this time I really don't want to, because there is nothing left for me to change. The book is done done, printed, out of my hands. If a review rips into the third act I can't go fix it, I'll just be stuck stewing on it for weeks while I'm suppose to be drafting book three.

One of the guys in my writers' group, who did a stats PhD before he dropped out to write thrillers, thinks I'm being ridiculous. His argument is basically: since the reviews are free to look at, and I could always just choose to ignore whatever's in them, there's no way looking can leave me worse off than not looking — atleast it does nothing, and it might even help going into the next book. He swears this isn't just his opinion but close to a proven result, some kind of theorem saying a perfectly rational person should always want more free evidence before making any decision, because more information only ever gives you more options and never forces your hand. I get why that sounds right in the abstract, but it still feels off for my actual situation, like he's leaving something out about what it's actually like to be a person who has to sit with a bad review for a month with zero ability to act on it. Is he right that I'm just being irrational here, or is there an actual gap in his reasoning that matches my gut feeling?
