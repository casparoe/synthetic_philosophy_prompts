Alright, so I'm on the technical steering committee for a mid-size open source project (5 of us), and we just ran into a voting situation that's been bugging me. I think it's some kind of known paradox but I have no idea what it's called or how people usually deal with it.

We had a big RFC to decide on (adding a new plugin API). Our bylaws say a proposal passes only if it (a) gets a security sign-off AND (b) is backwards compatible. Instead of a straight up-or-down vote, we each voted on the two sub-criteria seperately and let the rule decide the outcome. Here's how it went:

- Me: security=yes, backcompat=yes -> accept
- Sam: security=yes, backcompat=yes -> accept
- Priya: security=yes, backcompat=no -> reject
- Dev: security=no, backcompat=yes -> reject
- Ravi: security=no, backcompat=no -> reject

Majority says security=yes (3-2) and majority says backcompat=yes (3-2), so by our own rule the RFC passes. But when Sam went around and asked people to just say "accept or reject the RFC overall" as a gut check, it came out 2 accept vs 3 reject. Same five people, same votes basically, opposite answer depending on how you ask the question.

Now half the committee wants to scrap voting on sub-criteria and just do a straight up-or-down vote on the RFC itself, and the other half says that defeats the whole point of having explicit criteria in our bylaws in the first place. Both camps are pretty dug in and just keep repeating their own point instead of dealing with the other side's.

Can you write the memo I'd send round before next week's meeting? I want it to actually recommend one voting procedure for us to standardize on going forward (not just re-litigate this one RFC), give real reasons a non-philosopher committee member would find convincing, and take the strongest version of the side you're NOT recommending seriously instead of just asserting logic is on your side. Keep it to something like half a page to a page.
