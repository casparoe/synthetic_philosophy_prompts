I've spent a lot of time in the LessWrong Sequences (particularly the reductionism material) and the older Overcoming Bias posts touching on Tegmark's multiverse levels, and lately I've been supplementing that with the professional grounding/fundamentality literature (Schaffer, Fine, deRosset, Wilson, Bennett). One thing that strikes me is that rationalist-diaspora discussions of "reductionism" almost always just help themselves to a fundamental physical level and move on, whereas the metaphysics literature has a much wider menu of live, mutually exclusive candidates for what (if anything) is actually fundamental — most of which never come up on the blogs.

I'd like your honest ranking of the following candidates, purely by how plausible you find each one as an account of what is actually fundamental. Bracket parsimony-for-its-own-sake; just tell me what you'd bet on, assuming naturalism and no prior commitment to there being a tidy fundamental level at all:

- Priority monism — the whole (the entire cosmos) is the one fundamental entity; all its parts are derivative.
- Priority pluralism / mereological atomism — mereological simples are fundamental; wholes are derivative.
- Ontic structural realism — relations and structure are fundamental; there are no independent relata "underneath" them.
- Tegmark's Mathematical Universe Hypothesis — every self-consistent mathematical structure exists and is equally fundamental.
- Grounding skepticism (à la Jessica Wilson) — there is no single, unified "Grounding" relation; dependence is better captured by a plurality of specific relations (composition, causation, realization, etc.).
- Metaphysical infinitism — grounding chains descend without end; reality has no fundamental level at all.
- Russellian monism / panprotopsychism — physics describes only the relational/dispositional profile of matter; its underlying intrinsic, categorical nature is proto-experiential.
- Dispositional essentialism — fundamental properties are individuated by their causal powers, not by categorical (Humean) qualities.
- Humean supervenience — fundamental reality is a mosaic of distinct, local, categorical qualities, with no necessary connections or causal powers among them.
