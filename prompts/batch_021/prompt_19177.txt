Hey, quick question that's been bugging me — I'm co-teaching a graduate seminar on philosophy of cognitive science with a colleague from psych, and we just got to Friston's free energy principle. My understanding is that the theory basically says organisms act so as to minimize surprise, i.e. keep their sensory input as predictable as possible. But if thats really the whole story, wouldn't the optimal agent just find a dark, silent room with zero stimulation and stay there forever, since nothing is more predictable than that? Real animals do the opposite — they explore, get bored, take dumb risks, seek out novelty — so why do so many cognitive scientists and philosophers still treat this as a serious unifying theory of brain and behavior instead of a neat bit of math that gets the actual behavior backwards? Is there something im missing, or is this as damning as it sounds?
