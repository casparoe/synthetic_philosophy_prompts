I've been working through the corrigibility literature that circulates on the Alignment Forum and LessWrong, and I've come to think the shutdown problem is more resolved than people give it credit for. The MIRI corrigibility paper (Soares, Fallenstein, Yudkowsky, and Armstrong) spends a great deal of effort hand-crafting a utility function that makes an agent indifferent to being switched off without breaking its other incentives, but Hadfield-Menell, Dragan, Abbeel, and Russell's later off-switch game shows that this hand-crafting is unnecessary: give the agent ordinary Bayesian uncertainty about its own reward function, let it treat a human's choice to hit the switch as evidence about that function, and deference falls straight out as the expected-utility-maximizing policy. Given that this result is nearly a decade old, why do people in the alignment community still write as though a fully general solution to the shutdown problem is an open question, rather than something the off-switch game essentially settled?
