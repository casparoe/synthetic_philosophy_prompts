I chair the judging panel for a small first-novel prize -- five of us, all novelists, way too invested for what's basically a $2,000 purse. Back in spring we agreed in writing that a book only wins if it clears two seperate bars: the prose has to sing, AND the ending has to earn everything before it, not just one or the other.

We just tallied the vote on this year's front runner. Prose sings: 3 yes, 2 no. Ending earns it: 4 yes, 1 no. So criterion by criterion it clears our own rule. But when I asked each judge point blank "should this book win," it came back 2 yes, 3 no -- because each of the three no judges loved exactly one of the two things and not the other, so applying our own rule to their own ballot, no is exactly where each of them should land. They're not being inconsistent; the panel just doesn't add up the same way twice.

Now we're split down the middle: half the panel wants to just honor the up-or-down vote, half says we signed off on the two-bar rule in writing and have to honor that instead. I owe the foundation's board a memo by Friday on what happens to this year's book and what our standing procedure should be going forward. Write me that memo -- an actual recommendation, not a split-the-difference one, that still takes the up-or-down camp's objection seriously instead of just overruling them.
