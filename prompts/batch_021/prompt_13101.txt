I'm a novelist assembling a small collection of short fiction that dramatizes single hard ideas from decision theory, in the tradition of Greg Egan's or Ted Chiang's conceptual science fiction. For the next piece, I want to work with the idea of an acausal threat: coercion from an agent one will never meet and cannot causally interact with at all, but which can predict one's choices accurately enough that merely being the kind of agent who yields to pressure is itself dangerous. I'm especially interested in the proposed countermeasure of adopting a surrogate goal — committing publicly and verifiably, in advance, to respond only to threats against some invented, otherwise unimportant stake, and never to threats against what one truly values, so that a rational threatener has no reason to aim anywhere else. Write a short story of around 1,500 words centered on a character — human, corporate, or artificial — who adopts this strategy, dramatizing both the mechanics of the ploy and the cost of spending one's days guarding a value one knows is a decoy.
