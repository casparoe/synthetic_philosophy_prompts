I write about science and ideas for a living, which is apparently why my friend Mara has asked me, rather than anyone in either family, to give the toast at her wedding next month. Mara is finishing a dissertation on Bertrand Russell, and she and her fiancé have said openly — including in the epigraph they chose for the wedding program — that they've built their marriage on the argument of his *Marriage and Morals*: that sexual jealousy is a habit of mind a sufficiently rational, unpossessive couple can mostly unlearn, not a law of nature, and that strict exclusivity isn't what makes a marriage last. Roughly half the room is Mara's philosophy department, who will recognize exactly what's being invoked and will also remember that Russell's own attempt to live this way — in his second marriage, to Dora Russell — collapsed amid precisely the jealousy and recrimination he had argued a rational couple could rise above. The other half are grandparents and family friends who have never heard of Russell and would be quietly appalled by a toast that sounded like a defense of open marriage. Write the toast: about five minutes long, warm enough for the second group, honest enough that the first doesn't wince, built around one light, not solemn, moment where you admit that the philosopher didn't, in the end, manage to live by his own theory, and shaped so the whole thing reads as an act of love rather than a lecture or a warning.
