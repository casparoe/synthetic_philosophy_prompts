I've been doing genealogy as a hobby for a couple years now, building out my family tree in a big spreadsheet, and I got stuck on something a few weeks back that I can't shake. Curious what you think of it, I don't have any background in this stuff so I can't tell if I'm onto something or just being dumb.

The way I store data is rows like [Person A] - [relationship] - [Person B], e.g. "Maureen - married - Tom" or "Tom - parent of - Sara". Simple enough. But then I started wondering what actually makes a row like that SAY something, instead of just being three names sitting next to each other. If I hand you the three items "Maureen", "married", "Tom" with nothing else, that's not automatically the fact that Maureen is married to Tom, its just three items in a pile. Something has to glue "married" onto the other two in the right way (and the order matters for stuff like "parent of" - Tom being Sara's parent isnt the same fact as Sara being Tom's parent).

So naturally I thought: fine, add a 4th column explaining how the relationship-word connects to the other two. But then THAT connection needs its own explanation of how it connects to the first three things, and that needs another one, forever. It never bottoms out. And this isn't just a spreadsheet quirk, it seems to infect any sentence at all - "the cat sat on the mat" has the exact same problem, what makes "sat on" actually do the relating instead of just being a third word sitting next to "cat" and "mat"?

Only fix I could come up with: relationship-words aren't the same kind of thing as name-words. "Tom" just sits there and refers to a guy. "Married" or "parent of" isn't like that, its whole job is to connect things, so it doesn't need extra glue, that's just built into what it is. But then I got suspicious of my own answer, because can't you ask the exact same glue-question about the connecting itself? Feels like I hid the regress instead of actually stopping it.

I poked around online and apparently this is a real old problem, something called "Bradley's regress" and the "unity of the proposition," and Frege and Russell are supposedly mixed up in it somehow, but I don't have the background to know if my "different kind of thing" move is basically the standard response people give, or a total dead end.

So: where exactly is my reasoning shaky? Is my fix basically reinventing something Frege or Russell already tried, and if so did they actually get it to work where I didn't? And is there a cleaner solution out there that I'm just not aware of?
