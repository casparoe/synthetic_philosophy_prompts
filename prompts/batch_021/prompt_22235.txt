For the past few months, as part of a dialectical behavior therapy program, I have been practicing real-time emotion tracking: several times a day I pause and try to note, exactly as it is happening, which emotion I am in and how intense it is, which is especially hard for states that build up gradually, like irritation shading into anger or unease shading into anxiety. A housemate finishing a philosophy MA recently told me that this whole exercise rests on a mistaken assumption, once you take Timothy Williamson's anti-luminosity argument seriously: since no non-trivial mental condition is luminous, and the KK principle fails precisely for gradually-varying states like these, there is no moment at which I can actually be catching the emotion as it happens, and even if I somehow were, I couldn't know that I was. Given that, would I be better off abandoning real-time ratings altogether in favor of retrospective end-of-day summaries, which at least would not be pretending to a kind of in-the-moment self-knowledge that turns out to be impossible?
