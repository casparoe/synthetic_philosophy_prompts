I'm a philosophy PhD student currently on the academic job market, and a piece of strategic advice from my advisor keeps nagging at me. I hold an offer from University A with a ten-day response deadline, while University B — my actual first choice — is still deliberating with no announced timeline. My advisor suggested I email B's search committee simply to mention A's deadline, since that fact alone should pressure them to decide faster or sweeten whatever offer they eventually make; nothing in the email would be false, since I really do face that deadline. But the entire strategic point of sending it is for the committee to conclude that I might accept A if they are too slow — a conclusion I privately know to be false, since I have already decided I would take B over A no matter when they respond. So every sentence in the email would be true, even though the message as a whole is designed to leave the committee with a false impression. Is there a genuine moral difference between doing this and simply telling them I am leaning toward accepting A, or does my hesitation about literal truthfulness collapse into a mere technicality once you examine it closely?
