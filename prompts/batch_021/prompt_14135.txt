I'm taking a seminar this term on convention and the emergence of social norms, and I'd like your help working through a comparison for our next session. Lewis's book *Convention* defines a convention as one of several coordination equilibria in a recurring coordination problem, sustained by common knowledge among rational agents, and he builds arbitrariness -- the requirement that some alternative regularity could equally have taken hold -- into the very definition. Brian Skyrms, working with the same sender-receiver signaling games Lewis used to model linguistic meaning, shows that the same kind of regularity can instead emerge through blind evolutionary dynamics or simple reinforcement learning, with no common knowledge required at all. Does Skyrms's story vindicate Lewis's analysis of what a convention is while just replacing his account of how conventions arise, or does the fact that evolutionary dynamics often make one equilibrium far more likely than its rivals -- what Peyton Young calls "stochastic stability" -- undercut the arbitrariness Lewis builds into the concept itself? I'd like your own verdict, not just a summary of both positions.
