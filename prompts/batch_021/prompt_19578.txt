I'm writing a dissertation chapter on spacetime substantivalism and want to pressure-test the standard "sophisticated substantivalist" escape from the hole argument. Sophisticated substantivalists, following Butterfield and Brighouse, grant Leibniz equivalence—treating diffeomorphic models as representing the very same physical possibility—while still insisting that spacetime points are genuine substantival individuals; they simply deny that haecceitistic differences between such models are physically significant. Rynasiewicz and others have suggested that this move leaves the substantivalist with nothing left to disagree with the relationalist about, reducing the dispute to a merely verbal one. I'd like you to construct the strongest version of that deflationary argument: show why every strategy a sophisticated substantivalist might use to locate a genuine, non-haecceitistic difference between her position and relationalism—whether by appeal to primitive trans-world identity, modal realism about unactualized possibilia, or some other account of individuation—fails to recover a substantive metaphysical disagreement.
