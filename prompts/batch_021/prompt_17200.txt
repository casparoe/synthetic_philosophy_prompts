ok so i've kind of fallen down an effective altruism rabbit hole lately and found this objection to consequentialism called "cluelessness" that i can't stop thinking about. the idea is that consequentialism says whether an act is right depends on its actual consequences, but basically every action ripples out into these massive unpredictable effects on the future (like even something as small as donating to a bednet charity ends up changing who has kids with who and when, which changes who even exists down the line) and we have almost no way to know if those ripple effects are good or bad overall. i think it was hilary greaves (might be misremembering) who split this into "simple" cluelessness, where the unknown effects are basically random so they cancel out and dont matter, vs "complex" cluelessness, where we do have some lopsided evidence about the effects but its too messy/unsystematic to actually use — and thats supposedly the version thats actually scary.
