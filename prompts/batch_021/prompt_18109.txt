I'm on the board of our small animal rescue (all volunteer, no city funding). A few years back we started putting our live release rate on every newsletter and grant application because that's the number our biggest donor and the state grant program actually care about, and its up around 97% now. Thing is, I've noticed we quietly stopped being open-admission -- we turn away alot of the "hard" cases now (old, sick, aggressive, the summer kitten litters) so they don't hurt the number, and most of those animals just end up at the county shelter, which still takes everything and still euthanizes. So our number looks amazing but I don't actually think fewer animals are dying overall, we just made sure it's not our paperwork that says so. We've also got a couple dogs that have been sitting in a cage here for two-plus years now instead of being put down, and honestly I'm not sure that's better for them either. Half the board wants to keep chasing the number since its in our mission statement and the donor asks for it by name every year; the other half (me included) thinks the number itself has become the problem, but nobody agrees on what we'd do instead. Can you write the memo I'd bring to the board meeting -- it needs to actually recommend something we can vote on, not just list pros and cons, and it has to take the "this is what our donors and mission care about" side seriously instead of just brushing it off.
