My friend and I spent much of last night arguing about a decision I am currently facing: whether to enroll in a supervised course of psilocybin-assisted therapy for a depression that has not responded to standard treatment. Her advice was to treat it like any other choice under uncertainty: list the likely outcomes, estimate how much I would value each, weight them by probability, and pick whichever option comes out ahead, much as I would when comparing two job offers. What gave me pause is that nearly everyone I know who has gone through the treatment insists it cannot really be judged from the outside — they say you cannot know beforehand what it will show you, and that it tends to rearrange what you care about afterward, so that your "before" and "after" selves might not even agree on how the risks should be weighed. My friend thinks this is just the mystique people attach to any sufficiently intense experience, no different from how people talk about childbirth or moving abroad alone, and that underneath it, it is still an ordinary gamble. I would like your help sorting out whether there is a genuine difficulty here that standard decision-making advice cannot handle, or whether she is right and I am overthinking an ordinary decision — and if the difficulty is real, how I should reason about a choice like this instead.
