I'm on my college's honor council (the student-faculty board that hears cheating cases) and next week's case has turned into a real fight. Our code has a standard non-toleration clause: if you know about a violation and don't report it, that's a violation too. A senior found out her freshman brother faked data in a lab report, tried to talk him into turning himself in, he refused, and she let it go after that. He got caught anyway a month later. Now we're deciding what happens to her.

Half the council wants the clause applied exactly as written, no exceptions, since the moment 'but he's my brother' counts as an excuse you've basically told everyone reporting is optional if they love the person enough. The other half keeps citing Confucius - the Analects bit where a governor brags about a guy who turned in his own dad for stealing a sheep and Confucius says real uprightness is fathers and sons covering for each other, not that - plus the 'remonstrate gently but don't defy' line about serving parents, which someone pointed out fits her better anyway since she did push back on him first. One member who took a comparative ethics seminar keeps insisting this isn't just 'family loyalty vs. the rules' but a genuinely different structure, 'role ethics,' where the relationship is supposedly constitutive of who you are rather than a duty layered on top of an already-existing self, so 'just be impartial about your brother' isn't even coherent advice on that view. Somebody else made the obligatory 'this is just Mencius vs. Mozi again, impartial love makes you fatherless' joke, which is a bit much but not wrong.

Can you draft the memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting? I need an actual recommendation, not 'here's two views' - what happens to her under the code as written, and whether we add some kind of family exception going forward. Take the reporting-collapse worry seriously too, since its the strongest card the no-exceptions side has.
