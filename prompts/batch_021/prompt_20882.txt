I'm a retired experimental physicist who's spent retirement reading way more mathematical logic and AI safety material than is probably healthy (my kids call it a late midlife crisis). That's somehow landed me on the technical advisory panel of a small AI-safety nonprofit. This week we owe a memo to a team proposing a "tiling" architecture -- a self-modifying agent that only rewrites its own code, or launches a successor, after formally proving the new version keeps some safety property intact. Our resident logician says this runs straight into the Löbian obstacle: by Löb's theorem, a proof system can't certify from the inside that its own proofs, or an equally powerful successor's, are generally trustworthy without already being able to prove the thing outright -- so there's a real hole in bootstrapping an unlimited chain of self-verifying successors this way. Half the panel wants to reject on those grounds, the other half thinks its a fixable engineering wrinkle. I need our memo back before Thursday's meeting -- could you draft it: a genuine recommendation (approve, reject, or approve with conditions) with reasoning the team could act on, and don't just wave away the logician's point even if we don't fully side with her.
