I've been a longtime lurker on rationalist-adjacent blogs and forums, and something that happened to me at work last month has been bothering me in a way none of that reading has quite equipped me to resolve, so I wanted to lay it out here and get a second opinion.

I am a backend engineer, and I had spent most of a day on a genuinely tricky piece of concurrency code, tracing every branch by hand until I reached about as high a level of confidence as I ever reach about anything technical. Just before I opened the pull request, the most senior engineer on my team walked past, glanced at my diff for perhaps ten seconds, said, "there's a race condition somewhere in that locking, I'd hold off," and then disappeared into a meeting before I could ask her anything further. In three years of working with her, I cannot recall a single instance of her making an offhand claim like that and turning out to be wrong. I spent the following hour going back through the code specifically hunting for whatever she might have meant, and I still have not found it: shown the diff cold, with her comment removed, I would sign off on it exactly as confidently as I did the first time.

So I now seem to be holding two things at once. Looking directly at the code, it still seems correct to me, with exactly the same force it did before she said anything. But I also believe, on reflection, that given her track record and how little information a ten-second glance can actually contain, I am probably wrong, and that merging right now would be a mistake. What I cannot pin down is what my actual belief about the code is supposed to be in that situation, or whether it even makes sense to hold both attitudes at once: that the code is correct, and that I should not act as though it is correct. It reminds me faintly of arguments I have come across for why two honest reasoners should not knowingly agree to disagree, but that is always framed as two people comparing notes, whereas this is just me against a fact about my own unreliability, which feels like a different sort of problem. Before I decide whether to hold off on merging, I would like to understand whether holding both attitudes at once is simply a mistake on my part, or something a careful reasoner can legitimately do, and why.
