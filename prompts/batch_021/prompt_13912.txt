Question (10 points): "The COMPAS recidivism-risk controversy is often summarized as a clash between two families of fairness criteria that cannot both be satisfied once re-offense rates differ across groups. Identify the two families of criteria at stake, state as precisely as you can the condition under which they can be jointly satisfied, and explain why the resulting tension is a mathematical fact about the criteria themselves rather than a defect that a better dataset or a better-tuned model could fix."

Student answer: "Northpointe defended COMPAS on the grounds that it was well calibrated: within any given risk decile, Black and white defendants reoffended at essentially the same rate, so the tool satisfied predictive parity. ProPublica's investigation instead focused on equalized odds, and found that COMPAS violated it in a specific way: Black defendants who did not reoffend were far more likely than white non-reoffenders to be rated high risk, while white defendants who did reoffend were more likely than Black reoffenders to be rated low risk — a higher false positive rate for Black defendants and a higher false negative rate for white defendants.

Kleinberg, Mullainathan, and Raghavan's analysis of the COMPAS data showed that this was not a fixable flaw. Stated precisely, calibration, equal false positive rates, and equal false negative rates can all be achieved together only when the classifier is a perfect predictor of the outcome. Intuitively, calibration forces the score to track the true reoffense rate within each group, so if Black defendants reoffend at a higher rate in the data, a calibrated score will assign high scores to a larger share of Black defendants than white defendants; among Black defendants who don't go on to reoffend, that raises the false positive rate mechanically. So the disagreement between Northpointe and ProPublica was never really about whose statistics were correct — both descriptions of COMPAS's behavior were accurate simultaneously, and no amount of retraining could have satisfied both criteria at once given the underlying gap in base rates."
