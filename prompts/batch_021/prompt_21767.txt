So this happened at work last week and I still can't stop thinking about it. We're choosing a fraud-detection API to switch to. Vendor A's been around for years and publishes its false-positive rate every quarter, steady at 4%. Vendor B is a newer startup that won't share any numbers at all — could be way better than that, could be way worse, literally nobody outside the company has the data.

Two guys on my team, Devon and Marco, got into it over Slack about which one to pick (I was just watching, not involved):

Devon: we should go with Vendor A, since with B we genuinely have no idea what we'd be getting, and that's too risky to sign off on.

Marco: that's not really an argument by itself — if we truly have "no idea," our best guess for B should be centered around 4% too, by symmetry, so we should be indifferent, not prefer A. Refusing to put a number on it and calling it "too risky" is basically the Ellsberg paradox, its a documented bias, not a reason.

Devon: ok, but even if the expected value nets out the same on average, if B turns out to be the bad draw, say a double-digit error rate, that's a client relationship we don't get back, and unlike with A we can't put a ceiling on how bad it could get. Weighting that downside more isn't bias, its just being sensible about unknown unknowns.

Marco: where's "double-digit" even coming from, you just picked a scary-sounding number out of thin air. And that move works for literally any unknown option, you can always invent a worse worst case and never switch off whatever's already known. If you're gonna weight worst cases like that you need a principled reason for the number, and for why it applies here and not everywhere, otherwise its just status quo bias with extra steps.

They never really resolved it. I looked up the Ellsberg paradox afterward and get the rough idea but honestly can't tell who's actually winning at each step here. Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who's got the better of it at each point, plus whatever you think both of them are missing?
