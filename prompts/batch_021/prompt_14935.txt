Now that I'm retired from the bench I've taken up reading philosophy, and a daft little argument at home last week won't leave me alone. My grandson came home and announced, quite confidently, that his teacher told the class Everest grows a few millimetres every year because of the tectonic plates. My wife just believed it outright, no questions asked, while I caught myself wanting to see the actual measurements before accepting it - and when she called me out for being difficult, I couldn't really explain why, since I don't think either the teacher or the boy was lying to anyone. Then it struck me that whatever source I went and checked would just be somebody else's word too, so I'm not sure where "checking for yourself" is even supposed to stop. Was my wife basically right that taking someone's say-so is already enough to know something, or was my old lab instinct to want independant confirmation the more defensible one here?
