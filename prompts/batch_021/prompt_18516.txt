So in my school's philosophy club we do this thing every week where the sponsor teacher (Mr. Halloran) puts up a weird logic puzzle and we argue about it for like 40 minutes. This week's was about conditional statements and it turned into this whole back-and-forth between two members, Kavi and Ren, that never really got resolved and its been bugging me since.

The setup: there's graffiti on the gym wall, and everyone agrees it was either Danny or Priya who tagged it (not both, and nobody else had access). Mr. Halloran put up two sentences, called (a) the "indicative" one and (b) the "counterfactual" one, and asked if they actually say the same thing:
(a) "If Danny didn't tag the wall, Priya did."
(b) "If Danny hadn't tagged the wall, Priya would have."

Kavi said obviously yes — both sentences are just saying "one of them's guilty, and it's not Danny, so it's Priya," and swapping "didn't/did" for "hadn't/would have" is just a grammar thing that doesn't touch the logical content (move 1). Ren disagreed hard: she said you can totally believe (a) while doubting (b), becuase (a) is about what we can conclude from the evidence we actually have (Danny-or-Priya, and the footage clears Danny, so it's Priya), while (b) asks what would've happened in a scenario where Danny never did it at all — and maybe in that scenario Priya never even picks up the can either, since she only did it because Danny dared her. So a world where Danny's innocent might also be a world where Priya's innocent (move 2). Kavi came back with: if (a) is true, then in every situation where Danny's not the culprit, Priya is — that's literally what (a) says — so Ren can't just invent a Danny-innocent scenario where Priya's also innocent, that would make (a) false too, not just (b) (move 3). Ren answered that (a) isn't "true" in that strong, holds-across-every-possible-scenario sense — we only assert it because it's overwhelmingly likely given what we currently know, not because we've ruled out every conceivable way Danny could be innocent, including ones that clear Priya too (move 4). Kavi said fine, but then that just shows (a) is shaky in exactly the way Ren says (b) is, so she can't lean on (a) as solid ground while calling (b) the shaky one (move 5). Ren said there's still an asymmetry: (a) stays anchored to the actual world and our actual evidence, while (b) makes you imagine going back and rewriting history, and once you do that, other stuff you were relying on (like Priya's motive) is free to change too, which is just a different question than what (a) is asking (move 6).

They both refused to budge and it kind of fizzled when the bell rang, which bugs me because I think they were each onto something real, not just one of them.
