Ok so I fell down a rabbit hole this week that started with rereading Eliezer's old "cartoon guide to Löb's theorem" post and somehow ended with Gödel's 1951 Gibbs lecture, and now I've got a question I can't shake even though its probably unanswerable. Quick recap in case I'm mangling it (not a logician, just a longtime lurker): the Löbian obstacle that Yudkowsky and Herreshoff formalized in the tiling agents paper says a formal reasoner basically can't derive "whatever my successor proves using my own axioms is actually true" as a general schema, because that's exactly the shape Löb's theorem forbids -- proving it for arbitrary statements would let the system prove everything, i.e. it'd be inconsistent. So a sound agent can never fully vouch for a same-strength successor from the inside, and the workarounds people reach for (parametric polymorphism, probabilistic reflection, that kind of thing) all give up either some strength or some certainty to route around it. I mentioned this to a friend doing a logic PhD and she just shrugged and said the Gödel connection is overrated internet-philosophy stuff, which bugged me enough to want a second opinion.

Here's the thing though: Gödel's own disjunction from that lecture -- either the human mind outruns every finite formal system, or there exist Diophantine problems absolutely undecidable for us -- sure looks like a sibling of the same self-reference machinery to me. So, two questions. First, do you think Gödel would've read the Löbian obstacle as more evidence for the "mind isn't mechanical" horn of his own disjunction, or would he have insisted it cuts the other way and shows humans hit the identical wall whenever they try to certify their own future reasoning? Second, given how cagey he was about airing his more speculative philosophical views while he was alive, do you reckon he'd have been quietly sympathetic to the whole MIRI-style project of formalizing self-trust for AI, or would he have waved it off as a fact about mechanical systems with nothing to say about real (maybe non-mechanical) minds?
