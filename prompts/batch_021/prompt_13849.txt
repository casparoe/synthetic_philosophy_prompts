For our Ethics Bowl team's case on disaster-triage protocols, I've been reading John Taurek's paper "Should the Numbers Count?" He argues that we are not morally required to save the greater number of people rather than the smaller number when we can only save one group. His reasoning is that although it makes sense to weigh two different injuries that might happen to the same person against one another, there is no comparable basis for adding the losses of separate people into one greater loss. Since this seems to establish that favoring the larger group is a mistake that no defensible moral theory could accept, doesn't it follow that any triage protocol directing first responders toward the larger of two groups is actually unethical, and that they should instead flip a coin whenever they must choose between groups of unequal size?
