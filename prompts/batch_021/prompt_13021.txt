As a PhD student in cognitive science, I keep encountering papers that invoke Karl Friston's free energy principle and predictive processing accounts of perception (as developed by philosophers such as Hohwy and Clark) almost interchangeably, as though the latter is simply the former applied to cortex. I would like a sharper picture of how the two actually relate. Please compare them directly: is predictive processing—with its commitments to hierarchical message-passing, precision-weighted prediction error, and specific claims about cortical microcircuitry—a strict consequence of variational free energy minimization, or does it import empirical content that the free energy principle, understood as a general claim about self-organizing systems bounded by a Markov blanket, does not by itself supply? Given whatever distinction you draw, which of the two frameworks would actually be undermined if cortical recordings failed to show the predicted error units, and which would emerge unscathed? Justify your answer.
