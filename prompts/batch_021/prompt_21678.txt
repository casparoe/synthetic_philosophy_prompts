A few months ago I moved from a technical research role into AI governance work, and one idea that keeps coming up in the multi-agent safety literature is the so-called commitment race: the observation that a credible, irreversible commitment can function as bargaining power, since whichever party binds itself first forces everyone else to react to a fait accompli. This gives agents capable of self-binding an incentive to commit earlier, faster, and more drastically than their rivals — even though the same haste and irreversibility that make a commitment powerful are what make it reckless, foreclosing the reflection that might catch a catastrophic miscalculation or an unwinnable standoff. Watching two organizations trade increasingly public, increasingly hard-to-retract pledges over a contested model release last month made the idea feel considerably less academic than it had in seminar rooms. I would like to see this dynamic explored in narrative rather than in another position paper: please write a short story of no more than 1,500 words, featuring whatever kind of agents you find most compelling (people, states, corporations, or AIs), whose plot turns on a commitment race — ideally with at least one party who understands the trap clearly and still cannot step outside it.
