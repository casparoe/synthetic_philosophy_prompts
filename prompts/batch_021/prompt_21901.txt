So I've gone down a bit of a rabbit hole on some decision-theory/AI-safety blogs about "acausal threats" and this idea called "surrogate goals." As far as I can tell: if a super advanced agent worries about being threatened or blackmailed by some other agent it has zero actual causal contact with (no back-and-forth, no history, just both sides reasoning about what the other would logically do), the standard move is to precommit to never cave to any threat, since that kills the whole incentive to threaten you in the first place. The surrogate goal trick adds a twist - you also invent a fake goal you secretly don't care about and precommit to caving on THAT one instead, so a rational threatener aims at the decoy rather than at what you actually value. Honestly this sounds to me like classic Schelling-style commitment/deterrence stuff just mixed with an old fashioned decoy trick, dressed up as some brand new insight. But then again, Schelling's whole framework kind of assumes some observable reputation or ongoing interaction to make a commitment credible, and in the acausal case there isn't any of that going on - so maybe it really is doing something different? Can you properly compare surrogate goals with classic Schelling-type commitment/deterrence reasoning, and tell me stright up whether surrogate goals is genuinely doing something novel given the acausal setup, or if that's overselling it?
