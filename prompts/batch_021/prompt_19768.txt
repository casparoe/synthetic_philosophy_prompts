My great-uncle Walt died last week at 94. He flew supply planes in Korea, then spent the next seventy years as a dead serious amateur Hegelian who'd corner you at thanksgiving to explain that war isn't a tragedy you just endure -- it actually keeps a nation's ethical life from going stagnant, the way wind keeps a calm sea from turning foul -- and that Kant's whole perpetual-peace, league-of-nations idea was a lovely dream no sovereign state could ever really be bound by. He had a paperback Philosophy of Right held together with tape and was not kidding around about any of this. Before he got sick he told my mom his eulogy better not turn into either "he died so we'd be free" or "war is hell, so senseless," because both would be lying about what he actually thought. Thats me now, and im a novelist, not a philosopher, so I'm scared I'll default to one of those two anyway. My cousin Renata, who did two tours and hates everything about war now, is going to be sitting right up front. Can you draft the eulogy -- five minutes or so, read aloud -- so it actually comforts that room, her included, without softening what Walt believed?
