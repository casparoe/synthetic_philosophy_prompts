Q: Some intro treatments say the Church-Turing thesis has basically been proven, since Turing computability, λ-definability, and general recursiveness all turn out to pick out the exact same class of functions. Explain why most people working on the history/philosophy of computability reject that framing. Then say something about what the thesis does and doesn't license us to conclude -- does it have anything to do with why the halting problem is undecidable, or with why some problems can't be solved efficiently?

Student answer: "The three formalisms being extensionally equivalent is a genuine theorem, but it only shows that three independently motivated formal definitions agree with each other -- it doesn't show that any of them captures the informal, pre-theoretic idea of an 'effective procedure,' because that informal notion was never made precise enough to be the kind of thing a proof can be about. That's the real reason the thesis is a thesis and not a theorem. It's also why Turing's route to it is usually treated as doing more independent work than Church's: Church leaned heavily on the fact that lambda-definability and recursiveness, two separately motivated formalisms, happened to coincide, whereas Turing argued directly from an analysis of what a human clerk does when mechanically following instructions step by step. That's part of why Gödel is often said to have found Turing's version the actually convincing one. Given all that, the thesis is exactly what's doing the work in the proof that the halting problem is undecidable: since 'algorithm' can't be formally defined except via something like a Turing machine, the Church-Turing thesis is the premise that lets us derive, mathematically, that no Turing machine can decide the halting problem for Turing machines. And it covers efficiency too, in a sense -- part of why we think NP-hard problems can't be solved efficiently by any physical computer is that no computational model has ever beaten a Turing machine badly enough to threaten the thesis, so intractability is basically already built into the same guarantee."
