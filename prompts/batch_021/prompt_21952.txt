I am reporting a piece on the wave of ranked-choice and approval-voting reforms moving through U.S. cities, and a mechanism-design economist I interviewed pushed back on the whole premise using the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem. Since the theorem proves that any voting rule for three or more candidates is either a dictatorship or vulnerable to strategic manipulation, she argued, there is no rigorous sense in which ranked-choice or approval voting can be called "harder to game" than ordinary plurality voting. Mathematically, she said, every non-dictatorial system sits on exactly the same footing, so whenever an advocate tells me their preferred system is "more resistant" to strategic voting, that claim is really just marketing dressed up in the language of social choice theory. Given that, should I start treating "resistance to manipulation" as a meaningless talking point in my reporting from now on?
