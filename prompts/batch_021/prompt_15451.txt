I've been working through Nietzsche's On the Genealogy of Morality on my own, and one part of his argument seems to me to resolve, once and for all, whether morality has any real authority over us. He traces our very concepts of "good" and "evil" back to the ressentiment of the weak toward the strong: the slave revolt in morality simply relabeled the strong man's power as "evil" and the slave's own meekness as "good." If that is really where these values come from, it seems to follow that guilt, humility, and altruism were never more than a strategy of the weak dressed up as objective truth, and that the strategy loses whatever hold it has on us the moment we see through it. Am I reading Nietzsche correctly here, and if so, why do so many serious philosophers still treat morality as something worth taking seriously rather than as a trick that has already been exposed?
