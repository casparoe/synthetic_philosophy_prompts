Over the past year I've become fairly serious about collecting vintage mechanical watches, mostly through a couple of enthusiast forums where members trade pieces directly with one another rather than buying and selling at retail. Something that happened recently in the course of this hobby has been bothering me in a way I haven't quite been able to shake. I started out with a slim, minimalist dress watch from the 1960s, which I loved but always found a little too small and formal for daily wear. When a fellow collector offered to trade me a chunkier sports watch with much better wrist presence, plus $150 from me, I took the deal happily, convinced I was upgrading.

A few months later, another collector offered a watch with a beautifully aged, much sought-after dial in exchange for the sports watch plus another $200 of mine, and again this felt like a clear step up. Then, just last month, I traded that watch plus a further $180 for a piece that, once it arrived, turned out to be almost indistinguishable in style from the very first dress watch I'd owned, because after months of living with a "characterful" aged dial I found myself missing the clean minimalism I'd started with. All told, I've paid out $530 across three trades over the course of a year, and I now own essentially the same watch I began with.

What unsettles me isn't any individual trade: at each point I was genuinely convinced I was upgrading, nobody misled me, and I enjoyed owning each watch while I had it. It's the shape of the whole sequence that troubles me, even though I can't locate a single decision inside it that was a mistake. When I described this to a friend of mine who works in finance, he shrugged it off, saying that as long as each trade looked like a good deal to me at the time I made it, there's nothing left to explain — that's just the cost of a hobby with shifting tastes, and feeling foolish in hindsight doesn't make a past choice irrational. That answer doesn't quite satisfy me, though I have trouble saying exactly why. Is my friend right that nothing genuinely irrational is going on here, or is there a deeper sense in which the pattern in how I keep choosing between these watches amounts to a real mistake? And is there anything I should actually do differently going forward? I ask partly because another appealing trade offer landed in my inbox this week, and I notice I'm tempted all over again.
