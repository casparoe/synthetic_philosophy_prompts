I chair my university's Academic Integrity Board, which can impose sanctions ranging from a warning to expulsion but currently applies one evidentiary standard, 'clear and convincing evidence,' to every case. A subcommittee proposes a sliding scale instead -- easier to prove for a warning, harder for expulsion -- and members disagree about why this would be justified. Some argue that the same evidence can amount to knowledge that a student cheated when only a warning is at stake but not when expulsion is -- echoing pragmatic encroachment; others reply that the facts never change, only the contextually salient standard for 'proven' does -- closer to epistemic contextualism; a third group worries that either way, letting the sanction fix what counts as proven makes guilt depend on our choice of punishment. Please draft the short decision memo I'll bring to the Board: recommend a specific evidentiary policy, say which (if either) of these framings it commits us to, and engage seriously with the strongest objection to your recommendation.
