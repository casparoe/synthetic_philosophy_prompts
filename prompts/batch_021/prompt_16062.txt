I've been chewing on this for weeks and it won't leave me alone. It started from a dumb thought: when I read "Sara is late," it feels like ONE fact, not just two seperate things (Sara, and lateness) sitting next to each other for no reason. Went down a rabbit hole and found there's an old philosophy problem about exactly this, the "unity of the proposition," and something called Bradley's regress that's supposed to show why it's genuinely hard to explain.

As I understand it: if some relation ties Sara to being late, you can ask what ties THAT relation to Sara and to lateness, and any relation you name to answer needs its own tie, forever. People call this regress "vicious," like it actually proves something, not just a harmless infinite way of describing things. Apparently Bradley used it to argue relations aren't fully real, and Frege supposedly heads it off by making concepts "unsaturated" so they don't need extra glue to attach to objects.

None of that clicks for me yet. Without heavy notation, can you explain why this regress is supposed to count as vicious rather than just an endless-but-harmless fact, and whether Frege's fix actually stops the regress or just relocates the same problem one level up?
