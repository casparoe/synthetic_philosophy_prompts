A friend of mine who recently completed jury duty described a moment in deliberations that has stuck with me. She was confident the evidence made the defendant's guilt more likely than not, and probably even highly likely, but when a fellow juror demanded that she convert this into an exact number, asking whether it was sixty percent, seventy-five, or something else, she found she had no principled way to prefer one number to its nearest neighbors; naming any of them felt like manufacturing a precision the evidence simply did not support. He took her hesitation as proof that she had not done the work a genuine probability judgment requires. I would like to know whether he is right, or whether there is a respectable rationale behind her reluctance to settle on a single number.
