So here's a situation I did not expect to end up in. I'm a volunteer citizen member on my county's Habitat Conservation Advisory Committee - got roped into it a few years back because I run the local Audubon chapter's little fish-and-amphibian survey group, not because I have any actual science training past high school biology. Ever since I read a pop-science book about how surprisingly hard it is for biologists to actually agree on what a "species" even is, I've had a bit of an obsession with the question, but up until now it was just a fun fact to bring up at parties. Now I actually have to use it to make a real decision, and it is a lot less fun.

There's a little minnow (a shiner, if that means anything to you) living in one creek on the north side of the county that's been cut off from the main river population for something like 8,000 years by a waterfall. It turns a different color when it's breeding, it spawns about a month later than the river fish do, and the one genetic study we've ever scraped together the money to pay for says it's still nested inside the river population's family tree rather than being its own separate branch - plus there's a trickle of interbreeding right where the creek empties into the river. Half my committee wants to call this its own species (or at least what our contracted biologist keeps calling an "evolutionarily significant unit") so it automatically gets a protected buffer under our habitat plan, which would kill a 40-unit affordable housing project the county has been fighting to get built for six years. The other half wants to call it just a weird population of the regular shiner and not worth blocking housing over. Our own ordinance just says the buffer kicks in if a population "constitutes a distinct species or subspecies," full stop, and never says whose definition of species we're supposed to use - and two committee members think this "evolutionarily significant unit" idea is just our biologist inventing a category that isn't actually in our ordinance so we can dodge the real question. I called my brother-in-law, who's an actual working biologist, hoping he'd clear it up for me, and he just laughed and said something like "oh boy, we've never agreed on that either," which did not help at all.

I'm the one who got asked to draft the recommendation memo that goes to the full committee before we vote, in three weeks. Could you write it for me? It needs to actually land on a specific recommendation - list it, don't list it, or some kind of provisional or interim call, if that's a real option and not just me chickening out of the question - with a concrete timeline attached to whatever we do. And it has to take the losing side seriously rather than just being polite about them on the way to dismissing them, because honestly, having now spent way too many evenings loosing sleep over a fish, I don't think either side here is being unreasonable.
