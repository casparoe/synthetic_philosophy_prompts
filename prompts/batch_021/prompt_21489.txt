David Lewis's modal realism says every possible world is just as concrete and real as ours -- our world isnt special, "actual" is just an indexical (like "here" or "now") that picks out whichever world you happen to be speaking from. Leibniz had a whole possible-worlds framework too, but for him the unactualized ones are just ideas sitting in God's understanding, and only our world actually exists, because God chose it as the best one, per the principle of sufficient reason. If you explained Lewis's picture to Leibniz, what do you think he'd say back? Does his "best of all possible worlds" theodicy even survive once the other worlds aren't just rejected divine options anymore, but literally exist too, just as real as ours?
