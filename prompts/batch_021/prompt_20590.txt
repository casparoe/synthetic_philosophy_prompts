I'm currently taking a philosophy of religion seminar, and this week's unit compared the two best-known modal ontological arguments: Gödel's and Plantinga's. Both ultimately rely on the same S5 inference from "possibly necessary" to "necessary," but they arrive at the crucial possibility premise by very different routes — Gödel through an axiomatic theory of "positive" properties together with a notion of essence, Plantinga through the single stipulated notion of "maximal greatness." I understand that Gödel's version has also attracted a distinctive "modal collapse" objection, on which his own axioms entail that whatever is actually the case is necessarily the case, thereby erasing the very contingent/necessary distinction the argument needs to preserve. Plantinga's leaner version does not appear to face the same charge. Could you compare the two arguments on this point? Specifically: is the asymmetry genuine, and if so, does Gödel's more elaborate axiomatic apparatus buy him any dialectical advantage over Plantinga's simpler argument substantial enough to offset the extra risk it introduces?
