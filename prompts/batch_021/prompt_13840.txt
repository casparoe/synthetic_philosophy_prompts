Everyone already knows the fun-fact version of this by now — brilliant physicist was secretly an anti-Trinitarian who'd have been in real trouble at Cambridge if it came out — but there's a piece of the puzzle that isn't clicking for me. Can you help me understand the actual argumentative link between those two positions, rather than just the fact that they happened to coexist in one very odd genius? Relatedly, what was actually at stake for Newton in the fight with Leibniz over the "bad watchmaker" jab — wounded pride, or a substantive disagreement about what divine perfection is even supposed to look like?
