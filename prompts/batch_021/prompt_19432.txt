So we had this comp committee meeting at work (I'm one of the ICs on our eng team who got roped into it) where we're redesigning next years bonus pool formula, and two people on it, Nina and Marcus, got into a really good arguement about it. Nobody's ratings are in yet, so literally nobody on the team, including them, knows who'll end up top-rated vs bottom-rated. Roughly how it went:

1. Nina argued for the flatter formula: whatever we pick should make the bottom-rated person's payout as high as possible, since none of us knows yet who that'll be, and that's the fair way to choose a rule you might end up on the wrong end of.

2. Marcus said that's overly cautious: if you genuinely don't know your odds, the rational move isn't to fixate on the worst case, it's to go with whichever formula does better on average — and he thinks the steeper, more top-heavy formula actually does that, because it retains the senior folks who'd otherwise leave, which keeps the whole pool bigger next year, bottom-rated person included.

3. Nina said "it'll grow the pool for everyone eventually" is exactly the kind of thing you can't just assert — it's the standard line trotted out whenever someone wants to justify giving more to whoever's already winning, and she's not handing out downside protection on the strength of a promise like that.

4. Marcus replied that then her objection isn't really about fairness anymore, it's a factual dispute about whether the incentive effect is real — if the steeper formula genuinely does leave the bottom-rated person better off than the flat one would, she'd have to admit it's the fairer formula by her own standard, so she needs to argue the facts, not just call it unfair.

5. Nina's comeback was that if "it secretly helps the worst-off too, trust us" can justify basically any amount of extra pay at the top with zero verification after the fact, then the fairness standard isn't doing any real work — somebody has to actually be on the hook for that promise coming true, or its just a story the people who benefit tell themselves.

They still haven't picked a formula and now they want my vote. Can you go through moves 1 through 5 and say who had the stronger point at each one (both being partly right is a fine answer too), and flag anything neither of them thought of that actually matters here? End with a one-line verdict: \verdict{Nina}, \verdict{Marcus}, or \verdict{tie}.
