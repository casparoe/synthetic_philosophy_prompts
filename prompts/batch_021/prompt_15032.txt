Random thing that's been bugging me and I can't tell if I'm overthinking it. My grandpa has gotten really rigid about his routine since he retired — same breakfast every day, same walk at the same time down the same two blocks, and he straight up refuses to try the new bakery that opened two doors down even though my grandma keeps begging him to. If plans change on him last minute he gets weirdly agitated about it. I brought this up half-jokingly at my lab's journal club (I'm doing a PhD in computational psychiatry) and one of the senior postdocs, who's kind of the resident theory person, said something like "yeah that tracks, the brain's basic job is to minimize prediction error, so now that he's older and doesn't have as much new stuff he needs to learn, his brain's just settled into whatever setup gives it the least surprise, he found his local minimum." Everyone laughed and we moved on to the next paper, but its been rattling around in my head since.

Because if that story were the whole truth, it seems like it should predict something way more extreme than "likes his routine" — it should predict that literally any brain, young or old, would want to just go sit alone somewhere quiet and unchanging and not move, since that's about as low-surprise as you can physically get. And yet basically nobody does that, including my grandpa, who still watches new shows, picks fights with my uncle about politics every Sunday, and got weirdly emotional over a new grandkid photo last week. So either "brains just minimize surprise" is too simple a story to be doing any real explanatory work, or there's some way it's supposed to account for stuff like curiosity and novelty-seeking that my postdoc's comment just skipped over. Can you help me sort out what's actually going on here, and whether her explanation of my grandpa was a real explanation or kind of a non-explanation dressed up in science-y language?
