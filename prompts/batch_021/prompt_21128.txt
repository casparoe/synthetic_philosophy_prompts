For my animal ethics seminar, we've spent the past few weeks on Singer's utilitarianism and Regan's rights view, and this week we turned to a brief, curious aside in Nozick's *Anarchy, State, and Utopia*. Rather than extending his rights-based side-constraints downward to animals, or extending an aggregative calculus upward to persons, Nozick tentatively proposes treating animals under a broadly utilitarian calculus while reserving side-constraints exclusively for persons. Could you explain the rationale behind this asymmetric, two-tier proposal, and then present the strongest objections to it? I'm particularly interested in whether Nozick can draw the line between the two tiers without simply presupposing species membership, and how the view is meant to handle cognitively sophisticated animals and humans who lack the capacities he treats as morally significant.
