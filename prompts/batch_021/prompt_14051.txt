My friend Theo is getting married in three weeks and asked me, his housemate from undergrad, to give the best man speech. Complication: Theo apparently proposed to his fiancée Ana by way of Kavka's toxin puzzle. The story is that their first real conversation, in our decision theory seminar last year, was a two-hour argument about whether you can actually rationally intend to drink the toxin, and it became this running bit between them - he told her, and I quote, that marrying him is basically "the toxin puzzle, except you actually want to drink it." Now everyone coming to the wedding already expects me to do something with this, and to make it weirder, the professor assigned the actual Kavka paper again this semester, two weeks before the wedding, which Theo insists is a sign.

I don't want to just make a vague "haha nerdy inside joke" reference though, because going through the paper properly this time, the part thats sticking with me isn't the toxin itself, its the timing: the billionaire pays out the moment you form the intention at midnight, well before you'd ever have to actually drink the stuff, so by the time drinking-time rolls around the next day you have zero remaining reason to go through with it - and the real puzzle is whether you can even sincerely form that intention in the first place, knowing in advance you won't want to follow through once the payoff's secured. That gap - you bind yourself now to a future moment where plain self-interest will leave you nothing left to go on - seems to me like it's actually just the structure of a wedding vow, not a cute analogy to it, and I want the speech to use that instead of flattening it into a one-off pun.

Could you write me the toast? It needs to run about three minutes out loud, include one actual joke that both the five or six philosophy people in the room and the thirty guests who've never heard of Kavka can laugh at, and land as a warm, genuine thing to say about Theo and Ana rather than a decision theory lecture with cake at the end.
