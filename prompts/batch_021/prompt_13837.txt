I started taking an SSRI for anxiety about three weeks ago, and something my psychiatrist said at the last check-in keeps bugging me. She mentioned it usually takes another few weeks to fully kick in, but that patients almost always report that whatever they were most anxious about going in ends up feeling a lot more "eh, 50/50, we'll see" once the medication is working, even though nothing about the actual situation itself changes. Right now the thing I'm most anxious about is whether to leave a stable job for a riskier one, and at the moment I'm leaning hard toward staying put, mostly out of dread about how bad the downside would feel.

Here's what's been nagging me: if I already basically know, with pretty high confidence, that Future-Me in a month is going to look at this exact same choice and feel alot calmer and more neutral about it than I do now, isn't it kind of inconsistent for Present-Me to stay this anxious? I generally think you should take your own future judgment seriously — if I knew for a fact my opinion on something was going to flip later, it seems weird to just ignore that now. But also, "borrowing" the calm now, before the pill's even done anything, feels like cheating somehow, like I'd be talking myself into a feeling rather than it being earned. I brought this up with my roomate, who took one epistemology class in undergrad, and she said it reminded her of some kind of principle about trusting your future opinions, but couldn't remember the details, or why it might not even apply here. Is there an actual principled answer to how much weight I should give my anticipated future (medicated) attitude right now, or does this just not have a clean resolution?
