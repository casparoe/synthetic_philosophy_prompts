So I fell down a rabbit hole this week reading about Williamson's anti-luminosity argument and the KK principle in epistemology (I think the book's called Knowledge and Its Limits, might be misremembering the title). KK is basically the idea that if you know P you should automatically be in a position to know that you know P, and luminosity is the claim that whenever your in some mental state, you're always in a position to tell that you're in it. I get that he dresses the whole argument up with this "margin for error" stuff about not being able to perfectly discriminate between close cases, but underneath that isn't it basically just Gödel's incompleteness theorem or the halting problem again -- a system cant fully verify its own internal states from the inside, so of course iterated self-knowledge breaks down eventually, same diagonalization trick just pointed at minds instead of machines. Computer scientists and logicians already nailed that whole class of result decades earlier, so why do philosophers still treat Williamson's version like such a huge, hard-won discovery?
