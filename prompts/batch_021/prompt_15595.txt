Large language models now seem to take in stride the kind of common-sense updating that used to be held up as an unsolved problem for artificial intelligence: without being told to check every fact that stays the same, they reliably infer that repainting a chair won't change its weight, or that moving a mug across a table won't alter how many legs the table has. Since this is essentially the challenge Daniel Dennett analyzed as "the frame problem" in AI and philosophy of mind, why do philosophers continue to treat it as an open problem rather than something transformer-based architectures have, for practical purposes, already solved?
