Write an essay evaluating the popular claim that al-Ghazālī's critique of causal necessity in the Seventeenth Discussion of *Tahāfut al-Falāsifa* (The Incoherence of the Philosophers) anticipates Hume's account of causation. Weigh the genuine parallels—such as the shared reliance on "custom" and the shared denial that observation ever reveals a necessary tie between cause and effect (Ghazālī's stock example being fire's contact with cotton)—against the disanalogies: whether Ghazālī's occasionalism actually eliminates necessary connection from the world or merely relocates it into God's causal agency, and whether his fallback "hypothetical concession," that even a granted natural power in fire could still be divinely overridden, strengthens or weakens the comparison with Hume. Don't settle for a non-committal answer: reach and defend a definite verdict on whether it is apt to call Ghazālī a precursor of Hume, or whether the resemblance is only superficial.
