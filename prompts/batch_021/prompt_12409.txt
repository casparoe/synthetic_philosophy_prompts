I'm a philosophy major, and a situation from earlier this year keeps nagging at me, because it feels like it should connect to something we've covered in one of my courses this semester, though I can't quite pin down what. For months, I confided in someone I knew only by a screen name on a hobby forum and came to trust her judgment completely, while separately finding a coworker at my part-time job self-important and hard to trust. A few months ago, I discovered — and she confirmed — that the two of them are one and the same person, which came as a complete shock. I don't doubt this for a second, yet my reactions haven't merged the way I would have expected: thinking of 'my forum friend' still brings warmth, while thinking of 'my coworker' still brings suspicion, as though two different people were involved rather than one. Now that we've been placed on the same student committee, I have to decide how to actually treat her, and I'm not sure whether one of these reactions is simply mistaken, or whether it can be rational to respond so differently to a single person depending on which guise I encounter her in. Which reaction should win out, if either, and is there a principled way to decide?
