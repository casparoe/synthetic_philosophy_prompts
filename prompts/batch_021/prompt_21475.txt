I'm a novelist, and this whole Tolstoy "What Is Art?" thing has been messing with my head lately -- if the bar for good art is that it infects people with a feeling that unites them across class lines (basically Christian brotherhood, in his terms), then most literary fiction, mine included, is looking pretty shaky. Next month im interviewing, for a books podcast, a scholar who defends the theory almost wholesale. Thing is, whenever I bring up a case that seems to break it -- King Lear, late Beethoven, even Tolstoy dumping his own War and Peace into the "bad art" pile -- she's got a stock comeback ready: "that's not a counterexample, that IS the theory working, your urge to defend it is just the aristocratic training talking." Can you write me six or seven questions that keep tightening the screws on that move so its harder to reach for each time, ending on one that makes her say what would actually count as evidence against Tolstoy's universality criterion?
