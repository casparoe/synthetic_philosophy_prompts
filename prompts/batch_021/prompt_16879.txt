Reconstruct this argument in your own words, state the metaphysical premise on which it depends, and explain how, if at all, it relates to Gödel's ontological proof of the existence of God. Here is the student's answer:

"Gödel believed that the human personality does not simply end at death. His grounds for this were not religious in the ordinary sense but followed from a broader conviction he held throughout his life, namely that the world is rational: nothing occurs without a sufficient reason, and nothing of value is produced in vain. Applied to human beings, this principle suggested to him that a personality as intricately developed as ours -- one that typically reaches maturity of character and understanding only after decades of experience -- would be senselessly squandered if it were annihilated just as it became capable of exercising that maturity. A rational world, he thought, would not invest so much in forming a mind only to destroy it at the very moment this investment starts to pay off. Gödel worked this argument out in letters to his mother rather than in any formal publication, which is consistent with his lifelong caution about committing his more speculative philosophical and theological convictions to print.

This argument is best understood as an extension of the ontological proof rather than as an independent piece of reasoning. In the ontological proof, Gödel defines a God-like being as one possessing all 'positive' properties and shows, from a small set of modal axioms, that such a being necessarily exists. Since perfect goodness counts among the positive properties, a God-like being would not permit a personality to be annihilated just as it reaches its potential; the survival of personality after death is therefore a corollary of that being's existence and goodness, not a separate thesis. Gödel kept the proof itself private for many years out of caution, but by 1970, apparently satisfied that the argument was sound, he allowed it to be published, so that the ontological proof and the afterlife argument it supports entered the public record together."
