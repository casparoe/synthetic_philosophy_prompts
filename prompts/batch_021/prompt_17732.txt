Next month I'm interviewing an experimental philosopher for a piece on whether philosophical intuitions are actually universal or just an artifact of whoever happened to be sitting in the room when the survey got run. She's a great get because she's basically lived through the whole arc of that sub-field: she was involved in a couple of those early-2000s papers everyone still cites, running things like Gettier-style knowledge vignettes and a version of Kripke's Gödel/Schmidt reference case on Western vs. East Asian undergrads. They found real divergence -- East Asian participants leaning more descriptivist about reference, more willing to call the Gettier subject a knower -- and those results got used everywhere as ammo for the argument that philosophers should stop treating intuitions as evidence at all. Then, a decade or so later, she ended up on one of the much bigger, better-resourced replication projects, run across way more countries with tighter translation controls, and that project found the original gaps had mostly shrunk or vanished. I want to talk to her about basically living through her own field's reversal.

Could you draft me seven or eight interview questions that don't just let her narrate that arc in the abstract? I've fallen down a total rabbit hole reading the x-phi replication literature prepping for this, so here's specifically what I want covered: one question that really pins her down on whether the shrinkage was a methods story (bad translations, samples that were "cross-cultural" in name only, underpowered original studies) versus a story about intuitions genuinely converging over time -- since those two explanations definately carry different implications for how much weight anyone should put on intuitions generally; one on the meta-level puzzle of whether convergence even rescues intuitions as evidence, given that everyone could just be sharing the same bias rather than tracking anything real (the way people sometimes argue about disgust and moral intuitions); and one that pushes on whether "cross-cultural" x-phi has ever really sampled deep cultural difference or just reran the same undergrad questionnaire in a second WEIRD-ish country. For each of those three, add a quick follow-up keyed to whatever answer she's most likely to reach for, so I'm not just lobbing the question and moving on -- and please, nothing generic like "what first drew you to this." Maybe end on a question that just makes her call it: does she now think intuitions are more universal or more parochial than she believed starting out, and what result would actually change her mind again.
