Rudolf Carnap rarely put politics at the center of his technical work, but he was not apolitical. In his Intellectual Autobiography, he traces a lasting sympathy for socialism and a rationally planned economy back to his years in the German youth movement, and he remained a close collaborator of Otto Neurath, whose proposals for economic planning "in kind" (coordinating production in physical units rather than through market prices) were among the specific proposals that Mises, and later Hayek, targeted in the socialist calculation debate. Carnap also helped draft the Vienna Circle's 1929 manifesto, which explicitly linked the rejection of metaphysics to a broader program of rational social modernization. Yet Carnap held that ultimate value judgments are not the kind of thing empirical science can settle, and his later Principle of Tolerance treated the choice between rival logical frameworks as a pragmatic matter of fruitfulness rather than one of discovering the uniquely correct system.

Suppose Carnap had lived to see contemporary arguments that sufficiently powerful machine learning and computation finally dissolve the epistemic objections Hayek raised against Neurath-style planning. Drawing specifically on the commitments above, rather than on generic impressions of logical positivists as science enthusiasts, how do you think Carnap would have responded? And would his own Principle of Tolerance have given him any reason to treat the choice between planned and market economies the way he treated rival logics: as a pragmatic question to be settled by fruitfulness rather than by truth?
