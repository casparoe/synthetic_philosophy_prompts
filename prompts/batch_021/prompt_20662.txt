I live in a small town, and for years people here have complained that we're quietly getting poorer because so many of us drive to the bigger town nearby to shop, while almost no one from there ever comes the other way, so more money leaves our local economy each month than flows back into it. A town councillor is now pushing a "buy local" campaign designed to actively discourage residents from shopping out of town, on the theory that a place only grows richer by holding on to its money rather than letting it constantly drain away to its neighbours. It sounds sensible, and I can't immediately spot the flaw in it, but treating the local cash balance itself as the real measure of a town's prosperity feels too simple to me. Is this way of thinking about how a local economy gets richer or poorer actually sound, or is something important missing from it?
