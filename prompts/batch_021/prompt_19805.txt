So a few of us in my department's formal epistemology reading group have been chewing through that Scott Aaronson essay on why complexity theory should matter to philosophers (the one that makes the rounds every couple years), and after last week's session it turned into everyone at the pub trying to one-up each other with jokes about it. Every attempt was bad in the exact same way tho -- they were "two nerds talk about big-O notation" jokes that couldve been slotted into literally any CS-adjacent topic, they didn't actually depend on knowing what the underlying ideas were.

The part of the essay that actually got real discussion going was the logical omniscience problem: standard epistemic logic models your knowledge as closed under logical consequence, so if you know P and know P->Q you know Q -- and in fact you instantly know every theorem entailed by anything you believe, for free, no proof search required. Aaronson's whole point is that this is exactly the assumption computational complexity rules out for any real reasoner. I want a joke that actually hinges on that gap, between "knows X" in the idealized closure sense and "knows X" for something that's actually resource-bounded, rather than just gesturing at "haha computers are dumb." If you can fold in the generate-vs-verify asymmetry from NP somewhere too that's a nice bonus, but the main thing is it should only really land for someone who gets both the epistemic logic and the complexity theory side, not a generic nerd joke wearing a P=NP costume.
