My grandma died last month, and near the end she told me, completely calm, 'I'm not scared, I know I'll see grandpa again.' Not 'I hope so,' not 'I believe so' — just stated flat, like a fact she wasn't even open to being wrong about. I mentioned it to my friend Dan afterwards and he got kind of worked up, saying that if she can't point to any actual evidence for an afterlife then being that sure is just irrational, no different from being 100% certain about any other claim you can't back up. We argued about it for like an hour and neither of us budged. Heres the thing though: it didnt feel like she was making a bet on how things turn out, the way she would about whether it's going to rain tomorrow. It felt like a different kind of thing, but I can't explain why that should even matter or get her off the hook. So was Dan right that her certainty is exactly as irrational as any other unfounded belief, or is there something going on with how this kind of certainty works that makes the comparison unfair?
