Ok so I just spent like an hour and a half arguing with my friend Priya about Cicero's De Senectute and I need to channel that energy into something useful. Her whole position is that it's basically old-man copium -- she especially hates the bit where Cato says losing your sex drive is great because its like escaping a "savage tyrant," and the part where he compares dying in old age to an apple falling off the tree once its ripe instead of being torn off green. I'll admit both of those are weak, but I think the FIRST argument he makes -- the one about how old age only looks empty if "doing things" only means physical stuff, and how Fabius Maximus and blind old Appius Claudius were still steering Rome through pure judgement, not muscle -- genuinely holds up.

Anyway, my grandad turns 90 next month and I got roped into giving the toast. He was a trial lawyer for almost 60 years and his old partners still call him for advice on cases, so the "counsel over muscle" thing fits him weirdly well. It's a mixed room: half family who wouldn't know Cicero from Seneca, half his old law partners who will absolutely clock a classics reference if I get it right. Can you write me a 4-5 minute toast that leans on that first argument (Fabius or Appius can get a mention) and deliberately leaves out the sex-drive line and the ripe-apple-death thing?
