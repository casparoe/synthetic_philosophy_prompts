So I've been teaching Frege in my philosophy of logic seminar this term, and we inevitably ended up down the rabbit hole of his political diary from 1924 -- the notebook that got dug up decades after his death and turned out to be full of nationalist, anti-Weimar, antisemitic bile, from the same guy who basically gave us modern quantificational logic and the sense/reference distinction. Dummett, who did more than anyone to make Frege central to analytic philosophy, apparently found it pretty devastating to reconcile with his admiration for the guy. Anyway, one of my students asked a question that's been bugging me since: if Frege somehow got transplanted into Germany today, what do you think he'd actually make of stuff like the EU, mass immigration, or the rise of the AfD? I don't want the lazy answer ("he said awful things about Jews, so he'd obviously hate immigrants") -- I want you to reconstruct his views with some actual texture, using what we know of the specific things he was hostile too (parliamentary democracy and universal suffrage, the Weimar coalition parties, assimilated vs. "eastern" Jews, the French) and work out how those commitments would plausibly cash out in today's Germany.

Theres also a second, maybe harder question I keep going back and forth on with colleagues: does any of this connect up with his actual philosophical work in an interesting way -- his war on psychologism, his weird platonism about "thoughts" existing in a mind-independent third realm, his whole obsession with objectivity and rigor over anything psychological or contingent -- or is looking for that kind of connection just an intellectually lazy move, a version of the genetic fallacy, that we ought to resist? I'd like your actual take on this, not a wishy-washy "there are considerations on both sides" kind of answer.
