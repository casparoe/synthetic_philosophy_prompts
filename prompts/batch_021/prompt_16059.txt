So this happened to a friend of mine and I can't stop turning it over, especially since it bumps right into some stuff from the judgment-and-decision-making course I'm TAing this semester as part of my cog sci PhD. She was on the academic job market last cycle, got two offers, took the one she actually wanted, signed the contract, moved cities, started teaching — completely done, no way to undo any of it now. A few months later she found out, kind of by accident, that an old grad-school friend of hers sits on the hiring committee at the OTHER school, the one she turned down, and could probably tell her things like whether she was their actual first choice or just next in line after someone else said no, or whether they'd have matched or beaten her current salary if she'd pushed harder in negotiations. None of this changes anything going forward — there's no decision left for it to feed into, she's not un-signing anything. She keeps flip flopping on whether to just ask. Her reasoning is basically "why not, more information can't make me worse off, worst case I just ignore whatever I hear," but she also admits, kind of sheepishly, that shes a little scared to actually ask and can't really say why beyond a bad feeling in her stomach.

This is bugging me because I'm pretty sure I've taught a version of the idea she's leaning on — something about how a rational Bayesian agent should never be made worse off by getting free information before making a decision, since you can always just act as if you hadn't seen it. Except her situation isn't really "before a decision" anymore; the decision already happened. So I genuinely can't tell whether that principle even applies here, or whether she's just borrowing the vibe of a rationality argument to justify a curiosity that has nothing to do with rationality at all. Is there an actual principled answer to whether she "should" want to know in a case like this, or does it just dissolve into a question of whether she personally handles rumination better or worse than regret, with no theory backing either answer?
