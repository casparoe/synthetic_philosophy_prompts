In three weeks I'm marrying into a Jain family, and my future father-in-law — a retired professor who spent his career on Jain epistemology — has asked me to give the reception toast. He has already warned me, kindly, against the one move he says every non-Jain speaker makes at these events: reaching for the blind-men-and-the-elephant story and stopping exactly where it becomes comfortable — "so every viewpoint is equally true" — rather than following it through to what anekantavada actually claims, namely that each viewpoint is a partial standpoint (naya), true only conditionally and "in some respect" (syadvada), and that treating any single one as the unconditioned whole truth is exactly the one-sided dogmatism (ekantavada) the doctrine exists to block. I've done enough reading these past weeks to see that he's right that this isn't the same thing as relativism, but I don't trust myself to turn that distinction into five minutes of prose that also has to land with my side of the family, who have never encountered any of these terms. Could you write that toast: one in which anekantavada does real work in framing how two families and their different convictions are being joined, precise enough to satisfy my father-in-law and warm enough to survive being read aloud at a wedding?
