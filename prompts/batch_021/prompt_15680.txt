I'm about two-thirds into a first-contact novel where the "aliens" never actually show up -- they just broadcast a threat: comply with a list of demands, or they'll run simulations of everyone who refuses and make those copies suffer. A friend who spends way more time in the rationalist corners of the internet than I do sent me down a rabbit hole on "acausal trade" a few months back, and one idea that came out of it, this thing called a surrogate goal, honestly makes for better sci-fi than anything I'd have invented myself. So instead of hand-waving it, I've actually managed to line up a call next week with a decision theorist who works on exactly this stuff -- defends using surrogate goals as a defense against acausal extortion -- for background before I write the big interrogation-room scene with my government-advisor character.

Could you draft me 8 to 10 interview questions for that call? I don't want the "so what's a surrogate goal, explain it to me like I'm five" opener -- I want questions that actually lean on the parts of the position that are supposed to be doing the work: something on why a rational extorter wouldn't just see through the swap and go after the real target once it knows the trick is possible, something on how a threat against a thing everyone knows is a stand-in is even supposed to be credible in the first place, something on whether this is really different from the blunter "precommit to ignore every threat, full stop" advice that comes out of functional decision theory, and something on what happens to the whole calculation once the threat against the surrogate actually gets carried out sometimes -- since it was presumably chosen to be cheap, not free. For each one, throw in a quick note on the answer she'll probably reach for and a sharper follow-up question that doesn't just let her rest there. If theres a clean way to tie one question back to a plain old real-world commitment device -- MAD, hostage-taking, whatever -- I'd like that in there too, since I always like it when the sci-fi turns out to rhyme with something mundane.
