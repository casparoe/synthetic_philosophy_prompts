My grandfather was a retired Presbyterian minister who audited the philosophy of religion seminar I'm taking now, where he became fixated on David Lewis's essay "Do We Believe in Penal Substitution?" Because Lewis notes that we let a friend pay someone's fine but never let a friend serve someone's prison sentence, my grandfather insisted his own preaching use only the language of a debt paid on the cross, never the language of Christ "serving a sentence" in our place. He was equally firm that the essay vindicates nothing — it ends in open puzzlement, not proof — and he hated sermons that invoked philosophers to sound more certain than the argument warranted. That is presumably why I've been asked to give his eulogy this Saturday, to a room split between his old congregation, who want the plain comfort of the gospel, and his seminar friends, who will notice any philosophical misstep. Please write a five-minute eulogy that gives the first group real comfort without giving the second group anything to wince at.
