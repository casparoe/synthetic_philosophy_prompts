ok so slightly weird combo of hobbies but bear with me. i spend an unreasonable amount of time reading rationalist blogs/LW stuff, and completely unrelated to that i somehow ended up on my university's history department curriculum committee. there's a proposal on the table from a junior faculty member to add a required "counterfactual history" module to the intro historiography seminar -- training students to build explicit "what if X had gone differently" scenarios, Niall Ferguson style -- paired with a unit debunking loose "X was inevitable" language that shows up all over our existing courses (she's citing Isaiah Berlin's old point that calling outcomes inevitable quietly lets the people involved off the moral hook).

half the committee is into it, framing it as a corrective to the lazy determinist narratives students pick up ("the USSR's collapse was inevitable," etc). the other half, including our resident E.H. Carr stan, thinks its a slippery slope -- teach students that nothing was ever really inevitable and you lose the ability to talk about real structural constraints at all, and the seminar just curdles into moralizing "who's to blame" history dressed up as causal analysis. there's also a compromise floating around where any counterfactual used in class has to pass a "minimal rewrite" test (change one thing, trace it forward a couple steps, no six-link speculative chains) rather than free-form what-iffing.

i have to write the memo that goes out to the full faculty before the vote -- can you draft it? need an actual recommendation (approve / reject / approve with conditions), not a "both sides have merit" cop-out, and it has to seriously engage the structural-constraints worry instead of just waving at it.
