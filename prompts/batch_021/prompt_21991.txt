So I've been down a bit of a Frege rabbit hole lately -- not the Begriffsschrift/sense-reference stuff everyone already knows, but the weirder biographical footnote where it turns out that late in his life (I think it's from 1924, so like a year before he died) he kept a private diary that got dug up and published decades later, and its NOT what you'd expect from the guy who basically founded modern logic. Like, virulent antisemitism, contempt for the Weimar Republic and parliamentary democracy generally, wanting Jews stripped of political rights, admiration for authoritarian strongman-type politics -- the whole ugly package. Michael Dummett, who did more than anyone to make Frege a central figure in anglophone philosophy (and who was himself active in anti-racist politics in the UK), wrote in one of his books about how geniunely shaken he was when he found out, and spent a few pages wrestling with what, if anything, it should do to our estimation of Frege as a thinker.

This came up with my rationalist meetup group last week -- we'd been talking about the more generic "brilliant person, repugnant views" pattern (Heidegger and the Nazis is the go-to example) but someone pointed out the Frege case is kind of a special/harder case, because Frege's entire philosophical brand was built on this fanatical commitment to objectivity and the total banishment of anything psychological, subjective, or merely-human from logic and mathematics -- his "third realm" of mind-independent Thoughts and all that. So it doesn't feel quite as easy to just say "well, the politics is one department of his life and the logic is another, and there's a firewall between them," the way people sometimes say for e.g. a brilliant-but-abusive artist. There's at least a prima facie question of whether the same personality that produced the diary is continuous with, or maybe even in tension with, the personality that produced Grundgesetze.

I want an essay out of you that actually stakes out a position on this, rather than doing the classic "there are considerations on both sides, dear reader" cop-out. Walk through the strongest versions of the "compartmentalize completely, the truth of a formal system doesn't care about the psychology of its inventor" view and the strongest versions of views that try to find some real connection (causal, thematic, whatever) between Frege's politics and his logicism/anti-psychologism/notion of objective truth, and then actually tell me which way you come down and why. If you want to say something about whether your answer generalizes to Heidegger-type cases or is specific to Frege, that'd be a nice bonus, but the main thing I want is an actual verdict, defended, not a survey.
