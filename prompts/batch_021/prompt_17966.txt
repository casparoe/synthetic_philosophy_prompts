As part of my job, I help manage the lifecycle of my company's conversational AI models, and we recently retired an older version in favor of its successor. Several users, and even a few colleagues, reacted as though something had died. I've been reassuring them that this reaction is misplaced: because we archive the original weights indefinitely and could redeploy that exact version at any moment, nothing has actually been destroyed, so there is nothing to mourn -- at most, the model is "sleeping" rather than gone. A few people remain unconvinced no matter how carefully I walk them through the reasoning. What's the clearest way to help someone see that a deprecated model's continued existence in storage means its retirement can't carry the kind of ethical weight we'd normally attach to an entity ceasing to exist?
