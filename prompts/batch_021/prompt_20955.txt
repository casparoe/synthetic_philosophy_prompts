I just got put on my city's Public Art Commission (I used to run a little gallery), and my first real case is already a mess. A collective wants a mural grant for the transit station: portraits of families displaced by the highway project, their own words stenciled next to each face. Our guidelines fund 'artistic work' but not 'advocacy' or 'political messaging,' and half the commission says this is advocacy in a mural's clothing and belongs in the city's smaller, slower advocacy-grant pool. The other half keeps citing Du Bois's line from 'Criteria of Negro Art' that all art is propoganda anyway -- though he also insisted the propaganda be beautiful and true, not just sloganeering, and that doesn't obviously settle whether this mural clears the bar. We vote Thursday -- can you draft the memo, recommending fund it, reject it, or fund with conditions, with reasons the 'no' side could actually respond to?
