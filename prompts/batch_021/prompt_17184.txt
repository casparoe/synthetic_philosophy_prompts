Been falling down a rabbit hole for a piece I'm sketching out on modal logic and "proofs" of God's existence -- specifically Gödel's ontological argument, the one Dana Scott tidied up from his notebooks, not the Anselm "greatest conceivable being" version everybody already knows. I get the basic machinery well enough (positive properties, God-likeness as having all of them, necessary existence itself counting as a positive property...), but the thing that keeps nagging at me is the "modal collapse" objection -- that Gödel's own axioms apparently force the conclusion that every truth is a necessary truth, so there's no real contingency left anywhere in the world. Sobel gets most of the credit for spelling this out clearly, and I know there've been patch jobs since (Anderson's revised axioms, among others) that try to save the "God necessarily exists" conclusion while dodging the collapse. Can you write me an essay that actually wrestles with whether the collapse objection goes through as advertised, and if it does, whether that's fatal for the proof or just a bullet a commited theist could bite? Don't give me a wishy-washy "both sides have a point" essay -- give me an actual verdict and defend it.
