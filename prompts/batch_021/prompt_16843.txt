At work last week, two colleagues on my machine-learning team had an argument I still haven't been able to settle for myself, and I'd like an outside assessment of it.

The dispute arose over whether to trust our churn-prediction model — trained only on North American accounts — when extending it to a newly acquired set of European accounts on different pricing plans. (1) Priya argued this can't be justified in principle: Hume showed that any defense of relying on induction is circular, since the only reason to think the model's past accuracy predicts its future accuracy is itself an inductive inference, which quietly assumes the very thing it needs to prove. (2) Dan objected that this can't be the whole story, since otherwise no forecasting of any kind could ever be justified, yet models routinely beat chance on genuinely new data, so something must underwrite the practice even without a deductive proof. (3) Priya then brought in Goodman's grue: even granting that induction "works" on the training distribution, nothing in the data alone says which pattern to project forward, since for any finite training set there are alternative models that agree with ours on every North American account so far yet diverge arbitrarily on the European ones, fitting the evidence just as well. Which pattern counts as the "natural" one, she said, is a fact about our inductive biases — architecture, regularization, feature encoding — not a fact the data hands us, and that is just a modern dress for Goodman's point about entrenched predicates. (4) Dan replied that this is exactly what cross-validation is for: we compare candidate models against held-out data and let the evidence adjudicate. Priya answered that cross-validation only adjudicates among hypotheses using more data from the same distribution, so it is silent on the very question at issue — how the model behaves on the new European accounts — and that reaching for it here quietly presupposes the projectibility that is in dispute.

Neither of them budged. Could you go through the exchange move by move, say whether each of the four numbered moves holds up, and note anything you think both of them are missing?
