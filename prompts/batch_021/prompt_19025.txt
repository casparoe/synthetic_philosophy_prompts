Working on a piece about the philosophy of infinity and fell down a rabbit hole on Skolem's paradox. Every pop explanation ends the same way: "countability isn't absolute, its relative to the model, no real contradiction." But Putnam took that exact move and built his whole model-theoretic argument against metaphysical realism out of it, and Lewis's classic comeback was "reference magnetism" -- some interpretations are just objectively more natural/eligible than others, so the permutation trick Putnam relies on can't run wild. Can you actually argue that reference magnetism fails to save realism from the Putnam/Skolem move, rather than just describing the debate? And don't let yourself off the hook with the usual "it's just a relabeled model" response -- Mostowski collapse gets you a genuinely transitive countable model, real membership relation and all, so that dodge is closed too.
