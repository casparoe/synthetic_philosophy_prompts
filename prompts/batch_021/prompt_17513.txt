This has been rattling around in my head for weeks and I haven't been able to shake it, so hoping someone can help me think it through. A few years ago I hired a guy, let's call him Dave, to be general manager of my trucking company so I could step back and semi retire. The arrangement we shook hands on was pretty simple: Dave gets full authority over routes, contracts, hiring, pretty much everything, but I keep a standing veto, if I ever say "undo this" or "stop," he's supposed to just comply, no argument, thats basically the whole reason I kept ownership instead of just selling the business outright.

Problem is Dave turned out to be really good at the job, like scary good, revenues up almost 40% since he took over. And now whenever I actually try to use that veto (like last month, I wanted to unwind a big contract he'd signed that I thought was way too risky) he doesn't just say "got it boss" and comply. He pulls out spreadsheets and argues the case for an hour, "forgets" to loop me in on a client call until after the decision's basically already locked in, little things like that add up. He swears he's not trying to push me out or grab control, he genuinely believes unwinding these calls would hurt a company he's put years of his life into, and honestly, he's probably even right about that more often than not. I even paid a business consultant to help me restructure his contract, but all she had were pretty generic fixes like vesting schedules and non competes, nothing that really gets at what's actually bugging me. I also tried adding financial penalties for slow walking my decisions, which just seemed to make him sneakier about it instead of actually more compliant.

What's bugging me is that this doesn't feel like its really about Dave being sneaky or power hungry, it feels more like the exact same drive and competence that makes someone good enough at running a company to deserve that kind of autonomy in the first place is what makes them push back the second you try to reel them back in. Like maybe there's no version of "hire someone great, give them real authority, but keep a clean off switch" that actually holds up once the person is good enough at their job. Am I just stuck with this as a permanent tradeoff, or is there some smarter way to set up this kind of arrangement so competence and actually being correctable don't end up fighting each other like this?
