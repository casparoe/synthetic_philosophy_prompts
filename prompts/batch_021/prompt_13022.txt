I play viola in my high school's orchestra and edit our arts-and-ideas magazine. This spring our philosophy club is bringing in a professor of nineteenth-century aesthetics to speak before our concert, which opens with a Wagner overture, and I've been asked to interview her beforehand for a piece that will run alongside the program notes. Her research centers on Schopenhauer's claim that music alone, among the arts, copies the will directly, rather than through the Platonic Ideas that the other arts -- painting, sculpture, poetry -- are said to copy. I don't want questions she could answer by just restating that thesis. Could you draft seven or eight interview questions, each with a short follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give? I want at least one question on each of the following: (1) the tension between Schopenhauer's claim that the will, as thing-in-itself, lies outside space, time, and causality, and his claim that music -- arguably the most temporally structured of all the arts -- is its most immediate copy; (2) whether his specific mapping of the bass, the inner harmonic voices, and the melody onto the grades of the will's objectification (inorganic nature, plant and animal life, conscious human striving) is meant as a literal metaphysical correspondence or just a suggestive illustration, and what would actually hang on the difference; (3) how the theory is supposed to survive opera, song, and program music, where a piece seems tied to a particular represented scene or feeling rather than to willing "as such"; and (4) exactly where her account of his view comes apart from Hanslick's formalism, given that both deny music depicts particular emotions, but for what look like opposite reasons. Please end with a question about what kind of musical experience, if any, would count as evidence against Schopenhauer's view.
