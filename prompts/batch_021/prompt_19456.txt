Okay so I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole this week. "Clifford's principle" gets tossed around on rationalist blogs constantly (usually to shut down any talk of faith, hope, self-fulfilling optimism, whatever) so I finally went and actually read the 1877 essay plus James's reply in The Will to Believe, and honestly the fight is way less lopsided than the two-sentence LW-summary version makes it sound. Clifford's shipowner example is genuinely airtight as far as it goes, but James's stuff about live/forced/momentous options, and about beliefs that partly cause their own truth (there's a bit about a mountain climber needing confidence not to fall — pretty sure that's in the same essay, or maybe the one right next to it in the collection, I forget which), isn't just "I want it to be true so I'm allowed to believe it." He's actually trying to carve out a narrow, principled exception, and I don't think it's fair how often he gets flanderized into a stand-in for faith-based reasoning in general.

So here's what I'd like: an actual dialogue between Clifford and James, not the potted version where Clifford just says "evidence!" and James just says "but faith!" Give them both the exact same hard case and make them fight it out properly: a surgeon, the night before a genuinely risky operation she's only performed a handful of times, starts to spiral with self-doubt; a friend tells her that confidence measurably improves surgical performance, so for the patient's sake she should just resolve to believe she'll succeed, evidence be damned. Have each of them react to that case in their own voice, then go back and forth atleast a couple rounds, actually engaging with what the other just said instead of talking past each other. I want to see whether Clifford can actually absorb the case — maybe by arguing "sufficient evidence" is itself sensitive to the stakes, and to the fact that confidence is causally useful here, so believing she'll succeed isn't really a violation of his principle at all — and if so, I want James to have a real answer for why statistical evidence that confidence helps surgeons in general isn't the same as evidence that she in particular will pull this off. And I want Clifford to get in a real jab at James too: if "live, forced, and momentous for me" is all it takes to license passional belief, what actually stops anyone from calling their favorite delusion a genuine option? Neither of them should fold easily or dodge the other's best shot — by the end I want to genuinely not be sure who won.
