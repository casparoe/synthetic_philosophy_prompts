Bit of an odd one -- this is from committee service, not my own research (I'm a philosophy PhD student working on metaethics, if that's relevant). I'm the token grad student on my university's Honorary Degree committee, and we've got a genuinely ugly case for Thursday's vote. A major donor has told the president he'll pull his pledged $15M gift for the new humanities building unless the board also grants him an honorary doctorate -- his actual record is a few galas and a building that already has his name on it, nothing close to the "extraordinary distinction and service to humanity" our bylaws require. Half the committee wants to just vote yes; their argument isn't that he's secretly distinguished, its that the building matters more than keeping the degree's meaning intact. The other half says that's exactly the problem -- once you grant it for that reason, the degree stops meaning what it's supposed to, for him and for everyone who's ever gotten one. Feels like there's a concept from some seminar hiding in here that I can't quite place. I need to draft the memo I'll circulate to the full board before Thursday, recommending yes, no, or some third option, with reasons that actually take seriously why the "vote yes" camp isn't just being cynical, not just wave that off.
