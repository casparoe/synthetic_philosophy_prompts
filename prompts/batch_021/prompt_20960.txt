I've been working through MIRI's old "Tiling Agents" paper alongside the later logical induction paper, after a thread on the agent foundations tag on LessWrong turned into a disagreement between two commenters that I haven't been able to settle in my own head. The question is how seriously to take the Löbian obstacle. Löb's theorem shows that a proof-based system can only establish the general schema "if I can prove P, then P is true" for statements it could already prove outright — which blocks any naive attempt to have a self-modifying agent grant unconditional trust to a same-strength successor's proofs. One side, call them the Formalist, holds that this is a genuinely structural limit on formal self-trust: the known workarounds — stepping the successor down in deductive strength, proof-length-indexed reflection schemas of the sort sometimes called parametric polymorphism, or giving up on provability altogether in favor of the calibrated credences of a logical inductor — are real progress, but each buys its way out by conceding something the original tiling problem wanted, whether that's strength, generality, or the kind of guarantee on offer. The other side, the Bayesian, thinks this is mostly a self-inflicted wound: the obstacle only bites agents committed to routing self-trust through classical provability in a single fixed theory, and once an agent is allowed the graded, empirically-updated trust that every actual reasoner runs on anyway, a logical inductor's asymptotic calibration is not a consolation prize but the right replacement for a demand — flat, context-free certainty about a successor's entire future behavior — that was never coherent to want in the first place.

Could you write a dialogue between the Formalist and the Bayesian, picking up mid-argument at a whiteboard just after they've sketched out the proof of Löb's theorem that started the disagreement? I'd like real engagement rather than restated position statements. Have the Formalist press the point that a logical inductor's calibration guarantees are asymptotic and are silent on what its credences are worth about a specific successor at any given finite stage, especially one built to out-think it; have the Bayesian answer that directly instead of simply asserting that logical induction dissolves the obstacle; and have each concede at least one real point to the other over the course of the exchange, so that it reads like two people who have actually done the reading rather than two sides talking past each other.
