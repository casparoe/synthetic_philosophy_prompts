For my philosophy elective, I've been reading about functionalism in the philosophy of mind, and I came across Ned Block's "China Brain" (sometimes called the "Chinese Nation") thought experiment from his 1978 paper "Troubles with Functionalism." The setup: every person in China is assigned to act as a single neuron, linked to a handful of neighbors by radio. Together, the whole population reproduces the same causal and functional organization as a human brain, wired up to an artificial body for sensory input and behavioral output. Functionalism defines mental states purely by their functional role, so multiple realizability is supposed to hold across neurons, silicon chips, and people-with-radios alike, meaning this system would have to count as genuinely conscious as long as it's organized correctly. But Block thought this consequence was absurd, since it's hard to shake the sense that nobody would really be "home" in such a system.

I'd like you to write a short story (roughly 800-1200 words) that takes this scenario seriously as fiction rather than treating it as an abstract puzzle. Please tell it from the point of view of one specific person playing the role of a single neuron in the network. I'd rather the story sit with the question of whether the system as a whole feels anything than resolve it neatly by the end.
