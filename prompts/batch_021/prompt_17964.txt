I keep seeing Yudkowsky's Generalized Anti-Zombie Principle cited on LW as basically a knockdown of Chalmers zombie argument - the gist being that if p-zombies are conceivable, then your own belief that you're conscious can't actually be caused by the fact that you're conscious, since zombie-you would type out this exact same sentence for the exact same physical reasons. He thinks that's absurd enough to reject the whole zombie setup.

But I vaguely recall Chalmers already anticipates something like this in the "paradox of phenomenal judgment" bit of The Conscious Mind, where he just bites the bullet on epiphenomenalism and says our judgments about qualia are justified by direct aquaintance rather than by being caused in the usual way. So is GAZP actually landing a blow on that move, or is it basically rerunning an objection Chalmers already had an answer for (however unsatisfying)? Walk me through exactly where the two arguments make contact, then give me an actual verdict on who wins - not a "both sides have a point" cop out.
