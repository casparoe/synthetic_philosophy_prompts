So this happened to me a couple months ago and I'm still not sure if I did the smart thing or the sucker thing, and it turned into a whole debate when I brought it up with friends over drinks last weekend. I was travelling somewhere I didn't speak the language, missed my last connection, and ended up stranded overnight with a dead phone and about $10 to my name. The guy who ran the guesthouse I'd stayed at earlier in the trip happened to be at the station and recognized me. He offered to put me up for the night and wire me enough cash to get home, no paperwork or anything, but only after he spent a solid twenty minutes grilling me about my job, my family, stuff like that — clearly trying to figure out whether I was actually going to pay him back once I was safe. He even mentioned he's turned other stranded tourists down before and just left them at the station because he wasn't convinced they were good for it. He said something like "I'm usually a pretty good judge of who actually pays and who doesn't, and I only help the ones I'm sure about." I promised I'd wire him the money the second I got home, he seemed satisfied with that, and he helped me out.

Here's my problem, and the thing my friends couldn't agree on: I'm home now, completely fine, and realistically I am never going to see this guy again — no contract, no mutual friends, no future trips back there planned, nothing tying us together. One friend said, only half-joking, that I basically won free money off a guy who made a bad bet, and that wiring it back now changes literally nothing about what already happened to me, so I'd be kind of dumb to bother. That argument bugs me but I couldn't explain why its wrong, if it even is wrong. Is there an actual reason, beyond just "because I promised" or generic guilt-tripping about honesty, for it to be the rational move to pay him back here? Or is my friend basically right that once the risk already resolved in my favor, sending the money is just throwing away cash for nothing?
