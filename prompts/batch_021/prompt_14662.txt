I'm a second-year philosophy PhD student working mostly on early modern epistemology. This term, though, I'm taking a comparative seminar that pairs Descartes with a non-Western text each week, and this week's session set the First Meditation alongside Zhuangzi's "butterfly dream" -- the anecdote that closes the Qiwulun, the second chapter of the Zhuangzi, often rendered "Discussion on Making All Things Equal."

My professor's framing was that both thinkers notice that waking experience carries no internal marker distinguishing it from a sufficiently vivid dream, and that both therefore arrive at the same skeptical upshot: at any given moment, we cannot rule out from the inside whether we are asleep or awake. On her reading, the only substantive difference is that Descartes wasn't willing to leave things there and spent the rest of the Meditations rebuilding certainty via the cogito and an appeal to a non-deceiving God, whereas Zhuangzi simply left the doubt unresolved.

I'm now drafting a term paper that takes this parallel as its point of departure, and I'd like your help with its central section: why, unlike Descartes, Zhuangzi seems to have felt no pressure to resolve the doubt his parable raises, and what that asymmetry might reveal about differing conceptions of epistemic certainty in early Chinese versus early modern European thought. Before I build the argument out, though, I want to make sure I'm not overextending the parallel: is the butterfly dream really doing the same kind of work as Descartes' dream argument -- generating a genuine, general doubt about whether the external world exists or the senses can be trusted -- given that it concludes a chapter otherwise preoccupied with the relativity of perspectival judgments (what counts as "this" and what counts as "that," what is affirmed, shi, and what is denied, fei); and given that the passage itself doesn't end with anything resembling an unresolved epistemic worry, but with the cryptic line about "the transformation of things" (wu hua)? If the parallel doesn't hold up the way my professor's framing suggested, I'd rather revise my thesis now than after committing twenty pages to a comparison that doesn't survive contact with the text.
