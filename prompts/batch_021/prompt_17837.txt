A close friend of mine is a surgeon. Six months ago she performed a routine procedure exactly by the book — correct technique, correct dosages, nothing overlooked — and the patient died from an allergic reaction so rare that the hospital's review board agreed no one could have anticipated or prevented it. She was formally cleared of any error, yet she has not been able to shake the feeling that she is somehow responsible: she replays the operation constantly and has started turning down cases. When I described this to another friend, he said her guilt is simply irrational — she did nothing wrong, so there is nothing to feel guilty about, and she should regard the outcome exactly as she would if the same freak reaction had struck a colleague performing an identical procedure across town. He added that she cannot have it both ways: surgeons who take extra pride in operations that go unusually well, for reasons beyond their skill, do not get to disown outcomes that go unusually badly for reasons beyond their control. I objected that she is entitled to feel differently about a death she was causally embedded in than one she merely heard about, but I could not say what exactly that feeling would be tracking if not fault. We argued about it for the better part of an hour and got nowhere. Who has the better of the argument here, and if my objection is more than sentimentality, what exactly is it responding to?
