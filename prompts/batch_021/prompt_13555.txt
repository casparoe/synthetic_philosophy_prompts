Question: "Lewis defines a convention roughly as a regularity R in action such that it is common knowledge in a population that everyone conforms to R, everyone expects everyone else to conform to R, and everyone prefers to conform to R given that everyone else does, because the situation is a recurring coordination problem with more than one possible equilibrium. Explain (a) why Lewis needs common knowledge rather than mere mutual knowledge here, and (b) why he denies that conventions arise from agreement. Illustrate with an example of your own (not the driving-side-of-the-road one from lecture)."

Student answer: "For Lewis a convention is a self-perpetuating solution to a recurring coordination problem — a situation with two or more equilibria where what matters is that everyone does the same thing, not which thing they do. He needs common knowledge rather than mutual knowledge because mutual knowledge just means I know P and you know P, while coordinating requires the whole hierarchy — I know you know, you know I know, I know you know I know, and so on — otherwise neither of us can be fully confident the other will actually conform, which undercuts the whole point of making conformity rational for each person individually. Once the regularity is established, precedent does the rest of the work: each new instance of everyone conforming gives everyone fresh reason to expect conformity next time, so nothing needs a central enforcer. This is really Lewis cashing out what an implicit agreement looks like — nobody ever calls a meeting to settle these things, so the 'agreement' has to be tacit, built up through repeated interaction instead of spoken aloud, which is also why the same problem can get solved differently in different populations. A decent original example is the little routine where two people arrive at a doorway together and one says 'after you' and steps back — nothing makes either person the one who should go first, but once that pattern is set, everyone conforms to it and expects everyone else to as well."
