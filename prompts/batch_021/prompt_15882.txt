Quick one for you – I'm putting together a fiction packet for my modal metaphysics seminar this term, something to sit alongside the Lewis and Kripke readings instead of more secondary lit. Could you write me a short story (few pages, doesnt need to be long) that actually dramatizes the fight between counterpart theory and transworld identity, rather than being a thinly-disguised lecture? Specifically: Lewis says nothing is ever strictly in two worlds at once, so "Humphrey could've won the election" gets made true by some winning counterpart of his elsewhere -- similar enough to count, but numerically a different guy. Kripke's objection (the famous footnote) is that this just changes the subject, since Humphrey himself couldn't care less what happens to some other man who merely resembles him. I want a character to actually run into this concretely -- say they encounter, or get compared against, a counterpart of themselves who diverges along one axis of similarity (memories, say) but not another (body, or vice-versa), so the multiple-counterparts-under-different-similarity-relations mess actually bites and "is that me" doesn't get a clean answer. Don't wrap it up with a tidy moral at the end -- I want the metaphysics load-bearing for the plot, the way Ted Chiang or Greg Egan handle this stuff, not decorative.
