After 35 years running a physical chemistry lab I retired and fell down a philosophy-of-economics rabbit hole, and I keep coming back to these "no-trade" theorems (the Milgrom-Stokey result, I think it's called). As I understand it, they prove that two fully rational people should never trade stocks with each other on the basis of private information, since the very fact that the other guy is willing to trade at that price tells you something and should make the deal evaporate. So doesn't that mean basically all the trading volume we see on the market everyday is just irrational people flailing around -- and isn't that great news for a retiree like me who's put in the hours reading annual reports, since I should be able to clean up against all that irrational money?
