Senior year we had this elective called Logic and Decision Theory, and the one unit everybody actually remembers is the one on Hofstadter's idea of superrationality: basically, if you and someone else reason in exactly the same way about a one-shot prisoner's dilemma, and you both know that about each other, you're supposed to cooperate with zero communication and zero enforcement, purely because whatever move you land on, their identical reasoning lands them on the same move too, so the only outcomes actually "available" are both-cooperate or both-defect, and mutual cooperation just pays off better. Our teacher, Mr. Alvarez, would not let anyone get away with calling this "basically Kant's whole could-this-be-a-universal-law thing but with math" -- he kept hammering that superrationality is supposed to work for a completely selfish agent with zero moral commitments, purely from the symmetry of the situation, wheras Kant needs you to actually will the maxim as a universal law on moral grounds. That distinction is the whole reason the ~15 kids from that class will not let me get away with fudging it in my speech.

Heres the part that makes it actually relevant to graduation and not just a flex: our whole class has been curved on pretty much everything since freshman year, so theres this unspoken thing where helping a classmate study can tank your own rank, and a bunch of us definitely defected on each other more than anyone wants to admit out loud in front of our parents. I want the speech to actually use the real logic of superrationality on that situation, not water it down into a "let's all just be nice to each other" message, and to explain why cooperating was arguably the correct move even for someone who only cared about their own outcome. The catch is that the seminar kids will notice immediately if the argument is wrong or flattened into golden-rule stuff, but the other 80% of the auditorium is parents, younger siblings, and teachers who have never heard the word "superrational" in their life and just want a graduation speech that's warm and a little funny and doesn't make anyone feel publicly called out about the grades thing. Can you actually write the speech for me, like four to five minutes long, that pulls both of those off at once?
