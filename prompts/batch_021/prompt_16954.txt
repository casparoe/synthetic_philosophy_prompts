My closest friend from graduate school is getting married in three weeks, and she has asked me to give the toast. She is finishing a dissertation this spring defending Hume's view that fidelity to a promise is what he called an "artificial virtue" -- binding only because we have all bought into a shared social convention, not because of any natural fact about promise-breaking itself -- and over the years she has dismantled every rival account I have floated at her, from Prichard's claim that the obligation is simply self-evident to Scanlon's attempt to ground it instead in a convention-independent principle against manipulating other people's expectations. She has warned me, half-joking, that if the toast implies the vows bind because of some deeper moral fact, or because she and her partner were "meant to be," she will heckle me from her seat. I still want the room to feel that what they are doing today is genuinely and permanently binding, not a pleasant social game they have opted into. Write me a five-minute toast, warm rather than lecture-y, that stays honest to her Humean view for the philosophers who will be listening for exactly this, while landing just as well for the family members who have never heard of Hume.
