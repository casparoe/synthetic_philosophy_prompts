For my seminar on classical Indian epistemology, we're currently working through the opening sūtras of Gautama's Nyāya-sūtra, and I've gotten stuck on the definition of perception at 1.1.4: "indriyārthasannikarṣotpannaṃ jñānam avyapadeśyam avyabhicāri vyavasāyātmakaṃ pratyakṣam," standardly rendered along the lines of "perception is a cognition that arises from the contact of a sense organ with its object, that is non-verbal, unerring, and of the nature of ascertainment." My professor mentioned in passing that the term "avyapadeśya" ("non-verbal," or "not associated with a name") has been glossed in ways that pull in opposite directions by the commentarial tradition, but we moved on to inference before she could explain what actually turns on the disagreement. Could you lay out what's philosophically at stake between these readings, and in particular how each one sits with the sūtra's own closing requirement that perception be "vyavasāyātmaka" (determinate, in the nature of a settled judgment)? I'm especially trying to understand why later Naiyāyikas felt driven toward a two-stage nirvikalpaka/savikalpaka account of perception in order to hold both clauses together at once, and whether pressure from Dignāga's rival, thoroughly non-conceptual definition of pratyakṣa played any role in that development.
