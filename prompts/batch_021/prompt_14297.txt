At a family dinner I attended recently, my friend Nadia and her brother Theo argued about a five-year survival estimate their father's oncologist had given him, based on a large patient registry matched on cancer type and stage. Theo argued the number was close to meaningless for their father specifically, since their father lacks several risk factors common in that registry -- he was younger than most patients at diagnosis, never smoked, and carries a biomarker linked to distinctly better outcomes -- so the doctor should instead have relied on the narrowest group of patients genuinely comparable to him, even if that group is small. Nadia replied that this gets the logic backwards: keep narrowing the group and eventually you are comparing him only to himself, at which point there is no "rate" left at all, just a single outcome that will turn out to be 0% or 100% after the fact; the registry figure is trustworthy precisely because it rests on enough cases to be statistically stable, and swapping in a hand-picked subgroup because it feels more relevant is not obviously more accurate, only more comforting. Theo came back with: doctors already narrow the group somewhat, since they quote figures for "this cancer, this stage" rather than "all cancer patients," so Nadia's own preferred number is itself just an arbitrary stopping point on the same slope, and she has no principled way to say why stopping at "type and stage" is fine while stopping at "type, stage, age, smoking history, biomarker" is not. Nadia conceded that type and stage are themselves a choice, but said at least it is a choice backed by decades of large, stable samples, whereas Theo's preferred subgroup might contain a dozen people, which is not more trustworthy merely for being more specific.

Neither of them would budge, and I have been turning the disagreement over since. Could you go through the exchange move by move and say who has the better of it at each step, rather than just declaring an overall winner? I would also like to know whether there is a principled place to stop narrowing the comparison group, or whether Theo and Nadia are both missing something that would dissolve the disagreement rather than settle it.
