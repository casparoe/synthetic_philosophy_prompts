I sit on my department's committee for a university teaching award, and one of this year's nominees is a colleague who, as everyone knows, will have substantial discretionary funds to distribute once she becomes associate dean next year. No one has said anything as crude as 'vote for her and you'll be rewarded,' but since I learned about the funding, I've noticed myself feeling real admiration for her teaching, not just polite approval of her file, even though her classroom work is solid but unremarkable -- no different from several other nominees. I'm not faking this, and I'm not consciously trading my vote for favors: the feeling simply arrived, and its cause, honestly traced, is the funding, not anything she does with her students. I know exactly why I feel it, and the explanation has nothing to do with her teaching, yet the admiration doesn't feel like an error -- given what's at stake for me, it feels perfectly reasonable. A colleague told me this is just an ordinary conflict of interest and that I should recuse myself and stop overthinking it, but that misses what's actually bothering me, which isn't only how I should vote but the admiration itself. Is he right that I'm overcomplicating something simple, or is there more going on here?
