"The exclusion argument treats mere distinctness of M from P as sufficient grounds for competition between causes, but this seems to prove too much. So either the exclusion principle is false as a general claim about causation and can't be doing the specific work Kim needs it to do against the mental, or something else — maybe multiple realizability, maybe something particular to the mental as such — is really driving the argument, and 'exclusion' by itself is a red herring."
