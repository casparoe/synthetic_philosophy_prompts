While reading about free will and moral responsibility on my own, I came across something that caught me off guard. P.F. Strawson, who argued in "Freedom and Resentment" that our reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, indignation—make sense whether or not determinism is true, turns out to be the father of Galen Strawson, who has argued, in what he calls the "Basic Argument," that nobody can ever be truly or ultimately morally responsible for anything, since nobody can be responsible for the very character that produces their actions in the first place. I can't tell whether the son is refuting the father, or simply asking a different question than the father was asking. Could you compare the two positions directly: what exactly does each Strawson think moral responsibility requires, and does Galen's argument actually undermine the ordinary, reactive-attitude-based responsibility his father describes, or could both be true at once? I would also value your own judgment on which Strawson comes closer to capturing what actually matters when we blame or forgive someone in everyday life.
