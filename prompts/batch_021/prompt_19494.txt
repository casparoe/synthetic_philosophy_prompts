I help moderate a smallish online forum for people who like inventing and solving number/logic puzzles -- nothing official, just a hobby thing that's been running about 8 years now. Every year one of us designs an "open puzzle" for the group, and this year's was basically: we've got about 40 people signed up for the meetup, and the challenge was to split them into groups of 5 across 6 rotating activity sessions so nobody repeats a groupmate across any session, plus a couple of other fiddly conditions I'd butcher trying to explain properly. Whoever solves it first gets their actual arrangement used as the real seating/grouping chart at the meetup, plus their name added to our little "wall of fame" page.

This year one of our longtime members (goes by Tesseract, don't ask) submitted a "solution" that isn't an actual grouping at all. He did some kind of averaging argument -- basically, if you imagine every possible way of making the groups and count up how often the bad condition shows up, the average comes out way under one bad case per arrangement, so atleast one arrangement out there has to have zero bad cases, meaning a valid solution definitely exists somewhere. I can follow the gist of it but not enough to check it myself. He wants the prize and the wall of fame slot for this. Problem is, we still don't have anything to actually seat people with at the meetup in three weeks, and he's admitted he has no clue how to find the specific arrangement his own proof says is out there among (his rough estimate) something like 10^40 possibilities -- way too many to stumble on by hand or even brute-force on somebody's laptop. Half the mod chat thinks this is a completely legit solve -- apparently real mathematicians get famous doing exactly this kind of proof, I've been reading about some guy named Erdős -- and the other half thinks the whole point of the puzzle was to produce something usable, and proving a needle exists somewhere in a haystack the size of a planet isn't the same as handing us the needle. I even fell down a wiki rabbit hole on some school of math philosophy, intuitionism or constructivism or something like that, where apparently a chunk of mathematicians don't think a proof like his should count as showing something "exists" at all unless you can actually produce it.

I've got to bring something to the mod chat before we run out of time to sort the real meetup logistics.
