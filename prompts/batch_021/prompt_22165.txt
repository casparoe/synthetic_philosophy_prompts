I'm a retired chemist who's spent the last year finally getting into philosophy properly, working through the Stanford Encyclopedia some evenings instead of pop-science books. This week I hit Fitch's paradox of knowability, and it kind of stopped me in my tracks: the proof that 'every truth is knowable' collapses into 'every truth is actually known' takes maybe five lines of ordinary modal logic, no fancy premises needed. In my old field, once you had a derivation that tight, the argument was over and everyone moved on. So I dont get why anti-realist and verificationist philosophers are still out there today defending the idea that truth can't outrun our capacity to know it - shouldn't Fitch's result have closed that case back in the 60s? What am I missing about why this hasn't ended the debate the way a result like that would in my field?
