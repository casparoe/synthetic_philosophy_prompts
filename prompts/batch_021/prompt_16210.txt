ok so this year I'm taking AP Physics and we just finished the special relativity unit, and completely separately I'm in my school's philosophy club, where we've been arguing about presentism vs eternalism — basically whether only the present moment is real, or whether the past and future are all "out there" existing just as much as right now. These two things collided in my head this week because one of the older kids in the club said relativity already settled this back in the 20th century and everyone still doing the philosophy side of it is just behind on the science.

His arguement was this thing about relativity of simultaneity — I think it's originally from some combination of a paper by a guy named Rietdijk and one by Hilary Putnam (not 100% sure I've got that exactly right), but the version I actually understood was Penrose's "Andromeda paradox" one, with two people walking past each other on a sidewalk, one walking toward Andromeda and one walking away from it. Because of how their "planes of simultaneity" tilt at that distance, what counts as happening "right now" on a planet near Andromeda ends up being like days apart for the two of them, just from ordinary walking speed. And the argument is basically: since there's no physical fact about which of them is really right about what's simultaneous way out there, and neither slice gets to count as more "real" than the other, you're stuck saying every event on both of those slices (and everyone else's slices too) is real — which just is eternalism, the whole block universe thing. So presentism is supposedly toast.

I feel like there has to be a decent comeback to this that isn't just "relativity is only about what we can measure, not about what's Really Real," because that feels like a cop-out that dodges the argument instead of answering it, but I honestly can't construct one myself that would survive someone who actually knows the physics well. Could you write the strongest genuine argument for presentism against this specific challenge? Like one that takes special relativity completely seriously — no denying it, and no "secretly there's a preferred frame, physics just hasn't found it" hand-wave unless you can actually defend that move against the obvious "isn't that just ad hoc" objection — and that directly engages with why the step from "no observer-independent simultaneity" to "all times are equally real" might not actually go through.
