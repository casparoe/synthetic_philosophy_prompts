Hey -- for the aesthetics unit I want an essay on the "aesthetic testimony" puzzle: can someone justifiably believe that, say, a poem is subtle or a chord change is genuinely moving just because a critic whose taste they trust says so, without ever engaging with the work themselves? Pessimists (the label's mostly down to Robert Hopkins) think theres something defective about a belief formed that way even when the testifier is dead reliable -- often by leaning on some kind of acquaintance requirement on aesthetic judgment, or on worries about aesthetic "autonomy," the idea that a verdict has to be earned through your own response to count as knowledge. Optimists think this is basically snobbery and that aesthetic testimony works like testimony about anything else. Lay out the strongest version of each side, including how this maps onto (and comes apart from) the more familiar debate over moral testimony, and then actually pick a winner -- under what conditions, if any, can testimony alone give someone real aesthetic knowledge, rather than mere true belief or secondhand parroting of a verdict? Don't just split the difference.
