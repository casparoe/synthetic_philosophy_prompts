I'm currently taking an upper-level seminar on normative ethics, and for the last two weeks we've been working through the trouble supererogation causes for act-consequentialism. The textbook worry, which goes back to Urmson's discussion of saints and heroes, is that if an act's rightness just consists in producing the best available outcome, then any act that improves on some other permissible, available option cannot merely be praiseworthy "extra" — on pain of contradiction it must actually be required, erasing the very distinction between duty and heroism that the category of supererogation is supposed to mark. Our professor presented two consequentialist attempts to make room for the category anyway. The first, satisficing consequentialism, weakens rightness from "optimal" to "good enough," so that any outcome clearing some threshold below the best is merely permissible, and anything better than that counts as supererogatory. The second, Portmore's dual-ranking act-consequentialism, keeps a fully maximizing standard for ranking outcomes impartially, but ties obligation instead to a separate, agent-relative ranking of what the agent has most reason to do, on which self-interested reasons can outweigh purely impartial ones — so the impartially best act can be morally best without being required.

For my term paper, I want to defend the claim that Portmore's view succeeds in securing genuine supererogation where satisficing consequentialism does not, on the grounds that the satisficing threshold is arbitrary in a way that agent-relative reasons are not. I can feel the intuitive pull of this asymmetry, but I haven't managed to state precisely what makes one move principled and the other ad hoc, and I'd like your help building the strongest version of the argument before I commit to it in writing. Specifically, I'd like you to do four things: spell out exactly what the standard arbitrariness objection to satisficing consequentialism amounts to; explain whether a structurally similar objection can be pressed against the agent-relative-reasons move in dual-ranking act-consequentialism, and if it can't, say precisely why not; consider the best reply a committed satisficer could offer in response; and finally, give your own honest assessment of whether the asymmetry I want to defend actually survives that scrutiny.
