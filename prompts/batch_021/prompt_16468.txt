I'm a novelist finishing a book whose narrator spends chapter after chapter insisting he had no real choice but to become the man he is, and I've been asked to interview, at an event tied to the launch, a philosopher known for defending Sartre's account of bad faith against its critics. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate bad faith as simply "lying to oneself." Include at least one question on the paradox of a single consciousness being simultaneously the deceiver and the deceived, and how bad faith is meant to differ from both an ordinary lie and simple self-ignorance; one on whether the café waiter really illustrates bad faith or merely diligent professionalism, since skilled role-play need not involve fleeing one's freedom; and one on how bad faith can count as a free choice at all if choosing it already presupposes the very freedom it's meant to suppress. For each of these, add a follow-up question keyed to the deflection she's most likely to reach for.
