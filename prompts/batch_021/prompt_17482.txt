Du Bois's "art as propaganda" thesis from "Criteria of Negro Art" against its Harlem Renaissance critics. Could you draft eight or nine questions, including at least one on how she squares Du Bois's claim that "all Art is propaganda and ever must be" with his simultaneous appeal to Beauty in that same essay as a standard that seems independent of any didactic purpose; one on how she answers the objection, pressed by Hughes and the younger Renaissance writers, that judging art by its propaganda value just trades white condescension for a Black respectability politics of its own — especially given Du Bois's own editorial distaste for novels like McKay's Home to Harlem; and one on whether the propaganda thesis is meant as a general theory of art as such, or as a claim tailored to the specific political emergency facing Black artists under Jim Crow, since those two readings seem to invite very different objections. For each, please add a follow-up question keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
