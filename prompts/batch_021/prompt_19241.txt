Quick one for a piece I'm noodling on about algorithmic risk-scoring — basically the whole ProPublica-vs-Northpointe COMPAS mess. I keep seeing different fairness definitions get invoked by different camps in these fights — calibration within groups, equalized odds, predictive parity, statistical/demographic parity, individual fairness, and counterfactual fairness — and Kleinberg/Mullainathan/Raghavan and Chouldechova already proved you cant have the main statistical ones simultaneously once base rates differ across groups. Say you're stuck designing a pretrial or lending tool and can only really commit to protecting ONE of those six when push comes to shove: how would you tier them, best to worst, in terms of which is most worth fighting to keep? Give it to me as a JSON tier list, S down to D, like:
```json
{
  "S": ["..."],
  "A": [...],
  "B": [...],
  "C": [...],
  "D": [...]
}
```
and toss a short clause next to each item on why it landed where it did.
