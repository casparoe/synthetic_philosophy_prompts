Okay so I've been down a rabbit hole on some effective altruism / rationalist blogs reading about "evidential cooperation in large worlds" (ECL) -- I think the older name for basically the same idea was "multiverse-wide cooperation via superrationality" or something close to that. The basic idea as I understand it: if the universe (or multiverse) is spatially infinite, or just really really big, there are tons of agents out there whose decisions are correlated with mine even though I could never causally interact with them or even find out what they did. So under evidential decision theory, me choosing to act cooperatively/altruistically toward value systems other than my own right now is evidence that my "correlated twins" elsewhere are doing the same in return, and this can net out to real gains from trade across wildly different values, no causal contact required.

I mentioned this to my housemate, who did a lot of Kant in undergrad, and she immediately said this is just the categorical imperative wearing a cosmology costume -- "act only according to that maxim by which you can at the same time will that it become a universal law," just re-derived with game theory and a big universe instead of pure reason. I wasn't convinced but honestly couldn't say exactly why not, since on the surface they do both seem to boil down to "act as if your decision procedure were shared by, or mirrored in, everyone else."

Could you actually dig into this comparison for me? Where does ECL structurally overlap with Kantian universalizability, and where does her analogy break down -- e.g. in terms of what's doing the justificatory work (a priori consistency of a maxim vs. contingent empirical facts about how large and correlated the world happens to be), what "universal" even means in each case (literally every rational being vs. just the subset of agents whose decision procedures are similar enough to yours to be correlated with it), and whether ECL even needs the other party to share your terminal values the way Kant's test presupposes a shared rational nature between you and everyone else. Not gonna lie, I still can't decide which of the two is more defensible as an actual action-guiding principle rather than just a cute thought experiment, so I'd definately love your final take on that too, with reasons.
