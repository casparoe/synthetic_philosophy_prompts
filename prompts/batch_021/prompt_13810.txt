I'm a PhD student wrapping up a prelim paper on metaphysical fundamentality, and I want to check a formal point before I lean on it further. Grounding is standardly treated as a strict partial order -- irreflexive, asymmetric, and transitive -- and this formal profile is typically what gets cited as the reason a grounding hierarchy cannot loop back on itself and must eventually bottom out in something fundamental. Given this, I don't see how papers that take seriously the possibility of an infinitely descending grounding series with no fundamental level at all (or, relatedly, gunky worlds with no mereological simples) can be formally coherent. Doesn't treating grounding as a strict partial order already rule out that possibility? What, precisely, would such authors have to deny in order to make room for it?
