I'm about two-thirds into my new novel and there's a scene I keep botching. Two of my characters—Neda, a philosopher of mind who's spent her whole career on classical Islamic philosophy, and Tomas, her old grad school rival who became a stubbornly reductionist neuroscientist—get snowed in together at a conference hotel bar, and I want their oldest running argument to resurface: Avicenna's "flying man" (I've also seen it called the "floating man"), the guy who's created whole and suspended in empty air, numb to his own limbs and blind to everything around him, who Avicenna says would still know that he exists even though he'd have zero grounds to affirm he even has a body. Neda thinks this shows the self can't just be the body or the brain; Tomas thinks the whole thing is a trick of the imagination dressed up as metaphysics.

Could you draft their exchange for me, maybe 600-900 words? I don't want Tomas to be a pushover who just restates "well maybe your imagining it wrong" a few different ways—I vaguely remember reading that there's a real objection along the lines of "conceivability doesn't get you all the way to possibility," kind of like what people say against Descartes' version of this move, plus something about the thought experiment quietly assuming there's already a unified someone doing the floating, which seems like cheating. And Neda shouldn't just recite Avicenna back at him either, she needs actual comebacks, since she's supposed to be the sharper of the two, at least on this subject. Basically I want two people who've had this exact fight before and know exactly where the other's soft spots are, landing real blows, not a Philosophy 101 pamphlet with two names slapped onto it.
