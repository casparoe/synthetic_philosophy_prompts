I spent something like thirty years as a conservation biologist, and a depressing chunk of that time went into what we euphemistically called "prioritization exercises" – i.e. deciding which populations or species we actually had the budget to save when we couldn't save them all. We basically always just did the arithmetic: more individuals, more genetic diversity, more evolutionary distinctiveness saved per dollar wins, roughly speaking. So it kind of rocked my world when a friend from my retirement book club (we've somehow drifted from novels to philosophy papers, don't ask) handed me Taurek's old "Should the Numbers Count?" paper, where he argues that if you can only save a group of five strangers or one different stranger, the mere fact that there's five of them doesn't by itself obligate you to save that group over the one, since there's no single person for whom the five actually "outweigh" the one — he even floats just flipping a coin as the fairer way to decide.

I've since fallen down a bit of a rabbit hole reading the responses to Taurek: Parfit's "Innumerate Ethics" pushback, Scanlon's contractualist attempt to rescue numbers through his version of the rescue case without going full aggregation, Kamm's much more elaborate balancing-argument stuff, various weighted-lottery proposals as a compromise between coin-flipping and just always saving the majority, and a newer wave of people like Otsuka pushing back on all of the above. Honestly at this point I cant tell anymore which of these is a live, respected position versus more of a historical curiosity nobody really defends today — probably a symptom of coming at this completely fresh at 68.

So here's my ask: could you give me your own ranking of the major positions on "whether the numbers should count" when we're forced to choose who to save? I'd like it as a tiered JSON list, something like:

Please throw in ones I haven't mentioned if they belong, and don't feel like you need to hedge everything — I'd rather see where you'd actually put your chips than a diplomatic non-answer.
