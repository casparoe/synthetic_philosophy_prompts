I am a PhD candidate in philosophy and the graduate-student representative on my university's Research Integrity Board. We are handling a case in which a PhD student, having admitted to fabricating control-group data in a co-authored manuscript, faces our standard first-offense sanction: a one-year suspension and forfeiture of her stipend. Her labmate and co-author, who is undisputedly innocent of any wrongdoing, has petitioned the Board for permission to serve the suspension and forfeit his own stipend in her place, since her suspension would terminate her visa status and end her PhD outright; he is not asking that she go unpunished, only that someone else bear the sanction assigned to her. Our guidelines are silent on whether a sanction may be discharged by someone other than the responsible party. The Board is split: several members note that we already let a lab absorb a student's fines without objection and want a principled reason to treat the suspension differently, while one member has invoked David Lewis's essay on penal substitution to press exactly this question. Please draft the memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting: it should recommend, with a concrete timeline, whether and on what terms to grant the petition, and it should engage seriously with the strongest case for granting it rather than simply asserting that a sanction cannot be transferred.
