It started from the classic "the present king of France is bald" case. His position was that once Russell says "the present king of France" doesn't denote anything (since there's no king), you end up in basically the same place as Frege or later Strawson: the sentence just has no determinate truth value, its neither true nor false, because the "logical subject" fails to pick anything out. He thinks Russell dressing this up with quantifiers and propositional functions is just a fancier repackaging of the same reference failure, not an actual escape from it. I was definately convinced the whole point of the paper was to avoid that outcome and get a real truth value (false, not gappy) out of the sentence, but when he asked me to explain how the quantifier analysis actually forces falsity instead of just relocating the "failure to denote" problem into the existential bit, I kind of fumbled it and we gave up and went to bed.

So can you settle this properly for us? Is my friend right that Russell's analysis, dressed up differently, reduces to the same truth-value-gap outcome as Frege and Strawson, or is there something specific about translating "the F is G" into something like "there is exactly one F, and it is G" that genuinely forces a determinate truth value even when the description fails to denote -- and if so, which piece of that translation is actually doing the work?
