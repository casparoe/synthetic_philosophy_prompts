quick one for a piece I'm noodling on about the logic of "if... then." I fell down a rabbit hole on David Lewis's 1976 triviality proof -- the one where he shows you basically can't have both (a) the probability of an indicative conditional always equal the conditional probability P(B|A), the way Adams's thesis says it should, and (b) that conditional being an ordinary proposition that updates by normal conditionalization like everything else -- do both and credence collapses into near total triviality. as I understand it this is the exact thing Stalnaker had hoped his own truth-conditional semantics for "if" (roughly: "if A, B" is true at a world when B holds at the closest A-world) would vindicate, and lewis's proof is generally taken to have kneecapped that hope.

could you write me a dialogue -- 15-20 exchanges, set right after the two of them sit through a talk on the proof -- between a philosopher who takes the result to show indicative conditionals just don't have truth conditions at all (à la Adams and Edgington: "if A, then B" is just a way of voicing your conditional credence, full stop, no proposition there for a truth-value to attach to) and a Stalnaker-style holdout who wants to keep truth conditions and has to actually explain how their semantics survives lewis's proof rather than just insist that it does. I want real engagement, each one responding to the other's specific last move rather than restating their opening position, and neither should come off as the strawman.
