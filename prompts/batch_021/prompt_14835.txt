I first came across the fink/mask distinction sideways, through a paper by Michael Fara that appeals to the idea of a "masked" ability, rather than a removed one, to defend compatibilism against certain worries about whether an agent could have done otherwise. That paper is actually what convinced me to sign up for a graduate seminar this term on powers and dispositions, since I wanted to understand masking in its native metaphysical habitat rather than only in its free-will application.

Over the past few weeks we've worked through C. B. Martin's original electro-fink counterexample to the simple conditional analysis of dispositions, David Lewis's reformed conditional analysis offered in response, and then Alexander Bird's poison-and-antidote case, which is supposed to show that masking is a genuinely separate problem that Lewis's fix does not solve. I tried to lay all this out in a short paper, and my instructor's comment on the draft was that I had "basically described a mask and called it a fink" -- not the most confidence-inspiring note to get back a few weeks before the final paper is due, especially since every summary I can reconstruct from memory or lecture notes seems to blur exactly the line I'm apparently getting wrong.

What I need now is a genuinely careful explanation of exactly where the two phenomena come apart, at a level of precision I could actually reuse in the paper. Concretely: in terms of what happens to the disposition's categorical (or intrinsic) basis, why does a finkish case involve that basis itself being gained or lost right around the moment of the stimulus, while a masked case involves the basis persisting intact with something else intervening further downstream in the causal process leading to the manifestation? And at that same level of precision, why is Lewis's requirement that the basis persist through the stimulus period generally thought to immunize the conditional analysis against finks while doing nothing at all against masks -- to the point that some responses in the literature apparently need to bolt on an entirely separate "no interference" clause? I'd also be curious, though this is very much secondary, whether adding such a clause is a legitimate repair or effectively an admission that the conditional-analysis project has quietly turned into something else; but the main thing I need is the fink/mask distinction itself, stated precisely enough that I can defend it if my instructor pushes back on it again.
