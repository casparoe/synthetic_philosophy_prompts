After years of encountering the Dutch Book argument on rationalist blogs as the standard justification for Bayesian coherence norms, I finally worked through the epistemic utility theory literature, beginning with Jim Joyce's accuracy-dominance argument for probabilism. My best reading is that it amounts to the same move in different clothing: instead of a bookie exploiting your incoherent betting prices for money, a legitimate scoring rule like the Brier score exploits your incoherent credences for accuracy points, and either way the incoherent agent is guaranteed to end up worse off no matter which world turns out to be actual. Given that the underlying exploitability structure appears identical, what exactly is the substantive difference that entitles Joyce's argument to count as a genuinely 'nonpragmatic' vindication of probabilism, rather than a more abstract restatement of the Dutch Book point?
