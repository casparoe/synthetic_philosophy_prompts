Over dinner last night, a friend and I ended up in a long, unresolved argument about transworld identity versus counterpart theory. Following Kripke's well-known objection to Lewis, they insisted that when we say Hubert Humphrey could have won the 1968 election, we are talking about Humphrey himself existing in another possible world, not about some merely resembling stand-in who wins there instead. I countered, in Lewis's spirit, that counterpart theory secures the truth of that claim just as well without positing one and the same individual spread across worlds, and that the rival view runs into real trouble explaining how a single individual could bear incompatible intrinsic properties — a different height, a different temperament — in different worlds. Neither of us budged, so I want to approach the disagreement from a different angle.

Write a short story of roughly 800–1200 words that dramatizes this dispute rather than arguing for either side directly. The plot should turn on a character who, through some device internal to the story, comes into contact with what may or may not be a counterpart of themselves in another possible world, such that something practically or emotionally significant hinges on whether that other person truly is them or merely resembles them. The story need not settle the metaphysical question, but it should make vivid why the difference is not merely verbal.
