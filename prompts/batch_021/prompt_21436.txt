So I started a new job about two months ago doing QA on my company's customer support chatbot - I read through flagged conversations where something went wrong and write up short incident reports for the engineering team. My manager wants these written in plain language, stuff like "the bot assumed the customer meant their most recent order" or "it got confused about which plan the customer was on" or "it tried to hand off to a human but the intent didn't register so it just looped." One of my teammates keeps flagging this in review though - his argument is that the model doesn't assume or get confused or try anything, its just outputting tokens based on patterns, and writing it up like that will eventually get engineers (and execs skimming the reports) thinking there's some little mind in there making choices, which isn't true and could lead to bad calls down the line, like blaming "the bot's judgement" instead of fixing the actual training data or prompt.

I kind of see his point, but when I pushed back and asked if we should also stop saying the thermostat in our office "thinks" its too warm, or that my GPS "wants" me to take the next exit, he didn't love the comparison but couldn't really explain why the chatbot is any different and not just more complicated. Honestly I catch myself doing the same thing when I use these tools for my own writing too, I'll say something like "oh it thinks I meant the shorter draft" without even noticing.

Now I'm stuck writing these reports every week and genuinely don't know where the line is, or if there's even a principled one, between language that's harmless shorthand and language that's actively misleading people about what's going on inside the system. Is my teammate right that we should just ban this kind of talk in the reports, or is he drawing a line that doesn't really hold up once you think it through? I've got a comms background, not CS, so my instincts here are more about clarity than the technical side, but it feels like a bigger question than just a style-guide call.
