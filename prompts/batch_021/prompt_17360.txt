A few of us are founding a small political party that grew out of our city's tenant organizing scene, and we're stuck on one clause in the draft constitution: should we let organized internal caucuses (an eco-socialist caucus, a "tenant power" caucus, whatever people want to form) exist and run joint slates in our internal elections, or ban factions outright? One of the founders keeps citing Simone Weil's essay on abolishing political parties, arguing that her whole case against parties — that they run on collective passion, pressure members to defend the group's line instead of actually thinking things through, and end up caring more about their own growth then about truth or justice — applies just as much to a caucus inside a party as to a party inside a democracy, so we should ban them now before they calcify. A few other founders think that's paranoid and that banning factions just drives disagreement into informal cliques with none of the accountability. I have to bring a recommendation to the founding congress in two weeks — can you draft the memo: allow, ban, or something in between, and don't just wave away the anti-faction argument, it actually needs to be grappled with.
