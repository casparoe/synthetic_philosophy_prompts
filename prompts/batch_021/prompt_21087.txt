I write about behavioral science and decision-making for a living, which is probably why a minor HR policy at my own office has been nagging at me more than it should.

My firm runs an annual bonus draw for its sales team, and I've now been through two versions of it, a year apart, in a way that's left me wondering whether I was consistent.

Last year the choice was between a guaranteed $4,000 bonus and a draw with a 96% chance of that same $4,000, a 3% chance of $20,000, and a 1% chance of nothing; I took the guaranteed $4,000. This year there was no safe option, only a draw with a 4% chance of $4,000 (96% chance of nothing) against a draw with a 3% chance of $20,000 (97% chance of nothing); this time I went for the long shot at $20,000.

A colleague with an economics background says the two choices can't both be rational: if I truly preferred the sure $4,000 last year, consistency requires preferring the 4%-shot at $4,000 over the 3%-shot at $20,000 this year too, so switching to the long shot means one of my choices had to be a mistake. Both felt obviously right at the time, and I can't immediately say what's wrong with his reasoning. Is he right that I've contradicted myself, or is his standard of rationality the thing that's off?
