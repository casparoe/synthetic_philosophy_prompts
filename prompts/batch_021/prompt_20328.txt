So I had this whole argument with my friend Dan this weekend that started as a dumb game and spiraled into an actual two-hour fight about what "rational" even means, lol. We were using one of those custom wheel-spinner sites to decide how to split some leftover cash from our group trip fund, and Dan, who just finished a game theory class and loves this kind of stuff, rigged up two separate spins with numbers he picked himself, kind of as a bit. In the first one I had to choose between (a) a guaranteed $400 for the fund, or (b) a spin where 89% lands on $400, 10% lands on $2000, and 1% lands on $0 - I took the guaranteed $400 without even hesitating. Then for the second spin the choices were (c) an 11% shot at $400 and otherwise nothing, versus (d) a 10% shot at $2000 and otherwise nothing, and this time I went with (d), figuring that since I'm probably walking away with nothing either way, I might as well go for the bigger number.

Dan pounced on this immediately and said the two picks dont add up: he claims that if you actually work out the math, there's no single consistent way of valuing the outcomes that makes both of my choices the "rational" one at the same time, so one of them has to be a mistake. I tried explaining that giving up a sure thing just feels way worse than giving up a long shot, even though the odds move by the same amount on paper either way, but he said feelings aren't a justification and that I basically just proved his point about people being bad at risk. Part of me thinks he's technically right and I should just concede, but another part feels like there's got to be a principled reason my preferences aren't crazy even if they don't fit whatever rule he's using. Is he right that I was being irrational here, or is there a real case that his standard of "rational" is too narrow? Kind of need an actual tiebreaker since neither of us is backing down.
