ok this might be a dumb question but it's been nagging me while I prep for my AI safety reading group — doesn't Hadfield-Menell's off-switch game basically settle the shutdown problem already? The idea is that if you keep the agent uncertain about its true reward instead of hardcoding one in, letting the human hit the switch becomes the expected-utility-maximizing move for the agent, since the human's action is evidence about the reward. So corrigibility just falls out for free once you add uncertainty — no tension with being a rational expected-utility maximizer at all. Why do people still treat this as a deep open problem and write whole papers on incomplete preferences and utility indifference, as if "just make the agent uncertain about its reward" isn't already a formal proof that a rational agent won't resist being shut down?
