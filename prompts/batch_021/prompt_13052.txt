A student came to my office hours last week, visibly unsettled after finishing an introductory astronomy course. Having taken in the scale of the observable universe and the billions of years of cosmic history in which human beings are a late and vanishingly brief arrival, she had concluded that nothing she or anyone else does can really matter. When I suggested that meaning need not be measured against the universe, and that it is enough for our lives to matter to the people around us, she objected that this merely changes the subject rather than explaining why size and duration should bear on importance at all. I would like to give her a genuine answer rather than a consolation: is there a way to grant her every fact about the universe's scale and still show that her inference from those facts to the conclusion that nothing matters does not actually go through?
