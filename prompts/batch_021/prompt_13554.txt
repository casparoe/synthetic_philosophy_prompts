Two friends of mine, Marco and Elena, got into it at dinner over a mutual friend who cheated on his girlfriend. Marco said you genuinely can't blame the guy: he is who he is -- impulsive, insecure, basically shaped into that by his own dad -- and none of that was ever up to him. He'd just read about Galen Strawson's 'Basic Argument' and ran through it: you act the way you do because of how you are; to be truly responsible for how you act you'd have to be truly responsible for how you are; but whenever you point to yourself 'shaping' your own character, that shaping was itself done by an earlier you that you also didn't choose, so it regresses forever and nobody's ever ultimately responsible for anything -- and it's not even about determinism, since a random uncaused impulse wouldn't be 'you' being responsible either, just a different flavor of luck. Elena said that's a neat trick but it proves way too much: by that logic not even God could be responsible for his own nature, since 'choosing' it would require reasons coming from a nature he already had, or no reason at all. Marco said: right, exactly, that's not a bug, that's the whole point -- it shows 'ultimate' responsibility is impossible for any being whatsoever, not just unlucky humans, which is what makes it a real argument instead of a cheap shot. Elena said fine, but then that kind of responsibility was never a coherent thing to want in the first place, so losing it costs us nothing -- ordinary responsibility, acting from your own settled values and being responsive to reasons, survives untouched, and that's all blame ever actually needed. Marco said the timing is suspicious: Elena's swapping in a cheaper notion of responsibility right when the expensive one turns out unaffordable, conveniently letting her keep being mad at the guy. Neither one budged. Can you go through it move by move, say whos right at each step, and point out anything both of them are missing?
