Okay, this one's been rattling around in my head since it came up in an old LessWrong-adjacent thread about near-death experiences and what, if anything, they should do to your credences. You probably already know the story: A.J. Ayer, the logical positivist, choked on something (a piece of fish, I want to say?) back in 1988 and was clinically dead for a few minutes. When he came back he wrote this whole essay about seeing a bright red light and some "ministers" in charge of space who apparently weren't doing a great job of it -- and then, famously, hedged the entire thing by saying it had only "slightly weakened" his conviction that death is the end. He very deliberately did not convert to anything, he just got marginally less smug about his atheism afterward, which I personally find hilarious.

What really gets me is the tension with his own verification principal -- dude built his whole career on the idea that a sentence like "there is life after death" is literally meaningless unless you can say what observation would confirm or disconfirm it, and then he arguably has the single most relevant experience a person could possibly have on that exact question, and his training is apparently so deep that he still won't fully commit either way. Can you write me a short joke about this -- one-liner, Q&A, mini dialogue, whatever format works best -- that actually turns on the substance of verificationism and Ayer's specific reaction to it, rather than just being a generic "old philosopher nearly dies, hijinks ensue" bit? Basically I want someone who's actually read Language, Truth and Logic to get noticeably more out of it than someone who's just vaguely heard of the guy.
