So I've been down a rabbit hole about mech interp realism lately and I keep failing to resolve it in my own head, so let me outsource the thinking a bit. The original Olah-school "circuits" agenda basically bets on three claims: features are the fundamental unit of neural nets, features compose into circuits, and (this is the one I find spiciest) roughly the same features/circuits show up across different models and tasks trained on similar data -- "universality." Taken seriously that sounds like a strong realist claim, like these are natural kinds the network is basically forced into, a mini natural-abstraction-hypothesis playing out in the weights. But then theres also superposition and polysemanticity, the fact that which "feature" you even find depends on your SAE's dictionary size, sparsity penalty, and random seed, plus those interpretability-illusion results showing a neuron's apparent "meaning" can flip depending on which dataset you probe it with -- and all that pushes me toward thinking features/circuits are more like Dennett's real patterns: a stance we adopt because it predicts behavior well, not a discovery of joints that were already sitting there in the matrix before we went looking.

Can you write me a dialogue between two people hashing this out -- say, two friends who stay behind after a mech interp reading group and end up arguing until whoever runs the venue starts stacking chairs around them. One is a card-carrying realist defending something close to the full Olah picture, including universality. The other is a Dennett-flavored deflationist who thinks "real" is doing way more work in the realist's mouth than it can support. I want actual back-and-forth, not two monologues talking past each other: the realist needs a real answer to the basis-dependence/hyperparameter problem instead of just shrugging it off, and the deflationist has to genuinely grapple with universality and with cases where interp findings generated novel, falsifiable predictions that panned out. At some point I'd like them to try tabooing the word "real" the way the Sequences are always on about with verbal disputes, and see whether the disagreement actually survives that or dissolves into people just preferring different words. Don't resolve it neatly at the end -- I'd rather it close with both of them still disagreeing, but now able to state clearly what they're disagreeing about.
