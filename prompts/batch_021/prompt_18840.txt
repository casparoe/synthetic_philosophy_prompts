So a few weeks ago I went down a total rabbit hole on decision theory videos on youtube (started with the sleeping beauty problem, ended up somewhere way weirder) and found this thing called the absent-minded driver problem. The setup is basically: there's a driver on a highway with two exits that look completely identical from the inside, and he needs to take the second one to get home, but the puzzle assumes he has literally zero memory of whether he's already passed the first exit by the time he's sitting at an intersection - like his memory doesn't carry over between the two spots at all. So he's stuck using the exact same strategy (some probability of continuing vs getting off) at both places, because from where he's sitting in the moment they're indistinguishable. Getting off too early lands him somewhere he doesn't want, missing both of them completely lands him somewhere else he doesn't want, and only hitting the second one exactly gets him home.

Here's the part thats been messing with my head for days. If you work out the best probability of continuing ahead of time, before the drive even starts, like a normal planning problem, you get one specific number (I think it comes out to 2/3, don't hold me to the exact fraction). But apparently if you're actually sitting at the intersection in the moment and reason it out fresh - like "given that I'm at some intersection right now, what's the chance this is the first one vs the second one" - and then use that belief to decide what to do, you land on a different answer, even though it's supposedly the exact same rational guy using the exact same information the whole time, nothing new has happened to him in between. Some sources call this the difference between the "planning optimal" strategy and the "action optimal" one. I've read a couple explanations of why this happens and they mostly just restate the math without it ever clicking for me intuitively. Can you explain, in plain english, why the beforehand answer and the in-the-moment answer don't just agree, and what that's actually revealing about rationality or memory or self-location or whatever's really going on underneath it? I don't need the formal proof, more the version where it actually makes sense in your gut.
