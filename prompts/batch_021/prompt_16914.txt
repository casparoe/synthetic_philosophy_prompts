So a while back I went down a rabbit hole on Henry Sidgwick (the utilitarianism guy from way back) and found out that besides writing his big ethics book, he spent like the last two decades of his life co-founding and running a society that investigated ghosts, mediums, telepathy, all that spooky stuff. At first I figured it was just a weird hobby totally separate from his philosophy, but its not - in the ethics book he talks about this "dualism of practical reason," basically that he couldn't find any rational proof that doing your duty and looking after your own self interest always line up, and without that the whole edifice of ethics kinda wobbles. His hoped-for fix was that if you could actually prove, scientifically, that there's an afterlife where the universe balances the moral books (good rewarded, bad punished), that would patch the hole.

I've been chewing on this alot and my gut says that even if his ghost-hunting society had found airtight proof of that kind of afterlife, it still wouldn't have actually solved the dualism, it just would've made it stop mattering in practice. Can you make the strongest case you can for that - that proving cosmic justice is real doesn't resolve the underlying rational tension between the self-interested standpoint and what he calls the "point of view of the universe," it just makes the conflict never come up? Dig into why empirical proof couldn't do the philosophical job he needed it to do, don't just summarize his ethics for me.
