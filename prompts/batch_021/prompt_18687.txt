So this argument happened at a work happy hour a few weeks back and its been rattling around in my head since. Our team lead pulled up the weather app that said "70% chance of rain" for that afternoon, it never rained, and Dana said "well that forecast was just wrong." Marcus jumped in and it turned into a whole thing. Roughly in order:

1. Marcus said you can't call a single 70% forecast "wrong" just because it didn't rain that one time - a number like that was never a claim about this one particular afternoon in the first place.
2. Dana said ok, then what group of afternoons IS it a claim about - every day in history with a similar radar pattern? every day this month? every day in this city? - because different choices of group hand you different answers for the exact same afternoon, so the forecaster could just pick whichever group makes them look right after the fact.
3. Marcus admitted that's a real problem but said it doesn't make forecasts meaningless, it just means the model has to fix its reference group in advance, before the outcome, not cherry-pick it afterward.
4. Dana said maybe it's simpler to just drop the idea of a "real" chance of rain on one particular afternoon altogether, and say the 70% is just the model's own confidence, checked over time by whether its confident calls pan out at roughly the rate claimed.
5. Marcus said that gives up too much, because it treats the weather like its uncertain only because we don't know enough, when there's presumably some actual physical chanciness in the atmosphere that day, not just in our model of it.
6. Dana said chaotic isn't the same as chancy though - a chaotic system is still deterministic under the hood, so with a good enough computer and perfect starting data you could in principle compute exactly what it's going to do, and calling that "physical chance" smuggles in more than ordinary weather chaos actually gives you.

Nobody won, everybody just had to go back to their desks. I don't know enough probability theory to sort out who had the better of it at each step.
