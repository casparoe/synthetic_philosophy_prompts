I'm a PhD student in cognitive psychology. At my qualifying exam, I presented a well-known finding from our subfield as settled fact, the way it is stated in every textbook. A committee member later had me track down the original study, and it turned out the data never really supported the claim — the interpretation had simply propagated for years through uncritical citation. As it happens, independent, more rigorous work since then does support the same conclusion, so what I said was true. My advisor says that settles it, since I was right in the end. But my only actual basis for the claim at the time was an unchecked citation chain, and that still bothers me. Was there something improper about how I asserted it, even though it happens to be true?
