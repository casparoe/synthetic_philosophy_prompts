Okay this is going to sound dumb but it's been legitimately bugging me, and it happened, of all places, in my social philosophy seminar. There's like 15 of us in a room that seats 25, no assigned seats, nothing official, but by the second week everyone had just kind of picked a spot and stuck with it for the rest of the semester. Fast forward to week 9, a girl transfers in from the other section and sits down in the chair my friend Mia has used since day one. Mia didnt say anything to her face but was fuming after class about how "that's literally my seat," and got kind of annoyed at me for not immediately taking her side. To be fair to her, it's not COMPLETELY baseless: our professor started handing back graded essays in little piles based on where everyone sits, because she got sick of calling names one by one, so there's now technically a tiny practical reason to keep sitting in the same place.

What me and my friends couldn't settle over lunch (we burned like 40 minutes on this instead of studying for the actual midterm, RIP) is whether Mia has an actual legitimate complaint or whether she's just being territorial and dressing it up as a principle. Nobody ever agreed to a seating rule out loud, no one signed up for anything, and nothing is physically stopping the new girl from sitting wherever. And yet somehow, within like two weeks, it started to feel like there WAS a rule, to the point that sitting in "someone else's" spot felt like an actual violation instead of an honest mixup. So where is that "should" even coming from if no one ever explicitly decided anything? Does it matter that the whole arrangement was completely random to begin with, that it definately could've just as easily shaken out with Mia two seats over and it'd feel exactly as "hers" right now? I want an actual answer, not just "people get territorial," because that doesn't explain why the rest of the friend group instinctively sided with Mia even though we all agreed, out loud, that no such rule was ever officially real. Basically: is Mia onto something real here, or are we all just being weirdly tribal about a chair?
