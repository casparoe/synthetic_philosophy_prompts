I teach medieval Jewish philosophy and sit on my synagogue's ritual committee, which is finalizing a new Yom Kippur machzor before it goes to press next month. The sticking point is the Avodah service, the traditional liturgy that re-enacts the ancient Temple's sacrificial rite in graphic detail and then voices the hope that it be restored. One faction wants that hope kept as a literal petition, since our current machzor softens it into a vague plea to restore "worship," which they consider an evasive fudge of what the traditional text actually asks for. A second faction wants to keep the softened wording, pointing to Maimonides, who in Guide III:32 treats the sacrificial cult itself as a concession -- instituted only because the Israelites could not yet be weaned off the idolatrous worship they already knew, an intermediate stage on the way to the higher worship of prayer -- so that praying for sacrifice's literal return mistakes a historical accommodation for an ideal. A third member objects that this move is selective: the very same Maimonides devotes two entire books of the Mishneh Torah (Sefer Avodah and Sefer Korbanot) to sacrificial and Temple law in exhaustive, unapologetic detail, and rules in the Laws of Kings that the service resumes once the messianic Temple is rebuilt. I need a one-page recommendation to bring to next week's vote. Please draft it: take a clear position on the wording, and address head-on whether the "mere accommodation" reading of Maimonides can survive contact with his own law code, rather than letting each faction simply invoke whichever Maimonides is convenient.
