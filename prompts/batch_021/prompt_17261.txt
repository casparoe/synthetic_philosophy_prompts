One of my favorite former grad students is getting married in a few weeks and asked me to give the toast, which is a little terrifying -- half the room is my department and will catch every reference, the other half is her family, who as far as I know have never discussed ethics on purpose in their lives. Here's what I want to build it around: she's an animal-welfare utilitarian who thinks eating meat is a real wrong but never gave much thought to flying; her fiance thinks flying is a real climate wrong but has zero qualms about a burger; and somewhere in the last few years together they basically struck a moral trade -- he went vegetarian, she barely flys anymore -- without either one changing their mind about the other's issue at all. (I think this is basically the Toby Ord thing, gains from trade across a moral disagreement instead of within a market.) I want the toast to use that idea to say something true and a little moving about the two of them, but it can NOT come across as "they compromised their principles for each other," since that's exactly backwards and would honestly annoy both of them a bit -- the whole point of a moral trade is that nobody waters anything down. Can you write me something like a five-minute toast that pulls this off for both halves of the room?
