My friend and I spent most of a long train ride arguing about infinite ethics, and specifically about what Nick Bostrom calls "infinitarian paralysis": the worry that if the universe already contains an infinite amount of value, then any finite good or bad thing anyone does leaves the total exactly as it was, since no finite difference can alter an infinite sum—so total-sum consequentialism seems to entail that nothing any of us does actually matters. My friend took this as a decisive refutation of aggregative consequentialism; I thought the better response was to give up on totals (switching to averages, say, or to some more local, dominance-based criterion) rather than concede that our actions are morally inert. Neither of us budged.

Write me a joke about infinitarian paralysis. It should be funny to someone who genuinely understands the problem, meaning the punchline needs to turn on the real structure of the argument (or on one of the standard escapes from it), rather than being a generic joke about infinity or about navel-gazing philosophers.
