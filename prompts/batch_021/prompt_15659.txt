I've been reading up on expressivism in moral philosophy for a piece on the state of play in metaethics, and the Frege-Geach problem seems to settle the debate more decisively than philosophers let on. As I understand it, the fact that a sentence like "lying is wrong" can sit unasserted inside a conditional (as in "if lying is wrong, then getting your little brother to lie is wrong") and still support a valid modus ponens shows that the sentence carries the very same truth-apt content whether it's asserted or embedded — which is exactly what expressivists deny moral claims have. If the logic really is that clean, why do philosophers like Simon Blackburn and Allan Gibbard keep building increasingly elaborate expressivist theories instead of just conceding that cognitivism wins this particular argument?
