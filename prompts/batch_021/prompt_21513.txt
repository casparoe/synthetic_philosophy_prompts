Okay so this is kind of a random ask but here goes. I'm on my school's Youth Giving Board — it's this program our school runs with a local community foundation where a small group of us get real grant money (its $15,000 this year) to give to a nonprofit by the end of the semester. We're down to two finalists and the group is completely deadlocked. One is "Bright Start," which gives micronutrient supplements to malnourished kids in one district and has a stack of actual studies showing it reliably cuts stunting for like $50 a kid — nobody disputes the near-term numbers are great. The other is the "Delta Rights Collective," a scrappy little group trying to get the regional government to rewrite water-rights law; if it actually works it could reshape water access for way more people than Bright Start ever touches, but it's just as likely to go nowhere, or even backfire on farmers who currently get by on informal access, and honestly no one can say which.

We got into a huge argument about this on Wednesday. Half the group (led by this girl Aisha) says it's obvious: fund whoever has the cleanest evidence and biggest "good per dollar," so Bright Start, done. But my friend Marcus dug up this old EA forum post about something people call "cluelessness" that's been stuck in my head since — basically the idea that once you trace out ALL the effects of either choice (more kids surviving into an already resource-strained area vs. a policy fight that ripples through the local economy for decades) you genuinely have no clue whether you're helping or hurting on net, so "just fund whoever does the most expected good" isn't the neutral, safe default it looks like, it just LOOKS that way bc we're quietly ignoring most of the consequences. Then somebody pointed out we're exactly as clueless about Delta Rights's long-term effects, so that can't be a reason to prefer it either, and now everyone's just annoyed and stuck.

Our advisor wants everyone on the "steering" sub-group (I got voted onto it, lucky me) to bring in a one-page memo Tuesday actually reccomending something — full funding to one, a split, whatever — with real reasoning behind it, not a philosophy lecture and not just "more evidence = better." Could you write that memo for me? It should land on one clear recommendation, but it actually has to reckon with the cluelessness problem instead of quietly ignoring it like Aisha's side is doing, and it should be fair to why smart people in the group are still tempted by whichever option it doesn't end up picking.
