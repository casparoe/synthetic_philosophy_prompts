So I'm about two-thirds through a novel and there's a scene I can't quite pull off on my own. Two characters, Mara (a game theorist) and Dev (her ex from grad school, now a philosophy prof), are at a dinner party where someone talks them into demonstrating the centipede game for the table with real cash — a small pot that doubles every time it gets passed, four or five rounds if nobody grabs it. Dev ends up holding the pot at a point where passing it again would still be the better move for both of them long-run, and he pockets it instead, which kills the mood at the table (there's history between these two I'm handling seperately in the book, so don't worry about that part, just the argument). I went down a rabbit hole a few weeks back trying to actually understand why grabbing early is supposed to be the "rational" choice, and I don't trust myself to reproduce that argument accurately, which is where you come in.

What I need is the fight the two of them have once the other guests wander off, and I want it to be the real disagreement rather than two people trading "it's rational" against "it's not nice." Mara should make the honest case for backward induction — that once you unravel the game from the last round, there's no non-arbitrary round to stop unraveling at, so taking the money as soon as it's in your hands is the only subgame-perfect equilibrium there is. Dev should hit back with the actual paradox in that reasoning, not just his hurt feelings: if Mara genuinely had common knowledge that he was rational straight through, the game getting as far as it did before he grabbed should already have told her that assumption was false, so what was she even entitled to believe about him in the meantime? Mara needs a real answer to that rather than restating backward induction louder — something in the spirit of what I think was Aumann's rebuttal, that common knowledge of rationality doesn't just fall apart the moment an unexpected node gets reached — and then Dev gets one more genuine push back, not a mic-drop. It's important to me that Mara is defending the logic that cost her the bigger pot and Dev is attacking the logic that just made him richer, so let that irony do some work if it helps.

Both of them already know cold what backward induction and the centipede game are, so skip any throat-clearing and drop me straight into the argument itself — ten to twelve lines total, alternating, no stage directions, just what they'd actually say to each other. It should hold up for a reader who does game theory for a living, not just for me.
