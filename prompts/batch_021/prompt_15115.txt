I am a PhD candidate in philosophy specializing in normative ethics, and for the past year I have served as the de facto ethics consultant on my hospital's Crisis Standards of Care task force, which is now revising our ventilator-allocation protocol ahead of next winter's respiratory-virus season. The protocol we improvised during the last surge ranked patients strictly by SOFA-score-derived survival probability and reassigned ventilators away from lower-scoring patients whenever a higher-scoring one arrived, on the reasoning that maximizing expected lives saved is simply what fairness requires once a resource cannot stretch to everyone who needs it. Two members of the task force -- an intensivist and our hospital's disability-rights liaison -- spent the summer reading in the philosophical literature and are now pushing back hard, essentially reviving Taurek's argument that treating a larger number of separate people's survival as simply outweighing a smaller number quietly assumes we can sum distinct people's stakes into one aggregate loss, when arguably each person's claim to their own life is complete in itself and is not diminished by how many other people also happen to be at risk. Rather than strict maximization, they want the task force to adopt some form of weighted lottery among everyone who clears a minimum threshold of expected medical benefit -- the kind of middle path between strict maximizing and a simple coin toss that keeps surfacing in the literature responding to Taurek. The rest of the task force, more sympathetic to Parfit's rejoinder that ignoring the numbers altogether is its own kind of unfairness to those we could have saved, worries that any lottery, weighted or not, means knowingly standing by while we could have saved more people -- a conclusion they see as indefensible given how publicly accountable this protocol will be.

I have been asked to write the first draft of the memo the task force will discuss and vote on next month, and I would like your help producing it. Please draft a memo that recommends one specific protocol design -- stating the clinical exclusion criteria, whether and how maximization, a weighted lottery, or some combination of the two applies (and at which stage of triage), and how reassignment would work for a patient already on a ventilator -- together with a realistic timeline for finalizing the protocol's language, training staff, and putting it into effect. Because the disagreement here is genuinely philosophical rather than clinical, I need the memo to take the anti-aggregative worry seriously on its own terms rather than raise it only to set it aside, while still giving the maximizers a real answer for why treating the numbers as automatically decisive is not simply the obvious default. I would rather you commit to a defensible position than split the difference for the sake of appearing neutral.
