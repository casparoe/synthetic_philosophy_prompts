A few of us in an informal philosophy-of-math reading group have spent the last few weeks slowly working through the whole saga around the continuum hypothesis — the Gödel and Cohen independence results, and then the later "search for new axioms" stuff that Gödel himself kicked off and that people like Woodin and others picked up once it became clear ZFC alone was never going to settle CH one way or the other. None of us are actual set theorists, just philosophy people who got sucked into this, but honestly its become the most heated part of the whole group. Some people are way more persuaded by the forcing-axiom side of things (Martin's Maximum, PFA) than by anything like V=L, someone else thinks the whole "there's one true answer waiting to be discovered" framing is misguided and that Hamkins-style multiverse pluralism is actually the more intellectually honest stance, and somebody dug up that old Freiling symmetry/dart-throwing argument from the 80s that nobody could agree was genuinely insightful vs. just smuggling in the conclusion it wants to reach.

The candidates we've been arguing over: V=L, forcing axioms like PFA/Martin's Maximum, Woodin's Ultimate L program, Freiling's axiom of symmetry, large cardinal axioms in general, and Hamkins' generic multiverse view.
