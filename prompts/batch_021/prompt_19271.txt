My friend Meera was on our hospital's covid ethics committee, and she pushed hard for using a lottery to decide who got the new antiviral when there wasn't enough for everyone who qualified, instead of just giving it to whoever the doctors ranked as most likely to benefit. She got a lot of grief for it, people still bring it up like she personally cost lives for the sake of "fairness." She's leaving for a job at another hospital, and a few of us are doing a dinner for her — I offered to say something. I recently fell down a rabbit hole about a philosopher, i think his name was Taurek, who argued something similar decades ago: you shouldn't just let the numbers decide, because each person only ever looses their own one life, no matter how many other people there are. Can you write me the toast? Like three minutes, something I'd actually stand up and read with a drink in my hand — I want it to make the real case that what she did held up, not just shrug and say "it's complicated," but it also has to land for the nurses in the room who are still kinda sore about it, without pretending her way didn't cost anything.
