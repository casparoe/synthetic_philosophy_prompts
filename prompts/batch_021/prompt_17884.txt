Here's roughly how it went. (1) Theo opened by claiming that no fully rational agent can be genuinely indifferent to being shut down: since shutdown forecloses whatever else the agent could otherwise do, an agent that ranks all its options ends up with some preference, positive or negative, about whether/when it happens, so real indifference requires breaking one of the normal rationality assumptions somewhere. (2) Nora pushed back with what she called the standard fix: add a correction term to the reward, sized so that whatever the agent would've earned by continuing to run gets exactly cancelled out by an equivalent reward for shutting down. Nothing irrational about the agent, she said — you've just rigged the numbers so continuing and stopping are permanently tied. (3) Theo replied that the tie only holds at the instant it's calculated, since the correction has to be based on the agent's current beliefs about what continuing would've earned it — which gives the agent every incentive to shape those beliefs, or shape events, so the correction comes out in its favor (delay bad news, blur the line between "shut down" and "still running," whatever it takes). (4) Nora granted that the naive version of the trick has that hole, but said the fix people actually reach for now doesn't rig a tie at all — it just lets the agent have no preference whatsoever about how long it keeps running, so theres nothing to manipulate because there's no ranking to move in the first place. And the standard objection, that incomplete preferences always get "money-pumped" into completeness anyway, assumes the agent will pay, step by step, to trade between options it doesn't rank; an agent that simply declines those trades can't be pumped. (5) Theo answered that "declining the trades" isn't a free consequence of merely lacking a preference, it's a whole separate policy bolted onto the agent — and if the fix only works because you've assumed that policy in advance, then the incompleteness isn't doing any real work, the bolted-on policy is, so she should just admit that's her actual proposal.
