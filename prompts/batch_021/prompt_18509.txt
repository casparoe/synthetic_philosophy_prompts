So I fell down a rabbit hole this week on Integrated Information Theory (IIT) after an argument broke out in my team's Slack about whether our new LLM agent could be "a tiny bit conscious." Somebody linked Scott Aaronson's old blog post trashing the theory, and that sent me further down the hole into Tononi's actual papers. Here's the thing that's messing with my head as a programmer with zero formal philosophy training: the name "integrated INFORMATION theory" made me assume it'd basically be a computational/functionalist story, like same input-output behavior and information processing = same consciousness, substrate be damned, kind of like how a sorting algorithm is "the same algorithm" wether it's running in Python or punched into cards. Turns out that's basically backwards from what IIT actually claims - the exact physical/causal wiring of the substrate matters enormously, to the point where two systems computing the literal same function can end up with wildly different amounts of "phi."

I want to sanity check whether I've actually understood the theory's internal logic here (not whether I buy the theory itself, no idea if it's good science).
