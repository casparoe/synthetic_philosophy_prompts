I sit on my state's clemency board, and next week's closed session includes a petition that has split us in an unusual way. The petitioner's lawyer has filed a brief built on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: our client did what he did because of the way he is; no one can be truly responsible for the way they are, since any earlier choice that shaped his character was itself made because of the way he already was at the time, and the regress has to bottom out in genetic and environmental luck he never chose; therefore he cannot be responsible for his crime in the basic-desert sense our guidelines invoke when they ask whether he has "accepted responsibility." The brief goes out of its way to note that the argument doesn't depend on determinism, so the usual quantum-indeterminism reply doesn't touch it. Half the board wants to strike this as a rhetorical trick; the other half can't find the flaw. Our options are to grant clemency outright, deny the petition, or commute the remaining sentence to supervised release with conditions. Please draft the memo I'll circulate beforehand: recommend one of the three, engage the argument on its own terms rather than retreating into a compatibilist just-so story, give the losing side of the board the strongest version of their view rather than a caricature, and say what, if anything, can still justify your recommendation once basic desert is off the table.
