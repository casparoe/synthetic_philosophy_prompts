I'm a PhD student in philosophy of science, currently drafting a dissertation chapter on the transition from epistemic to ontic structural realism, and there's a step in the argument that I can reconstruct formally on paper but still haven't managed to explain in plain terms to my reading group. I understand the original motivation for structural realism as a way of splitting the difference between the no-miracles argument and the pessimistic meta-induction: what survives theory change, on Worrall's telling, is mathematical structure rather than the ontology of the underlying entities, as in the move from Fresnel's elastic-solid theory of the ether to Maxwell's electromagnetic theory of light. I also understand, at least in outline, why Newman's 1928 objection to Russell's earlier version of this view is supposed to trivialize it: given the right cardinality, almost any domain of individuals can be organized so as to instantiate almost any structure, so that saying "the world has such-and-such a structure" threatens to collapse into a near-tautology rather than a substantive empirical commitment.

What I can't get an intuitive grip on is why this is supposed to be a distinctive problem for Worrall's epistemic structural realism specifically, and why Ladyman's later move to an ontic version is supposed to genuinely dissolve the difficulty rather than just relabel it. The papers I've read say OSR escapes Newman's problem because it treats relations as ontologically primitive rather than as sets of ordered tuples given in purely extensional terms, but every time I try to reconstruct, on my own, why that shift actually blocks the "any sufficiently large domain will do" result, rather than simply stipulating that it does, I lose the thread. Could you walk me through this in fairly intuitive terms, without leaning too heavily on the formal Ramsey-sentence machinery, and also tell me whether, in your assessment, the ontic move genuinely earns its escape from Newman's objection, or whether it just relocates the same worry one level down, into the notion of a "purely structural" relation?
