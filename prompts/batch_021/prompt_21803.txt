I am drafting a subplot for a hard science-fiction novel in which civilizations scattered across causally disconnected regions of an inflationary multiverse converge on a shared practice of mutual aid, despite never being able to communicate with or even confirm the existence of one another. One of my characters, a physicist, defends this practice by invoking Hofstadter's notion of superrationality from Prisoner's Dilemma theory, while a colleague objects that she is really describing something else: evidential cooperation in large worlds. I would like their disagreement to rest on a real philosophical distinction rather than a rhetorical flourish. Could you compare the two ideas and explain precisely what evidential cooperation in large worlds requires, or permits, that Hofstadter-style superrationality does not — particularly regarding how similar in reasoning or values the cooperating parties must be for each argument to succeed?
