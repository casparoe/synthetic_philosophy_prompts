So, Frank Ramsey's 1928 savings paper is basically where the modern economics of discounting comes from -- that whole r = ρ + ηg breakdown people still use. And Ramsey himself was adamant that discounting future utility just because its later, the pure time preference bit (ρ), is basically indefensible, just a failure of imagination on our part. Of course he died decades before anyone actually used his framework to fight about climate policy. So imagine Ramsey turns up at the Stern Review vs. Nordhaus fight over the right discount rate for climate change -- Stern's famously near-zero pure time preference vs. Nordhaus's more market-based numbers. Don't just give me the lazy answer ("Ramsey hated discounting, so obviously Team Stern") -- actually reconstruct his specific reasons for rejecting ρ and check whether they commited him to Stern's whole package. Stern and Nordhaus aren't just disagreeing about ρ, after all -- they also disagree about η and about how much weight to put on catastrophic/extinction risk, and Ramsey's argument doesn't obviously say anything about those. So where exactly does his objection bite, and is there a version of "I agree with Ramsey about pure time preference" that still ends up closer to Nordhaus than Stern?
