Okay so this has turned into an actual thing in my relationship and I could use an outside sanity check. My fiancé's family is pretty hardcore Reformed/Calvinist (his mom actually corrected me once for saying "I chose to come to church," like I'd said something rude), and I grew up in a much more generic "God gave us free will, the end" kind of household. That difference has gotten a lot more real lately because we're actually praying together now about a big decision, whether he should take a job that'd mean moving us across the country, and somehow every family dinner turns into a low-key theology seminar. A couple weeks ago his uncle, who apparently reads theology books for fun, told me I should look into "Molinism," since its supposedly the reasonable middle position that keeps real free will while still keeping God fully sovereign over everything, and its what a lot of Catholics and even some Protestants land on instead of straight-up predestination.

So I read up on it a little, and near as I can tell the whole thing hinges on this "middle knowledge" idea: before God even decided to create anything, he already knew with total certainty exactly what every possible person would freely choose in every possible set of circumstances, and only after knowing all of that did he pick which circumstances to actually bring into existence. But isn't that functionally identical to Calvinism? If God already knew, with zero chance of error, precisely what I would choose in every situation before I even existed, and then deliberately set up the exact situation I'd end up in, my choices seem just as locked in as they'd be under straight predestination, he's just knowing it in advance instead of decreeing it, which honestly feels like a distinction without a difference. Is Molinism actually doing real philosophical work here, or is it basically Calvinism wearing a free-will costume so everyone can feel a little better about it?
