I'm a PhD student in History and Philosophy of Science, currently drafting a dissertation chapter on the visual epistemology of the Vienna Circle, and I keep running up against a connection that a couple of secondary sources gesture at without ever quite spelling out. I want to pressure-test the idea before I commit to it in writing, especially since when I floated it in my reading group nobody pushed back, which makes me suspect I might be the one missing something.

Here is the claim the chapter is being built around. Otto Neurath's Vienna Method of Picture Statistics, later systematized as ISOTYPE, looks to me like a direct, public-facing realization of the picture theory of meaning from Wittgenstein's Tractatus. Wittgenstein held that a meaningful proposition is a picture sharing its logical form with the state of affairs it depicts; Neurath's pictograms are literal pictures standing in for statistical and social facts, and they are explicitly designed so that quantity is shown by repeating a fixed unit-icon rather than by resizing it, precisely so that the pictorial structure tracks the quantitative structure of the fact instead of merely gesturing at it. On my reading, ISOTYPE is Neurath cashing out Wittgenstein's abstract semantic thesis in a pedagogical register, for a public that cannot be assumed to share a spoken or written language. If that is right, I would expect ISOTYPE to inherit the Tractatus's most notorious difficulty: a picture can depict a fact, but it cannot depict the depicting relation between picture and fact, which on Wittgenstein's view can only be shown, never said. So my tentative view is that, on this narrow point about picturing, Neurath and Wittgenstein end up as tacit allies, however sharply they disagreed elsewhere about metaphysics and about what counts as illegitimately unsayable. Am I right to draw the connection between ISOTYPE and the picture theory this tightly, and, if the saying/showing problem really does carry over, how would Neurath, given his own explicit commitments, have wanted to respond to it?
