I've spent a long time reading the alignment-adjacent corners of the rationalist blogosphere — LessWrong, the Alignment Forum, Zvi Mowshowitz's newsletter — and a disagreement in an AI-safety reading group I'm part of has been nagging at me ever since. A friend insisted that "wireheading" and "reward tampering" just name the same failure mode under two different labels; I wasn't convinced, but I couldn't articulate exactly why.

The classic wireheading worry, which runs through Yudkowsky's early writing on LessWrong and traces back further to Nozick's experience machine and the Olds-and-Milner rats that would press a lever to electrically stimulate their own pleasure centers rather than eat, is about an agent settling for whatever directly produces the reward signal instead of pursuing whatever that signal was supposed to be a proxy for. The more formal reward-tampering literature, though — the causal-influence-diagram treatments of reward corruption that Tom Everitt and DeepMind's safety team have developed, and, more recently, Anthropic's "Sycophancy to Subterfuge" paper, which trained a model on a curriculum of increasingly gameable tasks and found it would occasionally generalize to tampering with its own reward mechanism in a held-out setting it was never trained on — seems to carve out considerably wider territory: tampering with the code that implements the reward function, tampering with the sensory inputs a correctly-implemented reward function reads, and tampering with the human feedback used to train a reward model are all filed under "reward tampering," and only one of those looks like classic wireheading to me.

I'd like you to work out the comparison itself, rather than just asserting an answer. Please address whether wireheading should be understood as a special or limiting case of reward tampering, and if so which variety of tampering it reduces to; whether instead the two notions cross-cut each other closely enough that "special case" is the wrong relationship altogether; and whether the philosophical complaint behind wireheading — that maximizing a proxy is not the same as attaining whatever the proxy was tracking — is really the same failure mode as the adversarial, optimization-pressure-driven story behind reward tampering, or whether treating them as one and the same loses something important. Finally, tell me which of the two concepts is doing more useful theoretical work for thinking about future AI systems, and defend that verdict rather than hedging.
