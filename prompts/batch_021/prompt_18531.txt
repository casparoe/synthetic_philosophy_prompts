Write a dialogue between a Bohmian and an Everettian arguing about whether Bell's theorem actually forces you to give up locality. The Bohmian's happy to admit their theory is nonlocal (the pilot wave depends on the whole configuration at once, thats just the price of admission), but the Everettian claims many-worlds gets to keep locality -- no signaling, no spooky action, the weird correlations are just what you'd expect once you realize theres no single outcome to be correlated in the first place. Have the Bohmian really press on that move (is "no signaling" actually the same thing as the locality Bell was worried about?), and make it a real fight -- both sides need genuinely good arguments, nobody's allowed to strawman the other.
