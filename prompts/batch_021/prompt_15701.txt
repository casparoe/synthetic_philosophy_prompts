I spent my working life as a physicist studying irreversible processes and the statistical arrow of time, and it is only since retiring that I have had the leisure to read philosophy and literature side by side. Lately I have become preoccupied with Borges's essay "A New Refutation of Time" (collected in Other Inquisitions), in which he turns Berkeley's denial of matter and Hume's denial of a continuous self against time itself: if nothing enduring underlies either the world or the self, he argues, there is nothing for succession to be a succession of, and time dissolves into a series of self-sufficient, unconnected moments. With characteristic self-undermining candor, he even admits to having written essentially the same argument twice, years apart, unable to discard either version — and the essay closes with his confession that he himself remains, quite helplessly, caught up in the very time he has just argued out of existence. I would like you to write an original short story that takes this specific argument as its engine, rather than merely borrowing Borges's usual furniture of mirrors, tigers, and labyrinths as decoration. Let the plot or structure itself enact the dissolution of substance and self, and then the same reversal at the close, where the protagonist cannot escape time even once every reason for believing in it has been dismantled; if you can also make it brush up against a more familiar scientific route to doubting time's reality (entropy, say, or the relativity of simultaneity) without collapsing the two very different arguments into one, so much the better. The story should stand on its own as fiction, but a reader who knows the essay ought to recognize exactly which moves it is dramatizing.
