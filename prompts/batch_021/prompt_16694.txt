While prepping for a debate-club round on the JFK assassination over break, I ran into a logic puzzle that I can't quite untangle, and I'd like to sort it out before our next practice session.

In the intro logic elective at my school, we learned that any English conditional of the form "if P, then Q" is symbolized as the material conditional P ⊃ Q, which comes out false only when P is true and Q is false — so in particular, whenever P is false, the whole conditional is automatically counted as true, no matter what Q says. My teacher presented the "if...had...would have" construction (what I've since learned are called counterfactuals) as essentially the same connective, just relocated into a hypothetical past, rather than as some fundamentally different kind of sentence.

The trouble started when I came across a pair of sentences on a philosophy blog that was described as a classic puzzle for exactly this assumption: "If Oswald didn't kill Kennedy, someone else did," and "If Oswald hadn't killed Kennedy, someone else would have." Assuming the Warren Commission's lone-gunman conclusion, both sentences have a false antecedent, since Oswald really did kill Kennedy — so by the false-antecedent rule I just described, the material conditional treatment should count both of them as automatically true. Yet everyone seems to agree that the first sentence is true while the second is clearly false, since there's no particular reason to think a backup assassin was standing by. My debate partner insists this shows that classical logic simply can't handle reasoning about hypothetical history, and that we should basically distrust formal logic altogether whenever a counterfactual comes up.

I'd like to actually get to the bottom of this rather than just take my partner's word for it: does this pair of sentences really expose some kind of failure or gap in classical logic itself, or have we been misunderstanding something more basic about how indicative conditionals like the first sentence and counterfactual conditionals like the second are supposed to be analyzed in the first place?
