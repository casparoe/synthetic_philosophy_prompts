I write about science and health for the paper here, which I guess is why they roped me into being the "public member" on the county's fire fatality review board -- someone has to be able to explain the findings to people outside the field. We've got a case splitting the board right down the middle and I need a memo for Thursdays meeting.

Here's the case: an elderly tenant died of smoke inhalation when frayed wiring behind her stove caught fire. The landlord had been warned twice in writing and had bought the replacement part, but it sat in his truck for three weeks. The city's follow-up inspection was four months overdue because the inspector's office lost two people to retirement and never backfilled them. The smoke detector had been chirping a low-battery warning for two weeks; her home health aide, who visited three times a week, noticed it, but fixing it wasn't technically her job.

Our report form has exactly one box for "primary cause" and one for "contributing factors," and whatever goes in the first box decides whether the landlord gets referred to the county attorney. Half the board wants to name him outright. The other half says that's arbitrary, since strictly speaking the fire doesn't happen if any of those three people had acted differently, the aide included. Nobody wants to just write "systemic failure" either, since the family deserves a straighter answer than that.

Can you draft the memo I'd bring to the board? I need a real recommendation for what goes in each box, and what if anything gets left off the form entirely, a genuine principle behind wherever you draw the line -- not just "the law says so" -- and honest engagement with the strongest objection to the line you draw.
