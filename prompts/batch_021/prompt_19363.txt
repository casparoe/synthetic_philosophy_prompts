Me and my friend Jae got into a two hour arguement last night that I can't stop replaying in my head. It kicked off because I mentioned I'd finally set up a recurring donation to one of those global health charities that funds bed nets and cheap medicine in poor countries, kind of an effective-altruism-lite move on my part. Jae, who loves poking holes in stuff, said the whole idea of calculating how much "good" that donation actually does is basically a fantasy.

His argument was that the second you actually save a kid's life, you've changed the entire future course of that community in ways nobody can trace -- maybe the kid grows up and cures a disease, maybe they end up straining local resources or caught up in a conflict, maybe the money that would've gone to someone else instead (if you hadn't donated) would've had totally different downstream effects of its own, good or bad. And he kept insisting this isn't the boring "the future is uncertain so just use probabilities" point -- his claim was that for effects this indirect and cascading we don't even have a principled way to guess at probabilities in the first place, so the whole approach of adding up expected good and bad outcomes and picking whichever option scores higher quietly falls apart, not because we're bad at the maths but in principle.

I tried pushing back that surely all these unknown ripple effects should roughly cancel out on average, so you could still trust the comparison, but Jae said that's just a hope dressed up as an argument, since theres no actual reason to think the unknown effects are symmetric rather than skewed in some direction we just can't see. I didn't have a good comeback in the moment and now I'm second-guessing whether it's even coherent to make careful decisions (donations, career choices, whatever) by weighing up expected outcomes at all. Who's got the stronger case here -- is there a decent way to rescue the "try to do the most good" approach from this kind of objection, or should I be taking Jae's point more seriously than I have been?
