So this is something I've been chewing on for a while after noticing that every year or so there's a story about a teacher or professor getting in trouble for reading a slur straight out of an old book or court transcript during a lecture, purely to quote it exactly, not to call anyone anything. That sent me down a rabbit hole reading about the philosophy of slurs, which turns out to be a whole real field, who knew. Near as I can tell there's two main camps: one side says the ugliness of a slur is baked right into its meaning, so the word itself semantically carries something derogatory no matter who says it or why, and the other side says that's the wrong picture entirely, and it's actually more like a social rule against ever uttering the word, full stop, that has nothing to do with meaning at all, kind of like a taboo.

What's been bugging me is two things that seem to trip up both sides. First, if it's really about meaning, why does simply mentioning the word, like quoting it or a linguist writing it on a whiteboard to talk about how it sounds, still feel gross to people, even though nothing false or insulting is technically being claimed about anyone? Normally quoting a lie doesnt make you a liar. But if it's just a blanket "never say it" rule instead, that doesn't explain the second thing, which is that people from the group targeted by the slur can often use it on each other completely fine, sometimes even warmly, like the rule just doesn't apply to them, or flips depending on who's talking. Neither explanation seems to get out of this free, and I haven't found anyone online really settle it for me (probably because it isn't settled, lol).

Could you write me a dialogue between two philosophers, one from each camp, who both happen to catch the same kind of news story and end up arguing about it. I want real back and forth, not just each of them repeating their opening speech over and over - they should actually respond to the specific point the other just made, especially on the mentioning thing and the reclaiming thing, and neither one should come across as the obvious strawman who's losing. If one of them has a clever move to explain away mentioning or reclaiming, I want the other to have an actual comeback to that exact move, not just a shrug and a change of subject.
