ok this has been bugging me for a while and i can't fully resolve it myself. the Happier Lives Institute and the whole WELLBY approach to cause prioritization basically cash out "how good is this intervention" as a change in self-reported life satisfaction (0-10 scale), and their big controversial result a while back was roughly that StrongMinds (group psychotherapy for depression) beats GiveDirectly-style cash transfers per dollar on that metric.

but doesn't that walk straight into the experience machine? if the currency is a subjective self-report, then in principle whatever intervention makes people *say* their life is better, full stop, wins -- it doesn't actually have to track anything about their circumstances, relationships, or contact with reality. thats basically the thing Nozick's thought experiment was supposed to make us suspicious of.

so what are the most important implications of the experience machine for whether EA-adjacent orgs should be treating SWB/WELLBY scores as the metric for comparing charities? is there a real disanalogy that gets the WELLBY crowd off the hook (life satisfaction judgements aren't the same as raw hedonic buzz, etc), or does leaning on self-report just quietly smuggle in a hedonist/internalist theory of welfare that most people, confronted with the experience machine, would actually reject? want your honest take, not a rehash of the ea forum debate.
