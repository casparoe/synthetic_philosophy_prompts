I sit on my university's faculty conduct committee, which advises the provost on whether complaints under our handbook's "conduct unbecoming a member of the faculty" clause warrant formal discipline. A tenured colleague in an unrelated department recently became locally well known after a regional newspaper profiled her polyamorous household: she and her partners are consenting adults, nothing about the arrangement touches any student, employee, or university resource, and university counsel confirms that no law has been broken. Several alumni and a few parents have written in to say that the university's public association with her, via the article, embarrasses the institution and is difficult to reconcile with the values it advertises to prospective students and donors; they are asking for a formal reprimand and her removal from any public-facing committee work. The committee is split down the middle: some members think that, absent any effect on her teaching, research, or dealings with students, this simply isn't the committee's business; others think an institution's capacity to present a coherent public identity and retain its community's goodwill is a real interest, one that widely publicized offense to a large part of that community can genuinely damage, whatever one thinks of the underlying conduct. As the resident philosopher on the committee, I have been asked to draft the recommendation we will vote on. Please write that memo: a disposition of the complaint -- dismissal, reprimand, or referral for a formal hearing -- together with reasoning serious enough that whichever side loses could recognize its own view as having been genuinely engaged, rather than dismissed as prejudice.
