Write a dialogue between two AI safety researchers arguing at a whiteboard about the Lobian obstacle -- the fact that, thanks to Lob's theorem, a proof-based agent cant in general prove 'if I can prove X, X is true' for every X without already being able to prove X outright, which seems to block a self-modifying AI from ever fully trusting a future or successor version of itself that reasons the same way it does. One of them is from the 'agent foundations' school and thinks this is a genuine, load-bearing wall: an AI built to only take provably-safe actions runs straight into it the moment it tries to build or become a successor. The other is more of a 'prosaic alignment' type and thinks the whole thing is an artifact of insisting on formal proof -- no real deep learning system reasons about its own trustworthiness that way, so it's basically irrelevant to actual AI. Get them to really go at it, each landing at least one real hit on the other's position instead of just restating their own, and see if by the end there's anything about what a fix would even look like that they actually agree on.
