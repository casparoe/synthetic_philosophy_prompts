I cover the psychology of belief and misinformation, and I recently came across what epistemologists call the dogmatism paradox, usually traced to Gilbert Harman. As I understand it, the argument shows that if I genuinely know a claim P to be true, I am already in a position to know that any evidence which might later surface against P has to be misleading, since genuine evidence cannot tell against something true, and so I am entitled to disregard it without examination. That seems to hand the entrenched experts and die-hard believers I profile a real philosophical license to dismiss their critics rather than engage with them. For a piece on why smart, credentialed people so often refuse to update their views, how should I distinguish someone who is legitimately exercising that epistemic entitlement from someone who is simply being closed-minded?
