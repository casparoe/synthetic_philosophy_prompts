That whole back-and-forth sent me down a rabbit hole into philosophy of medicine, specifically this naturalism vs. normativism fight over what "disease" even means, and now I'm kind of obsessed with it.

Anyway, I co-host a tiny philosophy-adjacent podcast (genuinely small, don't get excited) and we landed a guest for next month who's built her whole research career defending Christopher Boorse's biostatistical theory -- the view that disease is just statistically subnormal functioning relative to your reference class, no value judgments required. I don't want her to just get to restate the theory cleanly for 40 minutes, I want to actually press on it. Can you draft me 8 to 10 interview questions, each paired with a follow-up keyed to whatever answer she's most likely to give? I definitely want one on the reference-class problem -- why age and sex get to be the privileged subgroups when you carve a species up into "normal" populations, and what stops that same move from just defining a disease out of existence in whatever subpopulation it happens to be common in. I want one that uses her own disease/illness/sickness trichotomy against her -- whether "illness" is quietly doing all the value-laden work she claims "disease" manages to avoid. I want one that throws a hard historical case at her, like drapetomania or homosexuality's run in the DSM, and asks how a purely naturalistic theory was ever supposed to protect us from those, given that the reasoning at the time also got dressed up in statistical, naturalistic-sounding language. And I want at least one built around my mom's actual situation: how is BST even supposed to apply to something like fibromyalgia, where for years nobody could point to the specific dysfunction being measured -- was it a disease that whole time on her view, or not, and how would she even know either way. No throat-clearing questions like "what drew you to this field" please, I want ones she can't just answer on autopilot.
