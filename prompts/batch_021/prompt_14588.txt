As a PhD student in formal epistemology, I've been surveying attempted solutions to the problem of logical omniscience: the fact that the standard tools for modeling belief and knowledge—possible-worlds semantics, Bayesian credence functions—entail that any agent automatically believes every logical truth and everything entailed by what they already believe, which no actual bounded reasoner does. Various formal frameworks try to avoid this consequence while still letting "belief" and "knowledge" do real normative work, rather than just stipulating non-omniscience by fiat and giving up on modeling any rational constraints on belief at all.

I'd like your judgment on how well each of the following actually solves the problem, as opposed to merely relocating or trivializing it:

- Impossible-worlds semantics (Hintikka; Rantala's urn models)
- Awareness logic (Fagin & Halpern)
- Algorithmic knowledge (Halpern, Moses & Vardi)
- Resource-bounded step-logics (Konolige; Elgot-Drapkin & Perlis)
- Logical induction (Garrabrant et al.)
- Plain syntactic/sentential belief models (belief as an unclosed set of sentences)
