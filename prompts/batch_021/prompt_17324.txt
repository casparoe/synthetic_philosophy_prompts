My friend and I spent the better part of an evening arguing about Garrabrant et al.'s logical induction framework, and I still haven't resolved it in my own mind. My friend's contention was that, since a logical inductor is a computable sequence of belief states that provably cannot be exploited by any efficiently computable trader, we effectively already possess an algorithm we could run today to extract a genuinely trustworthy credence for any specific open conjecture, such as the Riemann Hypothesis, just by reading off its current price. I objected that this seemed too convenient given how intractable such conjectures are, but I couldn't quite articulate what was wrong with the inference. Does the inexploitability theorem really license treating a logical inductor's present-day output as a reliable credence for a particular open problem, or is my friend's argument quietly smuggling in something the theorem doesn't actually deliver?
