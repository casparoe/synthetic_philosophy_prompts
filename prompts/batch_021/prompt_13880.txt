Having spent my career as an experimental physicist before turning to philosophy in retirement, I thought I had finally found a case where physics settles a long-standing metaphysical dispute outright. Special relativity tells us that simultaneity is relative to the observer's reference frame, so there is no frame-independent fact about which spatially separated events happen "at the same time." Endurantism holds that a persisting object is wholly present at every moment of its existence, which seems to require exactly the sort of frame-independent "moment" that relativity rules out; perdurantism, which treats a persisting object as a four-dimensional whole extended through time, seems to sidestep this difficulty entirely. Doesn't this mean that special relativity settles the endurantism/perdurantism debate decisively in favor of perdurantism?
