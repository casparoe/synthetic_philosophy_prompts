I like to open my graduate seminar on the philosophy of language with a joke, and this term I would like one about Moore's paradox — the peculiar unassertability of sentences like "It's raining, but I don't believe it." Write me such a joke, but make sure the punchline turns on the paradox's actual technical structure rather than on a generic contradiction: it should exploit the fact that the oddity is a pragmatic feature of first-person, present-tense assertion (and arguably of belief itself), not a logical one, given that "It's raining, but she doesn't believe it" is perfectly fine. A joke that would land just as well for "it's raining and it's not raining" won't do.
