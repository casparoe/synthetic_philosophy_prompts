My sister's about to finish her PhD in cosmology, and a few weeks ago she mentioned over dinner, kind of offhand, that a chunk of her field is quietly bothered by 'Boltzmann brains' — the idea that if the universe really does go on forever as a near-empty de Sitter space, random thermal fluctuations will eventually cough up way more freak brains with false memories than there were ever real observers who evolved the normal way, so a 'typical' observer should apparently expect to be one of those freaks rather than someone with an actual cosmic history. I havent been able to let this go and have lost several nights to arxiv over it. There seems to be a genuine split: the Dyson/Kleban/Susskind and Bousso/Freivogel line treats Boltzmann-brain domination as a real reductio that should rule out certain de Sitter vacua or measures, while Sean Carroll argues the whole line of reasoning is 'cognitively unstable' and self-undermining (you can't trust the reasoning that led you to worry you're a Boltzmann brain in the first place), so it should just get assigned zero credence up front instead of being treated as a live statistical hypothesis — and then Don Page comes back and says ordinary Bayesian reasoning rules it out anyway, no cognitive-instability move needed. I even found a 2025 philosophy paper (Adam Elga, I think?) still fighting about this, so its clearly not settled.

Can you write me an essay that lays this disagreement out properly and then actually picks a side instead of shrugging — is Boltzmann-brain domination a genuine empirical defeater for a cosmological model, a self-undermining non-starter, or something else?
