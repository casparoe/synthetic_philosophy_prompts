Student's answer: "Interpretability results bear on this question, but they don't simply settle it in favor of realism. The relevant background worry is the 'job description challenge' that Jerry Fodor raised against connectionist models in the 1980s: a state's being reliably correlated with some external property isn't enough to make it a representation of that property, since paint on a thermometer's wall correlates with temperature too. A real representation has to play the right causal-explanatory role — the system has to consume the state as standing in for what it represents, not merely carry information about it the way a symptom does. Sparse-autoencoder work is well placed to answer this challenge, because when a linear probe can decode a concept like sycophancy from a layer's activations with high accuracy, that shows the network has factored the concept out as its own variable rather than smearing it across unrelated dimensions — exactly the dedicated causal role the challenge demands. Superposition is the current explanation of why raw neurons look polysemantic in the first place: the network is packing more features than it has dimensions, so each neuron ends up doing several jobs at once, and an autoencoder's task is to un-mix those directions back into one feature per dimension.
