Lately I've been reading Elizabeth Anscombe's pamplet against Oxford giving Truman an honorary degree, where she argues Hiroshima and Nagasaki don't even count as a hard case for the doctrine of double effect, because the whole point of the bombs was to kill masses of civilians and terrify Japan into surrendering -- the deaths were the means, not a foreseen side-effect of hitting some legitimate target nearby. She died in Jan 2001, before the whole drone strike / signature-strike era of the war on terror, where a lot of targeting gets done off patterns of behaviour rather than confirmed identities, and civilian deaths get chalked up as regretted-but-unintended fallout of hitting a legitimate target. Working it through with her actual reasoning rather than just a vibe of 'she'd have disapproved', do you reckon she'd have judged that kind of campaign the same way she judged Truman, or is the means/side-effect structure different enough that it would've counted as a genuinely hard case for her?
