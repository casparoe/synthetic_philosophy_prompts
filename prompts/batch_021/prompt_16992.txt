Over the course of a long career in experimental physics I made heavy use of Bayesian inference without ever worrying much about its philosophical justification, so retirement has finally given me time to look into the foundations properly. The Dutch book argument for probabilism strikes me as a wonderfully clean piece of reasoning: any credence function that violates the probability axioms exposes its holder to a combination of bets, each fair by their own lights, that guarantees a loss whatever happens. I gather the very same argument, simply run across time rather than at an instant, is what forces a rational agent to update solely by conditionalizing on new evidence, since anyone who plans to revise their credences in any other way can likewise be confronted with a book of bets that guarantees a loss no matter what occurs. Given that both requirements rest on the same underlying coherence argument, why do so many Bayesian epistemologists still treat conditionalization as a substantive, independently debatable extra assumption rather than as a straightforward corollary of probabilism itself?
