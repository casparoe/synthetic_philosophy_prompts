For my seminar on Plato's Laws we've spent the last two class periods on Book 1 and 2, and it kind of upended how I thought about Plato and drinking. I had this Republic-era image of him as basically "reason good, appetite bad, wine obviously bad," but in the Laws the Athenian Stranger actually spends ages defending regulated drinking parties against Megillus and Kleinias, whose home cities (Sparta and Crete) just ban the stuff outright. His argument is basically that getting a bit drunk under the right conditions - someone sober in charge to keep order, and only once you're past a certain age - works like a stress test for a person's self-control, the same way facing danger tests courage, and that a decent lawgiver should use that instead of just prohibiting wine. He's also got this whole graded age system (basically no wine as a teenager, moderate amounts through your twenties, and only really cutting loose once you're up around forty) that's supposedly there so older men loosen up enough to sing and dance in that Dionysus-chorus stuff without embarrassement, since apparently getting older makes your character go "stiff" otherwise.

Anyway, I was arguing about this with my roommate because it maps so weirdly onto our own campus, which has a total split personality: officially we're "dry" under 21, everyone sits through the dumb freshman-year AlcoholEdu module, RAs write you up for a beer in your room - but the actual social life runs on frat pregames, beer pong, and rush-week hazing, supervised by whichever brother happens to be least drunk that night. So here's what I'm actually asking: going off what Plato really argues in the Laws, not the pop version of him, what do you think he'd say about this setup? Would he side with the strict abstinence policy, or would he say the policy is aiming at the wrong problem and the real issue is that our parties have no "sober person legitimately in charge" and no age-based structure whatsoever? Would something like a hazing ritual count, on his own terms, as a corrupted/ill-ordered version of his "test of character" idea, or is that not even the same category of thing to him? And does his age-graduated system map onto a 21-and-over drinking age at all, or is it doing something completely different? I want an actual concrete prediction out of this, not just "it's complicated," even if you have to caveat it some.
