During my career as an experimental physicist, much of my work involved what engineers call embedded systems: dedicated microcontrollers wired into a larger apparatus, reading sensor data and driving actuators through a clean, well-defined interface with the rest of the instrument. Since retiring, I have been trying to catch up on the AI safety literature, and I keep encountering "embedded agency"—the problem that Demski and Garrabrant formulated in their well-known write-up for the Machine Intelligence Research Institute—discussed as though it were a deep, unsolved puzzle in the theory of rational agents. As far as I can tell, it simply redescribes the arrangement I spent decades building: a resource-limited decision-making unit, physically embedded within a larger system, interacting with that system through fixed sensor and actuator channels. Why, then, do researchers in this area treat it as a novel conceptual difficulty rather than a familiar engineering constraint with well-understood solutions? I would be grateful if you could set me straight.
