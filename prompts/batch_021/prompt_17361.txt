"Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility (REU) theory is designed to accommodate the classic Allais preference pattern (choosing the safe option in the first pair of gambles but the risky option in the second, even though standard EU theory says a rational agent should choose consistently across both) as rational rather than as a mistake. (a) State which axiom of von Neumann–Morgenstern expected utility theory this preference pattern violates. (b) Explain the formal role of Buchak's risk function r, and explain precisely how it differs from simply plugging a concave utility function into ordinary EU theory. (c) REU theory is often praised for still respecting stochastic dominance. Explain why, and explain how this feature is supposed to distinguish REU from Kahneman and Tversky's prospect theory."

And here's my classmate's answer, word for word:

"(a) This pattern violates the Independence Axiom: the two choice situations differ only by a common outcome that gets substituted in for another, and a rational EU-maximizer's ranking of the options shouldn't change under that kind of substitution, but people's actual preferences flip anyway.

(b) In standard EU theory, risk-aversion has to come entirely from the shape of u: money has diminishing marginal utility, so u is concave, and that's the only place risk-aversion can live. REU adds a second function, the risk function r, which reweights the probability attached to each outcome, similar to the decision weights Kahneman and Tversky introduce in prospect theory. Buchak requires r to be convex, since that's what makes an agent count as appropriately risk-averse and therefore rational. So instead of multiplying u(x_i) by the raw probability p_i, you multiply it by r(p_i), which lets the agent be more or less sensitive to a given probability than its face value suggests, and this is what generates the Allais pattern, since r(1)-r(0.99) can be much bigger than r(0.11)-r(0.10) even though both are 0.01 in probability terms.

(c) Because r is just an increasing function of probability, and increasing transformations preserve order, REU can never end up ranking a stochastically dominated gamble above the gamble that dominates it. This is exactly what Kahneman and Tversky's prospect theory lacks, which is why Buchak's risk-weighting gets her the Allais result without also giving up stochastic dominance the way prospect theory does."
