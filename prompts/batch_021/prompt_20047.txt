For an AI-safety reading group at my school, I've been working through the Löbian obstacle to building self-improving AI that can trust its own future reasoning. As I understand it, Löb's theorem shows that a consistent formal system can only prove the schema "if I can prove P, then P is true" for those P it can already prove outright — it never gets that assurance for free. Since an AI that checks its own proposed modifications, or writes a successor that reasons the same way, is essentially trying to establish exactly this reflection principle about itself, doesn't the theorem prove that it can never be sure its own future reasoning (or its successor's) is sound — and doesn't that mean any sufficiently long chain of self-modification is mathematically guaranteed to eventually break down? People in the group keep citing fixes involving probability instead of strict proof, or hierarchies of successively different proof systems, but that feels like relabeling the problem rather than solving it — if the underlying logic is inescapable, why would swapping frameworks change anything? Am I missing something about what the theorem actually establishes, or does it really rule out stable self-improving AI?
