I'm a novelist, and completely by accident I ended up on the three-person board of my writers' collective's little mutual-aid fund -- dues plus the odd bequest, meant to catch members when life falls apart. A former member just left us a decent lump sum, "for emergencies, no strings," and now two emergencies have landed in the same month and there's only enough money for one.

Option A pays in full for Renata's follow-up treatment; her doctor says that without it she's very likely dead within the year. Option B splits that same pot five ways between five other members each drowning in something serious but not lethal -- an eviction, a kid's hospital bill, a repo that'll cost someone their job. None of the five will die without the money, but each will be genuinely wrecked. There's no other money coming, so it's really one or the other -- the kind of clean dilemma I'd cut from a workshop story for being too pat, but here we are.

One of us says its obviously A, you don't weigh a life against inconveniences. Another says calling foreclosure and a sick kid "inconveniences" is insulting, and that automatically picking the one dying person no matter how many people you leave in real ruin can't be right either -- the numbers have to count for something. The third wants to just flip a coin between Renata and the group of five, since neither side has a knock-down argument and a coin is at least fair to both.

Can you write the memo I'm sending before Friday's vote? I need an actual recommendation -- A, B, the coin, or something else -- plus the reasoning, the strongest case against whatever you pick, and a rule of thumb the fund can fall back on next time, because it will.
