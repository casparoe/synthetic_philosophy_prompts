For the past few months, a few friends and I have kept circling back to a disagreement that started as a throwaway comment over dinner and has been nagging at me ever since, even though I don't have any formal training in philosophy and have mostly just been chewing on it on my own. It began when my friend Mara argued that when someone says "cheating on your partner is wrong," they aren't stating a fact that could be true or false; they're doing something closer to booing or wincing, venting disapproval dressed up in the grammar of a factual sentence. I found this fairly convincing at first, since it does seem to explain why moral disagreements almost never get resolved the way factual disagreements do, no matter how much evidence anyone brings to the table.

What's been bothering me is something I noticed the next time the subject came up, during an argument about whether a mutual friend was wrong to keep a wallet he found instead of trying to return it. Mara built her whole case out of perfectly ordinary logical steps: she got us to agree that "keeping something valuable you find, when you could return it, is wrong," then got us to agree that "if that's wrong, then what our friend did is wrong," and from those two claims concluded that what our friend did was wrong. That's just an ordinary "if this, then that; this; so that" argument, the same shape you'd use to reason about any plain fact. But if calling something "wrong" is really just a way of booing at it, I don't see how that middle step is supposed to work, because nobody boos at an "if" — in that sentence Mara isn't disapproving of anything, she's just linking two claims together. When I pointed this out, she said I was overthinking a metaphor, but I can't shake the feeling that she wants moral language to behave like ordinary fact-stating language whenever it's doing work inside an argument, and to shrink back down to a mere grunt of approval or disapproval the moment someone asks what it actually means. Is she being inconsistent, or is there some way for both halves of her view to fit together after all?
