I'm a philosophy PhD student and the graduate representative on my department's climate and mentoring committee. Faculty currently give some students unofficial extras -- reading drafts outside formal advising, checking in when a student is struggling -- that everyone treats as supererogatory, not part of the job. The committee is debating whether to make a minimum amount of this 'informal mentorship' an expectation in annual reviews, aimed specifically at improving support for first-generation and international students. Some members think formalizing it is exactly what would kill it: once mentorship is evaluated, it becomes compliance rather than a gift, and needy students get box-checking instead of care. Others think leaving it optional just lets the same generous few absorb the cost while everyone else free-rides. Please draft the memo I'll bring to next week's meeting: recommend a specific policy -- status quo, a mandate, or something in between -- and if you land on a middle path, say exactly what stays optional and what doesn't, and why that line addresses the formalization worry rather than just splitting the difference.
