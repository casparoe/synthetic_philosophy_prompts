Over dinner last week, two old friends of mine, Elena and Marcus, fell into a long-running dispute about a third friend, Theo, who during a solo cross-country drive left a tip at a small-town diner worth nearly as much as the meal itself, at a place he will almost certainly never eat at again. I could not settle it myself and would appreciate your help.

(1) Elena said this is a clean case of the handicap principle at work: Theo is demonstrating, through a costly and otherwise pointless act, that he can afford to be lavish -- the same logic biologists invoke to explain the peacock's tail.

(2) Marcus objected that this gets the principle backwards: a handicap only functions as a signal if some receiver updates their assessment of the signaler and behaves differently as a result, profiting from having assessed correctly. No such receiver exists here -- the server will never see Theo again, and the friends at the table already knew he was well-off. A costly act nobody can act on isn't a signal, just waste.

(3) Elena replied that the receiver could be Theo himself: the tip reinforces a self-image of generosity that pays off elsewhere, so the signal is addressed to his own future dispositions rather than to the server or the table.

(4) Marcus said that move quietly changes the subject. If what's doing the work is a standing disposition rather than a case-by-case costly choice, this is much closer to Robert Frank's account of emotions as commitment devices -- where the whole point is that the disposition isn't switched on and off strategically -- than to Zahavi's handicap principle, and running the two together hides a real difference in mechanism, not just terminology.

(5) Elena answered that the gap is smaller than Marcus thinks: a disposition like this only remains a reliable marker of someone's actual generosity if keeping it up is genuinely more costly for an ungenerous person than for a generous one, which is just the differential-cost logic of the handicap principle relocated from single acts to standing character traits.
