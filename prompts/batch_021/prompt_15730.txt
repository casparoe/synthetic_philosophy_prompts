I have just committed to writing my master's thesis on whether Hilary Putnam's model-theoretic argument against metaphysical realism actually succeeds, and in tracing the argument back to its technical source I discovered that it leans heavily on Skolem's Paradox: the fact, following from the Löwenheim–Skolem theorem, that if ZFC has a model at all, it has a countable one, even though that very model satisfies sentences asserting the existence of uncountable sets. Skolem's own moral from this was comparatively modest — that notions like "uncountable" are relative to a choice of model rather than absolute — whereas Putnam wants to leverage essentially the same construction into a wholesale argument that reference is indeterminate for any theory whatsoever, thereby undermining metaphysical realism as such. Before I build a thesis chapter around engaging Putnam on his own terms, I would like a precise comparison of the two positions: does Putnam's stronger conclusion actually follow from Skolem's result together with commitments Skolem himself already accepted in his 1922 argument, or does Putnam need further assumptions — for instance, about permuting the domain, or about the irrelevance of "intended" models — that a committed mathematical realist could reject without denying anything Skolem showed? Please justify your answer, and note any point at which Putnam's own presentation of the argument seems to be doing more work than the bare Skolem construction can support.
