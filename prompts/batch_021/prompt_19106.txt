A friend and I had a long argument last night after discovering that we had each heard a different explanation for why a mutual friend's startup failed. The version I had heard put the blame on a larger competitor's aggressive price cuts; the version my friend had heard put the blame on an earlier internal decision to delay a key product feature, which is what let the competitor pull ahead. Neither version contradicts anything the other says; they simply foreground different parts of the same sequence of events. My friend maintains that at most one of these can be the real cause, and that calling both accounts 'true, just from different angles' is really only a polite way of avoiding the question of what actually happened. I disagreed but found it hard to say exactly why. Is there a defensible sense in which both versions could be true at once, or does saying so just mean that nothing is really true at all?
