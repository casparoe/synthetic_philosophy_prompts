Okay so this is kind of random but in my physics class we got sidetracked for like ten minutes talking about time travel (someone asked if its actually possible and my teacher couldn't resist going off on a tangent lol), and she brought up the grandfather paradox -- like if you go back in time and kill your own grandpa before your parent is even born, then you'd never exist to go back and do it in the first place. I got kind of obsessed and went down a wiki rabbit hole after school, and it turns out there's an actual split in how philosophers try to resolve this. One camp basically says killing grandpa would just bump you into a separate branching timeline/parallel universe instead of erasing the original one, which is basically what every time travel movie does. But then there's this other way weirder answer that I think came from a philosopher named David Lewis(?) back in like the 70s, where he argues time travel to the past is totally logically fine and there's only ONE timeline ever, but the time traveler can NEVER actually succeed in killing grandpa -- not because some cosmic paradox-police force stops them, but just because of dumb ordinary bad luck, the gun jams, they trip, they chicken out, whatever -- and its not really a "coincidence" so much as just a fact about what already happened, if that makes sense (it kind of melted my brain ngl).

Can you write me a short story, maybe 600-900 words, that commits to that second weirder resolution instead of the parallel-universe one? I want it centered on one time traveler who's obsessed with going back to stop something specific from happening to someone in their family, and have them try again and again only to keep getting foiled by totally mundane stuff, until they slowly start to realize, with actual dread, that it's not bad luck at all, that the universe already "knows" they failed and nothing they do can change that, and you never bring in an actual time-cop character or magic force to explain it away. Basically I want the internal logic to stay airtight the whole way through instead of it just being vibes-based like most time travel stuff ends up being.
