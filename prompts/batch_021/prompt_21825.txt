I cover science and ideas for a living, and last year a local foundation roped me onto its small grants committee -- I think they wanted a 'generalist' in the room. We're deadlocked on this cycle's biggest award between a tutoring program with years of solid data behind it (clear numbers served, clear test-score gains) and a coalition lobbying the city council to loosen zoning, on the theory that more housing eases the affordability crisis over the next decade or two.

Two committee members have taken this way further than a normal grants meeting and are stuck in a genuinely interesting fight. Marcus says the zoning grant is just riskier than the tutoring program, and that expected value with honestly wide error bars still favors it given how much bigger the stakes are if it works. Dana says that's the wrong category: it's not 'we don't know which of two understood outcomes will happen,' its that for almost every story about the upzoning helping (more supply, lower rents) there's an equally plausible story about it backfiring (displacement, luxury development soaking up the new supply, a backlash that guts reform for a decade), and nothing tells us how to weigh those stories against each other rather than just listing them. She calls this 'real cluelessness,' and says it's a different animal from ordinary uncertainty. Marcus counters that the tutored kids' lives will also cascade unpredictably for decades, so either Dana has to say we're clueless about the safe option too, which paralyzes the whole committee, or she owes everyone a principled reason the two cases are actually different.

I have to bring a recommendation to the full board Thursday. Could you write the memo? Give an actual bottom line -- fund the tutoring program, fund the zoning coalition, split it, or something else -- and really engage with whether Dana's right that this is a different kind of not-knowing, rather than just flattening it back into risk-talk, or letting 'cluelessness' become a standing excuse to always default to the safe, legible option.
