Explain what work the notion of incommensurability is still doing given this mathematical continuity, and explain how Kuhn's appeal to shared values in the Postscript is meant to block the inference from 'paradigms are incommensurable' to 'there is no rational basis for choosing between them.'"

My classmate's answer:

"Kuhn's paradigms are not just theories; they include the shared methods, standards, and exemplary solved problems that a scientific community works within during periods of 'normal science.' A paradigm shift happens when anomalies the old paradigm cannot handle build up into a crisis that is eventually resolved by adopting a new paradigm, as when Newtonian mechanics gave way to Einsteinian relativity. Kuhn calls the two paradigms 'incommensurable' because although Einstein's equations reduce to Newton's at low velocities, the concepts involved are not actually the same: Newtonian mass is a fixed, conserved quantity, while relativistic mass varies with velocity and is interconvertible with energy, so the same word picks out a different concept in each theory. Because the two paradigms are built out of different, mutually untranslatable concepts, there is, in a strict sense, no shared language in which to compare them directly, statement for statement. Kuhn tries to block the charge that this makes theory choice irrational by pointing to values like accuracy, consistency, scope, simplicity, and fruitfulness that scientists on both sides of a revolution still accept. Since these criteria are shared across paradigms, applying them to Newton's and Einstein's theories functions like a neutral checklist that settles which theory is objectively better, which is why Kuhn thought scientific revolutions amount to rational progress rather than mere fashion or persuasion."
