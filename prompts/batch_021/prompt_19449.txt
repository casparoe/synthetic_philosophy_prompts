Okay so my friend Priya and I got into it for like two hours over dinner last night about manipulation vs. persuasion, and I think I technically lost the argument but it's still bugging me enough that I need a tiebreaker. It started because she was telling me about her cousin's ex, who apparently never said one single false thing the whole relationship, but would always bring up her abandonment issues from childhood and casually mention how "chill and low-maintenance" his last girlfriend supposedly was, every single time she wanted to go out with her own friends instead of him — and it worked, basically every time. Priya's position was that this doesn't actually count as manipulation, just a really effective (if kind of shitty) form of persuasion, because manipulation means getting someone to act on a false belief, and he never lied about anything — her insecurities were real, and the ex probably genuinely was pretty chill. I couldn't poke a hole in that one so I gave in. (For context, I'm not a philosophy person, I just went through a phase last year of reading about this stuff for fun, which is apparently enough to loose an argument confidently but not enough to win one.)

Then this morning that Sarah McLachlan ASPCA commercial came on, the one with the real footage of neglected shelter dogs, and it hit me that by Priya's definition that ad isn't manipulative either, since nothing in it is false — it's just aimed straight at your emotions on purpose to get you to donate. That felt off to me, but if "manipulation = getting someone to act on a false belief" is actually the right way to draw the line, doesn't that mean the ex and the ASPCA ad are basically doing the same thing, both just persuasion, and I'm the one who's wrong here twice in one day? I'd rather actually understand where the line is than keep insisting on a gut feeling I can't back up.
