For years my working rule in difficult conversations has been simple: keep going as long as I still feel like I'm being reasonable, and stop the moment I notice I've lost my composure. A friend recently pointed out a problem with this. Some of the worst things I've said in arguments came out at moments when I felt completely level-headed, and only afterward could I see that I had already stopped thinking straight well before I actually stopped talking. That's unsettling, because it suggests my in-the-moment sense that "I'm still fine" can fail exactly when it matters most, right around the point where things start to go wrong. What strikes me as even stranger is that deliberately double-checking myself mid-conversation doesn't obviously help, since any second look I take is produced by the very faculty that just failed me, and I don't see why it should be any more trustworthy than the first. Is there a genuine problem here, or am I just describing ordinary bad self-control in an overly fancy way? And if it is a real problem, is there any way to actually guard against it, or is this simply a limit on self-monitoring that no amount of care can fix?
