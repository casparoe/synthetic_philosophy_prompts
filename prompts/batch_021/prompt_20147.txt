In my seminar on collective decision-making, we covered the Condorcet paradox and the discursive dilemma back to back, and both were presented as cases where majority voting turns individually coherent inputs into an incoherent group output. This has left me thinking that the discursive dilemma is essentially just Condorcet's paradox restated in terms of propositions rather than preference rankings, and that List and Pettit's judgment-aggregation framework mainly dresses up an old eighteenth-century observation in new notation. Is that a fair way to understand the relationship between the two, and if not, why do theorists treat the discursive dilemma as a genuinely distinct discovery rather than just a special case of Condorcet's result?
