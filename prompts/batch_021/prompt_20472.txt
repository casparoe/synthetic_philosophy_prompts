I'm taking a seminar this term on the philosophy of machine learning, and one issue from our unit on interpretability has been difficult for me to resolve. Researchers often say that a network "represents" some property whenever that property is linearly decodable from its hidden activations by a trained probe, but it is well known that probes can sometimes decode information a network never actually uses downstream, and a sufficiently expressive probe can find structure almost anywhere one looks. So decodability by itself does not seem able to carry the weight that talk of "representation" is meant to bear. Could you spell out precisely what would need to be true of a network's internal causal organization, as opposed to merely its activations, for a representational claim to be warranted, and say whether you think a realist stance toward these representations is actually defensible, or whether a more deflationary position, instrumentalist or perhaps fictionalist, is the more honest one to take?
