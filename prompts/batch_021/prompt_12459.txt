I spent thirty years as an experimental physicist, and since retiring I have started reading philosophy in earnest with a small group of fellow retirees. A puzzle from my working life keeps returning to me. In our largest experiments we would test many candidate signals against the data at once, and for any single signal that cleared our usual statistical threshold, I was entitled to believe it was probably real rather than noise; yet I also knew, simply from how many signals we tested, that some number of those apparent discoveries were bound to be flukes, though I could never say in advance which ones. I feel exactly the same tension about my career as a whole: I published close to two hundred papers, I would still defend the central claim of any one of them today, and yet I am fully confident that at least one central claim, somewhere among that many papers, is false. My reading group insists that I cannot rationally hold on to all of these beliefs at once, but that verdict strikes me as too quick, and I cannot say exactly why. Where, if anywhere, does the inconsistency actually lie, and which belief, if any, should I revise?
