Here's the student's answer, retyped from their handwriting but otherwise verbatim:

"Zorn's Lemma: if (P, ≤) is a nonempty poset in which every chain has an upper bound in P, then P has a maximal element.

Let P = {S ⊆ V : S is linearly independent}, ordered by inclusion. P is nonempty since ∅ ∈ P (the empty set is vacuously independent).

Let C ⊆ P be a chain and let U = ⋃_{S∈C} S. To see U ∈ P, take any finite v_1,...,v_n ∈ U; each v_i lies in some S_i ∈ C, and since C is totally ordered, one of the S_i (say S_k) contains all the others, so v_1,...,v_n ∈ S_k and are therefore independent. So every finite subset of U is independent, meaning U itself is independent, so U ∈ P. U is clearly an upper bound for C.

So P is a nonempty poset in which every chain has an upper bound, so by Zorn's Lemma P has a maximal element B. But a basis is just a maximal linearly independent subset of V, so B is a basis for V. QED.

Role of AC: the argument is non-constructive — it tells us B exists without giving any way to actually write it down. Zorn's Lemma is actually a somewhat weaker assumption than the full Axiom of Choice, which is why it's preferable to invoke it here rather than AC directly."
