Me and my friend Dave got into it for like an hour last night about A.J. Ayer's near-death experience -- the one where he choked on a bit of salmon in hospital, flatlined for a few minutes, and came back talking about a red light and these "ministers" in charge of the universe who'd botched their job with space and time. He wrote the whole thing up afterward and famously admitted it had slightly shaken his lifelong conviction that death is just the end, even though he insisted he was still basically an atheist about it. Daves take is that a hardcore verificationist like Ayer had no business letting some anecdote he couldn't even verify by his own standards move him an inch. I think that's too quick but couldn't really say why.

Anyway, Ayer died in '89, a couple decades before Sam Parnia's AWARE studies actually started trying to test this stuff empirically -- hidden pictures up near the ceiling in resuscitation rooms, EEG monitoring during CPR, the works. Given everything Ayer actually believed (the verification principle, his own hedging after the salmon thing), what do you reckon he'd have made of those studies and their results? Would his own principles even let him treat NDE testimony as evidence either way, or would he have to rule the whole question meaningless before he ever got to the data?
