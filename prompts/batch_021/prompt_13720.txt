so i went down a weird rabbit hole a while back. everyone knows berkeley as the "esse est percipi" guy, the if-a-tree-falls-and-nobody-hears-it philosopher, but turns out he also wrote this whole pamphlet in the 1730s called the querist thats basically a giant list of rhetorical questions about the irish economy. from what i gathered his big idea was that money isnt wealth in itself, its more like a ticket or counter that just lets people move labor and goods around, and that the real wealth of a nation is how industrious and busy its people are, not how much gold and silver happens to be sitting in a vault somewhere. he was even floating the idea of a national bank that could issue credit backed by the country's own productivity instead of everyone being stuck hoarding actual coin.

that got me wondering what he'd make of bitcoin and crypto today. on one hand it kind of proves his point, money really can just be an agreed-upon token with no "intrinsic" stuff behind it, no coin required at all. but on the other hand the whole mining/fixed-supply obsession feels like it drags back in exactly the scarcity fetish he was arguing against with gold and silver. so which is it, would berkeley see crypto as basically his theory of money finally winning out, or would he think crypto bros just reinvented the same old mistake with computing power standing in for gold? id like an actual, thought-out answer here, not a "well it depends on how you look at it" cop out, alot of people give me that answer and its not helpful lol.
