I'm writing a piece on the history of the Society for Psychical Research and would like a philosophical point checked. In The Methods of Ethics (1874), Henry Sidgwick identifies what philosophers call the "dualism of practical reason" — the apparent conflict between rational egoism and rational benevolence — and, as I understand it, goes on to resolve that conflict within the book itself. Since he had therefore already settled the technical argument before co-founding the SPR and taking up the investigation of mediums and telepathy in the 1880s, I'd like to present his psychical research in my article as a separate, essentially personal enthusiasm, distinct from the philosophical system for which he's remembered. Is that a defensible way to draw the line, or would historians of philosophy see a stronger connection between the two pursuits?
