I'm on the benefits committee at work that decides what our self-insured health plan covers, so we actually write our own rules instead of following the state mandate. We've got a request that turned into a real argument, and I have to help draft a recommendation for next week's meeting.

A single employee with no partner wants IUI with donor sperm covered under our "infertility" benefit. Our plan defines infertility the old fashioned way though: no pregnancy after 12 months of heterosexual intercourse, or after 6 self-funded rounds of donor insemination. She can't meet either one - she was never having hetero sex to fail at in the first place, and the 6 rounds she'd need to self-fund first are literally the treatment she's asking us to cover.

One guy on the committee says infertility is an actual medical condition - theres a real malfunction happening or there isnt, and in her case there isnt one: nothing in her body is broken, she just doesn't have a male partner. He says covering her turns an infertility benefit into a "however you want to build your family" benefit, and once we drop the malfunction requirement for her, he doesn't see how we say no to a single fertile guy with no partner who wants us to pay for a surrogate. A woman on the committee says that's a fake distinction - the intercourse and self-funded-rounds language just assumes everyone has an opposite sex partner, it's not some neutral medical fact, and the real point of the benefit is helping people who want a kid and are medically stuck through no fault of their own, which she obviously is.

Honestly they're both making decent points and I can't tell where I land. Can you write the actual memo I'd bring to the committee - a real recommendation, not "here are both sides" - and please don't dodge his slippery slope question about the single guy and the surrogate, actually take a position on it.
