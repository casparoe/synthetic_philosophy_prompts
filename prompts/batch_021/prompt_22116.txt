Ok so this happened to me about a month ago and i still haven't fully shaken it off. I cover science and ideas for a living, and I put out a pretty measured piece on the whole octopus-consciousness debate — hedged, caveated, the works, nothing spicy. A big account with something like half a million followers screenshotted one sentence out of context, and within a few hours I had this wave hit me: hundreds of quote-tweets and replies, people I've never heard of calling me a hack, screenshots of my face circulating in group chats I'm apparently not even in. Told a couple of reporter friends about it and they just shrugged, "that's the internet, everyone gets ratio'd eventually, dont read the replies" — which, sure, but it didn't really explain the feeling.

What's stuck with me is this: I DMed a handful of the nastier accounts just to ask, calmly, if they'd actually read the piece, and a bunch of them backed off almost instantly — "oh I didn't mean anything by it, that's just how you gotta dunk on this stuff," "idk man everyone's saying it." Like none of them individually actually believed the thing "everyone" was collectively screaming at me. I keep comparing it to an actual heckling incident from years back, at a live panel, where a genuinely annoyed room of maybe eighty people booed a dumb answer I gave — that felt awful too, but a totally different species of awful. There were faces. I could've walked up to any one of them afterward. By the next morning it was just over. This online version has no faces, doesn't really end, and somehow still counts as "true" months later, like a verdict that everyone and no one delivered. So I guess what I'm actually asking is: is "internet public opinion" a genuinely different kind of thing than an old-fashioned mob, or than a single named critic writing a takedown under their own byline? Does the anonymity and scale change who's responsible for what happened to me, or whether it even makes sense to feel "condemned" when no one in particular did the condemning? Would genuinely rather understand the shape of this than be told again to just log off.
