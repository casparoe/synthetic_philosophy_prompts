malik's take: this is a textbook cascade — person 1 switches for some random reason, person 2 has a weak signal so just copies them, and once two people in a row do the same thing everyone after that just imitates instead of using their own info. but he says every one of those people is still being bayes-rational given what they'd seen, so its totally fair to call the whole thing "rational."

dana said thats a dodge: if nobody after the first two is actually using their own info, the whole grade's decision is really only worth like two people's opinions dressed up as a consensus, and its weird to call that "rational" when almost nobody's actual information ever got used.

dana said ok fine, but then cascades are supposed to be fragile — like one kid with clearly better info should be able to flip the whole thing — so why did a pretty convincing contrarian post barely move anyone? malik didn't really have a real answer beyond "guess that kid just wasn't popular enough."
