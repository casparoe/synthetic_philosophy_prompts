In Plato's Gorgias, Socrates argues that rhetoric is not a genuine art but a knack for producing conviction without knowledge, and Gorgias himself boasts that his rhetorical skill once persuaded a reluctant patient to accept treatment when the attending physician's own arguments had failed. Socrates also insists throughout on short, cross-examined exchanges rather than long uninterrupted speeches, precisely because the latter allow errors to go unchallenged. Suppose Plato were introduced to AI safety via debate, a scalable oversight proposal in which two advanced AI systems argue opposing sides of a question before a human (or weaker AI) judge who cannot verify the underlying claims directly. Would he conclude that debate's adversarial, cross-examined structure answers his objection to rhetoric, given its resemblance to his own preferred dialectical method? Or would he maintain that a judge lacking the relevant expertise remains at the mercy of whichever debater is more persuasive rather than more truthful, particularly when a dishonest debater can hide a flaw inside a long, intricate argument the judge cannot fully unpack?
