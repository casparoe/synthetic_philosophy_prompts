I'm the 'public member' on my hospital's psychiatric ethics committee -- I cover science and ideas for a living, which apparently qualifies. Hard case for Thursday, and the committee's split, so I need an actual memo, not a shrug.

Two years ago, stable and by everyones account competent, a patient with bipolar I signed a psychiatric advance directive with what her chart calls a 'Ulysses clause': naming the exact drug to force on her if she went manic again, overriding any objection she might make in the moment, plus a line reading, 'ignore manic me, I mean this.' She's manic now, refusing the drug, lucid enough that nobody will call her flatly incompetent -- just running on different priorities than the woman who signed it. Policy says we honor a Ulysses clause 'unless the objection reflects a competing, capacitated judgment' -- and nobody here agrees what that means in practice.

Draft the memo I'll circulate: a specific recommendation, the reasoning, and an honest version of the strongest case against whatever we land on. Don't just retell Ulysses and the sirens and call it settled -- we've all had that class.
