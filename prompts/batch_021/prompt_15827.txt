hey, random Wittgenstein thing that's been bugging me while prepping for comps. In On Certainty §341-343 he sets up the hinge propositions idea:

"the questions that we raise and our doubts depend on the fact that some propositions are exempt from doubt, are as it were like hinges on which those turn... it belongs to the logic of our scientific investigations that certain things are indeed not doubted... If I want the door to turn, the hinges must stay put."

that sounds pretty fixed and structural, hinges have to stay put or the whole door (inquiry) doesn't work. But then way earlier, at §96-97, he uses a totally different picture for what seems like basically the same phenomenon:

"It might be imagined that some propositions...were hardened and functioned as channels for such empirical propositions as were not hardened but fluid; and that this relation altered with time, in that fluid propositions hardened, and hard ones became fluid... the river-bed of thoughts may shift."

here the hardened/hinge-like propositions can apparently soften and shift over historical time. So which is it? Is "the hinges must stay put" just a claim about what has to hold at a given moment for a particular inquiry to run at all, leaving room for that same proposition to lose hinge-status later on (like the bed shifting), or is Wittgenstein actually committed to hinges being fixed for good once they count as hinges? My advisor says im overreading two loose metaphors that were never meant to fit together, but that seems off for someone this obsessive about how he phrases things. Is there an actual defensible reading that reconciles "must stay put" with "may shift", or is this just a real tension in the text that people tend to gloss over?
