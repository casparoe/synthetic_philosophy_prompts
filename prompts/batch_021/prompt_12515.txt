I'm working on a story that hinges on an allegation two anonymous sources were willing to discuss but not confirm outright. The first, a former employee, told me the claim "sounds about right" but that they "wouldn't stake their reputation on it," which left me thinking it was probably true, though far from certain. The second, who still works there, was more guarded and, if anything, pointed the other way: all they would say was that they "wouldn't be shocked either way," which left me somewhat less confident than before we'd spoken. Neither conversation settled anything outright; each one only nudged my confidence a bit.

Here's what's nagging at me: when I try to work out how confident I should be overall, I get a different answer depending on the order in which I factor in the two accounts. Considering the former employee's hedge first and the current employee's second leaves me more confident than doing it in the reverse order, even though it's the same two conversations either way. That feels like it shouldn't be possible if I were reasoning about the evidence correctly. Is this order-sensitivity a sign that I'm making a real mistake in how I'm combining hedged, uncertain testimony, or is it an unavoidable feature of updating beliefs this way — and either way, how should I actually be doing it?
