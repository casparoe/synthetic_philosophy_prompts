I'm a student member of my high school's Honor Council, which can discipline "conduct that violates the moral standards of the school community" even when no other rule has been broken. A case has come to us: several seniors, all eighteen, have been playing poker for real money among themselves off campus. Nobody is in debt or was pressured, no one outside the group is affected, and our handbook's gambling rule, which covers on-campus conduct only, doesn't reach it. Parents and teachers still want them punished, arguing that a school depends on shared standards, and that quietly tolerating this, even though it harms no one in particular, erodes something the community needs. Other Council members say we should only discipline conduct that harms an identifiable person, since "some people are offended" would justify punishing almost any unpopular private choice. The Council is evenly split. Please draft the memo I'll bring to our vote: it should recommend how we should rule, sketch a workable test for similar future cases, and take the "shared standards" side's strongest version seriously rather than dismiss it.
