My partner's doing a physics postdoc on inflationary cosmology, and a couple weeks ago, kind of late at night, she went on this whole tangent about "Boltzmann brains" while we were lying in bed that I have not been able to shake since. As I understood it (and correct me if I've mangled the physics): if the universe just keeps expanding forever into a cold, empty de-Sitter-ish equilibrium, then given enough time -- not "billions of years" time, but truly absurd amounts of time -- random thermal fluctuations in that emptiness will occasionally, purely by chance, assemble a fully functioning brain out of the vacuum, complete with a full set of false memories of a whole life it never lived. And because there are so vastly more ways for a brain to pop into existence like that than there are ways for one to arise the "normal" way (billions of years of low-entropy structure formation and evolution), a theory where the universe goes on forever like that predicts these lone, momentary "Boltzmann brains" should hugely outnumber real, embodied observers like us -- and from the inside there'd be no way to tell which kind you are. Some cosmologists apparently take this seriously enough to treat it as a strike against those particular models, precisely because of that self-undermining prediction.

Anyway its now living in my head rent free and I'd rather turn it into fiction than keep doom-scrolling about it at 2am. Feel free to write it from the pov of the momentary brain itself, or a scientist who can't stop thinking about it, or whoever -- up to you.
