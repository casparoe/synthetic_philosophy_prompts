I never studied philosophy formally; what I know of it I've picked up on my own over the last few years, mostly through papers, recorded lectures, and long arguments in a small online discussion group I help run. Once a month or so we manage to get an actual researcher to join us for an hour of questions, and next month's guest has built her career defending the claim that perceptual experience itself -- not merely the beliefs or judgments we form on the basis of it -- can be altered by what we already believe, expect, or want to see. She likes to point to things such as hunger changing how large food looks, or radiologists perceptually "seeing" tumors that untrained viewers miss entirely, as evidence that cognition reaches all the way down into perception rather than stopping at the level of belief.

I'd like the group to get past the point where she simply restates and re-illustrates the thesis with a new example, so could you draft nine or ten interview questions with that in mind? I want at least one question that presses the standard reply that what's really being penetrated in examples like these is attention, eye movements, or memory rather than the perceptual experience itself; one that asks her to distinguish genuine top-down penetration from cases that might just be perceptual learning, where practice quietly builds new bottom-up feature detectors without any belief reaching into perception at all; one that raises the worry that if belief can shape experience this easily, perception stops being an independent check on what we believe, and using it as evidence becomes circular; and one built around the early-vision/late-vision distinction that people like Pylyshyn and Fodor rely on to argue that early visual processing is informationally encapsulated from the rest of cognition. For each of those four, add a short, specific follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, rather than a generic "can you say more about that." Please close the set with a question asking what single observation or experiment would actually change her mind, because in everything I've read on this debate, that question never seems to get asked.
