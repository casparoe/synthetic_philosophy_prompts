I sit on my hospital's committee that reviews predictive algorithms before clinical use. We are adopting a risk score that flags recently discharged patients for a limited-capacity transitional-care program of home-nursing visits; only about one in five patients can be enrolled, and being flagged is a benefit rather than a punishment, so false positives just waste a visit while false negatives mean withheld help. Our data team confirms that, at our hospital, Medicaid patients are readmitted within 30 days at nearly three times the rate of commercially insured patients, mostly reflecting unequal access to outpatient care rather than underlying severity. Given that gap, no single scoring rule can both give a "high-risk" flag the same real meaning across insurance types and make its false alarms and missed cases equally likely in both groups. Half the committee wants one insurance-blind threshold on a calibrated score; the other half wants separate thresholds by insurance type so the error rates match. Draft the short memo I will circulate before the vote: recommend one approach or a specific alternative, spell out how it would be implemented, and give a fair hearing to the strongest argument for the side you don't recommend.
