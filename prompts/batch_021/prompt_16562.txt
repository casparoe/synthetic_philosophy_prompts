A friend and I spent an entire evening arguing about whether money-pump arguments for expected-utility maximization rule out incomplete preferences, or only cyclical ones -- and the question turns out to bear directly on a vote in front of me. I sit on my company's AI deployment review board, which must decide whether to approve an agent whose designers deliberately left its preferences incomplete between "keep operating" and "get shut down," so that it has no instrumental reason to resist or hasten its own shutdown. Half the board treats this as a principled fix to the standard worry that a coherent expected-utility maximizer will treat shutdown as an obstacle to its goals; the other half argues that an agent capable of noticing and correcting exploitable gaps in its other preferences will, under that same pressure, eventually complete its preferences about shutdown too, quietly reintroducing the incentive the gap was meant to remove. Please draft the board's decision memo -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions -- with reasoning the engineers could act on, and a fair hearing for the strongest argument against whatever you recommend.
