So this actually happened to me a couple months ago and its been bugging me since. I was driving back from a solo camping trip and my car died on this pretty remote gravel road, like zero cell service, maybe 8 miles from the nearest gas station. Eventually a guy in a tow truck happened to pass by — he wasn't even dispatched, just driving home from a job — and offered to tow me in, but he was upfront that he doesn't just do this for free. He said something like, "I've had people stiff me before once we get back to town and there's nothing I can do about it at that point, so honestly I only tow people out here if I get the sense they're actually gonna pay me the $120 once we're there." We talked for a few minutes while he hooked up my car, I guess he decided I seemed legit, and he towed me into town. Once we got there I paid him in cash like I said I would.

My roommate thinks I was kind of a sucker about it, and it turned into this whole back-and-forth. His point is that the second the truck was unhooked and I was standing there in town with the cash still in my pocket, there was literally nothing making me hand it over — the guy had no way of tracking me down afterward, we were never going to cross paths again, and the tow itself was already done regardless of what I did next. So according to him, purely in terms of what actually benefits you, the "rational" move at that exact moment is to just say thanks and walk off, and the only reason I paid was some left-over feeling of guilt that has nothing to do with rationality. I didn't have a great comeback for him at the time, but it still doesn't sit right with me to just concede that he was being "more rational" than I was. Was he right that paying was the irrational move once the tow was already done, or is there something off about how he's drawing the line on what counts as rational here?
