We were going through Aumann's backward induction paper, the one arguing that common knowledge of rationality (CKR) forces the backward induction outcome in games like the centipede game, so both players "take" at the very first move even though continuing would make everyone richer.

Leah said the result is basically airtight: if its common knowledge that both players are rational, backward induction unravels the whole tree from the last node backward, so a rational first mover has to defect immediately. That's just what the theorem shows, full stop.

Dev pushed back: if the game actually reaches round 2, doesn't that already refute common knowledge of rationality, since player 1 deviated from what CKR was supposed to guarantee? And if CKR is already broken at that point, why should player 2 keep reasoning via backward induction for the rest of the game? Seems like the whole argument needs CKR to hold at nodes that, by its own logic, could only be reached if CKR had already failed.

Leah answered that Aumann's notion of rationality isn't a claim about what's actually been observed so far, its about whether each player's entire strategy (covering every node, including ones that never get reached) is optimal given beliefs about the others that are fixed from the start - what Aumann calls "substantive" rationality, as opposed to mere "material" rationality on the realized path. So reaching an off-path node doesn't refute anything, since nothing was ever claimed about what would actually happen, only that the whole contingency plan is a best response.

Dev said fine, maybe that's technically consistent, but it just relocates the issue: now the theorem is smuggling in a very strong assumption that beliefs about everyone's strategy are fixed and common knowledge from move one, immune to anything that happens in the game. That's a substantive and contestable claim about how rational agents actually form and revise beliefs, not a free consequence of "rationality is common knowledge" - so the theorem doesn't settle whether real rational agents would play this way, it just proves a conditional where the antecedent does all the disputed work.
