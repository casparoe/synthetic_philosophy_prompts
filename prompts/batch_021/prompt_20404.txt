I'm a backend engineer at a mid-sized startup, and something that happened during a recent code review has been nagging at me ever since. One of our most senior engineers left a fairly blunt comment on a teammate's pull request for a customer-facing feature, calling it "irresponsible" to merge code without any unit tests and "exactly the kind of shortcut that eventually causes an outage." Taken purely on its own merits, I think he's right about that. What bothers me is that a few of us who work closely with him know that he routinely pushes untested changes to the internal tooling repository he mostly maintains alone, where no one reviews his commits closely enough to ever call him on it. The teammate who received the comment has no idea any of this goes on. When I mentioned it privately to another colleague, she just shrugged and said, "He's not wrong that it's bad practice, it just feels off for him, of all people, to be the one saying so."

That reaction has stayed with me longer than I expected. Is there a meaningful difference between whether a piece of criticism is correct and whether the particular person voicing it has any right to be the one voicing it? Does it matter that his own untested code sits in a technically lower-stakes repository, or does the same underlying principle apply either way? And if the teammate eventually learned about all this, would that give him a legitimate reason to shrug off the feedback, or would that just be a convenient way of dodging a valid point by going after the critic instead of the substance? I'd like to reason through this properly rather than settle for something like "everyone's a hypocrite sometimes," since that doesn't really capture what feels unresolved to me about the situation.
