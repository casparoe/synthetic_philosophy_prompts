My older sister is finishing a philosophy PhD and getting married this summer; she and her fiancé have decided not to have children. She has held this view since she first encountered David Benatar's asymmetry argument in college, and she is always insistent that it is not a lifestyle preference: on her view, even a person whose life goes well is harmed by having come into existence, because the absence of pain in someone who never exists is a real good, while the absence of pleasure in someone who never exists is not a loss to anyone, since no one exists to miss it. I am her younger sibling, still in high school, and I have been asked to give the toast. Most guests will be expecting the usual warm jokes about future grandchildren; a handful will be philosophers from her cohort who will notice immediately if I soften her position into "kids just aren't for her." Could you write a five-minute toast that stays affectionate and funny, gets the real shape of her argument right for the people who will be listening for it, and still finds something genuine to celebrate in two people who have thought this hard about not having children?
