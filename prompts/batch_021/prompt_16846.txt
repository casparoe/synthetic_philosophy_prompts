My copy editor Renata's retiring after 34 years fact-checking everything I write, right down to whether a character's eyes are still the same colour in chapter twenty as they were in chapter one, and I'm giving the toast at her retirement party next friday. She's also bought exactly one lottery ticket a week for as long as anyone's known her, always saying "I already know this ones not it" -- which her nephew, a philosophy PhD student, reckons is basically the same move as her day job: swearing every fact in a manuscript checks out, and swearing in the same breath that the finished book still has an error in it somewhere. Half the room's going to clock that as the lottery paradox and the preface paradox and will be listening for whether I actually get it right; the other half just want a nice toast about Renata. The one thing I can't do is pretend those two beliefs secretly reconcile -- she's built her entire career on insisting they don't. Can you write a short, warm, funny toast that makes that unresolved tension the actual shape of the compliment, and not just a clever aside?
