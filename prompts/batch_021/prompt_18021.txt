Quick recap in case the setup's slipping your mind: two players alternate turns, the pot on the table grows every round, and on your turn you either grab it (ending the game, with you getting the bigger share) or pass it along. My friend's position was the standard textbook one: backward induction says you reason from the very last round backwards — the last mover would obviously grab, so the second-to-last mover should grab anticipating that, and so on — which supposedly means two "rational" players grab on move one, before the pot's grown at all. Which is obviously a weird result, and also not what happens when actual humans (or even game theorists!) play the thing in lab experiments.

Doesn't the very fact that you're still in the game already undercut the common-knowledge assumption that was supposed to justify grabbing back in round one? My friend thought I was overcomplicating a settled result; I thought she was hand-waving away an actual paradox, and we never got anywhere.
