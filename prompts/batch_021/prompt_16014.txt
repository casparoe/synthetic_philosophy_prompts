I clerked for a federal appellate judge for two years right out of law school, and she's retiring after almost thirty years on the bench. I got roped into giving the big toast at her retirement dinner next month, which is stressing me out becasue the room is going to be split right down the middle: half of it is other judges, ex-clerks, and lawyers who will catch every jurisprudential in-joke, and the other half is her actual family, who mostly know her as "grandma who works alot" and would have zero idea what "the canons of construction" even means. To make it harder, she's a proud, pretty hardcore textualist -- the kind of judge who'd cut a clerk off mid-sentence for saying "but obviously Congress meant..." -- and she's said more than once, half-joking, that if anyone gets up at her retirement and starts speculating about her "real motives" or the deeper "purpose" behind her career instead of just describing what she actually did and ruled, she will heckle them from the front row.

So I'm stuck with a genuinely fun problem: a toast is supposed to be exactly the kind of thing she's spent her whole career being suspicious of -- reading a life for its meaning and purpose instead of just its text. I've never written anything like this before, and the one good lawyer joke I know I already used at my cousin's wedding, so I'm coming up empty. Can you write the toast for me (something like 4-5 minutes read aloud)? I want one real textualism-vs-purposivism joke in there that actually lands for the lawyers without losing the family completely, and I want the speech itself to work within her own interpretive commitments -- not just namedrop "textualism" as a fun fact, and no big detours into her "true purpose" or what she "really" stood for unless you can find a clever way around that restriction. Mostly I just want it to land as love, not as a CLE lecture.
