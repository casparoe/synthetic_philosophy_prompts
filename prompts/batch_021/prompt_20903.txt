My younger brother is getting married next month, and I'm giving the best man's toast. He and his fiancée met at fifteen, were together for four years, broke up for almost a decade afterward while they each built separate lives, and found their way back to each other three years ago. At their engagement dinner, she read aloud a letter he'd written her at seventeen, promising he'd never love anyone else — a promise he plainly didn't keep, which the two of them now find funny rather than sad.

I've spent a lot of my free time this past year reading about the philosophy of persistence on my own — what makes something the same thing over time, particularly the debate between endurantism and perdurantism — and I can't stop turning that old letter over in my head in light of it. If the perdurantists have it right, the boy who wrote those words and the man who'll be standing at the altar next month aren't strictly the same thing at all, just two stages of one long series — so what is he actually promising this time, and to whom?

One of the other groomsmen is finishing a philosophy doctorate and will notice immediately if I dodge this with a line about "people change, but love remains," while nobody else in the room has ever given the question a moment's thought. Could you write me a five-minute toast that takes this seriously enough to satisfy him, without turning into a lecture, and that still sends everyone back to their seats feeling they've heard a toast and not a thesis defense?
