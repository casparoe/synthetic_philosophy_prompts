My aunt Carol is retiring next month after 34 years doing river and old-growth forest conservation work for the state, and I somehow got roped into giving the toast at her retirement party. Shes basically the reason there's still old growth left along the river where we grew up. A few years back I went down a rabbit hole reading about the philosopher Derek Parfit, I think it started with some obituary of his that I stumbled on, and one of his big examples is honestly almost exactly my aunt's career: a government picking between what he calls "Depletion," using up a resource fast for the people alive right now, versus "Conservation," saving it so later generations are better off. His whole point (which I looked up again last night to make sure I wasn't misremembering it) is that you can't actually say Conservation is better *for* any specific future person, because who even gets born in fifty or a hundred years depends on tiny stuff like exactly when people happen to meet and have kids, which the policy itself changes over time. So under Depletion a totally different set of people ends up existing then under Conservation, not the same people just worse off, and the Depletion people were never going to exist at all if we'd gone with Conservation instead. Apparently this whole thing is called the nonidentity problem and there's a big literature on it that I've only half followed.

This is stuck in my head now because it seems to mean my aunt didn't "save" anyone in the way a toast normally wants to say she did -- there's no specific future kid out there who's better off than they otherwise would've been because of her, since the version of that exact kid in the world without her never would've existed either way, full stop. And yet what she did obviously still matters a huge amount, and I don't want to get up there and say something I now think is kind of confused just because it's the easy, normal thing to say at a retirement party.

Here's the situation: it's a pretty mixed room, maybe 25 people who worked with her at the agency who will absolutely know who Parfit is and will clock it if I get this wrong, and another 40ish who are just family and neighbors who want a nice toast and could not care less about population ethics. I've got about 5 minutes. Could you write me a toast that actually takes the nonidentity problem seriously instead of quietly ignoring it, but still works as an actual toast -- something honest about the puzzle that doesn't turn into a lecture, and that still leaves people glad they came and maybe a little teary by the end?
