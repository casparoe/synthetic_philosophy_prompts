Over the past year I have been teaching myself Bayesian epistemology, with no formal philosophy background, and I keep getting stuck on Bas van Fraassen's Reflection Principle. As I understand it, the principle says that if I am currently certain I will hold a particular degree of belief in some claim at a later time, then my present degree of belief in that claim, conditional on holding that future belief, should already match it. I am about to begin several months of intensive psychotherapy, and everyone I know who has done something similar says they came out believing quite different things about their family, their career, and what matters most to them, without being able to point to any specific new fact they learned. I do not know which direction my own views will move, only that they probably will, and that from the inside it will feel like clarity rather than manipulation. Please describe the most important implications of the Reflection Principle, and of its well-known difficulties in cases like anticipated memory loss or belief change that is not driven by ordinary evidence, for how much weight I should give right now to opinions I only expect to hold once an experience like this has reshaped me.
