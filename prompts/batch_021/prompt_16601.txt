I'm writing a dissertation chapter on meaning-constitution and want to see how a real exchange between the two most serious camps on rule-following would actually play out, rather than each side's position summarized in isolation. Please write a dialogue between a Kripkensteinian who accepts the sceptical paradox and defends the sceptical solution's shift to communal assertability conditions, and a McDowellian who holds that the paradox only gets a grip because of an illegitimate demand for a reductive, non-normative fact of the matter, and who instead defends a non-reductionist realism on which grasping a rule is a sui generis capacity manifest in practice. I want the McDowellian to spell out concretely why the sceptical solution still concedes too much to the sceptic, rather than just asserting that it does, and I want the Kripkensteinian to push back on the suggestion that rampant Platonism and reductive dispositionalism exhaust the alternatives. At some point, have them work through the case of a lifelong solitary rule-follower who has never belonged to any community, since that's where I suspect the sceptical solution's commitments are most exposed and where the McDowellian's alternative has to earn its keep. Please make sure they are actually responding to each other's specific moves turn by turn rather than delivering parallel lectures.
