I am a philosophy PhD student writing a dissertation chapter on population ethics and existential risk, in which I model humanity's expected future population across a range of extinction scenarios. A committee member has pushed me to incorporate a correction based on the Carter–Leslie Doomsday Argument, on the grounds that my own temporal position among the roughly 100 billion humans born so far should itself count as evidence against there being a very large future population. I am hesitant, since that correction only goes through if I adopt the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA); switching to the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) cancels the update entirely, but at the cost of generating Bostrom's Presumptuous Philosopher absurdity elsewhere in the model. Could you set out the most important implications of choosing SSA over SIA (or vice versa) for whether I should include this doomsday-style correction, and say whether some third option, such as Neal's full non-indexical conditioning, genuinely dissolves the dilemma rather than merely relabelling it?
