I've been reporting on the "do LLMs actually understand anything" debate for a peice I'm working on, and a cognitive scientist I interviewed kept steering me away from the Chinese Room — which is the only version of this most people I know have actually heard of — and back toward Stevan Harnad's 1990 symbol grounding problem paper instead. His point was that Harnad wasn't just restating Searle, he was offering an actual mechanism for how symbols stop being meaningless tokens: ground the "elementary" symbols in iconic representations (raw sensory analogs) and categorical representations (learned invariants that let a system sort, say, horses from mules), built up through real sensorimotor interaction with the world, and then let more complex symbols inherit meaning by composition — his go-to example was defining "zebra" for someone who's never seen one as "horse" plus "stripes," once "horse" and "stripes" are each already grounded. What the guy really wanted to know, and what I can't find a straight answer to anywhere, is whether a modern multimodal model — trained on text and images, or even hooked up to a robot arm — actually satisfies Harnad's specific conditions for grounding, or whether it's just grounding theater: swapping pixels in for words without ever getting the kind of world-involving causal hookup he thought was required.

Could you write me an essay that actually takes a side on this, instead of doing the "there are many perspectives" dodge journalists like me are always tempted to write? Walk through Harnad's own criteria seriously rather than just gesturing at "well, bodies matter," give the strongest version of the opposing case (that meaning might be recoverable from text-and-image statistics alone, no sensorimotor hookup needed — the distributional-semantics line, plus whatever you make of that "octopus" thought experiment people use against it), and then actually commit: has something like a frontier multimodal system already solved the grounding problem, is it on a path to solving it, or is grounding structurally out of reach for any system trained the way these things are trained, no matter how big it gets?
