So this happened in my formal epistemology reading group. We do this dorky weekly thing where everyone privately assigns a credence to some trivia claim before the answer's revealed, just to track our calibration (that week's claim was some obscure bit of history trivia, doesn't matter what). Two people in the group, Mara and Owen, have had near-identical calibration scores all semester -- so by any reasonable measure, peers on this stuff -- but this week they landed on wildly different credences, and before the reveal they got into it.

Mara said: if we're genuine peers here, the rational move for both of us is to just average our numbers -- that's what equal-weight conciliationism says to do. Owen said no way: he'd walked back through his reasoning twice, found nothing wrong with it, so why should the mere fact that Mara disagrees move him at all? Mara said that's exactly the move he's not allowed to make -- rechecking your own reasoning and finding it convincing isn't independent confirmation of anything, it's just trusting the same calculator that might be broken. Owen's comeback: plenty of serious epistemologists reject equal-weight conciliationism outright, so if he's required to defer to any peer who disagrees with him, he should be defering to THEM about conciliationism too -- which would let him stay put after all, since by his own lights conciliationism eats itself. Mara's response was that conciliationism can just carve out an exception for disagreements about disagreement -- conciliate everywhere else, but not on whether to conciliate -- which keeps the view intact. Owen wasn't havin it: he said the one exception being exactly the one that would sink the whole view is way too convenient, like she'd written herself an immunity clause.

Neither budged and honestly I think they both scored real points. Could you go through it move by move -- Mara's opening appeal, Owen's "I checked and found no error," Mara's independence point, Owen's self-undermining move, and Mara's exception clause -- and say who's ahead at each stage? Also, is that last move of Mara's principled, or is it really just the immunity clause Owen accuses it of being? Curious what you think both of them are missing, too.
