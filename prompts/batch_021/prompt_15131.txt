I'm a philosophy PhD student and hold the graduate-student seat on my university's Research Integrity Committee, which hears cases involving serious violations of research and lab-safety protocols. Two matched cases are on our docket. A chemistry doctoral student ran a scale-up reaction alone, skipping the mandatory second-person check; nothing went wrong, and the lapse surfaced only in a later audit. A doctoral student in an unrelated lab skipped the identical check under matched deadline pressure, and this time a mislabeled reagent caused a fire that left a labmate with serious burns. The fact-finding subcommittee has found both decisions equally negligent and equally foreseeable at the time -- nothing distinguishes the students except how things turned out. Our guidelines direct us to set penalties "in proportion to the harm caused," and the committee is split between recommending expulsion in the second case (a reprimand only in the first) and insisting that identical decisions warrant identical sanctions regardless of outcome. Please draft the memo I'll circulate recommending how to resolve both cases and how the harm-proportionality clause should be read going forward -- engaging honestly with the strongest reason a sanctioning body might have for letting outcomes, and not just culpability, matter.
