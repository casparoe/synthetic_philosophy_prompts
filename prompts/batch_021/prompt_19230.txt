Otto Neurath's Isotype project aimed to convey statistical and social facts through a standardized system of pictorial signs, on the premise that a well-designed picture language could communicate certain information more directly and universally than ordinary verbal language. At roughly the same time, Wittgenstein's Tractatus was developing, in a very different vein, the idea that a proposition represents reality by functioning as a logical picture of a state of affairs, depicting it in virtue of sharing its logical form rather than resembling it. Compare these two appeals to "picturing" as a mode of representation: do Neurath and Wittgenstein mean substantially the same thing by treating a sign as a picture, or does Neurath's practical project rest on a notion of depiction that is fundamentally different from, and perhaps even in tension with, Wittgenstein's logical one? Justify your answer with reference to how each account would need to explain a picture's capacity to misrepresent.
