Between novels, I sit on the editorial board of a small literary quarterly. The strongest nonfiction submission this cycle lays out David Benatar's asymmetry argument in careful detail and uses it to justify both the author's decision never to have children and her sense that her own life would have been better never begun; the essay never mentions suicide and closes on a note of wry endurance rather than despair. Two board members want it rejected outright, arguing that any essay concluding "better never to have been" reads as a suicide note regardless of authorial intent and shouldn't run without a clinical disclaimer that would flatten its voice; the rest of the board calls this a bad-faith conflation of a serious minority position in academic ethics with self-harm advocacy. Our submission guidelines bar only material "promoting self-harm," language drafted with a very different kind of piece in mind. Please draft the decision memo I'll bring to our next meeting: recommend publish, reject, or publish-with-conditions; specify any conditions precisely enough for our production editor to act on; and take the worried members' concern seriously on its own terms rather than waving it away.
