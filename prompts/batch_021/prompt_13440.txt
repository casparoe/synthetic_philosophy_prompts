I am working on a dissertation chapter on moral address, and I keep encountering a claim, common in the literature on blame, that hypocrites can lack "standing" to blame others for faults they themselves share. This has always struck me as suspicious, since the tu quoque response is a textbook ad hominem fallacy: pointing out that a critic does not practice what they preach does nothing to show that the criticism itself is false. If a hypocrite's accusation can be perfectly true despite their hypocrisy, then denying them "standing" looks like a more respectable-sounding way of doing exactly what the fallacy warns against: discrediting the message by discrediting the messenger. Is there a genuine distinction being drawn here, and if so, what exactly is it tracking that the fallacy does not already cover?
