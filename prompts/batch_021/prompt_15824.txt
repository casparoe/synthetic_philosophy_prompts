At work, we recently wrapped up the postmortem for a major outage, and two colleagues disagreed sharply about how to write the root-cause section, in a way I still can't quite adjudicate. One colleague argues the incident should be explained as an instance of a general, recurring pattern: given our mix of aggressive retries, shared connection pools, and slow health checks, a cascade like this was bound to happen sooner or later, and the write-up should mainly document that pattern so other teams can recognize and avoid it. The other thinks this misses what actually explains the incident, and wants a detailed, chronological account of the specific decisions and coincidences involved — a config pushed at an unlucky moment, an alert that fired late, an on-call engineer who made a reasonable but wrong call — since in her view only that story accounts for why the outage happened exactly when and how it did. Both accounts strike me as true, and the same disagreement resurfaces every time we write up a past failure, not just this one. Is there a principled way to decide which kind of explanation is the correct one, or to understand why we seem to need both?
