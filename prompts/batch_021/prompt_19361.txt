Next week's guest is a philosopher who's built her whole research profile around a "precautionary" argument for animal sentience -- basically the same asymmetric-cost reasoning that got UK regulators to start treating octopuses and crabs as sentient beings a few years back, except she wants to push it much further out, to insects and maybe even bivalves. Whenever someone tries to pin her down on a specific animal she doesn't want to commit to, her go-to line is some version of "the burden isn't on the animal to prove it feels something, its on us to prove it doesn't" -- which is a great line once, but by the fourth time she used it on me in the pre-interview call it started to feel like a rhetorical escape hatch.

Could you draft me 8-10 questions that don't just let her restate that line? Tack a follow-up onto each one keyed to whatever dodge she's most likely to reach for, and end with a question about what kind of evidence would actually count against her own view.
