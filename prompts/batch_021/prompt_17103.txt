I am drafting a dissertation chapter on meaning and normativity and want to avoid flattening a distinction that Kripke treats as pivotal to his reading of Wittgenstein. In Wittgenstein on Rules and Private Language, he insists that his response to the rule-following paradox is a 'skeptical solution' rather than a 'straight solution,' explicitly modeling the move on Hume's treatment of induction and causal necessity. Could you explain precisely what separates these two kinds of solution, why Kripke thinks the skeptical argument rules out any straight solution to the question of what fact makes it true that I mean plus rather than quus by '+', and why the resulting appeal to assertability conditions is meant to stop short of a bald error theory about meaning-ascriptions? I would also value your judgment on whether Boghossian's well-known stability objection shows that no such middle ground can actually be sustained.
