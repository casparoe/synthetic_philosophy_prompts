I've been reading about the history of climate policy for a debate-team brief on geoengineering, and I came across John von Neumann's 1955 Fortune essay "Can We Survive Technology?" His central claim was that humanity's real danger is not any single invention but the sheer acceleration of technological change outrunning our collective judgment. As examples, he pointed to industrial carbon dioxide gradually warming the planet and, more startlingly, to the near-future possibility of deliberately influencing large-scale weather and climate — a capability he considered especially dangerous because its effects could never be confined within one country's borders. He was also, however, one of the Cold War's most committed hawks, pushing hard for the hydrogen bomb and doubting that any arms-control agreement with the Soviets could be trusted or enforced.

Given both sides of his thinking, what do you think von Neumann would say about today's proposals to govern solar geoengineering through international treaties or moratoria that would bar any single country or company from unilaterally trying to cool the planet? Would his sense that uncontainable global technologies demand cooperative management win out, or would his conviction that agreements with untrustworthy rivals are worse than none at all lead him to oppose such a treaty? Please reason through the tension rather than simply asserting a verdict.
