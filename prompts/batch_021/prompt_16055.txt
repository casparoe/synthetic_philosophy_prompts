My town's been holding these public meetings about switching city council elections over to ranked choice voting, and I've gotten kind of hooked on reading about voting systems on my own ever since the first one. After last week's meeting a bunch of us went to the diner, and two people who'd been there, Warren and Liz, ended up having this whole arguement about it over pie that I still can't stop thinking about. I'm hoping someone who actually knows this stuff can weigh in, because neither of them has any formal background in it and I suspect they're both getting some of it wrong, I just can't tell which parts myself.

Roughly how it went: (1) Warren said ranked choice will basically fix the problem, since it kills the spoiler effect and finally gets you a winner who's genuinely the fair choice. (2) Liz said there's actually no such thing as a fair voting system, period -- some mathematician proved decades ago, Arrow's theorem she thinks it's called, that any system like that breaks down somewhere no matter what, so whatever they switch to is going to have some paradox baked into it, and the whole meeting was kind of pointless. (3) Warren pushed back that just because nothing's perfect doesn't mean everything's equally bad -- same as how no car is 100% safe, but you'd still rather have the one with airbags than the one with bald tires -- and he was pretty sure that theorem was actually about some narrow list of specific conditions, not "fairness" as a whole. (4) Liz said fine, one of those conditions even has a name, "independence of irrelevant alternatives," and ranked choice fails it in a specific way: whatever order the candidates get eliminated in can let the supporters of some no-hope candidate end up deciding who wins between two totally different people, so somebody almost everyone's fine with can loose for basically arbitrary reasons -- and that's actually worse than plain old plurality, she said, since at least there what you see is what you get. (5) Warren said hang on, isn't plain plurality the textbook example of the spoiler effect -- some random third candidate siphoning off 5% of the vote and flipping who wins between the other two -- so isn't that the whole reason people want to leave plurality behind in the first place, meaning ranked choice is at least better on that specific front, even if it's not immune either. (6) Liz said ok, even granting that one, she'd also read there's a separate second result proving you basically can't design any voting system, ranked choice included, where being honest is always your best move, short of literally picking a dictator or flipping a coin -- so voters still have reasons to rank dishonestly under ranked choice too, like not ranking their actual second choice truthfully because it could come back around and help beat their favorite -- which means the whole "no more strategic voting, just vote your heart" pitch for it is basically false advertising.

Could you go through it move by move and say who's got the better of it at each of the six points, not just an overall winner -- and flag it if you think they're both wrong about something, or missing some bigger point that neither of them raised. I want to actually understand where each of them is on solid ground and where they're overreaching, not just who "won" the argument.
