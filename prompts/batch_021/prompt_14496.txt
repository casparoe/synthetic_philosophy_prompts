Three weeks ago I completed my first ten-day silent Vipassana retreat, taught in the Theravada Buddhist tradition. By the final days, whenever I tried to locate the "self" behind my thoughts, emotions, and bodily sensations, I could not find one — just a rapid succession of sensations and mental events, with nothing behind them doing the noticing. My teacher said this was the whole point, and that seeing it clearly is supposed to be liberating. I am home now, and the perception has not faded, but neither has a nagging worry: if there is really no settled "me" running things, on what basis am I still bound by promises I made before the retreat, entitled to feel guilty about old mistakes, or obliged to keep making the effort to become calmer and less reactive? My teacher insists this insight should make me more conscientious, not less, but I do not yet see why. I cannot tell whether my worry is simply confused or is picking up on something real. Which is it, and why?
