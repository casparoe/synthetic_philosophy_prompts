Ok so this hypothetical came up during a design review last week and I can't stop thinking about it. Say a job runner flips a fair coin once, and nobody -- including the workers themselves -- ever learns the result. On heads it spins up exactly one worker to run a job. On tails it spins up two workers in parallel to redundantly run that same job. Every worker boots from the exact same image and starting state, has no shared memory with any other worker, and has no way of knowing if its the only one or one of a pair. The very first thing any worker does on boot, before processing a single event, is log its own best estimate of the probability that the coin landed heads. One coworker insists every worker should log 0.5, since nothing's happened yet to move it off the prior. I think it should be 1/3, because if you ran this a thousand times, two out of every three workers ever spawned would be ones spawned on a tails flip. We argued about it for half an hour on Slack and neither of us budged -- who's actually right, and where does the other persons reasoning go wrong?
