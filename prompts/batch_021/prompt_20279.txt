For my grandfather's wedding toast, I need help combining a family joke with some real philosophy. He spent thirty-six years as our local TV meteorologist, and the family joke is that he's never given a straight answer: ask him whether it'll rain Saturday and he hands you a percentage, not a promise. He's marrying his partner of eleven years this summer, and since I'm the grandchild who always pestered him about "the numbers," he's asked me to give the toast. Half the room are old colleagues from the weather desk; the other half are family who've teased him for decades about never committing to anything. For my IB Philosophy extended essay, I've been reading David Lewis on the Principal Principle, and I'd like the toast to put it to real use: explain why setting his confidence equal to the objective chance, rather than to Aunt Carol's aching knee, was never evasion but honesty, then build one real joke around the fact that today, for once, he's saying something with no hedge attached. Please write the toast: about five minutes, working for both halves of the room, warm rather than lecture-y, and careful not to make his forecasting sound wishy-washy or his vows sound like just another calibrated estimate.
