I'm a philosophy PhD student working on natural theology, and I'd like feedback on an argument I'm drafting for the modal-collapse section of a paper on the Leibnizian cosmological argument.

By way of background: the argument from contingency infers, from the existence of what's sometimes called the Big Conjunctive Contingent Fact (BCCF) — the conjunction of all contingent truths — to a necessary being whose causal activity explains it, on the grounds that any contingent explainer could simply be folded back into the BCCF and would leave the whole still unexplained. The modal collapse objection replies that if the Principle of Sufficient Reason (PSR) also demands an explanation of why the necessary being produced this contingent effect rather than some other one (or none), and that explanation bottoms out in the being's necessary nature, then the effect is necessitated by something necessary and is therefore itself necessary — collapsing the very contingent/necessary distinction the argument needs in order to get off the ground.

A standard reply treats this as resting on a modal-scope confusion, reinforced by an appeal to libertarian agency: from "necessarily, if the necessary being wills p, then p obtains" it doesn't follow that "if the being wills p, then necessarily p"; the willing itself is explained, satisfying PSR, by an undetermined act of agent-causation rather than by anything that necessitates one output over another, so the wide-scope necessity never infects the effect.

Here is my attempted rejoinder, which is what I'd most like pressure-tested. I want to grant the scope point but argue it doesn't save the theist, by turning it into a dilemma about what "sufficient" is doing in PSR. Either (a) a sufficient reason for a contingent fact must necessitate that fact given the reason, or (b) it need not, because reasons for free choices can be sufficient in some looser, rationalizing sense. If (a), agent-causal explanation collapses into ordinary necessitating explanation and the modal collapse is back on. If (b), the theist has bought back libertarian freedom for God at the cost of a reading of PSR too weak to have motivated the argument's own opening move — since on that reading, the BCCF could presumably have had its own loose, non-necessitating explanation, without needing to bottom out in anything necessary at all.

I'd appreciate comments on three things in particular: first, whether this dilemma is genuinely exhaustive, or whether there's a principled way of restricting "necessitating" reasons to non-agential contingent facts and "rationalizing" reasons to free choices, so that PSR could be uniformly satisfiable without either horn going through; second, whether I'm being fair to the scope-distinction defense as it's usually intended, or quietly attacking a weaker version of it; and third, whether my last step — moving from the availability of non-necessitating explanations for God's choices to the availability of one for the BCCF as a whole — trades on an equivocation between explaining a particular choice and explaining an unrestricted conjunction of all contingent truths.
