My sister is finishing a PhD in philosophy of mind on the causal exclusion problem: Jaegwon Kim's argument that if mental properties are merely realized by physical properties rather than identical to them, and every physical effect already has a sufficient physical cause, then mental properties look causally redundant, screened off by their physical bases. The standard escape from this -- saying that mental and physical causes systematically overdetermine every action -- is one she's always dismissed as too cheap. She's a committed non-reductive physicalist: she won't reduce her fiancé's decision to propose to "just" a brain state, but she won't reach for dualism to save its causal power either. She's getting married next month and has asked me, her non-philosopher brother, to give the toast; about a third of the guests are from her department and will notice if I fudge the argument, while everyone else just wants to hear that two people who love each other are building a life together. Write the toast: five minutes long, built around a single way of blocking the exclusion argument rather than a survey of every option on the market, warm enough for a wedding rather than a colloquium, precise enough that no philosopher in the room could catch me equivocating, and committed by the end to a real position on whether her decision to marry him does anything in the world over and above her neurons.
