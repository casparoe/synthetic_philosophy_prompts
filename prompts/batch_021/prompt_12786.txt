In "Truth and Politics" and "Lying in Politics," Hannah Arendt distinguishes the ordinary political lie, which conceals some particular fact while leaving the general framework of factual truth intact, from a distinctively modern kind of falsehood: large-scale image-making that tries to remake reality wholesale and to erase the very distinction between fact and opinion. In "On Bullshit," Harry Frankfurt distinguishes lying, which presupposes and in that sense still respects the concept of truth, from bullshit, which is produced without any concern for whether what is said is true. Compare these two distinctions. Does Arendt's modern political lie correspond more closely to Frankfurt's bullshit than to his notion of lying, and can Frankfurt's account of why bullshit is more corrosive of truth than lying help explain Arendt's claim that large-scale image-making poses a greater danger to our shared world than the ordinary political lie? Justify your answer with reference to both texts.
