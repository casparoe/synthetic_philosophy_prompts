A friend of mine and I spent most of Sunday afternoon arguing about accuracy-first epistemology, and neither of us came away persuaded. She is close to finishing a dissertation chapter defending Jim Joyce's non-pragmatic vindication of probabilism, and I kept pressing her on what I think is the argument's soft underbelly: the accuracy-dominance result only goes through once you've already restricted attention to "legitimate" scoring rules, so the vindication seems to smuggle in, through the choice of measure, exactly the structure it was supposed to derive from first principles. She countered, reasonably, that moving from one arbitrary measure to the whole class of strictly proper scoring rules was supposed to defuse exactly that worry. We then got sidetracked into immodesty -- the fact that, on this framework, a coherent credence function always rates itself as uniquely best by its own lights -- which I think is suspiciously convenient and she thinks is precisely the kind of self-support a good epistemic norm should have. Two pots of coffee later, we'd settled nothing, except a shared unease that the Brier score and the log score are both perfectly proper and yet treat confident falsehoods so differently that it's strange the framework doesn't seem to mind.

I'd like to send her a peace offering. Please write a short joke about accuracy-first epistemology and epistemic utility theory that I could plausibly text her tonight. It needs to actually turn on one of the theory's substantive commitments -- the defense of a particular scoring rule, the accuracy-dominance argument for probabilism, immodesty and self-recommendation, or the way the Brier and log scores diverge on confident error -- rather than being a generic joke about Bayesians or pedantic philosophers dressed up in borrowed vocabulary. It should be short enough to fit in a text message, and it should be the kind of joke that only really lands for someone who has sat with Joyce's or Pettigrew's arguments -- funny enough that she laughs before she remembers she's supposed to still be annoyed with me.
