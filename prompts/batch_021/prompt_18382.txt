I've been apartment hunting for the past few weeks and it somehow turned into this whole argument with my partner that's stressing me out more than the apartment search itself, especially since we have to give the leasing office an answer by Friday. There are three places left in the running -- I've just been calling them the Loft, the Brownstone, and the Studio. Whenever I compare any two of them head to head I get a totally clear answer: I'd take the Loft over the Brownstone because the commute is way shorter and that matters more to me then the extra room. I'd take the Brownstone over the Studio because for a little more rent it's way bigger and actually has windows. And I'd take the Studio over the Loft because its $300 cheaper a month, which is worth the longer commute. Each of those comparisons feels completely solid on its own, but they don't add up to one ranking -- Loft beats Brownstone, Brownstone beats Studio, Studio beats Loft, and round it goes.

I brought this up at dinner as a "decisions are hard, lol" kind of comment, and my partner, who spent a couple years as an options trader before going back to school, did not think it was funny at all. She said what I'd just described means I don't actually have consistent preferences among the three, and that if I were even a little willing to pay a small fee to switch to whichever place I currently liked best, someone could walk me around that loop charging me every single time, forever, since there's always a "better" option one step away. She's pretty into AI safety stuff lately, and threw in that this is supposedly the same basic argument people use to claim any sufficiently smart AI will end up acting like it's maximizing one fixed goal whether it was built to or not, which honestly just made me more confused, not less.

I said the whole thing felt like a cheap shot anyway -- nobody is actually going to charge me a switching fee, I'm signing exactly one lease and then this is over, so a story about endless trading that will never happen in real life shouldn't tell me anything about whether my actual, one-time choice is fine. She said the fee and the repetition are just there to make something vivid that's already a problem the moment the loop exists, exploited or not. She's been half-jokingly calling me irrational ever since, and I honestly can't tell if she's onto something real or just overselling an argument that doesn't actually apply to someone making a single decision the way I am. Who's actually right here?
