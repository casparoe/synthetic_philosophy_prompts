For as long as I can remember, I've prided myself on being disciplined - waking up early, pushing through a difficult degree, building a business from nothing while others walked away. Friends and family often praise me for how hard I've worked, and I used to accept that praise easily. Lately, though, it has started to feel unearned: when I trace where my drive and habits actually come from, it's my temperament, how my parents raised me, and a string of early lucky breaks - and even my later efforts to become more disciplined seem to be downstream of that same starting point rather than something separate I can take credit for. I mentioned this to a close friend, who told me not to worry: the brain isn't a fully predictable machine, there's genuine randomness in how it fires, so my choices were never simply forced by my genes and upbringing, and that alone is enough for me to genuinely deserve the credit I get. Her answer doesn't quite satisfy me, though I can't pin down why. Is she right that this bit of randomness settles the matter, or was my original unease pointing at something real?
