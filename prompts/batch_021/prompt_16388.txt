Half the committee says the interbreeding data settles it: they're not reproductively isolated, so not a real species, delist them. The other half says that's the wrong test entirely, that what matters is whether they're a diagnosably distinct lineage regardless of the old gene flow, and wants to either keep the listing or protect them as a distinct population instead. I have to draft the short recommendation memo we vote on Tuesday. Can you write it -- pick delist, reclassify, or keep, and defend it, but actually engage with the fact that the two sides aren't just reading the same data differently, they're working from different definitions of what a species even is.
