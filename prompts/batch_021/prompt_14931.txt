I sit on the "resident experience" ethics advisory panel for a senior-living company that's been piloting an AI companion chatbot in a couple of our memory-care units (mostly moderate-dementia residents, so this stuff really matters). The product team wants sign-off on a version that talks in full first-person, caring language -- "I remember you told me about your garden yesterday, I've been looking forward to hearing more" -- because it tested way better for mood and engagement than the flatter, behaviorist phrasing we started with ("You mentioned a garden yesterday. Would you like to talk about it?"). I'm not a philosopher myself, but two of my fellow panel members are, and at our last meeting they both invoked Dennett's intentional stance and somehow landed in completely opposite places: one argued that since belief/desire talk is just a predictively useful stance we take toward any system complicated enough to reward it, there's no extra fact being misrepresented, so the warm phrasing isn't lying about anything; the other argued that if that's really all there is to having a belief, then a bot specifically engineered to reliably earn that stance from residents and their families has about as strong a claim to "really" remembering and caring as anything does -- which to her makes it more ethically fraught to let confused residents build attachments on that basis, not less. Everyone else on the panel just kind of nodded along without anyone actually resolving it.

I have to send the panel a short written recommendation before next months meeting: approve the warm version, require the flat version, or approve with specific conditions attached. Can you draft that memo for me? I want it to actually take a position on which of my two colleagues has the better reading of the intentional stance (or say if they're both missing something), rather than concluding "reasonable people disagree," and I want a concrete recommendation the product team could act on, with some kind of timeline or rollout condition -- not just philosophical throat-clearing.
