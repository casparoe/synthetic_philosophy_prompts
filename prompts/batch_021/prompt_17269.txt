My grandma was diagnosed with early Alzheimer's a few months ago and now basically runs her life through this little notebook she keeps in her purse -- appointments, names, notes on conversations from that morning, so she doesn't loose track. Reading around about it, I stumbled on this philosophy idea called the extended mind thesis, which apparently says something like her notebook can count as literally part of her mind/memory, not just a tool for it. That got me thinking about Locke, since his whole theory of personal identity is built on memory and continuity of consciousness rather than a fixed soul or body. So if my grandma's memory is functionally shifting into this notebook, what would Locke actually say is happening to her identity -- does his own view commit him to saying the notebook's become part of "her," or is there a way to read him that avoids that?
