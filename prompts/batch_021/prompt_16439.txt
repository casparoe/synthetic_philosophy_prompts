I've been part of a small ACX-adjacent reading group for a couple of years, and last month we worked through Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument together. Afterward, two regulars, Marcus and Zoe, got into an extended disagreement over coffee about a test case one of them had raised: Alicia, who resigned the moment she became convinced — correctly, as it turned out — that her company was about to collapse. I was just listening, and I'd like an outside adjudication.

(1) Marcus: The physical state of Alicia's brain at the moment of decision was already causally sufficient, given the causal closure of the physical domain, to produce the motor signals that typed her resignation email. Her belief is a distinct, higher-level property — multiply realizable, since the same belief could have been carried by very different wiring in someone else — and we shouldn't accept that her resignation was overdetermined by two independent sufficient causes every single time someone acts on a belief. So the belief itself, as a mental property, did none of the causing; it's screened off by its physical basis, and belief-talk is just a redescription after the fact.

(2) Zoe: Too quick. The belief was never a separate thing that could have gone off and produced the resignation on its own, apart from those neurons doing what they did — it's not two causes converging by coincidence. The neural process is what the belief consisted in on that occasion, so there's nothing left to overdetermine and nothing for the physical to exclude: just one cause, describable at two levels of grain.

(3) Marcus: You don't get to have it both ways. If the belief just is the neural event, you've reinvented identity theory, and now you owe me an answer to multiple realizability: this same belief could have been carried by a completely different neural (or silicon) arrangement in someone else, so it can't be identical to this particular neural property. Deny the identity and we're back to two distinct properties, and my exclusion argument goes through again.

(4) Zoe: It doesn't take full identity to block exclusion. All I need is that whatever causal powers the belief carries on this occasion are included among the powers of its physical realizer on that occasion — not that the belief-type is identical across every possible realizer. Different realizers can share an overlapping causal profile without the higher-level property being identical to any one of them. So multiple realizability and 'no competition' were never actually in tension; you're assuming a fight that doesn't need to happen.
