But Theo hit me with this thing called the "inverse gambler's fallacy" — apparently there's a whole literature (Ian Hacking, and some guy John Leslie with a firing-squad analogy) arguing that reasoning from "our universe happens to be fine-tuned" to "therefore there's probably a ton of other universes out there" is structurally the exact same mistake as a guy walking into a casino, seeing someone roll double sixes, and concluding there must've been loads of earlier rolls somewhere in the building that night. I tried the obvious anthropic comeback — we could only ever observe a universe compatible with our own existence in the first place, so the sample is massively biased in a way the dice case isn't — but Theo kept insisting the selection effect doesn't actually rescue the inference the way I think it does.
