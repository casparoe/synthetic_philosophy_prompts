As someone who writes about science and ideas for a living, I've spent the past year fascinated, and occasionally frustrated, by what's happened to a friend from graduate school — someone who used to work in software and is, by any normal measure, sharp and well-read. He's become convinced that an advanced civilization existed before the last Ice Age, and that mainstream archaeology has spent the better part of a century suppressing the evidence for institutional reasons. What makes this hard to write about, or even to raise with him over dinner, is that he isn't ignorant of the counter-evidence: he can quote radiocarbon dates and stratigraphy back at me just as readily as I can cite them to him. He's simply decided that peer review, standard dating methods, and academic consensus are the wrong kind of evidence to trust on this particular question, while satellite imagery, independent stone-tool analysis, and his own intuitive sense of what looks "too sophisticated" for the period are the right kind. When I press him on why he draws the line where he does, he doesn't really have a further reason to offer; it functions for him as a starting point, not a conclusion he argued his way toward.

I keep wanting to describe this to my editor as a case of confirmation bias, or as someone who has simply fallen into an epistemic bubble, but the more I sit with it, the less either framing seems to fit. "Confirmation bias" implies that we are drawing on the same stock of evidence and that one of us is simply reasoning about it worse, and that doesn't seem quite right here: the disagreement seems to sit beneath the evidence, in what is even allowed to count as evidence in the first place — and I notice that I take plenty of things for granted in just the same unargued way he does, only different things. I can't decide whether that makes a disagreement like this more hopeless than an ordinary factual dispute, or whether it simply calls for an entirely different kind of response. What's actually going on in an exchange like this? And once a disagreement runs this deep, is there any principled way to make progress at all?
