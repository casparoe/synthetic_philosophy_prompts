I've served for several years on the awards committee of our regional volunteer search-and-rescue unit, and a recent case has split us in a way our bylaws don't settle. We give a standard "Meritorious Service" award to volunteers who do their assigned job well, and a rare "Above and Beyond" award for conduct that goes past what any volunteer could be asked to do. During a night search, the weather worsened and the coordinator recalled the whole team; one volunteer kept searching alone, off her assigned grid and out of radio contact, and found a hypothermic hiker who likely would not have survived until morning. Half the committee wants to give her the top award, since nothing in her role required her to keep going once she was recalled. The other half says that continuing after a recall is a safety violation, not a case of going beyond duty, and that honoring it — in the award itself and in how we train new volunteers — teaches people that ignoring a recall is what real commitment looks like. I need to bring a short written recommendation to next week's meeting. Please draft it: recommend which award she should receive, or a better resolution, and engage the safety-violation objection seriously rather than dismissing it as a technicality.
