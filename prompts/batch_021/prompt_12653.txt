My grandmother is eighty-six, has lived near family outside Durban her whole life, and after a bad fall last month announced that she wants to move into a small independent-living flat of her own rather than into any of her children's houses. It has opened a real rift between my aunt Thandiwe, who wrote her master's thesis on Ubuntu ethics, and my uncle Bongani, and for two weeks I've been relaying their argument back and forth without being able to settle it myself.

(1) Thandiwe says granting Gogo's wish would be an odd way to honor her: on the processual view of personhood that thinkers like Menkiti defend, nobody is fully a person just by being born human -- personhood is achieved and sustained through embeddedness in family and community, so removing her from that fabric diminishes the very thing that makes her a person, whatever she currently wants.

(2) Bongani answers that this proves too much: even Gyekye, who took the communal picture every bit as seriously as Menkiti, pushed back against exactly this, because a view that gives a person's own will no independent standing can't distinguish care from control -- Gogo's stated wish has to count for something the family can't simply overrule by appeal to what's good for her.

(3) Thandiwe replies that Bongani is smuggling in a liberal premise the framework was meant to reject: the capacity to want things for oneself is itself relationally constituted, not a private faculty that predates community, so it can't function as a trump card against the community that formed it.

(4) Bongani says that if so, the theory can never criticize even a smothering family, since any unwelcome pressure can always be redescribed as the very relationship that made the person's will possible in the first place -- and a theory that can never call that out has paid too high a price.

Neither will budge, and I don't know this literature well enough to referee it myself. Could you work through moves (1) through (4) and say who has the better of each one, and then say what, if anything, both of them are getting wrong about what Ubuntu actually implies for a case like Gogo's?
