Okay so this went from a dinner-party debate to extremely personal in about a month, which feels very on brand for my year so far. I'm on the benefits committee at my company (mid-size, self-funded plan, so we actually set our own coverage rules instead of just inheriting whatever the state mandates), and last year we finally agreed to cover GLP-1s like Zepbound and Wegovy for weight loss after saying no for two plan years running. The rule we landed on was BMI over 30 AND a qualifying comorbidity -- diabetes, hypertension, sleep apnea, that kind of thing -- to get approved. Then last month I got approved myself on an individual appeal even though my BMI is only about 31 and I don't have any of those comorbidities, and it's been bugging me since, because apparently our own committee doesn't agree on what we're actually treating. Is a high BMI by itself already the disease, so anyone over the line should qualify no matter what else is or isn't going on with them? Or is obesity more like a risk factor, and the real disease is the diabetes or hypertension it might eventually cause, in which case my approval was basically a mistake and nobody should get the drug until something's already gone wrong?

We've got a meeting in two weeks to set the criteria for next plan year, and since I wont shut up about this I got voluteered to draft the recommendation. Half the committee just wants to leave the current rule alone because changing it is a hassle, and our finance guy keeps saying we should just go with whatever's cheapest and stop overthinking it. Could you write that memo for me? I need something I can actually hand to finance and HR people, not a philosophy essay -- so it should land on ONE concrete eligibility policy for next year (keep it as is, drop the comorbidity requirement, something in between, whatever you think is most defensible), explain the reasoning in plain terms, and actually engage with the strongest version of whatever view it ends up rejecting, rather than just noting that BMI is an imperfect measure and calling it a day. Everyone on the committee already knows that part, and it's not really what's driving the disagreement.
