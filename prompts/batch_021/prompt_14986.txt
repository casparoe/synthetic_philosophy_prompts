Einstein's hole argument keeps striking me as a physics-flavored cousin of debates over haecceitism in modal metaphysics — whether two qualitatively identical possible worlds that differ only in "which individual is which" are really distinct, or whether that's a distinction without a difference. I'd like to see the dispute played out as a dialogue rather than summarized.

Write a dialogue between a manifold substantivalist and a relationalist about spacetime, staged right after both have worked through the standard hole construction: a diffeomorphism that acts as the identity outside some open region H but non-trivially within it, producing a second model that agrees with the first everywhere except inside H. Have the substantivalist reach for the move often called "sophisticated substantivalism" — denying that the two diffeomorphic models represent distinct possibilities in the first place — and have the relationalist press the charge that this quietly concedes everything relationalism ever wanted: if the models aren't physically distinct, the points' identity over and above their metrical role was never doing any work, and the remaining disagreement is merely verbal. The substantivalist shouldn't just fold, though: have them turn the question back on the relationalist by asking how a purely relational ontology is supposed to account for the metric field's status as a genuine dynamical, gauge-theoretic entity rather than a mere bookkeeping device for distances. Have both sides steelman each other rather than talk past one another, and let the dialogue end wherever the disagreement genuinely bottoms out rather than at a forced resolution.
