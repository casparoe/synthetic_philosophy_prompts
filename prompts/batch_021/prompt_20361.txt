Spent 35 years as an evolutionary biologist before retiring, and "function" was a word we threw around constantly without ever asking what we meant by it. Turns out philosophers have carved this up properly: etiological accounts (Wright, Neander) say a trait's function is whatever effect it was selected for, while causal-role accounts (Cummins) say the function is just whatever the trait currently contributes to some capacity of the system, history be damned. Everyones go-to illustration is the heart-valve-sounds example and honestly I'm bored of it. Give me a different, real biological example where the two accounts would acutally hand back different verdicts about a trait's function, explain why they diverge, and tell me which verdict sounds more like how biologists actually talk shop in the field.
