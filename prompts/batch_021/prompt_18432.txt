For my seminar on formal epistemology, I've been working through the literature on higher-order evidence and can't quite reconstruct one move in the debate. Christensen's well-known case—where you're midway through a piece of reasoning and learn you may have taken a drug that impairs logical judgment in a fraction of people—is supposed to show that rational updating on higher-order evidence can require lowering your confidence in P even though your first-order evidence for P hasn't changed. But that seems to force exactly the belief state the Enkratic Principle rules out: believing P while believing you're not rational in believing P. Lasonen-Aarnio's level-splitting view apparently accepts this cost rather than denying that higher-order evidence bears on first-order belief at all. What I haven't been able to reconstruct is the strongest objection to that view: roughly, that level-splitting insulates an agent's first-order beliefs from higher-order evidence in a way that makes gathering such evidence pointless. Could you spell out that objection more carefully than I have here, and say whether level-splitters have a good reply to it?
