so for my virtue ethics seminar we're doing a unit on franklin and i'm stuck on how two bits of the "arriving at moral perfection" chapter in his autobiography actually relate to each other.

first, after he adds Humility as his 13th virtue (a quaker friend had told him he came across as proud), he writes: "In reality, there is, perhaps, no one of our natural passions so hard to subdue as pride. Disguise it, struggle with it, beat it down, stifle it, mortify it as much as one pleases, it is still alive, and will every now and then peep out and show itself; you will see it, perhaps, often in this history; for, even if I could conceive that I had compleatly overcome it, I should probably be proud of my humility."

seperately, when he's explaining why he could never keep the virtue of Order, he tells a little parable about a guy who brings an axe to a smith to get the whole face ground bright, gets tired of turning the wheel, and settles for a dull spotty blade, telling the smith "I think I like a speckled ax best." Franklin adds that this is basically what happens to anyone trying to fix their habits: "something, that pretended to be reason, was every now and then suggesting to me that such extreme nicety as I exacted of myself might be a kind of foppery in morals... and that a benevolent man should allow a few faults in himself, to keep his friends in countenance."

are these actually the same self-undermining move showing up twice, or two genuinely different objections to the whole project? like, is the pride passage making a specific structural point -- that pride can't be eliminated because even successfully eliminating it becomes a new occasion for pride -- while the axe bit is a much more mundane point about willpower running out and people rationalizing it after the fact? or is there some deeper link between "i'd be proud of my humility" and "i guess i like a speckled axe" that i'm not seeing? also curious whether franklin himself seems aware he's raising two different problems here, or if he's just piling up charming anecdotes. not looking for a rundown of the 13 virtues, i've got that part already.
