Okay this is a weirdly specific thing to be stuck on, but I work in AI governance and at a seminar last week someone brought up "commitment races" as a reason letting AI agents negotiate on behalf of companies or governments could go really badly, and its been bugging me since bc I don't think I actually get the mechanism. The part I get: if you can credibly lock into a bargaining position before the other side does, you get leverage - classic Schelling, burn-the-bridges stuff. What I don't get is why giving agents BETTER commitment tech (more transparency, more verifiable precommitment, more time to actually reason) is supposed to make things more dangerous instead of less - naively, smarter and more transparent agents should just bargain their way to an efficient outcome instead of racing to lock in something dumb early. And if the race itself is the problem, why can't they just commit to a meta-rule like "don't commit rashly, wait and see what the other side does first" and dissolve the whole race that way? Can you spell out why this is supposed to be a genuinely hard trap, and not just something that gets bargained away by any agent smart enough to see it coming?
