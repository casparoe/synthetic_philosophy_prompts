Ok so I went down a rabbit hole this week after a friend linked me to Ian Hacking's "looping effects" stuff in the middle of an argument about whether autism/ADHD diagnoses are exploding becuase more kids "really" have it or because the categories themselves are shifting under our feet. The basic slogan -- classifying people changes them, which changes the classification, round and round -- makes sense as a slogan, but every time I try to state the actual mechanism precisely it kind of dissolves in my hands, and I don't think I could currently pass an ideological Turing test for someone who's actually read Hacking carefully, as opposed to absorbed the concept through three layers of blog-summary.

Two specific things are bugging me. First: Hacking makes a big deal out of "interactive kinds" (human kinds, like multiple personality or "the obese") vs "indifferent kinds" (natural kinds, like quarks), and the mechanism seems to hinge on people being *aware* of how they're classified and reacting to it -- I recall him leaning on Anscombe's stuff about intentional action "under a description" to make this work. But the go-to example everyone cites, autism, doesn't obviously fit that mold, since a lot of the actual looping there seems to run through parents/teachers/clinicians changing their behavior around a kid who might have zero awareness he's been classified at all. So is that still "the looping effect" in Hacking's proper sense, or does it quietly smuggle in a totally different, non-awareness-based mechanism, in which case isn't "the looping effect" secretly two different things wearing one name? Second, how is any of this actually distinct from bog-standard labeling theory (Becker, Lemert, Goffman) out of 60s/70s sociology, which already had "being labeled deviant changes your behavior, which can create secondary deviance"? I feel like Hacking is adding *something* on top of that with the philosophy-of-science gloss, but I can't articulate what it is beyond vibes. Would love an explanation that actually gets into the gears of the thing rather than just restating "labels change people who change labels" a third time in different words.
