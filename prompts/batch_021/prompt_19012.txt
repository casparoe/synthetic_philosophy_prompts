The protagonist of the novel I'm currently writing has to decide whether to undergo an experience she knows will fundamentally rearrange what she values, without any way of glimpsing that reshaped perspective from where she currently stands. While researching the philosophical shape of this dilemma, I noticed that L.A. Paul's account of transformative experience and Kierkegaard's discussion of the leap of faith in Fear and Trembling—particularly the "teleological suspension of the ethical" that licenses Abraham's leap—seem to circle the same impasse using strikingly different vocabularies. Could you compare the two directly? In particular, does Paul's attempt to preserve some form of rational agency across the transformation, by grounding the choice in what one currently values about self-discovery, actually succeed on its own terms, or does it quietly rely on something like the non-rational commitment that Kierkegaard makes explicit and even celebrates?
