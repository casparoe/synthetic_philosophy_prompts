I'm working on a piece about the philosophical roots of AI alignment ideas, and I've been down a rabbit hole reading old Yudkowsky essays on coherent extrapolated volition (CEV) -- the idea that a friendly superintelligence should act on what humanity would want if we knew more, thought faster, and were more the people we wish we were, extrapolated out to wherever those wishes converge. Struck me that this sounds a lot like the "idealized preference" or "informed desire" theories of well-being that philosphers like Peter Railton proposed decades earlier, where your good is defined by what a fully-informed, maximally vivid, idealized version of you would want for your actual, non-idealized self. Can you compare CEV to Railton-style idealized preference theories? Where do the two actually line up, and where does CEV's move from one person's idealized preferences to a *coherent* extrapolation across all of humanity create headaches that Railton's framework never had to deal with?
