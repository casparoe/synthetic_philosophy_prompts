I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology working on anthropic reasoning, and I'd like a dialogue that goes beyond the standard rehearsal of the Doomsday Argument and the Presumptuous Philosopher.

Please write a dialogue between two equally sophisticated philosophers, one committed to the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) and one committed to the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA), confronting the following case. Stipulate that all available physical and sociological evidence is exactly evenly balanced between two hypotheses about the future of Earth-originating intelligence:

- Hypothesis A: civilization remains broadly biological and human-scale, and goes extinct within a few centuries, for a total of roughly 200 billion observer-moments across all of history.
- Hypothesis B: within that same window, civilization transitions to large-scale digital minds, many of them run as multiple simultaneous copies of a single underlying program, eventually reaching something on the order of 200 quadrillion observer-moments.

Each philosopher should state and defend a credence in Hypothesis A vs. Hypothesis B, conditional only on the self-locating fact that one is an early, biological, uncopied observer, and each should engage directly with the other's actual argument rather than simply restating their own position. In particular, have them work out together whether the shift from ordinary biological humans to copyable digital minds forces any revision in how the reference class (for SSA) or the space of "possible observers" (for SIA) ought to be individuated, and whether this move helps either side blunt the standard objection to their own view (Doomsday for SSA, presumptuousness for SIA). Close the dialogue with the two of them isolating, as precisely as they can, the specific premise on which their disagreement ultimately turns.
