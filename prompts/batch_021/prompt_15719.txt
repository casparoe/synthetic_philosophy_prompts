At my company, we run online experiments before shipping product changes, and the usual practice on my team is to watch the p-value update live on a dashboard and stop a test the moment it crosses 0.05, rather than fixing the sample size in advance. I raised this in our experiment review, since I had read that practices like this are part of why so many published research findings fail to replicate, and our data scientist agreed that it undermines the usual guarantees behind significance testing. They then argued, however, that if we instead treated the same data as evidence and updated our belief that 'the new version is better' using Bayes' rule, exactly when we chose to stop wouldn't matter at all, since the data says what it says regardless of our intentions. I find this puzzling: how can the identical numbers be considered tainted under one way of analyzing them, yet perfectly trustworthy under another, purely because of why we stopped collecting them? Is our data scientist mistaken, or is there a genuine principle behind their argument that would settle the disagreement?
