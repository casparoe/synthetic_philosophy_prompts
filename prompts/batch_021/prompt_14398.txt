The list to sort:
- Universality: the same circuit motifs (curve detectors, induction heads, etc) show up again and again in independantly-trained nets of different sizes and architectures.
- Superposition/polysemanticity: individual neurons routinely fire for several unrelated concepts at once, with features stored as overlapping directions rather than one neuron per concept.
- Sparse autoencoders/dictionary learning recovering apparently monosemantic, human-legible features out of that superposed mess.
- "Feature splitting": the features dictionary learning finds change depending on the size of the dictionary, i.e. the same activations decompose differently at different resolutions.
- Linear probes and steering vectors (for things like sentiment, truthfulness, or Othello-GPT's board state) that causally move behavior in the predicted direction when you add or subtract them.
- Seed-sensitivity: two nets trained on identical data, differing only in random seed, ending up implementing "the same" function via visibly different internal circuitry.
- Cross-model and even cross-modality convergence of representational geometry as models and data scale up (the "platonic representation"-style results).
- Adversarial examples, where an imperceptible input tweak flips the classification, suggesting the model's category boundary doesn't track the human concept it's supposedly representing.
- Grokking case studies where a small model, after way more training than it needed to fit the data, becomes fully reverse-engineerable into a clean algorithm.
- "Interpretability illusions": pruning/ablating a model down to something legible changes its behavior on new inputs, so the tidy story you get back doesn't actually describe the original network.

If you think the list is missing something important feel free to add a line about it at the end, but the main ask is the tier list.
