So I had this weird argument with my sister that's been stuck in my head. She's about to go do this two-week "transformation" retreat thing, kind of a mix of therapy and spiritual bootcamp run by a guy with a pretty devoted online following. She showed me testimonials and the pattern is oddly consistent: something like 9 out of 10 people who go in doubting his main claim (that basically all our suffering comes down to one fixable misunderstanding, and he can get you to "see through" it) come out the other side saying they genuinely believe it now, not just going along with the group. She wants me to come too, and when I told her I was skeptical she hit me with "you're gonna believe it by day fourteen anyway, everybody does, so why not just save yourself the fight and believe it now instead of later."

Weirdly I didn't have a great comeback in the moment. Like, if I'm basically certain what opinion I'm going to end up holding in two weeks, isn't that already a reason to shift my current opinion toward it, same as if I somehow found out in advance what a reliable test result was going to say? But it also feels wrong to just adopt whatever belief you're sure you'll have later, no matter how you got there, and I cant quite pin down why those two situations are actually different. Was my sister onto something real there, or is there an actual flaw in that reasoning I'm not seeing?
