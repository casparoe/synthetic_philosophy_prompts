okay i've been chewing on evidential cooperation in large worlds (pretty sure that's oesterheld's old 'multiverse-wide superrationality' framing, renamed at some point) and i think i found a hole in it that nobody brings up. the pitch is that in a big enough world you should cooperate now because your decision correlates with agents you can't causally reach. but if the world's big enough for that correlation to exist, isn't every possible action already being taken by some version of me anyway -- a branch that gives everything to AMF, a branch that gives nothing, every branch in between? my choice just picks which branch im in, it doesnt change the overall distribution of outcomes. so why does anyone who takes ECL seriously act like their actual decision matters, instead of admitting the whole correlated-cooperation argument is moot since every outcome happens regardless? what am i missing?
