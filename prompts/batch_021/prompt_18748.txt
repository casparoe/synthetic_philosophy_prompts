Early in my career -- self-taught, out of a mediocre bootcamp, with no real professional network -- a senior engineer named Elena took an unusual interest in me. Over about four months she put in something like eighty unpaid hours pairing with me on real code, and, more importantly, personally vouched for me to the CTO at the fintech company where she was contracting, which is the only reason I got an interview at all. Elena was known in that small contracting circle for being unusually good at telling, early on, which unproven people were actually going to put in the work and which would flake; people said that's precisely why she could get away with betting on strangers the way she did, because she was so rarely wrong about it. When I asked how I could repay her, she told me she didn't want anything from me directly -- her only condition was that once I was established, I would do the same thing for somebody else starting out, the way an engineer had once done for her.

That was six years ago. Elena moved to Lisbon not long afterward, we lost touch completely, and there is no realistic way she would ever find out what I decide now, whichever way I go. I'm senior enough today to do for somebody else roughly what she did for me, and a talented but genuinely unproven junior developer I only know slightly, Maya, has asked me for exactly that kind of investment: months of unpaid mentoring and my personal reputational backing to land her first real role. Put plainly, saying yes costs me real time and puts my own credibility on the line if she doesn't pan out, and I can't identify any way in which it benefits me now: there is no contract, no shared circle in which declining would cost me anything, and nothing I do now can change what Elena decided about me six years ago. It's also not that I'm trying to keep some useful system running for my own sake later -- I'm fairly sure I won't need this kind of help again myself, and there's a decent chance I leave the industry entirely within a few years anyway. My partner says I'm overthinking a simple kindness, but that feels like a dodge, since by identical logic I could just as well call it needless self-sacrifice and leave it at that. I keep feeling that I owe something here, but when I try to state an actual forward-looking reason for that feeling -- something other than lingering gratitude or guilt -- I can't produce one. Is there a genuine rational case for following through, or would I just be dressing up sentiment as an obligation?
