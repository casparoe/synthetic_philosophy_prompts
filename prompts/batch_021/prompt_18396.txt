I'm one of three members on my condo association's board, and a recent decision has been bothering me. A resident asked us to force a neighbor to remove a fence, and under our bylaws we may only order removal if two separate conditions both hold: the fence exceeds the height limit, and the excess height can be shown to lower a neighboring unit's property value.

We voted on the two conditions one at a time. Two of us agreed the height limit was violated, and a different two of us agreed the fence was hurting property value, so both conditions passed, and by the letter of our bylaws we were obligated to order removal. Then, as a check, each of us also gave a direct yes-or-no verdict on removal itself, and that vote came out two-to-one against removal.

Nobody changed position between the two rounds, and no one contradicted themselves individually. One member, for instance, thought the fence was too tall but not damaging to property value, and voted no on removal accordingly - a perfectly coherent position on its own terms. Yet the board as a whole ends up committed to opposite outcomes depending on which vote we treat as the one that counts.

Something about this feels off, even though I can't point to anyone actually reasoning badly. Which vote should actually govern here - the two separate rulings on the conditions, or everyone's direct verdict on the outcome - and is there a principled reason to prefer one over the other, or does this kind of breakdown just come with the territory of deciding things as a group rather than alone?
