A friend and I spent most of a long train journey arguing about indicative conditionals, and I don't think either of us came away satisfied. It started from something that sounds almost too obvious to state: that your credence in a sentence like "If the trial drug works, then the FDA will approve it by next year" just is your conditional credence in approval given that the drug works. My friend wanted to hold onto this equation (essentially Adams'/Stalnaker's Thesis, P(if A, B) = P(B|A)) while also insisting that "if A, B" expresses an ordinary proposition with a determinate truth value, sitting in the same Boolean algebra as A, B, and everything built out of them. I objected that this combination is exactly what Lewis's 1976 triviality result rules out, but when he pushed back and asked me to actually show him the problem rather than just cite the result, I realized I could only gesture at it. I remembered that the proof works by conditionalizing on the conditional itself and on its negation, rather than just on the antecedent and its negation, but I couldn't reconstruct why that particular move is what does the damage.

Could you help me actually understand the mechanics here, at a level I could reproduce for my friend without hiding behind the phrase "triviality result"? Specifically: (i) walk me through, in an intuitive but reasonably precise way, what goes wrong when you assume P(A>B) = P(B|A) holds not just for your actual credence function but also for the credence functions you'd have on learning A>B or on learning its negation — and why self-conditionalizing on the conditional is the crucial move rather than an incidental one (relatedly, why doesn't conditionalizing on the antecedent and its negation alone already generate the contradiction)? (ii) How minimal can the set of "admissible" probability functions be before the result bites — do you really need a whole class closed under conditionalizing on arbitrary propositions, or does a small, fixed handful of functions already force the collapse? And (iii) how do the standard escape routes actually dodge the argument rather than just asserting an alternative — e.g., does Adams's non-cognitivism work simply by denying that "if A, B" names a proposition at all, so there's nothing to plug into the algebra, whereas Stalnaker's own reply instead lets the proposition expressed shift together with the probability function, and Jackson's material-conditional-plus-pragmatics account abandons the Thesis as a claim about truth-conditional content altogether? I'd like to know which of these, if any, actually lets you keep everything my friend wanted.
