For this week's seminar on higher-order theories of consciousness, compare David Rosenthal's higher-order thought (HOT) theory with Uriah Kriegel's self-representational theory. Both hold that a mental state's being conscious consists in its being targeted by an appropriate higher-order representation, but they diverge on whether that representation must belong to a mental state numerically distinct from, and extrinsic to, the state it targets, or can instead be an intrinsic feature of the very state it renders conscious. Set out the motivations behind each position, focusing on how Kriegel's move to an intrinsic, self-representational structure is meant to improve on extrinsic theories such as Rosenthal's with respect to (a) the threat of regress and (b) the possibility of a "targetless" higher-order thought — a case in which the higher-order state misrepresents there being a first-order state that is not, in fact, present. Then render your own verdict: does self-representationalism genuinely dissolve these difficulties, or does it merely relocate them within a single, more complex mental state? Justify your answer.
