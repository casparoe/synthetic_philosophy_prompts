I'm a PhD student in Islamic intellectual history, and I organize the Q&A portion of my department's graduate colloquium. Next month's guest is a historian of Islamic philosophy who defends the kind of occasionalism about causation that al-Ghazālī develops in the Seventeenth Discussion of the Tahāfut al-Falāsifa: fire does not burn cotton, God does, and the "connection" we observe between the two is God's habitual custom ('āda), not a necessity residing in the things themselves. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view. Include at least one question on what secures the reliability of everyday expectation and inductive or experimental practice if no natural necessity underlies it, one on whether explaining that reliability through "custom" quietly reintroduces the very law-like structure occasionalism was supposed to eliminate, and one on how her position actually differs from Malebranche's occasionalism, given how differently the two are motivated. For each, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give -- for the Malebranche question in particular, the follow-up should press her with the objection Ibn Rushd raises in the Tahāfut al-Tahāfut, that denying things their own causal powers makes demonstrative knowledge of the world impossible.
