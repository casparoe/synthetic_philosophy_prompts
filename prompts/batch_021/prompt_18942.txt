A consumer DNA test I took out of curiosity revealed that the man who raised me, whom I have always called Dad, is not my biological father; there was, it turns out, a mix-up decades ago at the fertility clinic where I was conceived. My relationship with my dad hasn't changed, and I don't see why it should — that he raised me isn't in question. But I can't shake the sense that the test told me something that was already true of me long before anyone had any way of knowing it.

A friend disagrees. She thinks I'm over-reading a technicality: "father" just means whoever actually raises and loves you, so the donor is a stranger in every sense that matters, and the test changed nothing real about who I am. I think it only changed what I knew, not the facts themselves — something about me was fixed before I was even born, whatever either of us happens to feel about family. We argued about this for most of an evening without getting anywhere.

Was there really a settled fact about who I am that the test only uncovered, or is my friend right that this sort of biological fact just isn't the kind that can bear on identity? I'd like to understand what's actually at issue here, rather than just whose reaction is more reasonable.
