I've followed discussions of evidential cooperation in large worlds (ECL) on the EA Forum and LessWrong since back when Oesterheld's argument was still commonly going by "multiverse-wide superrationality," and one part of the standard case has never quite clicked for me. The usual motivation leans on evidential decision theory: choosing to act cooperatively toward the values of distant, causally disconnected agents counts as evidence that relevantly similar agents elsewhere are doing likewise, so the policy pays off in expectation across a sufficiently large or infinite population even without any causal link between the parties. Yet most people making this argument are otherwise committed updateless or functional decision theorists, precisely because they think EDT gets cases like the smoking lesion problem wrong. Could you explain whether ECL's cooperative payoff genuinely depends on EDT-style evidential reasoning, or whether an FDT/UDT agent can recover the same recommendation through the logical-correlation machinery FDT ordinarily reserves for Newcomb-like cases? And if it's the latter, what exactly plays the role that "running the same algorithm" plays in a standard Newcomb case, given that ECL is explicitly meant to cover agents with very different values -- and presumably rather different reasoning procedures too?
