As a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, I've been working through the systematicity argument for the language of thought hypothesis and the connectionist replies to it. Fodor and Pylyshyn hold that only an architecture built from discrete, context-invariant symbols combined by a compositional syntax can explain why anyone capable of thinking that John loves Mary is thereby capable of thinking that Mary loves John. Smolensky's tensor-product representations were meant to show that a connectionist network could reproduce this same compositional structure using distributed vectors rather than classical symbols. Fodor and McLaughlin argued, however, that tensor-product binding cannot actually explain systematicity, since the vector encoding a constituent such as "John" varies with the role it is bound to, leaving no context-invariant representation for a structure-sensitive process to generalize over. Please argue against Fodor and McLaughlin: defend the claim that explaining systematicity does not require context-invariant constituents, so that tensor-product binding can do the explanatory work that Fodor and Pylyshyn originally demanded of a language of thought.
