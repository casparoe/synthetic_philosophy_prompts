My partner and I have spent three years trying, unsuccessfully, to have a child. Extensive testing hasn't found anything wrong with either of us; the only diagnosis on offer is "unexplained infertility." Our insurer denied coverage for in vitro fertilization on the grounds that infertility counts as a covered illness only when a specific bodily malfunction can be identified, and since nothing in either of us is malfunctioning, there is nothing to treat medically — wanting a child, however understandable, isn't itself a medical need. What makes this harder to let go of is that the same plan covers erectile dysfunction treatment without requiring anyone to locate a defect first. Something about the insurer's reasoning strikes me as off, though I can't rule out that I'm just motivated to find a flaw in an argument that happens to be inconvenient for me. Is "we can't point to a broken part, so there's nothing to treat" really a sound way to decide what counts as a genuine medical problem?
