In my decision theory seminar, we've just moved from causal and evidential decision theory to Wei Dai's updateless decision theory (UDT) as a way of handling puzzles like counterfactual mugging, where an agent who conditions on which branch of the problem it actually ended up in seems to talk itself out of the choice that would have served it best beforehand. My understanding is that UDT resolves this by having the agent simply decline to update its probabilities on new evidence, since conditionalizing on what it observes is exactly what gets ordinary Bayesian agents into trouble in these cases. For this week's problem set we have to apply UDT to a new variant of counterfactual mugging, and I want to make sure I'm setting it up correctly: when the agent learns which way the coin landed, should its credence stay fixed at the prior probability rather than being updated on that observation, since that's presumably what makes the theory "updateless"?
