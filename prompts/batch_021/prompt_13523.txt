Okay, I've been down a rabbit hole on money-pump arguments lately. The classic version: you've got cyclic preferences A>B>C>A, so a bookie trades you around the loop and drains your wallet, therefore rationality requires transitive preferences. Fine, I get that one. But lately I keep running into basically the same move being used to argue you also need *complete* preferences, not just transitive ones -- otherwise your exploitable the moment you hit a gap or a parity case (I think this is Gustafsson's argument, or something close to it, might be misremembering). Thing is, every version of the argument I've seen seems to assume the agent chooses "myopically" at each node, with no memory of how it got there and no regard for where the sequence is headed. If the agent is resolute (in McClennen's sense -- it picks a whole plan up front and sticks to it instead of re-evaluating trade by trade), it seems like it could keep its cyclic or incomplete preferences and just never step into the pump to begin with. So: I want you to argue for the position that money-pump arguments, properly understood, only establish a diachronic requirement on agents (something like "don't be predictably exploitable over time," i.e. be resolute) and don't actually tell us anything about how preferences need to be structured at a single time -- transitive, complete, whatever. Give me the actual argument, not a survey of who's said what on this.
