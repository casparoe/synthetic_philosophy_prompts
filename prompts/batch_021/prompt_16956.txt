Ok so this has been living in my head rent free since a dumb Slack argument at work last week, and I need a sanity check from someone outside my team. I'm a backend engineer at a mid-size e-commerce company, and because of some messy history with an old partner integration, every couple of months our data team discovers that two customer records that have existed separately in our DB for ages - say customer #48213 and customer #91045, each with it's own signup date, order history, support tickets, the works - actually belong to the same real human, and they run what we call an "identity resolution" job to merge them into one. Every single time this happens people are kind of stunned, because it changes stuff we thought we knew: that customer's lifetime value jumps, their churn score flips, sometimes a whole cohort analysis has to get redone.

My coworker Dan thinks the "being stunned" part is silly. His argument is that "48213 = 91045" and "48213 = 48213" are both just the equality operator returning True, so they're the exact same kind of statement logically, and the only reason one feels like a revelation while the other is too obvious to even say out loud is a fact about us (we just hadn't run the merge yet), not a fact about the statements themselves. There's no real difference between the two claims, on his view, just our ignorance. I get the appeal of that, but something about it doesn't sit right with me, like there's a genuine asymmetry between those two statements that isn't reducible to "we happened not to know X yet." I even tried explaining this to my partner over dinner and she couldn't tell who was right either. So who's actually right here - is Dan correct that I'm just being precious about a boring data-hygiene issue, or is there a real, principled reason why two equally true "sameness" statements can carry such wildly different amounts of information?
