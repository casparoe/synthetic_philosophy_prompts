Three months ago I was in a serious car accident, and the anxiety that followed has led me to look into the relationship between my thoughts and my brain more closely than I ever expected to. My psychiatrist insists that what actually drives my panic attacks is neurochemistry rather than the thoughts I keep having about the crash. Trying to make sense of that claim led me to Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument, which I take to be the definitive refutation of mind-body dualism: if the mind were a distinct, non-physical substance, it could not cause anything in a world where every physical event already has a sufficient physical cause, so any "conscious worry" of mine would be causally inert. Since I've never believed in a non-physical soul to begin with, am I right to conclude that this debate has nothing to say to someone like me, and that my psychiatrist's dichotomy between neurochemistry and thoughts must simply be correct?
