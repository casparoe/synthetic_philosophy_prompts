I spent my working life in celestial mechanics before turning, in retirement, to the history and philosophy of science, and I keep returning to Newton's theological commitments. Newton worried that gravitational perturbations among the planets would gradually destabilize the solar system, requiring God's periodic intervention to set it right again — a view Leibniz mocked, in the correspondence Samuel Clarke conducted on Newton's behalf, as making God a clockmaker who could not build a self-sufficient machine. Decades later, Laplace's refinement of perturbation theory showed these variations to be periodic rather than secular, and he is famously said to have told Napoleon that he had no need of that hypothesis to explain the system's stability. Given the voluntarist theology behind Newton's insistence, in the General Scholium, on an actively ruling deity, how do you think he would have responded to Laplace's result — would he have simply conceded the point, or found a way, within his own framework, to relocate divine action elsewhere?
