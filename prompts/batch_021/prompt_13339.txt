I'm part of a small committee at the warehouse where I work that runs an employee hardship fund - people can chip in a couple bucks from every paycheck if they want, and then if someone's going through something rough they apply and the committee votes on how much to give them out of whatever's sitting in the pot that month. Normally it's easy because we don't get more requests than we have money for. But this month we've got three applications and only $1,800 to work with. Denise needs $1,200 to fix her transmission or she can't get to work (and she's already behind on rent, so losing the job could snowball into losing the apartment too). Marcus needs about $900 for a dental procedure he's been putting off - he's been in real pain for weeks and it's messing with his sleep and his work. And Priya is asking for $1,000 to help cover her dad's funeral costs. That's $3,100 requested against $1,800 available, so there's just no way to give everyone what they asked for.

It turned into a real arguement at the meeting. A few people said the only fair thing is to split the $1,800 evenly (or proportionally to what each person asked for), because none of us has any business deciding whose situation is 'worse' than anyone else's - like, how would you even measure that Marcus's tooth pain outweighs Priya's grief, or that either one matters more than Denise possibly losing her apartment? But other people, me included honestly, think an even split is basically a cop-out, because handing Priya $600 toward a funeral and Denise $600 toward a repair she desperately needs to keep her job don't feel like equally good uses of the same $600 - splitting it evenly just quietly pretends we can't tell any difference between the cases, when it feels like we obviously can, at least a little. I'm not a philosophy or ethics person at all, I just can't stop chewing on this - I brought it up with my sister and she says I'm way overthinking a $1,800 decision, but I genuinely don't know who's right here. We meet again Friday to settle it and I told the committee I'd bring something in writing. Could you write that memo for me - one that actually recommends a specific way to split the $1,800 (not 'it depends'), gives real reasons for it, and takes the 'we have no way to compare these three situations' objection seriously instead of just talking past it?
