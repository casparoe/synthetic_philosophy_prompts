So I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole for like two weeks now, reading Wikipedia and Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy pages about how you'd explain "meaning" or "aboutness" in a brain without just hand-waving and calling it magic. The idea that's grabbed me most is teleosemantics - basically the claim (I think this is mostly credited to Ruth Millikan?) that a thought or a neural signal means whatever it was built by natural selection to track, its "proper function" as they call it, so a frog's fly-detecting neuron is about flies specifically, not about "small dark moving things" in general, because tracking flies is what kept its ancestors alive. And thats also supposed to explain misrepresentation: when the frog snaps at a bb pellet flicked past it, thats just the fly-detector malfunctioning relative to the job it was selected for, not some change in what it actually means.

What's been bugging me for days now is a Swampman-style objection I ran into: if lightning hit a swamp and by pure chance assembled a frog (or a person) thats molecule-for-molecule identical to a real one, that creature has zero evolutionary history, so on the teleosemantics story none of its detectors have a "proper function" at all, meaning literally nothing it does is about anything - even though it would snap at the exact same bb pellet in the exact same way a real frog would. That feels like its proving way too much, like content shouldn't hinge on facts about your dead ancestors instead of what's happening in you right now. But then I looked into the rival "causal" story (I think Fodor's "asymmetric dependence" idea is the fancy version of this) that doesn't need any selection history at all, and that one seems to run into the opposite problem, the "disjunction problem," where you can't explain why the signal means "fly" rather than the disjunction "fly-or-bb-pellet-or-anything-that-sets-me-off," since without appeal to function there's no obvious reason to pick out flies as the "real" meaning and bb pellets as the error. I'm totally self-taught on this stuff so I might be garbling half of it - my brother thinks I should just drop it and go outside more, lol.
