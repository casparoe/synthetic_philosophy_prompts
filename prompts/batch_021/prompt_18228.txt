Okay so I've been going back and forth on something for weeks and can't land anywhere, so I figured i'd ask you instead of my writing group (we brought it up at the last meeting and it just turned into an argument that went nowhere). I'm about two-thirds through my third novel, a quiet family drama, and there's one big set-piece near the climax—shifting timelines, unreliable narrator, a structure I basically reworked from a writer I admire—that everyone in the group who's also a working writer says is the strongest thing I've written, real "submit this chapter to a magazine on its own" territory. Meanwhile I gave the same draft to my mom and two friends who don't read much literary fiction, and none of them mentioned that chapter at all. What they kept bringing up instead was a much smaller scene from earlier in the book, where the main character just sits in the yard with her dying dog. I wrote that one in about twenty minutes, barely revised it, straight out of a memory of my own childhood dog. My friend actually cried reading it, and texted me a full week later saying it was still stuck in her head.

Now I can't stop turning this over. The clever chapter took me months, and if I'm honest some of that time went into thinking about how it would land with other writers, wanting them to see what I could pull off. The dog scene I wasn't thinking about impressing anyone, it just came out. Part of me thinks that makes it the "truer" piece of writing and the other one more of a performance, but another part of me worries that's just an excuse to avoid the harder formal stuff going forward. Is one of these actually the better writing, or are my two audiences measuring completely different things? And should this effect how I approach the rest of the book, or am I overthinking one weekend of reader reactions?
