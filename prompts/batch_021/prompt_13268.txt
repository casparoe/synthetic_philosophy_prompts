I'm a software engineer, and for the past couple of years I've volunteered with a small backcountry search-and-rescue outfit, mostly building the dispatch tool our coordinators use during call-outs. Since I wrote the thing, I also ended up on the ops committee. Last month we had a bad night where two groups got stranded in the same storm window and our one helicopter genuinely couldn't reach both sites before the pass closed. My tool's dispatch logic just defaults to "sort by number of people at risk, descending," so it recommended the group of five hikers over the one solo climber, and that's what the coordinator went with. The climbers family is furious, and its blown up into a real fight on the committee over whether "bigger group wins" should just stay a hardcoded rule.

One of our committee members, a retired ICU nurse who apparently minored in philosophy way back when, brought up some old paper -- Taurek, I think, maybe Taurak, I don't remember the spelling -- arguing you can't just add lives across different people like that. Her summary of his point was that there's no single person who loses five lives, there's just five separate people each losing their own one life, same as the climber loses his one life, so treating the group's loss as automatically five times weightier isn't something you get for free, it's something that needs an actual argument. She thinks that in a genuine tie like this, with no other distinguishing factor between the two groups, we should be flipping a coin or running a lottery weighted by headcount instead of defaulting straight to numbers. Half the committee thinks that's borderline reckless when we could reliably save more people; the other half thinks she's onto something real about how the current rule quietly treats the solo climber as if he counts for less as a person, not just as a number.

I have to bring the committee an actual written recommendation next month, something they can vote on and I can go implement in the tool if it changes. Could you draft that memo for me? I want it to land on one concrete rule (including exactly when it would and wouldn't kick in), suggest we revisit it after a season of real use rather than treat it as carved in stone, and seriously engage with the "numbers shouldn't just mechanically win" argument instead of brushing it off as obviously wrong.
