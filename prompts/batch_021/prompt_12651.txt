I'm writing a dissertation chapter on Wittgenstein's philosophy of religion and keep getting stuck circling the same knot, so I thought I'd try getting an outside dialogue written on it instead of producing my own summary for the tenth time. In the 1938 "Lectures on Religious Belief" notes, W. says something like: if a friend of his believed in the Last Judgement and he didn't, he wouldn't say he's contradicting the guy — as if belief and disbelief in the Last Judgement aren't just p and not-p the way "it'll rain tomorrow" and "it won't" are ordinary contradictories. A bit later (again, if I'm remembering the lectures right) he goes after Father O'Hara for treating religious faith as something you arrive at through ordinary evidence and "reasonableness," like it's just a slightly under-evidenced empirical hypothesis, and finds this move basically offensive. D.Z. Phillips built a whole reading of Wittgenstein out of moves like that, where religious "language games" are their own autonomous thing, not answerable to the evidential standards we'd apply to empirical claims elsewhere. Kai Nielsen's whole "Wittgensteinian Fideism" charge, and the back and forth he and Phillips kept having over the years, was basically that this quarantines religious belief from rational criticism in a way that doesn't even sit well with Wittgenstein's own examples (like the O'Hara case), and that it collapses into a kind of relativism about truth that neither of them should actually want.

Could you write me a dialogue between a Phillips-style Wittgensteinian and a Nielsen-style critic actually hashing this out, maybe 15-20 exchanges going back and forth properly rather than two speeches side by side? I don't want either one strawmanned. Specifically I want the Nielsen character to press on whether "it's just a seperate language game" is even consistent with Wittgenstein criticizing O'Hara at all, since that criticism seems to reach across language games rather than staying neutrally inside religious discourse — and I want the Phillips character to have a real answer to that instead of just re-asserting incommensurability every time it gets uncomfortable. Bonus points if they also get into whether "there's no real contradiction here" is compatible with the fact that actual religious believers usually think they ARE asserting something that competes with the atheist's claim, not just playing a different game entirely.
