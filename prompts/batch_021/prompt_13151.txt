hey so I'm working through Spinoza's Political Treatise (Tractatus Politicus) for a chapter on early modern natural right theory, and I keep getting stuck on the very end of it -- ch. 11, on democracy, which is also the last thing he ever wrote, since he died partway though finishing it. quoting roughly from my (kind of mashed-together) copy, here's the bit that's bugging me:

"...if we look to experience, we shall find that it is weakness that has done this: for it has nowhere happened that men and women ruled alike, but wherever both sexes are found together, men govern and women are governed, and thus the two live together in some sort of peace. But if women were by nature equal to men in strength of mind (fortitudo) and ability (ingenium), which above all give men power, then surely among all the many and different nations we know of, some would be found where the sexes rule equally, or where men are governed by women; and since this nowhere happens, we may conclude that women do not have equal right with men by nature, but necessarily give way to them."

then theres a bit more about jealousy wrecking things if men and women shared office, and then it just stops -- literally the last words of the book, mid-argument.

what I can't get straight is what kind of argument this is supposed to be, given the rest of Spinoza's system. he says elsewhere (Theological-Political Treatise ch. 16, Ethics 4p37s2) that "right" just is power, full stop, no extra normative fact sitting on top of it. and he's a necessitarian -- everything follows from God/Nature's attributes with the same kind of necessity, so in some sense every historical pattern is "necessary." so is the "no nation has ever had equal rule" move here doing any real modal work, or is it basically decorative and he's actually smuggling in something more like a fixed essence of "woman" (via the ingenium/fortitudo talk) that his own anti-teleological, no-fixed-kinds metaphysics shouldn't really allow him? is this a genuine crack in the system, or am I just misreading how contingency and necessity are supposed to work for him? not looking for the "product of his time" answer, I want the actual mechanics.
