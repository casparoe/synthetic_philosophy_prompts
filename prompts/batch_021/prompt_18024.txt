For my AP English Language class, we each have to write a research-based argument essay on a debate from the Harlem Renaissance, and I picked the disagreement between W. E. B. Du Bois and Alain Locke over what Black art should actually be for. I already know the soundbite version of this fight: Du Bois declared in his 1926 essay "Criteria of Negro Art" that "all art is propaganda," while Locke responded a couple of years later, in an essay called "Art or Propaganda?," with a view that leaned much closer to art for art's sake. But when I read a little further into both essays, I noticed that Du Bois and Locke actually agreed on quite a lot, including that Black art should be tied to racial self-expression and even to racial advancement, which makes me think their real disagreement has to be narrower and more specific than the two slogans suggest. I don't want my essay to just repeat the simplified version that gets cited everywhere.

Could you help me compare their positions in more depth? I'm especially curious what each of them actually meant by "propaganda," since I suspect Du Bois was using the word in a much broader or more neutral sense than we would today. I'd also like to know whether Locke's real objection was to the goal of using art to advance the race at all, or more specifically to the methods and audience that this goal seemed to require, such as writing in ways meant to persuade or reassure white readers. If it's useful, it would also help to place their dispute within the older philosophical debate between instrumentalist and autonomist (or formalist) theories of art, since I get the sense this particular argument didn't start with them. Finally, given the political stakes both men were responding to, whose account do you personally find more persuasive as a theory of how art and racial politics should relate to one another, and why?
