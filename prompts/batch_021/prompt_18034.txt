Six weeks ago I was diagnosed with Brugada syndrome and had an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator fitted: the device sits inertly until it detects the onset of a life-threatening ventricular rhythm, at which point it delivers an internal shock that resets my heart before the episode can prove fatal. I minored in philosophy a decade ago, and largely to keep my mind occupied during the recovery I have been revisiting old notes on the metaphysics of dispositions, which is how I ended up back in the fink/mask literature: C. B. Martin's original wire, rigged to what he called an electro-fink so that it turns live for exactly the instant someone tests it; David Lewis's reformed conditional analysis, offered in response; and Alexander Bird's poison-and-antidote case, which is supposed to survive Lewis's fix precisely because the causal basis — the poison already coursing through the victim's system — remains completely intact while something else intervenes further down the causal chain.

My own situation refuses to sit neatly inside that scheme, and it has been nagging at me more than a health scare probably leaves room for. The tempting reading is that the ICD is just a sophisticated antidote: my disposition to fibrillate is never touched, and the device only interrupts the route from trigger to death. But antidotes, on the standard description, work by stopping the harmful process before anything we would count as a manifestation occurs. My heart, by contrast, actually enters the dangerous rhythm — if only for a second or two — before being shocked back into order, so what the device blocks looks less like an impending manifestation than a manifestation already in progress. That does not sound like a fink either, since nothing about my heart's underlying disposition to arrhythmia is being altered by the device itself.

What would actually be useful, rather than another pass through wires and poisons, is one new example of your own devising — nothing involving electrical circuits, poisons, fragile objects, or medical devices — of a disposition whose manifestation is reversed partway through, rather than merely prevented (as with a mask or antidote) or faked by altering the underlying basis (as with a fink). Then, working from that example rather than mine, give me your best argument for whether this "interrupted manifestation" pattern is just an unusual-looking mask, a fink operating with a delay, or evidence that the fink/mask dichotomy was never exhaustive to begin with. A quick verdict on where my own ICD case falls would be welcome, but the new example and the argument built on it are what I am really after.
