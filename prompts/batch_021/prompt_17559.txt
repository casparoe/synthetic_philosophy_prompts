So i fell down a rabbit hole a while back reading decision-theory stuff on some AI alignment blogs, and I can't stop thinking about this thing called Parfit's Hitchhiker. Setup: you're dying of thirst in the desert, a driver stops who can basically tell when your bluffing, and she'll only give you a ride into town if she's convinced you'll actually pay her back once you arrive. Thing is, once you're safely in town, paying up does nothing for you anymore, so a purely self-interested "rational" person wouldn't bother - which means that same rational person never gets picked up in the first place and just dies out there. So the only people who actually survive are the ones who'd genuinely keep the promise even once it's no longer in their interest to. Apparently there's a name for this general idea, "rational irrationality" - being the kind of agent who'd act against your own narrow self-interest can literally be the rational move, once someone else can see it coming (kinda Newcomb-ish now that I think about it).

Can you write me a short story built around this? One thing though - please don't just restage the literal hitchhiker-in-a-car version, I've seen that exact framing a bunch already. Put the same underlying structure in a completely different setting (business deal, heist, whatever works) and have it hinge on the precise moment where doing the "irrational" thing stops being obviously in the character's interest. Doesn't need to be long, a page or two is plenty, and i'd rather you dramatize the dilemma than have someone explain the moral out loud at the end.
