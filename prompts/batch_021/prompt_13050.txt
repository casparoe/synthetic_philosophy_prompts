And here's the answer from one of my stronger students, which is exactly why I'm second-guessing myself -- it reads confidently and I don't want to dock someone for something I might be misremembering myself:

"Nietzsche opens 'Why I Am So Clever' by complaining that philosophy and religion have obsessed over 'the salvation of the soul' while ignoring what he calls the 'little things': nutrition, place and climate, and recreation, which he lumps together as 'the whole casuistry of self-interest.' On nutrition he is savage about German cooking specifically, blaming heavy food and beer for the sluggishness of German philosophy, and says he does much better on small, frequent meals than on heavy ones, avoiding alcohol -- which disagrees with him -- in favor of water. On climate, his claim is that great intellectual periods track dry air, and he lists places that suited his own constitution, like Nice and Turin, against the damp places that didn't. On recreation, his main point is that real thoughts come from walking around outdoors, not from sitting hunched over a desk.

The Cornaro material in 'The Four Great Errors' backs this up. Cornaro was the Renaissance nobleman who lived to an old age on a famously sparse diet and wrote a treatise recommending it to everyone. Nietzsche brings him in as a real historical case of exactly the light eating already recommended in 'Why I Am So Clever' actually working -- a frugal diet producing a long, vigorous life. So the 'error' the chapter title refers to is really the mistake other moralists made in not taking Cornaro's diet-longevity link seriously enough, not any mistake in Cornaro's own reasoning about his own case.

This is also where I'd locate 'the great health': it's basically Nietzsche's name for the sturdy, well-regulated physiological state you get once you've actually managed nutrition, climate, and recreation properly. So the three chapters amount to one continuous argument -- get the small physiological things right and the big things (strength, clarity, 'great health') take care of themselves."
