Random update on my life: my advisor's on sabbatical so I got stuck co-organizing the little workshop she runs every year between our phil-of-mind group and a bunch of neuroscientists from the med school, which means I have to give the opening remarks in about two weeks. I wanted to open with the standard multiple realizability pitch - pain isn't identical to any one physical state because its realized differently in humans, octopuses, hypothetical aliens, etc, therefore mental states aren't brain states, therefore psychology and neuroscience need each other but can't reduce to one another - since that's basically the founding justification for why this workshop exists at all. Then a postdoc told me the neuro people have been quietly annoyed by that exact framing for years, because of the whole Bechtel & Mundale point that the textbook examples don't actually survive once you look at how neuroscientists individuate brain states across species in practice. So now I dont want to stand up and give the naive philosophers' version and loose half the room in the first five minutes, but I also don't want to throw my own field under the bus just to look self-aware in front of the other camp. Can you write me the actual opening remarks - 5 to 7 minutes, spoken out loud, not read off a slide - that uses that tension to set up a good day instead of papering over it, and still leaves the dean, who's funding half of this and does not care about any of it, with some sense of why the argument even matters?
