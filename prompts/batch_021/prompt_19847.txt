So in my AP Euro class we got assigned a packet of Pascal's Pensées (not the wager stuff, everyone already knows that one) and there's this section on justice and force that's been bugging me for like two days now. Pascal basically says justice without force is powerless and force without justice is just tyranny, which is fine, but then he takes it further: since people can never actually agree on what's truly just (it's endlessly arguable) while it's obvious who has more force (no one really disputes that), society basically gave up on making justice powerful and instead just decided that whatever is powerful counts as just. Kind of bleak ngl, but I feel like I get that part as a cynical-but-realistic observation about how law actually works.

What's actually tripping me up is the next move he makes. If laws and customs really are just whatever force and habit happened to produce, with no deeper justification underneath, you'd think Pascal would want that exposed so people could question it. But he seems to argue almost the opposite -- that it's dangerous to let ordinary people see how arbitrary the whole system is, and that "clever" people should basically go along with custom on the outside while privately knowing better, instead of spreading that around. My friend from debate club says this just makes Pascal a "might makes right" relativist and nothing more, but that feels off to me since he's obviously a really serious religious thinker who cares a ton about truth, not just some cynic. There was also another part in the same packet about how force, intelligence, and holiness are basically different "orders" of greatness, and how demanding respect in one order using another one is the actual defintion of tyranny -- which feels connected somehow but I can't quite figure out how.

Can you help me actually untangle this? Like is there a real philosophical reason Pascal can consistently think justice rests on nothing but force and custom AND still want people to keep obeying that custom without question, or is he just being inconsistent (or kind of elitist) here? And does any of this connect to the seperate stuff he says elsewhere about human reason not being able to get all the way to truth by itself?
