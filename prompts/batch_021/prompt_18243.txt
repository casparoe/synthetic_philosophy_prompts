I am several chapters into a novel I'm calling, at least for now, *The Settlement* — the plot turns on a financial-fraud trial in which a young compliance officer ends up the only person criminally punished for a scheme her bank's executives designed, while the bank itself settles with regulators for a fine that is, in effect, absorbed by shareholders and customers rather than by anyone who actually did wrong. While researching the ethics of corporate settlements for the book, a friend who teaches philosophy of religion pointed me to David Lewis's short, oddly under-discussed paper "Do We Believe in Penal Substitution?," in which he takes completely seriously the standard objection to the doctrine of penal substitution: that punishment, unlike a monetary debt, cannot simply be discharged by an innocent or willing substitute, because only the guilty party can be said to deserve it. Lewis works through, using his own resources on causation, desert, and what it would take for one party's suffering or loss to "stand in" for another's, just how much of that objection can actually be resisted.

Lewis died in 2001, well before "too big to jail" became the standard shorthand for exactly this kind of secular substitution, in which a corporation's payment displaces the punishment individual wrongdoers might otherwise face. Reasoning only from the actual argumentative structure of his paper, rather than from the general shape of the contemporary debate, what do you think Lewis would most plausibly have concluded about corporate penal substitution of this kind? Does whatever lets him take the religious case seriously — perhaps something like the substitute's willingness, some relation of identity or authorization between substitute and wrongdoer, or the idea that what matters is punishment's point being served rather than merely its cost being paid — carry over to the relationship between a corporation and its shareholders, or does his own framework give principled reasons to think the corporate case is substantially worse off?
