This term, in my seminar on the metaphysics of propositions, we have been working through Bradley's regress and Frege's attempt to defuse it with the doctrine of unsaturated concepts, and I would like to examine the exchange in more depth than my notes allow. Write a dialogue between two philosophers: one convinced that treating the concept in a proposition like "Socrates is wise" as essentially unsaturated blocks the regress and secures the proposition's unity, and one convinced that this move only relabels the very thing that needed explaining. Have the skeptic press two objections. First, Frege's own "concept horse" problem suggests that we cannot even refer to an unsaturated concept without thereby treating it as a saturated object, which throws doubt on whether the saturated/unsaturated distinction is anything more than a fact about grammar. Second, if brute, unexplained "saturation" counts as an acceptable stopping point, a relation theorist could have stopped Bradley's regress in exactly the same brute way for ordinary relations, so it is unclear that Frege has bought anything beyond a new vocabulary. The Fregean should have genuine, non-question-begging responses to both objections rather than simply restating the doctrine. Let the two of them actually engage with each other's last point as the exchange develops, and end with the disagreement sharpened rather than resolved.
