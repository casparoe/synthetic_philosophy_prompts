This term I'm taking a course on welfare economics, and we recently covered Lionel Robbins' famous 1932 argument that interpersonal comparisons of utility are not genuine, falsifiable scientific claims but ethical value judgments smuggled into economic reasoning. As it happens, I currently serve as treasurer of my university's student societies council, where each year we must divide a modest discretionary grant among a dozen or so clubs, several of which insist that their members would benefit far more from the money than the other clubs' members would. Please describe the most important implications of the debate over interpersonal utility comparisons for how a committee in my position should go about adjudicating these competing claims, and for whether there is any principled way of dividing the money that does not simply presuppose an answer to the very question Robbins says cannot be settled.
