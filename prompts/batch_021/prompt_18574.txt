I'm taking a course on ethics and public policy this term, which is probably why a small episode in my dorm has stayed on my mind. Our resident advisor rearranged our floor's kitchen so that the recycling bin sits right beside the sink, while the trash bin was moved to an awkward corner several steps away. When I noticed that recycling had increased and asked her about it, she admitted the rearrangement was deliberate, based on research showing that small frictions shift behavior far more than people expect, and she maintains that this is harmless: no one was lied to, and everyone remains completely free to walk the extra steps to the trash can. A friend of mine disagrees. She points out that the RA could simply have posted a notice explaining the psychology and inviting people to opt in, but instead redesigned the room without telling anyone; to her, that choice to act on us rather than reason with us is precisely what makes the arrangement manipulative, regardless of the cause it serves. I can't decide who has the better argument, or even whether "free" and "manipulative" are the right categories to be disputing here. What's actually at stake philosophically, and does it matter that the RA's goal is a collective good like recycling rather than something more clearly for each resident's own benefit?
