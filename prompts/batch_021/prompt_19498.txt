I'm putting together a graduate seminar on social epistemology and want to push the discussion of testimony past toy cases like a stranger giving directions on the street. Please describe the most important implications of the reductionism/anti-reductionism debate about testimonial justification -- Hume's reductionism on one side, Reid's and Coady's anti-reductionism (including Coady's charge that any reductionist justification of testimony is ultimately circular) on the other -- for whether an individual co-author on a large experimental physics paper, the kind with several thousand listed authors, most of whom contributed to only a narrow slice of the detector or analysis pipeline, can truthfully be said to know the paper's central results. I'm less interested in a survey of the two positions than in what each would actually require for that co-author's belief to count as justified, and in which view better fits how such collaborations really operate.
