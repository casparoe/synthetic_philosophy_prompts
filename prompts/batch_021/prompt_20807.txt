Our neighborhood association has to decide how to spend a community grant, and there are four competing proposals: repaving the parking lot, adding a playground, planting a community garden, and installing solar panels on the clubhouse. To be fair, we asked everyone to rank all four in order of preference rather than just voting for one favorite. Tallying the rankings by awarding points for each position (three points for a first choice, two for a second, and so on) put the playground in the lead, but when another member instead compared the proposals two at a time, the garden won. Trying to settle things, we dropped the solar panels proposal, which had barely any support anyway, and recalculated the point totals for the remaining three — yet the winner flipped again, even though nobody had changed how they personally ranked those three relative to one another. People are now quietly accusing each other of favoring whichever counting method helps their own preferred project, and I keep assuming there must be some genuinely neutral way to combine everyone's rankings into one fair decision that we simply haven't found yet, but I'm starting to wonder if that assumption itself is wrong. Is there actually a fair way to do this, or is something more fundamental getting in the way?
