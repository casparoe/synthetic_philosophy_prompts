So I'm in a philosophy of physics seminar this term and we spent two whole sessions on Einstein's hole argument, and afterward me and a classmate ended up arguing about it in the hallway for like 40 minutes and then over text for the rest of the night. We agree on the setup: you take a solution (M,g) to Einstein's field equations, pick a "hole" region with no matter in it, and apply a diffeomorphism that's the identity everywhere outside the hole but smoothly drags the metric around inside it, giving you a new field g'. Because GR is generally covariant, (M,g) and (M,g') are both solutions, they agree on literally everything up to and outside the hole, and disagree inside it. So if spacetime points have determinate identities independent of what field values sit on them (manifold substantivalism), then all the physical facts outside the hole leave what happens inside radically underdetermined — same past, same laws, different future. That much we're not fighting about.

What we can't agree on is whether the standard rescue actually works. Her position: "sophisticated substantivalism" — keep spacetime points as real, mind-independent things, but drop haecceitism about them, so (M,g) and (M,g') don't represent two different possibilities, just two descriptions of the same one, since there's no fact about which point is "the same point" across the map except the structural role it plays. On her view this is a totally coherent position, like thinking persons are real without thinking there's some extra soul-pellet that grounds personal identity over and above the psychology. My position: once you say points have no identity beyond their qualitative/relational role, you've quietly given away the one thing that made substantivalism substantivalism in the first place, and you're just a relationalist who insists on keeping some leftover talk of "points" around for comfort. She thinks im conflating individuation with existence and that you can have one without the other; I think that distinction is doing way more work than it can bear here. Can you explain, in a really intuitive way and not just by restating the formal setup, why the hole argument is even supposed to generate indeterminism to begin with, and then actually settle this for us — is sophisticated substantivalism a stable position distinct from relationalism, or does denying haecceitism basically just concede the game? I'd like to know specifically where each of us is right or sneaking something past the other, not just a "reasonable people disagree" shrug.
