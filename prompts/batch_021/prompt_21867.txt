I host a small philosophy podcast for a mostly lay but philosophically engaged audience, and this past weekend an old friend from graduate school, who is finishing a book on Wittgenstein, stayed with us; we ended up arguing until nearly two in the morning about whether On Certainty actually answers the skeptic or merely relabels the problem. Her position was that once one stops treating hinge propositions such as "I have two hands" or "the earth has existed for a long time" as beliefs at all -- once one sees them as arational, action-embedded certainties rather than truth-apt claims that could in principle be justified -- the skeptic's demand for grounds simply gets no foothold, because there is nothing of the right kind there to be groundless. I kept objecting that this looks like a shell game: if hinges are not in the business of being true or known, it is mysterious how their being "in place" is what licenses the rest of our empirical knowledge, which certainly is in that business. The certainty involved is somehow supposed to be too primitive to need justifying, yet load-bearing enough to justify everything else.

Next month I am recording an episode with a guest who defends a far more careful version of my friend's view, and I would rather not spend the hour letting him simply restate the picture in more technical vocabulary. Could you draft eight to ten interview questions that turn on the actual mechanics of the position rather than inviting a summary of it? I would like at least one question that presses directly on the tension described above -- arational status versus epistemic load-bearing -- one that uses Wittgenstein's own remark about a tribe that consults an oracle rather than a physicist to ask whether the view can really avoid the conclusion that which hinges one holds is simply a fact about one's upbringing rather than anything a critic could fault, and one that exploits his river-bed image, on which today's hinge can harden out of yesterday's fluid empirical claim and later turn fluid again, to ask what "exempt from doubt" can mean if the exemption is so easily lost. For each of these three, please add a short, pointed follow-up keyed to the reply he is most likely to give. I would also like the sequence to build toward a closing question asking whether "hinge epistemology" is a first-order theory of justification at all, or whether presenting it as an answer to the skeptic already betrays the quietist spirit of the text it claims to be reading.
