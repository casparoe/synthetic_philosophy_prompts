I am a moral philosopher recently elected to the governing council of the Society for Psychical Research -- the body Sidgwick co-founded in 1882 and served as first president, hoping that empirical evidence of a future life might settle the very conflict between self-interest and duty he had diagnosed as unresolved in his own ethics. I need to draft the decision memo for our next meeting. Our journal has received a rigorous, preregistered forced-choice (card-guessing) telepathy study with a small but statistically significant effect. One faction on council wants it run as the lead article, since hedging now would betray the Society's founding purpose of giving anomalous results the hearing that other journals deny them. A second wants it published only alongside a commissioned skeptical rebuttal, fearing that anything less will read as exactly the credulity Sidgwick spent his career guarding the Society against. A third wants it rejected outright, statistics notwithstanding. Please draft that memo -- what we publish and how -- grounded in the evidential standards Sidgwick himself tried to uphold, and fair to both the boldness and the caution factions' worries.
