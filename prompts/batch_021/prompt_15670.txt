In my philosophy of perception seminar, we discussed Zenon Pylyshyn's thesis that early vision is cognitively impenetrable, meaning it forms a fixed module that no belief or desire can reach into and alter. This seems hard to square with Carrasco, Ling, and Read's (2004) finding that covertly attending to a stimulus increases its perceived contrast, even though nothing about the physical stimulus itself changes. Since this looks like a clear top-down effect on a paradigmatically "early" visual property, why do philosophers still treat cognitive penetrability as an open empirical question instead of simply concluding that Pylyshyn's encapsulation thesis has been falsified?
