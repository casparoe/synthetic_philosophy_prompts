Okay so we just started the operant conditioning unit in AP Psych (all the Skinner box stuff, rats pressing a lever to get a food pellet) and it made me think about something that's been bugging me about my friend Mia. She has this insane 1,247 day streak on Duolingo, and at this point I'm pretty sure she cares about the streak itself way more than she cares about actually learning Spanish. She's told me she just taps through the matching-word exercises on autopilot most days purely to get her XP in before midnight, and when she found out she'd have zero wifi at camp this summer she paid real money for a "streak freeze" instead of just letting the number reset. When I pointed out that she still can't hold a basic conversation in Spanish after three-plus years of "practicing" daily, she got kind of defensive and said the streak still counts for something.

It made me notice my own stuff too, ngl. My smartwatch gives me this little badge and a happy buzz when I close my exercise ring, and there's been more than one lazy day where I just shook my wrist around under my desk for like five minutes instead of actually moving, and it gave me almost the exact same satisfied feeling as a real workout would've. My friends at lunch think I'm way overanalyzing this and that its just normal laziness, but it feels like there's something more interesting (and maybe kind of concerning) going on when you start caring about the number or badge itself instead of the real thing it was suppose to be tracking. Am I onto something real here, or is this genuinely a non-issue and I need to chill? And if it is a real pattern, does it say anything worrying about how human motivation works in general, or about how careful we should be when designing things like grading systems, fitness apps, or video game reward systems?
