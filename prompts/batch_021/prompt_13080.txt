I've spent a good part of this year working through the Center on Long-Term Risk's material on threat-resistant bargaining, following on from the usual LessWrong entry points into acausal decision theory — Newcomb's problem, counterfactual mugging, and so on. One proposal from that literature I keep coming back to is the idea of "surrogate goals": since agents capable of accurately predicting one another (say, via source-code access or sufficiently correlated decision procedures) can threaten each other even absent any causal channel of interaction, a target might protect itself by credibly and publicly committing to treat threats against some invented, low-stakes "surrogate" value exactly as it would treat threats against its real values. The hope is that a threatener who gains nothing extra from targeting the real goal over the surrogate will end up threatening the surrogate instead, so the target can "capitulate" at essentially no real cost while its genuine values go untouched.

I'd like you to argue for the following claim, as rigorously as you can: surrogate goals do not meaningfully reduce the expected harm from acausal threats made by sufficiently sophisticated threateners. The core worry is that any agent capable of constructing a credible acausal threat in the first place is also capable of recognizing that following through against a professed decoy extracts nothing of real value from the target, and that in any setting where the threatener's payoff depends even slightly on reputation, precedent, or interactions with other potential targets — which seems like the normal case rather than some contrived edge case — the threatener does strictly better by directing the threat at the target's genuine values instead. I'd like you to engage seriously with the most natural line of defense here: that the threatener need only be indifferent between targets, with the surrogate then favored on grounds of convention, cheapness of execution, or some other tie-breaking consideration, rather than dismiss that defense out of hand.

I floated a version of this worry at a meetup a while back and got pushback along the lines of "that's exactly why the surrogate has to be linked tightly enough to the real goal that the threatener really is indifferent, full stop, with no room for reputational considerations to sneak back in" — so if your argument has a convincing response to that particular move, I'd especially like to see it.
