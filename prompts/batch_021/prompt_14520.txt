I'm moderating this week's department colloquium, where the speaker defends Ifeanyi Menkiti's processual account of personhood in Ubuntu thought: personhood is achieved, not given, conferred by the community through participation and moral maturation, so that infants and habitual wrongdoers count as persons only in an attenuated sense. Kwame Gyekye's classic objection is that this leaves basic moral standing hostage to communal approval; the speaker's usual response is that critics conflate this comparative, achieved sense of 'personhood' with the baseline moral status every human retains simply in virtue of being human. Draft seven or eight questions that make that response progressively harder to sustain: press the infant and disability cases directly, test whether the two senses of 'personhood' can really be kept separate once the community is doing the conferring, and end by asking what a community could do that would count as illegitimately stripping someone of personhood rather than merely never granting it -- for each, note the reply he's likely to give and aim the next question at it.
