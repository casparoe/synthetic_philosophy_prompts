My mother, an elder in her Presbyterian church for over thirty years, died this past Tuesday at seventy-eight. In her last decade she'd grown quietly preoccupied with the argument from divine hiddenness rather than the problem of evil, which she always said she'd made peace with. What unsettled her was recognizing herself in it: she admitted to me, and only to me, that there were long stretches when she was exactly the sincerely open, getting-nothing-back "nonresistant nonbeliever" that a perfectly loving God supposedly wouldn't allow to exist. She never left the church over it, but she also refused any comfort that struck her as too easy, and she told our pastor and me precisely what she didn't want said at her funeral: nothing about her now seeing God face to face, nothing implying the doubt had secretly been resolved all along. A few people from the small doubters' group she ran out of her living room will know exactly what's being alluded to; everyone else just needs comforting. I'm giving the eulogy on Saturday. Please write it — about five minutes long, honest by her own standards, and without turning into a lecture on the philosophy of religion.
