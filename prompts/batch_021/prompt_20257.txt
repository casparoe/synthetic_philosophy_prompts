My mom died on Monday. She was a hospice doctor for thirty years and a total stickler about the doctrine of double effect — she'd get quietly annoyed when residents compared high-dose end-of-life opioids to a trolley problem, insisting that trolleys are just about counting bodies while her cases turned on whether you're intending an effect or merely foreseeing it. This got personal fast: in her last week my brother wanted the team to just up her sedation and 'be done with it,' and I found myself giving him her whole speech about how relieving pain and intending death aren't the same act, even when they land in the same place. I'm giving her eulogy Saturday to a room thats half hospice collegues who'll notice if I fumble this and half family still raw from that fight who do not want a philosophy lecture. Can you write it, in a way that honors how precise she was without turning into a seminar, and without comforting anyone by fudging the exact thing she spent her career refusing to fudge?
