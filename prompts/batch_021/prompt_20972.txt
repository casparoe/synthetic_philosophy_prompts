For the past year my manufacturing firm has been absorbing new tariffs on imported components while hedging against currency swings that keep eating into our export margins, and trying to make sense of the policy logic sent me back to Hume's essays "Of Money" and "Of the Balance of Trade." I'm struck by how directly his price-specie-flow argument — that gold flows and price adjustments automatically erase any trade imbalance, so no amount of hoarding or protection can permanently secure a "favourable balance" — cuts against the reasoning behind tariffs meant to shrink a persistent trade deficit. Yet Hume also argued that a rising money supply gives domestic industry a genuine, if temporary, boost before prices catch up, which sounds like it could be repurposed into a case for short-term protection of struggling sectors. Setting aside developments he couldn't have foreseen — floating exchange rates, pure fiat currency, and a reserve-currency issuer running deficits for decades without the mechanism ever seeming to bite — how do you think Hume would actually judge today's tariff push? Would he see it as the same mercantilist error he spent those essays dismantling, or does his own reasoning leave a genuine opening for it?
