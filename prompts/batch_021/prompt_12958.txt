This past summer I read Richard Dawkins's *The Selfish Gene* for an independent project on evolutionary theory, and then, wanting to hear the other side, I worked through most of Elliott Sober and David Sloan Wilson's *Unto Others*. I came away more confused than when I started. Dawkins treats the gene as the fundamental 'replicator' and organisms, let alone groups, as mere 'vehicles' built to propagate it, so that anything which looks like it benefits a group is really just genes benefiting copies of themselves in relatives. Sober and Wilson argue instead that a group can be a genuine unit of selection whenever there is heritable variation in fitness between groups, and that dismissing this as 'nothing but' gene selection commits what they call, I believe, the averaging fallacy. To make matters worse, I found a discussion claiming that George Price's equation shows kin selection and multilevel selection models to be mathematically interchangeable, which made me wonder whether Dawkins and Sober/Wilson are even disagreeing about anything real, as opposed to just describing the same population genetics in two vocabularies. Two unrelated Wilsons turn out to be tangled up in this history, too: David Sloan Wilson, Sober's co-author, and Edward O. Wilson, who years later joined Nowak and Tarnita on a Nature paper attacking kin-selection theory itself and provoking a rebuttal signed by well over a hundred other biologists.

For a presentation in my biology class, I would like to cut through the competing quotations with one concrete case rather than more secondhand summaries. Could you construct a biological scenario, real or a clean thought experiment (something along the lines of Maynard Smith's old 'haystack' model would be a fine template, but you're welcome to invent your own), in which a committed gene selectionist and a committed multilevel selectionist would actually give different answers to some question about the case, rather than just redescribing the same allele frequencies in different words? And if, having tried, you conclude that no such example can really be built and that the dispute is merely verbal after all, that is a perfectly acceptable answer, but I would like you to use the example itself to show exactly where the two descriptions collapse into one another, rather than simply asserting that they do.
