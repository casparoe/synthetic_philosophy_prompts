So this happened after our local ACX meetup last month, at the potluck afterward -- not me, two other regulars. My friend Marcus has a final-round interview this week with a startup whose founder is one of those charismatic, guru-ish tech founder types with a big twitter following. (1) Marcus said something like: 'I've decided to just go ahead and actually admire the guy instead of faking it, because it'll read as more genuine in the interview, and wanting the job is a perfectly good reason to admire him.' (2) Lena, sitting next to him, said wanting the job is a reason to want to feel admiration, not a reason to think the founder is actually admirable -- those are different things, and he was sliding between them. (3) Marcus said a reason is a reason: if getting the job counts in favor of having the attitude, that's a reason for the attitude, full stop, and she hadn't said why the 'kind' of reason should matter to whether it counts. (5) Marcus said fine, but then she owes him an actual line between the 'right' and 'wrong' kind of reason that doesn't just cash out to 'the right kind is the kind that's true,' cuz every time he's seen someone try to draw that line it collapses into circular.
