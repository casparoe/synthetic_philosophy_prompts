Ok so I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole on information cascades / rational herding lately (started from an old Overcoming Bias post, then went down the econ literature, the Bikhchandani/Hirshleifer/Welch model if I'm remembering the names right), and the thing that strikes me every time is that basically every worked example is the exact same setup: an urn with mostly red or mostly blue balls, people privately draw one ball each and then announce a guess in sequence, and after two or three people have guessed "red" everyone just says "red" regardless of their own draw. It's a genuinely wild result -- a whole crowd converging on the same answer while every single person in it is being perfectly Bayesian -- but the urns make it feel bloodless and toy-ish, and I think fiction would land the idea alot better than another worked example.

Could you write me a short story (a page or two, doesn't need to be longer) that dramatizes an honest-to-god information cascade, not just generic conformity or peer pressure? I want it to actually be about people acting one at a time, each with their own private and imperfect read on some ambiguous, high-stakes situation, each mostly seeing what everyone before them decided rather than why -- and I want at least one character to explicitly walk through the reasoning of overriding their own gut read because it seems implausible that several people before them would independently be wrong. Bonus points if the ending shows how fragile the whole thing was, like one late piece of hard evidence undoing a consensus that in hindsight was only ever really resting on the first mover's shaky signal. Setting-wise I don't care, just please, anything but urns and restaurants -- I keep imagining something like a small crew on an isolated station or a long, silent transit where people have to radio in their read on a strange signal one by one without ever seeing each other's raw data, but take it wherever feels right.
