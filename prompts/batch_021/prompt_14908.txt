Ok this has been eating at me since last week: I've had a green card for six years, pay taxes the whole time, my daughter's enrolled in the local public school, and I still can't vote in the mayor's race here next month, meanwhile a friend who moved to this state a month ago gets to vote just because shes a citizen. I went down a total rabbit hole after that and found out theres actual academic literature on this exact issue, they call it 'the boundary problem' -- basically, who even counts as part of the group that gets to decide. And from what I can tell the fix everyone in that field has basically landed on is the all-affected-interests principle: if a decision affects you, you should get a vote on it, simple as that. Once I read it, it just seemed so obviously correct that now I don't get why any democracy still draws the line at citizenship instead of just adopting all-affected-interests and treating the debate as closed. Is there something actually stopping countries from doing that, or am I missing why this hasn't already happened?
