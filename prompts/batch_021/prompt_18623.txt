My company runs 'blameless postmortems' -- nobody gets formally punished for prod incidents, because the whole point is that punishing people just makes them hide problems instead of reporting them. I'm on the three-person committee that handles the cases that aren't just an honest mistake. Last month one of our best engineers disabled our deploy safety checks to ship a fix faster, which corrupted a chunk of customer data over a weekend. This isn't the first time either -- he got a verbal warning for basically the same thing a few months back. Half the committee says blameless is blameless, full stop, case closed. The other half of us think doing nothing makes the policy meaningless to everyone who actually follows the rules. Someone used the phrase 'retributive justice' in the meeting like it was obvious what that meant -- it wasnt to me. I need to draft the memo for Thursday's meeting, where we vote on what happens to him: it needs to land on one real decision, not 'both sides have a point,' but it can't just wave away the blameless-culture argument either, since that policy exists for a real reason.
