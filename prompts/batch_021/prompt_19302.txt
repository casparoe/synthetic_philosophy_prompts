I am a retired physiologist whose career focused on cortical electrophysiology, and in retirement I have been working through the philosophical literature that grew out of Sperry and Gazzaniga's split-brain research. Two accounts of the phenomenon strike me as particularly well worked out: Tim Bayne's "switch model," according to which a commissurotomized patient possesses, at any given moment, at most one integrated stream of consciousness that alternates between the hemispheres depending on task demands, and Elizabeth Schechter's proposal that such patients house two distinct conscious subjects while nonetheless remaining, in the ordinary sense, a single person. Please compare these two positions, addressing in particular how each would need to accommodate Pinto and colleagues' 2017 finding of residual interhemispheric integration in commissurotomized patients. On balance, which of the two accounts do you think handles the full range of experimental and everyday behavioral evidence more successfully, and why?
