Over the past year I have become increasingly absorbed in debates about free will and divine providence, mostly through apologetics videos and long threads on Catholic and Reformed forums, and that is how I first ran into Molinism. I understand the basic idea of "middle knowledge" well enough: prior to any decision to create anything, God already knows, for every possible free creature placed in every possible set of circumstances, exactly what that creature would (not merely could) freely choose to do, and this lets him providentially arrange history simply by choosing which circumstances to actualize, without ever forcing anyone's will. What caught my attention is that Luis de Molina presented this as little more than a faithful working-out of what Thomas Aquinas already held, while the Dominicans of his own day, Domingo Bañez above all, treated it as a betrayal of Aquinas and defended a rival account in which God "premoves" the will to its act as the genuinely Thomist position. The argument got heated enough that Rome convened a formal commission, the Congregatio de Auxiliis, to settle it, and after years of hearings the papacy simply declined to rule, letting both sides go on calling themselves Aquinas's true heirs, even though Aquinas himself had been dead for more than three centuries by the time any of this happened.

This is the question I have not been able to set aside: setting the popes and the commission to one side, what would Aquinas himself have said if he had been shown Molina's scientia media on one hand and Bañez's premotion on the other? I am not looking for "scholars still disagree" as an answer. I would like you to work through how Aquinas actually writes about God's knowledge of future contingents, his causal relation to the human will, and predestination, and use that to reach a real verdict: which side, if either, has the stronger claim to be continuing what Aquinas himself was doing, and at what specific point would he say the losing side departs from him? A split verdict is fine too, if you think Aquinas would have rejected the letter of both later systems while standing closer in spirit to one of them, but I would like you to commit to a specific, defended position rather than leaving the matter open.
