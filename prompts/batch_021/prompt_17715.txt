I'm teaching our graduate philosophy of language seminar this term, and we just spent two weeks in the weeds of two-dimensional semantics -- Chalmers and Jackson's primary/secondary intension machinery, epistemically possible scenarios, the works -- as the big rescue operation for explaining how something like "Hesperus is Phosphorus" can be necessary yet a posteriori. Half my students have quietly started treating the primary/secondary distinction as just Kripke with extra steps, though, and none of them can really say why someone like Soames spent a whole book (Reference and Description) arguing the machinery doesn't actually earn its keep -- that it either smuggles in a description of the "whatever satisfies such-and-such" variety, which reheats all of Kripke's own ignorance-and-error objections to classical descriptivism, or it retreats to something so thin and metalinguistic that it can't be doing the cognitive-significance work Frege wanted done in the first place. (Possibly I'm just procrastinating on grading their midterms by obsessing over this instead.)

Could you write me a dialogue between a committed two-dimensionalist and a Millian direct-reference theorist that actually gets into that specific bind, instead of just restating "Kripke refuted descriptivism" for the hundredth time? Have them go at it over "Hesperus is Phosphorus" specifically (swap in "water is H2O" instead if you think that one bites harder) -- I want the Millian pressing on where exactly the primary intension of "Hesperus" is even supposed to come from, and whether it can be pinned down without either (a) presupposing a description that an ordinary competent speaker might not have, or might have wrong, or (b) collapsing into a trivial metalinguistic gloss that can't distinguish the epistemic status of "Hesperus is Phosphorus" from that of "Hesperus is Hesperus." And I want the two-dimensionalist actually engaging with that rather than just re-asserting the formalism louder. Neither one should be a pushover -- honestly i'd rather it end in a real stalemate than a tidy resolution, since that's closer to where i think the literature actually stands, and it'd make a much better handout than yet another summary of the primary/secondary distinction.
