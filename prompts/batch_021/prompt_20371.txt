For some time now I have been working through the ethics of population growth on my own, largely prompted by debates over whether it is responsible to bring children into an already resource-strained world. In the course of that reading, I came across Derek Parfit's repugnant conclusion, which appears to show that a utilitarian who simply totals up everyone's wellbeing is committed to saying that an enormous population whose members' lives are barely worth living would be better than a much smaller population living wonderfully. This struck me as a genuine flaw in utilitarian population ethics, but then I read that philosophers can sidestep the whole problem simply by judging outcomes according to average wellbeing per person rather than the sum total. Since switching to that average measure seems to make the paradox vanish, doesn't this mean the repugnant conclusion was really just a technical quirk of one particular way of aggregating utility, rather than a genuinely deep problem for population ethics as a whole?
