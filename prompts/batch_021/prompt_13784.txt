My neighbors Mark and Sara got into an argument at dinner last week that I still haven't been able to settle for myself. Mark had strung a live wire along the top of his garden fence to keep next door's dog out, then, worried the neighbor's kids might grab it, added a proximity sensor and relay that cuts the power a split second before anything touches the wire and restores it right after. The side effect is that the dog stopped getting shocked too and kept coming back, which is what set off the argument.

(1) Mark said that since the wire has never once shocked anything, calling it "live" doesn't really mean much -- he might as well not have wired it to the charger at all.

(2) Sara said that's wrong: current runs through the wire continuously except for the split second around contact, so it's exactly as live as any ordinary fence wire; the cutoff is a safety feature bolted onto a live wire, not proof there's no charge.

(3) Mark replied that if that's the standard, then a dead wire hooked to a hidden timer that sends a pulse through it for one second every night at 3 a.m., while everyone's asleep, would count as "live" too by Sara's logic -- which seems like a distinction with no practical import, like calling a locked door "open" because the hinges still swing.

(4) Sara answered that no cutoff is perfectly reliable -- sensors lag, relays stick, power flickers -- and that's exactly why the current matters: it determines what would happen in the nearby cases where the cutoff is a touch slow or briefly fails, and Mark's wire would hurt someone in exactly those cases, while the 3 a.m. wire wouldn't, no matter what its timer happened to be doing. That's not a technicality, she said; it's the reason an electrician would treat the two wires completely differently.

Could you go through these four moves and say who has the better argument at each one, rather than just declaring an overall winner? I'd also like to know if there's something important that both of them are missing.
