A student of mine described a habit in seminar last week that has stuck with me. She has a cousin whose moral judgment she trusts more than her own, so whenever she is genuinely unsure whether something would be wrong—breaking up with a partner by text message, keeping cash a cashier handed her by mistake, reporting a friend's minor cheating—she simply asks him and does whatever he recommends, and by her own account this has never once led her astray. A classmate objected that this amounts to a kind of moral cop-out even granting that the cousin's advice is always sound, but she remained unpersuaded: if the advice is reliably good, why should taking it count against her? I would be curious to hear how you would adjudicate the exchange, and in particular whether your assessment would change if she deferred just as heavily to a friend's judgment about medical or financial matters.
