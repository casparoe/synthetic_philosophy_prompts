After a seminar on the semantics of conditionals, two of my graduate students, Nora and Sam, kept arguing in the hallway afterward over a version of Adams's well-known minimal pair: "If Oswald did not kill Kennedy, someone else did" (which seems clearly true) against "If Oswald had not killed Kennedy, someone else would have" (which seems clearly false). (1) Nora argued that the pair shows indicative and counterfactual conditionals cannot be handled by one semantic theory: the indicative's assertability tracks the conditional probability of the consequent on the antecedent, per Adams's thesis, while the counterfactual is evaluated by ranking whole possible worlds for overall similarity to ours; since the antecedent and consequent are held fixed and only the mood changes, whatever explains the flip in truth value must be a difference in kind, not degree. (2) Sam objected that Stalnaker's selection-function semantics already covers this with a single conditional connective: mood does not switch mechanisms, it switches what the selection function is sensitive to — worlds epistemically compatible with the conversational common ground for the indicative, worlds that are objectively or causally similar for the counterfactual — so one semantic rule suffices. (3) Nora replied that this concedes too little, since Lewis's triviality results show that if indicative conditionals get truth conditions from any worlds-ranking semantics at all, their probabilities cannot, in general, equal the corresponding conditional probabilities the way Adams's thesis says they do and our actual judgments confirm; given how well supported that thesis is, it is the idea of truth conditions via a selection function that should go, not just some detail of Stalnaker's version. (4) Sam answered that the triviality results only refute the thesis read as an exact identity holding for every probability function, not the very idea of truth conditions for indicatives; following Jackson, one can keep a truth-conditional semantics and explain the tight link to conditional probability pragmatically, via a robustness constraint on assertion, so the contrast is real but pragmatic rather than semantic. Neither would budge, and they've both since cornered me to settle it.
