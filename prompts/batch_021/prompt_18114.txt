During my career as a physicist, I spent a good deal of time on maximum-entropy reconstruction of spectra from incomplete data, so I felt an odd jolt of recognition on meeting van Fraassen's 'Judy Benjamin' puzzle after taking up philosophy in retirement. A soldier's position is uniformly distributed, a priori, over four sectors formed by crossing two territories, Red and Blue, with two company areas, First and Second. Headquarters radios her exactly one piece of information: conditional on her being in Red territory, she is three times as likely to be in the Second Company's area as in the First's. Updating by minimizing relative information (Kullback-Leibler divergence) from the uniform prior, subject to just that constraint, drives her credence that she is in Red territory at all, call it P(Red), down from one half to roughly 0.467, despite the message never mentioning Blue. Van Fraassen takes this as a decisive objection to entropy-based updating, since on his view a message about the internal breakdown of Red territory alone has no business moving credence in Red versus Blue. My own hunch, watching the arithmetic move, is that this objection quietly smuggles in some assumption the message was never obliged to honor, though I cannot state cleanly what that assumption is. Please argue that the shift in P(Red) is exactly what coherence requires, and that van Fraassen's objection ultimately fails.
