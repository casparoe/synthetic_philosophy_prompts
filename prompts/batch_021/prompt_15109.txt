Ok so I went down a rabbit hole this weekend after a philosophy podcast episode on "metaphysical grounding" - apparently that's different from causation, it's more about what makes what "exist in virtue of" what, if that makes sense. Basic idea seems to be that some facts/things are grounded in more fundamental facts/things, e.g. a chemistry fact is grounded in physics facts, a fact about a table is grounded in facts about the particles arranged table-wise, etc. Kinda reminded me of how at work a high level API call eventually bottoms out into a pile of syscalls, except apparently in metaphysics its not obvious there IS a bottom.

That's the part that's been bugging me since: does a grounding hierarchy have to terminate somewhere, in a layer of totally fundamental stuff that isn't grounded in anything further (like a base case in recursion), or could it just descend forever with no floor at all - regress all the way down? And if there's no guaranteed floor, does calling something "fundamental" even mean anything anymore? Apparently there's also an even weirder third option where the grounding chain loops back on itself instead of ever bottoming out, which sounds to me like it should just be incoherent (like a circular import in code, or a function that calls itself to define itself), but apparently some serious metaphysicians take that option seriously too.

I never took a philosophy class in college (straight CS major) so most of this is new territory for me, but I'd love an actual essay laying out the strongest arguments for grounding needing to bottom out in something fundamental (foundationalism) vs. it not needing to / maybe not being able to guarantee that (infinitism, and briefly whatever the "loopy" option is called), and then don't just leave it at "reasonable people disagree" - actually pick a side and defend it like you mean it. A friend doing a philosophy PhD told me this is a genuinely live, unresolved debate right now and not just me misunderstanding something, so I want a real verdict, not a cop-out.
