and here's what she turned in:

"Strawson's move in 'Freedom and Resentment' is basically to say both sides of the debate are asking the wrong question. Optimists (mostly utilitarians) say we can keep blaming and punishing people even under determinism because doing so is socially useful - it deters people and helps reform them. Pessimists say that's not enough, real responsibility needs people to have genuinely been able to do otherwise, which determinism rules out. Strawson thinks both sides are trying to justify our practices from the outside, when really they're grounded in something we already do naturally, what he calls the 'reactive attitudes' - resentment, gratitude, indignation and so on. Morality is basically built out of these reactions rather than some abstract metaphysical fact about free will, and Strawson's own compatibilism comes down to saying that being free just means acting on your own wants without somebody forcing you, which fits fine with determinism since determinism doesn't involve anybody forcing you to want what you want.

He also draws a line between excuses and exemptions. Excuses are things like 'he didn't mean to' or 'he was pushed' - they show the act doesn't really express what the person is like on the inside, so resentment isn't called for. Exemptions are different, they're reasons for viewing the whole person differently for a while or permanently, like if theyre a small child or seriously mentally ill, and here we switch to what Strawson calls the 'objective attitude' instead of getting mad at them. Determinism being true generally isn't the same kind of thing as one of these specific excuses or exemptions, so it can't just slide in and make the objective attitude appropriate for literally everyone.

Where this leaves us though is actually pretty bleak once you follow it all the way through: since none of us picked the character or upbringing that made us the sort of people who react and act the way we do, nobody is ever truly responsible for anything in the deepest sense - we just aren't able to drop the reactive attitudes even though they were never really justified in the first place."
