Last night a friend and I spent nearly two hours arguing about the Judy Benjamin problem after it came up in a reading group on formal epistemology, and we never actually settled anything. The setup, for reference, is this: Judy Benjamin is dropped into a region that is, a priori, equally likely to be any of four quadrants — she is in Red or Blue territory, and within whichever territory she is in, she is in the area held by Headquarters Company or by Second Company. She then receives a garbled radio message telling her only that, conditional on her being in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 that she is in the Second Company area rather than the Headquarters area; the message says nothing at all comparing Red to Blue directly.

My friend's position was that van Fraassen's own proposed fix — updating to the distribution that minimizes relative information (cross-entropy) with respect to the prior, subject to that new conditional constraint — is essentially forced on us once we grant that ordinary Jeffrey conditionalization has no direct purchase on conditional evidence of this kind, and that the resulting drop in her credence that she is in Red territory, even though the message never mentions Red versus Blue, is simply the correct if surprising upshot of taking the evidence seriously. I pushed back: since the minimum-relative-information answer depends on how the space of possibilities is carved into elementary outcomes in the first place, and shifts under seemingly innocuous redescriptions of the very same information, the method seems to smuggle in a symmetry assumption the radio message never licensed — so the "correct" update is underdetermined rather than uniquely fixed.

Could you help settle this? Specifically, for someone comfortable with ordinary Bayesian conditionalization but with no background in probability kinematics, please explain: (1) why the radioed conditional resists being handled as an ordinary Jeffrey shift over some pre-given partition, so that some further updating principle is needed at all; (2) the actual mechanism by which minimizing relative information ends up moving credence away from Red territory, in a way that's genuinely intuitive rather than just a restatement of the entropy formula; and (3) whether the parametrization-dependence worry I raised is a real problem for van Fraassen's proposal, or whether there's a principled way of fixing the "right" space of elementary possibilities that dissolves it. If you have a view on which of us had the better of the argument, I'd like to hear that too.
