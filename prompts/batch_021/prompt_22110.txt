As the resident novelist in the family, I've been asked to give the toast at my sister's wedding next month. She's a philosopher of physics who built her career -- dissertation, first book, tenure case -- on Einstein's hole argument, defending the relationalist conclusion that spacetime points have no identity apart from the web of relations they stand in. Half the guests will be her colleagues in philosophy of physics, who will notice immediately if I get the argument wrong; the rest will be family, who should never have to hear the word "diffeomorphism." The trouble is that the usual reassuring wedding-speech move -- telling the couple that they'll always be fundamentally themselves, no matter what -- is close to the thesis about identity she has spent a decade arguing against. Write me a toast, five to seven minutes long, that takes her actual position seriously, uses the hole argument as real material rather than a punchline, and still leaves the room moved rather than lectured at.
