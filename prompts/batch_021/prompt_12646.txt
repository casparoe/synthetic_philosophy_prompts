Been down a free-will rabbit hole lately and keep running into Galen Strawson's 'Basic Argument' against moral responsibility. Most explainers treat it like a fancy version of hard determinism, but Strawson insists its supposed to work even if determinism is false -- even against agent-causal libertarians (the Randolph Clarke/Timothy O'Connor 'the self can be an uncaused cause' crowd) who think real indeterminism could ground genuine self-determination. Can you actually build that argument rather than summarize it: the case for why tossing indeterminism into the causal story does nothing to stop the regress about being responsible for 'the way you are,' so the libertarian ends up in exactly the same boat as the determinist. Bonus points if you can work in how odd it is that his own dad, P.F. Strawson, made his name partly by basically sidestepping this exact regress in 'Freedom and Resentment.'
