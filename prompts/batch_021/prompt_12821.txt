I'm currently taking a seminar on aesthetics, and something from my part-time job this week has stayed with me in light of what we've been discussing in class. I write short recommendation blurbs for my university's film society newsletter, and this month, the deadline arrived before I had a chance to attend the only screening of one of the films on the list, so I never actually saw it myself. I put the blurb together from what two friends told me about the film and from several published reviews, phrasing it in my own words as, for instance, "a quietly devastating study of grief." My co-editor felt that something was off about presenting this as my own assessment of the film's merits when I had no direct experience of it whatsoever, while I pointed out that we routinely take other people's word for all kinds of things we have not verified ourselves, and I could not see why this case should be any different. Was there something illegitimate about writing the blurb the way I did, and if so, what exactly sets this case apart from the ordinary trust we place in other people's word on other matters?
