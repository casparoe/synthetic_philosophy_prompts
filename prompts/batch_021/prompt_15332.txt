Since retiring from a career in theoretical physics, I have been working through the game theory literature, and something has been puzzling me. Robert Aumann's notion of correlated equilibrium already establishes that rational players who condition their strategies on a shared signal can coordinate on outcomes that neither would reach by reasoning about their own payoff in isolation. Douglas Hofstadter's argument that "superrational" players should cooperate in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma seems to rest on precisely the same logic: each player reasons that the other, being equally rational and facing an identical problem, will arrive at the same decision, so both may as well cooperate. Why, then, do game theorists continue to treat superrationality as a controversial, non-standard proposal rather than simply a special case of Aumann's correlated equilibrium?
