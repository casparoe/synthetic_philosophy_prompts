So I was covering a longevity-science conference last month and ended up half-eavesdropping on a genuinely heated hallway argument between two of the invited speakers right after their panel on whether aging itself should count as a disease. Been turning it over since and can't decide who actually won each round, so wanted an outside read.

Renata (she's a biogerontologist) opened with: aging can't be a disease, because pretty much everything people point to — joint wear, hormone decline, immune senescence — is universal to the species past a certain age, and disease by definition means departing from how the species typically functions, not just being on the typical trajectory.

Jonah (he does philosophy of medicine) shot back that this is a sleight of hand: on that logic, if some toxin gave literally everyone a brutal degenerative illness from birth, the suffering would stop counting as disease the moment it became universal enough, which gets the direction of explanation backwards — severity and harm are what should make something a disease, not how rare it is.

Renata answered that a harm-only definition breaks just as badly the other way, since it would turn any unpleasant but perfectly normal state — grief, a bruise healing, plain exhaustion — into a disease, so you need some biological baseline that doesn't just track how bad things feel, or 'disease' has no edges at all.

Jonah replied that her baseline isn't neutral either: the second you define the reference class as 'people this age,' you've already guaranteed that anything common enough by 80 counts as healthy no matter how much function is gone since 20, so the 'neutral biology' is quietly smuggling in the conclusion she wants.

Renata's comeback was that comparing an 80-year-old to a 20-year-old proves too much in the other direction too: it implies everyone's technically been mildly diseased since their twenties, which just empties 'disease' of any real contrast.

Neither budged, and honestly both of them looked a little embarassed by how worked up they'd gotten once the next panel started. Could you go through this move by move, saying who has the better of it at each exchange rather than just picking an overall winner, and flag anything you think both of them are missing? End with one line: Winner: Renata or Winner: Jonah, whoever's overall position held up best across the exchange.
