I've read enough rationalist blogs to have internalized "shut up and multiply" as the right response to scope-insensitivity, but a case from my search-and-rescue volunteering keeps nagging at me. Our unit's one helicopter got two emergency calls at once during a storm window that would close the mountain pass by nightfall: a solo skier buried in a slide who would suffocate within the hour, and a van of five hikers stranded on an exposed ledge who would die of exposure in roughly the same window. Either rescue was equally certain to succeed, and equally fatal if we didn't come, and we could only reach one site before the storm closed in. The committee sent the helicopter to the five without much debate, but it's bothered me ever since that the skier's claim to being rescued doesn't seem any weaker just because five strangers elsewhere happened to need the same helicopter that night. Would it actually have been more defensible to give him equal, or at least proportional, odds through some kind of lottery, rather than letting the headcount alone decide who lives — or is that just scope-insensitivity talking?
