In my political philosophy seminar on Marx, we've been working through his account of exploitation in Capital: over the course of the working day, laborers produce more value than they receive back in wages, and the capitalist appropriates that surplus value. This makes it sound as though exploitation, on Marx's definition, is simply a matter of workers not being paid the full value of what they produce. But then I don't see how a fully democratic worker cooperative would escape the charge, since even there the members would withhold part of their own output as retained earnings for equipment, maintenance, and reserve funds rather than distributing everything as pay. Doesn't Marx's own definition commit him to saying that the cooperative's members are exploiting themselves, in essentially the same way employees are exploited under a capitalist employer? And if so, doesn't that mean his theory implies that abolishing capitalist ownership could never actually eliminate exploitation, only change who benefits from it?
