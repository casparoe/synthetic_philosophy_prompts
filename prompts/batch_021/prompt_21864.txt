About a year ago, after panic-selling my portfolio during a downturn and then losing money twice more chasing "sure thing" tips from friends, I gave my partner sole authority over the savings we are setting aside for a house down payment and told her, unambiguously, that she was never to release funds early at my request, no matter what reason I gave her in the moment. I wanted an unconditional rule rather than a judgment call she could make case by case, including for anything that might look like a great opportunity, because I knew that whatever tempted me next would feel just as convincing as the tips that had already burned me. Last week, a close friend invited me to invest in a business expansion that, after weeks of genuine diligence, looks sound, but the offer closes in ten days and my only capital is locked in that account. My partner has refused to make an exception, exactly as instructed, and considers this proof that the arrangement works. Was she right to hold firm regardless of how good my present reasoning looks, or does a rule that cannot tell a genuine opportunity from the impulse it was built to stop mean that the whole arrangement was a mistake?
