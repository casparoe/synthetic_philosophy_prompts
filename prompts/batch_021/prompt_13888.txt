So in my English class we did this short unit on Latin American short fiction and read Borges's "The Garden of Forking Paths," and I got kind of obsessed and went down a rabbit hole reading more of his stuff on my own. I found this essay called "A New Refutation of Time" -- it was originally in a collection called Otras inquisiciones but I read it in that English anthology Labyrinths that mixes a bunch of his stories and essays together. It's not really a story, it's an actual philosophy essay where he uses Berkeley (nothing exists except as perceived) and Hume (there's no continuous self, just a bundle of perceptions one after another) to argue that if neither matter nor the self really persists, there's no good reason to think there's one single continuous time that everything happens "in" either -- he even brings in the old Zhuangzi butterfly dream thing as an example. He's even got this little intro note where he points out that calling it a "new" refutation is already kind of self-defeating since "new" is itself a time-word, which felt like such a Borges move. But the part that's actually stuck with me is the ending: after all that, he basically admits that even though he just spent the whole essay "proving" time and the self aren't real, he still feels time as this violent thing (river, tiger, fire) sweeping him along and consuming him anyway, and the last line is basically him saying the world is real whether he likes it or not, and so, unfortunately, is he. My friend Dana says he's just being cute/ironic there, but I think he means it, and that's what makes it so unsettling to me.

Could you write me a short story (a page or two is fine, it doesn't need to be super long) that's built around that exact move? Like, a character who works out, through some real argument of their own -- it doesn't have to literally be Berkeley and Hume, you can dress it up however you want -- that time and the self aren't really real, and who actually believes it, and yet who still feels completely trapped and consumed by time anyway, and has to just sit there with that contradiction instead of getting to resolve it neatly. Please don't just retell "Garden of Forking Paths" or "The Circular Ruins" with the names changed, though if you wanna sneak in some of his other recurring images (labyrinths, mirrors, dreams inside dreams, that sort of thing) as background texture that would be cool. Bonus points if you can get some of that dry, fake-scholarly, footnote-y voice he does instead of writing it like a normal short story.
