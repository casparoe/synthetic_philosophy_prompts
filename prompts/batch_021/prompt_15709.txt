I spent my career as a field ecologist and only got properly into philosophy after retiring -- currently working through Sober and Okasha, slowly, dictionary open half the time. Something from my old PhD program's reunion dinner last month is still bugging me.

Two people I trained alongside, Renata and Iain, ended up on opposite sides of the units-of-selection question over dessert -- she spent thirty years on termite colonies, he went into theoretical population genetics. As best I can reconstruct it:

(1) Renata said if you partition fitness with the Price equation into within- and between-group covariance, in her termite data the between-group term dominates even though altruists always do worse than selfish types inside every single group. That's Simpsons paradox doing real explanatory work, not bookkeeping -- the group is a genuine unit of selection.

(2) Iain said run those same colonies through Hamilton's rule with the actual relatedness structure and you get the identical prediction -- the two formalisms are duals, as Queller and Frank showed decades ago. Calling the group story independently explanatory just means refusing to notice you've described one process twice.

(3) Renata granted that, but said equivalence doesnt crown either level more fundamental -- that's an interpretive add-on the algebra doesn't force -- and her termites are diploid, not haplodiploid, so whatever drives their eusociality can't be Hamilton's old "supersister" relatedness bonus, which fits a group-structured story better.

(4) Iain said that's a fair strike against one hypothesis about Hymenoptera, not kin selection generally, and reminded her that the one time someone inflated a point like that into a wholesale case against Hamilton's rule -- the Nowak/Tarnita/Wilson paper -- its additivity assumption got dismantled pretty thoroughly by the people who checked the math.

(5) Renata said fine, but nobody at the table was rederiving that from scratch over dessert, and in practice the group-selection framing is the one generating novel predictions about colony-level traits like caste ratios -- it earns its keep regardless of who's "more fundamental."

They went another round or two and never got anywhere, apparently as always. Could you go through this move by move, say who's got the better of it at each point, and tell me what you think both of them are still missing?
