I'm a PhD student in philosophy currently drafting a dissertation chapter on theories of propositional unity. Over the past month, a small reading group I run with two other graduate students has been working through F. H. Bradley's regress argument in *Appearance and Reality* and its long afterlife in the analytic tradition's attempts to explain what makes a proposition more than a mere list of its constituents. What has frustrated us is that most treatments we've found walk through the regress, then Frege's response via the unsaturatedness of concepts, then perhaps Russell's own difficulties in the *Principles*, and stop there, as though the question had been settled a century ago. There is, however, a further chapter to this story: philosophers such as Peter Hanks and Scott Soames have argued that the appeal to unsaturated concepts never really did the explanatory work Frege needed from it, and have proposed instead that propositional unity should be located in the cognitive or linguistic act of predication itself, rather than in any special feature belonging to the proposition's constituents.

Rather than read yet another survey that lays these two families of solutions out side by side, I would like to see them argued out directly, by two philosophers of equal sophistication. Could you write a dialogue between a Neo-Fregean, who maintains that treating concepts and relations as inherently "unsaturated," or gap-like, both halts Bradley's regress at its very first step and secures the unity of the proposition, and an act-theorist in the vein of Hanks and Soames, who denies that any such special entity is needed at all and instead grounds unity in an agent's act of predicating a property of an object.

I would like each character to genuinely press on the other's weak points rather than simply restate their own position. At a minimum, the act-theorist should raise some version of Frege's own "concept horse" difficulty against the unsaturatedness view, and the Neo-Fregean should push back with the suspicion that an act of predication itself requires some unsaturated ingredient to bind subject and property together, so that the act-based theory has not actually escaped Bradley's regress but has merely relocated it one level up, into the theory of acts. Please have them respond to what the other has actually said over several exchanges, rather than talking past one another, and end the dialogue with the two of them isolating exactly what would have to be true for either solution to count as genuinely successful, even if they do not settle which one wins.
