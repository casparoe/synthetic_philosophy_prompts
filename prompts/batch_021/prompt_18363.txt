I'm doing a phd in logic/phil of math and this keeps bugging me: is worrying that ZFC is inconsistent basically the same kind of worry as worrying that PA is inconsistent, or are they different in kind? Godels second incompleteness theorem blocks both from proving their own consistency, sure, but Gentzen gave us a consistency proof for PA relative to transfinite induction up to epsilon-0, which feels like real evidence, wheras for ZFC the best we've got is relative consistency proofs that just lean on still-stronger assumptions (inaccessible cardinals etc) -- it's regress all the way up. Compare the two worries properly: where does the PA/ZFC analogy hold, where does it break, and is it actually rational for working mathematicians to be way more alarmed by 'PA turns out inconsistent' than by 'ZFC turns out inconsistent,' given how much ordinary math apparently doesn't even need ZFC's full strength (reverse math etc)?
