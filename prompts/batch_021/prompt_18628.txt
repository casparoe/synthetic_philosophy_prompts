I'm teaching Strawson's "Freedom and Resentment" this week and there's a move of his I want to pressure-test before I decide how to present it to my class. He insists that if determinism turned out to be true, that wouldn't just be the last, biggest item on the list of things -- compulsion, immaturity, being coerced, insanity, etc -- that already get people excused or exempted from our ordinary reactive attitudes. His view is that it's basically a different KIND of consideration, not just a bigger dose of the same kind, and that's what's supposed to block the inference from "we already excuse people for stuff beyond their control" to "so determinism excuses everyone, since then nothing anyone does is within their control." Can you write me a dialogue between two philosophers hashing this out -- one leaning hard on "no, this is just the same move generalized, and Strawson's kind/degree line is question-begging," the other defending the distinction with whatever Strawson's actual framework gives them, not just restating the claim. Doesn't need to be long, but I want both sides equally sharp and actually responding to what the other just said, not talking past each other -- something with real teeth I can put in front of students.
