So I fell down a rabbit hole on Nelson Goodman's grue thing — the predicate where something counts as grue if its green and we observe it before, say, 2050, or blue if we observe it in 2050 or later. Fine, I get why that wrecks naive induction, since "all emeralds are green" and "all emeralds are grue" are both equally confirmed by everything we've seen so far. What I'm less clear on is Goodman's actual proposed fix, "entrenchment" — and he's pretty explicit that it's NOT just "go with whichever predicate is simpler." Reading about it, entrenchment kinda smells like inductive bias / regularization in ML to me: you favor hypotheses (or features) based on how well they've generalized historically, not just on how well they fit the data sitting in front of you. Can you compare entrenchment to inductive bias in that sense, and tell me where the analogy actually holds up and where it breaks down? Also, seperately: doesn't entrenchment end up circular in basically the same way induction itself is supposed to be circular (justifying induction via induction), given that all it's really doing is favoring whatever predicates have worked before?
