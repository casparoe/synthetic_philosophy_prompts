I'm the student rep on my college's Enrollment & Housing Planning commitee, and admin wants our recommendation on whether to grow next falls incoming class by about 40%. The only way to fit everyone is to convert most common rooms and half our doubles into triples and push intro class sizes way up, so campus life gets noticeably worse for current students -- but roughly 400 more kids who'd otherwise be rejected outright get in instead. Half the committee thinks it's obvious: more people benefit than are hurt, so total good goes up and we should grow it. The other half doesn't disagree with the logic, just where it leads -- run that same reasoning again next year, and the year after, and eventually we're a packed diploma mill nobody's happy at, so something's wrong with it even if nobody can say what. We're doing a population ethics unit in my ethics class right now and I'm pretty sure this is basically that repugnant conclusion thing, but I can't work out what a philosophically honest answer to our actual situation looks like. Can you write the memo I'd bring to our vote next Thursday? It needs to recommend an actual class-size number (or a rule for picking one), give real reasons, and take the "race to the bottom" side seriously instead of just asserting that more total welfare automatically wins.
