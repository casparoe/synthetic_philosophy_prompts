I chair my university's Academic Integrity Board, which hears cases of suspected AI-generated coursework based on little more than a detector's probability score and stylistic anomalies flagged by the instructor. The board is split on whether we should demand more of that evidence before finding a student responsible when the mandated sanction is severe, such as dismissal for a repeat offense, than when it is mild, such as resubmission with no notation on the transcript. Some members see this as no different from a court requiring stronger proof before a harsher verdict; others insist that whether a given student actually used AI is a fact about the world that cannot depend on what will happen to them if we rule against them, so letting the sanction shape our confidence threshold corrupts the fact-finding rather than improving it. Draft a short policy memo, to take effect next term, that states whether and how our confidence threshold should track sanction severity, and that answers the second group's objection rather than simply overriding it.
