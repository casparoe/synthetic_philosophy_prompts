My friend and I spent like an hour arguing last night about the Zhuangzi butterfly dream thing (he dreams he's a butterfly, wakes up, and can't tell if he's a man who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly dreaming he's a man). We actually disagreed about whether the moral is "nothing really matters" or "everything's fine either way," but we both just took for granted that the passage is basically Zhuangzi doing Descartes' dreaming argument, just centuries earlier -- using the fact that you can't tell dreaming from waking to show that none of your beliefs about reality can be fully trusted. If that's right though, why doesnt Zhuangzi ever make the next move Descartes makes and go looking for some bedrock belief that survives the doubt? Was that just an oversight on his part, or is Daoism in general just not interested in that kind of secure-foundations-for-knowledge project?
