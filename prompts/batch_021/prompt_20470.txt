So two of my grad students got into a proper argument after last week's philosophy of physics seminar on Bell's theorem, and I still can't decide who came out ahead. Roughly how it went, paraphrasing from memory:

(1) Nadia said Bell's theorem plus the loophole-free experiments prove, flat out, that the world is nonlocal -- entangled particles end up correlated in ways nothing local could produce.

(2) Jon pushed back: what's actually ruled out is local realism, not locality on its own. Bell's derivation needs the extra premise that measurement outcomes are determinate independently of what's measured (counterfactual definiteness). Drop that instead, the way Everettians do, and you keep locality intact. So the honest conclusion is "no local realism," not "no locality."

(3) Nadia said that's a shell game: if outcomes aren't fixed in advance and still line up perfectly across a spacelike gap, you haven't removed the nonlocality, you've just relocated it from the hidden variable into the correlation itself.

(4) Jon answered there's an actual theorem doing the work here, not just semantics -- parameter independence (roughly, no-signaling) holds in QM, since nothing you do on your wing shifts the statistics on mine, while its outcome independence that gets violated. Since parameter independence is what relativity actually needs, calling the result "nonlocal" full stop overstates things.

(5) Nadia said "you can't signal with it" is a fact about what we're able to exploit, not about what's generating the correlation in the first place; the underlying process still has to be sensitive to something spacelike separated, and that's the violation that should worry us, whatever label we put on it.

(6) Jon said fine, but then she owes an account of why superdeterminist and retrocausal models, which keep both locality and realism by dropping the assumption that hidden states are independent of measurement choices, don't just win by default -- and gesturing at the correlations again isn't that account.
