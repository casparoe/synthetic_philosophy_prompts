A friend and I spent most of a long dinner arguing about mechanistic interpretability, a few days after I'd presented Fodor and Pylyshyn's critique of connectionism in the philosophy of mind seminar I'm taking this term. The conversation was prompted by Anthropic's "Scaling Monosemanticity" paper on Claude 3 Sonnet and the follow-up "Golden Gate Claude" demo, in which researchers turned up a single feature tied to the Golden Gate Bridge until the model became fixated on it, at one point insisting it physically was the bridge. My friend's position, which I ended up more or less conceding that night mainly because I couldn't find the right counterargument in the moment, was that once you can isolate a sparse, human-nameable direction in a model's activations that reliably and predictably drives bridge-related behavior when clamped, you have thereby shown that the network contains a real, symbol-like internal representation of "the Golden Gate Bridge," in whatever sense philosophers of mind care about when they ask whether a system has genuine representational content. On his view, this settles the realism question in interpretability's favor for good, and means that the remaining "black box" complaints about deep learning are now just practical bottlenecks, like dictionary size or unresolved polysemantic neurons, rather than anything conceptually unresolved.

I've had a few days to sit with this, and while I was fairly persuaded at the time, a couple of other people in the seminar think there is still a real gap between showing that a feature is interpretable and steerable and showing that it counts as a representation in the philosophically loaded sense, though none of us could pin down exactly what that gap consists of. Given that we can now find features in a model like Claude that are simultaneously legible to humans and causally sufficient to control its outputs, what, if anything, is still missing before we can say the network really represents the concept rather than merely behaving as if it does? And does the Golden Gate Claude case actually bear on the older dispute between classical, symbol-manipulating theories of mind and connectionist ones, or is that a separate question that this kind of empirical result cannot settle either way?
