Otto — one of my logic students asked me last week what it would actually *feel* like to be a mathematician living inside a countable model of ZFC who genuinely, provably believes her universe contains uncountable sets, and I didn't have a good answer beyond the usual dry resolution. So: write me a short story (800–1500 words) that dramatizes Skolem's paradox from the inside. It has to actually earn its ending on the real logic — that "uncountable" isn't an absolute property of a set but depends on which bijections happen to live inside your model, so the enumerating function is real, it's just not *there* with you — not just vibe out a generic "we're all living in a simulation" twist. And please, no character who conveniently stops to lecture another character on the Löwenheim–Skolem theorem; if the math needs explaining, it has to happen through what occurs in the story, not through dialogue that could've been cut and pasted from a textbook. Tone is your call, comic or bleak, I honestly don't care, just don't cheat the logic to buy yourself a neater ending.
