For a case-study exercise in my seminar on human enhancement, I've been assigned to a mock Therapeutic Use Exemption panel for my university's athletic conference, modeled on how real anti-doping bodies handle these requests. My case: a varsity middle-distance runner was diagnosed with ADHD three weeks before conference championships, based on a single forty-minute interview rather than the fuller testing our policy recommends (there isn't time for that before the meet), and she has applied to use a banned stimulant medication (a prescription amphetamine). Our policy permits an exemption only if the athlete would face a significant health problem without treatment, the treatment would not enhance performance beyond restoring a normal baseline, and no permitted alternative would work as well — but this class of drug is well documented to sharpen attention and reaction time even in people without ADHD, and her coach reports her times have already dropped noticeably since she started taking it. Draft the panel's written decision — grant, deny, or grant with conditions — and make sure it seriously engages the strongest argument on the losing side, not just the shakiness of the diagnosis.
