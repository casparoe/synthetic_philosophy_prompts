I write novels for a living, which means my working life consists of populating worlds that everyone, myself included, agrees are not real. Next month, for a podcast on philosophy and fiction, I am interviewing a philosopher who defends David Lewis's genuine modal realism: the thesis that every possible world is exactly as concrete and real as our own, merely isolated from it in space, time, and causation. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond a restatement of the position. I want at least one question on the 'incredulous stare,' and whether she agrees with Lewis that the intuition behind it carries no real argumentative weight; one on Robert Adams's objection that an indexical analysis of actuality cannot explain why the suffering in this world should matter to us more than equally real suffering elsewhere; and one asking -- since Lewis's own semantics for fiction analyzes what is 'true in the story' via similarity among worlds -- whether an inconsistent or radically underdescribed novel can be said to pick out a genuine possible world at all, or whether this pushes her toward the same 'fragmentation' move Lewis needed for stories that contradict themselves. For each, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give.
