Ok, this actually happened to me last semester and it's been bugging me ever since. I teach at a small college, and my nephew ended up in one of my intro classes. Partway through the semester I caught him copying a couple paragraphs almost word-for-word from an old paper he found online. Policy here is zero discretion - any suspected plagiarism gets referred straight to the integrity office, no exceptions, and I've sent other students down that road for less. With him, I just quietly had him redo the section and never filed anything. Now part of me feels like a hypocrite, but another part of me thinks treating him exactly like I'd treat a random student would've been it's own kind of wrong, not the neutral "fair" option everyone assumes it is. Was I just rationalizing favoritism, or is there something real behind the sense that what I owe him and what I owe a stranger in my classroom aren't measured on the same scale?
