I'm a backend engineer on a mental-health screening app. One feature we're building takes each user's questionnaire answers and an ongoing log of their check-ins, and sorts the user into a likely category -- "generalized anxiety," "major depressive episode," "sub-clinical," and so on -- which a clinician then reviews before deciding on follow-up care. In some reading on the philosophy of psychiatry, I came across Ian Hacking's idea of the "looping effect" of human kinds: his claim that classifying a kind of person is not like classifying a kind of rock, because people become aware of the label applied to them and respond to it -- living up to it, resisting it, gaming it -- thereby changing the very thing the category was meant to track, which then pushes experts to revise the category, which changes the people again, and so on. That looks like a deeper problem than the concept drift or feedback loops we already design against in ordinary ML systems. Please describe the most important implications of Hacking's looping effect for a classification system like ours, and what, if anything, a team like mine could realistically do about it.
