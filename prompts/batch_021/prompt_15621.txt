Ok, this one's been rattling around in my head since I watched it happen. I was shadowing two ER docs, Priya and Marcus, for a piece on how physicians actually reason through fuzzy evidence, and they got into a real argument over a borderline pulmonary embolism case. The CT came back ambiguous — radiologist's read was "probably positive, call it 70%" — and separately the D-dimer came back elevated, with the lab's own algorithm putting it at "60% consistent with clot." Same patient, same starting probability, same two pieces of evidence — but Marcus folded in the CT reading first and adjusted for the D-dimer after, while Priya did it in the opposite order, and they landed on noticeably different final probabilities for whether to anticoagulate.

Marcus said that's disqualifying: if your answer depends on the order you happened to look at the evidence in, the method is broken and has no business anywhere near patient care. Priya said not so fast — neither piece of evidence was ever a sure thing, you're not conditionalizing on a flat-out fact, you're doing something softer (she called it "probability kinematics" or somthing, I didn't totally follow), and nothing says that has to commute. Marcus came back with: fine, but then the "final" number is just an artifact of who happened to glance at the scan first, and that's not something you can put in a chart. Priya said ordinary Bayesian updating has the exact same arbitrariness built into the choice of prior, so singling out order is a double standard. Marcus's last word was that this is different: same prior, same two pieces of evidence, both of them updating "correctly" by their own lights, and they still end up with different numbers — that's disagreement manufactured by the bookkeeping, not by anything in the world.

They never settled it and I had to run to my next interview. Can you referee this turn by turn — who's actually right at each step, and what (if anything) they're both missing — and say whether there's a real fix here, or whether this is just a genuine, uncomfortable cost of reasoning with evidence that never arrives as flat-out certainty?
