I'm a philosophy PhD student drafting the anthropic-reasoning chapter of my dissertation on existential risk, and I'd like to firm up one step before I finalize the section. On my reading of Carter and Leslie's Doomsday Argument, the shift in probability toward earlier human extinction is driven by the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) rather than the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) that most technical treatments credit for it: SIA weights hypotheses in proportion to the number of observers each predicts, so my own comparatively low birth rank is exactly what SIA would lead me to expect under a "doom soon" hypothesis. Could you help me formalize that inference more carefully, and then assess how much damage the further, well-known objection to SIA (sometimes called the "presumptuous philosopher" problem)—that it seems to force excessive confidence in hypotheses positing enormous or infinite observer populations—actually does to the chapter's overall argument?
