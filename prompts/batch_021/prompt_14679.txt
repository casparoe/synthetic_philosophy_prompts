I spent my career in climate science, mostly on extreme-event attribution: estimating how much climate change altered the odds of a given heatwave or flood by comparing model runs made with and without anthropogenic forcing. That work always struck me as straightforwardly counterfactual, so on retiring I finally read David Lewis's counterfactual analysis of causation with real interest -- and then James Woodward's rival interventionist account, which left me unsure the two theories are even competing to explain the same cases, since "rerunning the model without the forcing" looks a good deal like a Woodwardian intervention on the forcing variable itself. Construct a single, fully specified causal scenario -- with a definite candidate cause c and effect e -- on which a careful application of Lewis's theory and a careful application of Woodward's theory reach different verdicts about whether c caused e. Identify exactly which feature of each theory is doing the work, and tell me which verdict, if either, you find more defensible.
