I'm a backend dev, not a philosopher, but I fell down a decision theory rabbit hole after a friend sent me a blog post about the Allais paradox -- the classic "would you rather have $1M guarenteed, or a 10% shot at $5M / 89% shot at $1M / 1% shot at nothing" setup, compared against a version of the same choice with the safety net stripped out. That led me to this Lara Buchak "risk-weighted expected utility" theory, which as I understand it (and I could be getting this a bit wrong) says the standard expected-utility view can't ever justify the Allais pattern -- being certainty-loving in the first choice but gamble-loving in the second -- no matter what utility function you plug in, because you can get from one choice to the other just by cancelling out an outcome that's common to both options. Buchak's move is apparently to bolt on a totally seperate "risk function" that reweights outcomes by their rank from worst to best rather than by raw probability, so that caring extra about the worst case is its own rational ingredient rather than smuggled-in irrationality or curvature of the utility function. From what I can tell, people who write about this go back and forth on whether it's a genuinely new theory of rationality or just expected utility wearing a trenchcoat, and on whether it survives having to make the same kind of choice more than once.

Could you write me a dialogue between two people hashing this out -- one an old-school expected-utility die-hard, the other a committed risk-weighted-expected-utility type -- who run into each other at a bar after a poker game and end up scribbling the Allais choices on a napkin to settle it? I want real back-and-forth where they actually respond to each other's specific points instead of just restating their own side louder: the EU person should have a real comeback to "risk-aversion is its own legitimate rational ingredient," and the REU person should have a real comeback to both "isn't this secretly just EU with extra steps" and "doesn't this fall apart once you have to make the choice repeatedly." Neither of them should be a pushover, and they don't need to resolve it by the end -- I just want it to read like an argument between two people who each actually know what they think and why.
