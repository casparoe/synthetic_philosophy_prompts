I'm a PhD student in philosophy of science, and I TA an undergraduate seminar on scientific method where Hempel's raven paradox comes up every year as a set reading (we work through the relevant sections of his 1945 essay "Studies in the Logic of Confirmation"). I've always presented the standard resolution as more or less settled, but an argument that came up in my reading group last week left me genuinely unsure whether I understand the paradox correctly, and I'd like to sort this out before I teach the unit again on Thursday.

The paradox rests on two premises that look individually innocuous: Nicod's criterion, that an observed instance of an F that is G confirms the generalization "all F are G," and the equivalence condition, that whatever confirms a statement must equally confirm anything logically equivalent to it. Since "all ravens are black" is equivalent to its contrapositive, "all non-black things are non-ravens," observing a red apple — a non-black non-raven — technically confirms the claim about ravens. The conclusion I keep arriving at is that confirmation, on this analysis, cannot be tracking anything about the subject matter of the evidence at all, only its logical form, and that this generalizes well past ravens: it seems like any observation should confirm any universal claim it does not straightforwardly contradict, which would make the ordinary idea that some evidence is simply more relevant than other evidence a kind of convenient fiction. The usual Bayesian move is to grant that the apple confirms the raven claim too, just by an infinitesimal amount, but this has always struck me as conceding the fiction rather than dissolving it. It seems simpler just to say Nicod's criterion is false and reach for something like Popper's falsificationism, which was never in the business of tallying confirming instances and so never runs into this trouble in the first place. Is that basically right, or have I gone wrong somewhere in a way that would leave the ordinary notion of evidential relevance more intact than I currently think?
