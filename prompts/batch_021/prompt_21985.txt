Okay so this isn't for a class, I've just been chewing on it with people in my Buddhist logic reading group and I want a smarter interlocuter than my group chat. The debate is about how seriously to take Nagarjuna's tetralemma (catuskoti) once he starts applying it reflexively to nirvana and to emptiness itself, rather than just to garden-variety stuff like motion or causation. One camp -- I think this basically traces back to a Priest and Garfield paper, might've been called "Nagarjuna and the Limits of Thought" or something close -- reads the MMK as genuinely committed to true contradictions at the limits of conceptual thought: when Nagarjuna says there's no difference at all between samsara and nirvana (near the end of the nirvana chapter, ch. 25 I think) or that emptiness itself has to be empty (there's a verse like that around ch. 13), the idea is he isn't just running through four options and rejecting all of them, he's positively asserting something both true and false, and you need a paraconsistent logic so the whole system doesn't explode via ex falso quodlibet. The other camp leans hard on the old "no thesis" line from the Vigrahavyavartani -- the verse where he basically says that if he had a thesis he'd be at fault, but he has none, so he isn't -- and thinks this is all a category error: the four horns are only ever doing dialectical work against an opponents hidden commitment to svabhava, none of the seemingly assertoric bits are first-personal claims Nagarjuna endorses, and you never actually have to touch classical logic.

I feel like I get both positions fine on their own, but I've never seen them actually forced to engage each other's strongest move instead of just talking past one another. Could you write me a dialogue between the two of them, like they've cornered each other after a talk and neither one's letting the other leave, where the dialetheist has to answer why this isn't just Nagarjuna running reductios on opponents' own terms the whole way through, and the no-thesis person has to answer why "it's all merely dialectical" survives the moment he says something that reads as a flat, first-person assertion (the samsara/nirvana line seems like the hardest case for them). I don't need it resolved, I just want real back and forth from two people who both actually know their MMK, not a strawman propping up either side.
