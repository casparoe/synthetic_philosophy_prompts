In the closing sections of the Third Essay of On the Genealogy of Morality, Nietzsche argues that the modern scientific "will to truth" is not the great opponent of the ascetic ideal it takes itself to be, but rather the ascetic ideal's own last and most spiritualized offspring: the same instinct that once drove ascetic self-denial now drives the scholar to renounce every comforting illusion for the sake of truth. He suggests that this will to truth, once it turns reflexively on itself and asks what value truth itself has, must eventually consume the very ideal from which it sprang. Please argue that this same reflexive turn undermines Nietzsche's own genealogical project. Specifically, argue that the unflinching honesty with which he traces our moral concepts back to ressentiment, the internalization of cruelty, and the ascetic ideal is itself an exercise of the ascetic will to truth he diagnoses, and that a genealogy applied consistently to itself would leave Nietzsche no independent standpoint from which his unmasking of morality's origins could claim any special authority over us.
