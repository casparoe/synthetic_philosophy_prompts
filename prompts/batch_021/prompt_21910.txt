I've been working on a school project about Ubuntu philosophy, and I'd like to get past the usual shorthand — "umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu," a person is a person through other persons — to the sharper disagreement it tends to paper over: the debate between Ifeanyi Menkiti and Kwame Gyekye about just how relational personhood really is. Menkiti holds that personhood is processual rather than given at birth: it is a normative status earned over a lifetime through moral conduct and incorporation into community, so that a newborn counts as less of a person than a respected elder, and even a habitual wrongdoer can fall short of full personhood. Gyekye, though still a communitarian, thinks this goes too far, and argues that every human being has some minimal, pre-social capacity for reason and choice simply in virtue of being human, which is what grounds rights and dignity independently of communal recognition. Could you write a dialogue between the two of them in which Gyekye presses Menkiti on what his view implies about the moral status of infants and the severely cognitively disabled, and Menkiti presses back that Gyekye's "pre-social capacities" just smuggle Western individualism back in under a communitarian label? I'd like them to genuinely engage with each other's objections rather than simply restate their own positions in turn — a dozen or so short exchanges is plenty, and it doesn't need to end in a tidy resolution.
