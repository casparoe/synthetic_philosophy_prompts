I got roped onto my co-op building's board this spring, and now I'm apparently the one drafting our e-bike/e-scooter battery policy before the vote at next month's meeting, which turned out to be way more heated then I expected. Backstory: there was a lithium battery fire in a building a few blocks from us over the winter (nobody died but a family had to be relocated for months), and our management company wants a flat-out ban on charging or storing lithium batteries anywhere in the building, units included. Problem is, a good chunk of our residents are delivery riders who depend on their e-bikes for income, and they're furious. Their main argument is that their batteries are UL-certified with a "battery management system" that cuts the current the second it senses overheating or a short, and that in every safety test those batteries have never actually caught fire, so calling them dangerous doesn't seem fair to them.

Some other board members, and honestly part of me too, think that argument is missing something. The reason those batteries have never caught fire isn't that the cells themselves are any less volatile, its that the chip cuts in the second anything starts going wrong. If that chip gets crushed, waterlogged, or is one of the cheap counterfeit swap-batteries a lot of riders use because the certified ones cost triple, there's nothing left standing between the cell and somebody's hallway. There's also a competing proposal floating around to skip the ban and just require fireproof charging cabinets in the basement instead, which does nothing to make the batteries themselves less flammable, it just contains whatever happens if something does go wrong. I genuinely don't know how to weigh "it's been safe in literally every test we've got" against "it's only ever been safe because something else stepped in every single time," and I've got skin in this game myself since I ride for deliveries on weekends.

Could you draft the actual memo I need to send the board before the vote? I need it to land on one concrete recommendation, full ban, BMS-certified-only, mandatory fireproof storage, some combination, whatever you think is actually defensible, with a rough timeline for phasing it in, and I need it to seriously engage with the "it's been tested and it's never failed" argument instead of just brushing past it, because that's exactly the line the delivery riders are going to keep making at the meeting and I don't want to walk in with something that doesn't answer their best point.
