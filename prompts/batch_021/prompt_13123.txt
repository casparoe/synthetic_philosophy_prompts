I'm the customer representative on my genetic-testing company's science communication ethics panel, the only non-scientist on it; everything I know about genetics I've learned on my own since joining. We're reviewing wording for a new traits report that marketing wants to open with "This is your personal genetic code—here's what yours says about your risk of anxiety and depression," because it tested best with users. Company policy only requires consumer-facing language to be "scientifically accurate and not misleading." Our staff geneticist objects that DNA isn't a code with a meaning already built into it: the meaning, she says, is something we impose afterward, and the phrasing will leave customers feeling these traits are simply fixed and unchangeable. Marketing responds that "genetic code" is standard scientific usage, not their invention, and that hedging it would only confuse people. Please draft the short recommendation memo I'll bring to the panel on Friday: say concretely what we should require of this report's language, take a real position on whether "code" is literally accurate here or just a convenient metaphor, and treat whichever side loses fairly rather than dismissing it as naive or pedantic.
