Leibniz's identity of indiscernibles basically says there cant be two things that share literally every property—if x and y are alike in every single way, they're actually just one thing. Max Black pushed back on this with a thought experiment where you imagine a universe with nothing in it but two perfectly identical spheres, arguing they're still two distinct objects even though nothing distinguishes them, not even relationally. Here's the thing though: quantum mechanics says something even weirder than Black's spheres—two electrons (or photons, whatever) aren't just hard to tell apart in practice, there's supposedly no fact at all about "which one is which." If you could somehow brief Leibniz on this, what do you think he'd say—would it vindicate his principle, refute it, or would he say it's not even engaging with what he actually meant by "indiscernible"? Curious what his strongest comeback would be.
