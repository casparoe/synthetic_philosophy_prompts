A close friend of mine, who is finishing a PhD in astrophysics, and I spent most of a camping trip a few nights ago locked in an argument that I still haven't managed to shake off. We had driven out to watch the Perseids, and somewhere between meteors the conversation drifted from what we were watching to the sheer scale behind it: hundreds of billions of galaxies, each home to hundreds of billions of stars, all of it here for stretches of time we can barely picture and set to continue, in some form, long after every trace of us has disappeared. His argument, made with real conviction and considerably more numbers than I had on hand, was that intellectual honesty requires "getting some perspective": measured against a backdrop that size, no relationship, career, or cause any of us is attached to carries any weight worth taking seriously, and anyone who still feels otherwise is simply refusing to do the arithmetic. I pushed back on instinct, but every time he asked me to name the actual flaw in his reasoning, rather than just register my discomfort with where it led, I came up short.

I have kept returning to it since, and I arrive at the same place each time. It feels as though he wants me to redraw every picture of my life strictly to the scale of the universe, and something in me insists that this is the wrong kind of drawing to make -- that being in proportion to the cosmos was never the right test for whether something matters -- but I cannot turn that instinct into an actual argument, as opposed to a piece of wishful thinking from someone who would simply rather not feel insignificant. I would rather not answer him with "it still feels like it matters to me," since that concedes more or less exactly what he's claiming. Is there a genuine case for why the size and age of the universe are simply the wrong kind of fact to settle whether a human life or its projects matter -- something that actually engages his numbers rather than changing the subject -- or is his bleaker conclusion the more rigorous one? I would like to have something better than a shrug ready for him the next time this comes up.
