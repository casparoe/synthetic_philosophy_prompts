I sit on the grant allocation committee for a smallish EA-adjacent regranting fund — we move something like $300-400k a year across a handful of cause areas — and we've hit a genuine impasse that the chair wants an actual written memo on before we vote this Friday.

Normally we score every proposal for expected value with a rough Squiggle model (impact-if-it-works times probability-it-works) and fund down the ranked list till the money's gone, which is basically expected-utility maximization with the independence axiom quietly built in. This round the top two options are (A) a boring, extremely well-evidenced global health charity with a tight distribution of outcomes, and (B) a speculative biosecurity project our model says has meaningfully higher expected value but maybe a 70% chance of doing literally nothing — classic hits-based-giving stuff. Pure EV-maximization says dump it all into B. One committee member, who ran an Allais-style choice survey on the rest of us for fun at a retreat last year (of course we all failed it), is now arguing that since our own preferences over gambles violate independence in the textbook common-consequence way, we have no principled reason to let the independence axiom override our considered risk attitudes here, especially since this is a one-shot allocation and not the kind of thing that averages out over a long run of repeated grants. Another member says that's just a fancy rationalization for scope-insensitive risk aversion, and that the usual money-pump-style consistency arguments for independence still hold up regardless of how common Allais choices are. Somebody also brought up that Lara Buchak has a whole book working out a "risk-weighted" alternative to expected utility for exactly this kind of case, but none of us have actually read it, so I don't totally trust our secondhand gloss on what it says.

Could you write the memo I'd bring to the meeting? I need it to land on one concrete recommendation for this round's allocation, plus ideally a standing rule for future rounds — straight EV-maximization, an explicit risk-weighted departure from it, a split/diversification rule, or whatever you think is actually best defended — and I need the case for keeping independence treated seriously rather then strawmanned, since the consistency argument is clearly the strongest thing the other side has going for it. Please don't spend the memo re-explaining what the Allais paradox is, we're all long past that part; what I need is the actual argument for what it should imply for a committee spending real money once, not a gamble happening in the abstract.
