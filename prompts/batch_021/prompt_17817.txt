I'm one of the outside directors on the board of a small nonprofit forecasting institute -- the sort that runs calibration training and a public forecasting tournament -- and was invited partly because I've spent years writing about this world. The institute recently began trading its aggregated forecasts for real money on a regulated prediction-market exchange, both to fund its research and to demonstrate its edge publicly, and the board now has to settle a house staking policy. The fund's quantitative lead wants every position sized by full Kelly criterion against the institute's own probability estimates, arguing that anything more conservative leaves growth on the table, and citing the ergodicity-economics claim that ordinary expected-value reasoning applies the wrong average to an entity compounding over time. Several directors are uneasy: the fund is expected to cover part of the institute's payroll every quarter, its probabilities are calibrated but still just estimates rather than known odds, and a bad enough drawdown could sink the institute rather than merely sting it. Please draft the memo I'll bring to the board: it should recommend a specific staking rule -- not just "somewhere more conservative than full Kelly" -- with a stated review point, and it should credit what the growth-optimality argument gets right before explaining why, or whether, it needs to be constrained.
