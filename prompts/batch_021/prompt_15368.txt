here's the answer i gotta grade:

"The logical problem of evil says it's actually contradictory for a perfectly good, all-powerful God to exist given that evil exists at all. The evidential problem is weaker: it says the amount and kinds of evil we actually see (like animals suffering horribly with no one around to benefit from it) make it improbable that God exists, even though it's not a strict contradiction.

Plantinga's free will defense targets the logical problem. He argues God couldn't force free creatures to always choose good without them stopping being free, so it's possible every creature suffers from "transworld depravity," meaning in every world where they're free there's at least one situation where they'd freely go wrong. Since a world with free creatures who sometimes do evil is more valuable than one with no free creatures at all, God and evil can logically coexist, which refutes the logical problem.

Because the free will defense shows evil and God are compatible, it also answers the evidential problem: once free will explains why God allows the evil we observe, the amount of evil stops being evidence against God, so Rowe's argument fails for basically the same reason Mackie's does.

Hick's soul-making theodicy is basically the same idea as Plantinga's theodicy, since both are trying to lay out God's actual reason for allowing evil: Hick thinks hardship is necessary for people to grow spiritually and freely come to love God, which is why we're created at an "epistemic distance" from him instead of in his direct presence."
