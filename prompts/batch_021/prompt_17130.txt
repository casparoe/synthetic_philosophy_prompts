Okay so this has been eating at me for like two weeks now. I'm doing a PhD in cosmology, working on eternal inflation and the "measure problem" -- basically, once you accept that inflation never fully stops everywhere and you end up with infinitely many pocket universes and infinitely many observers scattered across them, you need some scheme to regulate those infinities before you can predict anything, like what value of the cosmological constant we should expect to measure. In group meeting my advisor admitted, almost as an aside, that every measure anyone has proposed is quietly smuggling in an answer to the old self-sampling-assumption-vs-self-indication-assumption fight from anthropic reasoning -- the same fight that gives you the Doomsday argument on one side and the presumptuous philosopher on the other -- and that there's no physics reason to prefer one measure over another, its basically philosophy of probability wearing a lab coat. I read Bostrom's book that weekend and now I genuinely can't tell if my whole subfield's predictions rest on anything firmer than an argument you could just as easily have at a bar. When I tried to hash it out with my advisor afterward he basically said "sure, but we have to pick something and get on with the physics," which, fair, but also not an answer.

So here's what I want, partly just to scratch the itch properly: write me a dialogue between two philosophers of probability, one committed to SSA and one to SIA, set right inside the presumptuous philosopher scenario itself -- two rival cosmological theories, equally likely given the physical evidence so far, where one predicts something like a trillion observers exist and the other a trillion trillion, and physicists are about to run a real, expensive experiment to settle which theory is true. Have the SIA person walk in and claim the experiment is basically unnecessary because their view already tells them the big-universe theory is a trillion times more probable, and have the SSA person push back hard on that rather than just gesturing at how absurd it sounds. But don't let the SSA side win by default either -- they should have to own the fact that SSA's own answer depends on a reference class nobody can motivate in a principled way, and that it has it's own ugly consequences like the Doomsday argument. I want real engagement, each one naming the specific move that makes the other most uncomfortable, not two people restating their axioms with more confidence. A page or so is plenty, and it's fine if they don't resolve it, as long as by the end it's clear exactly where the disagreement actually lives.
