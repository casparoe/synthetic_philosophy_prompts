She's also been taking the view somewhere more EA-coded: arguing that funders doing "hits-based" giving into x-risk work are often less rational than they think they are, and that real risk-aversion at the portfolio level is the correct call rather than a failure of nerve. I've spent way too much of my life arguing about this exact thing in EA forum comment sections, so I really don't want this episode to just be her restating the axioms and the Allais paradox one more time.

I want one built on the long-run objection (I think this is the one Johanna Thoma presses against Buchak): if a funder makes hundreds of roughly independent risky grants, risk-aversion on any one grant should wash out at the portfolio level by the law of large numbers, so is REU theory telling you to treat the whole portfolio as one gamble to be risk-weighted -- and if so, isn't "how many choices count as one gamble" now doing all the normative work, in a way that's totally up for grabs? So: doesn't that just import a purely prudential attitude -- how much risk I'll accept regarding my own one life -- into impartial tradeoffs between totally distinct people who, unlike me choosing behind a veil, never actually face a gamble at all?
