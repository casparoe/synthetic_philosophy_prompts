This came up at my rationalist meetup's reading group and it's been rattling around in my head since. We'd just gone through the classic Sleeping Beauty problem (fair coin; she's woken Monday no matter what, and woken again Tuesday with her memory wiped only if it landed tails), and two people from the group, Marcus and Ilana, ended up arguing about it for like an hour after everyone else left.

Marcus made the standard thirder move: if Beauty bets "tails" every single time she's woken, across many repeats of the whole experiment she wins two-thirds of the time, so that should just be her credence on waking.

Ilana said that conflates betting frequency with credence. If you instead arrange things so only her Monday bet ever counts (which happens for sure regardless of the coin, so it's a clean read on the actual flip), the thirder strategy loses its edge at even odds -- which shows credence and betting odds can definately come apart.

Marcus granted that specific betting point, but said he doesn't need betting anyway. He tweaked the setup so tails produces a million wakings instead of two, still with memory wiped between each. His claim: no sane halfer can wake up inside that version, know the full setup, and still insist credence(heads) is exactly 1/2 -- the sheer weight of probably-being-one-of-999,999 has to move you somewhat.

Ilana didn't budge. She said that's just self-indication reasoning (SIA) wearing a trenchcoat, and SIA is precisely what generates the Presumptuous Philosopher, where you're forced to favor whatever cosmological theory predicts vastly more observers, purely because that makes "you" less surprising. If the intuition pump runs on the same engine as that absurd case, she said, we shouldn't trust the pump.

Marcus's comeback was that he only needs ordinary conditionalization on "I'm awake right now" given how this particular setup samples her, not the full SIA principle you'd use to compare rival theories about the whole universe's population -- so the Presumptuous Philosopher doesn't automatically follow.

Neither gave any ground and I got roped in as tiebreaker, which I am absolutely not qualified for. Could you go through this move by move -- who's actually right at each step, not just who "wins" overall -- and point out anything both of them are missing?
