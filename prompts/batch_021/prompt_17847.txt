A friend and I had a long argument last night that started from the familiar Euthyphro dilemma but ended up stuck on the details of how Socrates actually argues for one of its horns, around 10a-11b. There he leads Euthyphro to agree that something is carried because someone carries it, and led because someone leads it, and not the reverse; he then extends this to "loved": the pious, he concludes, is loved by the gods because it is pious, not pious because it is loved, since being god-beloved is merely something that then happens to the pious rather than what makes it pious in the first place. My friend thinks the whole analogy is a sleight of hand: granting that being-carried presupposes an act of carrying doesn't obviously show that being-loved-by-the-gods must track some independent property of piety, rather than, say, the gods' own nature or shared preferences. I couldn't settle this on the spot. Could you reconstruct Socrates's argument as rigorously as you can, and then assess whether it genuinely rules out the "pious-because-loved" horn, or whether my friend's suspicion of a sleight of hand exposes a real gap that more sophisticated divine command theories might exploit?
