My father died two months ago, and my identical twin brother and I are now settling his estate, worth a bit over a million dollars. To avoid a costly fight, the estate attorney has asked each of us to tell her privately, without conferring beforehand, whether we will accept an even half or hold out for more. If we both accept half, the estate is split down the middle; if only one of us holds out, she honors that claim and the other gets whatever is left. But if we both hold out, the matter goes to formal arbitration, and once the fees are paid, we will each net less than a plain fifty-fifty split would have given us.

I negotiate deals for a living, and my instinct is that anyone in this position should hold out for more, since doing so leaves you better off whichever way the other side goes. But my brother is not an ordinary counterparty. We were raised by the same parents in the same house, and whenever we have had to decide something important separately, without conferring, we have ended up choosing identically far more often than coincidence allows, sometimes down to details neither of us could have predicted. That track record makes me distrust my own usual advice. If I am genuinely confident he will reason this through exactly as I would, it stops being obvious that holding out is still the smart move, though I cannot quite say why, or what should take its place. Is my hesitation tracking something real, and if it is, what does it actually recommend I do?
