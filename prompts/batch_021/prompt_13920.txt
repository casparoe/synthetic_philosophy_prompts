My daughter turns eighteen next month, and I've been asked to give the toast at her party. Before she was conceived, genetic counseling told us that waiting a few months would likely mean an unaffected pregnancy, while conceiving right away carried a real risk of the condition she was in fact born with; we didn't wait, for reasons that had nothing to do with her, since there was no her yet to have reasons about. Most guests have never put a name to any of this, but two are old friends of mine from a philosophy PhD program, now bioethicists, who will recognize it immediately as a non-identity problem case and will notice if I lapse into the natural but confused idea that a healthier version of her is the daughter I "could have had" instead. Write the toast. It should run three or four minutes, be honest about how hard parts of these eighteen years have been, and be warm about exactly who she is, without ever resting on the claim that everything worked out for the best, since there's no coherent alternative version of her to weigh her life against -- only her, or no her at all.
