I'm a retired evolutionary biologist - spent most of my working life studying paper wasps, relatedness, and a fair bit on worker policing - and since I stopped doing fieldwork I've gone a bit down the philosophy of biology rabbit hole (currently working through Okasha's book on levels of selection, and I finally got round to Sober and Wilson's "Unto Others"). It's made me see some of the old fights from my working life rather differently, which matters right now because my old friend and grad-school labmate, Meg, is retiring after forty years in the field, and her students have asked me to give the toast at the dinner. Meg basically built her career defending inclusive fitness theory against the group selectionists - she was one of the signatories on that big Nature rebuttal in 2011 (137 of them signed it, if i remember the number right) after Nowak, Tarnita and Wilson tried to argue that eusociality is better explained by selection acting on whole groups than by Hamilton's gene's-eye-view kin selection.

Here's my problem: about half the room will be fellow biologists who lived through the whole kin-selection-versus-group-selection business and will catch every reference to Hamilton's rule and worker policing, and the other half will be her husband, her three kids, and a bunch of neighbours who have never heard the phrase "unit of selection" in their lives. I don't want to just explain the theory and have the toast turn into a conference talk, but I also don't want to flatten it into nothing so the biologists in the room feel like her whole professional life got skipped over. What I really want is for the actual structure of the debate - relatedness, cost, benefit, who you back and why - to be doing real work in the toast itself, rather than just sitting there as decoration. I keep half-thinking there's a good bit to be built out of applying Hamilton's rule reflexively to the toast itself - how much effort I "should" be putting into praising her, given the r between us - but I can't quite land it myself. Could you write me something like a five-minute toast along these lines: something that gets both halves of the room laughing at the same moment, and that feels like a tribute to Meg rather than a lecture on kin selection?
