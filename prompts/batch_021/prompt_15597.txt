I've been asked to give the keynote at a Fourth of July naturalization ceremony, where a federal judge will swear in about 150 new citizens. The judge, a former legal historian, wants the speech built around Frederick Douglass, but has asked that it not lean only on the righteous anger of 'What to the Slave is the Fourth of July?' She wants it to reckon with the fact that Douglass spent a decade agreeing with Garrison that the Constitution was 'a covenant with death, and an agreement with hell,' before breaking with him around 1851 to argue -- from the text alone, not the framers' intentions -- that it was, in his own words, 'a glorious liberty document.' Write the speech: it has to move new citizens who know none of this history, while still holding up for the judge and the legal historians in the room, who will notice if Douglass's actual argument -- and his reasons for changing his mind -- get flattened into generic uplift.
