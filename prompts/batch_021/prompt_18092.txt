I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, and a disagreement broke out in the comments beneath a recent post on incentive design that I'd like a neutral outside read on. The post described a hypothetical but realistic mid-sized school district that began tying small budget bonuses to year-over-year gains in standardized test scores. Average scores rose sharply for two years, after which a state audit turned up widespread teaching-to-the-test and a handful of outright cheating scandals (erasures, score inflation) concentrated in the highest-gaining schools. Two commenters, whom I'll call A and B, spent several replies disagreeing about how to classify the case using the regressional/extremal/causal/adversarial Goodhart taxonomy that's well known in those circles.

A: This is a clean case of adversarial Goodhart. Once test scores became the target, teachers who understood the incentive reacted strategically — drilling test-specific tricks, and in the worst cases cheating outright. That's exactly the pattern of an agent exploiting a known gap between proxy and goal.

B: The cheating is adversarial, sure, but ordinary teaching-to-the-test isn't — that's causal Goodhart. Test scores were never a cause of learning, just a noisy downstream correlate of it, so pushing directly on the proxy was never going to raise the actual goal, whether or not anyone meant to game anything.

A: But causal Goodhart is supposed to bite even on someone not trying to game the system at all — that's the whole point of the category. Here, teachers changed their behavior specifically because they knew the score would be rewarded. That deliberate reaction to a known incentive is what makes it adversarial rather than a mere structural artifact.

B: Intent can't be what settles the classification, or the categories stop doing any real work — you could relabel any instance of teaching-to-the-test as "adversarial" just by asserting the teacher was aware of the bonus. And some of the district's biggest gains were probably regressional besides: whichever schools happened to get an unusually strong cohort or an easier test form that year would show up as top gainers regardless of behavior, and would be expected to regress the next year.
