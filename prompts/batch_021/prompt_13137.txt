So I've been getting into decision theory on my own time (a lot of it started from AI safety blogs), and this "money pump" argument keeps coming up. As I understand it: say you like A more than B, B more than C, but also C more than A -- someone can exploit that by trading you round the loop, taking a bit of money off you each time you switch, until your back where you started but broke. Since that seems to prove that any truly rational agent, even a future superintelligent AI, just plain has to have fully transitive preferences (ie act like its maximizing one single consistent utility function), why do decision theorists and philosophers still write whole papers arguing about whether rationality really requires this? Feels like the money pump pretty much settles it already, no?
