Been down a rabbit hole again after re-reading some old LessWrong/MIRI stuff on logical induction, and the "problem of logical omniscience" thing has been stuck in my head. The issue, as I understand it: the standard way of modelling belief (the old Hintikka-style possible-worlds epistemic logic, but honestly plain Bayesian epistemology has the same problem) treats an agent's knowledge/credences as closed under logical consequence -- if you know P and P entails Q, you know Q, full stop, and you already assign the correct credence to every theorem, every tautology, whether some giant number is prime, the best move 40 ply deep in a chess position, all of it, instantly and for free. Everyone agrees real bounded reasoners (human or AI) cant work like that, which is basically why logical induction exists -- so credences about logical/mathematical claims can be genuinely uncertain and improve with more thinking time instead of being fixed by fiat.

What I've never seen anyone do is make this actually bite. Someone in a discord I'm in claimed that a truly logically omniscient Bayesian could never use a cryptographic scheme in the first place, since the plaintext (or private key) is already logically determined by the ciphertext, so a "rational" agent would already know it -- which sounded either really deep or totally confused, and i couldn't tell which. So: can you construct a fully specified example -- not just the abstract "agent knows P, P entails Q" template, but something with actual numbers, propositions, or payoffs attached, in a bet, a game, a cryptographic setup, a negotiation, whatever setting makes it vivid -- where treating a reasoner as logically omniscient forces a specific credence or decision that's clearly the wrong call, and a resource-bounded, logically-uncertain version of that same reasoner obviously does better? I'd like it concrete enough that I could actually check it myself, and if possible steer clear of the classic "old evidence for general relativity" chestnut -- I've seen that one enough times, I want something you actually had to build rather than pull off a shelf.
