And here, reproduced exactly as submitted, is the student's answer: "Popper's phrase 'metaphysical research programme' comes from his work in the 1970s, where he argued that the core claim of Darwinism — that species change over time through the survival of the fittest — cannot actually be tested or falsified. His problem was circularity: 'fitness' is defined as whatever traits lead to more offspring, but then we say the fittest survive and reproduce more, which just means that whatever survives and reproduces more is defined as fit. Put that way, 'the fittest survive' is true no matter what happens, the same way 'all bachelors are unmarried' is true no matter what happens, so it doesn't tell us anything we could check against the world. Darwin himself coined the phrase 'survival of the fittest' to capture this idea, and it was exactly this phrase that Popper thought reduced natural selection to a tautology rather than a real empirical law. On these grounds, Popper placed the whole theory of evolution outside science proper, closer to his other favorite examples of unfalsifiable belief systems, astrology and psychoanalysis, which only look like they explain things because they can accommodate any possible observation after the fact. Later on, in a piece he published in 1978, Popper said he had changed his mind about how testable the theory really was. He pointed out that natural selection does generate predictions we can check — for instance, about the sequence of forms in the fossil record, or about the biochemical similarities we'd expect among related species — and that these predictions could in principle fail, which is exactly what falsifiability requires. So he walked back his harshest claim and admitted natural selection belongs on the scientific side of the line after all. Overall, I'd say the popular summary is basically fair but a bit too neat: Popper really did think Darwinism failed his falsifiability test at first, lumping it in with the pseudosciences, and he really did later say he had changed his mind, so 'he said it wasn't science, then took it back' isn't wrong so much as compressed."
