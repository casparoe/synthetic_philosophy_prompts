The question, worth 20 points, read:

> Parts (a) and (b) both involve Omega, a perfect predictor.
> (a) [6 pts] State what UDT recommends in Newcomb's Problem (Omega has placed $1,000,000 in an opaque box just in case it predicts you will take only that box, and $1,000 in a transparent box regardless), and explain — in terms of the relationship between the agent's decision procedure and the predictor's simulation of it — why this is the recommended action.
> (b) [8 pts] State what UDT recommends in the counterfactual mugging (Omega flips a fair coin; on tails, it asks the agent for $100; on heads, it pays the agent $10,000 if it predicts the agent would have paid on tails), and give the expected-value reasoning behind that recommendation. Contrast this with what an agent reasoning according to CDT (causal decision theory) or EDT (evidential decision theory) would do once it has actually observed the coin land tails, and explain why the recommendations diverge.
> (c) [6 pts] UDT's behavior in (b) is often summarized by saying the agent acts "as if it had precommitted" before the coin was flipped, even though no actual precommitment occurs. Explain precisely what "updateless" refers to here — that is, exactly what it is that the agent does not update — and why this lets UDT reach the verdict a real advance commitment would produce, without requiring one.

The student answered:

> (a) UDT one-boxes. The predictor's forecast is generated by simulating the same decision procedure the agent will actually run, so the agent's choice and the box's contents aren't two independent events linked by a correlation the agent merely observes from outside — they're two outputs of a single computation. Two-boxing would require that computation to disagree with itself, so the agent should one-box and collect the $1,000,000.
> (b) UDT pays the $100. Comparing the policy of paying to the policy of refusing from the standpoint of before the coin is flipped: paying nets 0.5 × (–$100) + 0.5 × ($10,000) = $4,950 in expectation, while refusing nets $0. A CDT or EDT agent, by contrast, only computes expected utility after conditioning on its observations, so once it has actually seen the coin land tails, it reasons that the heads branch — and the $10,000 that went with it — is no longer causally or evidentially reachable, and that hanging on to its $100 can only help; hence it refuses.
> (c) Updateless decision theory, which Eliezer Yudkowsky developed out of his earlier timeless decision theory (TDT) specifically to correct TDT's mishandling of the counterfactual mugging, gets its name from the fact that the agent never actually updates on the observations it receives: on being told the coin came up tails, it continues to assign heads and tails equal 50% credence and reasons about the $100 exactly as it would have beforehand. Because the two branches remain equally live for it in this way, the heads branch's $10,000 still counts toward the expected value of paying — which is exactly what a real advance commitment would achieve, so the agent gets the benefit of having precommitted without ever needing to have actually done so.
