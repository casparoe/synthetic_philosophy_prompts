Compare finkish dispositions (as in C. B. Martin's electro-fink) with masked dispositions (as in Mark Johnston's and Alexander Bird's antidote cases) as counterexamples to the conditional analysis of dispositions. Although the two are routinely treated as a single family of objections, they arguably exploit different weaknesses in the analysis. Does David Lewis's reformed conditional analysis -- which requires the disposition's causal basis to persist through the stimulus -- succeed against one of these counterexamples but not the other? Justify your answer by explaining precisely what the persistence requirement rules out in each case.
