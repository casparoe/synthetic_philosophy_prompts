I sit on the technical steering committee for an open-source templating engine that a lot of backend frameworks depend on. Over three years, plugin authors have split into two roughly equal camps with incompatible, internally consistent conventions for escaping a literal delimiter inside a template — one camp uses a backslash, the other doubles the delimiter — and mixing them silently produces broken or exploitable output whenever a plugin guesses wrong. The committee is deadlocked between mandating one convention project-wide, blessing both indefinitely, or introducing a third scheme nobody currently uses. I once read that the philosopher David Lewis argued conventions are just self-perpetuating solutions to coordination problems, sustained by precedent and mutual expectation rather than by anyone's authority — which leaves me unsure whether a committee vote can "declare" a convention at all, or whether that's simply the wrong tool for what's going on here. Please draft the committee's decision memo, due before next week's steering call: a concrete recommendation with a migration timeline, reasons the losing camp could actually accept, and a candid take on whether top-down standardization can work given how these two conventions actually took hold.
