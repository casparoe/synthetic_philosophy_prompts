I'm reporting a piece on the psychology of trust inside companies and came across a founder who ends every leadership offsite with a tightly run group drinking session, on the theory that a controlled loss of control is a safer way to find out who can hold it together under pressure than waiting for a real crisis to expose it. A rotating "sober captain," always someone senior, sets firm limits and can end the session at will, and new hires and anyone driving that night are excluded outright. The founder says the exercise reveals more about a person's trustworthiness than months of sober collaboration ever could; skeptics on his own team call it manipulative amateur psychology dressed up as leadership wisdom. Before I write this up, I want to know: is there a serious idea buried in this practice, or is it just an elaborate license to drink at the office?
