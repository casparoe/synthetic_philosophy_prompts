Quick question for a book I'm drafting - I've got a linguist character trying to work out an alien species' language completely from scratch, just from watching how they react to things around them, with pretty much no shared reference points to start from. Researching this got me into Davidson's "radical interpretation" stuff, and it seems like his whole method leans on a "principle of charity" that basically forces you to assume the aliens mostly hold true, sensible beleifs. But that seems like it rigs the whole project from the start - if the method requires treating the aliens as basically thinking like us just to get interpretation off the ground, then it can never actually detect real alienness, it'll just keep projecting human logic onto their behavior and mislabel that as "understanding." Given that's a real limitation of Davidson's approach, what's a more realistic method my linguist could use instead to actually pick up on a genuinely alien way of thinking?
