At a reunion dinner, two former students of mine fell into a disagreement: Lena now works in public-health communications, Marcus sells home-security systems, and the dispute concerned a practice at Marcus's company. Before giving any statistics on how the system reduces risk, he and his colleagues open every pitch with a short, true news clip about a recent burglary in the customer's own town.

The exchange went roughly as follows.

(1) Lena called the practice manipulative: it leans on one vivid, frightening incident precisely because that incident will crowd out the customer's sense of the town's actual, low burglary rate, rather than leading with the rate itself.

(2) Marcus replied that vividness cannot be what makes something manipulative: nearly all effective communication, including the public-health campaigns Lena herself designs, relies on stories and images rather than bare statistics, so condemning his pitch on this ground would condemn most persuasion whatsoever.

(3) Lena answered that the trouble is not vividness as such, but its function here: the clip is chosen and timed to outweigh the base rate in the customer's judgment, yielding a conclusion that would not survive their seeing the full statistical picture, whereas her own campaigns use vivid cases to convey an accurate risk rather than to override one the audience already grasps.

(4) Marcus countered that deliberately exploiting a known bias cannot be the test either, since Lena's own favored tactic, leading with precise statistics, exploits a 'numbers sound authoritative' bias just as much. The fairer test, he said, is whether the pitch would survive being made to a fully rational, fully informed version of the customer; since he and his colleagues sincerely believe the system reduces real risk, it would.

Neither budged. Please work through the exchange move by move, saying who has the better of it at each step, not simply who wins overall, and note any consideration bearing on manipulation versus legitimate persuasion that neither speaker raises.
