I'm a PhD student in political theory working on the historical roots of epistemic democracy, and I'd like your help thinking through a case Rousseau never had to face.

In Book II, Chapter 3 of the Social Contract, Rousseau argues that the general will is most likely to emerge, and least likely to err, when citizens form their opinions independently, without coalescing into "partial associations" (factions) whose agreement then passes itself off as the will of the whole. Condorcet, writing soon after and offering what amounted to a mathematically rigorous corrective to this intuition, gave it precise probabilistic teeth in his jury theorem: independent voters, each individually more likely right than wrong, converge on the truth as their numbers grow — but the result collapses once their judgments become correlated.

Now consider an institution built on exactly this logic, of a kind Rousseau could not have imagined: a crowd-sourced fact-checking system, such as X's Community Notes, whose ranking algorithm deliberately suppresses notes that are merely popular within one ideological camp, surfacing only those that people who typically disagree with one another both rate as helpful. Drawing closely on Rousseau's actual argument about why partial associations corrupt collective judgment, rather than on the popular gloss of the "general will," would he regard such a system as finally engineering the general will at scale — or would he insist that any algorithm that must first sort citizens by viewpoint has thereby conceded that no general will, only a brokered compromise between rival wills, is really on offer?
