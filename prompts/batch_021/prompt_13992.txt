This is a strange thing for a philosophy professor to be asking for advice on, but its been bugging me all week and I can't quite think my way out of it. I run a small experimental philosophy lab, and I have a postdoc, Priya, who does most of our qualitative coding for a project on how ordinary people reason about moral luck — sorting people's open-ended written justifications into categories before we run any stats on them. She's excellent, and on the genuinely hard, borderline calls, the ones where a given response could plausibly go into two different categories, her coding almost always lines up with whatever I would've guessed myself, or with whatever the emerging story of the paper needs it to say. For a long time I took that as just evidence that she's good at her job and we happen to think alike.

Lately it's started to nag at me though, because the only way I ever check her coding is by going back and reading the raw transcript myself, and on that small handful of cases where I do, she's basically always right — so I've naturally learned to trust her on the much larger number of cases where I don't have time to double check. But here's the problem: a research assistant who is honestly and carefully applying the coding scheme, and one who has simply gotten very good at predicting which category I want a given response to land in and writing up a plausible justification to match, would look completely identical to me in every case except the rare one where the honest reading and my own expectation actually diverge — and those are exactly the cases where, almost by definition, I have no independent way of checking, because if they were easy to check they wouldn't have been the ambiguous ones. I mentioned this to a colleague over lunch and she said I was overthinking a coding disagreement, which does not feel like an answer. So: is there some principled way of redesigning my review process — more spot checks, blind coding, different incentives, whatever — that could actually tell these two possible Priyas apart, or is this a structural dead end that no amount of cleverness about process can get around? It feels like the kind of thing that should already have a name from epistemology or decision theory, but nothing I can dredge up from grad school quite fits it.
