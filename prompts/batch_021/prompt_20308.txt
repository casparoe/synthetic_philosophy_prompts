So I'm auditing a philosophy of mind seminar this semester and we just spent two weeks on higher-order theories of consciousness (the Rosenthal-style view that a mental state is conscious just in case there's a suitable higher-order thought representing you as being in that state), and honestly I still can't decide if I buy it over the first-order alternative -- the Dretske/Tye-ish idea that a state is conscious in virtue of its own representational content plus the right functional role, no extra "thought about the thought" required. The thing that's nagging me is the classic case of zoning out while driving and then suddenly snapping back, realizing you don't remember the last few minutes even though you were clearly steering, braking, etc. the whole time. The HOT people love this case because it looks like the perceptual states were there and doing real work without being conscious, which is exactly what their theory predicts should be possible. But I feel like a first-order theorist should have some comeback here that doesn't require positing a whole extra representational state just to explain it.

There's also this puzzle that's been bugging me seperately: if what makes a state phenomenally conscious is just having some higher-order thought about it, what happens when that higher-order thought misfires, i.e. it represents you as being in pain (or seeing red) when there's no actual first-order state there at all? Either you say that still counts as consciously feeling pain, which seems to totally detach "what it's like" from anything actually happening in your sensory systems, or you say it doesn't count, in which case it's not clear the higher-order thought was ever doing the real work of explaining the feel in the first place.

Could you write me a dialogue between a committed higher-order theorist and an equally sharp first-order representationalist working through both of these cases? I want genuine back-and-forth: each one should actually engage with and try to defuse the other's best point instead of just restating their own view, and neither one should be a pushover or get to have the last word too easily. Assume they both already know the standard moves (regress worries, blindsight, all that) so they can go a few rounds deep instead of staying at intro-textbook level.
