My friend Marcus is making Eagle Scout next month, and his mother has asked me to give the court of honor speech, since we've been in the same troop since sixth grade. I'd like to build it around Benjamin Franklin's project of moral perfection from his autobiography, because Marcus has genuinely kept a Franklin-style chart of virtues taped inside his binder for years -- a running, half-joking campaign against his own chronic lateness, which is almost exactly the battle Franklin himself admitted losing, against the virtue he named Order. The difficulty is the audience: our scoutmaster has actually read the Autobiography and will wince if Franklin gets flattened into a generic inspirational quote, especially since Franklin's own conclusion was that he never got near the perfection he was chasing, and that congratulating yourself on your humility is itself a sly form of pride; everyone else in the room, meanwhile, just wants to feel proud of Marcus without sitting through a philosophy lecture. Could you write the speech -- about four minutes long, meant to be read aloud -- so that it stays honest to that conclusion, crediting Marcus's real progress without ever implying that he, or Franklin, actually arrived somewhere?
