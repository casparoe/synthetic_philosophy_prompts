I recently joined my synagogue's ritual committee, and our first substantive disagreement concerns the traditional petition, recited every Shabbat in the Musaf Amidah, asking that the sacrificial service be restored once the Temple is rebuilt. Trying to work out where I stand before our next meeting, I turned to Maimonides' explanation of sacrifice in the Guide of the Perplexed III:32: God commanded the sacrificial cult only as a concession to Israelites who knew no other mode of worship, since a people habituated to their neighbors' sacrificial idolatry could not be weaned onto prayer alone in a single step. Then I came across Nahmanides' objection, in his commentary on Leviticus 1:9, that this cannot be the whole story, since Cain, Abel, Noah, and the patriarchs were all offering sacrifices to God long before there was any surrounding idolatry from which to be weaned.

I don't want a summary of this dispute; I want to see it actually fought out. Please write a dialogue between two committee members who have each read the same sources: one who defends Nahmanides' objection and argues that, quite apart from how the dispute over origins is resolved, Maimonides' own legal rulings on the rebuilt Temple settle the question in favor of literal restoration; and one who defends Maimonides' accommodation theory against Nahmanides' objection and argues that its own logic — sacrifice as a bridge from idolatry to prayer — should make us want that bridge finished, not rebuilt, regardless of what Maimonides' law code assumes. Have each press the other on their hardest case rather than letting them talk past one another, and let the dialogue end with the committee's practical question — what, if anything, to change in the liturgy — still unresolved between them.
