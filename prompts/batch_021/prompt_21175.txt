Over the past few months, my therapist has raised the possibility that I have mild alexithymia, since I often act on anger, anxiety, or sadness well before I consciously register the feeling, and am not always confident, even in hindsight, that I've identified the emotion correctly. This has led me to Timothy Williamson's anti-luminosity argument in Knowledge and Its Limits and the challenge it poses to the KK principle. Please describe the most important implications of that argument for how much epistemic weight I should place on my own real-time self-reports of emotion, distinguishing where the margin-for-error reasoning behind anti-luminosity genuinely bears on a case like mine from where my situation is simply an ordinary empirical failure of introspection that the argument was never meant to address.
