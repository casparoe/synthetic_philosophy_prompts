So me and my buddy Dave have had literally the same argument for like three years now about whether this one crypto coin is gonna moon or crash. every couple months we hash it out again, both lay out our reasons, and both walk away thinking exactly what we thought before. zero movement, either direction, ever.

recently i went down a rabbit hole (started on some rationalist-adjacent blog, don't remember which) and landed on this thing called Aumann's agreement theorem. as i understand it: if two people are genuinely rational, and each one knows the other is genuinely rational and honest, then just telling each other your gut-feeling probability back and forth, over and over, without even saying WHY, is supposed to be enough to make you both land on the same number eventually. you don't even have to swap your actual evidence, just the raw number, repeated a few times, and it converges.

that kind of broke my brain because it obviously does not match me and Dave at all. so i wrote up my own half-baked theory about why, and i want someone to poke holes in it:

- i don't think its that either of us is bad at doing the math in our heads or flat out lying about our number in the moment.
- i think the real issue is we don't actually start from the same "prior," we just act like we do. dave is fundamentally a "number go up" optimist and i'm fundamentally a "this is a bubble" pessimist before either of us even looks at new info, so our starting points were never equal to begin with, only our access to info kind of overlaps.
- theres also a second thing going on, which is neither of us fully believes the other guy is being rational when he says his number - we each secretly think the other is a little deluded or too emotionally invested. so the "we both know the other is rational" part quietly fails, even though neither of us would ever admit that mid-argument.
- so my actual claim is this: any time two people disagree for YEARS while each insists the other is a reasonable, rational, honest person, at least one of those claims has to be false. either they don't really share a starting prior, or they don't really trust the other's rationality, or somebody's number isn't fully honest. all three being true at once, forever, shouldn't be possible.

am i even representing the theorem right, or messing up something important about it? and does my "can't have all three at once" claim actually hold up logically, or is there a hole in it somewhere? also curious if this is a genuinely interesting angle or if it's like, the first thing every person who reads about this theorem thinks of and i just don't know that yet.
