I'm writing a novel whose protagonist is a philosophy translator spending a fellowship year re-translating Spinoza's unfinished *Tractatus Politicus*, and I want a pivotal scene to rest on genuine philosophical tension rather than atmosphere alone. The treatise breaks off, famously, not long after this argument in its final chapter, "Of Democracy," for excluding women from citizenship (Elwes's translation):

"If, again, anyone should say that it is not by nature, but by institution only, that women are under the authority of men, we may reply that experience itself abundantly teaches otherwise. For it has never happened that men and women ruled together; wherever men and women are found, there we find men ruling and women submitting, and this arrangement obtains so uniformly that we may fairly conclude it is founded in nature and not in custom. For if women were by nature equal to men in strength of mind and in that capacity on which right chiefly depends, surely among nations so many and so various some would be found where both sexes rule alike, and others where men are ruled by women and brought up so as to make less use of their abilities; but since this nowhere happens, we may assert with perfect propriety that women have not, by nature, equal right with men, but that they necessarily give way to men, and that thus it cannot be that both sexes should rule alike, much less that men should be ruled by women."

The manuscript ends only a little further on, with just the posthumous editors' note appended: "Reliqua desiderantur" — the rest is wanting. I want my protagonist's disquiet at this passage to track a real philosophical fault line rather than a generic modern objection, so help me locate it precisely: does this argument actually follow validly from Spinoza's foundational identification of natural right with power, as he states it earlier in this same treatise and in the *Ethics*? If the inference is valid, what does that tell us about the premises rather than about Spinoza's reasoning here; and if it is not valid, exactly where does it fail?
