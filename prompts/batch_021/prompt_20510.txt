A friend and I spent much of last night arguing about a situation from my job, and I still can't decide which of us has the better view. Through an email I was never meant to see, I recently discovered that my manager has spent the past two years deliberately protecting me from anything that might dent my confidence: rerouting client complaints about my projects to other staff, giving me only assignments she was confident I would succeed at, and privately asking my teammates to soften their feedback before it reached me. Until that discovery, I would have described those two years, without hesitation, as the most successful and fulfilling stretch of my career. Our argument wasn't really about whether she was wrong to do this (we agreed that's a separate question), but about whether those two years were actually as good for me as they felt at the time. My friend says yes: nothing about my actual day-to-day experience has changed, and feeling capable and satisfied is presumably the point of doing a job well. I feel strangely cheated instead, as though something of real value went missing, even though my experience at the time was, by hypothesis, exactly as good as it seemed. Neither of us could quite explain what, if anything, I actually lost. Am I entitled to feel cheated, or is my friend right that I'm being ungrateful about an arrangement that only ever worked in my favor?
