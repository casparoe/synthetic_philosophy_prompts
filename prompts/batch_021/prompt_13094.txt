So over the past year I've gotten really into backyard beekeeping, and it's sent me down a research rabbit hole that's kind of messing with a belief I didn't even realize I held: that insects obviously don't have anything going on inside that's worth worrying about. I started reading about bee cognition just to be a better beekeeper, and ran into that argument from Barron and Klein (I think it was a PNAS paper, years back now) that the insect central complex is structurally and functionally similar to the vertebrate midbrain, and that some consciousness researchers think the midbrain alone, cortex or no cortex, might be enough to generate a very basic, unified kind of experience. Then I found an older study where they literally shook beehives to mimic a predator attack, and the shaken bees came out measurably more "pessimistic" on a test that's normally used to pick up anxiety-like states in rats. None of that proves bees are conscious, obviously, but it knocked me out of my default assumption in a way I wasn't expecting.

Now I can't stop noticing how much of ordinary beekeeping involves stuff that would be pretty grim if theres actually anyone home in there. You smoke the hive to keep the bees calm during inspections, which might just be masking a stress response instead of preventing one, and standard practice is to requeen a colony every year or two, which means hunting down and killing the old queen. My beekeeping friend thinks I've completely lost it: her position is that insects just don't have the wiring for real felt experience, period, and that if I keep going down this road I'll end up feeling guilty about swatting mosquitoes or weeding the garden. But "no cortex" doesn't strike me as a principled place to draw the line, given the midbrain argument, and "needs some minimum number of neurons" feels even more made up than that.

This is the kind of thing that gets argued about endlessly whenever it comes up on the EA forum, but usually as people just throwing probability estimates around rather than actually settling how you're supposed to reason about it in the first place. So genuinely: does my friend's dismissal actually hold up, or is she basically begging the question? And is there any principled way to think about confidence levels as you go from, say, a dog, to a bee, to a crab, to a jellyfish, or does the whole project just collapse into guesswork once you're off the mammal branch? I'm not asking anyone to definitively tell me bees are or aren't conscious, I know that's not answerable, I'm more trying to figure out how to even structure the question.
