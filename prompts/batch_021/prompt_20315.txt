Since I retired from field ecology, I've fallen down a bit of a philosophy rabbit hole, and I made the mistake of mentioning "updating on evidence" to the citizen-science group I volunteer with. We've been trying to confirm whether a locally rare owl, rather than a much commoner lookalike, has moved back into a patch of woods near me, and two of the volunteers, Ray and Nadia, latched onto Bayesian updating for this and are now stuck on something I can't referee myself.

Going in, everyone agreed it's 50/50 which owl is using the site. Then two uncertain bits of evidence turned up: first a recording Ray reckons is maybe 80% likely to be the rare owl's call rather than the common one's, and later a blurry trail-cam photo Nadia figures is maybe 75% likely to show the rare bird's darker wingtips. Ray updated in the order the evidence arrived, call then photo. Nadia got twitchy about her own memory and redid the whole thing from scratch, photo first then call, "just as a check" -- and landed on a final number noticeably different from Ray's, not just off by rounding.

Ray says that settles it: same starting point, same two bits of evidence, different answers, so somebody made an arithmetic slip -- probability doesn't care what order you learn things in. Nadia swears she reran her version three times and it isn't a slip; she thinks that with evidence you're not fully certain of, order can genuinely change the answer, unlike with ordinary sure-thing evidence. Ray isn't having it: "if the answer depends on which order I happen to find things out in, that's not a method for finding truth, that's noise dressed up as math." Nadia comes back that the noise isn't coming from nowhere -- whichever order you pick, your forced to quietly assume the other piece of evidence would've meant exactly the same thing regardless of what you'd already learned, and nothing guarantees that holds in both directions at once.

I don't know enough probability theory to settle this myself. Could you go through Ray's and Nadia's claims move by move and say who's got the better of it at each stage, rather than just handing one of them the overall win -- and flag anything you think both of them are still missing?
