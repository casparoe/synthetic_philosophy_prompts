Random request but this is squarely in my wheelhouse, since explaining ideas like this to normal people is basically my day job. My friend Mara is getting married in three weeks and asked me to give the toast at the rehersal dinner -- mostly, I think, because I once profiled a philosopher for a piece on decision-making under uncertainty, and now the whole friend group is convinced I'm secretly qualified for this kind of thing. I do actually want to build the toast around something real though, not the usual "when you know, you know," because I don't think that's actually true of how Mara ended up with Dev, and I'd feel weird saying it in front of everyone.

Backstory: two years ago Mara was genuinely torn between staying with her ex of six years, who was by any normal measure a "good match" on paper, and taking a leap with Dev, who she'd known maybe four months at that point. She's told me more than once that it wasn't that Dev was better, and it wasn't that the two of them were "equally good so may as well flip a coin" either -- it just didn't feel like either of those things. That's basically Ruth Chang's point about hard choices, I think: some choices are hard not because we're missing information or because the options are secretly tied, but because they're "on a par," a real fourth relation sitting alongside better/worse/equal, and her small-improvement argument (a tiny sweetener added to one option in a genuine tie should tip it ahead, but in cases like this it doesn't) is a pretty good explanation for why Mara could never just make a pros-and-cons list and be done with it. The part I most want in there is her idea that committing to one option isn't discovering which one was secretly best all along -- the committing itself is what generates the reason, going forward, that makes it the right choice.

So: can you write me an actual toast out of this, something that'd run about 4-5 minutes read aloud, where the idea is doing real work instead of getting name-dropped? It has to explain why choosing Dev wasn't Mara finding the "better" guy, without that landing as "so it was arbitrary" or making anyone spend the night thinking about the ex. A chunk of her old philosophy cohort will be at this dinner and will clock a Ruth Chang reference instantly; Dev's side of the family has never heard the word "incommensurable" and never needs to. It should work as a warm, slightly funny toast first, full stop, and only be a philosophy lesson by accident. One decent joke somewhere, please, and it should end on an actual toast line, glasses up.
