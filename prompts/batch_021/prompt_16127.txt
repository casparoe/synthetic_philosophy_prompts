Quick bit of context: I'm a backend/ML engineer at a small consumer-lending startup, and because I built the underwriting model I somehow got pulled onto our "Responsible AI Council" -- a very extra name for six people arguing over Zoom every other Thursday. The model outputs a probability-of-default number for every applicant, which drives pricing and approval decisions. The current version buckets applicants pretty coarsely (state, broad age band, credit tier), but our data science lead wants to ship a v2 that slices much finer -- zip code, income band, five-year age bracket, plus behavioral stuff like which of our other products someone has used. It backtests noticeably better, tighter calibration curves across the board, so on pure metrics it's an easy yes. Legal and the outside ethics consultant we bring in for these meetings are blocking it, and it's become a real fight.

Here's the bit I'm stuck on: everyone keeps collapsing two different objections into one. Objection one is the familiar "this reconstructs race and neighborhood history through the back door" fair-lending worry, which I get fine. Objection two, which the consultant keeps trying to explain, is wierder: even if the finer slicing were completely clean of any protected-class proxy, there's apparently no principled reason that particular grouping is the "correct" one for pricing any given applicant -- you could equally well bucket the same guy by profession, or by his specific repayment history with us, or a dozen other reasonable partitions, and each hands back a different number for the same human being. We actually ran one test applicant through both versions: v1 priced him at roughly 4% probability of default, the new zip/income/behavior slicing put him at almost 8%, and when a teammate rebucketed him by profession and tenure instead he came out under 2%. Nobody on the data science side can answer "why this reference class and not another" with anything better than "it backtests well," and the consultant sent around a philosophy paper (something by a guy named Hájek, "The Reference Class Problem is Your Problem Too") that I read half of on the train and did not fully recover from.

I have to send the council a short written recommendation before Thursday: basically whether v2 ships as-is, gets blocked, or ships with specific conditions, plus a timeframe, since launch is currently penciled in for three weeks out. Could you draft that memo for me? I need it to actually engage with objection two as its own real problem instead of folding it back into the bias conversation and calling it a day, and whatever it recommends, I want it to lay out the strongest version of the case for the option it's turning down, since half the room will read this looking for ammunition against whatever I land on. A short table of conditions at the end would be great if we go that route, but the reasoning that gets us there matters more than the formatting.
