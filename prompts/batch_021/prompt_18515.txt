For my senior capstone project on existential risk, I've been reading Nick Bostrom's work on anthropic reasoning, and I keep running into one exchange about the Doomsday Argument that none of my sources ever fully resolve. I'd like you to write a dialogue between two thinkers who represent both sides of it as sharply as possible.

The first should defend the Doomsday Argument on the basis of the Self-Sampling Assumption, arguing that because you should reason as though randomly sampled from all the humans who will ever live, your comparatively low birth rank is genuine evidence that humanity's total population will end up smaller than you might otherwise expect—and so evidence of a nearer extinction. The second should reject this by invoking the Self-Indication Assumption: once you also factor in the mere fact that you exist at all, which is itself more likely under hypotheses where more observers exist, the anthropic shift toward doom cancels back out.

The exchange shouldn't stop there, though. Have the first thinker press the second on the "presumptuous philosopher" problem that the Self-Indication Assumption seems to invite, and have the second give a genuine answer rather than conceding the point. Both sides should get their strongest moves, and each should actually be responding to what the other just said rather than restating their opening position.
