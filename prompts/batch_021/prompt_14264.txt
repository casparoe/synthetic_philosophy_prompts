One philosopher I grabbed coffee with insisted that, per Dennett's "real patterns" paper, if attributing a belief buys you more predictive leverage than sticking to the design stance, the pattern is exactly as real as it needs to be, full stop, and current LLMs clear that bar easily. A colleague of hers practically choked on her coffee and said that's a bad reading -- that Dennett puts thermostats and chess-playing computers on a continuum precisely because "real" patterns have to be stable ones, and LLMs are famous for asserting P in one turn and flatly denying it a few messages later depending on how the prompt is framed (the sycophancy thing), which should make the "belief" pattern wash out as noise rather than count as real.

I'd love to actually watch these two hash it out rather then just paraphrase them past each other in an article.
