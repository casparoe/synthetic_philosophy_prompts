So I teach a graduate seminar that covers formal epistemology and a bit of AI safety, and most weeks I try to open class with a joke related to whatever we covered last time, partly to wake everyone up and partly on the theory that if you can laugh at a piece of material you've probably actually understood it. Last week was the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents -- the thing where a proof-based agent can't just adopt "whenever I can prove X, X is actually true" as a blanket rule about itself, because Löb's theorem says that if the system could prove that conditional, it would already have to be able to prove X outright. It's basically what stops such an agent (or a successor it's trying to build/verify) from fully vouching for its own future reasoning, which is a real headache in the tiling-agents literature.
