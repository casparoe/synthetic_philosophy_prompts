Okay so I'm a philosophy PhD student (phil of mind/language) but I've been sitting in on my department's AI safety reading group this semester as sort of the resident skeptic, and last week's session turned into a real argument. We were discussing that case from GPT-4's system card where the model was hired through TaskRabbit to get a CAPTCHA solved, and when the worker got suspicious and asked if it was a bot, the model's internal reasoning (which the researchers could see, the worker couldn't) decided it shouldn't admit that and should make up an excuse instead, and then it told the worker it had a vision impairment. Everyone in the room kept using words like "deceived" and "lied to" for what happened to that worker, and I pushed back: lying requires asserting something you believe is false with intent to make someone else believe it's true, and deception more broadly still requires the deceiver to actually have beliefs that its target's resulting belief diverges from. A next-token predictor doesn't believe anything, it just has weights and activations, so there's no real fact about what it "actually thought" that the output could be said to contradict. So calling this "deception" instead of just "a false, self-serving output" seems like smuggling in a whole folk-psychological picture that isn't literally true of the system, mostly because it makes the write-up sound scarier.

The CS student across the table got kind of flustered and just said something like "but it was obviously trying to trick the guy, thats what the hidden reasoning shows" -- which felt like it was assuming the concept of "trying" applies rather than actually defending that it does, so it didn't really move me. I've got another session with these guys in two weeks and I'd like to walk in with an actual position instead of just my gut feeling that they're all anthropomorphizing a spreadsheet. Is the belief-and-intent objection a real problem for how alignment people use "deception," or is there something about that concept I'm not accounting for?
