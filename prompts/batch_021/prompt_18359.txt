I'm a public health reporter writing a retrospective on a 2022 outbreak traced to a single wedding reception. An epidemiologist I interviewed says the outbreak simply reflects a general pattern of room size, ventilation, and event duration — one that predicts this outbreak about as well as it predicts a dozen others on record. The county's contact tracer, who reconstructed the night guest by guest, insists the real explanation is a particular, unrepeatable sequence of small events: a broken air handler, a long toast, one still-asymptomatic guest who worked the room. My editor wants one clean cause for the piece. Do I have to choose between these two accounts, or can both be right, and how should that shape what I write?
