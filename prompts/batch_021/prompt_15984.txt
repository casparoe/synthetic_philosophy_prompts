So in my epistemic game theory seminar we just spent two weeks on Aumann's agreement theorem and the Milgrom-Stokey no-trade theorem - the whole "you can't agree to disagree" and "rational agents with common priors shouldn't be able to speculate against each other on private info alone" result. Some of us were arguing about it over coffee afterwards because it basically implies that most of what looks like informed trading in real markets (or even just arguing with people online tbh) has to either be irrational or resting on different priors somewhere, since if it where common knowledge that you want to trade with me, that fact alone should already move my beliefs enough to kill the deal.

Totally separately, in my history of economic thought class we've been slogging through Keynes, and it hit me that he was kind of already circling this exact territory decades before Aumann, just from a completely different angle - the beauty-contest passage in ch. 12 of the General Theory, "animal spirits," and his "weight of evidence" idea from the Treatise on Probability, where he's arguing against people like Ramsey (who wanted probability to just be subjective personal betting odds) that probability is an objective logical relation and that the amount of evidence behind a probability is a separate axis from the probability itself.

So what I can't stop thinking about is: what would Keynes actually say if someone explained Aumann and Milgrom-Stokey to him? Would he even grant the common prior assumption the theorems need, given how hard he pushed back on treating probability as just personal degrees of belief? And would he read the no-trade result as basically vindicating his beauty-contest picture of speculative markets (i.e. real trading volume is way higher than "rational" agents should produce, so the model's picture of the trader must be missing something) or as a genuine threat to it? Can you reconstruct what you think his most plausible position would actually be, and argue for why that's the best reading of his views rather than just a vibes-based guess?
