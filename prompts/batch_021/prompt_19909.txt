Coherence arguments — the money-pump and Dutch-book style arguments used to motivate the axioms of expected utility theory — establish that any agent whose choices avoid exploitation must be representable as maximizing the expectation of some utility function. Since a sufficiently capable, self-improving AI system would presumably eliminate any exploitable inconsistencies in its own preferences, doesn't this mean such a system is mathematically guaranteed to become a ruthless, single-minded optimizer pursuing some fixed goal? If the coherence theorems really establish this, it is puzzling that so many researchers in AI safety still treat "goal-directedness" as an open empirical question rather than as a settled mathematical consequence of advanced agents being coherent.
