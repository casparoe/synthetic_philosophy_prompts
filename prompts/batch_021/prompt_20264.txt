Ok so I fell down a rabbit hole reading about Einstein's "hole argument" against spacetime substantivalism and now I really want to see it played out as an actual dialogue. Quick recap in case I'm garbling any of this: because GR's field equations are diffeomorphism invariant, you can take a solution (M,g), smoothly warp the metric only inside some little "hole" region H while leaving everything outside H untouched, and the warped g' is still a solution, one that agrees with g everywhere outside H but disagrees inside it. If spacetime points are real individuals in their own right, independant of the metric (ie substantivalism), this seems to mean GR can't determine what happens inside H even given complete data on its entire past -- which is basically the move Earman and Norton used to argue that substantivalism buys you an totally unmotivated indeterminism.

Can you write me a dialogue between a relationist who thinks this is pretty much fatal for substantivalism, and a "sophisticated" substantivalist who thinks you can dodge the indeterminism by denying that spacetime points have any identity across the two solutions that's independent of the metric (so g and g' were never actually rival possibilities to begin with). I want the relationist to really press on whether that move just quietly gives away the whole store -- like, isn't "sophisticated substantivalism" just relationism wearing a substantivalist nametag at that point -- and I want the substantivalist to have an actual comeback, not just a shrug. Neither of them should strawman the other or cave immediately, they should each be responding to the specific thing the other just said.
