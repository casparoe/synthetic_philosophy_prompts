In my graduate seminar on philosophy of mind, we have spent the last few weeks working through David Chalmers's two-dimensionalist case against a posteriori physicalism, and this week's discussion got unexpectedly heated. Chalmers accepts Kripke's point that statements like "water is H2O" or "heat is mean molecular kinetic energy" are necessary yet knowable only a posteriori, and he explains this via the primary/secondary intension distinction: the primary intension of "water" could, evaluated at some other epistemically possible scenario considered as actual, have picked out something other than H2O (XYZ, say), even though the secondary intension is fixed once we know what actually fills that role at our world. His argument against type-B materialism then turns on the claim that phenomenal concepts are not like this. For a concept such as "the feeling I am now having," Chalmers holds that primary and secondary intension necessarily coincide: there is no epistemically possible scenario in which the concept, considered from the inside, could pick out something other than the very phenomenal quality it actually picks out, in the way "water" could have picked out something other than H2O. This asymmetry is what is supposed to block the type-B materialist's preferred move of treating phenomenal-physical identity statements as just another Kripkean a posteriori necessity, on a par with water and H2O. A few students in the seminar revived what is essentially a Stalnaker-style metasemantic worry about this move, and I would like a rigorous brief on their side of the question to bring back to class.

Please argue that Chalmers's claim — that primary and secondary intension necessarily coincide for phenomenal concepts but can come apart for terms like "water" — is unmotivated, and that once this asymmetry is denied, the two-dimensional argument against a posteriori physicalism loses its force. I would like you to engage with the actual mechanics of how primary intensions are supposed to be fixed in the first place (whether via a priori conceptual analysis, via canonical descriptions of epistemically possible scenarios couched in some neutral core vocabulary, or by some other means), rather than resting content with a general deflationary shrug about the reliability of conceivability arguments. In particular, show exactly where and why a type-B materialist is entitled to resist Chalmers's asymmetry, rather than simply asserting that intuitions about zombies and inverted qualia cannot be trusted.
