So I've been down a rabbit hole on the Knobe effect for a piece on folk psychology and blame, and I've hit a debate in the xphi literature I can't quite referee myself. The classic finding: people say a CEO 'intentionally' harmed the environment when it was a side effect of a profit move, but say the exact same kind of decision was NOT intentional when the side effect helped the environment instead -- even though the CEO gives the identical 'I don't care either way, I just want the money' line both times.

What I can't figure out is why this happens. Knobe's own line is basically that moral badness leaks straight into our concept of intentional action, full stop. But there's a rival 'deep self' camp (Sripada's work, I believe) that says it's got nothing special to do with morality -- it's about whether the side effect fits the agent's actual cares, and harm/help cases are just the most vivid version of a wider pattern.

Can you write me a dialogue between one philosopher from each camp? Don't worry about the framing, a conference bar after a talk is fine, whatever -- I just want them to actually grapple with cases that are hard for their own side, like a character whose deep-seated values point one way while the outcome's moral valence points the other, rather then just restating their own framework past each other. Neither one should be the obvious strawman.
