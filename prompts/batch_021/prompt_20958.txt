Bit of background: I run the parts counter at an auto shop, never went to college, but Rachel did a whole philosophy PhD and her specific area is modal metaphysics -- she calls herself a "card-carrying Lewisian modal realist," which from what I understand after years of her explaining it to me over holidays means she thinks every possible world is just as real and solid as this one, that "actual" is basically like saying "here" or "now" -- it doesn't mean special, just means the world we happen to be standing in -- and that there's no literal me-in-another-world, just "counterparts" that resemble us but aren't strictly us. About a dozen people coming to the wedding are her whole grad school department, people who work on this stuff for a living, and everyone else is family who has never heard the phrase "possible world" in their life.

Here's my problem. I wrote a draft where I said something like "out of every possible version of your life, you found each other in this one," and she read it and text back (nicely, she wasn't mad) that this is basically false given what she actually believes -- there's no guarantee your counterparts even meet in most worlds, let alone marry each other, and I shouldn't act like this world being "actual" makes it the one that counts, since on her view that's just an indexical fact about where we're standing, not some metaphysical prize it won. She said she'd rather I say nothing about possible worlds at all than say something sentimental that isn't true by her own lights. But it feels wrong to give a wedding speech for her that just pretends she isn't a philosopher, this is like the biggest thing in her head most days. I've rewritten the thing four separate times now and it either comes out fake-sweet or turns into me trying to teach grandma what a counterpart relation is.

Can you write me the speech? Something like 3-4 minutes read out loud, that actually respects her metaphysics (or atleast doesn't say anything a real Lewisian would wince at) but still lands as a warm, genuine toast for people who've never heard of David Lewis. Bonus points if you can sneak in one line that her grad school friends will clock as an actual philosophy joke while everyone else just hears a nice wedding joke.
