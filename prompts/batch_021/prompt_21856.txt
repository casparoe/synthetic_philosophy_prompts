Classic move in the Humean vs anti-Humean laws-of-nature debate: "every gold sphere is under a mile in diameter" is true but purely accidental (nothing stops a bigger one, we just never made one), while "every U-235 sphere is under a mile in diameter" is true AND lawlike, since anything that big goes supercritical and blows itself apart long before reaching that size. Same grammatical shape, same track record so far, totally different modal status - which is awkward for Humeans, since lawhood is supposed to be read off the actual pattern of events, not smuggled in from outside. I want a brand new pair like this - not gold/uranium, not an obvious reskin of it, genuinely seperate subject matter - where the same trick works. Then be honest: could a Lewis-style best-system theorist actually sort your two generalizations correctly just from the pattern of events, or would they have to quietly borrow something anti-Humean to get the right answer?
