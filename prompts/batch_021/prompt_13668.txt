I'm a novelist working on a book about a fictional Victorian woman who moves from evangelical piety, through the biblical criticism fashionable in her day, into a settled unbelief that nonetheless keeps a markedly religious emotional register. George Eliot's own intellectual trajectory is my model for this, and I want my character's inner life to be intellectually specific rather than a vague "crisis of faith." Years before she wrote fiction, Eliot (then Marian Evans) published a stinging essay on the evangelical preacher Dr. Cumming and translated Feuerbach's *The Essence of Christianity*, whose central claim is that God is nothing but humanity's own best qualities -- love, conscience, reason -- alienated, projected outward, and worshipped as though independent of us. I'd like you to compare this Feuerbachian account of religion with the religious sensibility that actually emerges in Eliot's mature fiction. Does a figure like Dinah Morris's preaching in *Adam Bede*, or the communal, quasi-national faith Daniel Deronda embraces by the end of that novel, simply dramatize Feuerbach's thesis in narrative form, or does either case resist or complicate it? I want an answer with real argumentative substance to it, not the standard "she lost her faith but kept her Victorian moral seriousness" summary.
