I have spent a while thinking about Hume's actual argument against accepting reports of miracles, which is more specific than the popular "extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence" slogan it often gets reduced to: a reasonable person, on his view, weighs the strength of a piece of testimony against the weight of our uniform experience of the very law of nature the reported miracle would violate, and real-world testimony almost never clears that bar regardless. Suppose Hume were somehow brought up to speed on deepfakes and other tools for fabricating convincing video and audio evidence. Would he treat a fabricated video of an apparent miracle as simply a new, unusually persuasive kind of testimony, to be weighed by exactly the same reasoning as an old traveler's tale, or does the sheer existence of such technology change the shape of his argument, rather than just handing it a fresh example to work through?
