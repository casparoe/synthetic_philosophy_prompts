A friend of mine works remotely for a company based elsewhere and just got a raise, but she's not sure it means much. She lives in a smallish town that has absorbed a lot of well-paid remote workers like her over the past several years, and local wages, rents, and everyday prices have all risen sharply along with them, to the point that her raise may not buy her any more than her old salary did. And yet the town doesn't feel poorer to her—there is visibly more building, more employment, more general bustle than five years ago, even though the same stock of real goods should, on paper, just be chasing a bigger pile of dollars. She's asked me whether the town is actually better off or whether this is some kind of collective illusion, and while I've seen the phrase "money illusion" thrown around on economics blogs, I don't think it actually settles the question. What's the right way to think this through?
