Two friends of mine spent most of a long car ride arguing about a puzzle from the philosophy of probability known as the Judy Benjamin problem, and I still haven't managed to settle it in my own head. The setup: you're dropped somewhere in a territory divided into a Red half and a Blue half, each further divided into two zones, so there are four equally likely zones to start, each with probability 1/4. You then receive a radio message telling you, with certainty, that if you're in Red territory, you're three times more likely to be in Red-2 than in Red-1. The message says nothing at all about Blue, or about how likely Red is compared to Blue.

(1) Leo said the principled way to update is to take, among all distributions consistent with that one constraint, whichever is closest to your prior in the Kullback–Leibler sense -- what's often described as "minimizing cross-entropy" relative to the prior. He said doing that computation properly moves your probability of being in Blue from 1/2 up to about 0.53 (and Red down to about 0.47) -- even though the message never compared Red to Blue -- and that this is simply what the correct generalization of Bayesian updating to conditional evidence delivers, so the sense that it's paradoxical is a mistaken intuition one should drop.

(2) Mira said that can't be right: since the message says nothing comparing Red and Blue, a rational agent's confidence about which color she's on shouldn't move at all. The only sane update, on her view, keeps P(Red) = P(Blue) = 1/2 and redistributes mass only inside Red, to 1/8 and 3/8. If a formal procedure disagrees with that, the procedure is what's wrong.

(3) Leo replied that "just hold the Red/Blue split fixed" isn't an assumption-free default either -- it's exactly as much of a substantive modeling choice as the KL update is; it just happens to be the choice that matches her intuition.

(4) Mira answered that the two choices aren't on equal footing: fixing P(Red) = P(Blue) respects a symmetry that the problem itself states outright, since the new information doesn't distinguish Red from Blue, whereas the KL update ignores that symmetry entirely, treating all four zones as one undifferentiated pool. A general-purpose rule that fails to preserve a symmetry explicitly present in a given case, she said, counts against the rule, not against the symmetry.

(5) Leo said this misdiagnoses things: the prior is symmetric between Red and Blue, sure, but nobody is updating the prior -- they're updating on a piece of information that is itself asymmetric, since it concerns Red and says nothing about Blue, and nothing guarantees that a symmetric prior plus asymmetric information must produce a posterior that preserves the old symmetry. That, he said, is the very question at issue, not something either of them gets for free.

They kept going like this for the rest of the drive without either budging. I was in the car for all of it and still can't adjudicate it myself.
