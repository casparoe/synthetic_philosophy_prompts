Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have been reading my way into epistemology, and I keep returning to Popper's falsifiability criterion — the touchstone, throughout my working life, for separating serious hypotheses from empty ones. Think of his own favorite contrast: Einstein's risky eclipse prediction against the endlessly accommodating theories of Freud and Adler. It strikes me that conspiracy theories are simply the popular version of the Freud/Adler failure mode, since any inconvenient fact can always be absorbed by quietly expanding the alleged cover-up. If that is right, Popper has already given us a clean, general test for when a belief forfeits its claim to rationality, which leaves me puzzled as to why philosophers still devote whole books to adjudicating, case by case, which conspiracy theories are more or less reasonable, rather than simply treating the entire category as settled.
