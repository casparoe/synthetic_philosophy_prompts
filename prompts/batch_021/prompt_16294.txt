My great-aunt died last week — she taught Islamic philosophy for something like 30 years and her whole thing, not just professionally but like actually believed it, was al-Ghazali's occasionalism. She'd get genuinely annoyed if you said the kettle "caused" the water to boil. I got asked to give the eulogy, which is funny timing because I'm in a philosophy of religion seminar right now and we just finished the unit on Ghazali and causation. She left a note saying whoever eulogizes her can't say the usual stuff, like "she shaped so many students" or "her influence lives on," because that gives a creature real causal power, and on her view she was never a cause, only ever an occasion for whatever God habitually chose to do around her. Also nothing carries its own power forward from one moment to the next on her view anyway, its all just getting recreated by God each instant, so even "her effect continues" isn't a comfort she'd have accepted. Can someone write this eulogy so it actually respects that instead of quietly ignoring it, but still lands for a room that's mostly non-philosopher family plus a few of her old colleagues who will absolutely clock it if the doctrine's wrong. Needs to be about 5 minutes spoken.
