My uncle, a dairy farmer who never opened a philosophy book, used to justify his gentleness with animals by saying that a man cruel to a dumb creature ends up cruel to his own family -- never that the animal's own suffering was the point. At the wake, a philosopher friend and I argued for two hours over whether this makes him a kind of folk Kantian, since Kant, in the Metaphysics of Morals, denies that animals are ends in themselves and grounds the whole duty against cruelty in what it does to a person's character rather than in anything owed to the animal. I'm giving his eulogy tomorrow to a room of family and neighbors, plus that same friend in the third row, who will know at once if I quietly sentimentalize my uncle's view, or Kant's, into something it isn't. Write the eulogy so the room leaves convinced my uncle was a genuinely tender man about animals, without ever claiming -- falsely -- that he thought they mattered for their own sake.
