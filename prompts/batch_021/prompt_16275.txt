I'm plotting a novel in which two estranged colleagues—a logician who has never stopped believing in Gödel's ontological proof, and the former student who broke with her over it—meet again after many years and finish the argument they started. I need their exchange written as a dialogue of several hundred words, one that earns its place in the book by staying honest to the logic rather than settling for a "faith versus reason" shorthand. The logician should defend the proof past the obvious objections and engage seriously with the modal collapse problem—the result that Gödel's axioms on positive properties, run through S5, appear to entail that whatever is true is necessarily true—reaching for one of the genuine technical responses proposed in the literature rather than changing the subject. The former student should press exactly where that response is weakest, using the actual structure of the axioms rather than a generic complaint that no argument could prove God exists. Neither character should win outright; end the scene with both of them seeing more clearly what the other's position costs them.
