Two of my former doctoral students are getting married next month, and I have been asked to give the toast. Both wrote dissertations on grounding and fundamentality, though from opposite ends of the same disagreement: he defended the orthodox Finean picture, on which grounding is a strict partial order — asymmetric, irreflexive, transitive — and, on the further, stronger assumption he was always most committed to, well-founded, so that every chain of grounding has to terminate somewhere in fundamentalia, things not themselves grounded in anything further. She spent her dissertation resisting exactly that last assumption, pressing cases of mutual or symmetric dependence and arguing that nothing rules out the whole edifice resting, as she puts it after a glass of wine, on something more like turtles all the way down. (She flirted briefly with Schaffer-style priority monism in her second year, before her committee, me included, talked her out of it as a way of dodging the question rather than answering it.) They apparently got together arguing about exactly this at a Pacific APA reception, and as far as I can tell, neither has conceded an inch since.

I would like the toast to turn on this disagreement rather than just mention it in passing. Ideally its central joke treats the marriage itself as a kind of test case: is one of them the more fundamental partner, with the other grounded in them, or is this the mutual, possibly non-well-founded dependence she was defending all along, or does their union need some further fact to ground it that neither has published on yet? The complication is that roughly half the guests are philosophers who will notice immediately if I mangle the logic of ground, while the other half are parents, cousins, and college roommates who have never heard "grounding" used this way and shouldn't need a glossary to enjoy the joke. Could you draft about four minutes of remarks that manage this: affectionate rather than pedantic, funny without turning into a seminar, and ending on a toast line I can actually deliver with a raised glass?
