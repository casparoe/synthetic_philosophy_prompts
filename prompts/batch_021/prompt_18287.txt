Okay so I fell down a rabbit hole this week re-reading old Less Wrong / Overcoming Bias era posts on acausal trade -- the idea that two agents who can never causally interact (say becuase they're in causally seperated regions of a spatially infinite universe, or one is just a hypothetical the other is reasoning about) can still end up cooperating with each other purely by reasoning about each other's decision procedures, sort of like an extreme version of the twin prisoner's dilemma. My reading group ended up arguing about this for way too long -- specifically about whether it's a real enough phenomenon that, say, an aligned AI's designers should actually build in a willingness to make concessions toward the values of hypothetical unaligned AIs elsewhere in the universe/multiverse (basically Oesterheld's "multiverse-wide superrationality" pitch, if I'm remembering the framing right, partly motivated by wanting to avoid worst-case outcomes), versus whether the whole framing is a bit of a category error -- like, calling it "trade" smuggles in connotations of promises and enforcement that can't possibly apply when there's zero causal contact and no way to ever check whether the other side held up its end.

Could you write me a dialogue between two people hashing this out? One is a convinced acausal trader who's happy to lean on stuff like the provability-logic "FairBot" result (two agents who can inspect each other's source code proving mutual cooperation via Löb's theorem) as evidence that cooperation-without-causation isn't just hand-waving, and who wants to push that logic outward to much vaguer, unobservable trading partners. The other is a sharp skeptic who'll grant the tidy shared-source-code cases but keeps pressing on what's left once you drop common knowledge of source code -- their worry is that at that point all you actually have is your own credence about what's out there, and dressing that up as "trade" rather than "expected value reasoning under uncertainty" is doing illegitimate rhetorical work. I want both characters to be genuinely sharp and actually responding to each other rather than talking past each other -- the skeptic shouldn't just be missing the point, and the trader should have real comebacks instead of just biting bullets -- and it'd be great if partway through they had to cash things out in one concrete toy scenario instead of staying fully abstract the whole way through.
