On one influential refinement of causal decision theory, rational choice in cases like Death in Damascus must be confined to self-ratifying options: options that maximize expected utility conditional on being performed. Neither staying in Damascus nor fleeing to Aleppo is self-ratifying, since updating on either choice gives the man reason to think he would have fared better choosing the other. The standard fix has him randomize instead: a mixed strategy between the two can be self-ratifying, but only given the further stipulation that Death cannot predict the outcome of the randomizing device. Please argue that this fix does not rehabilitate ratifiability as a requirement of rational choice, but merely conceals the puzzle's original instability behind an unmotivated asymmetry between what Death can and cannot foresee. Address, in particular, whether anything short of saving the theory could justify treating the chance device as unpredictable, given that Death is otherwise stipulated to predict the man's reasoning and resulting behavior with near-perfect reliability.
