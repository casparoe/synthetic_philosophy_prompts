I recently joined a geopolitical risk consultancy as an analyst, and our forecasting platform fuses qualitative source reporting into numerical probabilities using minimum relative-entropy (cross-entropy) updating whenever a report arrives as a conditional odds statement rather than a flat probability. During onboarding, my team worked through the classic "Judy Benjamin" scenario as a sanity check on the method: a soldier is initially equally uncertain whether she is in Red or Blue territory, and independently equally uncertain whether she is in the headquarters area or the non-headquarters area of whichever territory she is actually in, so each of the four combinations starts out with probability one quarter. A radio report then tells her that, given that she is in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 in favor of the Red headquarters area over the Red non-headquarters area. When I reproduced the minimum-relative-entropy solution by hand, the overall probability of being in Red territory dropped from one half to under 47%, even though the report never compares Red to Blue and leaves the headquarters/non-headquarters split within Blue exactly even. Since the report is entirely internal to Red territory, I have been assuming this cross-partition shift must be a defect in our vendor's implementation, or an error in my own derivation, that is letting information leak somewhere it has no business going. Could you help me pin down which step is producing this mistake, so that I can raise it with the vendor?
