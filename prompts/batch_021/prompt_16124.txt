For my birthday last month, my aunt gave me a gift certificate for a session in a sensory-deprivation float tank, and the experience has stuck with me more than the relaxation itself did. You lie in a sealed pod of water heated to skin temperature and so saturated with Epsom salt that you float without any effort; the lid shuts out every trace of light, there is no sound, and you're told to position your arms and legs so that no part of your body touches any other part. About ten minutes in, I completely lost track of where my skin ended and the water began, and I honestly could not have told you whether my arms were crossed on my chest or stretched out flat — for a stretch of time it genuinely felt as though I had no body at all. What struck me afterward is that this was nothing like drifting off to sleep: if anything, I felt unusually alert and clearly still myself, still thinking, still mildly annoyed that the session was taking so long, even though by that point I had no sensory evidence whatsoever — no touch, sight, sound, or felt sense of my own limbs — that I possessed a body. We had just finished a unit on consciousness in my AP Psychology class, which is probably why I couldn't just enjoy the float and instead spent the whole drive home turning it over in my head.

When I described it to two friends, they went in completely opposite directions. One said the experience proves nothing, since whatever was doing the "noticing" inside that tank was still just my brain, a physical organ that had simply stopped receiving signals from the rest of my body for a while — a neurological quirk, not a discovery. The other said it was actually significant, on the grounds that I had apparently managed to be fully aware of myself while being aware of absolutely no bodily sensation, which suggests that whatever "I" refers to isn't simply identical to my body. Neither argument feels fully airtight to me, but I can't pin down exactly where either one goes wrong, and a bit of searching online mostly turned up articles about relaxation and stress relief rather than anything addressing the actual question. Does an experience like this really tell us anything about whether a mind or self could exist independently of a body, or is my second friend smuggling in a much bigger conclusion than the experience can actually support? I'd like a careful, honest verdict on which of my two friends is arguing more soundly, and why.
