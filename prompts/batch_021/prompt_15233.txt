Okay, this one's a bit weird, but I've been on a George Eliot kick lately (blame Middlemarch) and can't stop noticing how close her ethics ends up to EA/rationalist stuff, despite predating it by something like 150 years. She translates Strauss's Life of Jesus and then Feuerbach's Essence of Christianity back to back in her twenties and thirties, comes out the other side with the actual Christian metaphysics gutted, and then famously tells Myers in that Trinity garden that of the three words "God, Immortality, Duty," the first two are basically unbelievable to her but the third is still absolute and non-negotiable - i.e. keep the action-relevant part of the moral system, drop the metaphysical scaffolding it used to rest on. Then Middlemarch ends on that whole passage about "unhistoric acts" and people who "rest in unvisited tombs," quiet unglamorous goodness mattering even when nobody's watching or counting it.

Given all that, plus her sympathy for the Comtean "religion of humanity" crowd, what do you think she'd actually make of present-day EA if she saw it - earning to give, shut-up-and-multiply utilitarianism, GiveWell spreadsheets, longtermism and x-risk, the whole apparatus? Part of me thinks she'd recognize it as her own instincts finally getting systematized properly; part of me thinks she'd be deeply suspicious of it as exactly the kind of totalizing abstract system (think Casaubon, or Bulstrode's self-serving rationalizations) that ends up loosing track of actual particular people. Which impulse do you reckon wins out, and why?
