So I need a sanity check on something that's been bugging me since office hours last week, if you don't mind indulging me. A student in my philosophy seminar on personal identity — sharp kid, doing a joint degree with physics — came by right after we'd covered teleporter and fission thought experiments, and announced he's taken up free solo climbing, no ropes, the kind of thing where one slip means you die. When I asked if he'd actually thought through the risk, he laid out this whole argument: on the many-worlds view, every time theres a moment where he might die, the universe branches into a version where he falls and a version where he doesn't, and either way his own stream of consciousness can only ever continue in a branch where he's still around to have experiences — there's no branch where he "experiences" being dead. So the only real cost of his climbing, he says, falls on the people who'd grieve him in the branches where he dies, not on "future him," whos guaranteed, from the inside, to keep waking up alive no matter how many times he does this. When I pointed out that the branches where he survives get rarer and rarer the more he climbs, he waved it off — rarity is a third-person worry, he said, and the guy actually living through it doesn't experience being a thin sliver of the multiverse, he just experiences being alive, full stop. He even brought up a cleaner version of the idea, some device wired to a radioactive atom with a 50/50 chance of shooting you on every tick, and insisted his climbing is basically a messier analog of that same machine.

I told him this sounded like a rationalization wearing a lab coat, but when I actually sat down to locate the flawed step, I got stuck, and a colleague of mine in the physics department just said "have him read about decoherence," which wasn't much help. So: even granting him the many-worlds interpretation for the sake of argument, is there a solid case that his conclusion doesn't actually follow — that surviving-in-some-branch reasoning shouldn't license indifference to risk the way he thinks it does? And does it matter that his climbing "branches" aren't as clean as the radioactive-atom case, or is that a distinction without a difference? I'd like something with actual teeth to bring back to him next week, not just a shrug about how reckless he sounds.
