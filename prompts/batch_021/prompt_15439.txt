okay so I'm on my school's Honor Council (the student group that hears academic integrity cases and recommends a sanction to the dean) and I got stuck drafting the memo we vote on this Thursday. The case has the whole council split. During finals a proctor says she personally watched a kid looking at his phone under his desk during a closed-note exam, but by the time she got over there the phone was already put away, theres no camera in that room, and the kid swears he was just adjusting his watch. A violation here means an automatic F on the final plus a permanent-record note, so it actually matters. Our bylaws require "clear and sufficient evidence" but never define what counts. Half the council thinks one credible adult's eyewitness account just is evidence, full stop -- we don't usually demand backup every time we believe what someone tells us, so why start here. The other half thinks testimony with nothing independent behind it isnt enough to justify something this serious, especially since eyewitness memory is famously unreliable. I only realized afterward that this is basically the reductionism vs anti-reductionism debate about testimony (long story, I've been on an epistemology youtube kick). Can you actually draft the memo I'm bringing in -- it needs to land on one real recommendation, not a "both sides have a point" cop-out, and it has to take the strongest version of whichever side it ends up rejecting seriously instead of just brushing it off.
