Now that I'm retired I've finally had time to read philosophy for the first time since university, which is probably why a decision about my own retirement savings has been bugging me way more than a purely financial question should. I spent almost forty years as a geophysicist modelling earthquake hazard for insurance companies, so I like to think I have decent instincts about probability, but my son-in-law (who works in finance) doesn't think my hesitation over the following choice makes any sense. He's helping me figure out what to do with a chunk of savings. Option A is a fixed annuity: a guaranteed $12,000 a year for life, no surprises. Option B is a fund that, based on some Monte Carlo runs he did on historical market data (funnily enough, the same technique I used for decades on hazard curves), comes out to roughly a 90% chance of $15,000 a year, an 8% chance of $9,000, and a 2% chance of a bad stretch landing me around $3,000 a year.

I redid the numbers myself afterward, and even plugging in a fairly conservative curve for how the value of money tapers off as you have more of it (log utility, if you want to get technical), option B still comes out ahead. So it's not just that I'm failing to account for losses hurting more than equivalent gains help, which is the first thing he assumed. He says that settles it and that sticking with the annuity is just fear dressed up as prudence. But when I actually introspect on what's bothering me, its not really the size of that 2% outcome, its more about how much say a small sliver of bad probability should get over the whole decision, almost regardless of the dollar figure attached to it. I mentioned this to an old colleague and she said it reminded her of some paradox from a decision theory course she took decades ago, though she couldn't remember the details, which has only made me more curious rather than less. Is there some genuinely rational basis for sticking with the sure thing here, or should I just admit I'm rationalizing plain old fear and switch to option B like the numbers say?
