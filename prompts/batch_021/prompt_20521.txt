I've been reporting on debates over AI and the mind, and one argument keeps resurfacing. Gödel's first incompleteness theorem establishes that any consistent formal system powerful enough to express arithmetic contains a true statement that the system itself cannot prove. As John Lucas argued in 1961, and Roger Penrose later developed at book length, a human mathematician reasoning about such a system from the outside can nonetheless recognize that this statement is true. Since that seems to be a matter of rigorous mathematical demonstration rather than philosophical speculation, I'm puzzled why so many philosophers of mind and cognitive scientists still resist the Lucas-Penrose conclusion that the mind cannot simply be a computer executing a fixed algorithm. Doesn't Gödel's theorem, combined with this observation about mathematical insight, amount to a genuine proof that mechanism about the mind is false?
