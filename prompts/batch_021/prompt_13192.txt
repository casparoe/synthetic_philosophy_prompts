I've been re-reading von Neumann's 1955 Fortune piece "Can We Survive Technology?" for a story I'm drafting, and the bit that sticks with me isn't his fear of any single gadget but his bigger claim -- that humanity used to survive its own worst inventions by literally having somewhere else to go (open frontiers, empty land, etc), and once that option closes up there's no clean technical or political fix left, just patience and flexibilty. Thing is, in his actual day job he was a hawkish Cold War guy who didn't trust arms-control treaties with the Soviets as far as he could throw them. So genuinely curious: given those two instincts pulling in opposite directions, whats your best guess at what he'd say about solar geoengineering today -- like a startup or a mid-size country just going ahead and dimming sunlight to cool the planet with basically zero binding global agreement in place? Does the "no clean fix, just muddle through" von Neumann shrug that off as inevitable, or does the hawk in him see it as exactly the reckless unilateral move he'd have warned against?
