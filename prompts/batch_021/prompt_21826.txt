I'm working on a feature about the mathematical foundations of machine learning, and several researchers I've interviewed describe Solomonoff induction as having settled the old philosophical debate over Occam's razor. Because Kolmogorov complexity supplies an objective, machine-independent measure of how simple a hypothesis is, and Solomonoff's universal prior weights every hypothesis by exactly that measure, they say that preferring simpler theories is now a proven theorem rather than a matter of scientific taste. If that's an accurate characterization, why do machine learning researchers still argue so fiercely over which inductive biases or priors to build into their models, instead of simply engineering ever-better approximations of this one, already-optimal prior?
