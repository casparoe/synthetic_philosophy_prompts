I compete on my school's ethics bowl team, and I keep running into the same problem when we prepare cases. When I feel strongly that something is wrong — for instance, that it is acceptable to care more about people close to me than about total strangers, or that a certain practice is simply repugnant and therefore wrong — I cannot tell whether that reaction is a genuine moral insight or just an ingrained bias produced by my upbringing, my culture, or evolution. What troubles me more is that I cannot find a neutral test for telling the two apart that would not also disqualify intuitions I am not willing to give up, such as the conviction that unnecessary suffering is bad, since that conviction presumably has just as much of a causal history behind it as the ones I am suspicious of. Could you describe the most important implications of this difficulty for two practical things: how much weight I should give my own convictions during a round when I cannot independently verify where they came from, and how I should respond when a teammate's gut reaction to a case conflicts with mine and neither of us can prove that the other is the one who is merely biased?
