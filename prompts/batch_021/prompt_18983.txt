"Explain how Millikan's teleosemantic theory of mental content is meant to solve the disjunction problem, using the standard example of a frog's fly-detecting mechanism firing in response to BB pellets as well as flies. Then explain why Davidson's Swampman thought experiment is typically taken to pose a special difficulty for teleosemantics, over and above whatever difficulty it poses for causal or informational theories of content more generally."

Here is the student's answer:

"The disjunction problem arises for purely causal or informational theories because the frog's fly-detecting mechanism is triggered by BB pellets as reliably as by flies, so if content is simply whatever reliably causes a state to fire, the mechanism's content should be disjunctive -- fly-or-BB-pellet -- rather than simply fly, leaving no room for the mechanism to misrepresent a BB pellet as a fly. Millikan's teleosemantics addresses this by grounding content in the mechanism's proper function: the detector exists, and was reproduced across generations, because it succeeded often enough in picking out flies specifically, a fact about its selection history rather than about its current causal dispositions. Content is then fixed by the Normal conditions for performing this function -- roughly, the conditions that typically obtain when the mechanism is triggered in a healthy, well-functioning frog -- so a BB pellet, which triggers the mechanism only rarely, counts as a malfunction rather than a shift in content. Millikan's own example of Swampman, a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a person assembled by chance and lacking any evolutionary or learning history, was designed to show that her theory extends smoothly to brand-new organisms, since Swampman's belief-forming mechanisms could acquire proper functions almost as soon as he begins interacting with his surroundings. This is generally regarded as one of the more elegant successes of teleosemantics, since rival causal and informational theories have no comparable resources for explaining how a newly created organism could have contentful states at all."
