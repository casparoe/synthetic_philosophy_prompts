While researching a novel, I have spent the past year interviewing two unrelated elderly former dancers by telephone, without ever seeing a photograph of either, and can now tell their voices apart instantly. Amused by this, they have proposed that when we finally meet next week, no one will tell me who is who, to see whether I can work it out just by looking. My editor is certain I will know at once, since a voice and a face belong to the same person, so knowing one this well must mean I already, in effect, know the other. Something about that strikes me as wrong, though I cannot locate the flaw, and I would rather not embarrass myself in front of two women I admire. Is my editor right to be so confident, or am I right to doubt it?
