My friend's older brother got diagnosed with stage 4 cancer a few months ago, doctors gave him something like a 10% shot if he does the full treatment. Pretty early on he decided he's just gonna believe he'll be one of the ones who makes it, and he's stopped listening to any of the survival odds stuff, tells people "doubt will kill me faster than the cancer will." He's also been putting off writing a will and hasn't really said proper goodbyes to anyone, because in his head that would mean admitting he's going to lose.

This turned into a whole argument between two of my other friends, Sam and Dee, over text (I was just watching, wasn't part of it, honestly can see where both of them are coming from):

(1) Sam: he doesn't get to just decide the odds don't apply to him. There's actual data here, and choosing to believe something the evidence doesn't support, then making real decisions off the back of it like delaying the will and skipping goodbyes, that's reckless not brave.

(2) Dee: but not deciding is still a decision, he has to wake up and live his actual remaining days either way. why is defaulting to "I'm probably gonna die" any more rational than defaulting to hope, neither one is what the evidence actually forces on him.

(3) Sam: that's a false choice though, he could hope and fight for it and still get the will sorted "just in case," those two things aren't opposites.

(4) Dee: sure, maybe on the will, but your missing the bigger part, the belief itself seems to actually be helping him, he's eating better, sleeping better, showing up to every treatment, so its not like the belief is just sitting there doing nothing.

(5) Sam: fine, hope helps, I'm not denying that, but there's a difference between a hopeful attitude and fully convincing yourself the odds are basically in your favor when they're not, and it's the second one that's making him skip the will and the goodbyes. and if he's wrong, that's not only his problem, everyone who loves him is now unprepared too.

Can you go through this point by point and say who's got the better argument at each numbered step, not just who "wins" overall? And is there anything important both of them are missing that neither one said?
