I've never studied philosophy, but a running disagreement with my brother has been on my mind for months, and I could use a perspective from outside the family. He is an unusually disciplined person -- up before dawn every day, never misses a workout, and built a small business on the side while working full-time -- and he can be pretty dismissive of people who, in his words, "just don't have the willpower" to do the same, including a mutual friend of ours who has spent years failing to get his finances in order. When I pushed back on this a while ago, I pointed out that my brother's discipline itself looks less like something he built than something he lucked into: our parents drilled routine and delayed gratification into both of us as kids, it took with him and never really took with me, and by his own admission, self-control has simply always come easier to him than to most people. That annoyed him. His response was that if none of that counts, then nobody could ever really take credit for anything, which he thinks is obviously false, since people plainly do earn their achievements sometimes.

I've since brought it up with a couple of friends, and none of us can get past a certain point in the reasoning. I don't actually want to tell my brother that his success is fake, but when I try to picture the version of him who built his discipline entirely on his own -- through choices that weren't already shaped by a personality and upbringing he never picked -- I end up going in a circle. Any deliberate decision to become more disciplined would have to be made by someone who already cared enough about discipline to bother, and that caring isn't itself something he chose; it was simply there, or it wasn't. And if his willpower had instead just appeared in him one day for no particular reason, that would be a stroke of luck rather than anything to be proud of. Either way, I can't locate the point where credit is supposed to attach to him specifically, rather than to the circumstances that produced him. Is there a real flaw in that reasoning that I'm missing, or is my brother the one missing something when he insists that people obviously do deserve credit for who they turn out to be?
