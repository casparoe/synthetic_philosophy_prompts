Hey, so I'm working on a longer feature about how physicists and philosophers actually think about "chance," and I went down a total rabbit hole into David Lewis's Principal Principle — the rule that your credence in some event should just match the objective chance of it, if that's all the relevant info you've got. Seems fine on its face, but then I hit this thing called the "undermining problem." If chance is a Humean, best-system kind of thing (basically just a summary of the actual pattern of events across the *whole* history of the world, past and future), then knowing the chance at time t doesn't fully pin down your credence, because there's always some tiny chance the future unfolds in a wild, frequency-defying way that would retroactively undercut the very fact that the chance at t was ever really x to begin with. Lewis tried to patch this in "Humean Supervenience Debugged" with what people now call the New Principle: instead of conditionalizing on "the chance of A is x," you conditionalize on the entire theory of chance for the world at once, Tw.

A philosopher friend of mine, who I was picking her brain for the piece, thinks this patch basically just relabels the problem instead of solving it, since Tw is exactly as "undermine-able" by the actual future frequency pattern as the original single-time chance claim was — so you havent gained anything, you've just moved the same worry up a level. Honestly I didn't totally follow why that's supposed to be fatal rather than just a cost you swallow and move on from. So: can you build the strongest version of her case for me? I want an argument that the undermining problem isn't some technical wrinkle a cleverer conditionalization can patch over, but instead points to a genuine, principled incompatibility between Humean/best-system chance and the Principal Principle — such that we should actually prefer a primitivist or propensity view of objective chance, since that kind of view doesn't generate the undermining worry in the first place. Please don't play both sides evenhandedly here, I want the sharpest one-sided version of that specific argument, written for someone who already gets the basic setup of Humean chance and the Principal Principle.
