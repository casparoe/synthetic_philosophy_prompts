So I'm a philosophy PhD student who got stuck presenting on Sidgwick for our Victorian ethics reading group next week, and instead of actually prepping I've spent the whole week down a rabbit hole on his psychical research side-career, which honestly might be more interesting than the ethics. Here's the thing that's stuck with me: the back half of the Methods of Ethics is basically Sidgwick admitting defeat -- he can't rationally reconcile egoism and utilitarian duty (the famous "dualism of practical reason") unless the universe turns out to have some kind of moral government backing it up, e.g. a God who makes sure virtue and self-interest coincide in the end. And then, seemingly not satisfied with leaving that as an open philosophical wound, he goes and co-founds the Society for Psychical Research, becomes it's first president, and spends the rest of his life personally debunking mediums, table-rappers and telepathy claims -- his own wife Eleanor was even more ruthless about it and later ran the SPR's statistical "Census of Hallucinations." Basically the most rigorous professional skeptic of his era, motivated the whole time by really, really needing one of these people to turn out real. (I might be fuzzy on the details but I'm pretty sure there was even some agreement he made with his friends Myers and Gurney to try to signal each other from beyond the grave, just in case.)

Anyway, our reading group has this dumb tradition where whoever's presenting has to open with a joke about that week's philosopher, and I've got nothing. Could you write me a short joke about Sidgwick and psychical research? It doesn't have to be gut-busting, but I want the humor to actually come from that tension -- a lifelong debunker whose own ethical theory needs the ghosts to be real -- rather than a generic "wacky Victorian philosopher believed in ghosts" joke. Ideally it should land for someone who's actually read the concluding chapter of the Methods and knows why he needed the result to go a certain way.
