I'm a retired experimental physicist (spent about thirty-five years doing condensed-matter work, of all things, so relativity was never my actual day job, just something I loved since a grad-school course) and for the last year or so I've finally been reading philosophy properly instead of just picking up bits from pop-sci books. Lately I've fallen down the persistence-through-time rabbit hole -- endurantism vs perdurantism -- and I wrote up the little argument below thats been rattling around my head. I'd love your comments before I embarass myself showing it to the philosophy reading group I just joined.

Here's the gist. Endurantists say an object is "wholly present" at every moment it exists -- it has spatial parts but no temporal parts, so the whole cat is right there at 3pm and the whole cat is right there at 3:01pm too, numerically the same thing both times. Perdurantists say the cat is really a four-dimensional "worm" stretched through spacetime, and what's "at 3pm" is just one temporal part or stage of it, the way my left hand is a spatial part of me.

What strikes me is that endurantism quietly assumes there's a fact of the matter about which events count as "the same moment," so that the whole object can be present there together. But special relativity says simultaneity isn't absolute -- two observers moving relative to each other will disagree about which distant events are simultaneous with "now." So which slice is the one the cat is wholly present on? If you relativize "wholly present" to a reference frame, two observers moving relative to each other end up disagreeing about how much of the cat currently exists, which seems like a much stranger thing to disagree about than ordinary perspectival stuff like apparent size. Perdurantism doesn't seem to have this problem: the four-dimensional worm is just there, invariantly, and each observer's "now" just carves out a different cross-section of one and the same object, the way a diagonal slice through a loaf of bread is still a fact about one loaf. So it looks to me like relativity quietly hands the perdurantist a pretty significant advantage for free.

Two things nag at me though. First, I have a strong feeling this exact move has already been made by someone -- a name like Balashov rings a bell, but I read it in a footnote somewhere and can't swear to it -- so I don't know if I've found a nice example or just reinvented a wheel that already has three well-known dents in it from replies I haven't seen. Second, I suspect I might actually be smuggling in an argument for eternalism (the block-universe view that past and future are just as real as the present) rather than for perdurantism specifically, and I gather those are supposed to be independent debates -- you can apparently be an eternalist endurantist. If that's right, my relativity argument might be doing less work than I think it is.

So: is the core argument actually valid, or have I snuck in a premise that just begs the question against the endurantist? What's the standard endurantist comeback to the relativity-of-simultaneity worry -- surely there is one? And am I right that I've conflated the persistence debate with the presentism/eternalism debate, and if so, can the relativity consideration be rescued as an argument for perdurantism specifically, or does it only really buy you eternalism? Don't go easy on me, I'd genuinely rather find out this is a well-worn dead end now than in front of the reading group.
