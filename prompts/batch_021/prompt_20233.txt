For my dissertation I am hand-coding motivational-interviewing transcripts with a co-coder, sorting client utterances into "change talk" and "sustain talk." We keep encountering utterances that resist confident classification either way. My co-coder treats every such case as a shared mistake waiting to be corrected: she assumes each utterance really is one or the other, and that a sharper codebook plus more calibration sessions will eventually let us see which. I have started to suspect that past some point there is nothing further for either of us to be tracking—that certain utterances simply do not determinately belong to one category or the other, however carefully we specify our criteria. As a compromise we added an explicit "ambiguous" category, only to spend our next meeting arguing over which utterances counted as clearly ambiguous versus clearly one way or the other, which felt like the same dispute recurring one level up. Is my co-coder just overlooking a fixable methods problem, or is there a genuine disagreement underneath this that no amount of calibration can resolve, and if so, what should that change about how we report our reliability statistics?
