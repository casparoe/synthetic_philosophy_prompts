My grandmother died last week -- she'd spent almost forty years on the foundations of quantum mechanics and was a hardcore Everettian, not the pop-sci "multiverse" version but someone who actually cared about where the Born-rule probabilities come from and what branching does to personal identity. She told my mom more than once that she didn't want anyone at her funeral saying she's "still alive somewhere in another branch" -- she thought that kind of quantum-immortality talk was exactly the sloppy reasoning about identity she'd spent her career arguing against. I'm a physics PhD student myself (foundations, like her), and I got asked to give the eulogy, which is terrifying since a few of her old colleagues will definately be there to notice if I fudge the physics, while most of the room is just family who need actual comfort, not a lecture on branching wavefunctions. Can you write the eulogy so it's honest to what she really believed about many-worlds and death, but still comforting rather than clinical?
