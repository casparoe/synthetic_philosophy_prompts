Ok so lately I've gotten kind of obsessed with this theory of consciousness called Integrated Information Theory (Tononi came up with it, Christof Koch is the other big name attached to it). The part that stuck with me is "phi" - a system is conscious to the exact degree that it integrates information, and weirdly the theory says only ONE specific grouping of a system's parts actually "exists" as the conscious subject: whichever grouping maximizes phi. Any bigger or smaller set of parts that overlaps with it doesn't separately count, even though its physically right there too. That's apparently why a photodiode, or even a big enough grid of interconnected logic gates wired the right way, could technically have a little flicker of experience according to actual IIT people, which a lot of critics think is nuts.

Totally seperately I was reading about Leibniz and ran into his "mill" argument - imagine blowing a thinking machine up to the size of a windmill and walking around inside it: you'd just see gears pushing on gears, never anything that looks like a perception happening, so real perception can't come from parts arranged together, only from truly simple "monads" that have no parts at all. My first thought was that this just wrecks IIT, since IIT's conscious thing is still, at the end of the day, a bunch of parts, however integrated. But then I remembered Leibniz also thought pretty much everything, even a rock, is built out of monads that have their own tiny dim "perceptions," which sounds suspiciously close to the panpsychist vibe people accuse IIT of having. So now I genuinely can't tell if Leibniz would look at IIT and see exactly the mechanist mistake he was warning against, or if he'd see it as dimly groping toward something he already believed 300 years earlier, just with the "experiencer" wrongly located in the physical circuit instead of the immaterial monad underneath it. Could you actually reason through this using Leibniz's own principles and give me a real verdict on where he'd land - which specific parts of IIT he'd accept, which he'd reject, and why - rather than just concluding he'd have "mixed feelings" about it?
