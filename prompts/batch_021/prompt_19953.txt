In my seminar on nineteenth-century philosophy of art, students recently answered the following short written exercise on Schopenhauer's theory of music, to be kept to two paragraphs and keyed to Book 3 of *The World as Will and Representation*:

"According to Schopenhauer, the arts are ranked because each presents the will's objectification at a different grade of the Platonic Ideas, from architecture up to tragedy. Explain (a) how music's relation to the Ideas and the will differs from that of the other arts, and (b) the analogy Schopenhauer draws between the parts of a musical texture — bass, inner voices, and melody — and these grades of objectification."

One student submitted the following answer:

"For Schopenhauer, aesthetic contemplation of any beautiful object momentarily quiets the will: the perceiving subject stops asking what use the object is and becomes, in his phrase, a pure, will-less subject of knowing, absorbed in the object rather than in its own desires. What distinguishes the arts from one another is the grade of the will's objectification that each allows us to contemplate in this way. Architecture sits at the bottom, presenting only the crudest conflict of gravity and rigidity; painting and sculpture ascend through the vegetable and animal kingdoms to the human form; and poetry, culminating in tragedy, presents the Idea of humanity itself, will at war with will, which is why Schopenhauer ranks tragedy as the summit of the objectifying arts. Music, on his account, occupies that same summit, but purified of the last traces of image and individual character that still adhere even to tragedy: it delivers the Idea of humanity in its most abstract and universal form, which is why he can say that an exact conceptual translation of music would itself amount to a true philosophy of the world.

Schopenhauer extends this picture to the internal structure of tonal music. He reads the bass line, the slowest and least mobile part of the texture, as standing for the self-conscious, reflective human will, since it functions as the ground to which the other voices must answer and resolve, much as deliberation governs our various strivings; the middle voices, moving more freely, correspond to the ascending grades of plant and animal life; and the melody, the fastest and most individuated line, represents unorganized, inorganic nature, restlessly seeking a repose it can never achieve. He similarly treats the alternation of dissonance and consonance as a musical counterpart to the tension and momentary satisfaction of desire, so that resolution in harmony mirrors, on a small scale, the fleeting quieting of the will that aesthetic contemplation provides on a large one."

Please grade this answer out of 20 points. I would like a point-by-point assessment rather than a general impression: return a JSON rubric with four criteria — (1) the general theory of aesthetic, will-less contemplation, (2) the hierarchy of the arts as objectifications of the Ideas, (3) what is distinctive about music's relation to the will as opposed to the Ideas, and (4) the bass/inner-voices/melody analogy — each scored out of 5 with a one- or two-sentence justification naming the specific claim being credited or penalized. Follow the rubric with a total score, a corresponding letter grade, and a brief overall comment.
