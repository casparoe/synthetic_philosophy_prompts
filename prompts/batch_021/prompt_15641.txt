I'm a PhD student in political theory currently drafting a dissertation chapter on the normative underpinnings of Marx's critique of capitalism, and I've run into a disagreement with my advisor that I can't quite resolve on my own. The dispute concerns how the concept of exploitation that Marx develops in Capital — via the distinction between labor and labor-power, and the extraction of unpaid surplus labor-time that this distinction makes possible — relates to the earlier concept of alienation (Entfremdung) from the 1844 Manuscripts, where the worker is described as estranged from the product of her labor, from the activity of laboring itself, from her fellow workers, and from her own "species-being" (Gattungswesen). My advisor takes something close to the Althusserian line that Capital marks an "epistemological break" with the earlier, Feuerbach-inflected humanism: on this view, the mature theory of exploitation is a structural, quasi-scientific account of extraction that owes nothing to the earlier picture of an alienated human essence. I'm inclined to think the two concepts can't be separated so cleanly — that the critique of exploitation only manages to tell us something is wrong with capitalism, rather than merely describing how it functions, because it is quietly leaning on that same background picture of unalienated, self-directed labor as a human good.

Could you help me think this through by comparing the two concepts directly? I'd like you to address, specifically: (1) whether "exploitation" in the mature, surplus-value sense can do genuine normative work — give us a reason to condemn capitalism, rather than just a mechanism to explain it — without covertly relying on the humanist premises behind alienation; (2) conversely, whether the theory of alienation could retain its critical force without the more determinate apparatus of surplus-value that the concept of exploitation provides; and (3) whether one of the two concepts should be regarded as conceptually or normatively prior to the other, or whether they are best read as two independent critiques that merely happen to share a target. Please don't just survey the positions — reach and defend an actual verdict on which concept, if either, is doing the more fundamental work in Marx's mature thought.
