In my philosophy club, we've been working through the Vienna Circle, and I came across Otto Neurath's Isotype project: the international "picture language" he devised to present statistics through standardized pictograms instead of words. Around the same time, I was reading Wittgenstein's Tractatus for a class assignment and noticed that his picture theory of meaning also describes propositions as "pictures" that share a logical form with the facts they depict. Since Neurath was famously impatient with the more mystical side of Wittgenstein's philosophy, I'm curious whether their two notions of a "picture" are actually related, or just a coincidence of vocabulary. Could you compare Neurath's picture language with Wittgenstein's picture theory, focusing on what each takes a picture to be and how it connects to what it represents? Then tell me whether Isotype should count as a practical realization of Wittgenstein's theory, or whether it instead rests on an incompatible view of representation, and justify your answer.
