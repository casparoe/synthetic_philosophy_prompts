I'm a PhD student in political theory, and this weekend I'm giving the eulogy for Helen Aldrich, the professor who supervised my dissertation. She spent her career on Rawls's account of public reason: on how citizens holding different comprehensive doctrines, religious and secular, could converge in an overlapping consensus on shared political principles without giving up what they privately believed. Helen was also a lifelong, devout churchgoer who held herself to an unusually strict version of her own theory: she would tell you plainly what she believed and how it sustained her, but she refused to argue from her faith as though it gave her a reason the rest of us were bound to accept, because she thought that was exactly the move her own scholarship said public reasoning has to avoid. The service is at her church, and the room will be split between her congregation, who knew her only as a devoted member of their community, and a contingent of political theorists -- several of them career critics of her restrictive reading of Rawls -- who will be listening for whether I get the distinction right. Please write the eulogy, roughly five minutes long: her faith needs to come through as the real, sustaining thing it was for her, in something like her own voice, without ever tipping into asserting her beliefs as reasons the rest of us are bound to share -- and it still has to feel, to the people in the pews who loved her simply as a woman of faith, like an honest eulogy rather than a philosophy lecture.
