A friend and I spent much of an evening arguing about G.E.M. Anscombe's 1956 protest against Oxford's decision to award Harry Truman an honorary degree despite his role in ordering the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki. By the end of the argument, I had come around to my friend's view: that Anscombe's condemnation of Truman is at bottom an empirical wager about body counts, since she is implicitly betting that a naval blockade or continued conventional bombing campaign would have forced Japan's surrender at a lower cost in civilian lives. Given that a fair number of historians now argue that a blockade-and-invasion strategy would likely have killed just as many, if not more, Japanese civilians, does this mean the historical evidence has largely vindicated Truman over Anscombe, by her own lights?
