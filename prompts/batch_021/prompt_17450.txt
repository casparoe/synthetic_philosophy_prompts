I am a PhD student in moral psychology, and this semester I have been helping my advisor screen undergraduates who are applying for research assistant positions in our lab. Across several rounds of interviews, I have noticed that I consistently rate candidates who answer quickly and maintain steady eye contact as more "engaged" and "sharp," even when a transcript of their answers reads no differently from that of a candidate who pauses more or looks away while thinking. A labmate who studies autism pointed out that this pattern would systematically disadvantage autistic applicants and anyone who simply needs more time to formulate an answer, and now I cannot decide whether my reaction is tracking something real about engagement or is just an unexamined bias. Is there a principled way to tell whether a reaction like mine reflects a legitimate preference rather than a bias, and should the answer change how I use these ratings going forward?
