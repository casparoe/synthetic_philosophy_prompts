"Frankfurt's target is PAP: the claim that an agent is morally responsible for an action only if she could have done otherwise. He attacks it with the case of Black and Jones. Black wants Jones to do A and has a device that will force Jones to do A if Jones shows any sign of choosing otherwise, but Black never has to use it because Jones goes ahead and does A on his own, for his own reasons. So the actual sequence contains no trace of Black at all, yet Jones could not have done otherwise, since any attempt to do otherwise would have triggered the device. Frankfurt concludes that Jones is morally responsible for A despite lacking alternative possibilities, so PAP is false.

This matters for the free will debate because the classical argument for incompatibilism moves from 'determinism rules out alternative possibilities' to 'determinism rules out moral responsibility' using PAP as the bridge premise. If PAP is false, that argument no longer goes through, and Fischer builds on this to defend semicompatibilism, i.e. compatibilism about both free will and moral responsibility, since responsibility no longer needs the sort of control over alternatives that determinism supposedly threatens.

The most serious reply is the flicker-of-freedom strategy, which Fischer himself defends: even in Frankfurt cases some thin alternative survives, since Jones could at least have exhibited the prior sign that would've triggered Black's device. So there is technically still an alternative possibility, just a much less robust one than PAP's defenders originally had in mind, and this lets them hang onto the principle in a weakened form."
