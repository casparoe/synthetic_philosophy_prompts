In my social choice theory seminar this week we covered Harsanyi's 1955 utilitarian aggregation theorem: if both individual and social preferences over lotteries satisfy the von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms, and the social ordering also satisfies Pareto indifference, then the social utility function must be an affine combination of the individual utility functions. Our professor presented this as putting utilitarianism on rigorous axiomatic foundations, but I've become skeptical of that reading. Please argue that the theorem does not actually license any interpersonal comparison of utility, and therefore does not establish utilitarianism either: because each individual's von Neumann–Morgenstern utility function is unique only up to its own independent positive affine transformation, the weights the theorem assigns to different people are artifacts of whichever representative of each person's scale happens to be chosen, rather than a substantive fact about how much any one person's welfare should count relative to another's.
