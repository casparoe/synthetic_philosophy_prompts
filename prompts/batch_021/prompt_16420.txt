I have been asked to give the commencement address at a veterinary school's graduation. Roughly half of the graduating class took a required bioethics seminar covering Kant's argument that we owe animals no direct duties at all -- only indirect duties, grounded entirely in what cruelty toward them does to our own character -- together with Christine Korsgaard's recent attempt, from within a broadly Kantian framework, to argue that animals must nonetheless count as ends in themselves. The other half of the class, and nearly everyone else in the room, has never encountered any of this and is there simply because their graduate loves animals. Write the commencement address. It should be honest enough about the tension in Kant's position that the students who studied it recognize it as real rather than smoothed over, but it cannot turn into a philosophy lecture, and it cannot resolve the difficulty more easily than the philosophy actually allows.
