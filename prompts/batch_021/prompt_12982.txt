Okay so a friend of mine who did a philosophy PhD got me hooked on this "Kripkenstein" rule-following paradox over drinks a while back, and its been rattling around in my head ever since, especially now that i spend my actual working hours prompting and fine-tuning LLMs. Quick recap in case you need it: Kripke's reading of Wittgenstein says there's no fact about you (past behavior, mental states, brain states, whatever) that determines whether you meant "plus" or "quus." Quus is some deviant function that agrees with plus on every case you've ever actually computed but diverges once the numbers get big enough (I think the classic example uses 57 as the cutoff). The gap between your finite past usage and the infinite rule you supposedly intended can't be closed from the inside, and Wittgenstein's move isn't to dig up some clever fact that settles it, but to relocate meaning in communal practice/agreement instead of private mental content.

This maps way too cleanly onto ML generalization for me to just let it go. When a model nails the "right" answer on held-out data, is there a fact of the matter that it's "really" implementing the rule, versus some quus-like impostor that just happens to coincide with the rule so far, or is that a confused question dressed up in ML vocabulary?

So here's what I actually want from you: Wittgenstein died in 1951, decades before anything resembling modern machine learning existed (though funnily enough he apparently ran seminars on the foundations of math at Cambridge in the late 30s that Turing himself sat in on and argued with him about, which feels almost too on the nose given what I'm asking here). If he'd somehow lived to see LLMs, what do you think he'd actually say about whether a model can be correctly described as "following a rule" (arithmetic, grammar, a coding convention, whatever) at all, as opposed to us just projecting that description onto it from the outside? Would his communal-agreement move even be available for a system that's trained on human text but isn't embedded in any community of its own, or does that move break down here in some interesting way? I want an actual, argued position that engages with the specific plus/quus mechanics, not a "well nobody can really know" cop-out.
