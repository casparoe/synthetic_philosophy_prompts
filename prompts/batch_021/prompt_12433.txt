Been reading Hume's essay on miracles lately — his argument that testimony alone can basically never justify believing a miracle happened, because the testimony would have to be less likely to be false than the miracle is to be true. He backs this up with a few reasons why real miracle reports always fall short in practice: never enough witnesses with solid enough reputations that they'd loose something by being caught lying, people just loving a good "wow" story and spreading it uncritically, these reports mostly coming out of uneducated/isolated communities back then, and how every religion's miracles basically cancel out every other religion's.

Hume obviously never had to deal with deepfakes or AI generated "eyewitness" video/audio that can fake all of that at scale for basically free though. If he came back today and saw this, do you reckon his argument still holds up as is, does he need to bolt on a new reason to the list, or does cheap synthetic "evidence" kind of break the whole thing? Walk me through his actual reasoning here, not just the "extraordinary claims" one liner everyone reduces him to.
