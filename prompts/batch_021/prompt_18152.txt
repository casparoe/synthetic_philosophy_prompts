I work as a backend engineer at a mid-size startup that ships an AI coding assistant, and I somehow ended up on our internal "Model Change Review Board" -- the group that has to sign off before we let the assistant's more autonomous features (auto-merging refactors, writing its own migration scripts, stuff like that) go out to customers. Our senior reviewers are drowning: the diffs the model proposes are getting long and subtle enough that nobody can fully vet them line-by-line before the weekly release window, especially anything touching concurrency or auth. Our eng policy just says a human has to "meaningfully review" anything before it ships, it doesn't say what that means once the model is faster and arguably sharper than the humans doing the reviewing. One of our engineers who's really into AI safety blogs pitched a fix based on that old "AI safety via debate" idea: spin up two instances of the same model, have them argue for and against "this diff is safe to merge" in front of a time-boxed judge (a rushed human, or even a cheaper/faster model) who never reads the full diff themselves, just the argument. Half the board loves it because it actually scales with our reviewer headcount, the other half thinks we'd just be outsourcing the decision to whichever instance is more persuasive rather then whichever one is right, and points out that a subtle bug could be buried inside an otherwise airtight-sounding argument in a way that's way harder to catch under a time limit than it was to write in the first place.

Nobody on the board has a philosophy background, me included, we're all just engineers, but we do need to vote Thursday. Can you draft the memo I'll bring to the meeting? I need an actual recommendation -- adopt the debate setup, reject it, or adopt it with specific conditions/limits -- plus the reasoning behind it, written so the skeptical half feels genuinely engaged with instead of steamrolled by "the theory says truth wins." If there's a reasonable middle-ground pilot or scope limit we could propose instead of an all-or-nothing vote, throw that in too.
