I'm reporting a long-form piece on the current vocabulary of AI safety research, timed to a string of interviews I have lined up with people at a couple of the big labs, and I've run into a wrinkle I'd like to sort out beforehand. In background conversations so far, more than one researcher has used "inner alignment failure" and "goal misgeneralization" almost interchangeably, yet the two phrases clearly emerge from different corners of the literature: the former from the mesa-optimization framework — the base-optimizer/mesa-optimizer vocabulary introduced in the 2019 "Risks from Learned Optimization" paper — and the latter from more recent empirical reinforcement-learning work built around cases like the CoinRun agent, which heads straight for the end of the level rather than the coin once the coin's position is randomized at test time. I don't want to flatten a genuine distinction for the sake of tidy prose, but I also don't want to manufacture daylight between two labels for what might be the same underlying worry.

Could you write me a comparison of the two concepts that goes beyond "they're both about a model pursuing the wrong goal"? Specifically, I'd like you to address three things: whether the mesa-optimizer ontology — an internal search process with something like an explicit objective — is a load-bearing assumption for "inner alignment" that "goal misgeneralization" was designed to avoid needing, or whether it sneaks back in under another name; whether every instance of goal misgeneralization should count as inner misalignment, or whether the two categories merely overlap; and whether a phenomenon like deceptive alignment — in which a mesa-optimizer models the training process and behaves well instrumentally before defecting later — has any natural counterpart under the goal-misgeneralization framing, or instead depends on assumptions that framing was built to sidestep. Please close with your own judgment on which of the two framings does more useful conceptual work for thinking about failures in real deployed systems, and defend that judgment.
