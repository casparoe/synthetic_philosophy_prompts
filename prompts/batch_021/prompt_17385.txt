I sit on the governance committee of our condo association, and we're rewriting the bylaw on who is entitled to vote at meetings. Currently it's one vote per unit owner. Some long-term tenants -- one has rented here fourteen years, longer than half our owners have even lived here -- want a vote too, since they live under our rules every day, more than some owners do. Meanwhile, several owners live elsewhere and rent out their units, so under the current rule they help decide matters that barely touch them. The building next door shares our driveway under an old easement, and a resurfacing vote next month will cut off their access for weeks, yet they have no say in our association at all. Someone suggested we simply vote on who should get to vote, but that only relocates the problem, since it doesn't tell us who that first vote belongs to. Every rule I've tried -- owners only, residents only, whoever is affected -- handles some case badly. Please draft the memo I'll bring to the committee before that vote: a recommended rule for who gets a vote, applied to the tenants, the absentee owners, and the neighbors, with honest treatment of the strongest reason to reject it.
