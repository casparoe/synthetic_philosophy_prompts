I'm writing my IB extended essay on David Lewis and I'm trying to connect two parts of his philosophy. His modal realism holds that every way a world could possibly be is realized by some concrete world that exists just as robustly as our own. He also wrote a short paper called "Do We Believe in Penal Substitution?", which asks whether it makes sense for Christ to be punished in humanity's place. Since every possibility is realized somewhere on Lewis's view, I've been assuming his real argument is that there exists some concrete world where our guilt is literally transferred onto Christ, and that this is what makes penal substitution coherent rather than a conceptual muddle. Given that none of us actually live in that world, how should I explain in my essay why Lewis thought this still gives believers here good reason to accept the doctrine?
