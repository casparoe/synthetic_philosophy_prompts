I spent the better part of thirty-five years running a vision science lab -- mostly psychophysics and some fMRI work on attention and contrast gain -- before I retired four years back, and lately I've fallen into the habit of reading philosophy of mind most evenings (my wife calls it my second adolescence, which is fair). Last month our little retirement reading group worked through some papers on what philosophers call "cognitive penetration of perception," starting with Pylyshyn's old arguments for the cognitive impenetrability of early vision, and I came away more puzzled than enlightened. In my old field, what they're describing -- expectations, beliefs, and stored categories reaching back down to shape what gets represented in visual cortex -- is just called top-down processing, and its about as controversial among vision scientists these days as saying blood carries oxygen; predictive coding models build practically the whole visual system out of top-down predictions meeting bottom-up error signals. So when Fodor, and later Siegel and that whole crowd, write about cognitive penetration like its some hugely fraught, maybe-not-even-real phenomenon that threatens the objectivity of perception, I keep thinking: haven't they just rediscovered something we settled in vision science decades ago and dressed it up in fancier language?

So here's what I'd like from you: actually compare these two ideas properly -- the philosophers' "cognitive penetration of perception" and the vision scientist's "top-down processing" (predictive coding included). Is the philosophical thesis just a redescription of the empirical one in fancier dress, or is there some further, more specific claim built into "cognitive penetration" that a garden-variety top-down effect wouldn't satisfy on its own? And if there really is some extra ingredient in there -- something that would make a case count as genuine penetration for the philosophers but wouldn't impress me as anything beyond routine top-down modulation -- I want to understand why that extra ingredient is doing so much work. What exactly is supposed to be at stake epistemically if perception turns out to be penetrable in that stronger sense, over and above what's already at stake given how thoroughly expectation-laden we already know perception to be?
