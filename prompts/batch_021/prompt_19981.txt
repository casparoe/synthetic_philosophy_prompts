Been going down a Thomas Hobbes rabbit hole lately and read that he barely even looked at geometry until he was like 40, when he stumbled on Euclid's Elements, saw one of the proofs, said something like "that's impossible," then traced it backwards proof by proof until he was convinced — and after that he basically decided political philosophy should work the exact same way. So from what I can tell, that's why Leviathan is laid out like a geometry proof: you start from a couple of self evident axioms about human nature and then deduce step by step, same as proving the Pythagorean theorem, that people have to hand over their power to one absolute sovereign. Doesn't that mean his whole case for absolute sovereignty is basically as objective and certain as a math theorem, and not just his personal opinion about politics? Feels like people should treat Leviathan with a lot more certainty then they do if thats really how it works.
