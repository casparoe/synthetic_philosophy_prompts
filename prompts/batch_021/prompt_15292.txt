I'm currently in the middle of the college application process, and something about it has been bothering me in a way I haven't been able to resolve on my own. To get a sense of my chances at the schools on my list, I've been using two different admission-chance calculators. The one built into my school's counseling platform estimates my odds by comparing me only to past applicants from my own high school who had similar grades and test scores and who applied to the same colleges; for my top-choice school, it gives me a figure of around 60 percent. A second, more general calculator that I found independently draws on a much larger nationwide pool of applicants with my same GPA and SAT range, regardless of which high school they attended, and it puts my odds at closer to 20 percent for that same school. When I raised this discrepancy with my guidance counselor, she told me that both figures were "technically accurate" given how each was calculated, which left me more confused than reassured.

What has stayed with me since then is a point a friend in my AP Statistics class made when I described the situation to her. She pointed out that if narrowing the comparison group changes the result this drastically, there is no obvious reason to stop narrowing where these two calculators happen to stop. We could keep specifying the group further and further still, say, to students from my school who play the same instrument as me and are also applying early decision, and the percentage would presumably keep shifting, right up until the group consisted of just me, at which point it is not clear that a "percentage" means anything at all. But if narrowing the group all the way down is absurd, it is not obvious why stopping at "everyone from my high school" or "everyone nationally with my scores" should count as the principled place to stop, rather than just a convenient one. I would like to know whether there is an actual, non-arbitrary way of deciding which group of people is the correct one to use for a calculation like this — and if there is not, whether that means a statement like "your chance of getting in is X percent" simply fails to mean anything for a specific individual applicant like me.
