I co-host a small philosophy podcast that grew out of a philosophy-of-mind reading group several of us originally found through LessWrong and Astral Codex Ten, and next episode's guest is a philosopher who has built her career defending Searle's Chinese Room argument in something close to its original 1980 form: syntax alone is not sufficient for semantics, and no purely formal symbol-manipulating system — however it is implemented — thereby has genuine understanding. She is sharp, has heard every stock reply in the literature, and tends to dispatch questions quickly with the canned rebuttal before a host can press further. I want to design a sequence of eight to ten questions that do not just let her restate the thesis, but that walk through the standard replies in enough depth that the follow-ups actually land.

Specifically, I would like at least one question on the Systems Reply and Searle's "memorize the rulebook" rejoinder, with a follow-up pressing on whether that rejoinder just relocates the intuition pump rather than answering it; one on the Robot Reply and the symbol-grounding problem; one on the Brain Simulator Reply and what exactly "the right causal powers" is supposed to mean if it is not covert property dualism dressed up as biology; one on the Churchlands' "Luminous Room" parody of the argument's inductive structure; and one on the connectionist "Chinese Gym" variant, where the single room's serial processing is replaced by a crowd of people implementing a parallel network. I would also like a question that updates the thought experiment for transformer-based large language models specifically — pressing her on whether a system trained to do nothing more exotic than predict the next token at massive scale is better modeled as the man in the room, the room-plus-rulebook system, or something the 1980 thought experiment does not cleanly cover — and a closing question asking what kind of empirical or conceptual discovery, if any, would move her off biological naturalism. For each question, please include a short follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give, the way a good interviewer would.
