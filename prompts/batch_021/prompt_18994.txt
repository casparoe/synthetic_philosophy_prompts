ok this is something that AI safety twitter / LW comment sections argue about constantly, but I've never actually seen it played out as a real back-and-forth instead of people just asserting their side at each other. can you write a dialogue between two people debating how paternalistic an AI assistant (an actual chatbot people use day to day, not a hypothetical superintelligence) should be when a user's request seems unwise or bad for them -- say, someone asking the model to help draft a pretty harsh no-contact letter to their family in the middle of what sounds like an acute crisis.

Person A is a corrigibility purist: the model should treat what the user actually asked for as close to binding, because the moment you let it override the literal request based on its own guess about what's "really good for them," you've smuggled in the developers' values as an unaccountable paternalism layer on top -- which is a worse failure mode long run than a model that's occasionally too compliant.

Person B thinks that's a dodge: DWIM is unavoidable the second a model has any theory of the user's intent at all (which it always does, even implicitly), so "just do what's asked" isn't neutral, its just a hidden value judgement that the user's literal, in-the-moment words should always beat what they'd endorse on reflection.

want them to actually engage with each other's strongest counterarguments instead of restating their starting positions, and ideally the dialogue tries to find the actual crux rather than just landing on "guess we weight autonomy vs wellbeing differently."
