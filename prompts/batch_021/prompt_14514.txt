A married couple I know are trying to decide whether to accept a lump-sum buyout of a structured settlement one of them is owed: a series of fixed, inflation-adjusted payments spread over the next fifteen years. A settlement-purchasing company has offered to pay out the remaining balance today, at a discount, and the two of them disagreed about whether to accept it, roughly as follows.

(1) She wants to take the lump sum: a guaranteed dollar today is simply worth more than a guaranteed dollar fifteen years from now, she said, and no one seriously disputes that money now beats money later.

(2) He said this runs two different things together. Discounting because the money could be invested and grow, because of inflation, or because the settlement company might not exist in fifteen years—these are all legitimate reasons to prefer the lump sum, but none of them have anything to do with time as such. Strip away growth, inflation, and counterparty risk, and imagine two payments of identical real, risk-free value, one now and one later: there is no obvious reason left to prefer the earlier one just because it is earlier.

(3) She replied that even granting all that, she still cares more about her near future than her distant future, and there is nothing irrational about a preference simply being shaped that way—people are allowed to care more about next year than about year fifteen, the same way they are allowed to prefer one flavor of ice cream to another.

(4) He said the analogy does not hold. Flavor preferences are preferences between different goods; what she is defending is treating one instance of a good as worth less than an equal-sized instance of the very same good, for no further reason than its date. A brute preference for 'sooner' over 'later,' with the amount of well-being held fixed, is not a taste—it is closer to discriminating against her own future self, for whom that future dollar will feel just as real as a dollar today feels to her right now.

Go through the exchange move by move and say who has the better of the argument at each step, not just who wins overall. Then say what, if anything, both of them are missing about the difference between pure time preference and the other, legitimate reasons people have for discounting the future.
