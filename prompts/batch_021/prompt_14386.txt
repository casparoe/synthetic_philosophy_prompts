My dad died three weeks ago and I somehow got stuck writing the eulogy, which is funny because I'm the novelist in the family, not the philosopher. He was a population economist who spent forty years sincerely defending a homemade version of Parfit's repugnant conclusion: a big family where nobody gets quite enough of him beats a small one where everyone's doted on, because the total comes out higher even if each of us is a little worse off. He always made a point of saying this got him to the repugnant conclusion and not the sadistic one -- more slightly-less-happy kids beats fewer very happy ones, sure, but he'd never say an actually bad life beats one more happy kid. Seven kids, three marriages, to prove it. Two of my half-siblings have always felt like evidence in an argument rather than actual children, and are half-expecting the eulogy to paper over that. A couple of his old economist friends who fought with him about this exact stuff for decades will be sitting in the second row and will definately clock it if I dodge it. He also told my mom once that he'd haunt us if we sanded it down into "he just loved having a big family." Can you write the eulogy -- honest about the real shape of his reasoning, something that lands for cousins who've never heard of Parfit, and doesn't read as either an endorsement or a cheap shot.
