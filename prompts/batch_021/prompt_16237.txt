I've spent a fair amount of time reading rationalist and effective-altruism-adjacent writing on decision theory, and I keep running into references to Lara Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility theory (REU), developed in her book Risk and Rationality, as a rival to orthodox expected utility theory. What I'd like is a careful comparison between REU and the standard way orthodox EU theory already accommodates risk aversion, namely by making the utility function concave over outcomes. Specifically: what can REU's separate risk function capture about an agent's attitude toward gambles that no concave utility function ever could, and why does capturing it force Buchak to relax the independence axiom (Savage's sure-thing principle) rather than simply adjusting the shape of the utility function? Finally, is REU best understood as a genuinely distinct normative theory of choice under risk, or as a more permissive redescription of ordinary risk-averse expected-utility maximization?
