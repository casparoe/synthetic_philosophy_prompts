So I was over at my friend Leo's place with him and his girlfriend Dana when their new puppy started whining nonstop after Dana stepped out of the room for like two minutes. Leo said "aww he genuinely missed you, dogs definitely feel that stuff." Dana, who'd been on a bit of a Descartes kick lately, said not so fast — Descartes thought animals are basically organic machines, and the whining no more proves theres an inner feeling than a smoke detector "feels" something when it beeps at smoke.

Leo said that's a cheap trick, because by that logic she can't know HE feels anything either — she's never seen inside his head, only his behavior, so if behavior doesn't prove feeling, the same doubt applies to him.

Dana said that's not parallel, because Descartes' actual reason for treating people differently wasn't behavior in general, it was language specifically — any adult can hold up their end of a brand new conversation on a brand new topic, and Descartes thought no animal (or machine) could do that even a little, so that's the real evidence, not just "acts a certain way."

Leo shot back that dogs obviously communicate pretty flexibly too — different whines/barks for different things, reacting sensibly to word combos they'd never heard put together before — so Descartes' line looks like it just got drawn wherever 1600s knowledge of animals happened to run out, not where the real distinction is.

Dana admitted the line probably has to move, but said wanting-signals aren't the same kind of thing as open ended reasoning about literally anything, so all Leo showed is the boundary's blurrier, not that its gone.

I've been chewing on this since it happened and honestly can't tell who actually had the better of it at each step. Can you go through their exchange point by point and say who's right where, and whether there's something about Descartes' actual view that both of them are missing?
