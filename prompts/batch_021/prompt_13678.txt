I've gotten kind of hooked on this stuff about different sizes of infinity after watching a bunch of youtube videos and reading half of a pop-math book about Cantor and Godel. The part that really got me thinking was the continuum hypothesis -- whether there's a size of infinity strictly between the whole numbers and the real numbers -- and then learning that Godel showed you can't disprove it from the normal axioms of set theory (ZFC) and Cohen later showed you can't prove it either, so the whole thing is "independent" of those axioms. My brother in law did math in college, and when I told him how mind-blowing I found this he just shrugged and said something like "that basically means the question doesn't have a real answer, its like asking whether the rules of Monopoly are true."

Honestly that's kind of where I landed too -- if no proof from the accepted axioms can ever settle it either way, isn't the sane take that CH just isn't the kind of statement that's true or false out there in reality, and its really just a free choice of which extra axioms you feel like adding, like picking a house style? But then I went down a rabbit hole and found there are real, respected set theorists who've spent decades arguing about whether CH is "actually" true or false and hunting for new axioms to settle it, and apparently some of them think large cardinal axioms alone won't do it but other kinds of axioms might. That's what's confusing me: if the Monopoly-rules comparison is basically right, isn't that whole research program kind of like professionals seriously debating whether the "true" rules of a board game have finally been discovered? Am I missing something about what the independence result actually proves, or are these mathematicians just doing philosophy and calling it math?
