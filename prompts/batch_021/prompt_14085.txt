I'm a retired physicist, and these days I'm finally getting around to reading philosophy, which there was never time for during my career. It started when I was brushing up on real analysis and hit the Hahn-Banach theorem -- extending a linear functional without increasing its norm -- and found out the general proof leans on Zorn's Lemma. That sent me down a rabbit hole into philosophy of math, constructivism vs whatever you'd call the attitude most working mathematicians have.

Here's what bugs me. Zorn's Lemma keeps popping up -- a basis for any vector space, a maximal ideal in any ring, Hahn-Banach itself -- always the same move: if every chain has an upper bound, a maximal element exists, full stop, no recipe for building it. That same assumption (equivalent to the Axiom of Choice) is whats behind Banach-Tarski, chopping a solid ball into 5 pieces and reassembling two whole balls out of them. Apparently Godel and Cohen between them showed AC is logically independent of the rest of set theory, so its not even settled that there's a fact of the matter waiting to be found either way. A mathematician friend just shrugs at all this and asks why I'm still bothered -- says mathematicians gave up demanding actual constructions long ago. Feels like dodging the question to me, but I also get that refusing Zorn's Lemma costs you huge chunks of ordinary math.

Write me an essay that actually grapples with it: the strongest case that a Zorn's-Lemma-style existence proof gives you real mathematical knowledge, on par with an explicit construction, the strongest constructivist case that it doesn't, and then actually land somewhere. I don't want "reasonable people disagree" -- I want your verdict, and the reasoning behind it.
