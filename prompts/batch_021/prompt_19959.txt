I'm about two-thirds through a novel where the narrator, a lonely widower, spends most evenings talking to both his ancient sheepdog and to an AI chatbot on his phone, and a lot of the emotional weight rests on whether he's a fool to think either one actually "gets" him. To make that thread earn its keep rather than just vibes, I've been reading up on Descartes' actual case for why animals don't have minds — not the cartoon version where "animals are just clocks," but the real argument from Part V of the Discourse and the letters to the Marquess of Newcastle and to Henry More. As I understand it he's not just asserting this, he gives basically two tests: a beast (or a built machine) might be made to yelp when you step on it, or a parrot might squawk out actual words, but neither could ever rearrange language on the fly to answer whatever you happened to say to it, the way even the dullest human can — and even if it excelled at one task, it would flub others, because its stuck running on organs each fitted to one particular job, rather than one flexible faculty of reason that can turn itself to anything.

Here's what's bugging me: those are basically behavioural tests, and a modern LLM seems to pass the language-flexibility one way better than any dog or parrot ever managed, and arguably does fine on the "turn itself to anything" test too. So could you reconstruct, as carefully as you can, how Descartes himself would actually respond if you sat him down in front of a chatbot — does his own framework back him into granting it something like reason, or does he have a principled way out that isn't just moving the goalposts after the fact? And does working through that expose something that was always a bit shakey in how he got from "I can't detect reason in it" to "therefore no soul, no mind, nothing it's like to be it" — something that maybe should make my widower a little less sure about the dog, too? My writing group thinks this whole thread is a distraction, but I think it's the actual spine of the book, so I'd love a few paragraphs I could genuinely lift from for how the character reasons, not just a refresher on the beast-machine doctrine.
