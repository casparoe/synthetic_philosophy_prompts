In my seminar on formal epistemology, we have recently covered anthropic reasoning and the observation selection effects underlying the Doomsday Argument. For a paper I am writing, I would like a dialogue between two equally sophisticated philosophers: one who accepts the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) and therefore takes the Doomsday Argument's shift toward an earlier end for humanity to be a genuine update, and one who accepts the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) instead, on which that shift disappears entirely. The SSA-defender should press the Presumptuous Philosopher case against SIA, and the SIA-defender should press the reference class problem against SSA, with each philosopher responding to the other's specific objection rather than simply restating their own position. The dialogue need not end in agreement, but it should make clear whether either assumption actually escapes the other's central charge, or whether the two positions are simply trading one cost for another.
