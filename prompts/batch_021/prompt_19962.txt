A close friend of mine has a long-standing habit of making blunt, cutting remarks that end up hurting the people around him, and whenever this is raised with him, he falls back on the same defense: that he has been this way since childhood, and that his bluntness is simply a fixed feature of his personality rather than something he chooses, so that holding it against him makes no more sense than holding someone's height or eye color against them. The most recent instance was an offhand comment about my appearance, made in front of several mutual friends at dinner; when I raised it with him privately afterward, he gave me the same response he always gives: that this is just how he is wired, and that it is not fair to blame him for it.

I mentioned the exchange to two other friends, and their reactions were split. One thought that getting upset with someone over a trait he never chose and supposedly cannot change is a bit like getting upset at the weather, and that I should simply adjust my expectations of him. The other thought the whole defense was a dodge, pointing out that this same friend manages to be tactful, even charming, with his boss and with people whose opinion of him he cares about, and seems to let the bluntness out mainly around people, like me, who he knows will not walk away regardless. I find myself somewhere between these two reactions: I do not want to be the kind of person who resents someone for a trait that is genuinely beyond their control, especially if there is real truth to the idea that personality is substantially fixed by temperament and upbringing rather than freely chosen; but I also cannot shake the sense that something is off about letting that explanation do all the work here. I would like help thinking through whether his defense actually holds up, and if it does not, what precisely is wrong with it, since "I can't help it" seems like the kind of thing that should carry real weight, at least in general.
