I'm several years into a long novel with a large cast and three interleaved timelines, and a problem I keep running into has started to feel like more than an ordinary craft issue. Whenever I change a single fact -- say, moving up the year a character discovers her husband's affair, or deciding that two characters never actually met before Chapter 12 -- I feel compelled to comb back through everything I've written to see what else the change might have broken, because I have no reliable way of knowing in advance which of the hundreds of other established details are downstream of it and which are simply untouched. Re-checking an entire book-length manuscript after every such change isn't realistic, yet I've watched more experienced novelists make comparable changes late in a draft with no apparent anxiety, seemingly confident that most of what they've written is unaffected without actually verifying it. I can't tell whether they're tracking something real that I'm missing, or simply haven't been caught out yet. Is there a principled way to know what a given change does and doesn't touch, short of re-verifying everything, or is this purely a matter of earned intuition -- and if so, what is that intuition actually tracking?
