Ok this is a slightly random one but its been rattling around in my head all week. I cover biotech and longevity stuff, and a couple years ago I somehow ended up on the volunteer scientific board of a smallish longevity-research foundation -- the kind of outfit that hands out early-career grants, nothing huge. Next Tuesday we're voting on something that sounds like a branding question but is actually a real philosophical landmine: whether to officially adopt, in our mission statement and grant language, the position that "aging is a disease," instead of the current fudge about "age-related decline." The push is coming from members who say the label has real teeth: the FDA doesn't currently treat aging itself as an approvable indication, so any drug trial has to be hung on a named disease instead, and funders and pharma partners apparently take you more seriously if you're framed as fighting a disease rather than fighting the human condition.

The hangup, for me and a couple of other philosophy-adjacent board members, is that this isn't just PR -- it rests on a real disagreement about what "disease" even means. If you go with something like Boorse's biostatistical theory, disease is just statistically subnormal functioning relative to a reference class, and because his reference classes are age-indexed, ordinary age-related decline counts as "normal for your age" and therefore isn't disease by definition, full stop. Some biogerontologists dodge this by insisting the reference class should really be peak young-adult function instead, but that looks a lot like reverse-engineering whatever reference class gets you the answer you wanted going in. And if instead you just say screw the statistics, aging obviously causes enormous harm so obviously it's a disease, you're conceding that disease-talk is normative all the way down -- which cuts both ways, because it also legitimizes the objection a couple of our older, more clinically-minded members keep raising: that formally labeling aging a disease medicalizes and stigmatizes old people rather than helping them.

Could you draft the memo I'm supposed to send round before the vote? I need it to actually recommend something -- adopt the "aging is a disease" language, keep the current phrasing, or some qualified middle option -- not just lay out considerations on both sides, and it needs to take the stigma objection seriously as a real cost rather than a talking point to dismiss in one line.
