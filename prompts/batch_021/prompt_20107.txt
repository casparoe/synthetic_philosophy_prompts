Last month I reported a colleague to our department chair for fabricating data in a co-authored paper, only to have him retort that I had no room to talk, since I altered several figures in my own dissertation years ago and never disclosed it. Wanting to think the matter through properly before it goes any further, I have been reading the philosophical literature on hypocrisy and standing to blame, including G.A. Cohen's essay on tu quoque and the ethics of condemnation. It does seem to be widely accepted among these philosophers that a hypocrite forfeits the standing to call out others for a fault they share. If that is correct, doesn't it follow that my colleague was right to dismiss my report as groundless, and that the "tu quoque is a fallacy" rule I learned in my critical-thinking course is simply mistaken?
