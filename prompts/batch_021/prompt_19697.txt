I've spent enough time reading rationalist blogs and forums that I keep running into the same intuition pump whenever machine consciousness comes up: Ned Block's "Chinese Nation" (sometimes called "China Brain") thought experiment against functionalism. Block's setup asks us to imagine the population of China equipped with two-way radios and organized so that each person plays the causal role of a single neuron; wired together in the right pattern and hooked up to an artificial body, the resulting system would satisfy any plausible functionalist description of a working mind. Functionalism seems committed to saying that such a system has genuine mental states, including whatever it is that a felt experience of pain amounts to, yet the conclusion still strikes many people as absurd, which is exactly why Block offered it as a problem for functionalism to answer rather than a proof of anything.

I would like to see this scenario dramatized rather than argued over abstractly. Could you write a short story of roughly 1000-1500 words, told either from inside the system (from the perspective of one "neuron" among many) or from outside it, following someone trying to work out whether the system as a whole is conscious? I would rather the piece earn its philosophical content through plot, detail, and voice than have characters simply recite the debate, and I would like the underlying question of multiple realizability - whether the right functional organization is sufficient for a mind, regardless of the substrate implementing it - to remain genuinely open at the end rather than resolved by authorial fiat.
