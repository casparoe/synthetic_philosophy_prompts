I've read enough rationalist blogs and forums over the years to notice that Bayes' theorem gets treated there as close to a complete theory of evidence, yet the sharpest technical objection to that stance—the problem of old evidence—almost never comes up. The problem, pressed originally by Clark Glymour against Bayesian confirmation theory, is that if a proposition E already has credence 1 for an agent—because she knew it before hypothesis H was even proposed—then conditionalizing on E cannot budge her credence in H at all, since P(H|E) = P(H) whenever P(E) = 1. The standard illustration is the anomalous precession of Mercury's perihelion, known for over half a century before Einstein showed that general relativity entailed it: everyone agrees this old datum strongly confirmed general relativity, even though no new prediction was ever at stake.

Write an essay that takes a real position on whether this problem is fatal to Bayesian confirmation theory. Engage seriously with at least two families of solution: (1) the Garber-Jeffrey move of denying agents logical omniscience, so that discovering the entailment "H implies E" counts as new evidence in its own right, and (2) proposals that relocate confirmation to counterfactual credences—what the agent's confidence in H would be had she learned E for the first time, holding fixed everything else she believes. For each, engage with the standard worry about it: that relaxing logical omniscience leaves the size of the resulting confidence boost essentially unconstrained by anything recognizably Bayesian, and that "subtracting" E from an agent's web of belief to define the relevant counterfactual is either arbitrary or smuggles in exactly the kind of unprincipled stipulation Bayesianism was supposed to avoid. Don't settle for concluding that reasonable Bayesians disagree—tell me which of these costs you think is worth paying, or whether the problem's persistence across more than four decades shows that no single, static credence function can play the role Bayesians assign it.
