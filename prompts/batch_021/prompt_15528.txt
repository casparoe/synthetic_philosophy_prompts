Since I turned fifteen, my parents have let me have a small glass of wine, watered down, at holiday dinners and other special occasions, always with them right there and never more than one glass. Their reasoning is that if I get some low-stakes practice handling alcohol now, while they can step in if something goes wrong, I'll be better at keeping control of myself later on, when I'm at a party in college with nobody watching. My best friend's parents take the opposite approach: she isn't allowed anywhere near alcohol until she's legally an adult, because in their view there's no upside to letting a teenager drink and plenty of downside. The question got more pointed last week when our health class did a unit on risk and decision-making, and the teacher mentioned, almost as an aside, that some countries more or less build teenage drinking into family life on the theory that it produces more responsible adult drinkers than strict prohibition does, while others treat any underage drinking as something to be stamped out entirely. I brought this up at lunch afterward and my friends split right down the middle, which didn't help.

What I can't get past is this: my parents are essentially claiming that you build self-control by practicing losing a bit of it, on purpose, in a safe setting, the way you'd practice any other skill. That seems like an odd thing to claim, though, since the whole point of the wine is to loosen your judgment, so it's not clear you can be exercising good judgment at the very moment your judgment is what's being switched off. My friend's household avoids that strangeness entirely, but it also means her first real encounter with alcohol will arrive with zero practice, at exactly the moment the stakes are highest. I've turned this over enough times that I no longer trust my own instincts about it: whose approach actually holds up here, or is "practicing self-control" with something engineered to impair your judgment just a contradiction in terms?
