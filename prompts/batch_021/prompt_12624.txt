What he wrote: "The fine-tuning argument says the fundamental constants of nature (gravity, the strong force, the cosmological constant, etc) fall into an extremely narrow range that allows for stars, chemistry and life, and if any of them had been even a little different nothing could ever have lived anywhere in the universe. Defenders of the argument, going back to Paley's watchmaker analogy, say this precision is too improbable to be an accident and is better explained by a designer who set the constants on purpose, same way finding a watch on a beach points to a watchmaker rather than random chance.

The main response is the weak anthropic principle: since we could only ever observe a universe thats compatible with us existing in the first place, its not surprising we find ourselves in a life-permitting universe, because if it wasn't life permitting we wouldn't be around to notice it. This fully answers the fine-tuning argument, since it shows the appearance of design is just a side effect of only observers being able to observe observer-friendly conditions, so theres nothing left over that a designer or a multiverse actually needs to explain.

Some people also bring in the multiverse on top of this: if there's a huge number of universes with different constants, its likely at least one turned out life-permitting, and that's obviously the one we'd find ourselves in."
