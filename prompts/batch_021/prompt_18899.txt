I'm a software engineer on the technical safety board for my company's warehouse-robot fleet. Our collision-avoidance protocol is specified as a "knowledge-based program" -- a robot reroutes only once it "knows" a collision is imminent -- and the safety proof our formal-methods team produced models every robot as logically omniscient: each is credited with knowing every logical consequence of whatever it senses. Last month, one robot's own sensor log already contained both its planned trajectory and another robot's last broadcast trajectory, and basic kinematics entails these would intersect within seconds -- so, on paper, the protocol already "knew" this, but the robot's time-bounded reasoner never actually chained the two facts together, and we got a near-miss. The board is now split between patching the reasoner and keeping our existing proof as the safety case, versus re-deriving the whole case around a model of bounded, "algorithmic" knowledge that only credits a robot with what it can actually infer in time -- which would delay our next rollout by months. Please draft the board's decision memo for next week's meeting: a concrete recommendation with a timeline, and a serious engagement with the strongest argument for whichever option you don't recommend.
