Hey, I'm putting together notes for our department's work-in-progress seminar on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument and want a reality check before I stand up and say this out loud. Quick recap for context: you do what you do because of the way you are (your character, values, etc); to be *truly* responsible for what you do you'd have to be truly responsible for the way you are; but you can only be responsible for the way you are if you chose it, and choosing it requires already having some prior set of values to choose with - wich just pushes the question back a step. Chase that regress far enough and the only non-arbitrary stopping point is being causa sui, cause of yourself, wich is incoherent (kinda the same move as that Nietzsche bit in Beyond Good and Evil about hauling yourself out of the swamp by your own hair - section 21 if memory serves). Strawson is explicit that none of this turns on determinism vs indeterminism - chucking randomness into the chain doesn't buy you responsibility, it just buys you luck.

Here's the bit I actually want your take on. This regress looks structurally identical to the regress of justification in epistemology - "responsible for X requires responsible for whatever produced X" is the same shape as "justified in believing p requires justified in believing whatever supports p." So shouldn't the three classic replies to the epistemic regress have direct analogues here, and shouldn't Strawson have to rule those out rather than just assume the chain must bottom out in self-causation or nowhere?

- foundationalist reply: some starting motives are just brute, "properly basic" grounds of responsibility (temperament, upbringing) that don't themselves need to be chosen
- coherentist reply: responsibility isn't a linear chain back to a first cause at all, it's a holistic feature of a sufficiently integrated, self-endorsing character (Frankfurt-ish)
- infinitist reply: the regress is real but not vicious, we just never need a first link

If the mapping holds, Strawson's whole argument rests on an unargued-for assumption that responsibility has to work like the foundationalist picture of justification, when the other two options are sitting right there.

1. is this analogy doing real work or am i just forcing two unrelated debates to rhyme

3. does Strawson's own move of making the argument independent of determinism cause trouble for my analogy, or is it actually neutral with respect to it
