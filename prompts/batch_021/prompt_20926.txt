My dad passed away three weeks ago and I have to give the eulogy this Saturday. A couple years back he read that biography of Frank Ramsey - Sheer Excess of Powers, I think it was called, about the mathematician/philosopher guy who died at 26 back in 1930 - and got weirdly fixated on one bit: how Ramsey refused to feel small next to the universe, his whole argument being that since he's the one doing the looking, the stars are actually the small ones and ordinary human stuff is what's big, not the other way round. Dad was sick a long time and I literally watched him get annoyed when a hospice chaplain gave him the "we're all just tiny specks in a vast cosmos, it puts things in perspective" speech - afterward he said that's exactly the kind of comfort Ramsey thought was backwards, and he didn't want it anywhere near his funeral. Problem is most of the room Saturday is gonna be his old workmates and my nan, nobody whos heard of Ramsey, and "you're insignificant so don't worry about it" is basically 80% of what people say to make death feel smaller. Can you write the eulogy so it actually comforts people but does it dad's way, not the usual way?
