Okay this is a dumb thing but it's been stuck in my head since we moved apartments last month. Our renters insurance wants an itemized inventory of "all items" in the place, and my partner and I spent like 45 minutes arguing over how to count stuff - like, does the desk plus the hutch that sits on top of it (they unscrew apart, but we've never once actually separated them) count as one item or two, or does a stack of ~20 records leaning against a crate count as one thing or twenty? He kept insisting theres a fact of the matter about what actually counts as one object, and I kept saying the whole question felt kind of confused - like it just depends on which sense of "object"/"exists" you're using, and neither of us has to be wrong.

Went down a rabbit hole afterward and found out this is basically the "special composition question," and that there's a real live debate (Hirsch vs. Sider, roughly) about whether disputes like ours are substantive metaphysics or merely verbal. Can you write me a dialogue between a mereological universalist and someone on the restrictive end - a nihilist, or an organicist like van Inwagen, whichever makes the better foil - who start out going back and forth on a case like the desk-and-hutch one and then end up arguing about whether their whole disagreement is substantive or just verbal? Want them to really engage with each other's moves - quantifier variance, the joint-carving response, all that - instead of just talking past each other, and neither one should get to be the pushover.
