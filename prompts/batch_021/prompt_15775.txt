I serve on my university's data governance committee, which reviews proposed uses of student data. The counseling center has a FERPA-compliant proposal (confirmed by university counsel) to run session transcripts and usage metadata it already collects for clinical purposes through a predictive algorithm that flags students at elevated risk of self-harm; only the resulting flag, not any clinical content, would reach the Office of the Dean of Students, which would then arrange a wellness check. The center's position is that since no one outside its own systems ever learns what a student actually said, no confidentiality is broken. Two student members on the committee insist this is still a serious privacy harm but have struggled to explain why in terms that survive that argument, and the committee is now split ahead of next week's vote. Please draft the short memo I will present to the full committee: recommend approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions, engage directly with the center's argument rather than simply asserting that privacy matters, and make any conditions specific enough to be audited.
