Over dinner last night, a friend and I ended up arguing about whether evolutionary biology really counts as science in the way physics does. She pointed out that Karl Popper, in his intellectual autobiography, once described the principle of natural selection as close to a tautology — since defining 'the fittest' as those that survive makes 'the fittest survive' true by definition — and that he accordingly classified it as a metaphysical research programme rather than a strictly testable theory. Because Popper is the philosopher most responsible for the falsifiability standard, she took this as him effectively conceding, decades ahead of time, the very charge that intelligent-design advocates still level against evolution: that it fails to meet its own discipline's evidentiary bar. I didn't have a good response in the moment. Was she right to treat Popper's remark as settling the matter, and is falsifiability even the right standard by which to judge evolutionary biology's scientific credentials?
