I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on algorithmic fairness, and I sit on my county's Pretrial Risk Assessment Oversight Board as its resident ethicist. The board must decide whether to renew its contract for the risk-assessment tool judges consult before release hearings. An outside audit found that, among defendants who were never rearrested, Black defendants were flagged as high risk far more often than white defendants; the vendor's rebuttal is that the tool is well-calibrated, since within every risk tier the actual rearrest rate is essentially identical across race. The board has split into two camps: one wants to compel the vendor to equalize the false-positive rates before renewal, the other wants to leave the scoring untouched, since a calibrated score "means the same thing" no matter who receives it. Both treat this as a fixable engineering problem; given how different the base rates of rearrest are between the two populations in our jurisdiction, I don't think a fix of that kind exists. Draft the short memo I'll bring to next month's meeting: reach one concrete recommendation, with an implementation timeline, and engage honestly with the strongest argument for whichever option you set aside.
