Random side gig: because I covered ICU triage a lot during the pandemic, my county health department roped me onto the panel finalizing our crisis-standards-of-care protocol, as the token "community member." We're stuck on one clause. Say a mass-casualty event leaves us with enough of a scarce antitoxin to save exactly one of two patient groups, whose medical claims are otherwise identical (same age, same prognosis, everything) -- except one group has 1 patient and the other has 5. Half the panel wants the rule to just be "save the bigger group, obviously, next clause." Our bioethicist won't stop citing a paper called "Should the Numbers Count?" by a philosopher named Taurek, who apparently thought you can't just add five people's stakes into something bigger than one persons stake, since nobody actually lives through the "combined" loss. We need real language for this before the vote on friday. Can you draft the short memo I'd bring to the panel -- pick a rule, justify it, and don't let the losing side's best argument off easy, whatever you land on?
