So I've read approximately a thousand blog posts referencing Aumann's agreement theorem at this point, and every joke about it I've seen is some version of "two rationalists walk into a bar and can't agree to disagree" -- which is fine I guess but doesn't really engage with why the thing actually works. The mechanism is way weirder than that: two people can converge to the exact same posterior just by repeatedly blurting a number back and forth at each other ("70%!" "no, 65%!" "ok, 68%!"...) without ever once sharing their actual evidence, purely because each announcement is itself evidence about what the other person must have seen. I think its originally from Geanakoplos and Polemarchakis if you want to get fancy about it. Can you write me a joke that actually turns on *that* mechanic -- the bare-numbers-no-reasons-given convergence thing -- rather than just the top-line "rational people can't disagree" slogan? Needs to actually land for someone who already knows how the theorem works, not just be technically accurate.
