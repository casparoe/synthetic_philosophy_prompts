A dispute among my housemates over some missing money has left me unexpectedly invested in a distinction in logic and language I'd never previously thought mattered outside a seminar room, and I would appreciate help working out who actually had the better of the argument. Sixty dollars went missing from our shared grocery jar; only the four of us hold keys to the cupboard where it's kept, and Jordan insists he did not touch it. As close to verbatim as I can recall, here is how the exchange between my other two housemates, Alex and Priya, went:

1. Alex: "If Jordan didn't take it, one of us did. There's no fifth option here: the money's gone, we're the only ones with keys, so ruling him out just leaves the rest of us."
2. Priya: "That's an accusation against me too, then, since I'm one of 'the rest of us.' You're saying that if Jordan hadn't taken it, one of us would have."
3. Alex: "That's not the same claim at all. I never said either of you would have taken it in some other scenario — I'm only pointing out what has to be true given what's actually happened. There's an easy possible world where Jordan doesn't take it and nobody does, because the money is never touched in the first place."
4. Priya: "Fine, maybe they're not strictly identical claims. But your version isn't as innocent as you're making it sound, either — it only goes through if you're already sure nobody from outside got in, and that nobody simply miscounted the jar. You're every bit as willing to point a finger at one of us as you're accusing me of being; you've just dressed it up as a deduction instead of an accusation."

I lack the background to say who is actually right, or at which step things go wrong, and would rather not wade in myself. Could you go through the four moves individually, assess each on its merits rather than simply picking an overall winner, and flag anything important that both of them seem to be missing?
