I've spent the last several years reading rationalist blogs and forums -- LessWrong, Overcoming Bias, Astral Codex Ten, and the like -- and one consequence is that the closest friend I made during my PhD and I have spent almost as long arguing, mostly for entertainment, about a particular thought experiment. Two players, unable to communicate, each secretly choose either to "split" or "steal" a shared pot of money: if you both split, you each walk away with a modest gain; if one of you steals while the other splits, the thief takes the whole pot and the other gets nothing; and if you both steal, neither of you gets a cent (we long ago agreed that any forfeited pot would go to charity, so at least it wouldn't be wasted). She has always maintained that stealing is simply the correct answer once you understand the payoffs, full stop, and that any discomfort with that conclusion is just sentiment dressed up as reasoning. I have never managed to articulate why it still feels wrong to me anyway, only that it does.

She is moving abroad for a new job next week, and before she leaves we finally decided to stop arguing about this in the abstract and actually run it for real money -- almost certainly the last chance either of us will have to settle it. We scraped together a $300 pot between us; tonight we are each going to write "split" or "steal" on a slip of paper and hand it, sealed, to a mutual friend, who will read both slips aloud at the same moment. There is to be no last-minute negotiating, since we agreed years ago that haggling in the moment would defeat the entire point of finally testing this honestly.

Here is what is actually needling me. She and I have spent a decade absorbing the same writers, running our reasoning through the same handful of intellectual habits, and generally arguing ourselves into strikingly similar positions on nearly everything else we have ever debated -- to the point that I can often predict her answer to a new question before she gives it. That fact seems like it should bear on what I write tonight, but I cannot work out whether it actually gives me a real, rational reason to split rather than steal, or whether I am just dressing up loyalty as logic and calling it an argument. Is there a genuine, rational case for splitting here, something that does not collapse into sentiment once you press on it, given everything I have just told you about how alike our reasoning really is?
