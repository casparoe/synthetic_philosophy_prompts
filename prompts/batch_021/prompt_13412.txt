In my graduate seminar on philosophy of cognitive science this term, we spent the first several weeks on Jerry Fodor's case against connectionism, especially the 1988 paper he co-wrote with Zenon Pylyshyn, which argues that any network lacking a combinatorial syntax cannot explain the systematicity and productivity of thought, and that a connectionist model adequate to the data would just be a bad implementation of a classical language-of-thought architecture underneath. For the second half of the course, our professor has switched us over to recent mechanistic interpretability papers -- the induction-heads account of in-context learning, the reverse-engineered finding that a small transformer solves modular addition using a genuinely Fourier-based algorithm, and the "indirect object identification" circuit found in GPT-2 small -- all of which their authors present as evidence that transformers implement structured, quasi-algorithmic internal representations rather than being inscrutable statistical blobs. For our term paper, she has asked us to reconstruct how Fodor himself would likely have reacted to this literature, and I would like to reason through the question with you before committing to a thesis.

Concretely: given his systematicity and compositionality arguments, together with his causal, asymmetric-dependence theory of mental content, would Fodor have read findings like these as belated vindication -- evidence that the networks which actually succeed are precisely the ones that smuggle in something like a language of thought -- or would he have dismissed them as local, cherry-picked circuits that fall well short of the fully productive, systematic representational structure he considered non-negotiable for genuine cognition? I would also like to know whether you think his asymmetric-dependence account of content could even be applied to something like a "feature direction" in activation space, given the superposition problem, whereby the same direction gets reused for many unrelated concepts depending on context -- or whether he would have taken this alone to show that such directions cannot be Fodorian representations at all. Please do not simply restate the 1988 argument back at me; I am looking for your best reconstruction of the view, argued through with reference to the actual mechanics of these interpretability results.
