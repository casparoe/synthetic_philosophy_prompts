I'm currently taking a philosophy of mind seminar in which we've been closely examining David Rosenthal's higher-order-thought (HOT) theory of consciousness, on which a mental state counts as a conscious state just in case it is accompanied by a suitable, roughly simultaneous higher-order thought representing the subject as being in that very state. One issue I haven't been able to resolve is that, since higher-order thoughts are representational states like any other, nothing in the theory seems to rule out a "targetless" HOT: one that represents the subject as being in pain even though no first-order pain state actually exists to serve as its target. Could you write a dialogue between a defender of Rosenthal-style HOT theory and a critic who presses this misrepresentation worry, in which they work through whether such a targetless HOT would make it phenomenally seem to the subject that she is in pain, and what that answer implies about whether the theory ends up locating qualitative character in the higher-order state rather than in the first-order state it's supposed to be about? Each speaker should engage directly with the other's strongest move rather than simply restating their own position, and the disagreement should remain unresolved by the end.
