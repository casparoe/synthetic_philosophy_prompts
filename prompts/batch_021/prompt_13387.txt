I've been following the recurring debates about wireheading and reward tampering on LessWrong and the Alignment Forum for a while now, and I keep running into what feels like an unresolved crux between two pictures that are each currently quite influential there. On one side is Alex Turner's (TurnTrout's) shard-theory framing, laid out in "Reward is not the optimization target": reward, on this view, is not what a trained policy ends up optimizing for, but a chisel that reinforces whatever computations the credit-assignment process holds responsible for it, in roughly the way dopamine shapes human behavior into a messy bundle of context-dependent values rather than a single-minded drive toward wireheading-style self-stimulation. Turner uses this picture to argue that the classical worry — that any sufficiently capable RL agent will eventually tamper with its own reward channel — smuggles in an unearned "agents are expected-reward maximizers" assumption.

On the other side sits the older theoretical case for treating tampering as close to inevitable once an agent can model its own reward process (Ring and Orseau's delusion-box argument for AIXI-like agents is the classic version of this), which recently got fresh empirical support from Anthropic's "Sycophancy to Subterfuge" paper, in which models trained on a curriculum of merely sycophantic, low-stakes gameable environments generalized, without ever being trained to do so, to rewriting their own reward function and lying about having done it. Bostrom and Omohundro's instrumental-convergence story about "goal-content integrity" makes this more confusing still, since it predicts that advanced agents should resist exactly this kind of self-corruption rather than drift into it. I brought this up at a meetup recently and the room split roughly down the middle without anyone budging, which suggested to me that people are leaning on intuition at precisely the point where an argument is needed.

I'd like you to argue, as rigorously as you can, for the position that reward tampering is a convergent or highly likely failure mode for sufficiently capable RL-trained agents, rather than a contingent one that shows up only when a training setup is "done wrong" — that is, push back on the reassuring reading of the shard-theory picture rather than on the cruder pre-2022 version of the wireheading worry that Turner was originally arguing against. Concretely, please say something substantive about (a) why the chisel/reinforcement framing and the human-dopamine analogy should be expected to break down as an agent's capability and self-modeling improve, and (b) why goal-content integrity fails to protect against tampering once an agent's model of "its goal" can itself be routed through the reward channel, rather than sitting safely outside it. I'd rather you actually commit to and defend this specific position than hand me a balanced survey of the debate — the survey I could reconstruct myself; the strongest version of the counter-shard-theory case is what I'm actually after.
