Quick one for a novel I'm plotting: I've got a scene right after Frege dies where two old colleagues are at the wake and word's just gotten out about his private diary -- all the antisemitic, anti-democracy stuff he'd apparently kept to himself his whole life. I want them to have a real argument about whether any of that touches his logic, no strawmanning either side. Push one of them past the usual "separate the art from the artist" line into something more specific: that his whole war on psychologism, plus the idea that thoughts live in an objective "third realm" (his actual term -- drittes Reich, which, yeah) untouched by anybody's opinion, isn't just compatible with contempt for democratic messiness, its the same impulse showing up twice. Have the other one channel something like Dummett's actual reaction -- appalled by the man, totally unmoved on the logic -- and give the strongest version of that: the arguments in the Grundgesetze stand or fall on their own, regardless of who wrote them. I want real back and forth, each landing actual hits on the other's specific point, not just trading position statements.
