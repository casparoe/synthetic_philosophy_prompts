I spent my career in numerical relativity, solving Einstein's field equations on a computer to simulate black-hole mergers, and grew used to treating coordinate freedom as a technical nuisance to be tamed by a gauge choice. Only in retirement did I discover the philosophical literature spawned by Einstein's own 'hole' construction, and one move in it leaves me uneasy. Earman and Norton (1987) argued that manifold substantivalism saddles general relativity with an indeterminism that neither observation nor the field equations can ever resolve. The rescue now favored by most philosophers of physics is to keep the manifold as a substance while denying that its points have any primitive, trans-world identity, so that models related by a hole diffeomorphism no longer represent distinct possibilities. To an outsider this looks like relationalism in disguise: once a point's identity is exhausted by the metrical relations it stands in, I don't see what work 'exists independently of the fields' is still doing. Please argue that this 'sophisticated', anti-haecceitist substantivalism does not really rescue substantivalism, but collapses into the very relationalism it was meant to avoid.
