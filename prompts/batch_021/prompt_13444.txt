I'm the engineering representative on my company's model risk committee, which oversees the machine-learning models behind our lending product. For most applicants our default-risk model is stable, but for "thin-file" applicants (little credit history) the predicted probability is fragile: refitting on different, equally defensible bootstrap samples swings the estimate anywhere from 4% to 22%. One committee member wants us to report these cases as a range rather than a single number, arguing that forcing a point estimate here manufactures false precision, and to route any applicant whose range straddles our approval cutoff to manual underwriting instead. Another member argues this doesn't resolve anything: a loan still gets approved or denied, so a sharp line is drawn somewhere regardless, and two otherwise-identical applicants could now get different outcomes depending on which model snapshot was retrained most recently -- and the committee could reverse the same decision a week later with no new evidence at all. Please draft the short memo I'll bring to next week's meeting: recommend a concrete policy, including exactly how a reported range should translate into an approve/deny/refer decision, and take the second member's inconsistency objection seriously rather than brushing past it.
