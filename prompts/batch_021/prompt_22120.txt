The question was:

"Explain what the gavagai example is supposed to show. Cover: (a) why 'rabbit,' 'rabbit stage,' and 'undetached rabbit part' are all equally good translations of 'gavagai' given the evidence available to a radical translator; (b) how the indeterminacy of translation thesis is supposed to be a *stronger* claim than ordinary underdetermination of theory by evidence, not just a special case of it; (c) whether indeterminacy of translation and inscrutability of reference are the same thesis or different ones."

Here's the student's answer:

"Quine's gavagai example imagines a linguist doing 'radical translation' — translating a totally unknown language with no bilingual speakers, no dictionaries, nothing to go on but the natives' behavior. Every time a rabbit hops by, the native says 'gavagai,' so the linguist's first guess is 'rabbit.' But Quine points out that 'rabbit stage' (a time-slice of a rabbit) and 'undetached rabbit part' fit the evidence exactly as well, since a whole rabbit, a rabbit-stage, and an undetached rabbit part are always present in exactly the same circumstances. No amount of watching the natives assent and dissent could ever break the tie, because all we have access to is behavior, and the three hypotheses predict identical behavior. So which translation is correct is indeterminate.

This is a stronger point than the everyday fact that evidence underdetermines theory — the fact that two rival physics theories could fit all possible observations and we'd have no way of knowing which one is true. Indeterminacy of translation is really that same problem showing up again, one level up: a translation manual is itself a theory we build to fit the evidence of behavior, and since that evidence will never be enough to settle between rival manuals, translation ends up in exactly the position physical theory is in — we simply can't ever gather enough evidence to know which manual is the one that's actually right.

Inscrutability of reference is this same idea under another name — its what people call the thesis when they want to stress that its reference specifically (what a term stands for) rather than whole sentences that's up for grabs, but its the same underlying phenomenon at a smaller grain size."
