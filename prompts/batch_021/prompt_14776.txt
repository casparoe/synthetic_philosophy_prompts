A friend and I spent a long car ride last weekend arguing about the Doomsday Argument, which we had both been reading about in connection with Bostrom's work on anthropic reasoning. She was defending the basic move: that one should reason as though one had been randomly sampled from everyone in one's reference class who will ever exist, so that finding yourself to be, say, the seventy-billionth human being rather than the seventy-trillionth counts as genuine evidence that our species doesn't have much longer to run. I raised what seemed to me the obvious response: that any hypothesis on which vastly more humans eventually exist also makes it more likely that you would exist at all to be doing this reasoning in the first place, which, I thought, should cancel the update rather than leave it standing. She answered with the "presumptuous philosopher" case against exactly that kind of move, where reasoning of the same shape lets someone settle a straightforwardly empirical question about the size of the universe from the armchair, and I did not have a clean reply. We also kept getting stuck on what the relevant "reference class" of observers was even supposed to be, without ever settling it between us.

I would like to see a properly worked-out version of this disagreement, since I do not think either of us argued our own position, let alone the other's, as well as it deserves. Please write a dialogue between two philosophers: one defending the Doomsday Argument on the basis of the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA), and the other resisting the inference using the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA). I want each to engage with the other's actual strongest moves rather than simply restate their starting position -- in particular, I would like the SSA-defender to press the presumptuous philosopher objection against the SIA-defender, and the SIA-defender to have a substantive answer to it rather than just conceding that the conclusion is embarrassing. I would also like at least one exchange in which they try, and fail to fully agree, on how the reference class ought to be specified, since that is the part neither my friend nor I could resolve. Please do not let either character simply concede by the end -- if the dispute is as genuinely unsettled in the literature as it seemed to us, the dialogue should reflect that.
