In my economics class, our group is writing a policy memo proposing a carbon tax, and while drafting the cost-benefit section, two of my groupmates ended up in a genuine argument about discount rates that neither would let go.

Ben opened by saying that any positive discount rate applied to future people's well-being is indefensible on its face: it treats a unit of harm or benefit as literally less important purely because of when it occurs, which is exactly what economists mean by "pure time preference," and which serious writers on the subject, starting with Frank Ramsey, have dismissed as an unprincipled bias rather than a reasoned position.

Nora replied that Ben was collapsing a real distinction: almost none of the discount rate used in actual climate-economic models is pure impatience. Most of it reflects the expectation that future generations will be richer, so an extra dollar of benefit matters less to them, plus the genuine uncertainty that any specific projected cost or benefit will actually occur that far out. Neither of those, she said, is pure time preference at all.

Ben conceded the distinction exists on paper but said it doesn't rescue the actual policy debate: the whole reason Nordhaus's models produce a higher discount rate than the Stern Review's is that Nordhaus doesn't set pure time preference to zero, while Stern essentially does. So if pure time preference is really as indefensible as Nora had just admitted, doesn't that undercut Nordhaus's numbers specifically, rather than just supporting some vague "a little discounting is fine" position?

Nora answered that this still runs together two separate questions: whether pure time preference is ethically defensible, and what discount rate climate policy should actually use. You could reject pure time preference completely and still land near Nordhaus's higher number once growth and uncertainty are properly accounted for, and you could accept a little pure time preference and still land near Stern's number under different growth assumptions. Which headline figure is correct, she said, is a separate fight from the one Ben thinks he has already won.
