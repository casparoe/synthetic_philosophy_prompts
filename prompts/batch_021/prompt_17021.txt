Write a dialogue between a Bohmian and a superdeterminist who meet at a loophole-free Bell-test apparatus and disagree about what the observed violations of the Bell inequalities show about locality. The Bohmian holds that we must accept an explicitly nonlocal guiding equation while preserving measurement independence — the statistical independence of the hidden variables from the measurement settings — since giving up that independence would make experimental reasoning itself unreliable. The superdeterminist holds the reverse: that measurement independence, not locality, has to go, since particles and detectors share a common causal past and so were never really statistically independent to begin with. The Bohmian should press the charge that superdeterminism is either an unfalsifiable conspiracy or an empty relabeling of the problem, and the superdeterminist should press the charge that Bohmian nonlocality covertly relies on a preferred relativistic frame of simultaneity; each should respond to the other's specific charge rather than simply restating their own view.
