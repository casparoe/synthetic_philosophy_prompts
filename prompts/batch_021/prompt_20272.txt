I cover science and ideas for a living, but a local arts nonprofit roped me into emceeing their photography show this weekend, and now i have to write my own opening remarks. The board chair wants me to open with Du Bois's 'all art is propaganda' line from "Criteria of Negro Art," since its basically this year's tagline. Except I actually read the whole 1926 speech this week -- it was originally delivered in honor of Carter Woodson's Spingarn medal -- and Du Bois's real argument is stranger and more specific than the slogan: it hinges on this claim that Beauty can't be pulled apart from Truth and 'Right,' and it's really about Black artists' right to show the full truth of their own lives on their own terms, not whatever version white publishers would print. Problem is, plenty of people pushed back on this even at the time -- a bunch of writers who answered his own magazine's survey on the same question said art shouldn't be yoked to any cause, period -- and two of tonight's photographers have told me privately they feel exactly the same way. Can you draft me about three minutes of opening remarks that actually engage with what Du Bois was arguing, not just the bumper-sticker version, and that doesn't dodge the tension with the photographers in the room?
