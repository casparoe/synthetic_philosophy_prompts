Ok, this is a bit of a rabbit hole, but I've lurked on LW-sphere blogs for like a decade and never actually stopped to think about why the site is literally called "Less Wrong" until recently. The whole vibe is that you'll never reach certainty, but you can keep updating toward beliefs that are closer to the truth then your old ones — which is basically Popper's old idea of "verisimilitude" (truthlikeness). Except it turns out Popper's actual formal definition of verisimilitude got wrecked pretty fast, by Miller and Tichy in the mid-70s if I remember right — his definition implies one false theory can never be closer to the truth than another false theory, which seems to gut the whole concept since literally every scientific theory we've ever had is technically false. So what are the most important implications of that failure for whether "getting less wrong over time" is even a coherent thing to aim for? And does the Bayesian, credence-based version of this that rationalists actually use dodge the problem, or does it just inherit the same mess in a different form?
