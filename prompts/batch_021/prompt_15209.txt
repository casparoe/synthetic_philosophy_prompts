I'm a PhD student in evolutionary biology and the graduate member of the three-person committee that awards my department's annual prize for the best student-authored theory paper. This year's strongest entry re-derives the standard kin-selection account of worker sterility in social insects by treating the colony, rather than the individual worker, as the unit under selection, and argues that this recasting exposes the colony's division of labor as a group-level adaptation rather than a mere byproduct of within-colony kin conflict. The senior member of our committee wants the paper disqualified, arguing that because the Price equation lets any such group-selection model be rewritten as an inclusive-fitness model with identical predictions, its "result" is just kin-selection theory restated in unfamiliar notation; the third member disagrees but hasn't managed to articulate why in a way that has persuaded anyone. Please draft the memo I'll bring to next week's final meeting, recommending whether to award the prize, decline it, or award it contingent on revision, with reasoning the senior member could actually engage with rather than a dismissal of the disqualification argument as merely terminological.
