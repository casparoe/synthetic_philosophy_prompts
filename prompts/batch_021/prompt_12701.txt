I'm a backend engineer, not a philosopher, but I've spent a good chunk of my commute this year working through philosophy of science after a colleague lent me Ladyman and Ross's *Every Thing Must Go*. What hooked me was how neatly "structural realism" seems to map onto something from my day job. The core idea — that our best physical theories give us reliable access to the structure of the world (the pattern of relations holding between things) rather than to whatever fills the role of the things themselves — felt a lot like the distinction between an interface and its implementation. So far, so intuitive. Where I got stuck is a specific objection that keeps coming up in the secondary literature, one that apparently traces back to a mathematician named Newman criticizing an early version of this idea in Bertrand Russell's work. As far as I can tell, the worry is that simply claiming "the world has such-and-such structure" is almost vacuous: given enough objects to play with, you can always define some relation among them that realizes whatever abstract structure you like, so the grand claim to know the world's structure quietly collapses into the far less exciting claim that the world contains at least so many objects.

I gather this is supposed to be a serious problem specifically for the "epistemic" version of structural realism, and that people like Ladyman try to escape it by going "ontic" instead — denying that there are individual objects with some hidden nature sitting underneath the relations for the Newman trick to trivialize in the first place. What none of the explanations I've found quite spell out for a non-philosopher is why that move is actually supposed to work, and why critics who raise the "relations without relata" problem (the thought that you can't have a marriage with no spouses, so to speak) think ontic structural realism just relocates the original difficulty instead of resolving it. Could you walk me through the underlying logic here at a fairly intuitive, gut level rather than in formal terms — why the Newman objection is genuinely damaging in the first place, why "going ontic" is supposed to blunt it, and whether the relations-without-relata reply shows that it doesn't actually succeed?
