I'm a junior double-majoring in cognitive science and philosophy, and this term's philosophy of biology seminar has spent several sessions on the adaptationist controversies that followed Gould and Lewontin's spandrels paper, which I'd honestly filed away as interesting-but-mostly-historical until a live case landed on my desk this week. I serve as a reviewer on the editorial board of my university's undergraduate research journal, and we've deadlocked two against two over a submission arguing that human music is a sexually selected adaptation for advertising cognitive and creative fitness, drawing on Geoffrey Miller's "mating mind" framework, with music's cross-cultural universality and emotional intensity offered as the central evidence. Two board members want to reject the piece outright, on the grounds that it's precisely the kind of unfalsifiable adaptive storytelling Gould and Lewontin's critique targeted, and that the author never seriously engages the rival possibility -- the one behind Pinker's remark that music is "auditory cheesecake" -- that music is a byproduct of other adaptations (language, auditory scene analysis, motor control, emotional signaling) rather than a target of selection in its own right. The other two think this objection proves too much: almost no adaptive hypothesis about a psychological trait could survive a demand for that kind of direct historical evidence, and holding this paper to a standard we haven't applied to other adaptationist submissions we've accepted looks less like rigor than like moving the goalposts after the fact.

We owe the author a decision by Friday -- accept, reject, or accept pending revisions -- and I'd rather not let it come down to an unexplained tie broken by whoever happens to chair the meeting. Could you draft the short recommendation memo I'll circulate beforehand? I'd like it to commit to a single recommendation, to spell out concrete, checkable revision criteria and a resubmission deadline in case we go that route (something more substantive than telling the author to "consider alternative explanations"), and to take the double-standard objection seriously rather than treating the spandrels critique as a conversation-ending trump card.
