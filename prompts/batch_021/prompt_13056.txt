heres how it went:
1. Owen said this proves Milo's still fully blameworthy even though he literally couldn't have done otherwise (the chip guaranteed it either way), since the chip played zero role in why he actually cheated.
2. Maya said not so fast, theres still an alternate possibility hiding in there: the possibility of him starting to lean away from cheating, which would've set off the chip. thats a genuinely different path, so alternate possibilities aren't gone, just pushed earlier.
3. Owen said that kind of "possibility" can't ground responsibility anyway, since Milo has zero control over whether the chip fires -- the only thing up to him is which way he leans, and once he leans toward cheating everything after that is just as fixed as if there were no chip at all.

Maya asked: does the chip read his brain before he's leaned either way, or only once he's already leaning against cheating? if it can already tell beforehand, then something about his brain was gonna make him cheat regardless of the chip, which just assumes determinism instead of proving anything new. and if it only reads him once he's already leaning away, there was a real window where he could've gone the other way.
5. Owen said fine, then make the chip scan individual neurons firing, faster than any "leaning" could even happen, so theres zero window, ever. Maya said at that point he's not even describing a Frankfurt case anymore, he's just baking in total determinism from the start, which kind of assumes the very thing that's supposed to be up for debate.
