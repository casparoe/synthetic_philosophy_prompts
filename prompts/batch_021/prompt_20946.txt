I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, but I've been reading about David Lewis's Best System Account (BSA) of laws of nature, and I can't shake the feeling that it's essentially the Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle applied to the whole history of the world. I'd appreciate comments on some notes where I try to work this out, since I want to know whether the idea holds up before I lean on it any further.

Under BSA, a law is whatever falls out of the true deductive system that best balances simplicity (fewer, shorter axioms) against strength (how much of the "Humean mosaic" of particular matters of fact it entails). That looks structurally identical to the MDL two-part code, where you pick the system S that minimizes L(S) + L(mosaic | S), with L denoting description length. On this reading, being a law is just being extractable from the optimal compression scheme for the world, and MDL's invariance results (that optimal codes agree up to a bounded constant regardless of which universal description language you pick) seem like they could do real work against the standard complaint that BSA's simplicity/strength trade-off is arbitrary or language-relative.

Two things make me doubt this dressing-up actually helps the Humean side, though:

1. Anti-Humeans like Armstrong and Maudlin argue that a BSA law can't explain the regularity it's drawn from, since it only counts as a law because the regularity already holds; citing it to explain the pattern is circular. In MDL terms, that becomes "citing your own compression scheme to explain the data it was fit to." That sounds like it makes the objection sharper, not weaker, but I can't tell if I've actually added anything or just re-described the same complaint in new vocabulary.

2. Lewis's own worry about chance in "Humean Supervenience Debugged" — I believe he calls it the "big bad bug" — is that objective chances, being part of the best system, supervene on the whole history, including the future, even though the Principal Principle wants those same chances to constrain rational credence about that future. A different future would yield a different best system and hence different chances, "undermining" the ones supposedly in force at the time. That sounds a lot like the trouble with prequential or online compression schemes that are implicitly optimized against a sequence including symbols not yet observed, then used to license a prediction about the still-unseen part.

I'd appreciate comments on:
- Whether the MDL gloss on BSA is basically sound, or rests on a disanalogy that makes it misleading rather than clarifying.
- Whether recasting objection (1) in compression terms adds anything, or is just cosmetic.
- Whether the parallel I'm drawing in (2), between undermining and prequential hindsight, is apt or just superficial pattern-matching.
- Which of the two objections is the more serious problem for a compression-flavored Humeanism, and whether there's a standard Humean reply to either that I'm clearly missing.
