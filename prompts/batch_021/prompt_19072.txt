I have been chosen to give the closing remarks at my high school graduation, and I would like to build the speech around Timothy Williamson's epistemicist solution to the sorites paradox: the claim that a sharp threshold between, say, child and adult, or incoming freshman and graduate, really does exist -- we simply can never know where it falls. That rules out the usual commencement-speech line that "there was no single moment, it all happened gradually," which is actually the opposite position, and I want the speech to take the stranger, less comforting one seriously instead. The complication is my audience: my philosophy teacher and the handful of classmates who took that elective with me will notice immediately if the idea gets flattened into a cliché, while most of the graduates and every parent in the room have never heard the word "sorites" and came for a graduation, not a logic lecture. Could you write that speech for me, about five minutes long, so that it rewards the first group without losing the second?
