okay so this is something that's been rattling around since a colloquium last month and I still haven't resolved it. I'm doing my PhD in philosophy of physics, and afterward a friend who's actually in the cosmology department and I got into it about Boltzmann brains at the bar. His position (basically the Sean Carroll "cognitively unstable" line) is that any theory whose future is a long-lived, near-equilibrium de Sitter phase is basically self-defeating, because given enough time, freak thermal/quantum fluctuations that briefly assemble something brain-shaped, complete with a full set of false memories, vastly outnumber observers who got here the normal way through stars and evolution -- so if the theory's right, you're almost certainly one of the fluctuations, in which case you have no business trusting the reasoning that led you to accept the theory in the first place. I pushed back that this smuggles in a typicality/self-sampling assumption that's doing way more work than people usually admit, and also that it's not obvious a fluctuation lasting an instant even has the right kind of causal structure to count as believing or remembering anything -- so maybe the "reasoner" in the reductio isn't a reasoner at all, and the whole argument never gets off the ground. We argued for like an hour and never actually settled who's intuitions about typicality were supposed to win out.

Could you write me a dialogue between two people holding these two positions -- one convinced that Boltzmann brain domination really is a fatal, decisive problem for a cosmological theory, the other convinced the argument is resistible (whether through the measure problem, doubts about the self-sampling assumption, or doubts about whether a momentary fluctuation is even the kind of thing that can have beliefs)? Id like them to be equally sharp and actually respond to each other's specific moves rather than just talking past one another -- e.g., if the skeptic raises the "these things can't really believe anything" point, the other side needs a real counter, not just a shrug. Doesn't have to be long, just something that feels like two people who've actually read the literature hashing it out, maybe set at that same bar table, since that's sort of where this started for me.
