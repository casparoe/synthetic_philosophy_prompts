My aunt died last week and I somehow got roped into speaking at her memorial. She'd spent something like 35 years running a little Madhyamaka meditation group out of her living room and knew Nagarjuna's Root Verses back to front — I'm just a software engineer who sat in on her Tuesday group sometimes, not a dharma teacher, so this feels way above my pay grade. Before she died she left a note with real specific instructions: don't say she's "in a better place," or "watching over us," or that her soul "carries on" — but also dont you dare say she "just ended, nothing more" either. She even preemptively banned the cop-out of calling it "an unanswerable mystery" — said that's not what emptiness means either. Problem is half the room is her actual sangha who'll 100% notice if I fudge this, and the other half is coworkers and cousins who've never heard "emptiness" used like that and just want to feel a bit better. Can you write me something like 5 minutes long that actually deals with this honestly instead of hiding behind generic "everything is impermanent" vibes?
