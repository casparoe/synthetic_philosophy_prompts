I'm working on a piece about probabilistic reasoning and keep running into Bas van Fraassen's "Judy Benjamin problem," in which a lost soldier receives a radio message giving a conditional probability about her location within Red territory, and updating by minimizing cross-entropy relative to her prior ends up shifting her confidence that she's in Red territory at all — despite the message saying nothing about Blue territory. This is often held up as proof that Bayesian conditionalization is simply incoherent whenever evidence takes an "if A, then probability p" form, which worries me, since much of the evidence I deal with as a reporter — weather forecasts, polling caveats, analysts' conditional estimates — has exactly that structure. Given this apparent flaw, how should I adjust the way I update my own beliefs whenever I'm handed this kind of conditional evidence?
