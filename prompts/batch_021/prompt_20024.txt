So I had this whole argument with my friend Marco last night (half of it over text while he was clearly falling asleep) about the "coherence arguments" that keep showing up in AI safety discussions -- the move where you say: if an agent's preferences are cyclic, like it prefers A to B, B to C, but then also prefers C to A, then it can be money-pumped. A savvy trader just walks it around that loop over and over, skimming a little off the top each round, and the agent has no principled way to refuse because at every single step it's freely choosing the trade it prefers in the moment. Marco's take was that this basically settles things for advanced AI: any system competent enough to not bleed resources for nothing will end up with acyclic preferences, and once you throw in a couple more axioms, that's just what it means to be an expected-utility-maximizer over some utility function, full stop. I felt like there was a gap in there somewhere but couldn't nail it down at 1am, and its been bugging me since, so now you get to be my proxy Marco.

Can you build the strongest version of the counterargument? Skip the easy stuff like "maybe it just doesn't care about resources" and actually get into the structure of the money-pump argument itself. Two things I especially want dealt with: first, does ruling out cycles (so the agent literally can't be pumped) actually force you all the way to a complete, well-defined utility function, or is there room for an agent with incomplete or context-sensitive preferences that's simply never in a position to be exploited, without ever being tidy enough to represent as an EU-maximizer? Second, what about an agent that still has "irrational" pairwise preferences if you isolate any two trades on their own, but that looks ahead, sees the loop coming, and just declines the first trade (or commits to a whole plan up front instead of choosing hop by hop)? Does that agent actually save its resources without secretly becoming coherent in the process, or is there some reason that trick doesn't really work -- like maybe it just hides the incoherence somewhere the money-pump story can't reach, rather than actually getting rid of it?
