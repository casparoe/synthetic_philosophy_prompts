I am a novelist, and over the course of three books I have noticed a troubling pattern: during revisions, my editor identifies some plot choice or character beat as not working; I keep it regardless, because it feels unmistakably right to me; and every time, months later, once reviews, readers, and my own distance from the manuscript have had their say, I end up agreeing that she was right and that I had simply been too close to see it. I am now finishing a fourth novel, and there is a late chapter about which I feel more certain than I have ever felt about anything I have written. My editor, unprompted, has singled out that very chapter as the one that fails. I have gone back over my reasons for keeping it and can find nothing wrong with them; the case for the chapter looks, to me, exactly as solid as it ever did. Given her record, should that change how much I trust my own certainty here, even though I cannot locate whatever flaw she is presumably seeing—or would it simply be confused to go on feeling entirely sure about the chapter while also believing, on the strength of her record, that I am probably wrong about it?
