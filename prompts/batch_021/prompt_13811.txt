For a side project, I implemented a toy version of Aumann's agreement theorem: two Bayesian agents share a common prior over a finite state space, each privately observes a noisy signal about the true state, and the two of them then take turns publicly announcing their current posterior probability for some event, each updating only on the other's announced number rather than on the signal behind it. Exactly as the theory predicts, after a handful of rounds the two numbers lock together and stop moving. That seems to show something quite strong: that two rational people never actually need to exchange evidence or arguments to resolve a disagreement, just their confidence levels, traded back and forth. But this is hard to square with experience -- I've had sharp, honest colleagues with whom trading confidence levels for hours, even after we had each fully laid out our reasoning, never closed the gap between us. Does persistent disagreement like that mean one of us was covertly violating some rationality assumption the theorem relies on, or is my simulation missing something that keeps its convergence result from generalizing to disagreements like ours?
