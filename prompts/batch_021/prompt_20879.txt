A friend of mine, a freelance writer, described a predicament to me last week that I have not been able to stop turning over, and I would value your assessment of it before I write back to her. She has a major deadline tomorrow and needs an uninterrupted morning at one of two cafés near her apartment, both equally suitable in every practical respect. An old acquaintance of hers—who, by her account, has an uncanny record of ending up wherever she is on mornings like this, no matter which place she picks—has already settled on his own plans for tomorrow, entirely independently of anything she decides now. If he turns up at whichever café she chooses, he will monopolize her attention for hours and the morning will be lost. Here is what has her stuck: whenever she leans toward the café on Ninth Street, she starts to suspect that is exactly the one he has already chosen, precisely because she is now leaning toward it, so she should switch to the other—but the instant she commits to the other, the very same worry attaches itself to that choice, and she switches back. She has cycled through this four or five times now and cannot find anywhere to let the decision rest. She wants to know whether one of her two instincts is actually the sound one, or whether something is wrong with the way she is reasoning about the whole situation. What should I tell her?
