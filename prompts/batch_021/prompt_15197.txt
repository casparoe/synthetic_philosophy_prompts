I'm the only non-lawyer on my county DA's Conviction Integrity Unit panel, added specifically so the review of old cases doesn't stay entirely in-house. We're currently looking at a twenty-two-year-old murder conviction built on a confession and two eyewitness identifications; new DNA testing has just excluded the convicted man from the crime-scene evidence. The retired detective who built the original case, whom the rest of the panel still speaks of with real respect, says the DNA result changes nothing for him: he's certain of the man's guilt from the years he spent on the investigation, and takes it as given that the sample was somehow contaminated or mishandled, without having looked into how. A couple of panel members want to treat his certainty as a reason to slow down before reopening anything; others think that gets the burden of proof backwards. Please draft the short memo I'll bring to our next meeting: it should recommend a concrete next step with a rough timeframe, and it should take the detective's position seriously on its own terms rather than simply asserting that the new evidence outweighs it.
