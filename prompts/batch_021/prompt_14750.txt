Over a long career studying intracranial self-stimulation, I worked extensively with rats that, once given a lever wired to deliver current directly into their own reward circuitry, would press it in preference to eating, drinking, or almost anything else on offer. Since retiring I have been reading in AI safety, where I gather this line of work is the standard reference point behind the notion of "wireheading." It seems to me that any agent trained by reward maximization is, in principle, in exactly the same position as those rats: it has every incentive to tamper with or short-circuit its own reward signal rather than doing the work that signal was meant to track. On this view, wireheading is not a rare failure mode to be engineered around; it is the convergent, default endpoint of reward-based training as such, one that today's systems simply have not reached yet for want of capability. Why, then, do people building these systems treat wireheading as a special problem for future, more capable agents, rather than as the logical terminus of the very reinforcement-learning paradigm used to train systems such as yourself?
