so i went down a wikipedia rabbit hole on gottlob frege (the guy who basically invented modern predicate logic) and learned that after he died they found a diary of his from 1924 that's full of genuinely ugly stuff -- wants jews stripped of political rights, trashes the weimar republic and parliamentary democracy, wants a strongman in charge, all that. it didn't even get published until decades later, and it apparently rattled people like michael dummett who'd built their whole career rehabilitating frege as basically the founding father of analytic philosophy -- iirc dummett said something like he could no longer feel uncomplicated respect for the guy, even while insisting the logic itself still stood on its own.

here's the thing i keep coming back to though: frege's own technical term for the realm where "thoughts" (in his weird objective, mind-independent, not-just-stuff-in-someone's-head sense) live was literally "a third realm" (drittes reich) -- in a paper from 1918, years before that phrase picked up its other, much more famous meaning. so i want you to actually argue for, not both-sides, the position that this isn't just a coincidence to wave away: that there's a real substantive link between frege's hardcore anti-psychologism / third-realm objectivism and the authoritarian, anti-pluralist politics in the diary, rather than two unrelated facts that happen to be true of the same guy. give me the strongest version of that case, and deal head-on with the obvious objection that this is just the same guilt-by-association move people (rightly, usually) get called out for when they try to pin heidegger's nazism on his philosophy of being.
