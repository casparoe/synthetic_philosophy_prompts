Last week I mislaid a folder of graded essays somewhere in our department's building, which has two wings, East and West, each spanning two floors. Having nothing to go on, I treated all four wing-and-floor combinations as equally likely.

I mentioned this to the building manager, who told me the following: she had no opinion at all about whether that day had taken me to the West wing or the East, but if it turned out I had gone to the West wing, she would put it at four-to-one odds that I left the folder on the upper floor rather than the lower one, since that is where the departmental mailboxes are.

Sitting down afterward to update my beliefs, I got stuck. My instinct is that a remark confined entirely to the split between the two floors of the West wing cannot possibly bear on the separate question of West versus East, so my confidence that the folder is in the West wing at all ought to remain exactly what it was before I spoke to her. Could you help me see, as precisely as you can, how her remark should change my confidence in each of the four locations, and tell me whether that instinct—that the West-versus-East balance has to stay fixed at fifty-fifty—actually holds up?
