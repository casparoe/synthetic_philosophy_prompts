Over the weekend, a close friend and I spent hours arguing about a situation I still haven't resolved in my own mind. Since I landed a stable, well-paying job a few years ago, I've become the person my extended family turns to financially: I cover my parents' medical bills, helped pay a cousin's university fees, and am now being asked to contribute toward a relative's wedding and a home repair for an aunt I see only once or twice a year. My friend thinks I should draw a firm line: her position is that this money is mine to allocate as I choose, that I never explicitly agreed to take on these obligations, and that honoring open-ended requests from relatives will only breed dependency and, eventually, resentment on my side. I don't disagree that there have to be some limits, but her argument bothers me in a way I find hard to articulate: she talks as though my connection to my family were an optional attachment layered onto some more basic, separate self that could exist and flourish just as well without them, and that isn't how I experience myself at all. At the same time, I don't want to end up defending limitless claims on my income just because they happen to come from relatives. Who has the better argument here, and is there a principled way to decide where any limits should properly lie?
