Been on a bit of a decision theory kick lately after re-reading some of Wei Dai's old posts on UDT and skimming the Arbital page on it, and I think I've finally wrapped my head around it, but I want to sanity check the core idea before I go around repeating it to people. As I understand it, the whole point of "updateless" decision theory is that the agent precommits to just never update on evidence at all -- it acts purely off its prior forever, which is exactly why its willing to pay up in Counterfactual Mugging even after Omega tells it the coin landed tails. Basically the agent hears the bad news, shrugs, goes "nope, sticking with my 50/50 prior," and pays the $100 anyway, treating the actual observation as basically irrelevant noise it's not allowed to react to.

Me and a couple people from my meetup's decision theory reading group got into an argument about this last week though. One guy insisted a UDT agent would still switch doors in the Monty Hall problem after the host reveals a goat, which confused me, because isnt switching doors literally just Bayesian updating on new information? If UDT agents are allowed to do that kind of updating, I don't get what's actually "updateless" about them, or why UDT gets treated as such a huge departure from EDT/CDT instead of just being CDT/EDT with extra steps and cooler thought experiments attached. Is "updateless" secretly referring to some narrow technical sense that doesn't really mean what it sounds like it means, and if so why does everyone on LW act like this is such a profound, cutting edge idea?
