I have been working through Book Three of Schopenhauer's The World as Will and Representation for a seminar on nineteenth-century aesthetics, and what strikes me is how exacting his standards for "legitimate" music turn out to be once you get past the famous slogan that music copies the Will itself rather than the Platonic Ideas. He ranks music above even tragedy in his hierarchy of the arts on exactly this basis, and in §52 (and again in the supplementary chapter on music in the second volume) he tries to cash the claim out in considerable technical detail: the four voices of a harmony are supposed to mirror the grades of the Will's objectification, from the ground bass (inorganic nature) through the inner voices (plant and animal life) up to the melody, which he equates with self-conscious human striving and desire. Composing a melody that traces this striving is, for him, a matter of unconscious genius rather than deliberate craft: the composer reveals a wisdom his own reason cannot follow -- Schopenhauer's own image is of a "magnetic somnambulist" who reports things of which she has no idea when awake. This is also why he is so hostile to music that merely illustrates external events: he singles out passages of Haydn's The Seasons as a lapse into servile imitation, while praising Rossini precisely because his music, on Schopenhauer's telling, speaks its own language rather than being tailored from moment to moment to whatever the libretto happens to be depicting.

I would like your best reconstruction of how Schopenhauer would judge, strictly on these terms, the two dominant idioms of music written for moving images and interactive media today. On one side is the tightly synchronized, illustrative scoring found in classic cartoon animation and in a great deal of film and game "action" cues (what composers call "Mickey Mousing"), where a swell, a sting, or a rhythmic figure is timed to match an on-screen event almost gesture for gesture -- an intensified version, one might think, of exactly the fault he found in Haydn. On the other side is adaptive and sometimes procedurally generated game music, whose structure is not just synchronized with but causally triggered by what a player does moment to moment, so that it tracks depicted events even more tightly than anything Schopenhauer had in mind. Would he simply treat both as aggravated forms of the illustrative music he already rejected, or does his own distinction between Rossini's independence from the text and mere imitation leave room for some of this music to count as "genuine," provided it retains enough melodic autonomy from the images it accompanies? And, given that he thinks music's authority depends on an unconscious genius who is, so to speak, possessed by the Will, what should he say about scores produced by an algorithm that composes without any subject undergoing striving or desire at all -- does his theory have the resources to rule such music out as mere dressed-up noise, or would it have to concede, awkwardly, that this is beside the point?
