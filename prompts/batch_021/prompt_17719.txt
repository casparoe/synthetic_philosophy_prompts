So von Neumann has this old essay from 1955 called "Can We Survive Technology?" where his main point is basically that technology advances faster than our institutions can adapt, and since the world is now fully "closed" (no more frontiers to run to, everything's finite and connected), the crises tech creates just keep recurring at bigger and bigger scale. His conclusion was pretty bleak: there's no permanent fix for this, only ad-hoc, crisis-by-crisis management forever. A lot of current existential-risk thinking seems to assume the opposite -- that its at least possible in principle to reach some kind of durable "safe" state (through institutions, treaties, the tech itself, whatever). Write an essay weighing the strongest arguments on both sides, then actually commit to an answer: was von Neumann basically right that permanent safety is a fantasy, or is durable safety actually achievable? Don't just hedge with "it depends" -- pick a side and defend it.
