I'm a retired physicist who's fallen into philosophy pretty hard since I stopped working full time a couple years back - mostly working through the "greats" one at a time with a coffee each morning, no particular system to it. Right now Im slowly chewing through Schopenhauer's The World as Will and Representation, and I hit the section on music (book 3, somewhere around section 52 I think) that's been rattling around in my head for a week now. His basic move, as I understood it, is that every other art - painting, sculpture, poetry, even tragedy - only gets at the will indirectly, by depicting the Platonic Ideas that the will objectifies itself into at various grades. Music skips that whole step: it isn't a copy of the Ideas at all, its somehow a direct copy of the will itself, running parallel to the whole phenomenal world rather than depicting any part of it. He even says the world could just as well be called embodied music as embodied will.

And then he gets oddly specific about the mechanics of it: the bass line is supposed to mirror inert, unorganized nature, the inner voices mirror the plant and animal kingdoms, and the wandering, ever-searching melody on top mirrors self-conscious human willing, with its restless departure from and return to the keynote standing in for desire, obstruction, and temporary satisfaction. I mentioned all this to my old lab partner over email and she just wrote back "sounds like you've been out in the sun too long," which, fair, it is a strange thing for a guy mostly remembered for being gloomy about everything to wax mystical about. But it's stuck with me anyway, and I keep wondering what it would actually be like from the inside - what it would feel like for someone to experience music the way Schopenhauer describes it, rather than just having the ordinary sort of feelings about it people usually report at dinner parties.

So here's what I'd like: write me a short story, a few pages is plenty, built around that specific idea - not a generic "music moves people deeply" story, but one where the plot or the ending genuinely depends on the particular distinction he's drawing, that music isn't a picture of anything, not even of emotions about something, but supposedly the will's own direct signature, one level closer to the thing-in-itself than any other art gets. Set it wherever serves that best - I keep picturing either an old composer near the end of his life, or maybe some scientist type like me who stumbles into it by accident, but that's just a nudge, not a requirement. I'd just like to come away from it able to point to exactly where the Schopenhauer is doing the work, rather than it just being wallpaper behind a nice story about music.
