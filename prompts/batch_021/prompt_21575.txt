Last night a friend who works in AI safety and I spent two hours arguing about how much weight "coherence arguments" can really bear. Her position was that the Dutch-book and money-pump arguments do genuine philosophical work: any agent that can be exploited by a cycle of trades leaving it strictly worse off has every incentive to eliminate that vulnerability, so any sufficiently capable, persistent agent will end up behaving as if it maximizes some expected utility function -- and that this is exactly why we should expect advanced AI systems to converge on stable, instrumentally convergent, goal-directed behavior regardless of what they were trained to want. I argued that this reads far more into the representation theorems than they can support: satisfying the axioms is compatible with a utility function gerrymandered after the fact to rationalize whatever the agent already does, so coherence alone secures no substantive goal-directedness over outcomes, and even completeness -- the axiom carrying most of the justificatory burden here -- isn't obviously something rationality requires, since agents with incomplete preferences can dodge the money pump without ever resolving their incomparabilities. We never got anywhere.

Write a dialogue that picks up exactly where we left off, between a defender of the strong claim that coherence entails expected-utility maximization and a skeptic who thinks that result is being asked to do far more work than it can bear. Have each character press the other's actual weak points instead of restating their own view, have the skeptic spell out what "goal-directed" could even mean if coherence alone doesn't deliver it, and end with each side stating the narrowest concession it cannot avoid making.
