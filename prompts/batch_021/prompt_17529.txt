So I've been working through Nietzsche's Genealogy of Morals on my own this year (no classes, just me, a beat-up paperback, and way too much time on the bus), and I think I've got the basic gist of the first essay: our whole modern idea of "good vs evil" didn't come from some pure insight into what's actually right, it came from a bunch of weak, resentful people who couldn't fight back against their masters physically, so instead they took revenge in their heads by flipping the entire value system around — calling meekness and humility "good" and calling the strength and pride of the people on top "evil." He's got this whole idea of "ressentiment," this repressed, festering bitterness, being basically the birthplace of morality as we know it.

Heres what's bugging me though: if that really is where our morality comes from — a bunch of bitter people cooking up values out of spite because they lost — doesn't that pretty much prove morality isn't real? Like, once you know the actual origin story of an idea is "people were mad and wanted payback," how can anyone still take it seriously as something objectively true or good? I brought this up with my buddy who took one intro philosophy course in college and all he said was "that's not really how it works" before changing the subject, which has been driving me nuts for like a week now. So did Nietzsche basically prove morality is a con, or is there some reason that where an idea comes from doesn't actually settle whether its true?
