Ok so this happened at my cousin's wedding last weekend and I can't stop turning it over in my head. My uncle does quant trading at some hedge fund (I don't fully get what that means day to day, but he's clearly very sharp about markets), and at the reception we ended up talking about a biotech company that has a big FDA decision coming up next month. Half joking, half serious, he offered to bet me $500 on whether the stock will be higher or lower 30 days from now, and told me I could pick whichever side I wanted.

My gut reaction was "sure, sounds like a free coin flip, why not," but it started nagging at me on the drive home. This is a guy who literally trades on exactly this kind of event for a living, and knows way more then me about how these things usually go. So if someone that informed is genuinely happy to take either side against me, doesn't that itself mean somethings off, like there's no actual edge for me on either side of that bet? I mentioned this to my roommate, who studied economics, and he said something like "you should be suspicious of a bet where the other guy is indifferent to which side he gets, because him even offering it tells you something," but he couldn't really explain the reasoning past that one line, and it kind of reminded me of that old joke about not wanting to join any club that would let you in.

I keep going back and forth on whether I was actually onto something or just being paranoid about a fun wedding bet. Was my hesitation logically justified, and if so what's actually going on underneath it? And does this kind of reasoning generalize in a worrying way — like, does it mean nobody should ever knowingly trade or bet against someone more informed than them, and if that's true, why does anyone trade stocks at all?
