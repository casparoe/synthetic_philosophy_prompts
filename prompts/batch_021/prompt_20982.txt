For the past few weeks I've been stuck on something that started out as an ordinary money problem but has turned into a puzzle I can't stop turning over in my head, and I'd like some help thinking it through. My younger brother has worked at the same auto repair shop for six years, and he's asked me to lend him close to forty thousand dollars — essentially every penny I've set aside for emergencies — so he can buy out his business partner and take sole ownership of the place. Over the years he has borrowed smaller amounts from me maybe half a dozen times, for things like car repairs or a rough month of rent, and he has paid back every single one, on time, without me ever having to chase him. Whenever those smaller requests came up I never hesitated for a second: I simply knew he would pay me back, and I was right every time. Nothing about his job, his habits, or his general trustworthiness has changed since the last time he borrowed from me.

And yet now that the amount on the table is large enough to wipe out my entire safety net if things go wrong, I find I can't honestly bring myself to say I know he'll pay it back, even though I would say exactly that without hesitation if he were asking for a few hundred dollars again. It isn't that I've learned anything new about him — the evidence sitting in front of me is the very same evidence it always was. I brought this up with a couple of friends over dinner and it turned into a minor argument. My wife thinks I'm being irrational about it: her view is that if the odds of him paying me back haven't moved an inch, then whatever made it true that I "knew" it before should make it just as true now, and that my hesitation is really just fear wearing the costume of caution. Part of me suspects she's right, but part of me suspects the situation genuinely is different in some way that has nothing to do with cowardice.

So here is what I'd like help with: is my wife correct that I should feel just as entitled to say I know now as I did back when the amounts were small, or is there some real, defensible difference between the two situations? And whichever of us has it right, what should that conclusion actually change about how I go about deciding whether to hand over the money?
