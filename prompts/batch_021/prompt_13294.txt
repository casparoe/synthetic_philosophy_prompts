While reporting a story on corporate mergers, I keep hearing HR consultants describe a pattern that I would like a clearer way to think about. When employees are told a full year in advance that their jobs will be eliminated for reasons that have nothing to do with performance, and nothing else about the situation changes over that year, two simple predictions both turn out to be wrong: that performance would hold steady until a sudden final-day drop, or that it would collapse immediately once the news landed. What actually happens is that effort erodes gradually, and the decline accelerates as the end date nears, even though, as one consultant put it to me, "the deal is exactly the same on day one as on the second-to-last day: keep doing the job, keep getting paid until the end." I cannot work out why knowing the fixed end point from the very start would produce that particular gradual, accelerating shape rather than either of those simpler patterns. What is actually going on here?
