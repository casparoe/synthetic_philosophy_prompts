So I've been making my way through Nietzsche on my own this year (no classes, just started with Zarathustra and then moved on to Ecce Homo and Twilight of the Idols), and theres something that's been bugging me. This is the same guy who gives us the Übermensch, eternal recurrence, "God is dead," the whole project of revaluing every value we've got - and then in "Why I Am So Clever" he spends pages and pages on why German beer ruins the spirit, why coffee doesn't agree with him, which climates set off his headaches, and exactly how big his meals should be, all stuff that honestly reads like a wellness blog. At first I just found it funny, like the guy's a crank about his stomach. But then I remembered he's also the guy who says moralities are basically symptoms of the body that holds them, that Socrates and Christianity were signs of physiological decline, that bit in the Gay Science about "the great health," and that line in Zarathustra where the body gets called a "great reason" and what we normally call reason or spirit is demoted to a tool of it. So now I honestly can't tell if the diet-and-climate obsession is doing real philosophical work (of course a philosopher who thinks it's all physiology in the end would obsess over his coffee and his weather) or if it's just biographical noise, from a guy who was sick constantly, that people are being too generous by reading meaning into.

There's a sharper version of this question that really gets me and I don't think I've ever seen answered properly. If Nietzsche's whole move is to unmask philosophies and moral systems as symptoms of the physiology of whoever holds them, doesn't that boomerang back on him? Why isn't his own dietary self-management, and even the "revaluation of all values" project itself, just another symptom of a guy with migraines and stomach trouble trying to manage his own decline, instead of some kind of special insight into how things really are? He obviously thought "great health" got him out of this somehow, but I've never seen it spelled out in a way that actually survives someone pushing back on it.

Could you write me a dialogue between two people who both clearly know Nietzsche's actual texts well, but land on opposite sides of this? One of them should argue that the diet/climate/physiology material is continuous with, and even central to, his core philosophy - not some sideshow next to the "real" ideas. The other should argue it's a personal idiosyncrasy, tied to his own chronic illness, that doesn't do the philosophical work the first person wants it to do, and maybe even sits badly with things Nietzsche also cares about, like perspectivism or values being actively created rather than just secreted by a body. I want genuine back and forth, each one responding to the other's actual last point instead of just re-stating their own view, and I especially want them to spend a few rounds on that self-referential problem - does Nietzsche actually have a way out of it, or is "great health" just a fancy name for "my physiology, but the acceptable kind"? Neither one should be easy to beat here, so make them both genuinely sharp.
