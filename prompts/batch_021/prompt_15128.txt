This term I am taking a decision theory course that is cross-listed between the philosophy and economics departments, and something my roommate did recently has been bothering me in a way that I suspect connects to material we have covered, even though I cannot quite work out how. He is really into vintage cameras, and over the past month I watched him carry out a sequence of trades with people from a collectors' forum. First he traded his Canon AE-1 plus $20 cash for a friend's Pentax K1000. A couple of weeks later, he traded that Pentax plus $15 for someone else's Nikon FM2. Last week he traded the FM2 plus $10 for a Minolta X-700. And yesterday he traded the Minolta plus $25 to get his original Canon AE-1 back from the very first person, who by then had apparently grown tired of it. As a result, my roommate now owns exactly the camera he started with a month ago, minus $70, and he seems entirely unbothered by this. When I pointed out that he had essentially paid $70 to travel in a circle, he said that at each step he genuinely preferred the camera he was about to receive to the one he already had, so every trade was a good decision at the time he made it, and that one cannot call someone irrational for making a string of individually good decisions.

That defense does not sit right with me, but I am struggling to say exactly what is wrong with it using the vocabulary from class. My professor mentioned, in passing, that examples like this are part of why many decision theorists think a rational agent's choices have to be describable as maximizing some single, consistent measure of value across everything they might face, rather than merely being reasonable one comparison at a time—but I do not see how one gets from "my roommate went in an expensive circle" to a conclusion that strong. Is there really something irrational going on here, given that he can defend each individual step and that his tastes may genuinely have shifted a little between trades? And if there is, could you walk me through how a small example like this is supposed to support the much bigger claim my professor was gesturing at?
