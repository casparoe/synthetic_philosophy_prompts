my best friend's getting married next month and her fiance is a metaphysician who wrote his whole dissertation defending perdurantism -- the view that persons are four-dimensional 'worms' made of temporal parts, rather than being wholly present at every single moment, which is how endurantists think persistence works. I'm giving the maid of honor toast, and a third of the room is his philosophy department, who'll clock it instantly if I fudge the temporal-parts stuff (I'm an endurantist myself, for what it's worth). Write me a toast that has real fun with the idea that 'the person I fell for' and 'the person who'll be sitting next to me at ninety' aren't, on his view, strictly the same thing -- just different parts of one worm -- and maybe even plays with what 'till death do us part' means if the parting's already baked into the worm the whole time. It still has to land as a toast about loving someone for life, not a lecture on mereology, so keep it warm and a little cheeky, and don't let the philosophers win by making it too technical for everyone else.
