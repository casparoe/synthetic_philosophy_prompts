So a few of us run a small discord reading group working through old lesswrong-adjacent decision theory posts, and last session (on acausal blackmail -- basically the sober, non-basilisk-flavored corner of that whole genre of idea) turned into a side argument between two people in the group. not me, i was just watching it happen in the chat. quick context in case you need it: the idea under discussion was "surrogate goals" -- instead of just committing to never give in to threats (including acausal ones, from predictors/simulators you can't causally interact with), you publicly act like you care a ton about some minor, cheap goal, so a threatener trying to extort you targets that instead of whatever you actually value.

here's roughly how it went:

1. Dev said this is just self-deception with extra steps -- you can't make yourself actually care about something you don't, and if a threatener is capable of simulating/predicting you well enough to blackmail you acausally in the first place, they're capable of noticing the surrogate is fake and threatening the real thing anyway.

2. Mira said that misses the point -- you don't need to sincerely care about the surrogate, you just need it to be common knowledge that you'll behave as if you do (i.e. pay costs to defend it). she compared it to a company with a strict no-negotiating-with-kidnappers policy even though plenty of individual employees would personally cave.

3. Dev came back with: ok, but then why wouldn't a threatener just design the threat to route around the surrogate on purpose, something like "this hits your real values no matter what fake target you point me at" -- which seems to break the whole strategy. and if a threatener can't even manage that, were they really scary enough to need this defense in the first place?

4. Mira said no strategy, including flat refusal (which is the standard recommendation anyway), survives a threatener who's stipulated to identify and verify your true values with certainty -- thats true by construction and isn't a special problem with surrogate goals. the real question is whether it helps against threateners who can't do that perfectly, and there it should still redirect some threats.

5. Dev's closer was that "can't verify your true values perfectly" is doing basically all the work in that answer, and that the strategy quietly needs threateners smart enough to be worth fearing acausally but dumb enough to fall for a goal that was, by Mira's own account, only ever common-knowledge-fake.

it looped a couple more times after that but it was mostly restating 3 through 5. could you go through this move by move and say who's got the better of each exchange -- not just who wins overall -- and flag anything you think both of them are missing that's already been hashed out by people who actually work on this stuff?
