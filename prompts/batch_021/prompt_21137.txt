Compare two versions of iterated elimination of dominated strategies in a finite normal-form game: one that eliminates a pure strategy only when some other pure strategy strictly dominates it, and one that also eliminates a pure strategy whenever some mixed strategy strictly dominates it. Explain whether and how allowing mixed strategies as dominators changes the final set of surviving strategies, with a concrete example. This mixed-strategy procedure is standardly presented as equivalent to rationalizability, the set of strategies consistent with common knowledge of rationality. Does that equivalence still hold once a game has more than two players, and does it matter whether a player's beliefs about different opponents can be correlated? Justify your answer.
