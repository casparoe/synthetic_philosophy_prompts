so in my ethics elective we got into utilitarianism and my teacher brought up negative utilitarianism, this offshoot where the only thing that actually matters morally is reducing suffering, not increasing happiness. it reminded me of a thought experiment i saw on a philosophy blog: theres a button that would instantly and painlessly end all sentient life in the universe - everything just stops, no pain, just nothing, forever. a strict negative utilitarian seems kind of forced to say pressing it is the right move since guaranteed zero suffering beats any future that has even a chance of suffering in it, but a normal ('positive') utilitarian would say obviously dont press it bc all the future happiness matters too. can you write me a dialogue between the two of them standing in front of the button, each genuinely trying to talk the other into their side? i dont want the negative utilitarian to just be a strawman doomer - they should have a real answer for why a universe of guaranteed zero beats a gamble on a mostly-happy one, and i dont want the positive utilitarian to just say "that's obviously insane" either - they should actually engage with the asymmetry argument (like how removing pain is good even with no one around to benefit from it, but pleasure that never happens isnt really a loss to anyone) instead of just dodging it.
