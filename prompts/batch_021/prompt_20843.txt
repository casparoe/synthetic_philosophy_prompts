I've read enough LessWrong, Overcoming Bias, and EA Forum material to have internalized Yudkowsky's point about scope insensitivity: the finding that people's willingness to pay to prevent, say, migratory-bird deaths barely increases even as the number of birds at stake grows by orders of magnitude, and his conclusion that we should "shut up and multiply" rather than trust intuitions that don't scale with the numbers. So when I finally read Taurek's "Should the Numbers Count?", my first reaction was that he'd simply dressed scope insensitivity up in philosophical language — that his refusal to say we're obligated to save the five over the one is just what you'd expect from someone who hasn't corrected for the bias. But Parfit evidently didn't think a debiasing exercise was an adequate reply, since he devoted an entire paper, "Innumerate Ethics," to rebutting Taurek's actual argument. Could you explain what, if anything, survives in Taurek's position once scope insensitivity has been fully corrected for — or does his argument simply collapse into the bias once that correction is made?
