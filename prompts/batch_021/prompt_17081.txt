I've been reading about the coherence arguments meant to show why any sufficiently rational agent -- including a future AI system -- must ultimately act as an expected-utility maximizer. The core move is that an agent whose preferences are cyclic or incomplete can be "money-pumped": walked through a sequence of trades that leaves it strictly worse off for certain. What troubles me is that this seems to prove less than it claims to. Given almost any pattern of choices, however erratic, couldn't one construct some utility function after the fact that makes those choices come out as "maximizing"? If so, it isn't clear to me what the money-pump argument actually forbids, beyond a kind of bookkeeping consistency. Where does the real force of the argument lie, and how do defenders of the argument answer this worry?
