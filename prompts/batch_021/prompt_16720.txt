I've been going down an AI-risk rabbit hole and keep bumping into this move where people say that any agent that can't be turned into a money pump (exploited through a cycle of trades that leaves it strictly worse off than where it started) basically has to be acting like it's maximizing some fixed expected utility function -- that's the whole "coherence argument" for why a sufficiently smart AI will end up with stable, VNM-rational goals even if it didn't start with any. Something about that inference bugs me though: not being pump-able for the handful of bets you're actually offered seems like a way weaker fact than there being one utility function that ranks literally everything, including situations you've never even considered. Can you build the strongest case you can that coherence theorems don't actually get you from the first thing to the second -- that they're being asked to carry more weight then they can?
