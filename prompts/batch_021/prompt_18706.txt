Question: State the premises and conclusion of Harsanyi's Social Aggregation Theorem. Then explain (a) why the theorem is often presented as deriving utilitarianism from expected-utility premises plus unanimity, and (b) the objection, associated with Sen, to reading the theorem this way.

Student's answer: Harsanyi assumes each individual has preferences over lotteries on social outcomes satisfying the von Neumann-Morgenstern axioms (completeness, transitivity, continuity, independence), so each individual i has a utility function U_i, unique up to positive affine transformation. He assumes society's preferences over the same lotteries satisfy the same axioms too, giving a social utility function U_0. The crucial extra premise is Pareto indifference: whenever every individual is indifferent between two lotteries, society is indifferent between them as well. From these premises Harsanyi proves that U_0 has to be a weighted sum of the individual utilities, U_0 = b + a_1U_1 + ... + a_nU_n. Because the social ranking is supposed to be impartial between persons, the a_i must all be equal, and this is exactly classical utilitarianism: rank outcomes by the sum of everyone's utility.

This result is often presented as showing that any decision-maker who is Bayesian-rational and respects unanimity has to be a utilitarian, but that overstates things. The gap Sen pointed to is that the U_i here are von Neumann-Morgenstern utilities, which represent how an individual ranks gambles — their attitude to risk — not their welfare in any richer sense. Two people could have identical well-being but different risk attitudes, which would give them different U_i, so summing the U_i isn't obviously the same as summing welfare. Harsanyi's theorem aggregates risk preferences, and calling the output "utilitarian" quietly assumes that vNM utility already is welfare, which is exactly what's in question.

It's also worth noting that Harsanyi's result directly refutes Arrow's impossibility theorem, since Arrow claimed no consistent social ranking could ever be constructed out of individual preferences, whereas Harsanyi constructs exactly such a ranking.
