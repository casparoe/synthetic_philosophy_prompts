I've been chipping away at this multi-generation family saga for almost two years now (working title's "The Orchard Years," might not survive to publication) and last week I finally reached the chapter where the grandmother, Ruth, dies. I plotted this death out in my very first outline, wrote every beat of the scene myself, and know precisely how and why it happens because Im the one who decided all of it. And yet doing line edits on that chapter I properly cried at my desk -- and this isn't even the first time, I've reread that same scene probably eight or nine times over various drafts now and I still tear up most times, which should definately have worn off by now but hasn't.

My partner walked in on me mid-sob and found the whole thing pretty funny -- his argument was basically "she's not real, YOU made her up, you know exactly how and why she dies because you're the one who decided that, so how can you possibly be sad about something you invented and completely control?" I put it to my writing group too and got a genuine split: half said of course you'd feel that, the other half said that's kind of just you performing an emotion for your own benefit, which is a little silly if you think about it. I didn't have a solid comeback for either camp and it's been nagging at me since.

What gets me is that the usual excuse for why fiction moves people -- that you get absorbed in the story and momentarily forget yourself for a while -- doesn't really fit here. I wasn't lost in anything; I was simultaneously fixing a comma splice two lines up while getting choked up over the same paragraph. So is my partner basically right that this is a confused or irrational reaction, given there's no actual loss happening and I have total knowledge and authorship over every fact involved? Or is there something real and defensible going on when you keep grieving a death you not only know is fake but personally engineered, over and over, edit after edit? I'd love an actual account of what's happening here, rather than just a "feelings don't follow logic" shrug.
