My grandma - Miss Odessa to pretty much the whole neighborhood - passed away two weeks ago at 88, and I'm giving her eulogy this Saturday. She ran a little storefront arts program on the same corner for something like 45 years, teaching neighborhood kids to paint banners for every march and church anniversary and block party going, and she had this one line from W.E.B. Du Bois that she'd quote at you the second you brought up "real art" versus "that other stuff" - "all art is propaganda and ever must be" - it was literally stitched onto a banner that hung over her worktable my whole life. The service is being held at the arts center instead of a funeral home, which is what she wanted, and that means the room is gonna be split right down the middle: half family and neighbors who are gonna hear the word "propaganda" and think Soviet posters or fake news, and half the old guard from the city's Black arts scene, some of them had actual out-loud arguements with her over that exact quote for decades, and they will 100% notice if I flatten it.

Here's the part I'm nervous about getting wrong. From what I've read since she died, Du Bois wasn't saying the message is all that matters and the craft can be garbage - he was pretty adamant that the art also had to be beautiful and honest, that's actually what made it propaganda worth having, and that part was everything to her specifically. She'd genuinely make a kid redo a whole banner if the lettering was sloppy or the faces looked like caricatures, and she'd say bad art for a good cause does more damage to the cause than no art at all does. So I don't want a eulogy that just says "grandma believed art should be political," because that's not really it and the arts-scene crowd will clock it in about two seconds, but I also can't get up there and give a lecture on Du Bois to a room half full of people who are already crying and just want to remember her. Can you write the eulogy for me - something like 5 to 7 minutes read out loud - that actually uses this idea to say something true about who she was, in a way that lands for both halves of that room?
