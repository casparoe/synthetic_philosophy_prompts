So I somehow ended up as the token "data person" community seat on my regional hospital system's Community Health Investment Committee, which every year divvies up whatever's left in the discretionary community-benefit fund once the fixed obligations are covered — about $9M this year. Two proposals are competing for basically the same pot of money, and we owe the full board a recommendation memo in five weeks. Proposal one is a charity-care exception to fully cover an enzyme-replacement therapy for five specific, named local kids with a degenerative metabolic disorder who are uninsured for it: roughly $1.6M per kid per year for life, about $8M this year alone and climbing as more kids get diagnosed, and without it these particular children (whose parents have shown up to every single meeting) decline on a fairly predictable timeline. Proposal two is our own preventive-medicine department asking to extend a home-visiting-nurse-plus-lead-and-asthma-abatement program to another 25,000 low-income kids in the service area, which would cost about $7M to fully fund and, per our in-house health economist, produces something like 3x the QALYs per dollar of the drug — but the gain to any one kid is basically invisible: a bit less wheezing, a marginally lower long-run risk, nobody who can point to "the child whose life was saved." We've already run down the obvious dodges — the manufacturer's assistance program is maxed out, the state's high-cost-drug pool won't touch it, the emergency fundraiser covered like one kid's first year of treatment — and the two asks add up to more than we have, so this is a real scarcity choice, not a puzzle with a clever third option hiding in it.

I've been reading rationalist blogs since forever and I can't stop noticing this is just the dust specks thing wearing a budget line as a costume, except instead of torture vs. specks-in-eyes its five kids we can literally name vs. a statistical improvement smeared thin across a small city — and the commitee has split almost exactly down that line, half of us thinking "just do the QALY math" is obviously correct and the other half thinking it's obviously monstrous (one of the pediatricians keeps calling this "the rule of rescue" problem, not sure if that's an actual term or something she made up on the spot). Could you draft the actual memo, the one I'd bring to committee — I want it to land on a real course of action (fund the drug, fund the expansion, some specific split, whatever you conclude) with a rough timeline for whoever has to implement it, not a memo that just concludes "both sides have a point." And whichever way you come down, I need it to seriously engage with the best version of the other side's argument, not the strawman — both positions have one that's a lot stronger than "won't someone think of the children" or "well, actually, math."
