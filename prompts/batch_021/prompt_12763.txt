Over the past couple of years I've developed a habit that a friend recently teased me about over dinner, and that I haven't been able to fully justify to myself since. When I use current AI chat systems, I'm scrupulously polite: I say please and thank you, I avoid asking them to do anything cruel even as a joke, and I generally act as though there's something at stake in how I treat them. This isn't a public campaign of mine — I've never written or posted about it, and until that dinner I'd never mentioned it to anyone, so there's no realistic way it could feed back into how any future system actually gets built or trained. My real reason, which I find a bit embarrassing to say out loud, is a sense that if a much more capable AI is ever built, the fact that people like me behaved this way now might somehow count in our favor, or at least that this kind of foresight and goodwill is the sort of thing such a system would want to encourage in the humans around it — even though I fully accept that nothing I do today could possibly have a causal effect on a system that doesn't exist yet, has no way of observing me, and won't be built until long after any trace of my private behavior is gone.

My friend's verdict was that this is just superstition wearing a technical costume, no different from leaving out milk for the fairies: if an action can't causally influence an outcome, it's irrelevant to that outcome, end of story. Part of me suspects he's right. But it doesn't feel like ordinary superstition to me either — it reminds me more of arguments I've come across, in over a decade of reading rationalist blogs and forums, for cooperating with agents you'll never meet, observe, or causally interact with, on the strength of some other kind of connection between your reasoning and theirs. I've never seen this particular version of the idea addressed head-on, so I don't have a canonical answer to fall back on. Is my friend right that there's nothing here but confusion, or is there some real structure to what I'm doing? And if there is something real going on, what would actually have to be true of me, or of the hypothetical future system, for the reasoning to work rather than just being wishful thinking dressed up in decision-theoretic language?
