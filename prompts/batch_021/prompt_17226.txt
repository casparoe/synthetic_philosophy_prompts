Been chewing on the regress problem (Agrippa's trilemma -- infinite regress vs circularity vs an arbitrary stopping point) and think I might have a way to dissolve it rather than solve it. Thinking of writing it up properly but want a sanity check first. The idea: split "justified" into two notions.

(1) Structural justification -- whether a chain of adequate reasons for a belief exists at all in the space of reasons, independent of whether anyone's actually traced it. I think this is always infinite, or at least indefinitely extendable with no principled stopping point, so infinitism is basically right about this one.

(2) Situated justification -- whether a given finite person's belief counts as justified given what they can actually produce when pressed for reasons. Foundationalism is basically right here: people stop at stuff that functions as basic in context (a perceptual seeming, etc.), not because the chain structurally ends there but because that's all a finite creature needs to do to count as justified.

Coherentism, on this picture, is just what (1) looks like once you stop insisting the structure has to be a straight line and let reasons loop back and mutually support each other -- so its less a genuine rival to infinitism than a description of the same web, viewed holistically instead of as a chain.

So the three positions aren't really competing solutions to one problem, they're each answering a different question, and the trilemma only looks forced if "justified" is doing one job. I've got a nagging feeling this is basically just Peter Klein's distinction between having justification available vs. actually deploying it, dressed up in new words, but I can't remember his view precisely enough to be sure it maps on cleanly. Can you give me honest comments -- does this actually dissolve anything, is it just relabeling an existing view (Klein's or someone else's), and what's the strongest objection to it?
