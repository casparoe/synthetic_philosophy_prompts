I sit on a three-person committee that funds small research grants only when a proposal is judged both methodologically sound and likely to have real-world impact. On a recent proposal, voting on those two questions separately produced a majority "yes" on each, so by our own rule it should have been funded — yet when I asked the three of us to vote directly on whether to fund it, a majority said no, even though nobody had changed their mind about anything in between. Two of my colleagues now disagree about which vote reflects the committee's real judgment: one says the criterion-by-criterion result is binding since it follows our stated rule, the other says the direct vote is what counts since that's what we each actually believe overall. Is one of them simply mistaken, or does something about combining judgments this way guarantee this kind of clash?
