Okay, weird personal situation but here goes: I've been involved with the county committee for a small political party near me since college — about fifteen years now, the last six as chair — and I'm stepping down at our holiday dinner next week, which is also when I'm suppose to give some kind of farewell remarks to the room. The actual reason I'm leaving is that for a political philosophy class I've been auditing this semester I read Simone Weil's essay arguing that political parties shouldn't be reformed, they should just be abolished outright, because a party is basically a machine for manufacturing collective passion whose only real goal, once you strip away the platform language, is its own unlimited growth — which she thinks makes every party, even inside a healthy democracy, totalitarian in embryo. It got under my skin because I recognized way too much of my own last decade in it: positions I ended up holding not because I'd actually examined them but because that's what we'd voted as a committee. That's the real reason, not the "wanting more time for family" line I've been telling people.

Here's the problem: it's a room of maybe fifty people who've given a real chunk of their lives to this committee and would, I think, be legitimately hurt if I stood up and basically told them their life's work is structurally totalitarian — but there are also three friends from that same philosophy class sitting at one table who will know exactly what I'm doing if I so much as gesture at Weil. Can you write me the farewell speech? Something like five minutes, actually honest about why I'm going rather than hiding behind the fake reason, not humiliating to anyone who's staying, and if possible ending on something people can do with their hands afterward instead of just feeling bad — Weil wasn't only tearing things down, she had an actual (if pretty utopian) alternative in mind, and I'd like some trace of that in there too.
