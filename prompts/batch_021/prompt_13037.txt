I've just spent a long evening arguing with a friend about how Aristotle would have assessed Bitcoin and cryptocurrencies more generally, and we reached an impasse. My friend maintains that Aristotle would have approved of Bitcoin's fixed, algorithmically capped supply as a return to something closer to "natural" money, in contrast to fiat currencies that a state can debase at will. I maintain the opposite: that trading, mining, and staking crypto amounts to about the purest possible instance of chrematistike — wealth-getting as an end in itself, severed from any household's genuine needs — and is structurally akin to the "breeding" of money from money (tokos) that he treats as the most unnatural form of exchange. I'd add a further wrinkle: since Aristotle treats money's value as resting on convention (nomos) rather than nature, I wonder whether he would have denied that something with no political community standing behind it even counts as nomisma. Drawing directly on his arguments about money in the Nicomachean Ethics and the Politics, work out as rigorously as you can what position Aristotle would most plausibly have taken, and tell me which of us has the stronger reading of him.
