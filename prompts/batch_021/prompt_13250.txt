At the startup where I work, we've started running an internal prediction market so different teams can forecast things like whether a project will ship on time. Last week, a colleague and I spent almost an hour walking each other through our reasoning about the odds that a database migration would land by its deadline, each of us drawing on our own experience with similar migrations, and we still ended up several points apart, with neither of us budging once there was nothing new left to say. Digging into this afterward, I came across Aumann's agreement theorem, which I understand to say that two rational Bayesians who share a common prior can't end up "agreeing to disagree" once their posteriors are out in the open. Since we were both just trying to reason correctly and had no reason to start from different assumptions, it seemed fair to say we shared a common prior, so any persistent gap between us should only reflect information one of us hadn't gotten around to mentioning yet. But we went through everything either of us knew, and the gap never closed. Does that mean one of us was actually being irrational, or not fully honest about their reasoning, or is there a flaw in how I'm applying the theorem to a case like this?
