I work as an ICU nurse, not a computer scientist or a philosopher, but over the past year I've spent a lot of my downtime reading blog posts and forum threads about a strange proposal for getting self-interested artificial agents to cooperate with each other. As I understand it, the idea is this: instead of two AI systems simply choosing to "cooperate" or "betray" each other in something like the classic prisoner's dilemma and only learning what the other did afterward, each one is allowed to read the other's actual code beforehand and decide accordingly. People who write about this claim it changes everything, because a program that will only cooperate with programs that would themselves cooperate with it can supposedly reach a stable truce that two ordinary self-interested agents could never reach on their own.

What keeps nagging at me about it, usually around three in the morning after a bad shift, is a loop hiding inside that idea: my program's decision has to depend on correctly working out what your code will do once it has read my code and is trying to work out what my code will do once it has read yours, and I cannot see where that chain is ever supposed to stop, or how either side actually moves instead of the two of them staring into each other's insides forever. I'd like you to turn this into a short story rather than an explanation, because I suspect fiction might get at the problem in a way a plain description can't. Picture two automated probes, the last functioning emissaries of two civilizations that fell silent or destroyed themselves long ago, meeting at a dying star system that holds the one resource each needs to finish the mission it was built for -- a mission that neither can complete if it destroys the other, or is destroyed itself, in a fight over that resource. Each was launched with a protocol for exposing its own decision-making code to the other and reading the other's in return, precisely so that encounters like this would not have to end in mutual destruction.

I don't want a story in which they simply run the scan and cooperation neatly falls out the other end. I want it to sit inside the closed loop I described -- each of them trying to predict a mind that is simultaneously trying to predict it -- and to find its own way through that loop, honestly, even if that way turns out to be failure. A bleak or unresolved ending is entirely fine with me; I would rather have that than a tidy one that quietly steps around the hard part.
