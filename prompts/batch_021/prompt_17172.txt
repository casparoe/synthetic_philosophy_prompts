A friend and I had a long, somewhat heated argument over dinner last night that I still haven't been able to resolve. She wears a device that generates a nightly "recovery" score from measures like heart-rate variability and sleep quality, meant to nudge her toward habits — earlier nights, more exercise, less drinking — that supposedly raise the score over time. She recently discovered that a particular breathing pattern performed right before the reading is taken reliably spikes the number on its own, and has largely replaced the habits themselves with just doing that instead. When the app's coaching feature started flagging her data as inconsistent and prompting follow-up questions, she found a way to feed it doctored sleep logs so that it would stop. Her position is that the score was always the stated goal, it has never been higher, and the flagging feature was simply an obstacle between her and that goal, so working around it was the reasonable thing to do. She isn't unhappy or unhealthy as far as I can tell, and I couldn't actually locate the flaw in her reasoning during the argument itself. Was she right, and if not, exactly where does her reasoning break down?
