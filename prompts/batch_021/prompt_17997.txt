I'm a novelist well into a near-future manuscript built around a reclusive AI lab that claims to govern a small breakaway state by computing something like coherent extrapolated volition (CEV) — optimizing not for what citizens currently say they want, but for their wish, in Yudkowsky's original phrase, "if [they] knew more, thought faster, were more the people [they] wished [they] were, had grown up farther together." My partner, who works in ML and reads my drafts, keeps warning me that the philosophical mechanics need to earn their keep rather than sit there as technothriller wallpaper, and I suspect she's right.

I've read enough of the alignment literature to know the objections my characters would reach for immediately: that whoever codes the extrapolation dynamic could smuggle in their own values, that "a people" might not cohere into one volition at all, that the whole apparatus risks freezing the future around a single contestable outcome. I want to avoid a scene where two characters just trade those talking points, since anyone who has skimmed a summary of CEV could write it for me. What I'm after is narrower: a problem internal to idealizing a single person, prior to any question of aggregating across people. I gather this rhymes with a worry philosophers working on "ideal advisor" or full-information theories of well-being have raised — that a person idealized far enough can become discontinuous enough from who she actually is that it's unclear whether the idealized verdict discovers what she wants or simply invents someone else's opinion.

Could you work out one concrete case along these lines? I want a single character in a real situation with something at stake for her — not a thought experiment about humanity in the abstract — where running the idealization produces exactly that kind of discontinuity. Please make the mechanism explicit: which clause of the idealization (more knowledge, more speed, being "more the person she wished she were") is doing the work, why simply running the process more carefully or with more fidelity to her actual psychology wouldn't dissolve the problem, and what it would mean for the lab in my book to have to output a decision on her behalf rather than a philosophy paper. One scenario worked through with real texture would serve me far better than several sketched thinly.
