Working on a piece about the philosophical roots of "post-truth," and pragmatism (Pierce, James, etc) keeps coming up as basically the origin of the idea that truth is just "whatever works" — i.e. if believing something helps you get through life, it counts as true for you. Since Pierce and James were both basically arguing for that same view, why do philosophers still make such a fuss about treating them as two seperate theories of truth instead of one pragmatist idea with two names slapped on it? I read that Pierce actually got annoyed enough at how his version was being used that he renamed it something uglier just to distance himself from it, but if they were saying the same thing that seems like a weirdly big overreaction. Trying to figure out how much pragmatism is actually to blame for the whole post-truth mess.
