My university's philosophy department is hosting a colloquium next month with a metaphysician who has built her reputation defending David Lewis's genuine modal realism: the view that absolutely every way a world could possibly have been is a way some other concrete, spatiotemporally and causally isolated universe actually is. I have sat in on two of her talks already, and on both occasions, when someone in the audience pressed on the sheer implausibility of the view, she deflected with some version of Lewis's own point that an incredulous stare records a fact about the audience rather than supplying a counterargument, then moved the discussion along before anyone could press further. I am moderating the Q&A this time and would like the questions to make that move harder to lean on.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions for the session, each with a short follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give? I would like at least one question on Kripke's complaint that counterpart theory changes the subject of ordinary de re claims about what could have happened to a particular person, rather than explaining them; one on whether her distinction between qualitative and quantitative parsimony is doing genuine philosophical work against the extravagance charge, or is simply redrawn wherever it is convenient to blunt each new version of it; and one on whether the Principle of Recombination can really secure a genuine plenitude of possible worlds — including worlds with fundamentally alien natural properties — without either smuggling in the very modal notions it is supposed to reduce, or resting on bare stipulation. Please end with a question asking what she would actually accept as evidence against genuine modal realism, something more substantive than another incredulous stare.
