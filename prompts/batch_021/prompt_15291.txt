In my twenties, I served as a soldier in a war that most people back home, myself included, now look back on as having had no real justification. During my time there I never mistreated a prisoner or targeted a civilian; every shot I fired was at enemy soldiers who were actively firing on my unit, exactly as I had been trained to do. Even so, years later I can't shake the feeling that I did something wrong simply by fighting on that side, regardless of how carefully I stuck to the rules I was given. When I've raised this with other veterans -- including some who fought in wars nobody seriously disputes were justified -- they tell me I'm being unfair to myself: a soldier's responsibility is to fight cleanly, not to personally settle whether the whole war was justified, and any soldier who fights by the rules stands on the same moral ground as any other, whichever side they were on. I understand the comfort in that, but it hasn't fully quieted the feeling. Were they right that how I fought is the only thing that matters morally, or is there something to my sense that being on the wrong side made what I did wrong, no matter how carefully I fought?
