Ok, this has been bugging me since it happened. I sit on the jury for a smallish science journalism prize, and this year we switched to ranked-choice voting because everyone agreed it'd be the fairest way to pick a winner among the five finalists. Afterward, one of the other judges admitted she didn't rank her actual favorite piece first — she put a solid, crowd-pleasing runner-up on top instead, since she figured her real favorite had no shot and didn't want her ballot to end up helping the entry she liked least actually win. That felt like exactly the kind of gaming ranked-choice voting is supposed to make pointless, and yet it clearly worked out fine for her anyway. I write about science and ideas for a living and still can't answer my own question here: is there some voting rule where being honest about your rankings is always your best strategy, no matter how many candidates their are, or is this kind of gaming just unavoidable once you've got three or more options on the ballot?
