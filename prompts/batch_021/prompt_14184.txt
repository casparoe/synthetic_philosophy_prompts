Okay, this is going to sound like a weirdly specific thing to still be chewing on, but a couple weekends ago I was at dinner with two friends from my partner's philosophy grad program, Theo and Priya, and they spent basically the whole meal arguing about philosophical zombies. I only ever took one intro phil class in undergrad, so I could follow along but I definately can't referee it myself, and neither of them will concede the other had a point, so I've been asked (by both of them, separately, which is very on brand) to get an outside opinion.

It started with Theo saying that since he can perfectly conceive of a creature physically identical to him down to the last neuron, but with nobody home inside, no experience at all, that shows consciousness can't just be a physical process — because if it were, the zombie would be inconceivable in the same way a married bachelor is. Priya said conceivability doesn't hand you possibility for free: she can "conceive," in some loose armchair sense, that water isn't H2O, but that's not actually possible, since if water=H2O is true at all it's necessarily true, so maybe he's making the exact same mistake, and he can only conceive of a zombie because he doesn't yet grasp that consciousness and some physical property are the very same thing described two different ways.

Theo's comeback was that Chalmers already deals with this using the two-dimensional semantics stuff: the water case only threatens his argument if you run together primary and secondary intension, since "water" could've picked out a different substance on Twin Earth, which is exactly why we're able to misjudge it — but phenomenal concepts, he said, don't work like that, because they fix onto experience directly by what it's like from the inside, not via some causal role a different substance could occupy instead, so the Kripke move just doesn't carry over. Priya wasn't having it: she said that's basically just declaring phenomenal concepts magically exempt from the whole apparatus, which is precisely what's under dispute — the "phenomenal concept strategy" people (Loar, Papineau, that crowd) argue phenomenal concepts refer to ordinary physical brain states too, just via a special introspective mode of presentation that doesn't reveal the physical nature underneath, which by itself explains why zombies seem conceivable even though they secretly aren't, no extra mystery required. Theo said it still just seems conceivable to him regardless, and Priya said that's exactly the disputed intuition again so it can't be what settles things. They kept going for another twenty minutes, into stuff like the "meta-problem of consciousness" and whether physicalism can even be stated coherently, but the four moves above are the real backbone of it and I'm probably flattening some nuance. Could you go through it move by move instead of just declaring an overall winner — Theo's opening move, Priya's reply, Theo's comeback, and Priya's response to that (T1 / P1 / T2 / P2 if that's easier to reference) — and say who's got the better of it at each one, plus flag anything you think they're both just missing entirely?
