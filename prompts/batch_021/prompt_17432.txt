And here's the answer, verbatim:

"Arrow's theorem (from his 1951 book Social Choice and Individual Values) says that when we're aggregating individual preference orderings into one social ordering, there's no social welfare function that can simultaneously satisfy Unrestricted Domain (it has to work for any possible set of individual orderings), the Pareto principle (if everyone prefers X to Y, so does society), Independence of Irrelevant Alternatives (society's ranking of X versus Y depends only on how individuals rank X versus Y, not on their views about some third option Z), and Non-Dictatorship (no single voter's preferences automatically determines society's ranking regardless of everyone else). This holds whenever there are three or more voters choosing among two or more alternatives - below that threshold the result doesn't apply.

This doesn't mean every voting system ever devised is secretly incoherent, or that democracy is a sham. It's a claim about a specific formal setup: converting individual rankings into a social ranking. With only two alternatives, majority rule is perfectly well-behaved and satisfies everything on the list, which is actually a separate, nice result in its own right. The bite comes specifically from the multi-alternative case, and it tells us that any procedure that meets the first three conditions must have a dictator, and any procedure with no dictator must break Unrestricted Domain, Pareto, or IIA somewhere.

Given this, I'd say scoring rules like the Borda count are a genuine way out. Borda doesn't just rank candidates, it assigns each one a numerical score based on position, then adds the scores up across voters. Because it's working with cardinal scores rather than a bare ranking, it isn't really the kind of function Arrow's proof is even about, so there's no contradiction in Borda satisfying Unanimity, Non-Dictatorship, and Independence all at once. That's actually a big part of why Borda counts and similar point-based systems have had a resurgence in voting-reform circles - they sidestep the impossibility result that dooms rank-only systems like plurality or IRV."
