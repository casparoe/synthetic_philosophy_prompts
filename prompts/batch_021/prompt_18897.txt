Write a dialogue between a Kantian moral philosopher and a philosopher defending Nomy Arpaly's account of moral worth about whether Huckleberry Finn's decision to help Jim, an enslaved man, escape to freedom has genuine moral worth. Center the exchange on the scene in which Huck tears up the letter that would have returned Jim to his owner and resolves, in the novel's famous phrase, to "go to hell" rather than turn him in — even though he sincerely believes, on the strength of the only moral education he has ever received, that helping Jim is a sin. Let the disagreement turn on whether moral worth can be grounded in an agent's emotional attunement to the reasons that actually matter, as Arpaly holds, or instead requires acting from the motive of duty, as the Kantian insists. Each side should engage directly with the other's strongest objections rather than simply restate its own position.
