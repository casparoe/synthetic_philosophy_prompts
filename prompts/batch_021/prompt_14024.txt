So I fell down a Wikipedia hole last night that started at "heat death of the universe" and ended at "Boltzmann brains" - the idea that in an old, high-entropy universe, random thermal fluctuations will occasionally just assemble a fully formed conscious brain out of the noise, complete with memories of a life it never lived. Apparently physicists take this seriously enough to use it as an actual argument against certain eternal-inflation cosmology models (if your model predicts these random brains outnumber real observers by basically an infinite ratio, that's a strike against the model), not just as a fun thought experiment.

What's really messing with my head is the statistical point that a random fluctuation is way more likely to spit out a brain with totally scrambled, nonsensical "memories" than one with a coherent life history - so if you actually are a Boltzmann brain, your memories (including whatever taught you physics and got you worried about this in the first place) are almost certainly garbage. The theory kind of eats it's own tail once you take it seriously.

It doesn't need to be hard-sci-fi accurate, but I want the narrator to genuinely confront that self-undermining logic - not just fear of being a brain in the void, but the specific vertigo of not being able to trust the reasoning that raised the possibility in the first place.
