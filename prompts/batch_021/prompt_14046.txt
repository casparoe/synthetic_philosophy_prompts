For my IB Philosophy internal assessment, I'm working with an excerpt from Bernard Williams's paper "Internal and External Reasons." I understand the basic contrast he draws: internal reasons are supposed to be anchored in the agent's own motivations, while external reasons are meant to hold regardless of what the agent happens to care about. What I'm struggling with is how this internalism is actually different from a straightforward Humean view on which reasons are always just about the most effective means to satisfying one's existing desires. Could you compare the two positions directly, addressing in particular: what Williams packs into the "subjective motivational set" beyond bare desires; how deliberation is supposed to revise that set rather than merely calculate means within it; and whether critics such as McDowell are right that these moves quietly let external reasons back in? In the end, is Williams's internalism a genuinely distinct position, or does it collapse into Humeanism after all? Please justify your answer.
