Here's the bit I can't work out on my own. Davidson's radical interpreter has to assign beliefs and meanings together, using charity as the wedge that gets interpretation off the ground at all - you assume the speaker is mostly right about the world and mostly rational, because there's no other foothold available. But then in the later triangulation stuff he gets much stricter: he says a creature can't have thoughts about objective, mind-independent objects at all unless there's a second creature responding to the same stimulus and to each other's responses - it's the triangle, not just one creature registering the world, that fixes what the response is even a response to. And seperately there's the "Nice Derangement of Epitaphs" line, where he argues successful communication never needed a shared prior "language" in the first place, just two people converging on a workable passing theory in the moment.

Given all three of those pieces, what do you think Davidson would actually say about whether an LLM could be radically interpreted in his sense at all? Does the triangulation requirement mean he'd deny it has beliefs or meaning full stop, on the grounds that it never triangulated with anyone and just absorbed a huge pile of already-interpreted text third hand? Or is there some way the human feedback used in training, or even just a single back-and-forth conversation, could count as a scaled-down triangulation or passing-theory-building in his sense? And does charity even survive contact with something that hallucinates fluently and contradicts itself within the same conversation - can you still assign it a body of mostly-true, mostly-consistent belief the way charity requires, or would he think the whole interpretive project misfires before it even starts?
