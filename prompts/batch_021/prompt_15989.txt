So a few weeks back I fell into a total wikipedia rabbit hole on Jeremy Bentham (the utilitarianism guy, "greatest happiness" and all that, also the dude who had his body preserved and put on display in a cabinet at some college in London lol) and found out something that never came up in any philosophy 101 video i've watched: back in like the 1780s he secretly wrote a whole essay defending homosexuality on strictly utilitarian grounds, basically arguing that the laws against it (which could get you executed at the time) were based on pure disgust and prejudice rather than any actual harm done to anyone. Apparently in his notes he went even further and applied that same "if it doesn't harm anyone its not wrong" logic to stuff people found way more disgusting back then too, like bestiality, just to stay logically consistent with his own principles. But the wild part is he never published any of it in his lifetime - he was way too scared of what it'd do to his reputation and to all his other reform projects, like the prison stuff and the poor laws.

This has honestly been bugging me since I read it. Kind of feels hypocritical for the founder of "do the most good even when its unpopular" to be the one guy who chickens out right when it's about himself.
