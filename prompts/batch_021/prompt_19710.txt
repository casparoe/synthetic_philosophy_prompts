I help moderate a mid-sized rationalist-adjacent forecasting-and-epistemics forum (something like a smaller cousin of LessWrong or the Metaculus discussion boards). We maintain a pinned "Community Consensus" wiki covering a handful of long-running empirical and methodological disputes that the userbase has argued out at exhaustive length and now treats as settled background. Over the past year, an increasing share of new threads have simply re-litigated these settled questions, often rehearsing arguments already addressed years earlier, and several moderators now want to adopt a formal rule: once an entry is tagged [Settled], any thread challenging it is automatically locked unless a moderator first certifies that it contains evidence or argument not already addressed in the consensus write-up. Proponents describe this as merely formalizing what good moderators already do informally. A few of us keep returning to the worry that the rule is structurally very close to Kripke's dogmatism paradox: we would be using our current confidence that P is settled to license screening out, in advance and without full examination, any future evidence against P -- exactly the move that argument shows knowledge (or something close to it) seems to license, despite this being an obviously poor policy for a community whose stated purpose is updating on evidence. I realize a community's provisional consensus is not quite the individual, factive knowledge state Kripke's argument concerns, and that this disanalogy may be doing real work, but the structural parallel still strikes me as more than superficial -- and I've seen people gesture at a related worry using Scott Alexander's "trapped priors" framing, though I am not sure whether that names the same phenomenon or merely a superficially similar one.

Could you draft the internal policy memo the moderation team would actually circulate on this question? It should come down clearly on whether to adopt, reject, or substantially modify the certification-gate proposal; specify concrete criteria a moderator could apply in the moment when judging whether a challenge thread clears the bar; and include a review cadence or sunset clause for revisiting [Settled] tags rather than treating them as permanent. Most importantly, I would like the memo to engage with the dogmatism-paradox worry on its merits, including taking a position on what, if anything, distinguishes legitimate triage of low-quality repetition from the kind of pre-emptive evidence-screening the paradox describes, rather than setting the worry aside as too abstract for a moderation policy to address.
