In my seminar on moral uncertainty and practical ethics this term, we discussed Toby Ord's concept of 'moral trade': the idea that people who disagree fundamentally, and perhaps irresolvably, about morality can still strike mutually beneficial bargains by each modifying their own behavior in a domain they are personally indifferent to but their counterpart cares about deeply — on analogy with how trading partners benefit from comparative advantage in economics.

Write a dialogue between two people negotiating exactly such a trade. Priya believes eating factory-farmed meat is a serious wrong but has no particular view on long-haul flying; Sam believes long-haul flying is a serious wrong, given its climate impact, but is indifferent to eating meat. Priya proposes the obvious deal: she cuts her flights, and he goes vegetarian. Sam, despite standing to gain from the arrangement, has genuine philosophical doubts about whether such trades are legitimate at all. Let Priya defend the trade and Sam press his doubts, with each responding directly to the other's strongest points, and let the dialogue end wherever it honestly leads rather than in a tidy resolution.
