Here are some of the main strategies people have proposed for getting a "reporter" to honestly translate what a predictor model latently knows, rather than just imitate what an unaided human watching the same observations would (possibly wrongly) conclude. I'm paraphrasing these from memory rather than quoting the report exactly, so go with the substance rather than hunting for exact wording matches:

- Careful case-by-case human investigation before labeling anything
- Cross-examination: penalizing inconsistent answers to logically related follow-up questions
- Penalizing answers about the same underlying fact that drift over time as new observations come in
- Regularizing toward whichever reporter is computationally cheapest/simplest, on the theory that honest translation should be less work than simulating a whole human belief system
- Amortizing one shared reporter across many different predictors so it can't just memorize one predictor's quirks
- Making the reporter answer from a compressed, structured ("Bayes net"-style) summary of the predictor's internal state rather than raw activations
- Adversarially searching for inputs where a human-simulator and a direct-translator would disagree, and prioritizing human labels for exactly those cases
- Amplification/debate-flavored training: reward the reporter for matching what a human would conclude given unlimited deliberation, not their off-the-cuff answer
- Skipping the reporter altogether and just using interpretability tools to try to read the fact straight off the network's internals
