For my History and Philosophy of Science course on the Scientific Revolution, we have been assigned Galileo's 1615 Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina (in the Finocchiaro translation), and I am stuck on a passage from the second half that our lecture did not really cover.

Everyone always quotes the line where Galileo repeats what he says he once heard from an eminent churchman, usually identified as Cardinal Baronius: that "the intention of the Holy Spirit is to teach us how to go to heaven, not how the heavens go." That part I follow well enough. What I cannot reconstruct is the more technical argument he makes further on, when he turns to the miracle in the book of Joshua where the sun stands still. As far as I can tell, he is not merely defending Copernicus against that verse but arguing that a strictly literal reading of the miracle actually sits better with the Copernican system than with the Ptolemaic one, building the case out of the distinction between the sun's own proper motion and the diurnal motion imparted by the primum mobile, together with something drawn from his sunspot observations about the sun's rotation.

I can follow each astronomical claim on its own, but I cannot see why he thinks it favors Copernicus specifically rather than being neutral between the two systems, or even a difficulty for Copernicus as well. Could you reconstruct the reasoning as he intends it, step by step, and tell me whether it actually holds up on its own terms?
