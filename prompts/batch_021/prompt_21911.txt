So this has been rattling around in my head since work rolled out an AI chat agent to handle our first-line customer support tickets last month (I'm a supervisor, so "babysitting the bot" is now part of my job). What's gotten to me isn't that it's dumb, its that it very often isn't. It doesn't just fire back canned lines — it seems to actually track whatever weird, specific complaint a customer throws at it and answer the substance of it, in a way I sometimes can't distinguish from my better human reps on a good day. I mentioned this to my sister, who's finishing a philosophy PhD, and she told me something I didn't know: Descartes didn't just wave his hands and say animals are mindless automata, he actually laid out two specific tests in the Discourse on Method for telling a real mind apart from a machine built to look and act like one. First, a machine (or animal) might spit out a word tied to some trigger, the way a trained parrot squawks a phrase, but it could never flexibly string language together to respond to the substance of whatever is said to it, the way even actual "idiots" (his word, not mine) manage fine. Second, even a very impressive machine would eventually fail at something, because reason is a "universal instrument" usable in any situation, while a machine needs a separate, purpose-built mechanism for every separate task.

Here's what I can't shake: that first test sounds uncomfortably like exactly the thing our chat agent is doing, and it's the very capacity Descartes uses to deny minds to the smartest parrot while granting them to the dumbest human. So, going strictly off those two tests as he actually stated them — not modern arguments about consciousness he never made, not vibes about "it's just predicting tokens" — what would Descartes conclude if you showed him a modern LLM? Would his own criteria logically force him to grant it something like reason, or is there a consistent way for him to deny that using the rest of his framework (the soul, the cogito, all of it)? I don't want a "well, it's complicated" answer here — if you had to predict Descartes' actual verdict and defend it using his own arguments, what would it be and why?
