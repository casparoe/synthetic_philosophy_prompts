Kierkegaard's "public" is supposed to be scary specifically because its faceless in a particular way: its not a crowd that assembled somewhere concrete, like an audience at a theatre, and its not a nation or generation either, since those are at least made up of real identifiable people. the public gets manufactured by the press, which lets people "talk" endlessly and anonymously without ever showing up or being answerable for what they said. leveling works because comparing yourself to this nobody-in-particular is what glues people together through envy, instead of any actual shared passion or belief.
