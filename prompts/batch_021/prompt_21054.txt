I'm a PhD candidate in economic history, and I sit on my department's dissertation-prospectus committee. The prospectus before us this term explains why sustained industrialization began first in Britain rather than in otherwise comparably advanced regions of continental Europe by reconstructing a single, contingent sequence of events specific to Britain, without proposing any general conditions whose presence or absence would also account for the ones that didn't industrialize. One committee member wants the candidate sent back to recast the argument around general conditions that could do the work of genuine covering laws; another insists that demanding law-like generalizations here is a category mistake, since the very thing to be explained is a unique, non-repeatable outcome, and that the narrative's own colligation of events is already explanatorily sufficient. Draft the short memo I need to circulate before we reconvene, giving our decision on the prospectus's explanatory strategy -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions -- along with reasons specific enough that the candidate could actually act on them, and a fair, non-dismissive treatment of whichever side's objection you don't ultimately adopt.
