ok so for a history-of-math project i went down a total rabbit hole reading about Russell's paradox and ended up at Frege's Grundgesetze der Arithmetik (the book where he tried to derive all of arithmetic from pure logic). one of his core axioms, "Basic Law V," basically says: the extension of a concept F is identical to the extension of a concept G if and only if F and G hold true of exactly the same objects. then in the appendix to volume 2, written right after Russell sent him a letter about it, Frege says something like: "Hardly anything more unfortunate can befall a scientific writer than to have one of the foundations of his edifice shaken after the work is finished. This was the position I was placed in by a letter of Mr Bertrand Russell, just when the printing of this volume was nearing its completion."

i get the basic idea of russells paradox (the set of all sets that dont contain themselves) but i dont really get the mechanics of how its specifically Law V that causes it — like what exact move do you make with that axiom that turns a weird self-referential concept into an actual contradiction instead of just a false statement? and honestly the thing that bugs me more is the bigger picture: if one of the most careful logicians ever built his whole system on an axiom that seemed totally self-evident and it blew up on him like that, how would we even know if something like ZFC (which modern math is supposedly built on) has a similar landmine buried in it that just hasn't been found yet?
