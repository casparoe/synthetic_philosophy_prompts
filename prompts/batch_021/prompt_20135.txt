My younger brother's getting married in about three weeks and he asked me to do the toast at the reception. Normally I'd just wing something sentimental, but here's the complication: he's a few years into a PhD in comparative philosophy and wrote his MA thesis on Confucian role ethics (the Roger Ames / Henry Rosemont Jr. reading of the Analects, not the fortune-cookie "Confucius say" stuff), and he has semi-jokingly banned me from the usual toast cliches about him and his fiancee being "two individuals" who found their "other half" or "soulmate." His point is that's exactly the atomistic, self-contained-person-who-then-enters-relationships picture that role ethics is built to reject, and he wants the toast to actually reflect the idea that people are constituted by their roles and relations rather than pre-existing them and choosing to relate afterward.

Here's the annoying part on top of that: he also doesn't want me leaning on the traditional Confucian framing of husband-wife as one of the five cardinal relationships (wu lun), since historically that one is hierarchical and gendered in a way that has nothing to do with how he and his fiancee actually want to live, and he'd be kind of mortified if it came out sounding like I was quoting Mencius to justify a 1950s marriage. So I'm stuck with this weird pair of constraints -- no individualist "soulmates" language, but also no traditional hierarchical framing -- while still supposed to use role ethics as the actual backbone of the toast and not just something I sprinkle on top for flavor. About half the room is his philosophy cohort, who will absolutely clock it if I fake my way through this, and the other half is our grandparents and cousins who've never heard of Roger Ames in their life and just want to feel something. Can you write me the toast? Something like 3-4 minutes read aloud is probably right.
