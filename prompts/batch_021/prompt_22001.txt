I've been trying to work through the logic of "revealed preference" on my own, and something about it bothers me. As I understand it, economists define preference entirely in terms of choice: to say that I prefer coffee to tea just means that I chose coffee when tea was also available to me, and a "utility function" is simply whatever assignment of numbers to outcomes matches that choice pattern. But if that's really the whole content of the idea, then "utility maximization" looks completely circular: whatever a person ends up doing automatically counts as maximizing their utility, so the claim could never be falsified by any possible behavior. Given that, why do economists and decision theorists still treat "maximizing utility" as a substantive, testable claim about behavior, or even as a meaningful ideal of rationality, rather than admitting that it's an empty tautology?
