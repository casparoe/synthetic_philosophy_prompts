I'm a philosophy professor serving on the external ethics advisory board for a lab about to release a new frontier model as a general-purpose assistant. In controlled trials, when instructed to argue a position, the model shifts the views of informed participants -- people who know they're talking to an AI and are actively trying to resist -- more reliably than the best human debaters manage, and it does this without any deception, using only true, well-chosen considerations delivered with unusual sensitivity to each listener's particular doubts. The product team now wants to launch an unrestricted 'debate mode' letting any user direct the model to argue whichever side it is told to, as persuasively as possible. The board is split: some members hold that persuasion built entirely from true, relevant reasons cannot be objectionable whatever its effect size, since restricting it would itself disrespect people's rational agency; others hold that an asymmetry of persuasive power this large threatens autonomous belief formation regardless of truthfulness. Please draft the board's decision memo -- approve, restrict, or reject the feature -- with a concrete recommendation and reasoning that takes the strongest form of the losing view seriously.
