I spent my working life as an experimental physicist, and it is only since retiring that I have had the leisure to read philosophy with any care. I am currently making slow progress through Pascal's Pensées, working from the French with an English crib beside it, and I have got stuck on the short fragment carrying the heading "Justice, force." As I have it in French:

"La justice sans la force est impuissante ; la force sans la justice est tyrannique. … Il faut donc mettre ensemble la justice et la force, et pour cela faire que ce qui est juste soit fort, ou que ce qui est fort soit juste. La justice est sujette à dispute ; la force est très reconnaissable et sans dispute. Aussi n'a-t-on pu donner la force à la justice … Et ainsi, ne pouvant faire que ce qui est juste fût fort, on a fait que ce qui est fort fût juste."

The crib I have renders this rather freely, so here is my own rough version instead: justice without force is powerless, force without justice is tyrannical, so the two must be joined by making the just strong or the strong just; but since justice is open to dispute and force is not, only the second of these has ever actually been achieved.

What I cannot settle for myself is what kind of statement that final sentence is. Two clauses earlier, Pascal seems to treat "make the just strong" and "make the strong just" as two equally acceptable ways of meeting the same requirement -- yet the outcome he then describes, force simply appropriating the name of justice, looks exactly like the "tyranny" he had just ruled out. Is he unmasking our justice as force in disguise, or does he think something real and minimally legitimate comes into being once force settles into unopposed custom? And does it matter here that he says elsewhere that the wise conceal from ordinary people the fact that law rests on establishment rather than on genuine equity -- does that context make this fragment's ending more cynical, or does it make the concealment itself the properly Pascalian response to a fallen world in which nothing better is on offer?
