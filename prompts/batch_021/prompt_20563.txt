I was an experimental physicist for about 35 years, and now that I'm retired I've been filling my time with philosophy books. Lately I've gone down a rabbit hole on revealed preference theory - the economists' idea that you can figure out what someone truly values just by watching what they choose, no need to ask them or peer inside their head, and then back out a 'utility function' that supposedly represents what they want. It reminds me alot of an identifiability problem from my old field: just like different underlying models can fit the exact same dataset equally well, pretty different sets of values (and so pretty different utility functions) could generate the exact same string of choices, especially once you factor in things like constraints, addiction, or preferences that have quietly adapted to whatever life handed someone. Could you write me a short story about a character - an AI, an alien anthropologist, whatever fits - who tries to reconstruct someone's (or some society's) real values purely from their behavior, and runs headlong into that problem? I don't want the twist to be some generic 'the AI just doesn't get humans' bit - I want the plot itself to turn on the logical point that the exact same choices could equally well be explained by quite different values underneath, so that no amount of watching from outside could ever settle which one was real.
