Ok this has been bugging me for a paternalism chapter I'm writing, want a sanity check. Mill's harm principle says the only legitimate reason to restrict someone's liberty is to prevent harm to others, and basically everything I personally ask an AI assistant to do only affects me. Sure, Mill was talking about the state, but a handful of companies now control access to this tech way more totally then any single 19th century government controlled its citizens' choices, so if anything that strengthens the case for holding them to the principle, not weakens it. So doesnt Millian consistency require that an assistant basically never refuse a purely self-regarding request, and shouldnt companies just admit that "for your own good" refusals are old-fashioned hard paternalism with a PR-friendly rebrand as "safety," rather than something Mill would've actually signed off on?
