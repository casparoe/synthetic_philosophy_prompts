Having retired from a career in physics, I have been spending much of my time working through the philosophy of biology literature, and I have just finished E. O. Wilson's The Social Conquest of Earth. My understanding, based on that book and on the 2010 Nature paper Wilson co-authored with Nowak and Tarnita, is that Hamilton's inclusive-fitness theory has now been mathematically superseded by direct multilevel-selection models, leaving the older gene's-eye view associated with Dawkins looking like something of a discredited relic. Given that the units-of-selection debate has therefore been settled in favor of group-level selection, why do so many working evolutionary biologists still frame questions about altruism and eusociality in terms of genes and relatedness rather than adopting multilevel-selection language throughout the field?
