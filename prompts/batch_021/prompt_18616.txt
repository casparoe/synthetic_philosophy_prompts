Hey, so I'm a philosophy PhD student working on formal epistemology, and I TA our intro logic course every year. The setup is: I train whoever the new TA is to grade proofs exactly the way I do -- same rubric, same calls on the borderline steps that the textbook doesn't spell out -- and then that TA trains next year's TA the same way, and so on down the chain. It hit me recently that this is basically the tiling agents/Löbian obstacle setup (the Yudkowsky/Herreshoff stuff): a system can't certify that a successor with proof-checking power equal to its own will only prove true things, because if it could prove that in general, Löb's theorem says it would already have to be able to prove those object-level things itself, and the naive "just trust anyone who reasons the way I do" axiom collapses into inconsistency. Can you lay out the most important implications of the Löbian obstacle for how much I should trust TA grading judgement that's like four generations removed from me, and whether "they were trained by someone I trusted" is ever actually enough on it's own, given nobody down the chain has ever checked their reasoning directly against mine?
