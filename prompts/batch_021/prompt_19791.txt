Random one for you -- I've been reporting on recidivism-risk algorithms (COMPAS being the famous one ProPublica did that big investigation on back in 2016) that courts use to predict whether a defendant will reoffend if released, and it struck me that this is basically a secular, statistical cousin of something Luis de Molina was doing back in the 1500s. His whole "middle knowledge" idea was that God knows, with total certainty, exactly what any possible free person would do in any possible circumstance -- so-called "counterfactuals of creaturely freedom" -- logically prior to even deciding which world to create. So if you dropped Molina into 2024 and showed him a tool like COMPAS, whats his most likely take? Would he see it as a crude attempt to approximate the same kind of counterfactual truths his scientia media assumed exist, or would something in his own framework -- like the grounding objection, the worry that these counterfactuals aren't grounded in anything prior to the agent's actual free choice -- give him reason to think no algorithm could ever really pull that off?
