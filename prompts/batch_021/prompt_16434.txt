Random thing that's been bugging me since a friend in consumer lending vented about it over drinks. Her team built a model that scores loan applicants for default risk, tuned so the scores mean the same thing across every demographic group -- a 20%-risk label really does mean a 20% default rate, whichever group your in. Seems like exactly what you'd want, except compliance noticed that among people who, it turns out, would NOT have defaulted, one group gets tagged high-risk way more often than the other, while among the ones who would've defaulted it skews the opposite way -- wrong about people in group-correlated ways despite meaning the same thing everywhere. Her boss wants both fixed, consistent scores AND equally-wrong-in-both-directions, and the data scientist keeps saying, essentially, pick one: you only get both if the groups default at the exact same underlying rate to begin with (they don't) or the model's flawless (it's not). Everyone in the room treats this as an engineering headache to route around, but it feels like it maps onto some debate I should remember from grad school and can't place -- like there's an actual philosophical knot under there, not just a bug. Am I onto something, and if so, which of these would you actually tell them to prioritize, and why?
