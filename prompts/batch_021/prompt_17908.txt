Random thing that's been bugging me since a long drive back from a conference a couple weeks ago, curious what you make of it. I left around 9pm, wired on conference coffee, and didn't get home till almost 2am. At 9 I was clearly fine to drive and clearly knew I was fine. By 1:30 I clearly wasn't fine, and knew that too, eventually pulling over for twenty minutes because I scared myself drifting toward the shoulder. Here's the part I can't shake, though: there's no particular minute along that drive where I "became" too tired to drive, and more than that, I don't think I was reliably able to tell, minute to minute, which side of that line I was on. A few times I did a little check-in with myself, "am I still okay to keep going?", decided yes, and kept driving. Looking back, I honestly can't say whether those check-ins were trustworthy, or whether I was already too far gone to correctly judge that I was too far gone.

I mentioned this to my partner, who has zero patience for this sort of thing, and she said something like: tiredness is just a vague, gradual property, there's no sharp cutoff, that's all that's going on, quit overthinking it. That's clearly part of the story, but it feels like it dodges the wierder bit, which is that even if I grant her the vagueness point entirely, there's a seperate and almost eerier question left over: whether I have reliable access to my own state from moment to moment at all, and whether, even on the occasions I happened to judge it correctly, I could have actually known that I'd judged it correctly, rather than just gotten lucky. I teach an epistemology seminar and this is exactly the kind of case I'd usually have a tidy answer ready for, but applied to my own dumb road trip it's got me stumped. What's actually going on here philosophically, is my partner's "it's just vagueness" response adequate, and if there's existing work that bears on this, does it actually dissolve the queasy feeling or just relabel it?
