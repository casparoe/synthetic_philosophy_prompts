ok so in my philosophy elective we read a chunk of G.E. Moore's Principia Ethica and it kicked off an argument between two people in my class, Farah and Devon, that never got resolved and its been bugging me since.

Moore has this thought experiment where you imagine two worlds, one as beautiful as you can possibly picture and one that's just a disgusting heap of garbage, and neither one has a single conscious being in it, ever, not even to glance at it once. He asks isn't it still true that the beautiful one is better, purely because it's beautiful, even though literally nobody will ever perceive either world? Farah said obviously yes, that's the whole point of imagining it totally isolated with nothing else around - if the intuition survives even once every possible observer is stripped out, that shows beauty just matters on its own. Devon said the intuition only feels that strong becuase when WE imagine the isolated world, we're sneaking ourselves in as a kind of observer of the thought experiment itself, so of course we'd rather the pretty one exist, that doesn't mean it'd actually be better with zero consciousness anywhere involved, ours included.

Farah's comeback was that the whole "isolating" move exists specifically to shut down that exact objection, you're told to picture it existing totally by itself, and the intuition survives even when people try it properly that way, so just calling it "contaminated" doesn't explain it away, it just re-asserts that it is. Devon replied that people getting the same intuition after "isolating" doesn't actually prove the isolating worked, people can convince themselves an exercise succeeded when it didn't.

Then Farah brought up the friendship part of the book, where Moore says loving someone for good qualities they genuinely have beats loving them for qualities they only seem to have, even if the lover feels the identical emotion either way, and said even Devon has to admit value depends on more than raw experience once he grants that. Devon said no, because in that case there's still a conscious person doing the loving, the only extra ingredient is that their belief has to be true, whereas Moore's beautiful-world case has zero consciousness in it anywhere, so it's a completely different kind of case that doesn't actually back her up.

They ended up just repeating themselves and asked me to settle it, which I honestly couldn't. Can you go through their exchange and say who's right at each step (not just who wins overall), and point out anything you think both of them are missing?
