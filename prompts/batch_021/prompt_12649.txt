At work we are in the middle of rewriting our engineering ladder, and I keep getting stuck on a question I now suspect is a genuine philosophical problem rather than just an HR headache: where exactly does "Senior" end and "Staff" begin? One faction on our leveling committee wants a checklist with sharp numeric thresholds (say, "has led at least three cross-team projects" or "five-plus years of relevant experience"), on the assumption that any lingering dispute just means the checklist isn't precise enough yet. The other faction thinks no checklist, however carefully written, will avoid arbitrary-feeling edge cases, and wants a purely holistic committee vote instead, on the assumption that "Staff-ness" has no sharp edges to begin with.

Trying to figure out who had the better instinct, I fell down a rabbit hole into the philosophy of vagueness and discovered there's an actual, long-running debate about exactly this kind of case, usually framed around Sorites-style predicates like "heap" or "tall." As I understand it -- I have no formal training in philosophy, so feel free to correct me -- epistemicists like Timothy Williamson hold that there really is a sharp fact about where the boundary falls (some exact project count, some exact year) that we simply can't ever know; supervaluationists like Kit Fine instead hold that the predicate is genuinely left unsettled among many equally admissible ways of making it precise, so that a given borderline case is neither determinately Senior nor determinately Staff, even though a claim like "she is one or the other" still comes out true overall. What I'd like from you isn't a verdict on which metaphysical picture is correct, but the most important practical implications of taking each view seriously in turn: what each position would actually tell my committee about whether a sharp cutoff is even the right kind of tool for this job, how to treat people who are sitting in the borderline zone when the new policy takes effect, and whether my two factions are really disagreeing about vagueness at all, or about something else entirely that's just wearing vagueness's clothes.
