So this has been on my mind since a conversation with my roommates last week. My younger sister (she's 22) found out about ADHD through TikTok about eight months ago — she started watching videos where creators list off symptoms, related a bunch of them to her own life, and eventually got herself formally evaluated and diagnosed. Fine so far, no complaints there. But since then I've noticed something that's hard to put into words: the more time she spends in these online communities, the more her self-description keeps shifting to match whatever the newest "symptom" content is. Six months ago it was all about disorganization and loosing track of time; now she's talking about "rejection sensitive dysphoria" and "time blindness," terms that weren't even in her vocabulary before, and she describes stuff she used to just chalk up to being a teenager as something she "always" had.

Part of me wants to just say she always had this and is finally getting the words for it, which seems healthy and good. But another part of me suspects the online community itself is shaping what even counts as a symptom in the first place, and her sense of herself is getting pulled along by that process, not just accurately described by it — like the category and her self-image are kind of evolving together in a loop, rather than there being one fixed pre-existing thing that the label just discovered. I don't want to be a jerk and tell her she's faking it, because it genuinely doesn't look like faking, but it also doesn't feel like neutral self-discovery either. Is there a good way of thinking about what's actually going on here, and does it point to anything useful about how I should talk to her about it?
