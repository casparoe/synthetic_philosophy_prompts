The question on the exam was: "State van Fraassen's Reflection Principle precisely, sketch the diachronic Dutch Book argument he gives for it, and explain one respect in which the Sleeping Beauty problem has been thought to put pressure on the principle."

Here's the student's answer, copied over exactly as written:

"Van Fraassen's Reflection Principle says that a rational agent's current credence in some proposition A, conditional on the supposition that her credence in A at some later time will be x, must itself equal x: Pnow(A | Pfuture(A) = x) = x. Informally, you should treat your own anticipated future opinions the way you'd treat the opinion of an expert you fully trust - if you already know Future-You will believe A with confidence 0.7, Now-You should believe it with confidence 0.7 too.

Van Fraassen defends this with a diachronic Dutch Book argument, which differs from the usual synchronic Dutch book arguments for the probability axioms, since those only need a single moment in time to construct the exploitative bets. Here a bookie offers you a bet now, plus a second bet later that's conditional on what your credence turns out to be at that later time; if your current credence and your anticipated future credence for the same proposition ever come apart, the bookie can set the odds so that you lose money no matter how things turn out, just because your own opinions shift in a way you already saw coming. Since this is basically just what Bayesian conditionalization already guarantees - updating on new evidence in a way that stays consistent over time - Reflection is best understood as a restatement of conditionalization: an agent conditionalizes correctly if and only if she satisfies Reflection.

The principle runs into trouble in the Sleeping Beauty case. Beauty is put to sleep, a fair coin is tossed, and depending on the result she's woken up once or twice with no memory of any earlier waking, then asked her credence that the coin landed heads. Her credence before the experiment starts is 1/2. Lewis's well-known answer is that on waking she should shift to 1/3, since there are three equally likely waking-scenarios (heads-Monday, tails-Monday, tails-Tuesday) and only one of them is heads, while Elga defended the more conservative view that she's gained no genuinely new information and so should stay at 1/2. If Beauty can already foresee, before she's even put to sleep, that she'll become a thirder on waking, Reflection seems to say she should be a thirder beforehand too - which seems wrong, since beforehand it's just a fair coin and 1/2 seems obviously right. This is one of the standard worries about applying Reflection to cases involving loss of self-locating information."
