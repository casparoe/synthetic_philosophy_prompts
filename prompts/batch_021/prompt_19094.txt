Okay so in my "Ethics of War" seminar we just spent two weeks on Anscombe and I'm still kind of chewing on it. We read "Mr. Truman's Degree," where she goes off on Oxford for wanting to give Truman an honorary degree - basically calling him a mass murderer over Hiroshima and Nagasaki - and paired it with bits of "Modern Moral Philosophy" where she coins "consequentialism" as basically an insult. My prof kept hammering on how her whole case against Truman hinges on one very specific move: the bombings don't get to hide behind double effect, because the civilian deaths weren't some foreseen-but-unintended side effect of hitting a military target, they WERE the mechanism - you kill enough people that the population's will to keep fighting collapses. So on her view its just straightforwardly murder, and it doesn't matter one bit that it maybe shortened the war and saved lives on net overall, because that's exactly the "ends justify the means" reasoning she thought was corrupting moral philosophy.

anyway, she died in early 2001, basically right before the war on terror kicked off, so she never got to weigh in on any of the stuff that came directly out of it. I'm curious what your best reconstruction of her view would be on two things specifically: (1) the drone/targeted-killing program, especially "signature strikes," where the military hits people based on patterns of behavior without actually confirming who they are, and (2) proposals for fully autonomous weapons where an algorithm, not a person, selects and fires on the target. Would she treat signature strikes as basically a mini-Hiroshima (targeting a pattern/population instead of a known, culpable individual), and would she say moral responsibility just shifts entirely onto the programmers and commanders in the autonomous weapons case, or does something in her framework just break down there?
