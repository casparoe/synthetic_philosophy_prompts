My father smoked roughly a pack a day for almost thirty years before quitting for good in his forties. Since I took up vaping last year, he has become newly and vocally concerned about nicotine addiction, delivering warnings about dependency and lung damage that he never once heeded during his own three decades as a smoker. This has led me to the philosophical literature on standing to blame, where the usual view seems to be that hypocrisy consists in a live, present inconsistency between what one condemns and what one does — which would imply that my father's standing was fully restored the moment he put out his last cigarette. That strikes me as too quick. Please argue for the position that sincere, long-standing reform diminishes but never fully restores the standing forfeited by past wrongdoing, such that my father's history as a smoker permanently limits his standing to blame me for vaping, regardless of how long ago he quit.
