A friend of mine, who has never studied any philosophy, recently described a dilemma to me that I haven't been able to stop thinking about. She has inherited the family hardware store and must choose between two buyers: a private equity firm offering considerably more money but known for gutting the companies it acquires and laying off longtime employees, and a local buyer offering less who would likely keep the store running much as it is. Overwhelmed, she asked her uncle — whose moral judgment she trusts far more than her own — simply to tell her which sale would be the right one, and said she would act on his verdict alone, without needing to hear his reasons. She mentioned, almost in passing, that she would feel no hesitation asking that same uncle, a retired accountant, to just tell her which offer was financially sounder, without walking her through the calculations. But outsourcing the moral question this way strikes her as different, even faintly troubling, and she can't say why. Is she onto something real, or is she just being morally squeamish for no good reason? I'd like to have something better than a shrug ready for her.
