I spent 35 years running an experimental physics lab before I retired, and lately I've been filling my evenings reading medieval philosophy, wich is new territory for me. I just finished the seventeenth discussion in Al-Ghazali's "Incoherence of the Philosophers," where he argues that fire doesn't necessarily burn cotton -- all we ever actually observe, he says, is one thing following another out of habit, never a real necessary connection. A footnote in my edition even pointed out that he calls this regularity "custom," which is literally the word Hume reaches for too. So as far as I can tell this is just Hume's constant-conjunction argument, practically verbatim, except Ghazali got there five or six hundred years earlier, and the only reason intro philosophy courses don't teach it that way is Eurocentrism in how the canon got carved up. Am I missing some real difference between the two arguments, or is this basically the same move just wearing different religous clothes?
