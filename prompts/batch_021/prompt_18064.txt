So I fell down a rabbit hole last week that started with someone on Hacker News making a joke about "Kripkenstein" in a thread that was actually about programming language semantics, and ended with me reading way more Wittgenstein than I expected to on a Tuesday night. I think I've got the headline private language argument okay -- the diary case, where a guy invents a sign "S" for some private sensation and writes it down whenever he thinks he feels it again, and Wittgenstein's point is that there's no real difference between him using "S" correctly and him just thinking he's using it correctly, so the whole "definition" never actually gets off the ground. Fine, nobody defends that guy.

What's got me stuck is the next layer of arguement, between people who all agree the diarist is toast but disagree about what the argument actually proves. Some of them (this is apparently closer to what Kripke does with it, and I think the label for this is the "community view") seem to think it shows that following a rule at all -- any rule, not just a private sensation-word -- requires an actual community around you that can catch you if you screw up, otherwise "whatever seems right to you is right" and the distinction collapses just like it did for the diarist. Other people (I think this is more the Baker & Hacker side, could be misremembering) think that's too strong, and that all you need is an established technique/practice with its own built-in standard of correctness -- so a Robinson Crusoe type, who grew up in a normal community, learned language and habits like everyone else, and then got shipwrecked totally alone for the rest of his life (no Friday, no rescue, nobody), could still genuinely follow a rule by himself. Like if he notches a stick every morning or always tallies his sheep the same way, there's a real fact about whether he does it right or wrong on any given day, purely because of the custom he's already got going, even with zero possibility of anyone ever checking him again.

Could you write me a dialogue between two people holding those two positions, arguing it out over Crusoe and his stick/sheep-tally specifically? I want real back-and-forth: the "you need a community" person has to actually engage with the obvious reply ("but Crusoe can clearly make a mistake and notice it later, isn't that enough?"), and the "practice is enough" person has to actually engage with the obvious worry on the other side (how would Crusoe himself ever notice if his sense of "same as before" just quietly drifted over 20 years alone with zero outside check -- kind of like a git branch that's never been pushed anywhere, so there's no way to tell if it's still "the same" project or has silently diverged). Neither one should be a pushover for the other to knock down -- I want two people who've clearly thought hard about this, actually landing blows on each other's specific points instead of just restating their own side louder. Doesnt have to be super long, maybe a page or so of back and forth, just want it to feel like a real argument between equals.
