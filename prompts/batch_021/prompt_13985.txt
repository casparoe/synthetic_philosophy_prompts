So my friend Dana talked me into a float tank session with her last weekend — total darkness, soundproofed room, water at skin temperature and so salted up that you just float there with zero effort. She's been on a whole meditation-and-consciousness kick lately. About twenty minutes in you genuinely lose track of where your skin ends and the water begins, theres no light, no sound, nothing to feel your way around with — it's supposed to be about as close to total sensory shutdown as you can get outside a lab.

She would not let it go over dinner afterward. Her argument was basically: the entire time in there she had zero sensory information coming in, no real sense of her own body at all, and she was still completely certain that she existed — still "her," still thinking, still aware. She says that proves the self isn't coming from the body, because if it were, wiping out all the bodily/sensory awareness should have wiped out the self-awareness too, and it obviously didn't. So to her this is basically evidence for a soul, or at least that the mind is something over and above the brain, and plain materialism can't be the whole story. I'm not religious and generally roll my eyes at this kind of stuff, so I pushed back that her brain was clearly still humming along the entire time whether she noticed it or not — and she just said "sure, but I wasn't aware of my brain doing anything, and I was still aware of myself, so how can those be the same thing?" I didn't have a good comeback.

It's been bugging me all week — I've got a strong hunch her argument is sneaking past something important, but I can't pin down exactly what. Does an experience like that actually tell you anything about whether a mind could exist independent of a body, or is there a real gap between what happened in that tank and what she's concluding from it? If there's a gap, I'd love for you to spell out exactly where the inference breaks down, not just whether you buy the conclusion.
