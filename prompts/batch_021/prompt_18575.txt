Two of my former PhD students -- who actually met in a reading group on Russell's ethics I ran back when they were in the program -- are getting married next month, and they've asked me to give the toast. Here's the wrinkle: both of them wrote dissertation chapters that lean on Marriage and Morals, and they've told me straight up that they don't want the safe "here's a cute story about how they met" toast, they want me to actually engage with what Russell argues in that book -- his case that jealousy is way less a fixed law of nature than people assume and gets inflamed mostly by ideas of ownership, his claim that the real backbone of marriage as an institution is children rather then sexual exclusivity, his impatience with conventional guilt about sex. Which is great fun to talk about in a seminar room, except this is a wedding, and maybe a third of the guests are philosophers who will clock exactly what im doing, while the rest are just the bride's aunts and the groom's college roommates, who are expecting something sweet and are going to be very confused by "trial marriage."

Could you draft me that toast? Three to five minutes, spoken out loud, not read off a page. I want it to take Russell's actual views seriously enough that the philosophers in the room recognize the real book and not a strawman of "free love guy says relax," but still land as a warm, funny thing to say about two people who love each other, not as a lecture. Extra credit if you can find a tactful way to gesture at the fact that Russell's own love life -- four marriages, one arrangement that was supposed to be enlightened and mostly just made everybody miserable -- was not exactly a triumphant proof of concept for the theory, without it turning into a joke at the couple's expense.
