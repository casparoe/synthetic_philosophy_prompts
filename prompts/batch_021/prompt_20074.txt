I'm a cognitive science PhD student working on the epistemology of mechanistic interpretability, and one issue keeps resurfacing in my work. Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) are typically motivated as a way of undoing superposition and recovering a network's genuine underlying features, yet increasing the dictionary size reliably causes coarse features to split into progressively finer ones, with several dictionary sizes achieving comparably low reconstruction loss and no principled criterion for treating any single decomposition as the correct one. Could you lay out what would actually have to be true for SAE-derived features to count as real components of a network's representational structure, rather than useful but somewhat arbitrary re-descriptions? In particular, does a Dennett-style "real patterns" criterion, on which something counts as real if treating it as a pattern yields genuine compression and predictive leverage unavailable at a lower level of description, actually dissolve this underdetermination, or does it merely relocate the dispute into a question of whose compression is the genuine one?
