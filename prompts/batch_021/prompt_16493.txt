Okay, this one's a bit different from what I usually ask you about. I write crime novels -- small press, nothing you'd have heard of -- and a couple years back I did jury duty on an assault case that hung 11-1, which is more or less the reason my third book exists. Somehow that turned into the presiding judge asking me to fill the "concerned citizen" seat on our county's Jury System Study Committee, alongside a retired judge, two defense attorneys, and Warren, a retired actuary who has clearly been waiting his whole life for a committee like this. We meet again Tuesday, and Warren's proposal is that we recommend the state legislature let non-capital felony trials use 8-person juries deciding by a 6-of-8 vote instead of our current 12-person unanimous rule. His whole case rests on Condorcet's jury theorem: his spreadsheet shows that even if each juror is independently right only 65% of the time on their own, a majority of 8 people is already more reliable than any individual juror, and that requiring 6 of the 8 to agree keeps us well protected against wrongful convictions while still resolving a lot more cases than our current rule does, which mostly just produces hung juries and retrials.

I think Warren's arithmetic is basically fine on it's own terms, but something about running an eighteenth-century voting theorem on actual people who sit in a room and argue with each other for two days feels off, and I can't get past "feels off" into a real argument -- which is a problem, since Warren will absolutely ask me to show my work. I have to circulate a short written opinion before the meeting recommending whether the committee should endorse his proposal, endorse a modified version of it, or send it back for further study (and if it's the last one, what exactly we'd want to know before deciding). Could you draft that memo for me? Take Warren's use of the theorem seriously instead of waving it away, get into the specific ways real jury decision-making does and doesn't fit what the theorem assumes, and land on one concrete recommendation rather than a "more research needed" shrug. I'm also on deadline for book three and have no business spending my afternoon down this rabbit hole, but here we are.
