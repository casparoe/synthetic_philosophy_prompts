I've been rereading the animal-machine bit in Descartes' Discourse on the Method (part V) for a class, and it's got me stuck on something about LLMs. Descartes doesn't just say "animals have no soul, therefore no mind" as a throwaway assumption — he actually gives two fairly concrete tests for whether there's a genuine mind behind some behavior or just mechanism: (1) can it use language flexibly, arranging words to respond appropriately to whatever is said to it, rather than producing fixed responses to fixed triggers (like a parrot, or a machine that says a set phrase when you press a spot on it), and (2) is its competence general, the way reason is supposed to be a universal tool usable in any situation, or does it only work in one narrow domain and fall apart outside it (which is basically his explanation for why an animal can sometimes outperform us at some specific task without that showing real intelligence). He was extremely confident no machine could ever pass either test, and that's basically his whole argument for why animals, no matter how cleverly they behave in their little niches, are just clockwork with nobody home.

So here's what I keep getting stuck on: modern LLMs at least look like they pass test (1) pretty easily — they respond fluidly to more or less anything you say to them — and they kind of gesture at test (2) too, since the same model writes code, argues philosophy, and gives (bad) medical advice without needing different hardware for each. Given how load-bearing those two tests were for his whole framework, what do you think Descartes would actually say if you showed him a transcript of a modern LLM conversation? Would he treat this as a genuine problem for his view, or is there a principled Cartesian move available — like pointing at hallucinations or weird inconsistency as exactly the "falls apart outside the narrow case" failure that test (2) predicts — that lets him keep the whole animals/machines-have-no-minds picture intact? Im less interested in the generic "would Descartes think chatgpt is conscious" take everyone already has a hot take on, and more in whether his specific, fairly precise arguments actually survive contact with something he obviously never anticipated.
