I've never studied philosophy or biology formally, but I've been mulling over a question on my own for quite some time: what do we actually mean when we say an organ or trait has a "function"? The more examples I turn over, the more two quite different answers seem to pull against each other. On one, a trait's function is whatever effect explains why it was favored by natural selection and has persisted in the population; on the other, a trait's function is just whatever it currently contributes to some capacity of the larger system it's part of, regardless of how it got there. I think these go by names like "etiological" and "causal-role" accounts, though I might be misremembering. Could you write a dialogue between a committed defender of each view, in which they test one another with hard cases — a malfunctioning organ that no longer does what it "should," a vestigial trait that seems to contribute nothing anymore, and a freak organ that assembles by sheer chance and so has no evolutionary history at all? Each side should answer the other's objections directly rather than simply restating its own position.
