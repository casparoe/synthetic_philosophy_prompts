I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and I sit on the small internal panel that has to sign off before we run anything built on a big scientific claim. A freelancer just pitched us a profile of an engineer whose all-analog machine -- continuous voltages, no bits -- allegedly computes a function no Turing machine can, and hes calling it a working "hypercomputer" that actually breaks the Church-Turing thesis, not just outrunning classical computers the way a quantum computer would. Half the panel calls this a category error dressed up as news, since the thesis is more like a mathematical convention then an empirical claim that any experiment could confirm or refute; the other half says that's an unfalsifiable-by-fiat dodge that's kept real hypercomputation research from getting a fair hearing here. Write me the memo I'll bring to Thursday's meeting recommending whether we run the piece, hold it for outside verification, or kill it -- with reasoning the freelancer and the skeptics on the panel could both actually respond to, not just an "extraordinary claims" shrug.
