Two people on my school's debate team got into an argument after practice that I still can't settle in my head.

Theo started it by saying we're too quick to write things off as "conspiracy theories": some of the biggest ones in history -- Watergate, the tobacco industry burying its own research linking smoking to cancer, the CIA's MKULTRA program -- were mocked as paranoid nonsense right up until documents and whistleblowers confirmed them. So the label itself, he argued, carries no information about truth; it just means "a claim about secret coordinated wrongdoing," which is sometimes real.

Nina didn't dispute the examples, but said what should actually worry us isn't the word "conspiracy" -- it's a specific move, where a theory never says what evidence would refute it and instead treats even an absence of evidence as further proof of a cover-up. That pattern of unfalsifiability, not the conspiracy claim itself, is what deserves the discount.

Theo replied that this is a hindsight trick: real cover-ups also look exactly like "no evidence" right up until the memo surfaces, so the test can't actually be applied in real time -- and besides, confident debunkers make the same move in reverse, waving away every anomaly as coincidence without ever saying what would change their minds either.

Nina's closing move was about scale: she said this can be made rigorous with basic probability, since even a small, independent chance that any one insider leaks each year compounds quickly once a theory requires thousands of people to stay silent for years -- so sprawling conspiracies deserve more default skepticism than small ones for structural reasons alone.

Practice ended before Theo could respond. Could you go through this move by move and say who has the better of it at each stage, rather than just naming an overall winner? I'm especially curious whether Nina's unfalsifiability test actually survives Theo's hindsight objection, whether her closing probability argument is as strong as she thinks it is, and whether there's a consideration neither of them raised that should really be doing the work here.
