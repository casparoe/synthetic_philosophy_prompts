In a graduate seminar on collective decision-making, we have been working through the literature on the discursive dilemma, starting from Kornhauser and Sager's original legal illustration: a three-judge panel must decide whether a defendant is liable for breach of contract, where liability requires both that a valid contract existed and that its terms were breached. Suppose two judges find that a contract existed but disagree about whether it was breached, while the third denies that a contract existed at all but would have counted the conduct in question as a breach had there been one. Voting issue-by-issue, a majority affirms both premises, so the rule "liable if and only if contract and breach" yields a verdict of liable; yet a bare majority of the same three judges (the two who reject liability on their own premises) would have voted against liability had they simply been asked for the bottom-line verdict. List and Pettit generalized this clash between premise-based and conclusion-based procedures into a formal impossibility theorem, often described as a cousin of Arrow's theorem for judgments rather than preferences: no aggregation rule can simultaneously satisfy universal domain, anonymity, and systematicity while guaranteeing a logically consistent collective verdict.

I would like to check an inference several of us in the seminar have been drawing from this result. If no procedure can guarantee consistency, doesn't that mean any panel of three or more judges who vote separately on the premises of a legal rule is bound sooner or later to hand down an incoherent verdict, in much the same way Arrow's theorem guarantees that majority rule over three or more options will eventually cycle? We have accordingly concluded that real multi-member courts must be at constant risk of self-contradictory rulings whenever premises are voted on separately, and that the only genuine remedy is to abandon premise-based voting altogether in favor of a direct, conclusion-based vote on the verdict itself. Is that really the lesson to draw from the List-Pettit result, or have we misunderstood what the impossibility theorem does and does not establish?
