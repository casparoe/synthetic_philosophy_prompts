Ok so I'm on my state's newly-formed sentencing reform commission (we advise the legislature on sentencing guidelines), and next month's meeting has gotten kind of heated. One commissioner's been on a Galen Strawson kick -- not the boring determinism-vs-free-will stuff, but his "Basic Argument": you can never be truly, retrospectively blameworthy for anything, because that requires being the ultimate author of your own character, and that regress bottoms out somewhere you didn't choose (genes, upbringing, or just luck if the universe's indeterministic). She wants us to formally drop "just deserts" language from the guidelines for something like Pereboom's quarantine model -- we'd still incapacitate dangerous people, but purely as prevention, like isolating someone with a contagious disease, not because they "deserve" it. Half the room calls this a cute seminar-room argument with no business in a policy document; the other half says if you steelman it, you also have to stop giving judges and cops merit-based praise for anything, which seems like a way bigger deal than anyone's admitting. Could you draft the short memo I'd circulate to the full commision beforehand -- a concrete recommendation on whether/how to change the guidelines' language, with a rough timeframe, that actually engages the "proves too much" worry instead of just waving it away?
