Write a dialogue between two members of a research team designing a self-modifying reasoning system, set at the whiteboard on the night before a successor version is due to be switched on. Because of Löb's theorem, their system cannot prove, for an arbitrary claim P, that its own proof of P guarantees P's truth, and so cannot in general certify that a successor running on the same axioms is trustworthy. One researcher wants to handle this the way the original tiling-agents proposals did: arrange proof systems into a hierarchy of strictly decreasing strength, so that each version can certify only a successor that is provably weaker than itself, never an exact copy. The other wants to give up provability as the currency of trust altogether and rely instead on a logical-induction-style calibrated credence, which can be extended to an exact copy of the system without running into the same obstruction, but only in a probabilistic rather than a certain sense. Have them argue it out, each pressing on the weakest point in the other's fix, over whether either proposal actually overcomes the Löbian obstacle or merely relocates it.
