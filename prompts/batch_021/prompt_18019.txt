So I had this argument with my friend Theo on the drive back from a wedding on saturday and it's been bugging me since. Theo's doing research adjacent to AI safety stuff and got me into this corner of the internet a while back, and we ended up spiralling into the "commitment races problem" - i think that's originally a Kokotajlo post on LessWrong or the Alignment Forum, could be misremembering who coined it. Anyway the basic setup: in bargaining, whoever commits first and hardest to a demand tends to win, Schelling's whole burn-the-bridge/throw-the-steering-wheel-out-the-window logic, but if both sides are actually capable of making commitments the other can verify somehow (source code, proof-based contracts, whatever), you get situations where neither side can back down without violating a locked-in commitment, and it turns into two people in a game of chicken who already tossed their steering wheels - like both sides building the Dr Strangelove doomsday machine at the same time.

Theo's take was that updateless decision theory basically defuses this: if an agent is updateless, it commits once, calmly, to whatever policy it would've wanted to lock in "from behind the veil," before it even knows who it's bargaining with, so there's no panicked scramble to commit fastest and hardest before the other guy does - the commitment was already the calm, considered, ex-ante-optimal one. I argued this is basically backwards: picking "the policy I'd have wanted to commit to from the start" doesn't make the race disappear, it just moves it into logical time, because you still need some procedure for picking which prior, or which policy, gets locked in, and that choice is itself a bargaining problem between all your possible logical counterparts. So updatelessness doesn't dissolve the commitment race, it just relabels one round of it as "rational" and pushes it somewhere less visible - if anything that seems worse, since it removes whatever friction or hesitation used to slow agents down before committing to something irreversible.

We went around in circles on this for like 40 minutes and neither of us gave in. Theo's strongest comeback was that I'm equivocating between two totally different kinds of commitment - an ex-ante one chosen behind a fair-ish veil over possible bargaining partners, versus an ex-post one chosen under an actual live threat - and that treating those as "the same race" is a category error, since only the second one has the panicky, self-destructive character that made the original problem scary in the first place. I don't think i have a great comeback to that one, honestly. Can you build out the strongest possible version of my side here - that adopting updateless/ex-ante-style commitment does not solve the commitment races problem and probably makes the underlying risk worse rather than better - and actually deal with Theo's ex-ante/ex-post distinction instead of just talking past it?
