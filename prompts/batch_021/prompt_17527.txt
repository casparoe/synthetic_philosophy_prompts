Since retiring from a career in theoretical physics, I have taken up philosophy in earnest, and a recent argument with a former postdoc of mine has stuck with me. She works on models of cosmic inflation and favors a version on which our universe is enormously larger, and so contains vastly more observers, than a more modest rival model that fits the data equally well so far. Her grounds for this preference involve no new observations: she argues that our mere existence should count as strong evidence for whichever theory predicts the most observers like us, effectively settling the question before any decisive observation is even made. It seems wrong to me that reflecting on our own existence could do the work of a telescope, yet I cannot pinpoint what, if anything, is wrong with her reasoning. Is she entitled to treat our existence as evidence in this way, or is there an illegitimate step hidden in the argument?
