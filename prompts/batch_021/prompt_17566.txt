Quick one — I've been down a Kepler rabbit hole for a phil of science seminar on demarcation and pseudoscience, and I want an actual argument out of you, not a summary. Kepler is genuinely scathing about the traditional astrology of his day (the zodiac signs, the houses, planetary rulerships), dismissing it as arbitrary numerology with no basis in physical reality — he's even got that crack about astrology being the foolish, bread-winning daughter of her wise but impoverished mother, astronomy. And yet in Tertius Interveniens, and later in the Harmonices Mundi, he holds onto his own reformed doctrine of aspects: certain planetary angles are supposed to be genuinely, harmonically effective on the soul, and they're priveleged over others because they correspond to regular polygons constructible by straightedge and compass. Argue that this "reform" doesn't actually escape his own objections to traditional astrology — that treating constructibility as the mark of a real harmonic relation is just as arbitrary, by his own stated lights, as the zodiacal apparatus he trashes. Don't just assert the parallel, actually engage with the specific reasons he gives for rejecting the old astrology and show where they'd bite his own theory too.
