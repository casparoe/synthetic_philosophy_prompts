Long-time lurker here, mostly on the rationalist-diaspora side of things, and I've got a real (not hypothetical, for once) situation that's been bugging me more than it probably should. Backstory: I bought a used pickup truck three years ago. Last month my mechanic was replacing some wiring under the dash and found a hidden aftermarket ignition interlock — like the court-ordered breathalyzer-to-start-your-car devices repeat DUI offenders get, except nobody ordered this one. Turns out the previous owner had a couple of DUIs, and a family member had quietly wired one in themselves, so the truck literally wont start if it detects alcohol on your breath past a certain threshold. Nobody disclosed any of this when I bought the truck, and I had genuinely no idea it was there for the entire three years I've owned it — the mechanic confirmed it's been fully wired and working the whole time, so this isn't a case where it was secretly broken and would've let me through anyway. And here's the thing: I have never once driven that truck after drinking. Not tipsy, not "probably fine," never — not because I was white-knuckling it against temptation, I just don't drink and drive, same as with every other car I've ever owned, it's not even a live question for me most nights out.

When I told this story at a barbecue, my brother-in-law said it actually doesn't reflect well on me at all — or rather, it doesn't reflect anything, because for three years I literally could not have driven that truck drunk even if I'd wanted to, so my clean record with it is worthless as evidence about my character. I said that seems backwards, since the interlock had nothing to do with why I never drove drunk — I didn't know it existed, wasn't relying on it, and would've made the exact same choice in a truck with no such device. He said intentions and counterfactuals are irrelevant, only what was physically possible matters, and since it wasn't possible for me to do otherwise, theres no credit due, full stop. Our group chat (which contains at least three people who will die on absolutely any philosophical hill given the slightest opening) has been going back and forth on this for a week with no resolution. Who's actually got the better of this argument, him or me, and is there a principled way to settle it instead of just trading intuitions?
