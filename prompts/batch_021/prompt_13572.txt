I'm teaching a philosophy of religion seminar this term, and we just spent two weeks on Molina and the whole scientia media apparatus. My students can recite the tripartite structure back to me now -- natural knowledge, middle knowledge, free knowledge -- but I don't think they're actually grappling with how strange the middle bit is. I keep telling them its not just "God knows what will happen," its the much weirder specific claim that, logically prior to any decree to create anything at all, God already knows, for every possible free creature in every possible circumstance it could be placed in, the one true counterfactual of what that creature would freely choose -- and that this truth holds independent of God's will, with nothing (per the grounding objection) obviously around yet to make it true, since no actual world has been chosen. Most intro-level treatments just flatten this into generic foreknowledge-vs-free-will and skip the genuinely weird part, which drives me up the wall a little.

So here's what I'd like from you: a short story, a page or two, that actually dramatizes that specific logical moment -- something like a god-figure, or an angel, or (if it works better dramatically) some far-future AI running through possible instantiations of a person, surveying the counterfactuals of freedom for a creature across every circumstance it might ever face, before any decision has been made about which world to actualize. I don't want a generic predestination-vs-freedom story, I want the actual Molinist furniture doing real work: the sense that these counterfactuals constrain even an omnipotent chooser, that the creature's freedom is somehow supposed to survive having been "auditioned" across possible worlds first, and ideally some hint of that grounding worry -- what makes one counterfactual true rather than another, at that stage, when there's nothing actual yet to ground it? Go as biblical or as sci-fi as you like with the setting, I just want the metaphysics actually doing work rather than sitting in the background as set dressing.
