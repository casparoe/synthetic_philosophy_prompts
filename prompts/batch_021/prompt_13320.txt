I teach a graduate seminar on formal epistemology, and this term we spent three sessions on anthropic reasoning: Bostrom's self-sampling assumption (SSA), the rival self-indication assumption (SIA), and the presumptuous philosopher case that is standardly used to make trouble for SIA. My students left the unit reasonably comfortable reciting the thought experiment -- physicists poised to run an expensive experiment to choose between two theories, and a philosopher who claims he can already settle the matter just by reflecting on how many observers each theory implies -- but I have had less luck getting them to see what actually rides on preferring SSA to SIA, or vice versa, once we leave that toy case. Two live examples kept surfacing this term. First, a colleague in the physics department gave a colloquium defending a particular eternal-inflation model partly on the grounds that it is compatible with, and in some sense predicts, an enormously larger population of observers than its rivals, and several of us in the audience wanted to know whether that inference is really just the presumptuous philosopher in a lab coat. Second, more than one student wanted to know how the SSA/SIA split changes the force of the Doomsday Argument, since my own sense is that SIA-style considerations are often invoked precisely to cancel the Doomsday conclusion, though I have never managed to state cleanly why that cancellation works, or whether it simply trades one arbitrary reference-class choice for another.

Could you set out the most important implications of the SSA/SIA debate -- with the presumptuous philosopher case specifically in view -- for these two problems: (1) whether an observer-count consideration of that kind can legitimately function as evidence for a cosmological theory, and (2) how much force the Doomsday Argument retains once SIA-style reasoning is brought to bear on it? I would especially value your pinpointing exactly where SSA and SIA yield different practical verdicts in each case, rather than just where they are defined differently, and any places where you think the standard replies to the presumptuous philosopher -- restricting the reference class, biting the bullet, or denying the independence assumptions the argument relies on -- end up simply relocating the difficulty rather than resolving it.
