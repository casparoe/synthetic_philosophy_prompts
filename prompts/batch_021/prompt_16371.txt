My old law school roommate is getting married in a few weeks, and since I'm the one who ended up writing novels instead of practicing law, I got roped into officiating (yes, the $30 online ordination), which means I owe her a short reflection before the vows. Here's the wrinkle: she and her fiancé have written an actual "Ulysses contract" into the ceremony -- three named people who get to intervene, plus a mandatory six-month wait before either of them can file for divorce. Tied to the mast, basically, but for a marriage instead of a boat. Its kind of moving, but it nags at me too, because Odysseus had the easy version of this problem -- we already know the Sirens are lying to him, so his future begging-to-be-untied self was never onto anything real. A future spouse who wants out doesn't get that guarantee. I want the reflection to actually sit with that difference instead of just doing the cute mast joke, and still land somewhere warm, and it has to work for both her law school friends who'll clock exactly what kind of contract this is and my family who won't have a clue what any of it means. Four minutes, one earplug joke if you can manage it.
