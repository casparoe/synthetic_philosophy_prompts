I'm a PhD student in metaphysics currently drafting a dissertation chapter that uses the fink/mask literature on dispositions to build a broader case against purely conditional analyses of dispositional predicates. I've worked through Martin's electro-fink, Lewis's two sorcerer cases in "Finkish Dispositions," and Bird's poison-and-antidote example often enough that I'm comfortable with each case taken on its own, but I keep tripping over something my advisor says almost in passing: that finks and masks are "different species" of counterexample to the simple conditional analysis (x is disposed to give manifestation M in response to stimulus C iff x would M if C obtained), and that this is exactly why Lewis's Reformed Conditional Analysis -- which patches the simple analysis by requiring that the disposition's intrinsic causal basis persist through the stimulus -- is standardly taken to neutralize finks while remaining vulnerable to masks and antidotes. A friend in my reading group thinks this is overstated; she suspects that, with enough redescription of the causal basis, any mask case can be rewritten as a fink (or vice versa), which would make the taxonomy more a matter of expository convenience than genuine metaphysical structure.

I'd like your help thinking this through properly, and ideally without just re-narrating the stock examples. First, state as precisely as you can the structural criterion that separates a finkish disposition from a masked one -- ideally in terms of what each does, or doesn't do, to the disposition's causal basis versus the causal process running from basis-plus-stimulus to manifestation. Second, use that criterion to walk through why the Reformed Conditional Analysis is generally thought to survive finks but not masks; I want the mechanics of the failure, not just the verdict. Third, adjudicate my reading group's disagreement: is the fink/mask distinction principled and exhaustive, or can every mask be redescribed as a fink under some suitable choice of causal basis? Finally, since this bears directly on my dissertation's argument, tell me where you actually come down: does the mask/antidote problem reveal something that no conditional analysis can fix even in principle, or is it the kind of defect that a sufficiently clever further reformation could still patch?
