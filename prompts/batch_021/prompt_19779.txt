I keep coming back to Wittgenstein's private language argument whenever alignment-adjacent discussions turn to whether a language model's self-reports — its stated confidence, its "reasoning," and so on — mean anything at all. The argument turns on the collapse of the "seems right"/"is right" distinction: if the only check on whether a sign is being used correctly is the speaker's own later impression that it matches an earlier use, there is no fact of the matter that could make the use correct or incorrect. Give a concrete example of a language model's self-report in which this exact structure recurs, and then say whether the fact that a third party can, in principle, inspect the model's activations directly actually supplies the missing public criterion — or whether the report remains empty in Wittgenstein's sense regardless.
