Question I gave:

"In Reasons and Persons, Parfit describes both a straightforward teletransportation case and a 'Branch Line' variant of it. Explain the difference between the two cases, and explain why Parfit thinks the Branch Line case makes a stronger argument for his claim that personal identity is not what matters in survival. What does he think matters instead, and how do the notions of psychological connectedness and psychological continuity figure into his answer?"

Answer I need graded:

"In the basic teletransporter case, a machine on Earth scans your body down to the atom, destroys it painlessly, and beams the information to Mars, where a new body is instantly built out of new matter to match. The resulting person on Mars has all your memories, personality and plans, so they're psychologically continuous with you, even though no matter was literally carried over.

In the Branch Line version, the scanner malfunctions: it doesn't destroy your body, it just damages your heart so you'll die of natural causes in a few days, while the replica on Mars is created as normal. So for a few days there are two people, both fully psychologically continuous with the original, and both with an equal claim to being you.

Parfit thinks the Branch Line case makes the stronger argument because it exposes that psychological continuity theories of personal identity are false: identity has to be a strict one-to-one relation, so since the original is related in exactly the same way to two different successors, being psychologically continuous with someone can't be sufficient for being that person. Since your continued existence can't just consist in this relation, and since you still die on the Branch Line exactly as you would from ordinary heart failure, Parfit's point is that we shouldn't take much comfort from psychological continuity - it's compatible with your literally dying.

Connectedness and continuity are really just two names for the same thing in Parfit's terminology - the persisting of memories, beliefs, intentions etc over time - so the Branch Line replica and an ordinary survivor have the same amount of it relative to you. Parfit thought this reductionist picture of persons was close to the Buddhist view that the self isn't a further fact over and above these psychological connections."
