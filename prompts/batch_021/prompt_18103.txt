So this argument with a coworker has been living in my head for like three weeks now and I can't shake it. I'm on the data platform team at my company, and one of the things our pipeline does is dedup incoming events before they hit the warehouse -- basically, if two events are identical across every single field we capture (user id, session id, timestamp down to the millisecond, the payload, all of it), we treat the second one as a duplicate delivery and silently drop it. Nobody had ever questioned this until last month.

In a design review, a coworker argued that this is actually wrong, at least in principle. His point was that two events could be genuinely seperate real-world occurences -- like, two different automated test runners firing the exact same click event in the same millisecond -- that just happen to be identical on every field we're able to record, and that calling them "duplicates" and deleting one is really just us losing real data because our schema isn't rich enough to tell them apart, not because they're actually the same event twice. I pushed back with something like "if literally nothing distinguishes them, even in principle, then there's no real difference, so they might as well be the same event" -- but the more I sit with that the less sure I am that it's actually a coherent thing to say, and I can't pin down exactly why.

It's been nagging at me because it reminds me of some thought experiment from a philosophy gen-ed I took as a freshman and mostly forgot -- something about two perfectly identical spheres or balls just floating alone in an otherwise totally empty universe, and whether "identical in absolutely every respect" even describes two things, or whether it secretly just describes one thing counted twice. I never got to the actual resolution in that class, if there was one. Could you walk me through the actual reasoning here, properly? Specifically: is my coworker's objection sound, is my "no possible difference, no real difference" instinct defensible or is it hiding a mistake somewhere, and is there any principled way to decide when indistinguishable really does mean identical and when it doesn't? We do have to actually settle on real pipeline behavior at some point, so I'd love an actual answer rather than a "well, it depends."
