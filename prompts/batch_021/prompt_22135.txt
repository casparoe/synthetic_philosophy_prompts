So in my history of ethics seminar we've been going through Bentham, including some of the stuff he never dared to publish where he argues consenual "unnatural" sex acts (his word for it was "paederasty," though he meant it way more broadly than the modern term) shouldn't be criminalized because they don't hurt anyone and just produce pleasure - pretty radical for the 1780s. I can't stop thinking about how he'd apply that same logic today to something like deepfake porn. By his own hedonic calculus it scores pretty high on extent and fecundity (a ton of people get real pleasure from it, easily and repeatedly), but the actual person depicted never consented and is often genuinely distressed once they find out, even though nobody physically touches them. Would Bentham's own framework actually have the resources to condemn that, given his whole thing was consent + no real harm = none of the law's business? Or does his calculus just kind of break down once the "harm" is reputational/psychological instead of physical?
