Sidgwick, and later Parfit, both argue that pure time preference — giving a future benefit or harm less weight simply because it lies further from the present, apart from any accompanying change in its magnitude or probability — has no rational or ethical basis, and this argument is now widely treated as more or less decisive within moral philosophy. Given that, I have never understood why economists such as Nordhaus continue to build positive discount rates for future generations' welfare into their climate-policy models: doesn't any positive rate simply smuggle back in, under another name, the very pure time preference that Sidgwick and Parfit refuted? I would value your assessment of whether mainstream climate economics is quietly resting on a premise that moral philosophy has already discredited.
