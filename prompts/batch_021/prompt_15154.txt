Since I retired a few years back after a career in low-temperature atomic physics (a lot of years thinking about Bose-Einstein condensates and why, in principle, you can never pin a label on "this atom" as opposed to "that atom"), I've gotten a little obsessed with philosophy -- to the point where I now run a tiny podcast out of my garage interviewing people on philosophy-of-science topics, maybe 150 regular listeners, mostly other retired scientists who never had time for this stuff the first time around (my wife says its a midlife crisis about twenty years too late). Next week I'm recording with a philosopher of physics whose whole research program is built around "weak discernibility": the idea that two fermions in the same joint state, even though no ordinary property tells them apart, still count as two rather than one because some relation -- like having opposite spin -- holds between them without ever holding of either one to itself. What's got me hooked is that she applies the same trick retroactively to Max Black's old two-spheres thought experiment: since each sphere is a mile from a sphere and nothing is a mile from itself, that relation is irreflexive too, so by her lights even Black's own example fails to refute the weak version of the identity of indiscernibles.

The trouble is shes slippery in exactly one spot: whenever somebody objects that the discerning relation seems to presuppose there are already two things around to relate, she waves it off as "confusing notation for metaphysics" and moves on. Can you draft me eight to ten interview questions that don't let her get away with that twice? I want at least one that goes straight at that circularity worry without accepting the notation dodge, one that ties this back to Hacking's old rejoinder that Black's symmetric universe might, given some unusual topology, just be one sphere rather than two, one about entangled states specifically -- given how non-separable they're supposed to be, is there even a determinate pair of relata there for the "opposite spin" relation to hold between -- and one that just asks flat out what would count as actual evidence against her view, rather than another objection she's already got a tidy answer for. For each of those, add a quick follow-up keyed to whichever dodge she's most likely to reach for first.
