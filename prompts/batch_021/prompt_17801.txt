Ok here's something that's been bugging me for weeks and I can't find the right words for it, so bear with me. Say I want to know if my bathroom scale is accurate. Every morning I step on it, it gives me a number that basically matches what I "expect" — which, lets be honest, is really just whatever the scale told me the day before. After months of this I'd feel pretty confident the scale is reliable, but I never once checked it against some other, independent scale — all my "evidence" that its accurate came from the scale itself. It feels like each individual reading was probably fine on its own, and a hundred fine readings in a row should add up to "the scale is basically trustworthy," but that conclusion feels like cheating, like I pulled a real fact (that the scale works) out of thin air using nothing but the scale. Is this an actual recognized problem in epistemology, or am I just confusing myself with bad reasoning? And if it's a real problem, why can't someone who likes a theory like reliabilism (where a belief counts as knowledge just because it happens to come from a reliable process, whether or not you know that) just shrug it off as no big deal?
