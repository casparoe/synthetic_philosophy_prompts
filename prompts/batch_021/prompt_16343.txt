Okay so this thing has been rattling around in my head for like two years now and I can't get anyone to give me a straight answer on it. My younger sister is just next level when it comes to reading a moral situation — she'll clock that a joke was kind of messed up before anyone else in the room even blinks, she's the one who called out our whole extended family for how we were gossiping about our cousin's divorce, stuff like that. And somewhere along the way I started basically outsourcing this kind of stuff to her. Like if I'm not sure whether its shady to regift a present, or whether I should say something when a coworker makes an off comment in a meeting, or whether ghosting someone is actually cruel or just how dating works now, I just ask her and do whatever she says. She has never once steered me wrong.

Me and my friend Dana got into it about this over dinner a few weeks back (poor waiter had to keep coming back to refill our water while we argued). Dana thinks the outsourcing thing is bad, but not in a "you're lazy" way — her point is that even when I end up doing the right thing because my sister told me to, I'm not actually being a good person when I do it, because I don't understand why its right, I'm just copying the answer off someone else's paper. She even challenged me to explain why regifting is shady without just repeating what my sister told me, and I honestly couldn't do it, which annoyed me more than it should have. Still, I pushed back with the obvious comparison: I trust my doctor's diagnosis without understanding medicine myself, and nobody says that makes me less healthy, or thinks I owe it to myself to go read a physiology textbook before I'm allowed to take the pills. So why is leaning on my sister for an ethics call any different from leaning on my doctor for a health call? Dana didn't really have a good answer for that one, she just kept saying "it's not the same, you'll see."

And then it gets even more confusing to me, because when I was like seven my parents just told me stealing was wrong and I believed them, full stop, I didn't reason my way to it myself — and nobody thinks seven year old me was some kind of moral failure for that. So genuinely, is there an actual difference between all these cases, or is Dana just repeating something that sounds wise but falls apart if you actually poke at it?
