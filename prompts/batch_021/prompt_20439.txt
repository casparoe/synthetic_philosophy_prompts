This year, for the first time, I have some discretionary money set aside to give away, and I've spent several weeks going back and forth on how to split it between two very different organizations. One is a long-established charity that provides a specific medical treatment to children in a region where a particular disease is common; it has been independently evaluated a number of times, and it can tell donors roughly how many children it reaches each year. The other is a much newer organization funding early-stage research into a cure for a disease that currently has no effective treatment at all — if the research eventually pans out, it could help far more people than the first charity ever will, but it might just as easily lead nowhere. When I described the dilemma to a friend who has given more thought to this kind of decision than I have, she was adamant that it isn't really a dilemma at all: we should fund the established charity, because that is the only one of the two where we can actually point to a real result, whereas the other is essentially a bet dressed up to look like a donation.

I found this convincing until it occurred to me that even the children helped by the established charity will go on to lead entire lives about which I know nothing — relationships formed or not formed, children of their own had or not had, countless decisions rippling outward in ways nobody could ever trace back to a single donation made years earlier. So whatever certainty the first option offers seems to run out fairly quickly too; it's just that everyone has quietly agreed to stop counting at the point where the numbers still look reassuring, rather than at any point that is actually principled. This leaves me stuck: it feels wrong to conclude that the two donations are equally justified simply because both eventually disappear into the same fog of unknown consequences, but I can no longer see what entitles us to treat one as safely calculable and the other as a mere gamble. Is there a principled distinction that survives this worry, or does my friend's argument quietly collapse under the same logic that was supposed to support it — and either way, what should that mean for how I actually make this kind of decision?
