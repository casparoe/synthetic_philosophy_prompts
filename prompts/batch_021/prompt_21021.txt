Retired chemist here, im a few months into teaching myself philosophy of language and I've hit a snag with Kripke. So does Kripke's necessary a posteriori actually break something load-bearing in Kant's setup, since it lets necessity come apart from apriority in a direction Kant didn't allow for? Or is Kant working with a different flavor of necessity than Kripke, so the two just talk past each other? Skip the textbook definitions -- I want to know whether this parallel actually holds up or falls apart once you look closely.
