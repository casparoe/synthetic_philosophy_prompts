I've been reading Stuart Russell's argument in Human Compatible that an AI system should infer a person's reward function from their behavior via inverse reinforcement learning, rather than being handed a hand-coded objective by its engineers. This seems to dissolve the value-specification problem: if IRL genuinely recovers the reward function that best explains an agent's choices, then simply observing more of someone's behavior should converge on an ever more accurate picture of what they actually want, with no explicit specification needed at all. So why do alignment researchers still list value specification among the hardest open problems in the field, rather than treating it as something IRL has already solved in principle, with only engineering work left to scale it up?
