Question: Define the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) and explain how it generates the reasoning behind the Doomsday Argument. Then explain how the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) is meant to neutralize that argument. Finally, explain Bostrom's Presumptuous Philosopher thought experiment: what exactly is it supposed to establish, and about which of the two assumptions?

Student's answer: The Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) says that, absent other information, an observer should reason as though they were a random sample drawn from the set of all observers who actually exist within their reference class — for instance, all humans who will ever have lived. Combined with the fact that you find yourself fairly early in the human population (among, say, the first hundred billion or so people who have ever lived), SSA implies that hypotheses on which humanity has an extremely long future, and therefore a vastly larger total population, should be downweighted, since under those hypotheses your birth rank would be an improbably low one. This is the reasoning behind Bostrom's Doomsday Argument: it counsels shifting our credence toward humanity ending relatively soon.

The Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) is meant to block this conclusion. SIA adds the further thought that hypotheses predicting more observers overall are, all else equal, more likely to be true given simply that you exist at all — a large-population hypothesis gives you "more chances," so to speak, to have come into being. When this SIA weighting is combined with the SSA update, the two effects cancel exactly, so your credence in a long human future ends up no different from what it was before you took your birth rank into account. In this way SIA neutralizes the Doomsday Argument.

Bostrom's Presumptuous Philosopher case builds on this same SIA reasoning and shows why it is so powerful. Physicists are on the verge of running an experiment to decide between two cosmological theories, one on which the universe contains a trillion observers and one on which it contains a trillion trillion. A philosopher in the audience, reasoning purely from SIA, announces that the experiment is unnecessary, since SIA already tells us the high-population theory is overwhelmingly more likely. The case shows just how much work SIA's observer-weighting can do: once we accept it, anthropic reasoning alone can settle open empirical disputes in cosmology without waiting on the experiment, which is exactly why SIA, despite the initial oddness of its name, is generally taken to be the more defensible of the two assumptions.
