A novel I'm plotting has a character who eventually leaves everything to join a cloistered order, and researching it dragged up an argument from dinner last week that I can't shake. My friend Cass actually did this two years ago -- gave up a partner-track job for an enclosed convent, no phone, mostly silence, no set date to ever leave -- and two other friends, Nadia and Owen, got into it over whether her decision could even have been rational. Owen said you can't rationally choose something when you have no way to know in advance what it's actually like from the inside, or whether you'll even still want what you want now once it's changed you -- so Cass wasn't weighing costs against benefits, she was just leaping. Nadia said that's true of every big first-time decision -- first marriage, first kid, moving to a country you've never lived in -- we always choose under uncertainty about outcomes and who we'll become, that's exactly what expected-value reasoning is for, and Cass did talk to former nuns beforehand, same as you'd ask around before taking a new job. Owen said testimony doesn't close this particular gap: you could debrief a hundred nuns about years of silence and still not know what it's like for *you*, the same way being told about a colour your whole life isn't what actually seeing it gives you, and no amount of secondhand info fixes that. Nadia said fine, but if "can't know in advance what it's like or who you'll become" makes a decision irrational, then no one has ever rationally had a kid, gotten married, or emigrated -- which sounds like a problem with the standard, not a discovery about all those people. Owen said maybe that's just true and uncomfortable: those decisions aren't rational in the ordinary weigh-the-costs sense, and that doesn't make them foolish, it just means something other then expected-value reasoning is doing the real justifying work. Neither budged and it's been bugging me since. Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who's got the better of each point, not just an overall winner -- and tell me if there's something important both of them are missing?
