So I'm a retired evolutionary biologist, and lately I've fallen down a rabbit hole of decision theory and AI-safety blogs (it's replaced birdwatching as my retirement hobby). Between Schelling on commitment -- burning bridges, throwing away the steering wheel in a game of chicken -- and this newer idea some of these blogs call the 'commitment race,' where agents scramble to lock themselves irrevocably into a hard line before the other side can, it all maps very neatly onto the war of attrition and hawk-dove games we've used for decades to model animal contests. As far as I can tell, its really just the old lesson dressed up for AIs: commit first, commit hardest, and you come out ahead, whether its a labour dispute or a stand-off between nuclear powers. So why do people in this field treat the 'commitment races problem' as some dangerous open puzzle, rather than just telling every agent to commit early and often?
