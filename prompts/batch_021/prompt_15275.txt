A few months ago I started doing flotation therapy for stress: you lie in a pitch-dark, soundproof pod, in water saturated with Epsom salt and heated to skin temperature, until you lose any sense of where the water ends and your body begins. Near the end of my last session I couldn't feel my arms, my legs, or any boundary of my body, yet I remained completely certain, the entire time, that I existed. I described this afterward to a friend who studied philosophy in college, and she insists it proves something specific: that whatever "I" am can't be identical to my body, since I had no evidence, even introspectively, that a body was there, while never once losing certainty that I existed. That inference feels too fast to me, though I can't say exactly where it goes wrong. Is she onto something real, or is she just mistaking an unusual sensory experience for a philosophical discovery?
