Goodhart's law is usually compressed into a single line: when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. But AI-safety researchers David Manheim and Scott Garrabrant have argued that this slogan actually blurs together four distinct failure modes: regressional Goodhart, where optimizing an imperfect proxy also optimizes its error term, so the top-scoring cases regress toward the mean on the true goal; extremal Goodhart, where pushing a proxy to unusually extreme values carries a system outside the regime in which it ever tracked the goal; causal Goodhart, where intervening on a proxy that merely correlates with, rather than causes, the goal fails to move the goal itself; and adversarial Goodhart, where an agent with its own incentives learns exactly where proxy and goal diverge and exploits that gap on purpose.
