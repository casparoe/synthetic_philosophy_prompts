I lost my younger sister three weeks ago. She worked in inverse reinforcement learning -- inferring someone's reward function just from watching what they do, since people are unreliable narrators of their own wants. I'm a novelist, not remotely technical, but the family's asked me to give the eulogy because I "have a way with words." Problem is, half the room at the memorial will be her old labmates who'll catch every reference, and the other half is family who won't know an MDP from a hole in the ground. She was maddeningly undemonstrative her whole life too, never told us what she wanted, so all I've got is thirty-some years of observed behavior -- same as any IRL setup. I know enough from her dinner-table rants to know her fields dirty secret: the reward function is never uniquely recoverable from behavior alone, there's always some degenerate reading that fits the data just as well (technically "felt nothing, ever" explains the record no worse than "loved us fiercely") unless you bring a prior to it. I dont want to just assert the generous reading with total confidence, she'd call that sloppy, but I can't stand up there and be totally agnostic either. Can you write the eulogy -- 7-8 minutes, warm enough for my parents, honest enough that her old colleagues don't cringe?
