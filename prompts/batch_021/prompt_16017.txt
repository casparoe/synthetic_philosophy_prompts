So I'm in a little reading group (mostly people left over from an old SlateStarCodex meetup, we've been doing this for years now) that just worked through Bostrom's Anthropic Bias, and it's left me wanting a short story that actually dramatizes the presumptuous philosopher instead of the usual two-guys-at-a-whiteboard framing you get in the papers. Setup, if it helps: two rival cosmological models are empirically indistinguishable except that one implies a universe (or a multiverse, if you wanna go Tegmark-y about it) with vastly more observers in it than the other, and a very expensive experiment is finally about to be run to settle which one is true. I want a character who's basically the presumptuous philosopher incarnate - theorist, crank, or even the AI doing the lab's Bayesian bookkeeping, whatever fits - who tells the experimentalists not to bother, because she can already tell them with near-certainty which theory is right, purely from the fact that she exists to ask the question at all, i.e. straightforward self-indication reasoning where you weight each hypothesis by the number of observers it predicts.

What I really don't want is for her to just be right, or to be a strawman who's obviously wrong so everyone gets to feel smart. Give her a real opponent, someone who pushes back with the actual cost of that move rather than a hand-wave - the reference class problem, or the fact that leaning on the sister assumption (SSA) instead would've pointed her toward a doomsday-flavored conclusion rather than a confident big-universe one, or (my favorite version, if you want to take it there) the sting that the very same style of reasoning that makes her so sure also seems to flip around and imply something bleak about how much Great-Filter-style winnowing is still ahead of us rather than behind - I think it was Katja Grace who pointed out that particular reversal. The physics itself can be totally hand-wavy, I just need the anthropic reasoning underneath to actually hold together and not just be gestured at.

I don't think the story even needs to reveal the collider result at the end - honestly its almost more interesting if the experiment ends up beside the point given everything that's already been "decided" from the armchair. Something like 1000-1500 words, more scene-and-character than pure dialogue-debate, please.
