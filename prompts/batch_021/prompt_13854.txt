So last night my friend Priya and I ended up in this hour-long argument after a planetarium show downtown, about whether the universe being unbelievably huge is actually supposed to make you feel small in some meaningful way. Priya had just read Thomas Nagel's essay on the absurd for a class, the one where he argues we can always step outside our own engaged, serious lives and watch them from a detached, kind of from-nowhere vantage point where they suddenly look arbitrary and unjustified, and that this clash between the "inside" and "outside" views (not any specific fact about how big the cosmos is) is what actually makes life absurd — and crucially, that we can't just refuse to take that outside view seriously, because we're built in a way that keeps dragging us back into it. I countered with this Frank Ramsey bit I half remember from an old logic course: apparently in some talk he gave to a Cambridge secret society back in the 1920s, he just flatly refuses to feel humbled by the stars, says his mental picture of the world is drawn in perspective rather than to scale, with people taking up the whole foreground and the stars shrunk down to the size of coins, and that treating physical size as if it settles questions of importance is basically a confusion. Priya's comeback was that this isn't really a response to Nagel at all, its just Ramsey opting out of the outside view by fiat, which is exactly the move she thinks isn't available to us.

We never landed anywhere and its been bugging me since. Could you actually compare what Ramsey is doing with what Nagel is doing here, specifically on this "does cosmic scale undermine significance" question? In particular, is Ramsey offering a genuine alternative to Nagel's picture of absurdity, or is Priya right that he's just helping himself to a move Nagel already ruled out? Whichever of them you think actually handles the vastness-of-the-universe worry better, say which and defend it.
