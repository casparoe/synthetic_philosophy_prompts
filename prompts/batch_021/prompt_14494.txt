So last night I got into a whole two-hour thing with my friend Marco about this "coherence arguments" stuff he picked up from some AI safety blog he's been obsessed with lately. His claim: any agent that doesn't act like an expected utility maximizer -- i.e. has preferences that are incomplete or intransitive somewhere -- can be turned into a money pump, taken to the cleaners through a series of trades that leaves it strictly worse off for literally nothing in return, and since no rational agent would let that happen to itself, rationality basically just IS being a full EU maximizer, transitive, complete, the whole package. Apparently this is a big deal in AI-risk circles because its supposed to mean any sufficiently capable AI will converge on maximizing some coherent utility function whether we design it to or not.

I pushed back with the one thing I remembered from a decision theory class years ago: that the money-pump argument only really bites if you assume the agent is "myopic," i.e. it'll always take whatever trade is strictly better right now with no memory of the pattern it's being led through. I said a smarter agent could adopt a policy in advance -- like Ulysses tying himself to the mast -- of just refusing the one trade that would complete the loop, and that this "resolute" move lets it keep incomplete (or even locally intransitive) preferences without ever actually bleeding money, so the argument gets you to "don't be exploitable" but not all the way to full EU maximization. Marco said that's basically cheating: if at that final trade the agent would genuinely prefer to take it, refusing anyway just relocates the irrationality -- now it's being akratic or dynamically inconsistent instead -- so I hadn't escaped the problem, just renamed it. We went back and forth for ages over drinks and never really got anywhere, partly because neither of us could reconstruct the arguments precisely enough to actually pin the other down.

I'd love to see this fought out properly by two people who actually know the material cold. Could you write a dialogue between a philosopher defending the coherence/money-pump argument for full expected-utility maximization and one who thinks rational agents can retain incomplete or otherwise non-standard preferences without ever being exploitable? I want genuine back-and-forth, not two monologues -- each of them should engage with the specific move the other just made (the resolute-choice reply, whatever the sharpest comeback to it is, and so on for several rounds), and neither should get to be the obviously-smarter one who wins in the end.
