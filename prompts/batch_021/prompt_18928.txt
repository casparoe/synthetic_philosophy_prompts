My uncle worked on South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission back in the 90s, and ubuntu was never just a nice word for him -- his whole approach to hearings rested on the idea that a person only really is a person through relationships with other people, which is why he pushed restoring relationships over punishing people. He died last week and I'm giving the eulogy. Half the room's gonna be old TRC and legal collegues who know exactly what 'umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu' means, and the other half is just family who want to hear about him as a dad and oupa. He always said the worst thing you can say about someone at their funeral is that they did it all on their own, so write me a eulogy where his relational view of personhood actually shapes how it talks about his life and death, without turning into a philosophy lecture for the family half of the room.
