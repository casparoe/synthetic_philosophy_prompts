Higher-order theories of consciousness hold that a mental state is conscious only when one is aware of being in it via some further, suitably related mental representation. Compare David Rosenthal's actualist higher-order thought (HOT) theory, on which this further representation must be an actual, ordinarily unconscious, thought, with Peter Carruthers's dispositionalist alternative, on which it suffices that the first-order state merely be disposed to produce such a thought. Carruthers motivates this shift chiefly by arguing that actualism cannot accommodate the richness and fine grain of ordinary experience without positing an implausible profusion of simultaneous higher-order thoughts. Assess whether moving to mere disposition actually resolves this difficulty, or whether it instead leaves it mysterious how a dispositional fact about a state could be what makes that state phenomenally conscious rather than merely poised to become so. Defend your verdict.
