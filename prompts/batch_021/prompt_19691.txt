At work, our error-rate dashboard has been flat and stable for two years straight. A colleague argues that my confidence it'll stay that way is no better justified than confidence in a rival prediction he invented on the spot: the rate stays exactly where it is until some arbitrary date he names, then jumps permanently to a new level. His point is that every reading we've ever taken fits his prediction exactly as well as mine, and that he can always push his date further into the future as we gather more data, so no amount of additional evidence could ever tell us which of us is right before that date actually arrives. I'm confident that betting on continued stability is the sane thing to do, but when I tried to explain why, I couldn't point to anything in the data itself that favors my forecast over his. Is there a genuine, principled reason to prefer my forecast, or is he right that it's really just habit dressed up as reasoning?
