Im a philosophy PhD student neck deep in Lewis's On the Plurality of Worlds for comps, and it bugs me that basically every "other possible worlds" story is secretly just the Marvel multiverse: portals, some other version of you crossing over into this one. Real modal realism is stranger and colder than that -- the other worlds are fully concrete, causally sealed off from ours, and whoever's living out some other possibility isn't you, just a counterpart: similar, but numerically a different person who will never hear of you or this world. Could you write me a short story (a page or two, any genre) that takes that seriously -- no portals, no shared minds -- and sits with the uncomfortable question it raises: if every way things could've gone is already happening, in full concrete detail, to somebody, why should it matter to me which of those somebodies I turn out to be? Don't resolve it too neatly.
