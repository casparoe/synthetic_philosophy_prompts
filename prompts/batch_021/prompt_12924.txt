Me and my friend Jonah had a proper argument last night about Taurek's "Should the Numbers Count" and whether Scanlon's fix for it actually works. Jonah reckons Scanlon's "tie-breaking" move - start from a 1-vs-1 case, which is a tie you could flip a coin on, then let the spare people on the bigger side break that tie - genuinely never sums anyone's claims together, so it dodges Taurek's objection clean. I think its aggregation wearing a trenchcoat: the second those spare people get to break the tie, their claims are being weighed as a bloc against the one persons claim, which is exactly the move Taurek says isn't available to us. Neither of us budged. Write me a dialogue between a hardline Taurekian and a Scanlonian contractualist standing at a mine collapse where they can each reach only one of two trapped groups in time (five down one shaft, one down the other) and have to decide right now. Get the Taurekian to push the "tie-breaking is aggregation in disguise" charge as hard as it goes, make the Scanlonian actually answer it instead of just repeating that numbers matter, and then let the Taurekian respond to that specific answer. Both should be equally sharp and actually engage with each other's points instead of talking past one another.
