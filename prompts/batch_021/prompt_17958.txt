I sit on my company's incident-response tooling review board, which approves on-call automation changes before rollout. Our bot maintains a probability distribution over four subsystems -- US-gateway, US-auth, EU-gateway, EU-auth -- used to decide which team gets paged, starting at 25% each when an incident opens. Partway through a recent incident, an automated signal amounted to "if the fault is in the US cluster, it is about three times as likely to be the gateway service as the auth service," saying nothing about EU. Our bot updates to the distribution closest to the old one by KL divergence that satisfies this constraint, and it returned roughly 35%/12%/26.5%/26.5% -- so overall US-cluster probability fell from 50% to 47% and EU rose to 53%, purely from a claim about the internal split within the US cluster. One engineer calls this a bug and wants a patch that redistributes probability only within whichever cluster a signal names, leaving the other cluster's total share untouched. A second engineer argues that patch is arbitrary: it protects the "cluster" split specifically, when a patch built to protect the "gateway vs. auth" split instead would return a different answer, with nothing to justify preferring one convention over the other. This has stalled Thursday's planned rollout. Please draft the memo I will bring to the board: recommend which update behavior we should ship, or propose a concrete third option if neither engineer has it right, attach a rollout timeline, and give an honest, substantive treatment of the strongest point on whichever side you don't choose.
