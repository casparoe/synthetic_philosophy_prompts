A friend and I spent last night arguing about a mix-up at the small delivery company I help run, and neither of us gave any ground.

We couldn't tell which of our two vans — the blue one or the white one — a missing package had gone out on the day before; each seemed equally likely. Independently, whichever van it was on had been just as likely to run the north loop as the south loop that day, so going in, all four combinations looked equally plausible. Then the blue van's driver called to say that if the package had been on his van, the odds were four to one that it went out on the north loop, since he defaults to that route whenever he's behind schedule, as he was that day; he was careful to add that this told us nothing about whether it had been his van in the first place. When I actually worked the probabilities through, the blue van's overall chance of having had the package moved off fifty percent anyway. My friend says that has to be a mistake: a statement entirely about what's true "if" it was his van, she argues, cannot be evidence about whether it was his van, so my numbers must be smuggling in some illegitimate assumption. I can't locate any such assumption, but I can't fully dismiss her instinct either.

Who's actually right, and where exactly does the other go wrong? We only have time to search one van's remaining stops before the day ends, so I'd genuinely like to know whether that phone call should change which one we choose.
