I spent my career doing bench neuroscience before retiring, and lately I've been having a lot of fun catching up on philosophy for the first time. One thing's been nagging at me though. Philosophers have kicked around the "brain in a vat" scenario for decades as basically a fun logical puzzle, but now we've got actual dishes of neurons that learned to play Pong through electrode feedback, and people with Neuralink-style implants moving a cursor around just by thinking. Doesn't that mean Descartes' old thought experiment has basically graduated from armchair speculation into a real engineering problem, and that skeptics finally have genuine scientific grounds for thinking we might actually be brains in vats, rather then it just being a hypothetical?
