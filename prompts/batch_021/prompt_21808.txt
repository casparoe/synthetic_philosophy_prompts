My dad turns 60 next weekend and I got roped into giving the toast. He's kept an actual Ben Franklin virtue-tracking chart on our pantry door since I was a kid -- 13 virtues, a weekly grid, a mark for every slip -- its a running family joke. I'm taking a seminar on Franklin's autobiography this semester, and the real story behind the chart (the speckled-axe bit about Order, the joke about being proud of his own humility, the part where he flat out admits he never got anywhere near moral perfection) is way better toast material than the inspirational-poster version my family believes. I don't want to get up there and pretend the chart worked and turned dad into a sage, but I don't want it landing as a roast about the garage he still cant organize either, and half the room only knows Franklin from the hundred dollar bill. Can you write the toast -- five minutes, built around the real, self-deprecating Franklin, that lands as true and warm instead of either of those?
