So this term I'm doing my program's required committee service as the grad student rep on my university's Student Conduct Board, and I've landed a case I can't stop mapping onto the responsibility seminar I'm taking right now (we just spent two weeks on Nagel and Williams on moral luck). Two unconnected students are up for hearing at the same session next Wednesday. Both were cited the same night for the identical code violation -- "reckless operation of a mobility device" -- after riding rental e-scooters at full speed through the main plaza during a packed passing period. One scooter clipped a student, who ended up with a fractured wrist and a mild concussion; the other rider was going, by all accounts, just as fast and just as recklessly, but the plaza happened to thin out about thirty seconds later and he didnt hit anyone. Under our code's sanction grid, "resulting injury" is what bumps a violation from Level 2 (probation, a remedial course) to Level 4 (suspension, possibly expulsion), so as written we're set to expel one of these guys and basically let the other walk with a warning, for conduct that by every witness account was the same.

Our board chair, a longtime student-affairs administrator who has zero patience for what she calls "philosophy-major logic," thinks this isn't even a hard question: the grid says what it says, harm is harm, and the injured student deserves to see the university take it seriously. I don't think that instinct is stupid, and honestly I don't think Nagel himself would say it is -- accepting that this is a textbook case of resultant luck (the two riders are equally at fault, whichever way it broke) doesn't automatically tell you that an institution should sanction purely on culpability and ignore what actually happened, since there might be real institutional reasons (deterrence, what's owed to the injured student, plain epistemic caution about how "identical" the two cases truly were) that survive the philosophical point. There's also a smaller thing I keep chewing on: Williams' idea of agent-regret suggests the rider who actually hit someone might reasonably be expected to feel, and maybe even express, something the lucky rider doesn't have to -- and I wonder whether that belongs somewhere in what we ask of him even if it shouldn't move his sanction level.

Could you draft the short memo (one page, max) I'll circulate to the Board before Wednesday? It needs to actually recommend an outcome for these two specific students -- same sanction, different sanctions, some third option -- and say briefly whether we should flag the sanction grid itself for review by the Dean of Students going forward. Please make the case for whatever you recommend in terms the rest of the Board would actually engage with, since most of them have never heard of moral luck and won't be moved by me citing a 1976 symposium at them; and take the chair's "harm is harm" position seriously as a real institutional view with real reasons behind it, not just a mistake to be corrected.
