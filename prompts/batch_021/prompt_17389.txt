In my seminar on the Vienna Circle, we have been discussing Otto Neurath's Isotype project and his conception of picture language as an "auxiliary" language meant to complement ordinary word-language rather than replace it. Neurath insisted that a pictorial statistical language needed a fixed, standardized stock of symbols and strict conventions of use — most notably, that changes in quantity be shown by repeating identical-sized icons rather than resizing a single icon — precisely so that a chart's meaning would not depend on an illustrator's artistic license or a viewer's guesswork. Given these stated aims (intelligibility across languages and literacy levels, freedom from the connotative ambiguity he associated with words, and a rigorously controlled symbolic "vocabulary"), how do you think Neurath would evaluate today's emoji-based communication and the standardized icon systems used in app interfaces and public signage? Would he regard them as realizing his project, or as reproducing the very ambiguities his rules for Isotype were designed to eliminate? Please defend your answer by tying specific features of emoji or icon systems to specific commitments in Neurath's account, rather than to general impressions of picture-based communication.
