This term I am taking a seminar on free will and moral responsibility, and we recently discussed Frankfurt's famous counterexample to the principle of alternative possibilities: the case in which Black, a neurosurgeon capable of manipulating Jones's brain, never actually needs to intervene because Jones decides entirely on his own to do what Black wanted anyway. Since Frankfurt shows that Jones is morally responsible even though he could not have done otherwise, it seems to me that this settles the free will debate in the compatibilists' favor. Given that, why do philosophers like Robert Kane still insist that free will requires the ability to do otherwise, when Frankfurt has already shown that alternative possibilities aren't necessary for moral responsibility?
