So this happened about two months ago and I still haven't really processed it. I got stung by a wasp while gardening and turned out to be way more allergic than I knew — full anaphalaxis, and by the time the ambulance got me to the ER my heart had actually stopped for a bit before they got it going again, the doctor said close to four minutes. The medical part isn't even the thing that's been bugging me though, it's what I experienced during that gap (I know "experienced" isn't really the right word if my brain wasn't doing anything, but bear with me). I had this incredibly vivid sense of being outside my body, totally calm, and then something like a light that wasn't really a light so much as this overwhelming awareness of everything being ordered and connected, plus a really strong impression that I was being shown something important that needed fixing. Nobody in my family is religious, I've never believed in anything like a soul, I'm the person in my friend group who always argues that the mind is just what the brain does and when the brain stops, that's it, full stop. So this has completely thrown me.

I told my friend Priya about it last week and we stayed up till 2am arguing about it, going in circles. She kept flip-flopping in a way that annoyed me — first she said it's obviously just what a dying brain does, totally explainable, don't read anything into it, and then twenty minutes later she was saying maybe I should be more open to not just being "a body," because clearly something happened that I can't just wave away, and wasnt I being closed minded for defaulting to the boring explanation just because it's what I believed beforehand? Neither of us could pick a lane, including me. Here's my actual problem, distilled: I still think, intellectually, that the idea of "me" surviving without my brain doesn't even properly make sense — like, what would even be the thing doing the experiencing? But the experience itself felt more real and more significant than almost anything that's happened to me, and I don't know what to do with that feeling weighed against what I think I know. Am I wrong to let this shake my views at all, or is Priya right that refusing to budge is its own kind of bias dressed up as rigor? Genuinely curious how someone would think through this properly instead of just grabbing whichever answer feels more comforting.
