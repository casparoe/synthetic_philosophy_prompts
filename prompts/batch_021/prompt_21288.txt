Had a two-hour argument with my friend Marcus last night that started, embarrassingly, over which side you're supposed to stand on when you're not walking up an escalator. He pointed out that in London everyone stands right and walks left, other cities do it backwards, and nobody ever sat down and voted on it, it just sort of settled into place — and since he's been reading Brian Skyrms he wanted to explain the whole thing via evolutionary game theory: replicator dynamics, correlated equilibria, nobody needs to know anything about anyone else's beliefs, the regularity just self-reinforces through blind trial and error. I've been ploughing through Lewis's Convention for a reading group and kept insisting thats not enough to make it a convention proper — for Lewis you also need common knowledge, everyone conforming and expecting everyone else to conform and expecting that expectation, and he's got that whole "basis for common knowledge" move specifically to stop the obvious regress objection (you know it, I know you know it, you know that I know that you know it...). Marcus's comeback was that this whole apparatus is doing no real work once you notice that Lewis's other favorite move, salience via precedent, is basically just path-dependence with a fancier name — so maybe the common-knowledge machinery is decorative and it's evolutionary dynamics all the way down. We never actually settled whether we disagreed about anything real or were just answering different questions (how conventions arise historically vs. what makes something a convention right now), and it's bugging me.

Could you write a dialogue between a committed Lewisian and a committed Skyrms-style evolutionary game theorist that actually works through this properly, instead of the half-baked version Marcus and I managed? I want the Lewisian to have to defend the common-knowledge/indication move against the charge that it's either psychologically unrealistic or just a relabeling of precedent, I want the evolutionary side to have to face the question of whether blind dynamics can ever generate anything with normative force (the sense that you *should* keep to the right, not just that you statistically do), and I'd like them to explicitly try — and maybe fail — to work out whether they're actual rivals or just have different explananda, the way Marcus and I never managed to.
