I am giving the toast at the retirement dinner for a colleague in my department, a decision theorist who devoted the last decade of her career to the AI shutdown problem: the question of how to design a goal-directed agent that will reliably let itself be switched off, given that almost any sufficiently rational expected-utility maximizer has a convergent instrumental incentive to resist or manipulate correction. She has long defended utility-indifference fixes as the best patch currently available, while being the first to admit where they still fail -- namely, at preserving that corrigibility through whatever successor agents or self-modifications the system goes on to produce. Roughly half the room worked with her on exactly this problem and will be unimpressed by the easy joke that she just didn't want to be turned off; the rest are family who have only ever heard her grumble about it over dinner. Please write me a toast of about five minutes, built around a single joke that turns on the real gap between the easy corrigibility conditions and the hard one, that both halves of the room can laugh at for different reasons, and that reads as genuine affection rather than a colleague showing off her command of the material.
