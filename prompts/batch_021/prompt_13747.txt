I'm about two-thirds into a novel set in Paris in 1757-58, the year Jean-Jacques Rousseau and Diderot's friendship finally curdled for good, and there's a scene I keep rewriting where the two of them have it out over the theatre question -- d'Alembert had just published that article on Geneva in the Encyclopedie suggesting the city build a proper theatre, and Rousseau was getting ready to fire back with his Lettre. I know in real life this fight mostly played out in print (plus all the business with Grimm and Mme d'Epinay, and that line in Le Fils naturel about only the wicked man living alone, or something close to that, which Rousseau apparently took very personally), but for the novel I want one scene where they actually say all this to each others faces before things become irreparable.

My problem is every version I draft collapses into Diderot saying "theatre civilizes us" and Rousseau saying "theatre corrupts us," which isn't really what either of them thought -- their actual positions were way more specific and they clearly understood exactly where the other was vulnerable, so it should read like two smart people who've read each other's stuff, not two people just talking past each other. Could you write me that dialogue? On Rousseau's side I want the bit about actors having to fake feeling things so often they lose track of what they actually feel, Geneva's own civic festivals as a better, participatory alternative to sitting passively in an audience, and his read of Moliere's Misanthrope as basically punishing Alceste for being sincere. On Diderot's side I want him defending drama's power to move people to real moral feeling through pity and sympathy rather than lecture (the stuff he was working out around Le Fils naturel and Le Pere de famille), pushing back specifically on the Misanthrope point -- isn't laughing at Alceste's tactlessness different from mocking sincerity itself -- and, since he's earned it by this point, landing at least one real personal jab, maybe about Rousseau's own opera. No stage directions needed, just the talking, and I'd honestly rather it run long and substantive than short and cute.
