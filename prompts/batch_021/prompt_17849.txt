I ran the standard "flicker of freedom" line on her: there's still some alternative sitting there, since Jones could at least have given off whatever prior sign would've triggered the device, so PAP isn't fully dead, just thinned out. Dee came back with the fancier buffer version -- I think this is the Mele and Robb case? -- where instead of watching for a sign, there's just a seperate deterministic backup process running the whole time, guaranteed to produce the same decision regardless, and it never actually has to intervene because Jones's own indeterministic deliberation gets there a beat earlier. Her point was that this kills the flicker for good, since there's no sign left for the Widerker-style dilemma (is the sign deterministic or not?) to even get a grip on. I said that's a shell game: if the backup is guaranteed to produce the exact same outcome no matter what Jones's own process does, there's now a live question about whether that process was ever doing any real work, which just sounds like the alternative-possibilities problem again, one level down. We went back and forth for like an hour over text and neither of us gave any ground.
