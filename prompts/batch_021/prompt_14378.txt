I'm a novelist and something that came up in my writing group last week won't leave me alone. I've been querying the same manuscript (a literary mystery thing, if genre matters here) for about six years now, something like 200 rejections and counting, and I still tell people, completely sincerely, that this is the one, that it's gonna break through eventually. One of the other writers in the group, Priya, called me out on it pretty gently but directly — she said that insisting a book will sell when there's basically zero evidence for it anymore (no full requests in ages, no editor interest, nothing) stops being determination at some point and just becomes lying to myself, and that I owe it to my partner and our actual finances to calibrate what I believe to the evidence in front of me, not the evidence I wish existed.

Here's the thing though: I don't think I could keep revising and sending the book out at all if I let myself fully believe the "rational" conclusion, which is that it's probably never happening. There's a version of this where the belief itself is doing work — a writer who's internally convinced they're done is a writer who quits, and a writer who quits definately doesn't sell the book, so keeping the faith almost changes the odds instead of just ignoring them. Priya's comeback to that was basically "that's exactly the kind of move that lets anybody believe anything they want about their own pet project forever, with zero accountability to reality, they'll just say the believing is part of what makes it come true."

I've been chewing on this for days instead of actually working on my revisions (very on brand) and I honestly dont know who's right. Is Priya right that you're never entitled to believe past what your evidence supports, no matter what it costs you to believe the truth instead? Or is there something real to the idea that certain beliefs are self-fulfilling in a way that legitimately changes the calculation? And if there is something to that, how would I even tell my situation apart from, say, someone who's convinced their obviously doomed startup is about to take off, when they'd say the exact same thing about their own belief mattering? Would genuinely love help thinking this through.
