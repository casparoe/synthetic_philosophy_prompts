So I'm a phil PhD student writing a dissertation chapter on medieval theories of causation, and I keep getting stuck on this comparison between Ghazali's occasionalism (from the Tahafut, the discussion on causality with the cotton-and-fire example -- discussion 17, I think?) and Hume on causation. On the surface they look almost like the same argument: neither one thinks we ever perceive a necessary connection between cause and effect, both say all we're really entitled to is constant conjunction, and -- this is the part that gets me -- they even converge on literally the same word, "custom," to explain why the world still looks regular even though nothing is actually necessitating anything. My office mate's take is that this is basically one skeptical move wearing two different costumes (one theological, one empiricist), and that the "custom" overlap is more than just a coincidence of translation -- its evidence they're onto the same underlying insight.

I'm not so convinced, but I also don't want to just hand-wave past it in the chapter. Ghazali's "custom" names God's habitual practice of creating associated events together, in a way that leaves Him free to break the pattern whenever He likes (Abraham not burning in the fire, etc) -- the regularity is basically a standing policy choice by an omnipotent will, renewed instant by instant. Hume's "custom," on the other hand, names a fact about our psychology: a habit of expectation that builds up in the observer after repeated exposure, and he's pretty carefully agnostic about whether there's some real necessitating power "in the objects" that we just can't access. What really sold me on there being a substantive difference is that the two views land on opposite sides when it comes to miracles: Ghazali's whole setup is basically designed to keep miracles live at every instant, while Hume runs the exact same "no necessary connection, just custom" analysis in "Of Miracles" to argue we should almost never believe testimony that one occurred. So could you actually walk through this comparison properly for me -- not the standard "look, both are skeptics about necessary connection" gloss, but really dig into whether the resemblance is doing real philosophical work, or whether it's more superficial than it looks once you notice "custom" is playing totally different roles in the two pictures. I want an actual verdict out of this, not a both-sides list.
