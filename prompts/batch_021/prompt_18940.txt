I'm drafting a dissertation chapter on the metaphysical costs of Molinism, and before committing more space to it I want to pressure-test my own grasp of the dialectic surrounding the grounding objection. Please write a dialogue between a committed Molinist and a philosopher pressing the grounding objection in the spirit of Robert Adams, as they work through a single concrete counterfactual of creaturely freedom -- for instance, that a particular defendant would freely accept a plea deal from one prosecutor but freely reject an identical deal from another. The Molinist should explain how such a counterfactual can be true, contingent, and settled prior to any divine decree (i.e., prevolitionally) without anything serving as its truthmaker, while the objector should press on whether "brute, groundless contingency" is a coherent category to invoke here or merely a label for the problem rather than a solution to it. Each side should engage the other's strongest formulation rather than a simplified one, and the dialogue should end with them isolating exactly where their residual disagreement lies: is it a substantive metaphysical dispute about what grounding requires, or does it ultimately reduce to a disagreement about who bears the burden of proof?
