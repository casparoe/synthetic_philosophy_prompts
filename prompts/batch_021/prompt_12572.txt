I spent something like 35 years running an analytical chemistry lab before I retired, and these days I mostly split my time between a vegetable patch and a stack of philosophy books I never had time for during my career. This summer I compared a fish emulsion fertilizer against a synthetic all-purpose one on my tomatoes, split across the sunny bed out front and the shadier one out back. Overall the synthetic clearly did better - something like 70% of those plants gave a good crop versus maybe 55% for the fish emulsion. But my neighbor, who helped me keep track of it all, pointed out that the fish emulsion actually wins if you look at the sunny bed alone, and it wins in the shady bed too. I'll admit I wasnt too careful about which plants got which - thinking back, I probably put the fish emulsion on more of the shady-bed plants since I'd read it was gentler on stressed starts. Now my neighbor insists the bed-by-bed numbers are what actually matter and I should switch to fish emulsion entirely next year, but tossing out the overall figure that fast doesnt sit right with me. So who's actually right, and is there a real principled reason to trust one of these numbers over the other, or is it just a matter of taste which one to believe?
