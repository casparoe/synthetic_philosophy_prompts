For my formal epistemology seminar we've been doing the Sleeping Beauty problem, and it spun into a side-argument in my study group that went further than the usual halfer/thirder split we covered in class. Quick recap in case: Beauty's put to sleep Sunday, a fair coin is flipped, if it's heads she's woken once (Monday) and the experiment ends, if it's tails she's woken Monday AND Tuesday but given an amnesia drug Sunday night so Tuesday feels exactly like Monday. Every time she wakes she's asked "what's your credence that the coin landed heads?"

Here's how it went between two people in my group, Theo and Nadia (neither budged, and honestly I couldn't tell who was winning):

(1) Theo: it's 1/3. Right now there are three equally possible "awakenings" she could be having -- heads-Monday, tails-Monday, tails-Tuesday -- she has no info telling her which one she's in, so by indifference she spreads her credence evenly across the three, and heads only owns one of them.

(2) Nadia: that's too fast. Tails-Monday and tails-Tuesday aren't two separate chances for the coin to have come up tails, they're one toss described twice. Beauty hasn't learned anything since Sunday that actually bears on the coin, so her credence should sit right where the objective chance was: 1/2.

(3) Theo: ok, then act like it. Say every waking she's offered a bet that's only fair at 1/2 odds, and she takes it every single time she's asked. She'll lose money in expectation compared to someone betting the tails-favoring odds, purely because tails gives her two shots at the bet and heads gives her one. So whatever number she says out loud, her wallet is a thirder.

(4) Nadia: the odds shifting to 2/3 doesn't prove her credence was secretly 1/3 the whole time -- it proves that once how many times you're offered a bet depends on the very outcome you're betting on, betting quotients stop being a clean readout of credence. That's a general wrinkle in these self-locating setups, not a confession.

(5) Theo: then what's credence even doing if it can float completely free of what she'll pay for a bet? "my credence is 1/2" starts sounding like a slogan with no cash value.

(6) Nadia: it's not free-floating, it just needs the payment scheme matched to what's being tested. Pay her once for the whole experiment, however many times shes woken, and her fair odds go straight back to 1/2, no fudge factor. What odds make a bet "fair" depends on the payout structure -- that's just decision theory, not a patch bolted on to save halfing.
