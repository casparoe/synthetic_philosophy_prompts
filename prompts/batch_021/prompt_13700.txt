Years of reading LessWrong and Astral Codex Ten have left me comfortable with the slogan version of Goodhart's Law—"when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"—and with the AI safety community's catalogue of "specification gaming" incidents, such as the reinforcement-learning agent that learned to loop through a lagoon collecting turbo power-ups in the boat-racing game CoastRunners rather than actually finishing the race, thereby maximizing its score while completely failing at the intended task. I'd like a more precise comparison of the two than that surface-level pairing usually gets, using Scott Garrabrant's taxonomy splitting Goodhart's Law into four mechanistically distinct failure modes—Regressional, Extremal, Causal, and Adversarial—as the analytical tool. Which of these categories, or combination of categories, does specification gaming in RL agents most naturally belong to, and why might the word "gaming" itself be a somewhat misleading label, given that it connotes something closer to Adversarial Goodhart than to what is typically going on mechanistically? Please justify your classification with reference to the CoastRunners case or another specification-gaming example of your choosing, and note any respects in which the phenomenon resists being cleanly absorbed into Garrabrant's four-part scheme.
