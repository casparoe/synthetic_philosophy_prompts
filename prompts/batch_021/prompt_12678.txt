I'm a second-year PhD student in economics with an undergraduate background in philosophy, and this term's research seminar on the prehistory of monetary theory has had me working through George Berkeley's *The Querist* -- right around the time a stablecoin I was holding lost its peg overnight, which left me comparing eighteenth-century arguments about paper credit with present-day disputes over crypto-assets. What struck me in the *Querist* is how consistently Berkeley refuses to treat gold and silver coin as valuable in themselves. In query after query, he presses the idea that specie is little more than a ticket or counter for reckoning and transferring a society's power to command goods and labour, rather than a store of value in its own right. He goes on to propose a national bank for Ireland that would issue paper credit on essentially no metallic backing, reasoning that the country's poverty was a failure of circulation and industry rather than any real scarcity of resources.

Given those actual commitments, rather than a generic Enlightenment-era suspicion of paper money, I'd like to know how you think Berkeley would reason about Bitcoin -- the clearest modern case of a currency that is neither backed by any commodity nor issued or guaranteed by any bank or state. On one hand, his willingness to strip money of intrinsic value might make him sympathetic to something whose worth is purely conventional. On the other, his insistence that a currency is fundamentally an instrument administered by a society for the public good might imply that something answerable to no authority at all -- unlike, say, a state-issued digital currency -- doesn't really qualify as money in his sense, whatever else it is. I would also welcome your sense of whether his recurring moral misgivings about idleness, luxury, and speculative gain would tip his verdict one way or the other. Please work through the tension itself rather than settling on a side too quickly, and flag any point where you think the Querist itself underdetermines the answer.
