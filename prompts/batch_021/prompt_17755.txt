At a monthly forecasting meetup I attend — the sort of group that runs calibration games and tracks its members' Brier scores — two regulars, Mira and Devon, got into a disagreement that neither has let go of. Mira had assigned a 70% credence to a specific transit bill passing that week's city council vote. (1) Devon objected that a council vote happens exactly once, so "70%" isn't a frequency of anything, and he doubted the number meant more than a mood dressed up in decimals. (2) Mira replied that meaningfulness doesn't require repetition — her credence only has to be coherent, in the sense that she'd be willing to bet at those odds on either side, and coherence is what makes a probability real. (3) Devon countered that this dodges the question of warrant: the only reason either of them should trust her 70% over some other number is that, across her past forecasts, the things she's called "70% likely" have come true roughly 70% of the time — so her credence earns its keep by quietly cashing out in frequencies after all. (4) Mira answered that her calibration record shows she's a reliable forecaster in general, but it can't tell them why 70%, rather than 55% or 85%, was correct for this bill specifically, since the vote belongs at once to many possible reference classes — transit measures, election-year ballot items, this council's past votes — each with a different historical pass rate, and no amount of calibration data picks out which class matters here. (5) Devon then suggested she was really tracking something like a propensity: an objective tendency of the current political situation to produce a "yes," the way a loaded die has a propensity to land a certain way even before it is rolled. (6) Mira replied that a die's propensity is at least anchored in a stable, repeatable physical setup, and it's not obvious a one-time human vote has anything analogous, so invoking propensity here just renames the mystery rather than resolving it.

Go through moves (1) through (6) and say who has the better of it at each step.
