A close friend and I spent the better part of two hours arguing over dinner a couple of nights ago about something that began innocuously enough and ended up somewhere neither of us expected. The conversation started with family size—how many children each of us might eventually want. I said I would rather have one or two and be able to give them everything I can offer: my full attention, financial security, and every opportunity to flourish. My friend, who grew up as one of six siblings and hopes for an equally large family of her own, pushed back. Her argument, in essence, was that even if each of her children ended up somewhat less well provided for than mine—less individual attention, fewer resources to go around—there would be so many more of them living genuinely worthwhile lives that the total amount of wellbeing she brought into the world would exceed mine. Toward the end of the evening, half-provocatively, she extended her own reasoning further: if more children with slightly diminished lives really can outweigh fewer children with excellent ones, why stop there? Why, considered impartially, shouldn't the best family simply be an enormous one, even if every child's life were only barely worth living, provided there were enough of them to outweigh a handful of flourishing children?

I found that extended conclusion clearly wrong, but every time I tried to pinpoint exactly where her reasoning broke down, I ended up relying on some claim that felt just as arbitrary as the one I was objecting to. I have a vague memory, from a single philosophy course I took years ago, that dilemmas of this shape have an established name and a substantial literature behind them, though I could not tell you a single further detail. I would like to understand what is actually going on in this disagreement: is my friend's original position a recognizable one with genuine defenders, does the extreme version really follow from it, and is there a principled place to draw a line between the two—or does every attempt to draw such a line just end up recreating the same kind of problem somewhere else?
