Ok, weird one, but it's been bugging me since last night's fight. My partner and I have this recurring argument about our arguments: whenever we disagree about something practical -- money, chores, how often to see his parents -- I get a very specific feeling of being completely, bone-deep certain I'm right. Over about a decade together we've noticed that when I get THAT exact feeling, I'm actually right only about half the time -- we kept score for a few months once out of morbid curiosity, and it came out basically at chance. His view is that once I know this about myself, its just irrational to keep acting on that feeling going forward, full stop. Here's the part that actually confuses me: I don't even disagree with him in the abstract -- the stats are the stats, and I'll admit the feeling in general is basically noise -- but in the middle of an actual disagreement, like tonight, it still comes on just as strong as ever, and overriding my read of THIS argument because of a stat about a bunch of other arguments feels almost as crazy as trusting it blindly would. So which is the irrational one here, sticking with the feeling in the moment, or claiming to believe the track record and then not acting like it? (And yes, I'm aware of the irony of an epistemology professor asking this.)
