My mother, a philosopher who spent forty years at an evangelical Christian university defending Molinism, held that God's middle knowledge of the "counterfactuals of creaturely freedom" — truths about what every possible free creature would do in any circumstance — lets him providentially weave history together without ever determining anyone's will. She liked to say that God knew, before the foundation of the world, that my father would propose to her under the oak tree behind her childhood home, but that God had not made him do it. She would just as readily correct anyone who reached for "everything happens for a reason" in a way that sounded fatalistic. I am giving her eulogy at a service that will include my father's Reformed relatives, who expect language about God's sovereign plan, and several of her former doctoral students, who will notice if her actual position collapses into either simple foreknowledge or outright determinism. Please write a five-minute eulogy that comforts mourners who have never heard the phrase "middle knowledge," without saying anything my mother would have considered a misstatement of the view she spent her career defending.
