I've always been fond of the old writerly cliché that a novelist is a kind of minor deity presiding over her own small universe, but a problem that surfaced while drafting my current novel has made me take the comparison more seriously, and rather more uncomfortably, than I ever intended to. Like most novelists, I construct detailed histories and temperaments for my major characters precisely so that their later actions feel earned rather than arbitrary: a reader should sense that when my character Elena betrays her sister partway through the book, she does so because of a particular slight at a family wedding, compounded by a long-standing pattern of quiet resentment, and not merely because the plot required a betrayal at that point. When I described this worry to my writing group, they thought I was overcomplicating a non-issue, but I haven't been able to let it go. In the actual moment of drafting such a scene, none of that carefully built backstory really constrains my hand: I could, without introducing any inconsistency into the world of the book, have Elena forgive her sister instead, since I am equally the one who decided what form her resentment would take, and equally free to decide, right now, that matters resolve differently than the earlier chapters seem to demand.

The more I sit with this, the more it seems to me that the sense of one event following inevitably from another, which I labor over and privately take some pride in, may be less a real chain forged by the earlier events themselves than a habit I instill in my readers, and in myself over successive drafts, simply by writing consistently. After all, both the "reason" and the "result" are just separate decisions of mine, and each one, at every point in the manuscript, answers to nothing beyond my own say-so at that moment. This matters to me beyond craft pride, because it opens onto a harder question I can't dismiss: if nothing in a character genuinely produces her later choices in this strong sense, I am no longer sure what it would mean to say she has anything like her own agency on the page, rather than my simply narrating, moment to moment, decisions that happen to be entirely mine. I would be glad for help thinking through whether this worry is ultimately confused, or whether there is something real in it, and, if the latter, what it should change about how I understand the little word "because" that I keep reaching for whenever I explain my own plots.
