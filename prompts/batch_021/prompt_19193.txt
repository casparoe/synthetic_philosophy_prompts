Ok so last night me and my friend Theo got into a two hour argument about laws of nature that started over dinner and finished on the sidewalk outside the restaurant, and I want to write up my side of it properly, but wanted a sanity check before I sink more time into it.

Theo was defending something like Armstrong's view: laws aren't just patterns, they're extra necessitation relations, N(F,G), holding between universals, and "all F's are G's" falls out of that as a kind of corollary. My pushback was basically two objections I associate with van Fraassen (I might be mangling the exact labels, something like the "inference problem" and the "identification problem"): first, it's not clear how a relation between two universals is even supposed to entail anything about first-order particulars — you can apparently hold N(F,G) fixed and still coherently imagine some F that isn't G, so the necessitating isn't actually being done. Second, even granting the entailment, calling N "necessitation" looks like a label glued onto a primitive relation we don't otherwise understand, rather than something we've independently verified deserves the name.

Theo's comeback was that my own Humean, best-system view has the mirror problem: if laws are just theorems of whichever deductive system best balances simplicity and strength over the whole Humean mosaic, the law can't explain any individual instance, since you'd be explaining a fact partly in virtue of itself. At least his relation is something distinct from what it necessitates.

I tried answering that the explanandum (one instance) and explanans (the whole best system) aren't identical, so it's not literal circularity, just "non-fundamental but still real" explanation, sort of like what I remember of Loewer's response to this kind of charge. Even saying it out loud I wasn't fully convinced, especially for a toy world where the best system is just one generalization barely removed from the raw facts.

Is my version of the anti-Humean objection to Armstrong fair, does my Loewer-ish comeback to Theo actually survive, and is there a standard move from this literature we're both clearly missing?
