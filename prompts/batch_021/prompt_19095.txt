I'm the student rep on my college's student life committee, and we've been told to send the Dean of Students an actual recommendation before next week's meeting about what to do with an anonymous "confessions" account that keeps posting screenshots and callouts naming specific students. It escalated a few weeks ago when a first-year got dogpiled with something like 800 comments over a dumb thing she said in a group chat, and she's now on a leave of absence. The options actually on the table are: pressure the app/account to require real names on any post that names a specific person, put out a statement condemning it without restricting anything, or set up some kind of reporting/mediation channel instead of touching the account at all.

Heres the complication. One of the faculty members on the committee works on Kierkegaard and keeps derailing things by saying we've diagnosed the problem wrong, that this isn't fundamentally an anonymity issue at all. He keeps bringing up the "present age" essay, the bit about "leveling" and "the public" as this abstraction that nobody's actually accountable for, and he points out that Kierkegaard's own worst mauling by the press -- the Corsair business, where he got turned into a running joke on the streets of Copenhagen for months -- was done by a paper everybody could name, not some anonymous account. So on his view, "just require real names" might not touch whatever mechanism Kierkegaard actually thought was doing the damage, and could even just repackage it. Half the committee thinks this is an interesting tangent with no bearing on next week's deadline; the other half thinks it means our whole anonymity-vs-accountability framing is confused and we should slow down. Can you draft the memo I'd actually bring to the meeting? I need it to land on one concrete recommendation with a timeline that the Dean could sign off on, and I want it to seriously work through what follows if the Kierkegaard point is right, rather than either waving it off or letting it become an excuse to do nothing.
