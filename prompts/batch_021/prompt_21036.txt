Here's roughly what I wrote, lightly cleaned up: Condorcet's theorem says that if you have a bunch of jurors who are each individually more likely than not to reach the right verdict (over 50% accurate, and independent of each other), then a simple majority vote among them gets more reliable the more jurors you add, approaching 100% certainty as the number of jurors goes to infinity. This is basically identical to why random forests work: each tree is a "juror" thats individually a little better than a coin flip, they all vote, and the crowd ends up smarter than any single tree. The reason random forests randomly subsample features per tree instead of just using the same features every time is to force the trees to be independent-ish, which is exactly the condition Condorcet's proof needs. Boosting is the same idea again, just with weighted votes instead of one-vote-each. In practice our ensembles never actually hit 100% no matter how many models we throw at it, but I figure that's just because you can never get true independence between models trained on overlapping data, so we're stuck with some watered down version of the theorem instead of the full guarantee.
