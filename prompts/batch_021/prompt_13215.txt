And here is Priya's answer, reproduced word for word:

"The simple conditional analysis (SCA) says that an object x is disposed at time t to give response r to stimulus s if and only if x would give r if x underwent s at t. For example, 'the glass is fragile' is supposed to mean the same thing as 'the glass would break if it were struck.'

A finkish disposition is a case where the very undergoing of the stimulus causes the disposition itself to switch on or off, so that the conditional and the actual disposition come apart. For example, imagine a soldier who is, by temperament, a coward — he is genuinely disposed to flee whenever he faces danger. Now suppose this soldier is secretly shadowed by a battlefield hypnotist who, the instant real danger appears, hypnotizes him into total fearlessness for as long as the danger lasts. The soldier still has the underlying disposition to be a coward, but it is never true that 'if danger appeared, he would flee,' because the disposition is switched off at exactly the moment it would otherwise be triggered. This is a counterexample to the SCA because it shows the disposition can be present even though the corresponding conditional is false. This strategy — a device that intervenes exactly at the moment of the stimulus — is what gives finks their name, after the live-wire case Lewis used to introduce the problem.

A masked (or antidotal) disposition is different: nothing switches the underlying disposition on or off at all. Instead, some other factor intervenes further down the causal chain, after the stimulus has occurred, and stops the manifestation from occurring. For instance, someone with a severe peanut allergy is disposed to have an allergic reaction if they eat peanuts. Suppose they take a daily antihistamine that completely blocks any allergic response. They are still allergic — their immune system is still sensitized to peanut proteins in exactly the way it was before — but it is no longer true that eating a peanut would make them react. This is a genuinely different problem from finking, because the disposition's basis, the sensitized immune system, never changes; only the pathway from stimulus to manifestation is interrupted.

Lewis's response to finking is to revise the analysis so that x is disposed to give r in response to s just in case x has some intrinsic causal basis B at t such that x's undergoing s while retaining B would be a complete cause of r. Requiring the basis to be retained throughout is exactly what rules out finks, since a fink works by destroying or creating the basis at the critical moment. Because this reformed analysis only cares about the basis persisting and builds in no assumption about what happens afterward, it ends up covering masking automatically as well: in the allergy case, the immune sensitization persists untouched the whole time, so Lewis's conditional is satisfied and the analysis correctly still calls the person allergic. Once the analysis is anchored to the persistence of the basis rather than to the outcome, finks and masks turn out to be two versions of a single underlying problem, and Lewis's one fix disposes of both."
