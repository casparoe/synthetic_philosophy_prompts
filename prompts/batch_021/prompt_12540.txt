Here's where I've gotten stuck. I get that the classic Carter-Leslie version updates your beliefs about how many humans will ever exist by treating your own birth rank as basically a random draw from that total, and that this is really just an application of what Bostrom calls the Self-Sampling Assumption. But then I read that the Self-Indication Assumption is supposed to cancel that update out almost exactly, because it front-loads extra prior weight onto "big population" hypotheses in a way that offsets the doomsday-favoring shift -- and separately, that SIA has its own problem in the "presumptuous philosopher" case, where it lets you out-bet a whole room of physicists on a cosmology experiment before its even run, just by counting up hypothetical observers. That last bit smells like a reductio to me, but I honestly can't tell if rejecting SIA on those grounds rescues the original doomsday-style pessimism or just leaves both positions equally undermined.

So: could you lay out the most important implications of this whole SSA/SIA fork for how seriously I should take the doomsday argument's conclusion that our species probably doesn't have all that much time left?
