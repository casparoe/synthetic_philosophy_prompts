Ok so I live in a small lake community that's basically self-governed by a property owners association, and there's an argument that's been simmering for a couple summers now that I genuinely cant stop stop thinking about. We've got 45 houses total. About half are lived in full time by people like me. The other half are owned by "seasonal" folks who show up for maybe 8-10 weekends a year, and a handful of those don't even come themselves anymore, they just run the place as an Airbnb all summer and collect the money. On top of that there's a couple houses owned by people who live somewhere else entirely and rent long-term to a tenant who actually lives here year round, same as me, except that tenant obviously isn't on the deed. And then down by the old mill there's 6 more houses that got carved out of the association decades ago for some reason nobody remembers, so they pay no dues and get no vote, but they still use our private road every single day since it's the only way in or out for them too.

Right now the bylaw is dead simple: one vote per house, at the annual meeting, owners only, doesnt matter if you live here 300 days a year or just show up for a handful of weekends. At the last meeting my neighbor Gary argued it should be based on who actually deals with the consequences day to day, which would mean the long-term tenant gets a vote and maybe the Airbnb investor doesn't. Someone else said no, dues are what pay for the road and the lawyer and the snowplow guy every winter, so it should just track who's actually funding the place. A third guy said it should be residency, full stop, which would boot the seasonal owners out entirely even though a couple of them have been coming here longer than I've been alive.

I brought it up with a couple friends afterward hoping for some kind of tiebreaker and they just disagreed with each other too. I don't know the first thing about political theory or whatever the actual term for this is, I just can't shake the feeling that everyone involved, including me, was kind of reverse-engineering whichever rule happened to benefit them personally and then dressing it up as a principle. Is there an actual non-arbitrary way to sort out who should get a say in something like this, or is that basically a fantasy and it always just comes down to whoever already has enough votes to write the rule first?
