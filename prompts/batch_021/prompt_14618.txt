Give an example of a case in which epistemic contextualism and pragmatic encroachment about knowledge (also known as subject-sensitive invariantism) deliver opposite verdicts about whether a subject knows a given proposition. The case should be distinct from the standard first-person bank cases that Keith DeRose uses to motivate contextualism, and should specify the subject's evidence, the subject's practical stakes, and the conversational context of whoever is attributing or denying knowledge to the subject. Explain precisely why each view yields the verdict it does, and why the standard bank cases do not, by themselves, distinguish between the two views.
