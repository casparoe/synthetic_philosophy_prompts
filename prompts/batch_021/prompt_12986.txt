At a work lunch, a coworker brought up Edward Nelson, the Princeton mathematician who announced in 2011 that he'd proved Peano arithmetic (ordinary number theory) was inconsistent after twenty-five years of work on it, then retracted the claim within days once Terence Tao and Daniel Tausk independently found the same flaw in it. It set off an argument between two other coworkers, Marcus and Dana, that I mostly just watched.

Marcus opened by saying the episode shows our confidence in the consistency of PA, or ZFC, is basically unearned: Gödel's second incompleteness theorem says no system strong enough for arithmetic can prove its own consistency, so believing these axioms are safe is closer to faith than knowledge -- no different from trusting that production code is bug-free just because it hasn't crashed yet. Dana objected that Gentzen actually proved arithmetic's consistency, back in 1936 -- a real theorem, not faith. Marcus replied that Gentzen's proof just assumes the consistency of a different system, built on transfinite induction up to a specific infinite ordinal (called epsilon-zero), which by that same theorem can't certify itself either, and isn't even obviously the "safer" choice, since it's neither weaker nor stronger than ordinary arithmetic, just different. He brought up Nelson again: a mathematician spent twenty-five years on an inconsistency proof that took Terence Tao mere days to find the hole in -- not what a settled question looks like.

Dana said the regress objection proves too much, since no system of deduction can certify itself from outside without circularity, and nobody treats that as grounds for general suspicion. She read the Nelson story the opposite way, too: the flaw was found fast and independently by two people, which shows the checking process works rather than that it's fragile. She also said Marcus had been sliding between arithmetic and set theory as though the case for each were identical, when PA at least has Gentzen's proof behind it and ZFC has nothing comparable. Marcus agreed that distinction was fair, but said it only made him more nervous about ZFC, not less, and fell back on history: Frege's system looked secure for years, in print and unchallenged, until a short, almost embarrassingly simple argument brought it down. He didn't see what rules out the same thing happening to PA or ZFC today, other than that it hasn't yet.

Could you go through this move by move, say who has the better of it at each point, and note what, if anything, you think they're both missing?
