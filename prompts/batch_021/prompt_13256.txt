So I fell down a rabbit hole this week that started with one of those old LessWrong-adjacent posts on Jeffrey conditionalization and radical probabilism, which linked out in a footnote to the Judy Benjamin problem, and now I can't stop poking at it. Setup for anyone who doesn't remember: Judy's dropped somewhere in a region split into four equally likely cells -- Red/Headquarters-Co, Red/Second-Co, Blue/Headquarters-Co, Blue/Second-Co, each starting at 1/4 -- and then she gets a garbled radio message saying that conditional on being in Red territory, shes 3x more likely to be in the Second Company area than the Headquarters one. Fine so far. What I can't get my head around is why the "standard" move -- minimizing relative information (cross-entropy) relative to her prior, subject to that one conditional constraint -- ends up shifting her odds on the *Blue* side too, so the Headquarters/Second split there stops being 50/50, even though the message said literally nothing about Blue. That just feels like it should be a screened-off, irrelevant chunk of probability space, and yet it isn't.

I get that ordinary Jeffrey conditionalization doesn't straightforwardly apply here, since the evidence isn't "here's your new probability over a partition" but a conditional probability statement, which is apparently why people reach for generalizations like minimum cross-entropy or Lewis-style imaging in the first place -- and those two reportedly don't even agree with each other on this example. What I want isn't just the Lagrangian-turn-the-crank derivation, I can follow that part fine, it's more like the kind of gut-level intuition people give for why Arrow's theorem holds despite the technical proof -- except here for why irrelevant-seeming info ends up mattering under cross-entropy updating, whether imaging dodges the wierdness or just relocates it somewhere else, and whether any of this should push me toward thinking there's no principled unique answer to "how do you update on a conditional" at all.
