So a few weeks ago I went down this rabbit hole on possible worlds and multiverse stuff and I stumbled onto this philosopher David Lewis and his whole "modal realism" thing, and honestly it's been living in my head rent free ever since. I always figured "possible worlds" was just a fancy way of saying "hypothetically, this could've happened," like, a way of talking, nothing more. But apparently Lewis actually meant it literally: every way a world could possibly be IS a real, concrete world, just as real as ours, sitting out there completely cut off from us in space and time, with zero causal contact. And "actual" doesn't mean special or central, it just means "the one we happen to be in," kind of like how "here" doesn't refer to some cosmically privileged spot, it just means wherever the speaker is standing. He even admits in his book that most peoples reaction to this isn't really an argument, its just what he calls an "incredulous stare," and he basically says fine, stare all you want, the theory still earns its keep by doing more explanatory work than the alternatives.

What really got me was two things that kind of gut-punch each other. First there's this comforting angle, like, somewhere out there a version of me did take that job, or did talk to that person, so nothing is really "wasted," every road not taken got taken by someone. But then I read further and found out that's not even right, because of this "counterpart theory" bit - the version of me over there isn't actually me, its just some other guy who happens to resemble me a lot, a totally seperate person. So I don't even get to console myself that "I" succeeded somewhere, its a stranger who looks like me from the outside. Second, if literally every possible way things could go already happens somewhere, some version of every war, every cure, every kindness, every atrocity, then what's the actual point of trying to make good stuff happen "here" instead of bad stuff, since both are happening regardless, just in a different zip code of reality. I think some philosopher (Adams? Adam-something? found it referenced somewhere) already threw this exact objection at Lewis back in the day, which made me feel a little less crazy for landing on it myself.

Anyway what I'd love is if you could write me a short story that actually sits inside this mess instead of being another "portal to a parallel universe" story, since apparently that's not even how Lewis's worlds are supposed to work, you can't visit them or send them a postcard, there's no door. Maybe center it on one totally ordinary person who, for whatever reason, actually comes to believe modal realism is literally true and has to live with it - what that does to how they grieve something, or make a big decision, or try to feel less alone, whichever direction feels right to you. I just want the story to genuinely wrestle with the incredulous-stare/counterpart/does-any-of-it-matter stuff rather than just being a vibes-based "many worlds" story that could've been written by someone who's never heard of Lewis at all.
