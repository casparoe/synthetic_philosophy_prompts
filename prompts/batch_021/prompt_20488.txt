My friend Nadia's getting married in a few weeks and asked me to give the toast, since I'm the novelist in the friend group and apparently that makes me contractually obligated. Here's the wrinkle: she and her fiance Cole are non-monogamous, and its not incidental -- they actually met in a Bertrand Russell reading group, and the whole shape of their relationship is built on Marriage and Morals, especially his bit about jealousy being learned rather than some fixed instinct. They want the toast to actually engage with that, not just wave vaguely at "open-mindedness," but Cole's parents and my own grandma will be sitting right there with no idea any of this is going on, expecting a normal toast about commitment. What's nagging at me on top of that is that Russell's own open marriage to Dora fell apart on more or less the exact point he thought he'd philosophically solved -- he admitted later just how much jealousy he actually felt in practice -- so I don't want to hold him up as the guy who cracked it, but I don't want to be a downer at somebody's wedding either. Can you draft a warm five-minute toast that's honest about all that, works for the friends who'll clock it if I'm faking the Russell, and still lands fine for grandma?
