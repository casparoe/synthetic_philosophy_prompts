So I've been going down a rabbit hole on the effective altruism forum and stumbled on this idea called "evidential cooperation in large worlds" (ECL) - I think it used to be called "multiverse-wide cooperation via correlated decision making" back when the original guy (Oesterheld?) wrote it up, but don't quote me on that. I'm a swe, not a philosopher, so I wrote my own explanation of it below just to check if i actually understand it, plus two objections i thought of. Could you go through it and tell me:

a) where am i getting the actual argument wrong (if anywhere)
b) whether my objections are already well-known responses that people in this space have dealt with, or if they're actually a problem
c) honestly, is this even an interesting idea or am i just being nerd-sniped by something with no teeth

Here's the writeup:

---

**Cooperating with people you'll never meet, in universes you'll never see**

The universe (maybe the multiverse, if that's your thing) is really really big, possibly infinite. So there's a ton of other decision-making agents out there we can never causally touch - too far away, maybe literally in a different Everett branch or bubble universe. Normal intuition: "who cares, I can't affect them and they can't affect me, so what I decide about their values doesn't matter."

ECL's move is: not so fast. Some slice of those agents reason kind of like we do - not because we're causally hooked up, but because similar minds grinding on the same decision problem tend to land on similar answers. Kinda like how two engineers who never met but came up through the same CS fundamentals will independently write suspiciously similar code for the same problem. If my reasoning process correlates with theirs, then my decision is *evidence* about their decision, zero causal channel needed. So if I choose to spend some of my resources on behalf of value systems totally unlike mine (say, whatever a weird alien civilization would want), that's evidence that alien-me's out there are doing the same thing for creatures with my values. Positive-sum trade with no causation, just correlation plus evidential decision theory (EDT).

Two things bug me about this:

1. This smells a lot like Newcomb's problem, except there's no predictor and it's not really two players - it's more like everyone everywhere is simultaneously deciding and hoping they're correlated with enough of the "right" other agents. Is that even the right reference class, or am I mashing together things that don't actually belong together?

2. The whole argument seems to *need* EDT to basically be correct, at least for this kind of case. But EDT has its own famous problem cases (smoking lesion, etc). If I'm suspicious of EDT generally, does that suspicion have to carry over and sink ECL too, or is there a principled way to accept the ECL argument without buying EDT as a general theory of rational choice?

Bonus objection: even granting all of the above, we have basically zero info on what these other agents actually value, so doesn't this just cash out to "be vaguely more cooperative and impartial in general" - advice that didn't need a multiverse argument to justify?

---

Let me know what you think, especially on 1 and 2 - those feel like the real technical crux to me and not just a vibes objection.
