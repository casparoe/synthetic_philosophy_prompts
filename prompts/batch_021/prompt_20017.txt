For the "joke of the month" segment in my school's philosophy club newsletter, I want to write something on causation by omission that would actually land with someone who has thought about the topic, rather than a generic "philosophers arguing about nothing" bit. What I keep getting stuck on is the problem this raises for counterfactual theories of causation: if my failing to water my neighbor's plants while she's away counts as a cause of their death, since they wouldn't have died had I watered them, then it seems every other person who also didn't water them — and who would equally have kept them alive by doing so — counts just as much as a cause. Could you write a short joke whose punchline actually turns on this proliferation of "causes" by omission, rather than being a generic nerd joke?
