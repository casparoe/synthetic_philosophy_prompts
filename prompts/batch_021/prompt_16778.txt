So I went down a Hacker News rabbit hole a while back and ran into this axiom-of-choice puzzle that I genuinely can't let go of. Setup: countably infinitely many prisoners, numbered 1, 2, 3, and so on forever. Each one gets a hat with some real number painted on it, literally any real, chosen however adversarially you like. Every prisoner can see every other prisoners hat but not their own, there's no communicating once the hats go on, but beforehand they get to agree on a strategy. The claim is that using AC, they can pick a strategy that guarantees only finitely many of them guess their own number wrong, no matter what the assignment turns out to be. The trick (I think) is: define two hat-sequences as "equivalent" if they only differ in finitely many spots, use choice to lock in one representative sequence for every equivalence class ahead of time, and then each prisoner guesses whatever the representative says their number should be, based on the class they can work out from everyone else's hats. I tried explaining this to a friend doing a math PhD and even she needed a minute before just going "yeah, that's AC for you," which honestly didnt make me feel any less crazy.

I've only got one semester of real analysis under my belt, so I know AC in the boring "you can grab one sock out of every pair in an infinite drawer of sock pairs" sense, but this hat thing feels like a totally different animal, like it's manufacturing real information about a number a prisoner has never seen, out of nothing. Could you walk me through, on a gut level, why this actually works and isn't secretly a contradiction? Specifically I'm stuck on this: if the hats were handed out via genuinely random, independent draws, each prisoner's own number should be statistically uncorrelated with anything they can observe, so how does "almost everyone guesses right" not step on that independence? Is the honest resolution basically "the representative-picking function is so non-measurable that 'probability of success' stops meaning anything," or is that a dodge? I'd like something that actually pins down where the weirdness lives instead of just shrugging and saying AC is non-constructive.
