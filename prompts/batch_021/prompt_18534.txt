A friend and I spent much of last night arguing about the Alignment Research Center's "eliciting latent knowledge" (ELK) problem, specifically about whether the distinction between a "direct translator" and a "human simulator" reporter is really principled. Her view was that, since both are defined purely by matching human labels wherever those labels are available, there is in the worst case no non-arbitrary criterion for preferring one over the other short of already knowing the ground truth -- so the distinction just restates the difficulty of ELK rather than resolving anything. I disagreed, though I couldn't fully articulate why. Please make the strongest case that the direct-translator/human-simulator distinction is doing genuine theoretical work: that there is a principled basis for it beyond mere behavior on the training distribution, and specify what that basis is.
