so a while back I fell down a rabbit hole starting from the ship of theseus thing and ended up reading about the "statue and the clay" version -- you squish a lump of clay into a statue, and now theres a statue and a lump sitting in the exact same spot, made of the exact same atoms, and apparently philosophers are genuinely split on whether that's one object or two. could you write me a dialogue between two people standing right next to the thing on the sculpting stand, arguing about it?

one of them is team "obviously it's one object, why multiply things for no reason, the statue just IS the clay, two names for the same stuff." the other pushes the leibniz's law move -- the lump was sitting around as a shapeless blob before the statue existed, and it could survive being squashed back into a ball while the statue couldn't, so they don't share all their properties and can't be strictly identical. don't let the one-object person just get flattened by that, i want them to have an actual comeback, and don't let the two-object person off easy either -- make them answer for what their view implies, like are there really two seperate objects that each weigh the full weight, both sitting in that exact same spot, and if someone chucks the statue through a window, did the lump break it too or does it weirdly get off scot-free. neither one should just win outright. end it with the two of them trying to flatly answer how many things are on the stand right now, one or two, and see if they can even agree.
