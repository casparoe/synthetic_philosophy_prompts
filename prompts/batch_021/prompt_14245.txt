So this happened in the car after a family therapy session and it's been stuck in my head ever since. The therapist told my brother and sister that our family's fights aren't really about any one person, it's a "system" we all participate in that keeps the pattern going (dad drinks, mom covers for him, the rest of us go quiet or pick a side). They argued about it the whole ride home:

(1) Nate said that's just a fancy way of letting everyone off the hook -- really it's simple, dad drinks, and if he stopped tomorrow 90% of our problems disappear.

(2) Sara said too easy: even if dad quit tomorrow we'd probably all just slide into the same roles with someone else standing in for him, because the pattern isnt inside dad, it's in how the rest of us react to each other.

(3) Nate said thats unfalsifiable -- you can't point to "the pattern," you can only point to what actual people do, and the pattern is just a name for the sum of everyone's choices, dad's most of all since he's the one who kicked it off.

(4) Sara said sure, he kicked it off, but a pattern can outlast whatever caused it, same way a habit outlasts its original trigger, so pinning it all on him now kind of misses what's actually keeping it going at this point.

Neither of them budged the whole drive. Who's actually right, at each step, not just overall, and is there something both of them are missing? End with a final line, either \answer{Nate} or \answer{Sara}, whoever you think has the stronger overall view.
