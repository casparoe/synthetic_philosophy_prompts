So we had this incident at work last month where a queue backed up and took down half our service, and the postmortem turned into a genuinely heated argument about whether "nobody set up an alert for queue depth" actually caused the outage, or whether that's just us picking someone to blame after the fact. That sent me down a rabbit hole reading about "causation by omission" in philosophy, and it turns out theres a real, surprisingly rigorous debate about this exact question. The classic example goes something like: your neighbor promises to water your plant while you're on vacation, doesn't, and it dies -- sure, we want to say the neighbor's not-watering caused the death. But it's also technically true that I didn't water your plant, and neither did some random stranger on the other side of the planet, and neither did basically everyone else alive, and the plant also wouldn't have died if any of them had watered it instead. So why isn't literally everyone on Earth a "cause" of your dead plant? From what I can tell, one camp says you need to smuggle in some norm (the neighbor was supposed to do it, the rest of us weren't) to fish the real cause out of that ocean of technically-true counterfactuals, and basically bites the bullet on omissions being genuine causes. The other camp says this whole mess just proves omissions were never real causes to begin with -- there's no physical process, nothing gets transferred, "nothing" can't cause "something" -- and what we're actually doing when we blame the neighbor is more like "quasi-causation" (I've seen that exact phrase somewhere, maybe from Beebee or Dowe, can't remember which) than describing an actual feature of the world.

I'd love to see this properly fought out instead of getting a summary of both sides. Could you write me a dialogue between two philosophers of causation who hold these two views, where they actually go back and forth -- each one responding to the other's specific argument instead of just restating their own position? Don't let either side be a pushover: the "omissions are real causes" person needs an actual answer to the everyone-on-Earth problem, not just "well obviously we mean the neighbor," and the "omissions are only quasi-causes" person has to grapple with the fact that we clearly treat omissions as real grounds for blame all the time -- we discipline people, or call them out by name in a postmortem doc, over stuff they specifically failed to do, not just stuff they actively broke. If it fits naturally, feel free to have one of them bring up something like my queue-alert example partway through. Doesn't need to be super long, maybe a page, but I want actual philosophical combat, not two people just taking turns monologuing.
