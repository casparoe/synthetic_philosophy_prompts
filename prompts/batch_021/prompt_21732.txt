I'm taking a seminar this term on early twentieth-century British ethics, and after we discussed how heavily Chapter VI of Moore's *Principia Ethica* — "The Ideal" — shaped the Bloomsbury Group's sense of what makes life worth living, we spent two more sessions picking apart the chapter's actual argument. What's been generating the most debate in our discussion section is how Moore's claim that the two greatest goods we can conceive are the enjoyment of beautiful objects and personal affection between people who rightly appreciate one another's good qualities of character interacts with two other pieces of his machinery: the method of isolation, which he uses elsewhere in the book (arguing against hedonism) via the case of two equally unwitnessed worlds, one exquisitely beautiful and one thoroughly hideous, to show that beauty carries some intrinsic value even apart from anyone ever perceiving it; and the principle of organic unities, on which a whole consisting of true cognition of something beautiful or good plus a fitting emotional response to it is worth far more than the mere sum of those two parts taken separately. Our TA set us the exercise of extending this framework to invented cases where cognition and emotion come apart, and I still can't settle on a ranking I'm confident Moore himself would sign off on.

Here are the eight cases we're stuck on. Please assess each purely for its own intrinsic value, holding fixed, per the isolation method, that nothing else in the universe is affected by it one way or the other:

A. A vast and intricate world of natural beauty exists for eons and is then annihilated; no mind of any kind ever perceives it, learns of it, or is affected by it.
B. The very same world is glimpsed, moments before its annihilation, by a single observer who forms a true and fittingly intense admiration of its beauty.
C. A counterpart world that is, on Moore's own contrasting case, comprehensively and irredeemably ugly exists for the same span, likewise unperceived by anyone.
D. Two people sustain a lifelong, mutual friendship grounded in an accurate appreciation of each other's honesty and generosity of spirit; the friendship is entirely private and has no further effect on anyone else's welfare.
E. A person feels adoring, warm affection for a partner they sincerely believe to be witty and kind, admiring these supposed qualities intensely — but the partner in fact has neither trait and is privately cruel.
F. A person accurately recognizes an acquaintance's cruelty and manipulativeness and responds to it with intense contempt and resentment.
G. A lonely aesthete spends a lifetime cultivating an exact, discriminating appreciation of beauty in art and nature, but has no friends or loved ones of any kind.
H. A person experiences a rapturous glow of admiration while looking at a genuinely beautiful landscape, where the glow is produced by a pharmacological accident entirely unconnected to any real perception of the landscape's beauty.

Please sort these eight cases into five tiers, from most to least intrinsically valuable, and within each tier order the letters from most to least valuable. Give your answer as a JSON object of exactly this form:

```json
{
  "great_goods": ["B", "..."],
  "minor_goods": ["..."],
  "negligible": ["..."],
  "minor_evils": ["..."],
  "great_evils": ["..."]
}
```

Every letter from A to H should appear exactly once across the five arrays.
