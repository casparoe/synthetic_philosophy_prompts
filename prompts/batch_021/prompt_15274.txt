My grandpa and his best friend Walt have this whole legend in our town about a play at home plate in the summer of '57, back when they were maybe 11 or 12. Walt was the runner, grandpa was catching, and the championship game came down to whether he was safe or out - grandpa says out, Walt always said safe, and nobody else on the field had a clear enough view to settle it. They argued about that exact same call every single 4th of july for something like 70 years and were still best friends the whole time, both of em sharp as tacks, each one always saying the other guy was way too honest to be lying, just wrong. Walt passed away last week and grandpa's giving the eulogy but ask me to help write it out since his hands shake too bad to type anymore. He wants to tell the story honest, including that he still thinks he was right (Walt can't defend himself now so he's not gonna suddenly act like he changed his mind just because), but without it coming off like he's still trying to win, or like almost 70 years of not agreeing means something was wrong with one of them all along. Please don't have him just say they "agreed to disagree" either, that's not really what it was and grandpa says that phrase makes his skin crawl. I read something once about how if two people trust each other completely and saw the exact same thing, they're basically supposed to end up agreeing eventually or one of them's being irrational, but that never felt right to me for these two. Half the town's gonna be at the funeral and they all know "the call" as basically folklore at this point, so it can't be all heavy, it needs a little funny in it too. Can you write it for him?
