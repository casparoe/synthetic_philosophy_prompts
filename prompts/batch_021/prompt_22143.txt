I got into it with someone on a discord server the other night about whether it even makes sense to say a language model "believes" things, and I want a more careful treatment than either of us managed at 1am. The framing I care about specifically is Dennett's intentional stance, not the watered-down pop-philosophy version where "intentional stance" just means "treat it like it has a mind if that's convenient." Dennett's actual bar is stingier than that: the intentional-level description has to buy you real predictive leverage that the design stance (i.e. just knowing the blueprint/algorithm) doesn't already give you for free, and he grades things on a spectrum -- a thermostat gets an extremely marginal, basically eliminable ascription of "belief" once you know how it works, whereas a person doesn't, because nobody's got a tractable non-intentional theory of full human behavior sitting around.

So where do LLMs fall on that spectrum, using Dennett's actual bar and not vibes? On one hand we have unusually good access to the design stance for an LLM -- we know the training objective is just next-token prediction on human text -- which you could argue makes belief-talk redundant in exactly the way it's redundant for the thermostat: once you know the mechanism, the intentional gloss isn't buying you anything extra. On the other hand, next-token prediction by itself doesn't tell you what the model will actually output in a given case without modeling something belief-like about the world, and about whoever it's currently simulating -- there's that whole "simulators" framing thats floated around the rationalist blogs, where the model itself is less like a single believer and more like a stage that can host lots of different simulated speakers with different, sometimes flatly contradictory, "beliefs." That second bit seems less like a reason to reject Dennett's framework outright and more like a genuine complication within it, since it's not obvious which thing -- the network, or whichever persona it's currently running -- is supposed to be the bearer of the belief that's being attributed.

Write me an essay that actually takes a side: does Dennett's intentional stance, applied honestly on his own terms rather than the pop version, license attributing beliefs to current LLMs, or doesnt it? "It depends what you mean by belief" isn't a satisfying final answer here -- if that's true, tell me which of the things we could mean actually earns the label, and defend that as your conclusion.
