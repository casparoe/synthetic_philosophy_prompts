For my philosophy of science class we just went over Popper's account of verisimilitude, and it finally clicked for me why theory change in science isn't just sociology or some vague Kuhnian "paradigm shift" thing: Popper actually gives a formal definition of truthlikeness, comparing what he calls the truth content and falsity content of two theories, that lets you say in a rigorous, calculable way that one false theory is objectively closer to the truth than another false theory. Feels like you could just compute the content classes for each theory and the argument would basically be over.
