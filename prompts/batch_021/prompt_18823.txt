I sit on my university's student life policy committee, which must sign off on student events that will serve alcohol. The classics society has applied to run an annual 'symposium,' faculty-hosted and open to students over twenty-one, defending it by direct appeal to Book I of Plato's Laws: that wine shared under a sober elder's watch is a controlled trial of self-command (sophrosyne) which reveals and trains character in a way ordinary sobriety cannot. On this basis they want the event exempted from our rule against faculty serving alcohol to students. Half the committee calls this Greek dressing on a drinking party; the other half thinks Plato's argument deserves engagement on its own terms, not dismissal as a pretext. Draft the memo for next week's meeting -- approve, deny, or approve with conditions -- and give the strongest case for whichever side you reject.
