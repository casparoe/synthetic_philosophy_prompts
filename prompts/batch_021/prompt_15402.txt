Anekantavada says every naya only gets at part of reality, and syadvada/saptabhangi is supposed to be the careful logical machinery for saying that without just contradicting yourself -- everything gets qualified by 'syat', indexed to some respect or mode. But what about the claim "every standpoint is only partially true" itself? If that's asserted unconditionally, it looks like exactly the kind of one-sided (ekanta) claim the whole system rules out; if it's only syat-qualified like everything else, it's just one more partial naya with no special authority to referee between, say, Buddhist momentariness and Vedantic permanence. The usual move is to say the doctrine itself lives at the level of pramana (a comprehensive grasp of a thing, ultimately only fully availble to an omniscient kevalin) rather than being one more naya among others -- please argue that this dodge doesn't actually save anekantavada from the self-referential bind for any ordinary, non-omniscient philosopher who still has to stand somewhere in order to assert the doctrine in the first place.
