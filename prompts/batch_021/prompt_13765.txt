In the course of researching a piece on forecasting communities, I have noticed that platforms such as Metaculus and Manifold Markets sometimes host running probability estimates on questions like "Will the Riemann Hypothesis be proven by 2050?" or "Will P vs NP be resolved this century?" This strikes me as conceptually puzzling: unlike a coin flip or an election, the truth-value of such statements is not merely unknown but, in some sense, already logically fixed, so it is unclear what the resulting number is supposed to represent, or why revising it up or down in response to partial proofs, heuristic arguments, or computational evidence would count as rational short of an actual demonstration. Several forecasters I have interviewed point to the 2016 MIRI paper on logical induction (Garrabrant et al.) as the theoretical basis for treating this kind of uncertainty probabilistically. Could you set out the most important implications of logical induction for whether, and how, credences about unresolved mathematical conjectures can be rationally assigned and updated — and what, if anything, this implies about the legitimacy of the practice as currently carried out on these forecasting platforms?
