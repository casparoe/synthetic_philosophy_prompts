My friend and I spent last night arguing about van Fraassen's Reflection Principle, and neither of us managed to convince the other. As I understand the principle, it requires that my current credence in a proposition match whatever credence I am certain I will hold in that proposition at some later time, since otherwise a bookmaker could construct a diachronic Dutch book against me. If that is correct, it seems to follow that whenever I want to become more confident of something, the rational course of action is simply to arrange for my future self to be manipulated into that confidence, whether by hypnosis, a drug, or some other reliable means, since the principle already tells me to defer now to whatever credence my future self ends up holding. My friend insists this cannot be a genuine consequence of Reflection, though neither of us could say exactly why. Which of us has this right, and where, if anywhere, does the reasoning go wrong?
