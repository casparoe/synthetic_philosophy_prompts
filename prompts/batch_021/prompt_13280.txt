I had a long argument with a friend last night, after our philosophy of science reading group, about Kuhn's notion of incommensurability, and neither of us budged, so I would like to see the strongest possible version of my side spelled out properly. My friend Priya, who is finishing a dissertation chapter on scientific realism, was defending what she takes to be Kuhn's mature position on the topic: that once you set aside the more incendiary rhetoric of Structure and look at his writing from the 1980s and 90s, incommensurability turns out to be narrowly local, confined to a small cluster of interdefined taxonomic or "kind" terms, with his standard illustration being how "mass" functions differently in Newtonian and relativistic mechanics. On this reading, the rest of a theory's vocabulary stays shared and comparable across the divide, which is precisely what is supposed to save Kuhn from the charge of wholesale relativism: two theories can still be weighed against common standards almost everywhere, they simply cannot be strictly translated into one another with respect to that one taxonomic patch.

I argued that this concession is smaller than it looks and does not actually rescue Kuhn from the relativist implications people worry about. The terms that end up in his incommensurable clusters are never idle bystanders: they are exactly the load-bearing theoretical concepts, tied by laws and inferential connections to most of the rest of a theory's content (think of how "mass" connects to force, weight, energy, and acceleration in mechanics). If meaning is holistic in anything like the way that is needed to explain why "mass" changes meaning across the Newtonian/Einsteinian divide in the first place, that same holism should spread the incommensurability outward along all those inferential connections, well past the "small subgroup of terms" Kuhn wants to quarantine it to. So either meaning is not really holistic in the relevant sense, in which case it becomes unclear why the taxonomic terms are incommensurable at all, or it is holistic, in which case Kuhn's containment strategy cannot do the work he needs it to do. Priya's fallback was that Kuhn can still allow full interpretive access across paradigms, along the lines of learning a foreign language from scratch rather than translating it word for word into one's own, and that this is enough for rational comparison even without translation in the strict sense. Please help me build the strongest version of my argument that Kuhn's localization move fails to defuse the relativism worry, engaging directly with Priya's interpretive-access fallback and with the mass example itself, rather than resting on general slogans about holism.
