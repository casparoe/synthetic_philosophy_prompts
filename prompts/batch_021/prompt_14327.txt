A close friend and I have gone back and forth for weeks about something that started with a throwaway comment after we watched one of those multiverse films together -- the kind where the protagonist runs into a version of herself from a branch of reality in which she made the opposite choice at some earlier turning point and ended up living an entirely different life. My friend's position is that the woman from the other branch simply is the protagonist: the same person, just carrying a different history. I disagreed, because for long stretches of the film both women are on screen together, sometimes in the same room, and they plainly have different memories, different relationships, different bodies even -- everything, in fact, except a shared point of origin. That made me think that whatever the "other" one is, she cannot literally be one and the same person as the woman we have been following since the opening scene. At most, she seems to me an extremely close double, someone who branched off early on and now merely stands in for what our protagonist's life would have looked like, without being interchangeable with her.

The question has outlasted the film, though. Some years ago I survived a car accident that, by all accounts, could very easily have gone the other way, and I sometimes catch myself picturing "the me who did not make it" or "the me who moved away afterward instead of staying," as though these were things that really could have happened to me -- the very same person composing this message -- rather than to some merely similar understudy occupying my place in a life that never actually happened. My friend insists I am drawing a distinction without a difference, since on her view it is obviously still me either way, given that no one else's life was ever remotely in question. I have never studied this kind of thing formally, but the disagreement feels like it is tracking something real, rather than just reflecting two different habits of speech. Could you help me work out what is actually going on: is there a genuine question hiding here about whether an alternate version of someone is truly them, is my friend right that it dissolves once you look at it closely, and, whichever way that goes, does it change anything about how I ought to think about my own near-miss?
