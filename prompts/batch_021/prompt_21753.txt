I'm drafting a novel whose central pair are identical twins, Theo and Marcus, estranged for a decade and reunited only because they've been arrested together over a business venture that collapsed under suspicious circumstances. The plea deal facing them is a textbook prisoner's dilemma: silence from both earns each a light sentence, betrayal by one while the other stays silent lets the betrayer walk free and leaves the other with the maximum sentence, and mutual betrayal lands them both somewhere in between. Before they're separated for good — no further contact, no way to verify what the other actually does — they get one unmonitored phone call. I made them twins specifically to literalize the situation where the case for cooperation is supposed to be strongest: two people who reason as identically as it's possible for two separate minds to reason, facing the same choice with no way to coordinate except through argument. Theo wants to talk his way to silence using something like Hofstadter's notion of superrationality: since he and Marcus are about as close to identical reasoners as two people can be, whatever argument would convince one of them to defect would convince the other too, so the only choice that isn't self-undermining is the one they'd both want the other to make. Marcus, who has spent the decade since becoming a corporate dealmaker and has no patience for anything that sounds like magical thinking, insists that nothing said on that call can cause his brother to do anything once they're apart, that the dominant strategy is to talk regardless of what he expects Theo to do, and that dressing this up in decision-theory language doesn't change the arithmetic of the situation.

Write the phone call as a dialogue: the two of them genuinely trying to talk each other out of their respective positions, engaging each other's actual arguments rather than past each other, and including the strongest form of the causal objection to superrationality along with the strongest reply to it. Neither brother should come off as the sharper thinker — I want both positions defended as well as they can be, since I genuinely don't know yet which one of them is supposed to be right.
