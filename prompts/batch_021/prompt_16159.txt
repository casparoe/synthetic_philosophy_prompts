In my seminar on the moral status of animals, I have been defending the following argument: since conscious pain in mammals is standardly tied to activity in the cortical 'pain matrix' — somatosensory cortex, insula, and anterior cingulate — and fish possess no neocortex at all, whatever fish display in response to noxious stimuli must be mere nociception, an unfelt physiological reflex, rather than genuine suffering. Given this, why do fish welfare scientists, and the regulators who now write aquaculture and slaughter standards partly on the strength of their research, continue to treat fish pain as an open empirical question rather than something the neuroanatomy has already settled?
