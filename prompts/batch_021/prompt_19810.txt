A friend and I spent the better part of last night arguing about vagueness, and by the end she had talked me out of supervaluationism and into the epistemicism that Timothy Williamson defends. What sold me was her claim that the exact cutoff for a predicate like "tall" — though nobody can currently state it — is the kind of thing that better evidence could eventually track down: a large enough study correlating precise height measurements with how competent English speakers actually apply the word should, in principle, let us close in on the boundary, much as better instruments let physicists pin down a constant more precisely. I've since found myself wondering why, given how much philosophical attention vagueness has received over the past few decades, nobody seems to have actually tried running a study along these lines. Is this really just an unfilled gap in the empirical literature, or is there some deeper reason such a study couldn't settle the question even in principle?
