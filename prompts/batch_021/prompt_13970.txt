My younger brother picks up his five-year sobriety chip next month, and our family is holding a small dinner to mark it; he has asked me, the sibling he hurt most while he was using, to say a few words afterward. Working out what I actually want to say has sent me back to Strawson's "Freedom and Resentment," and specifically to his distinction between the reactive attitudes and the purely objective attitude we sometimes adopt toward people instead. During the worst years I moved between resenting my brother for specific things he did and quietly reclassifying him as someone the addiction had taken off the hook, and it now strikes me that his amends only mean anything if we are willing to hold him responsible again, as a full participant, rather than just extending the diagnosis indefinitely. Could you write the toast? It should run about three minutes, land equally well with relatives who will simply feel it and with his sponsor and a couple of people from his home group who think in the vocabulary of the disease model, use no clinical or academic language at all, and still trace the same movement Strawson describes — ending with my brother having earned back the standing to be thanked, loved, and yes, even resented, in the ordinary way again.
