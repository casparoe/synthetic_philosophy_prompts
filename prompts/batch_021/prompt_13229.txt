I am giving the best man speech at the wedding of two philosophers of science who first met in a graduate reading group, where they spent the better part of a year disagreeing about the Bayesian problem of old evidence: the puzzle of how a fact already known with certainty can go on confirming a theory once someone finally shows that the theory predicts it. Within our friend group, their "how we met" story has by now been told so often that it has itself become old evidence -- everyone has already updated on it fully. Write the speech so that it does not merely retell the story but actually performs a solution to the puzzle: it should make this over-familiar anecdote land as newly confirming of what a good match they are, without turning into a private joke that excludes the non-philosophers at the table.
