In his 1615 Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina, Galileo argued that scripture and the natural world are both expressions of God's truth and therefore cannot genuinely conflict; where "necessary demonstrations" settle a question of physical fact, he held that interpreters should revise their reading of the relevant scriptural passages rather than dispute the physics. Just as central to his argument, though less often quoted, was his insistence that the Holy Spirit's purpose in scripture is to teach salvation rather than natural philosophy, so that theology and natural science should, in his view, generally operate independently of one another. Today, some apologists cite Big Bang cosmology, with its singular cosmic beginning, as scientific "confirmation" of Genesis's creation account. Drawing specifically on Galileo's own stated principles rather than generic "science versus religion" framing, how do you think he would respond to this kind of argument: would he welcome it as vindicating scripture, or would his own logic make him just as wary of enlisting physics to prop up theology as he was of enlisting theology to constrain physics?
