So I'm plotting a novel with a minor character who's weirdly obsessed with whether space is a "real" thing in its own right, and this past weekend I watched my friend Dana (shes a physics postdoc) and her partner Marcus (hes finishing a philosophy of science PhD) spiral into a full argument about it at dinner, starting from Newton's bucket. I jotted it down for the book, but now I'm curious what you'd make of it, because I honestly can't tell who won.

Dana's opening move: spin the bucket, wait til the water's rotating right along with it so there's zero relative motion between them, and the surface is still concave. So whatever the water's rotating relative to, it isn't the bucket — it has to be space itself. Point Newton.

Marcus: that's outdated, Mach already handled this — the water's rotating relative to the rest of the matter in the universe, not empty space, and Einstein liked the idea enough to try to build it into general relativity.

Dana: except GR doesn't actually deliver that. There's a solution to Einstein's equations — the Gödel universe, I think — that's a whole rotating cosmos with nothing outside it to rotate relative to, and the rotation is still real and measurable. So absolute rotation survives, even in GR.

Marcus: that's a weird toy solution, not our actual universe, and Mach's principle is a claim about the real world, not every mathematically possible one.

Dana: that's a dodge — if relationism is supposed to say what rotation fundamentally is, it doesn't get to wave away counterexamples just because they're "not our universe." And the real lesson of GR is that spacetime itself is a dynamical, physically real thing doing the explaining.

Marcus: bit rich, since that line is basically Earman's, and Earman's also the guy behind the hole argument, which says treating spacetime points as fully real individual things leads to an insane indeterminism. Feels like that cuts against "spacetime is real," not for it.

They never resolved it and moved on to arguing about who was paying. Could you go through this move by move — who's got the better of it at each step, not just overall — and point out anything you think both of them are missing?
