I'm drafting a near-future thriller, and for a pivotal chapter I need an internal document rigorous enough to survive a reader who actually knows some game theory. My protagonist sits on a fictional National Security Council review board deciding whether to activate "Threshold": a system designed to execute a fixed, non-overridable retaliatory cyberstrike against a rival state's power grid the moment one specific trigger condition is met. There is no human override, by design -- a threat someone could call off at the last minute isn't a credible deterrent. Fresh intelligence puts the rival six to eight weeks from fielding an equivalent system. Half the board wants to commit and announce now, arguing that whoever locks in a threat first sets the terms of the standoff; the other half fears that two such systems operating in ignorance of each other's exact triggers is exactly how an accident becomes permanent for everyone. The board's charter permits a reversible "pilot" activation, but proponents of full activation argue that reversibility defeats the entire point. Please draft the memo the board sends to the principals committee: a clear recommendation with a timeline keyed to the intelligence window, and a fair, undismissive treatment of the strongest argument on whichever side it doesn't recommend.
