My identical twin brother is getting married next month, and I've been asked to give the best man speech. Our whole lives, people have treated us as basically interchangeable — same face, same voice, teachers and even old girlfriends mixing us up without meaning to. A few years back I fell down a rabbit hole reading about Leibniz's principle of the identity of indiscernibles, and about Max Black's old thought experiment about two perfectly similar spheres alone in an otherwise empty universe, meant to show that two things can be exact duplicates of one another and still be two things, not one. I can't stop thinking about how directly that bears on my own life. My future sister-in-law is finishing a philosophy PhD and will be at the wedding, so a few guests will know exactly what I'm doing if I bring it up; everyone else, including our parents, will just hear a story about two brothers. Please write me a three-minute best man speech that is warm and funny rather than a seminar, built honestly around Black's spheres rather than merely decorated with the reference, and that says something true about what makes my brother, specifically, the person she fell in love with — without ever resorting to the usual line that he's "one of a kind," since as his twin, I'm living proof that, qualitatively, he isn't.
