Question was: "Explain how updateless decision theory (UDT) handles the counterfactual mugging problem. What does 'updateless' actually mean here? Is it fair to say UDT is basically just causal decision theory plus the ability to precommit in advance? Briefly note who introduced UDT." Here's one student's answer:

"In counterfactual mugging, Omega flips a fair coin. If it lands tails, Omega asks you for $100; if it lands heads, Omega will hand you $10,000, but only if it predicts you'd have paid the $100 in the tails case. A causal or evidential agent, once told the coin came up tails, reasons that handing over $100 now can't retroactively cause the heads-branch payout, so it refuses to pay. UDT instead picks the best overall policy in advance: paying whenever tails comes up has expected value 0.5(10000) - 0.5(100), way better than never paying (expected value 0), so the policy that pays wins and UDT pays. 'Updateless' just means the agent doesn't recompute its decision after updating on being told the coin came up tails - it commits to whatever policy looked best from its earlier, pre-observation vantage point and sticks with it no matter what it learns. Basically, UDT is old-fashioned CDT plus the power to bind yourself in advance - give a CDT agent a precommitment device before the relevant uncertainty resolves and it'll behave exactly like a UDT agent in any scenario you throw at it. UDT was introduced by Eliezer Yudkowsky as a fix for problems he noticed in his earlier timeless decision theory."
