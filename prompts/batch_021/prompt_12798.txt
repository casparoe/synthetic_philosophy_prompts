So I'm putting together a collection of short stories that each smuggle a classic thought experiment into an actual plot, and for the one about knowledge and perception I want to skip the black-and-white room lady -- everyone's already done Mary a thousand times over. I'm thinking of the weirder, less famous case from that same Frank Jackson paper: the guy (Fred, I think) who sees two distinct shades where the rest of us just see "red," and can sort a pile of ripe tomatoes into two piles that look completely identical to anyone else. Could you write me a short story, maybe 800-1200 words, from the POV of the scientist who's spent years studying him, the two of them circling the same argument they've never resolved? I want the story to hold both positions in real tension -- that Fred has access to some extra fact about color no amount of physics could ever capture, versus that he's just aquired a new discriminating skill and there's no further "fact" hiding anywhere -- without either of them ever saying words like qualia or physicalism out loud. End it wherever feels right, but I'd love it if we don't come away sure which side she lands on.
