I now sit on the adjudication panel for a regional foundation's Award for Civilian Courage, presumably because my novels have long been preoccupied with characters who do more than duty could ever ask of them. The panel is close to unanimous that this year's award should go to an off-duty paramedic who, defying the fire marshal's direct order to stay clear, re-entered a partially collapsed building to free a stranger trapped in a stairwell and broke her pelvis doing so; asked afterward why she went back in, she said only that she 'didn't see it as a decision.' Our charter restricts the award to acts that are 'freely chosen and beyond what duty, role, or office could demand,' and two objections are holding up the vote on exactly these grounds: one panelist argues that a paramedic's training creates a standing professional duty to render aid even off shift, so this was at most an extension of her job rather than something beyond it; another argues that an act she 'didn't see as a decision' cannot count as freely chosen at all. Please draft the short memo I will circulate before our final meeting: give a clear recommendation on whether she should receive the award, and make sure it engages honestly with the stronger of these two objections rather than the weaker one.
