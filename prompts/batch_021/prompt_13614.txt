I have spent the past few months reporting a feature on an effort to confirm that a large mammal survives in a remote stretch of the Annamite mountains along the Vietnam-Laos border, a region that has produced a handful of genuine zoological surprises over the past few decades. In assembling the evidence for the piece, I have noticed that it falls into a few distinct kinds, and one of those kinds is giving me trouble I cannot quite name.

There is a single camera-trap photograph, taken at dusk, that the field team believes clearly shows the animal, though an outside reviewer thinks it is too blurred to rule out a known species. There are fresh tracks and droppings from which a biologist worked out the animal's probable size and diet without anyone having laid eyes on it. A mammalogist who has worked in the region for two decades, and whom everyone on the team trusts without reservation, simply told me she has seen the animal twice and knows exactly what it is. And then there are several hunters who, independently of one another and of the scientific team, said they have always identified the animal not by any feature of its own but by comparison: they have a local word for it, but insisted that the word means nothing to an outsider until you are told the animal is "built like a small buffalo, but with a fox's face."

That last sort of claim is the one I cannot file away. My editor's view is that it is simply a folksier, weaker version of the mammalogist's eyewitness claim, secondhand testimony dressed up in metaphor and discounted the way any hearsay would be. That does not sit right with me: the hunters are not vouching for a fact the way the mammalogist is, and they are not reasoning from visible clues to an unseen cause the way the tracks were used; they seem to be doing some third thing, fixing what the animal is by triangulating between two animals they already know well. Is my editor right that this collapses into ordinary hearsay once you examine it closely, or is there a genuinely distinct way of knowing something at work here, standing on equal footing with seeing, inferring, and being told?
