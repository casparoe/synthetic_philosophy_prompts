ok so this happened at lab drinks last week and I havent been able to let it go. two people in my evolutionary genetics lab, both working on wild mouse populations with t-haplotype (the classic meiotic-drive system: a male carrying one copy gets it into way more than half his sperm instead of the usual 50/50, but two copies together and you're either dead as an embryo or sterile, depending which variant you've got), ended up arguing about how to even describe what's going on. they agree completely on the biology, it's just the description of it they can't settle.

(1) Theo: "the t allele doesn't care that it wrecks any male who inherits two copies - from its 'point of view,' hijacking meiosis to get itself into like 90% of the sperm is a straight win. it's sabotaging the rest of the genome just to spread itself."

(2) Ines: "that's a metaphor doing the job of an argument. there's no point of view and no sabotage, some sequences just end up with more copies than others. fitness is a property of the mouse, or fine, the genotype at a stretch - talking like the allele has plans just confuses people about what's actually happening."

(3) Theo: "but that language is earning its keep, it's not just decorating the story. it's the actual reason anyone predicted the rest of the genome should evolve suppressors against the distorter over time. you don't get an arms race unless there really are two conflicting interests running inside one mouse's own genome."

(4) Ines: "an arms race is just two allele frequencies pushing on each other under selection, you can write all of that down with zero talk of interests. and if a stretch of DNA gets to have 'interests' just because its transmission and the organism's reproduction come apart sometimes, why doesn't a single nucleotide get interests too? that can't be where you want to draw the line."

(5) Theo: "because the t-complex has its own transmission-boosting machinery and an actual target, the wildtype sperm - a bare nucleotide doesn't do anything like that. so the 'interests all the way down' thing doesn't actually follow."

it kept going a bit longer than this but they never really landed anywhere, and both of them seemed right about something at every stage, which is exactly why I can't referee it myself. could you go through it turn by turn and say who's got the better of it at each point (not just an overall winner), and then say what's actually going on underneath their disagreement that neither of them quite managed to name?
