I'm taking a seminar this term that just spent two weeks on James's "The Will to Believe" against Clifford's "The Ethics of Belief," and I've been asked to give the toast at my older brother's wedding next month. His fiancée is finishing her PhD in philosophy, so a good part of the room -- her cohort and a couple of her professors -- will know this material cold, while the rest of the family has never heard of either philosopher. To complicate things, a few relatives have been openly skeptical of the engagement, on the grounds that the couple only got together eighteen months ago and don't yet have "enough evidence" that this will last; that is, essentially, Clifford's position, repackaged as a complaint about my brother's love life.

Could you draft the toast? It should run about three minutes read aloud, turn on James's account of what makes an option genuine -- live, forced, and momentous -- and on his claim that faith in a fact can itself help create the fact, and it should give Clifford's shipowner some real job in the speech besides serving as a cautionary aside. It needs to be warm enough that my aunts don't feel lectured and precise enough that the philosophers in the room don't wince, and rather than politely splitting the difference between James and Clifford, it should actually land somewhere.
