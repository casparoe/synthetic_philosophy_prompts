Ok so I'm working on a novel and there's a scene I can't quite crack -- my protagonist has to give the eulogy for her old philosophy advisor, who spent his whole career defending Galen Strawson's Basic Argument. Not the usual "determinism is true so you're off the hook" argument -- the regress one: you do what you do because of the way you are, and you can't be responsible for the way you are (indeterminism doesn't save you either, he was clear about that), so nobody is ever really, ultimately responsible for anything, good stuff included, not just the bad. The guy used to genuinely bristle when colleagues called him "self-made" or said he'd earned his reputation, since on his own view that's incoherent -- you don't get credit for being the kind of person who works hard. Problem is the funeral has two audiences: half his old department, who'll clock it instantly if the eulogy quietly cheats back into ordinary desert-talk, and his sister's family, who don't care about any of that and just want to hear their brother was a good man and feel a bit comforted. Can you draft the eulogy -- something that'd take about 5 minutes read aloud? It has to actually respect the argument, no sneaky "he deserved to be remembered fondly" type lines, but still land as love and grief rather then a philosophy paper. Bonus points if theres a graceful way to work in his favorite bit of Nietzsche on causa sui without it turning into a lecture.
