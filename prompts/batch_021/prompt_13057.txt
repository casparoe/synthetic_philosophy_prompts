Regulars of the old Overcoming Bias and LessWrong anthropics threads will recognize the dilemma that Nick Bostrom sharpened in *Anthropic Bias*. Under the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) -- reason as if you were randomly drawn from the observers in your reference class -- the Doomsday Argument goes through: conditioning on your own birth rank among all humans who will ever live shifts credence toward a nearer, smaller-population extinction, seemingly on the strength of nothing more than your place in the birth sequence. Switch to the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) -- reason as if randomly drawn from all *possible* observers -- and, as Dieks and Olum argued, this update cancels exactly. But SIA buys that cancellation at the price of the Presumptuous Philosopher: a cosmologist's forthcoming experiment becomes redundant, since SIA already tells her, before any data comes in, that the theory positing vastly more observers is correspondingly more likely to be true.

Write an essay that weighs the Doomsday bullet against the Presumptuous Philosopher bullet, taking seriously at least one further contender (Bostrom's own reference-class maneuvers, Radford Neal's Full Non-Indexical Conditioning, or another) and how it fares on the structurally similar Sleeping Beauty problem. Reach and defend a specific verdict on which stance toward self-locating priors a Bayesian should adopt -- a conditional answer is fine, but do not conclude that the question is simply undecidable.
