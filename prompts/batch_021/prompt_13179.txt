and here's the student's answer:

"Non-reductive physicalism holds that mental properties are distinct from physical properties even though they're physically realized -- so mental property M is realized by physical property P, and physicalism is committed to the causal closure principle: every physical event that has a cause has a sufficient physical cause. The trouble is that if P is already sufficient to cause some physical effect P* (say, a wince), and M supposedly also causes P*, we have two full causes for one effect. Kim thinks we shouldn't accept this as genuine overdetermination, so M gets excluded as a cause, and mental causation collapses into epiphenomenalism unless something else can be done. This specifically threatens non-reductive physicalism because the reductive physicalist avoids the whole problem by just identifying M with P outright (one cause, not two), and the substance dualist avoids it because they reject physicalism, so the closure principle never gets a foothold in the first place. Davidson's anomalous monism escapes the same problem a different way: since it says every mental event token just is some physical event token, there's really only one event doing the causing rather than two competing ones, and since the whole exclusion worry depends on there being two distinct causes, token identity gets Davidson off the hook cleanly. Since mental causation basically can't be saved for a physicalist who insists on keeping mental properties around as genuinely distinct from physical ones, Kim's own considered conclusion is that we should just accept that mental properties are epiphenomenal and live with it, since the causal closure of the physical has to win out over our intuitions about the mind having real causal powers."
