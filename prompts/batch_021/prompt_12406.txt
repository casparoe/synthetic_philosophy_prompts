I'm giving a toast this Friday at the retirement dinner for a colleague, Warren, who's spent almost his whole forty-year career on the problem of verisimilitude -- trying to salvage some working notion of "truthlikeness" for scientific theories after Tichy and Miller torpedoed Popper's original definition back in '74. He's spent most of that time defending his own Niiniluoto-ish similarity measure against exactly the language-dependence objections that sank everybody else's fix, and he's famously allergic to anyone, himself included, ever saying the problem got "solved." Half the room is philosophers of science who'll still argue about truth-content versus falsity-content after their second glass of wine, the other half is family and college admin who just know him as "the guy who argues about which wrong thing is closer to right." Can you write me a 5-minute toast with one real joke about verisimilitude that lands for both crowds, that's affectionate rather than a lecture, and that doesn't accidentally claim -- even as a compliment -- that his life's work arrived anywhere, since its exactly the kind of thing he'd object to from the podium. Bonus if the toast itself ends up a decent example of verisimilitude -- closer to the truth about him than a straightforward tribute could get away with, without pretending to land on it exactly.
