I am currently taking a course on moral philosophy, and I have not been able to decide what to make of something that happens regularly at my part-time canvassing job.

Our script always opens by asking residents to donate $50 a month, a figure that almost everyone declines. Only once they have refused do we mention the $10 a month tier that we are actually hoping most people will choose, and a substantially higher proportion of residents agree to that figure than when volunteers, as we did last year, led with the $10 ask directly. The opening request does not seem to give anyone a better reason to think $10 is the right amount; it just changes how that number feels once $50 has already been turned down.

Is there something ethically questionable about using a technique like this, even though nobody is lied to or pressured to stay at the door? I would like to understand what, if anything, distinguishes it from a straightforward appeal to someone's reasons, and where the line should be drawn in comparable situations.
