So I'm 34, a backend engineer, and I've got two job offers sitting in front of me that are stressing me out way more than they should. Option A is a Staff Engineer role at a big company I already contract with — great pay (about 40% more than I make now), stock that's basically guaranteed to vest, a manager I actually like, chill hours. Option B is coming on as employee #4 (basically de facto CTO) at a friend's startup — the technical work itself is way more exciting to me and I'd have real ownership over decisions, but the pay is roughly half of Option A, the equity is probably worth zero, and my wife is (understandably) nervous since we've got a toddler and want to buy a house next year.

Being an engineer, obviously I made a spreadsheet: comp, growth, risk-adjusted equity, "mission fit," work-life balance, each with a weight from 1-10 and a score from 1-10 per option. Thing is, depending on how I set the weights I can get either option to win, and tiny tweaks (bumping "mission fit" from a 6 to a 7) flip the total even though my actual preferences haven't changed at all. That felt like fake precision dressed up as an answer, so I scrapped it. I even tried a sanity check where I imagined Option A's salary bumped up another 10%, just to see if that would make it the clear winner — and wierdly it still didn't feel obviously better than Option B, but B didn't feel obviously better than boosted-A either, and they don't feel exactly tied either. Which is confusing, because normally improving one option a bit should settle things one way or the other.

I brought this up with some engineer friends over drinks and one of them said the "rational" move is to just convert everything into dollar terms, including stuff like excitement and ownership, and go with whichever number's bigger — but that feels off to me too, like it's assuming these things are secretly all measured in the same currency when they don't feel like they are. So I guess my question is: is there something actually structurally different about a decision like this compared to, say, picking which cloud provider has lower latency, where more data would just resolve it? Or is my friend right and I should just quit being squeamish, force a number, and go with it? Curious if theres an actual framework for thinking about choices like this that isn't just "gather more data" or "trust your gut."
