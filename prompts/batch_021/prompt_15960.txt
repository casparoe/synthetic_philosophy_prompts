Working from established game-theory references, everyone teaches the Nash Bargaining Solution as the go-to answer for how two rational parties should split a surplus, but the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution gets way less airtime even though it swaps out Nash's independence-of-irrelevant-alternatives axiom for a monotonicity axiom instead. I cant decide if that swap is just a cute mathematical footnote or if it actually matters for real negotiations, like wage bargaining, where the size of the pie itself can shift mid-talks. Can you make the case that KS is actually the more realistic (or more normatively defensible) model for that kind of negotiation — specifically by arguing why monotonicity should matter more than IIA once the bargaining set itself is a moving target? Pick a side and really argue it, don't just lay both axioms out for me.
