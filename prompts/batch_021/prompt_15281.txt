At my company, every pull request needs two approvals before it can merge. I've noticed that once a PR gets its first approval, the second one often comes through within minutes, even for large or genuinely complicated changes, whereas the first reviewer typically spends much longer on it. I mentioned this to a teammate, and he said there's nothing wrong with it: a second reviewer can reasonably conclude that if a competent colleague already approved the change, it's probably fine, so there's no need to duplicate all that scrutiny. That reasoning sounds plausible to me, but I can't shake the feeling that something is off about how we're doing code review. Is my teammate right that this is just individually sensible behavior, or is there a real problem with our process that this reasoning is glossing over?
