The question, worth 20 points, read as follows: "Bayesian confirmation theory standardly holds that evidence E confirms a hypothesis H relative to background knowledge K just in case conditionalizing on E raises the probability of H — e.g., on the difference measure, c(H,E) = P(H|E) − P(H) > 0. Explain why 'old evidence' generates a problem for this picture, using the historical confirmation of General Relativity by the anomalous precession of Mercury's perihelion as your illustration. Then explain how the strategy of relaxing the assumption of logical omniscience is supposed to address the problem, and say how it relates to Howson's proposal that we evaluate confirmation using a counterfactual probability distribution."

Here is the student's answer, transcribed exactly as submitted: "The worry, first pressed by John Earman, is that if a scientist already assigns E probability 1 as part of her background knowledge K, then conditionalizing on E again cannot change her degree of belief in any hypothesis H: P(H|E) is just P(H∩E)/P(E), and since P(E)=1 this equals P(H), so the difference measure c(H,E)=P(H|E)−P(H) comes out to exactly zero. But this seems to get the history of science wrong. Astronomers had known about the anomalous precession of Mercury's perihelion for decades before Einstein published General Relativity, yet when it turned out that GR entailed exactly this anomaly, everyone — Einstein included — treated this as a major point in GR's favor over Newtonian mechanics: old evidence apparently can confirm a new theory after all, which the official Bayesian machinery seems to rule out. The standard way of rescuing Bayesianism here is to give up the idealization that agents are logically omniscient. On this view, what the scientist actually learns when GR is proposed is not E itself, which was already certain, but the further, logically contingent fact that GR entails E — a fact she could not simply have deduced in advance, since she is not a perfect logician. This is essentially Howson's counterfactual move: since her credence that 'GR entails E' was less than 1 before the derivation was carried out, conditionalizing on that discovery is exactly what raises her credence in GR, and the paradox dissolves once we stop assuming agents have unlimited logical powers."
