A friend and I spent much of last night debating Jain philosophy, and I ended up defending the view that anekāntavāda is really just a sophisticated form of relativism: since it holds that reality has countless aspects and that no single, non-omniscient standpoint (naya) can grasp the whole of it, and since syādvāda then qualifies every assertion with 'in some respect' (syāt), it seems to follow that no viewpoint can be judged more or less correct than any other. My friend disagreed but couldn't quite articulate where the reasoning goes wrong. Doesn't this mean Jain philosophers were being inconsistent whenever they argued, as they historically did, that their own synthesis was truer than rival positions like the Buddhist doctrine of momentariness or Advaita Vedānta's monism? Isn't anekāntavāda ultimately self-undermining in that sense?
