I sit on my university's honorary degrees committee and we've landed a nasty one. We're voting on an honorary doctorate for a former president credited with ending a brutal three-year war almost overnight, by ordering strikes on the enemy capital's water treatment and power plants. The resulting civilian death toll from disease that winter was awful, but still lower, historians reckon, than what a longer war would've cost. Half the committee wants to approve on exactly those grounds, lives saved on net. The other half is basically running Anscombe's old argument against giving Truman a degree over Hiroshima: deliberately wrecking a civilian population's ability to survive, in order to force a surrender, is murder, and murder doesn't get cancelled out by a lives-saved calculation. Can you draft the memo recommending what we actually do - approve, decline, or some third option - before thursday's vote? It can't just assert the Anscombe line and move on, it actually has to engage with the lives-saved case.
