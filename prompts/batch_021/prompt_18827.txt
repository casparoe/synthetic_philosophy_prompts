In my school's intro-to-philosophy elective, we just wrapped up a unit on utilitarianism, and a real situation from around the same time has been nagging at me ever since, because I can't decide whether it's actually a problem or whether I'm overthinking it. My school had one fully funded spot left on a competitive summer research program, and once our grades, test scores, and recommendation letters all came back essentially tied between me and another student, the selection committee decided to break the tie by having each of us write a short personal statement about how much the opportunity would mean to us, on the reasoning that they should give it to whoever wanted or would benefit from it more. I lost. The other student's statement was far more emotionally vivid than mine, full of language about how life-changing this would be, whereas I tend to write about things I care about in a pretty understated way. I'm confident neither of us was exaggerating or performing for the committee; they simply express themselves more intensely than I do, and I know for a fact they didn't want the spot any more than I did.

What's been bothering me is a broader question underneath this one outcome, and my friends and I keep going back and forth on it without agreeing. One friend thinks the committee's whole approach was flawed from the start, because there's no shared ruler for measuring how much two different people want or would benefit from the same thing, which means comparing us was never going to be meaningful, no matter how sincere we both were. Another friend thinks that's too extreme, pointing out that some comparisons seem obviously valid, like the fact that a person who has nothing clearly benefits more from a given sum of money than a millionaire does, so maybe expressed intensity is at least weak evidence of something real, even if it's a noisy signal. A third friend thinks subjective statements should have been thrown out entirely in favor of strictly "objective" criteria, like documented financial need.

I'd genuinely like your take on this, since we can't settle it ourselves: is there any principled, non-arbitrary way to compare how much benefit, need, or desire two different people have for the same thing, or does any such comparison eventually hit a wall once you accept that no one has direct access to what's going on inside someone else's head?
