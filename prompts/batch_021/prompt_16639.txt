During practice for our school's Ethics Bowl team, two of my teammates fell into a long disagreement about the Hart–Fuller debate that we never actually settled, and it has stuck with me since.

It started from the 1949 German case often called the "grudge informer" case, which Hart discusses in his article "Positivism and the Separation of Law and Morals." During the Nazi period, a woman denounced her husband to the authorities for a remark criticizing the regime, and he was convicted under a wartime statute that made such remarks a crime. After the war, a German court convicted the wife herself of unlawfully depriving her husband of his liberty, ruling that the Nazi statute was too iniquitous to count as valid law at all.

(1) Amara defended Hart's position: the statute really was law, however wicked, and if the postwar court wanted to punish the wife, it should have said so openly by passing a specifically retroactive statute, rather than pretending the Nazi enactment had never been law to begin with. (2) Devon pushed back with Fuller's response: a regime that governs through secret decrees, retroactive punishment, and courts with no independence from the party has already forfeited whatever makes a system count as law, so refusing to treat that particular statute as "law" isn't a dodge, it's simply accurate. (3) Amara replied that this only relocates the problem, since Devon still needs a non-arbitrary threshold for how much procedural rot voids a statute's legal status, and he doesn't have one. (4) Devon answered that plenty of legal distinctions have blurry edges without being useless — we still distinguish day from night despite dusk — and that however fuzzy the general threshold is, a regime that made criticizing the Führer a capital offense, decided ad hoc by local officials, is nowhere near that border.

Neither of them gave any ground. For each of the four moves, could you say whether it favors Amara, favors Devon, or is a wash, with a brief justification, and then say what you think Hart and Fuller's actual exchange adds that neither of my teammates raised?
