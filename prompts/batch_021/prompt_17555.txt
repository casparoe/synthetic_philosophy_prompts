ok this is a weird combo of things but bear with me. Last night I got into a two hour arguement with my friend Priya about Nozick's entitlement theory, specifically the Wilt Chamberlain bit -- she thinks "liberty upsets patterns" is basically a shell game, because it only looks like it beats patterned theories of justice if you don't look too closely at D1, and once you take the rectification-for-historical-injustice part seriously (all the conquest and theft that actually got us to any real starting point) the argument doesn't get libertarians anywhere near where they want to end up. She even brought up that bit where Nozick more or less admits that since we can't calculate what holdings would look like without centuries of injustice, something like maximizing the position of the worst-off might be the best available stand-in for rectification -- which, if you sit with it, is Nozick quietly borrowing half of Rawls to patch his own theory. I pushed back on a different point, that Wilt doesn't need to have deserved the 25 cents a ticket more than anyone else for D2 to be just, since entitlement theory was never a desert theory to begin with, it only cares whether anyone's rights got violated on the way there. We didn't resolve anything, we never do, but it's left me in a strange headspace for something else that's actually happening this week.

My uncle Gary died on Sunday and I'm giving the eulogy Friday. He ran a small chain of appliance stores, no academic background at all, but he read Anarchy, State, and Utopia in his twenties and it never really let go of him -- Thanksgiving every year involved some version of him and my aunt Ruth (his sister, lifelong union rep, has never once let him win) doing the Wilt Chamberlain bit across the table. Here's the actual problem: Gary was weirdly precise about this stuff for a guy who sold refrigerators, and the one thing he told me directly, more than once, was that nobody better stand up at his funeral and praise him for "earning" his money or "deserving" it because he worked harder than everyone else -- that genuinely got under his skin, because he didn't think that was even the argument he believed, whether he deserved it more than the next guy was beside the point for him. There are going to be a few guys from his old Tuesday night argument group at the service who will absolutely notice if I flatten this into a generic "he believed in hard work" story, and then there's basically everyone else -- cousins, his grandkids -- who just remember him as the guy who'd corner you about a basketball player during tax season and have no idea any of this even has a name.

So can you write the actual eulogy, something like five minutes long. It needs to get the real shape of his view right, including the desert thing, since that's exactly the part people who like the guy for the wrong reasons always flatten out -- but it can't turn into a philosophy lecture, and it still has to work as a eulogy for the people who've never heard of Robert Nozick and are just there to grieve their uncle. If there's a way to give the reading-group guys one moment that actually rewards them for knowing the argument, without losing everyone else, that would be amazing, but getting Gary right matters more than getting the joke right.
