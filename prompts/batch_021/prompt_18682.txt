I've spent the past few weeks reading AI-safety and game-theory writing for a piece on bargaining between highly capable agents, and one idea keeps nagging at me: the "commitment race." The worry, in outline, is this: when two sophisticated parties are heading toward a negotiation, each has an incentive to bind itself, fast and irrevocably, to a hard-line stance — a threat, a tripwire, a promise it cannot later break — because whoever locks in first and most credibly can force the other side to accommodate them. That rewards speed over reflection: both parties are pushed to commit before they've even finished working out what they actually want, since any time spent thinking it through is time the other side might use to commit first and box them in. The unsettling part is that the race to commit can manufacture exactly the disaster both sides were trying to avoid. I haven't come across fiction that captures this particular trap, as opposed to the more generic "two rivals who won't back down." Could you write me a short story, in any setting or genre you like, in which a commitment race of this specific kind — brinkmanship driven by a structural reward for outrunning your own deliberation, rather than brinkmanship alone — is the actual engine of the plot?
