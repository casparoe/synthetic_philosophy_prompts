I was recently appointed to the newly formed model-risk subcommittee at the asset-management firm where I work, and a live dispute has landed on my desk ahead of Friday's vote. Our flagship multi-asset strategy includes a reference-dependent overlay -- added years ago, with client sign-off, to track a well-documented regret-aversion in how clients evaluate outcomes -- that makes the strategy's revealed ordering over certain asset triples intransitive: it can prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A, depending on which holding currently serves as the reference point. A recent stress test showed that a sophisticated counterparty could exploit this with a round-trip trade cycle, extracting a small toll on each leg while returning our strategy to exactly where it started -- in effect, a money pump. Half the subcommittee wants to mandate strict transitivity across all overlays going forward, even though this means retiring a feature built specifically to reflect that documented client preference. The other half argues we can keep the overlay and close the exploit procedurally instead: once a rebalancing sequence begins, the strategy would be barred from re-entering any leg of a cycle it has already completed within the same window, on the theory that resolutely committing to a trading plan blocks the pump without requiring transitive preferences underneath. I need to circulate the subcommittee's decision memo before then. Please draft it: recommend one policy, justify it, and give a fair, substantive hearing to the strongest thing that can be said for the side you're not recommending.
