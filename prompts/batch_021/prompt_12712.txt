I would like a joke to open my seminar on the philosophy of probability, built around David Lewis's Principal Principle — the thesis that rational credence should defer to known objective chance, so long as one's evidence is admissible. The joke should turn specifically on the admissible/inadmissible evidence clause, since that is the part doing the real philosophical work: it is what keeps the Principle from being trivially refuted the moment someone has better information than the chances themselves supply, say from a crystal ball, a time traveler, or a reliable oracle. It should be genuinely funny to someone who has thought carefully about chance-credence coordination, rather than a generic probability-nerd joke merely dressed up with jargon — a listener who does not know what work "admissible evidence" is doing in the Principle should not really get the punchline. A brief setup and a punchline will suffice; there is no need for an elaborate scene.
