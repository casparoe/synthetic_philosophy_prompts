The topic was Kim's causal exclusion argument. Move 1, Mara: non-reductive physicalism can't actually deliver mental causation. If a physical effect already has a sufficient cause in its physical realizer, and we're not going to accept that every mental-physical pair is a case of systematic overdetermination, then the mental property sitting on top of that realizer isn't doing any causal work of its own - it's just along for the ride, so you're either a reductionist about the mental or an epiphenomenalist about it. Move 2, Owen: that argument generalizes way past the mind in a way that should make Mara suspicious of it - run the same reasoning on biology or economics and you'd have to say natural selection and recessions don't cause anything either, since they're realized in microphysical goings-on that already have sufficient physical causes. Since properties like these are multiply realizable (the same recession, or the same pain, could show up in all sorts of different underlying physical arrangements), the higher-level property is tracking a real causal-explanatory pattern the physical description alone would miss, so it can't just be idle. Move 3, Mara: that's an explanatory point, not a causal one - "the vase broke because it was fragile" is a fine explanation that doesn't require fragility to add any causal oomph over and above the microphysical process that actually broke the vase, and the whole question is whether mental properties are like that: good redescriptions rather than additional difference-makers. Move 4, Owen: brings up Yablo's proportionality idea - a cause shouldn't be more specific than the effect calls for. His example was Sophie the pigeon, trained to peck at red things, who's shown a scarlet target and pecks; the cause of the pecking is the redness, not the scarlet shade specifically, because Sophie would've pecked at anything red. Owen's claim is that a lot of mental causation is like that: the exact physical microstate is often too specific, gratuitously fine-grained, while the mental property is the one that's actually proportional to the effect - which would make it a genuine cause, not something excluded by the physical at all.
