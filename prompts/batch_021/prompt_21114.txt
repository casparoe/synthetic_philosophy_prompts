I've been assigned my classmate Owen's answer, and since the practice grading itself counts toward participation, I'd like an independent second read before I commit to a score I'll have to defend to her.

The question we were given was: "Explain the Sleeping Beauty problem and state the answers given by the 'halfer' and 'thirder' positions. Present the strongest argument you know of for one of these positions, making sure to explain why the case counts as an instance of specifically self-locating (rather than ordinary) belief. Finally, briefly explain one substantive way in which the Sleeping Beauty debate connects to the Doomsday Argument, and one important way in which the two cases differ."

Owen's answer was: "Sleeping Beauty agrees to take part in an experiment. On Sunday night she is put to sleep, and a fair coin is tossed. If it lands heads, she is woken once, on Monday, and the experiment ends after that day. If it lands tails, she is woken on Monday and again on Tuesday, but immediately after the Monday waking she is given a drug that erases her memory of it, so that on Tuesday she has no memory of already having been woken that week. Every time she wakes up she knows the entire setup but cannot tell what day it is or how the coin landed, and she is asked how confident she is that the coin landed heads. Halfers say her credence should stay at 1/2, since she knew all along that she would wake up at least once no matter how the coin landed, so waking up itself doesn't tell her anything she didn't already know about the coin. Thirders say her credence should be 1/3 for heads (so 2/3 for tails), because on any given awakening there are really three equally likely situations she could be in from her perspective -- Heads-and-Monday, Tails-and-Monday, and Tails-and-Tuesday -- and two of those three go along with tails. The clearest argument for the thirder position is a betting or long-run frequency argument. If the experiment were repeated many times and Beauty answered 'tails' at every single awakening, she would be right in roughly two-thirds of her awakenings overall, since a tails trial produces twice as many awakenings as a heads trial does. Because her evidence is exactly the same at every one of these awakenings, and because being right two-thirds of the time is only what we'd expect if her confidence matched that same ratio, her credence should track it: 2/3 for tails. This case counts as involving self-locating belief, rather than ordinary belief, because Beauty isn't uncertain about some external fact she could in principle look up, like who won an election; she is uncertain about her own location, i.e., which of several subjectively identical moments she currently occupies. David Lewis was the first to formalize this kind of belief, using what he called 'centered possible worlds,' which specify not just how the world is but who and when you are within it, and he used this framework to argue for the 1/3 solution described above. Adam Elga later pushed back against that argument, defending the more traditional view that the credence should simply stay at 1/2, on the grounds that no rational updating rule should let a person's credence in a proposition about a coin toss shift just because she has temporarily forgotten something. Beliefs about which centered world you occupy, like 'today is Tuesday,' are what Lewis calls de re beliefs, as opposed to ordinary de dicto beliefs about how things stand in the world independently of your position within it, and it is this de re character that makes it questionable whether Beauty can update by ordinary conditionalization at all. Finally, this connects to the Doomsday Argument, which also reasons from your location within a reference class -- there, the class of all humans who will ever be born -- to a conclusion you have no direct evidence for, namely how long humanity will survive. Just as Sleeping Beauty has to weigh how many of her possible awakenings fall under tails, the Doomsday Argument has us weigh how many humans would come after us if the species survives a long time, and treats finding ourselves this early as mild evidence against that outcome. One important disanalogy, though, is that Sleeping Beauty's case involves a single individual who is woken multiple times, whereas the Doomsday Argument's reasoning ranges over many distinct people across history, so it isn't obvious that the same indifference principle used to license the 1/3 credence carries over to license the Doomsday conclusion without further argument."
