In the Incoherence of the Philosophers, al-Ghazālī denies that fire possesses any inherent power to burn cotton: on his account, the two events are linked only because God habitually wills them together, not because there is a necessary connection between them in nature. Since Hume later argued much the same thing—that we never actually perceive a necessary connection between cause and effect, only constant conjunction—al-Ghazālī seems to have anticipated Humean skepticism about causation by roughly six centuries, arriving at it by a different route. Given this, why is al-Ghazālī generally classified as a theological occasionalist rather than simply as an early empiricist skeptic in the mold of Hume?
