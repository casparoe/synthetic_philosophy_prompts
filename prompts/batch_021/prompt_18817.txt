Been on a bit of an ECL kick lately -- evidential cooperation in large worlds, or MSR as i think it got called back when Oesterheld's original thesis was making the rounds on LessWrong. The idea that you should treat your own decision as evidence about what every sufficiently similar reasoner across a huge (maybe infinite) universe decides, so you end up in something like a giant acausal prisoner's dilemma with value systems you'll never make causal contact with, is one of those ideas i find completely compelling for about ten minutes and then completely insane for the next ten. I've tried explaining it at a couple dinner parties now and it always comes out sounding like either technobabble or a UFO cult recruitment pitch, so id rather see it done properly as fiction than keep butchering it out loud myself.

Could you write me a short story (something like 1000-1800 words) built around a character who actually has to *act* on ECL reasoning, not just muse about it over drinks? Setting's up to you -- a far-future civilization deciding how to spend its cosmic endowment, a lone crewmember past the point of any possible causal contact with home, whatever hook works -- but I want the plot to actually turn on the mechanics: why this only makes sense if you're running something like evidential rather than causal decision theory, and why the genuinely hard part in practice is figuring out who counts as being in your "correlated" reference class, rather than just some warm fuzzy cosmic solidarity with anyone who happens to seem nice. Please don't have a character explain the idea in a lecture to another character -- I want the tension to show up in what they actually choose to do, and I want the obvious objection ("why give up anything for agents I'll never meet or verify") to get a real hearing instead of being waved away.
