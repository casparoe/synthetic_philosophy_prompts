I'm serving on my university's committee that reviews building- and program-naming proposals this year (we grandly call ourselves the Honors and Nomenclature Committee, mostly we just approve things named after dead donors), and we've hit a case I can't just wave through. A very generous alum wants to endow a "Harry S. Truman Institute for Diplomacy and Statecraft," and the naming subcommittee was ready to say yes on the strength of Truman's post-war record -- Marshall Plan, NATO, all that -- until one of our colleagues, who works on just war theory, brought up Anscombe's old campaign against Oxford giving Truman an honorary degree back in the 50s. Her point, as he relays it, wasn't just "the bombs killed a lot of people," it was that the whole strategic logic of Hiroshima and Nagasaki was to kill enough civilians to break Japan's will to keep fighting, which makes the killing of innocents the means and not a side effect, and that no downstream consequences -- however many lives supposedly saved -- can launder that into something other than murder.

Another committee member, a WWII historian, doesn't think that settles anything: both cities had real military and industrial targets in them (an army headquarters, major shipyards, that sort of thing), so on double-effect grounds this might not be the clean case of intentional civilian-targeting Anscombe assumes, and anyway the invasion of Japan being planned instead was projected at the time to be far bloodier for everyone, Japanese civilians included, so refusing to weigh consequences at all looks like its own kind of moral recklessness. A third member just wants us to average out Truman's whole career and move on, which feels to me like its definately dodging the actual disagreement rather then resolving it.

I have to circulate a short memo to the full committee ahead of Thursday's vote, and "it's complicated" isn't going to cut it as a recommendation. Could you draft that memo for me -- accept the name, reject it, or accept with conditions (renaming, a contextual plaque, whatever) -- making sure it actually engages with Anscombe's argument on its own terms and gives the historian's strongest points a fair hearing rather than just noting that they exist?
