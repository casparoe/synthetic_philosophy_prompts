So I've been circling a piece about this "does the model actually represent that?" debate that's been bubbling up around interpretability research, and I sketched out an argument last night that I'd love for you to poke holes in before it goes anywhere near my editor. The trigger was the Golden Gate Claude thing Anthropic did back in 2024 -- they used a sparse autoencoder to pull a single "Golden Gate Bridge" feature out of superposition in Claude 3 Sonnet, clamped it way up, and the model started bringing up the bridge in basically every answer, even ones about how to cook pasta. Most of the coverage at the time (mine included, a little) treated this as basically confirming that there's a real, discrete internal representation of the bridge just sitting in there waiting to be found. My draft pushes back on that a bit using two things I half-remember from a philosophy of cognitive science class years ago: Ramsey's "job description challenge," the idea that you only earn the label "representation" for some internal state if it's actually being used or consulted the way a map is used, not just reliably correlated with the thing it's "about," and Dennett's "real patterns" move, where a pattern counts as real to the extent it buys some observer compression and prediction, which is a much weaker and more observer-relative claim than "the network has a concept of the bridge."

Where I get stuck is that the strongest card the realists have is that clamping the feature actually changes downstream behavior, which looks like the feature is being "used" rather than merely correlated, and I can't tell if that fully satisfies the job description challenge or just relocates it one level down. Could you tell me (a) whether im using Ramsey and Dennett fairly here or mangling them, (b) whether theres an obvious reply from either side that I'm missing entirely, and (c) whether this is even the right philosophical lens for what's going on with sparse-autoencoder features, versus some other debate I should be reading up on instead. Also flag it if the whole thing is too inside-baseball to work for a general "ideas" audience -- I keep going back and forth on whether the piece needs a whole section just explaining what "superposition" even means before any of this can land.
