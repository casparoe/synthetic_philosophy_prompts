Been down a rabbit hole on "commitment races," an idea from AI safety and game theory circles that's stuck in my head. Start with the classic game of chicken: whoever visibly throws their steering wheel out the window first wins, since the other driver's only move left is to swerve. Now picture a world full of agents -- AIs, nations, whatever -- all smart enough to know that trick and able to make genuinely irreversible commitments. Everyone's suddenly incentivized to lock into an aggressive stance as fast as possible, before they've properly thought it through and before the other side locks in first, because pausing to reflect just means losing the race. Worst case, both sides succeed, end up with incompatible commitments neither can walk back, and land on a disaster nobody wanted that a bit more patience would've avoided. Theres also this cruel twist where being the more careful, reflective party is actually a weakness, since it makes your "irrevocable" commitment less believable to the other side.

Think this'd make a great short story. Could you write one built around that exact mechanic -- the race to commit before you're ready, and the trap of not being able to un-commit after -- rather then just a generic conflict story? Setting's your call, AIs or otherwise.
