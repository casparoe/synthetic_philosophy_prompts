So my uncle Gary just retired after 41 years running the hardware store on Main St and I got roped into giving the toast. Backstory: I've been kind of obsessed with Ben Franklin's autobiography for a few years now, especially the part where he decides he's gonna become literally morally perfect and makes that daily chart tracking his 13 virtues. I got Gary into it too, and he actually ran his own version of the chart for a while (badly, he'd be the first too admit it). His favorite bit in the whole book, and mine, is the speckled axe story near the end, where Franklin admits he never actually got anywhere close to perfect and basically makes peace with that. Here's the problem: half the room is family who've heard him joke about "the chart" for years with zero idea where it's even from, and the other half is his old book club buddies who've actually read the Franklin book and will 100% notice if I butcher the point of the speckled axe thing, or make it sound like Gary came out the other end some perfected guy, which is basically the opposite of what he and Franklin both landed on. Can you write the toast? Four minutes tops, needs to be warm and genuinely a little funny, not a lecture about Franklin, and it can't end with Gary sounding like a finished product.
