I spent my working life as an observational cosmologist: an instrument-builder and watcher of skies, never once permitted to lay a hand on the thing being studied. Since retiring I've been working through Woodward's *Making Things Happen* and Lewis's old papers on counterfactual dependence, and I keep snagging on this: manipulability reads like a theory built with a chemistry bench in mind, and it survives contact with astronomy or paleontology only by retreating to interventions that are merely hypothetical -- at which point I'm no longer sure what distinguishes it from Lewis's counterfactuals in graph-theoretic dress. I've been asked, as the token scientist with no stake in the fight, to give the eight-minute opening remarks for a workshop putting philosophers of causation in a room with astronomers, paleontologists, and historical linguists -- people for whom the relevant intervention can never actually be performed. Please write those remarks: state the difficulty fairly to both sides, take an honest position on whether Woodward's hypothetical interventions quietly concede Lewis's point, and close on a live question for the room rather than a verdict -- no throat-clearing definitions either camp could recite half-asleep.
