I'm about a third of the way into a novel and I've hit a research snag more than a writing one. One of my POV characters, a mid-career lawyer named Priya, sits on a fictional national Law Reform Commission, and a whole subplot turns on a vote the commission takes on whether to recommend repealing an old public-decency statute as its being applied to a new fad: licensed, consensual "extreme modification" parlors doing voluntary branding, tongue-splitting, subdermal implants, that kind of thing, for adults who show up sober and sign a mountain of waivers. I want to actually put the commission's internal memo on the page -- the one Priya drafts the night before the vote -- and it needs to hold up as a real piece of institutional writing, not just a monologue dressed up as a document.

Years ago I audited a jurisprudence seminar and we spent a week on the old Hart-Devlin fight that came out of the Wolfenden Report -- Devlin's argument that a shared morality is part of what holds a society together and that the law can step in to protect it even where nobody's been harmed in the Millian sense, versus Hart's response that this proves a lot less than Devlin thinks it does. That's basically the shape of the disagreement happening on my fictional commission, just relocated from what Wolfenden was actually dealing with to body-mod parlors, and I'd love the memo to genuinely channel that fight rather than a generic "some people are uncomfortable with this" vibe.

Could you draft the memo? I need Priya to actually land somewhere -- repeal, keep the statute as-is, or license-and-regulate as a middle path -- with a concrete timeline attached, and whichever way she comes down, I need her to take the other side's strongest version seriously, not the strawman where its just squeamishness, since a couple of the other commissioners (and probably a chunk of my readers) are going to find that side more compelling than she expects going in.
