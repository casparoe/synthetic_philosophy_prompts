I grew up in a pretty religious household but drifted away from all of it in my twenties. This past year I actually tried going back to it seriously — praying every night, even doing the whole out-loud-talking-to-God-alone-in-my-room thing, going to a few different services, reading, journaling, genuinely trying to stay open to whatever was supposed to happen. Nothing did, though. No sense of presence, no answered prayer, nothing that felt like more than me talking into a void for a year, and its been bugging me since.

I mentioned this to a close friend who's very devout, kind of expecting some sympathy, and instead she said something like: if you're truly not sensing anything after putting in that much effort, its probably because some part of you is still resisting on some level, maybe pride or some sin you haven't owned up to, because in her view God reveals himself to anyone who honestly and fully seeks him, no exceptions. That stung, but it also nagged at me for a different reason — it feels like no matter what happens, her explanation can't lose. If I ever do find faith down the line, she was right all along. If I never do, she's still right, I was just resisting somewhere I couldn't even see myself. Is there something actually broken about an argument shaped like that, or is she onto something real that I should be taking more seriously instead of just getting defensive about it?
