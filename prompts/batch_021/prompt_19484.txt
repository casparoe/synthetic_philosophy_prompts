I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole reading about Newton's secret theological life for a seminar I'm running on early modern science and religion, and a colleague in the history department and I were arguing about it over coffee last week. Almost nobody realizes that the guy who gave us the Principia spent decades privately convinced that the doctrine of the Trinity was a corruption cooked up by fourth-century churchmen, and that keeping this quiet took real, concrete effort. Around 1675 he needed a special royal dispensation just to avoid being ordained a priest, which his Trinity College fellowship technically required, and later he wrote up his own textual case against two of the New Testament's main Trinitarian proof texts (1 John 5:7 and 1 Timothy 3:16) and sent it to John Locke to be published anonymously in Holland -- then lost his nerve and pulled it back before it went to press. I think one historian even has a name for the whole strategy, "Nicodemism," after the guy in John's Gospel who sneaks off to see Jesus at night so no one catches him doing it.

It feels like such a pure case study in the gap between what someone actually believes and what they can afford to say out loud, and I think that tension would make a much better short story than another straight biography chapter. Could you write me one (a page or two is fine) that dramatizes that particular bind -- a man who has to spend his whole public life performing a piety he privately regards as a fraud, all while knowing exactly what happens to a colleague who says the quiet part out loud (his own successor in the Lucasian chair, William Whiston, got thrown out of Cambridge for it, and Newton was still around to watch it happen). You dont need to nail every date or invent perfect period dialogue -- I care much more about it being grounded in the actual intellectual bind he was in than about it turning into a generic "genius has a dark secret" story. Pick whatever scene gives you the best hook: the night before the manuscript goes off to Locke, the dispensation letter arriving, watching Whiston's downfall from a safe distance, whatever.
