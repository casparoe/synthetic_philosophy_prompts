Six weeks ago I left a physics postdoc to join a small proprietary trading desk, where I am about to size the first live positions of a systematic strategy I built myself. The head trader insists that every position should be sized at full Kelly, since this is supposedly just what a rational long-run wealth maximizer is required to do; my own reading in ergodicity economics and geometric rationality has left me doubtful, but so far I haven't managed to separate the theoretically well-founded objections to full Kelly from the merely cautious ones. Before I commit real capital, I would rather get the philosophy right than default to a rule of thumb.

Please rank the following six positions on Kelly-style sizing by how defensible they are as pieces of decision theory, from most to least defensible:

A. Full Kelly is mandated because logarithmic utility is simply the correct representation of a rational agent's attitude toward wealth.
B. Full Kelly is mandated independently of any utility function, because it almost surely outperforms every other strategy in the long run (the Kelly–Breiman asymptotic-dominance result).
C. Kelly-optimal growth is the uniquely rational target even for a single bet, because wealth dynamics are non-ergodic and only time averages, not expectation values, are the meaningful quantity (the ergodicity-economics argument).
D. Sizing below full Kelly is justified only as a hedge against misestimating one's edge, not as a distinct rationality principle in its own right.
E. The Kelly framework does not apply outside literal, repeated, independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) gambles, since real-world bets are correlated with one another and a true long run never actually arrives (the Samuelson objection).
F. Full Kelly is only rational relative to the goal of maximizing long-run growth rate; an investor whose actual goal is to maximize the probability of reaching a specific finite target within a fixed horizon should generally choose a different fraction entirely.

Please give your answer as a JSON array of the six letters, ordered from most to least defensible, e.g. ["C", "A", "E", "B", "F", "D"] would mean you judge C the most defensible and D the least.
