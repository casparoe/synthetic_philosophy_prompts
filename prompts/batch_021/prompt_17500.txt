Every summer a bunch of us from my old MFA cohort, all working on novels, do a week-long writing retreat, and the last night always turns into one big, wine-heavy dinner. This year our most buttoned-up guy, who's in his 50s, got properly drunk and ended up crying about feeling like a fraud, then twenty minutes later gave the sharpest, warmest notes on everyone's chapters he's ever given. By the next morning he was mortified, insisting "that wasnt really me." Our youngest member, 24, tried to keep pace with him drink for drink, and something about that rubbed me the wrong way even though I can't say exactly why it's different from what the older guy did. Now one of the other writers wants to make the big drunk dinner an official yearly tradition, since its the only time anyone actually drops the act and tells the truth. I'm the one who organizes these retreats, so I have to decide whether to lean into that or nudge the group toward something more sober next year. Who's got the better instinct here?
