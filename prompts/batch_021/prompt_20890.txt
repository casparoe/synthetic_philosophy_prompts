I've been on a bit of a Kurt Gödel kick lately (started with Hao Wang's book on him), and I stumbled onto something that really surprised me: he apparently laid out, including in letters to his own mother, an actual argument for an afterlife that has nothing to do with religion, built instead on his conviction that the world is thoroughly rational. As best I can tell it goes something like: if the world really is rational, it can't be that so much unrealized human potential and unfinished striving just get tossed in the bin at death for no reason, so there has to be some kind of continuation. As a retired physicist that reminded me of how you don't throw out a well confirmed law because of one weird anomaly, you go looking for a hidden cause instead -- so now I can't stop wondering whether Gödel's reasoning actually holds up or if its just wishful thinking with some extra rigor bolted on. Could you write me a dialogue between two equally sharp people, one defending Gödel's argument on its own terms and one a committed naturalist pushing back hard, where they actually engage each others specific points round by round instead of talking past one another, and neither one gets to win too easily.
