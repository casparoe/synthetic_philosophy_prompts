So I'm about two-thirds of the way through my third novel, a slow-burn murder mystery, and I've hit a snag that's more philosophical than literary, and it's bugging me way more than it probably should. Back in chapter two I planted a small, almost throwaway detail: the victim was terrified of the dark, and everyone who knew him swears he hadn't let the hallway light go off in years, not even to save on the electric bill, yet when the body's found, that light is off. For most of the book this just sits there as one of those odd little facts nobody can explain, my detective included. Then near the end she finally works out her real theory of how the murder happened, and it turns out the theory explains the light being off perfectly, like the last piece of a puzzle clicking into place. She treats that as basically sealing the case in her mind, and I'll admit, writing it that way felt very satisfying to me too.

A friend in my writing group read the draft and said the light thing feels like a bit of a cheat. Her point is that since I already knew that detail long before I'd worked out the actual solution, of course I could go back and build a theory to fit it, and thats not the same as a theory predicting something nobody already knew. Honestly, its been almost a year, and I genuinely can't remember anymore whether I planned the ending around that detail from the start or came up with the ending separately and just got lucky it fit. Part of me thinks she has a real point, but part of me thinks that inside the story, my detective truly did already know that fact and only later found a theory that made complete sense of it, and that has to count as real evidence for her, not nothing.

It's got me wondering about something outside my book too. In general, if you already know some fact for certain, and later a theory comes along that accounts for that fact perfectly, does the fact actually give you good reason to believe the theory, or is that satisfying feeling of things "clicking into place" a little bit of an illusion, given that nothing was ever risked or predicted ahead of time? Basically, where's the real line between a theory being legitimately supported by an old fact it explains, and a theory just being cleverly reverse-engineered to fit something we already had lying around? My writing group is genuinely split on this and it's derailed the last two meetings, so I'd love an outside take.
