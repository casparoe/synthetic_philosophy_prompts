Please describe the most important implications of Ian Hacking's distinction between indifferent kinds and interactive kinds, and his related account of the looping effect, for how I should think about the "gifted kid burnout" pattern that gets discussed so often in the online forums I follow, where people identified as intellectually gifted in childhood report, as adults, chronic perfectionism, fragile self-esteem, and difficulty tolerating ordinary failure. I can't tell whether this pattern mostly reflects some pre-existing trait that the "gifted" label merely detected early on, or whether the label itself, and the different treatment it earned from parents and teachers, played a substantial role in producing the very traits now blamed on it. Does Hacking's framework actually help settle that question, or does it just redescribe the puzzle in fancier terms?
