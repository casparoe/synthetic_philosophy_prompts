In De Corpore, Hobbes argues that a proper definition in geometry must be genetic: it has to specify the motion by which the figure in question is generated, since for him we can have scientia — certain, demonstrative knowledge — only of constructions that we ourselves perform and can trace step by step from cause to effect. This same conviction underlay his decades-long refusal to accept John Wallis's refutations of his repeated claims to have squared the circle; Hobbes dismissed Wallis's symbolic algebra as a manipulation of symbols that obscured genuine geometrical insight rather than revealing it.

Suppose Hobbes were confronted with a result such as the four-colour theorem, whose only available proofs rest on exhaustive case-checking carried out by a computer and never surveyed in full by any human being. Would he count such a proof as scientia in his own sense of the term, or would he take it as an even more egregious instance of the symbol-shuffling he accused Wallis of practicing? Answer with reference to Hobbes's actual epistemological commitments in De Corpore and the Wallis controversy, not to the popular anecdote about circle-squaring alone.
