Longtime readers of Overcoming Bias and LessWrong will recognize Max Tegmark's Level IV "Mathematical Universe Hypothesis" as the closest thing physics has to David Lewis's modal realism: both hold that every consistent way reality could be is realized somewhere, and that our own world enjoys no privileged metaphysical status. Compare the two views on what actually grounds a world's existence and on the theoretical work the resulting plurality is meant to do. In particular, does Tegmark's identification of physical existence with mathematical existence let him escape the "incredulous stare" objection that has long dogged Lewis, or does it merely relocate the same difficulties — indexical actuality, and the individuation of worlds — onto the notion of a mathematical structure? Defend your answer.
