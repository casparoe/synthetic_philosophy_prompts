Galileo explicitly enlists Augustine as an authority for this move, and I would like my character's notes to reflect real discernment about exactly how much of the argument is borrowed from Augustine and how much is Galileo's own contribution, rather than treating "accommodation" as an interchangeable label for whatever either man happened to say.

Could you compare Galileo's use of the accommodation principle in the Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina with Augustine's own articulation of it, for example in his commentary on Genesis? If you could write this as a self-contained comparison of roughly 400 to 500 words, organized so that its distinctions could be turned directly into the substance of a scene rather than into background history I could already find in any popular retelling of the Galileo affair, that would be exactly what I need.
