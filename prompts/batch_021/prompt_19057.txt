Hilbert's program called for a finitary proof that formalized arithmetic could never yield a contradiction, and Gödel's second incompleteness theorem is widely credited with having closed off that project for good. Compare what Hilbert's program actually required of a consistency proof with what Gentzen's argument delivers, making clear why the latter is not simply precluded by Gödel's theorem: does Gentzen's result amount to a partial vindication of Hilbert's aims, or does it concede precisely the point on which Hilbert could not afford to compromise?
