A friend of mine, who is on the academic job market this year, has decided not to bother rehearsing for her upcoming interviews. Her reasoning runs as follows: "Right now, before any interview has even taken place, it's already true either that I'll get an offer or that I won't — reality doesn't wait around for the interview to settle the matter. But if it's already fixed which of those is true, then nothing I do between now and then can be what makes it go one way rather than the other. So all this rehearsing is just theatre; it isn't doing any real work." I suspect there is a flaw somewhere in this reasoning, but each time I try to state exactly where it lies, I run into a subtlety I can't quite resolve on my own. Is she right that her preparation is powerless to affect the outcome, and if not, where precisely does the argument go wrong?
