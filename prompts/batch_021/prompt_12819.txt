I'm a software engineer with zero philosophy background, but because IRBs are required to have at least one non-scientist member, I'm now that person on the board overseeing a university lab that studies split-brain patients (people who've had their corpus callosum cut, usually for severe epilepsy). A postdoc wants to re-enroll one of the lab's long-time subjects in a new study, but at her last consent conversation she was verbally agreeing and signing the form with her right hand while her left hand kept pushing the clipboard away -- and its not the first time her two hands have seemed to want different things. Half the board wants to treat this as an ordinary capacity question and just loop in a surrogate decision-maker; the other half thinks that dodges the actual weirdness, since by every normal test she's a single competent adult who's done these studies for a decade. I have to write the short memo for Monday recommending exactly what we do about her consent going forward. It needs to actually pick one concrete path and seriously engage the strongest objection to whichever path it picks, not just wave the objection away.
