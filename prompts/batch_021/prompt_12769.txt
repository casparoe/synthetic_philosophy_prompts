I spent something like thirty-five years doing condensed-matter physics before I hung up my lab coat, and since retiring I've been filling the gap with philosophy of science, which my old research group finds hilarious ("first superconductors, now Sellars," as one of them put it). Lately I've been stuck on structural realism, partly because it captures something I always felt in my gut as a working physicist — the equations and symmetries kept surviving intact from one theory to the next even when we were told the "stuff" underneath had completely changed (the way Fresnel's equations for light got carried over almost wholesale into Maxwell's theory, ether and all). I started with Worrall's original epistemic version, but then fell down a real rabbit hole of variants: Ladyman and French's more radical "there are no objects, just structure" ontic version, Esfeld's more moderate one where objects survive but only as thin things individuated purely by their relations, and then some newer information-theoretic and category-theoretic proposals that i'll admit I only half follow. My old postdoc, who now teaches a philosophy of physics seminar, and I got into a two-hour arguement over the phone last week about which of these actually survives the obvious objections — the Newman problem about "structure" collapsing into a trivial claim about cardinality, whether "relations without relata" is even coherent, and whether any of them really explain the pattern of continuity we see across big theory changes like Newtonian gravity to GR or classical E&M to QFT.
