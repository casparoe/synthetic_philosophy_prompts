A friend of mine works at a company where two of the managers, Sam and Elena, got into a disagreement over one of their employees. Daniel had always found his job tedious, but when the company announced a bonus for whichever employee showed the largest year-over-year improvement on an internal job-satisfaction survey, he set out to feel differently about the work. He kept a list of things he appreciated about it, spent more time on the parts he disliked least, and by the time the survey came around, insisted he had genuinely come to enjoy the job rather than merely put up with it. Sam and Elena ended up debating the case afterward.

(1) Sam said Daniel doesn't have a good reason to enjoy his job, since the only thing that got him there was the bonus, and the bonus has nothing to do with whether the job is actually enjoyable.

(2) Elena replied that if the enjoyment is sincere now, its origin doesn't matter: plenty of people force themselves to take up running, or to eat vegetables, for reasons that have nothing to do with taste, and nobody says the enjoyment they eventually come to feel is illegitimate just because of how it started.

(3) Sam answered that there's a difference between having a reason to want to enjoy something and having a reason to actually enjoy it. The bonus supplies only the first. If Daniel had instead discovered that the job let him mentor new hires, something he already cared about, that would be a reason for the enjoyment itself, not merely a reason to want it.

(4) Elena pushed back that the distinction doesn't survive contact with the case: Daniel could just as easily have zeroed in on mentoring precisely because it was the most promising route to the bonus. If tracing the history back far enough always turns up the bonus, then the mentoring case is no cleaner than the one Sam is trying to rule out.

Neither of them budged, and I haven't been able to settle it myself. Could you go through the exchange and say who has the better of it at each step, rather than just declaring an overall winner? I'd also like to know whether there's a cleaner distinction that would actually resolve this, whether either of them is groping toward it, and whether Elena's objection in (4) is decisive or just a complication.
