I'm putting together a session for my graduate seminar on formal epistemology and rational choice, and I want to pair Piccione and Rubinstein's 1997 absent-minded driver problem with a discussion of updateless decision theory (UDT) — a connection I don't think my students have ever been pushed to think through carefully.

As a reminder of the setup: a driver on a highway must exit at the second of two intersections that look exactly alike, and her imperfect recall means that, at any given intersection, she has no way of telling whether it is the first or the second. Under the standard payoffs (0 for exiting too early, 1 for driving straight through both, 4 for exiting correctly at the second), the probability of continuing that maximizes her expected payoff, computed before she ever sets out, is 2/3 — what's sometimes called the "planning-optimal" solution. This sits uneasily, however, alongside various attempts to work out what she should do once she is actually at an intersection and has to reason, from the inside, about her own self-locating uncertainty as to which intersection it is — a tension well-known for having prompted a direct published response from Aumann, Hart, and Perry.

I'd like you to compare how this tension is handled by an orthodox causal or evidential decision theorist, who evaluates the choice at each intersection conditional on the driver's current self-locating information, with how it is handled under updateless decision theory, which instead asks what policy the driver would have wanted to commit herself to before acquiring any information at all. Does UDT genuinely dissolve the puzzle, in your view, or does it merely relocate the difficulty — for instance, into the question of what it could mean for a memory-less agent to be bound by a prior commitment of which she retains, by hypothesis, no memory? I would be grateful for your own considered verdict as to which of the two approaches gives the more defensible general account of rational choice under imperfect recall, together with your reasons for preferring it.
