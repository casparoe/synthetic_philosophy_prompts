I'm a software engineer at a startup where we've built a mental-wellness app that sorts new users into categories such as "elevated anxiety risk" based on their onboarding answers, then tailors their check-ins and coping content accordingly. Our product lead argues that our data vindicates the categories: users placed in the "elevated anxiety risk" group are far more likely than similarly scored but uncategorized users from an earlier version of the app to still report anxiety symptoms months later. What she leaves out is that a number of these users have since started calling themselves "anxious types" on our community forum and pushing for features built around that identity, which has already led us to redefine the category's criteria twice. Something about treating the first fact as settling the matter feels off to me, but I can't quite say what. Is she right that the data shows the category is tracking something real and stable about these users, or am I missing something?
