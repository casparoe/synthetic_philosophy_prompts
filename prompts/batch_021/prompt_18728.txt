So a friend and I got into it this week about David Lewis's modal realism -- the view that literally every way a world could possibly be is a way some world actually is, and all these worlds are just as real and concrete as ours, "actual" is just an indexical word like "here" or "now" that picks out whichever world the speaker happens to be in. What really got to me is the moral side of it: if every possible variation of every event happens somewhere, to real people who suffer or thrive just as much as we do, does anything that happens here carry any extra weight? Lewis tries to head this off with something about our reasons for action being tied to the world we're causally embedded in, but honestly it still nagged at me for days after.

I'd love a short story out of this, maybe 1000-1500 words. I don't want the usual "portal to a parallel universe" pulp thing though -- I want it to actually sit with the metaphysics: the worlds can't causally touch each other at all, so no traveling between them, no messages, nothing, and a person's "counterparts" in other worlds are just similar people, not literally them split across timelines. Maybe center it on someone who's absorbed this picture seriously, not just as a thought experiment, and has to live with what it does to their sense of grief, or guilt, or whether being good is even worth the bother anymore. Go wherever you want tone-wise, i just don't want the metaphysics to be decoration, it should be doing actual work in the story.
