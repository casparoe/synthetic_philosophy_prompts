I'd love an outside opinion because I keep flip flopping on who's actually got the better of it at each step.

(1) Callum opened by saying Popper basically dumped Darwinism into the same bin as astrology and psychoanalysis -- his go-to pseudoscience examples -- because "survival of the fittest" collapses into "survival of the survivors" once fitness just means differential reproduction, so the theory can't be falsified and doesn't earn the label science.
(2) Renata said thats too strong: Popper never put the theory of evolution itself -- common descent, the fossil record, all of it -- in that bin, he thought that was extremely well corroborated. His actual target was the most general, unrestricted formulation of natural selection, which he called a "metaphysical research programme," and on his own account metaphysical research programmes can be hugely fruitful for guiding science even without being falsifiable themselves. She also pointed out he walked a lot of this back later, in that Darwin lecture thing, where he said he'd been wrong about testability.
(3) Callum's comeback was, ok, but if he recanted, why does the tautology charge still get treated as a live problem? He TA's intro bio and said the textbook basically defines fitness as reproductive success and then says the fittest reproduce more, which is exactly the circle Popper flagged -- recantation or not, doesn't the criticism still land against how selection actually gets taught?
(4) Renata answered that this is precisely what the propensity interpretation of fitness (she name-dropped Mills and Beatty) was built to fix: fitness is a probabilistic disposition grounded in an organism's actual traits, specifiable independent of whether it happens to reproduce a lot, so "the fittest survive" isn't empty even if the one-line textbook version is sloppy. Bad pedagogy isn't the same thing as a defect in the theory's logic, she said.
(5) Callum's last word was that he'd read somewhere that the "recantation" gets oversold -- that even afterward Popper kept saying the fully general principle of natural selection was close to tautological/metaphysical and only conceded that specific, local applications of it are genuinely testable, so treating the Darwin lecture as a full reversal flattens what he actually said.
