So I spent about thirty years doing observational astrophysics before I retired, and lately I've been going down a philosophy of religion rabbit hole -- specifically the fine-tuning argument for design. A few of us from my old department started a monthly reading group (mostly so our spouses can get some peace), and last month's chapter was a full-throated defense of the argument, cosmological constant and all. Something's been bugging me ever since, and it isn't the stuff the chapter actually spent pages rebutting -- the anthropic principle, the multiverse, Hacking's inverse-gambler's-fallacy worry about inferring a multiverse from a single fine-tuned universe. It's something more basic that didn't even get a paragraph: the argument keeps saying things like "the probability that the constants would fall in the life-permitting range by chance is astronomically small," but most of these constants range, in principle, over semi-infinite or unbounded intervals, and there's no such thing as a normalizable uniform distribution over an unbounded interval. Worse, even if you restrict to a finite range, the "probability" you get depends on which variable your suppose to be uniform in -- the coupling constant itself, its square, its logarithm -- which is exactly the reparametrization headache that used to drive us nuts when choosing "uninformative" priors for Bayesian fits back in my working days (presumably why people invented things like Jeffreys priors, to get a prior thats at least invariant under reparametrization).

So here's what I want: give me the strongest possible argument that this normalizability/measure problem is fatal to the probabilistic version of the fine-tuning argument -- not just an in-principle imprecision that better data could eventually fix, but a problem that shows the key premise was never well-defined to begin with. I know there are replies out there (something about restricting to a narrower, physically-motivated comparison range instead of the full mathematically possible one, and I think I've also seen someone gesture at rescuing things with a non-standard or hyperreal-valued measure), so please take those on directly instead of pretending no one's offered them, and tell me along the way whether reaching for something Jeffreys-prior-like actually helps the fine-tuning defender or just relocates the arbitrariness one level up. I don't need the pro-design side of the whole debate rehearsed for me -- I've read that part already. I just want the measure-problem case made as rigorously as you can manage, with the standard comebacks answered rather than ignored.
