A friend and I spent an entire evening arguing over the strangest section of Turing's "Computing Machinery and Intelligence": the Argument from Extrasensory Perception, in which Turing takes the statistical evidence for telepathy seriously and worries that it could invalidate the imitation game, proposing what he calls a "telepathy-proof room" as a safeguard against it. My friend's position was that once telepathy and clairvoyance are screened out this way, the imitation game is perfectly safe; I remain unconvinced. Give an example of a scenario, distinct from anything Turing himself considers, in which some form of ESP would still undermine the validity of the imitation game even inside a telepathy-proof room, and explain precisely why the room fails to rule it out in that case.
