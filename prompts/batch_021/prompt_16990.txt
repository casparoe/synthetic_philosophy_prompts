I sit on the Player Protection Advisory Committee of my state's gambling commission, and a rule we're revisiting stopped feeling abstract last month when my brother — who set a deposit limit on his betting account eight months ago — called me at 3 a.m., lucid and insistent, asking how to raise it immediately. Current policy lets a user lower their own limit at once but delays any increase for seven days. Several members want that delay shortened or removed: respecting someone's welfare, they say, just means honoring what they actually, presently choose — his clear request simply is his preference. Others reply that the entire point of a pre-commitment tool is to protect a person's welfare against exactly this kind of later reversal, not simply to track it. Please draft the memo I'll bring to our next meeting: recommend whether to keep, shorten, lengthen, or restructure the delay (with a duration, if you'd change it), and engage seriously with the side you reject rather than simply declaring one of his choices his "real" preference and the other one not.
