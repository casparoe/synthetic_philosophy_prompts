In my seminar on Borges and the philosophy of time, we've just spent two weeks working through "A New Refutation of Time," and I'm trying to reconcile it with "The Garden of Forking Paths" for my term paper. In the essay, Borges takes Berkeley's denial of matter and Hume's denial of a persisting self and extends the same reasoning to temporal succession, thereby proving that time does not really exist. Yet in the story, Ts'ui Pên's novel is built on the premise that time genuinely forks into multiple, equally real, simultaneous paths, which seems to presuppose that time (or a whole manifold of times) is real rather than illusory. Since these look like two incompatible metaphysical commitments, articulated within just a few years of each other, how should I make sense of this apparent contradiction in my paper? Is one of the two texts generally read as representing Borges's considered view, with the other treated as a mere thought experiment?
