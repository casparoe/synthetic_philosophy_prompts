My aunt was an analytic metaphysician - material constitution was pretty much her whole research career, all the statue-and-lump-of-clay stuff - and she passed away a few days ago. I've been asked to give the eulogy. She always insisted the standard move there applies to people too: youre constituted by your body but not identical to it, same as the statue and the clay have different persistence conditions (the clay survives being squashed into a ball, the statue doesn't) even though it's the exact same matter in the exact same place at the exact same time. So by her own lights, when the body dies the matter keeps going for a while but 'she' doesn't survive it the way the body does - not because some soul floats off, but because person and body were never strictly identical to begin with, meaning I can't just say 'she's resting peacefully now' as if 'she' and 'the body in the coffin' are obviously the same thing. Problem is half the room is her old philosophy department, who will absolutely clock it if I fudge the identity claim, and the other half is family who've never heard the word 'constitution' used like that and just want to feel better. Can you write the eulogy - it has to actually comfort people using only what her own view allows her to be, without turning into a metaphysics lecture or quietly saying something she'd have marked wrong on an exam.
