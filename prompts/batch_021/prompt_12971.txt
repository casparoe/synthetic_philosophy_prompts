One of my oldest friends, whose dissertation used the evolutionary game theory associated with Brian Skyrms to extend David Lewis's account of convention -- explaining how social norms, not just simple coordination conventions like which side of the road to drive on, become entrenched -- is getting married in three weeks and has asked me, the only member of the wedding party without a philosophy PhD, to give the toast. Half the reception will be people from her graduate program, and I have spent the past week reading enough Lewis to know they will notice if I get the framework wrong or use it lazily. Write me a five-minute toast that opens by describing the couple the way Lewis would: a coordination equilibrium, arbitrary in that other arrangements would have solved things just as well, held together by common knowledge -- then turns on the standard objection to Lewis, that mere self-interested coordination cannot explain why breaking a convention feels like wronging someone rather than merely making a mistake, so that it ends up saying something true about what they actually owe each other instead of just showing off the framework.
