A friend of mine has spent the past year telling me that running a marathon is one of his major goals, and he seems to mean it — he talks readily about race dates, gear, and training plans. In practice, though, he only ever runs when I organize a group session and more or less drag him along: he never goes on his own, quietly cancels whenever I don't chase him up, and looks visibly relieved on the rare occasions I suggest we skip a week. When I've pointed this out, he says the sessions matter precisely because they give him a push he can't give himself, and that needing the push doesn't make the goal any less genuinely his. I can't decide whether to accept that, or to treat how he behaves once the pressure is off as the more honest indication of what he actually wants. Which should I trust more — what he tells me he wants, or what he does the moment nobody is pushing him?
