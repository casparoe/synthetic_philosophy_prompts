I am taking a metaphysics seminar this semester, and something that happened over winter break has stayed with me more than I expected. My younger brother has a severe peanut allergy, but he is scrupulous about carrying an EpiPen. On the handful of occasions when he has accidentally eaten something containing peanuts, he has injected himself within seconds, before any serious reaction could develop, and as a result he has never actually gone into anaphylactic shock. At dinner one night, a family friend argued that since the reaction never actually occurs, it is misleading to go on calling him "allergic" in any meaningful sense; at best, she said, he is a healthy person who happens to carry around unnecessary medication. I was sure she was missing something, but I could not say exactly what, especially once she pointed out that the only moment that would ever reveal the allergy is precisely the moment he makes sure it does not happen. Is she right that his allergy is called into question by the fact that it never actually manifests, or is there a principled way to maintain that he is genuinely allergic regardless?
