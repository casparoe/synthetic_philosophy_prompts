I host an interview series on science and ideas, and next month's guest is an epistemologist whose new book revives a Wittgensteinian response to radical skepticism. Drawing on On Certainty, she argues that our most basic certainties — "hinges" — are neither known nor evidentially grounded, yet aren't arbitrary either, since they are the preconditions that make doubting and reasoning possible at all. Her standard line when pressed is that demanding grounds for a hinge is simply a confusion about what "justified" means. Draft eight to ten interview questions that don't let her rest on that line. Include at least one question on how "arational, not irrational" differs from "groundless, full stop"; one asking her to reconcile the view with Moore's proof of an external world — if the hinge that an external world exists can't itself be known or rationally defended, does that put my ordinary knowledge that I have hands in jeopardy, or not, and why; and one that presses the relativism worry with a live case: if a conspiracy theorist's foundational commitments are just as "arational" as hers, on what non-circular basis, if any, can she call them worse hinges to hold. For each question, add a short follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give.
