So I've been re-reading Eliezer's old Quantum Physics sequence on LW again (peak 2008 blogging, truly) and it dragged me into an argument with a friend on a rationalist-adjacent discord about whether many-worlds actually gets around Bell's theorem or whether thats just cope people tell themselves so they don't have to accept nonlocality. My friend is basically a closet Bohmian — thinks the pilot wave is obviously what's "really" going on — and keeps citing Maudlin's line that Bell's theorem proves nature is nonlocal full stop, no matter which interpretation you pick, because any theory that reproduces the CHSH correlations (up to Tsirelson's bound of 2√2 ≈ 2.828, not just the classical bound of 2) needs some influence connecting the two wings of the experiment. I'm more sympathetic to the Everettian move: there's no single outcome for anything to nonlocally influence, so what Bell's proof actually rules out is local theories with a single definite result at each end, and MWI just denies that assumption instead of denying locality (basically the old Deutsch-Hayden point that Everettian QM stays fully local/seperable even though it violates the inequality). The more we argued though, the more my prior crept up that we might agree about literally every equation and every experimental fact and just be fighting over which unknowable, interpretation-relative thing gets to keep the word "local" — which feels like a dumb thing to have raised our voices about.

Could you write me a dialogue between the two of them actually working through this, calling the Bohmian "B" and the Everettian "E"? I don't want a rehash of "collapse bad, pilot wave good" — I want them steelmanning each other and genuinely in the weeds: what exactly Bell's theorem rules out, how Jarrett's split of the locality condition into parameter independence and outcome independence bears on which one each interpretation is really giving up, and whether Bohmian nonlocality even deserves the name given that the quantum equilibrium hypothesis guarantees you can never exploit it to signal anything. Have B press E on whether "there's no single outcome" really avoids nonlocality or just relocates it into the branching structure (something has to guarantee the branch where Alice sees spin-up lines up with the right branch of Bob — isn't that correlating itself a nonlocal fact?). And have E press back on whether an influence that's provably unusable for signalling earns the label "real" nonlocality, or whether that's Bohmians smuggling in more metaphysics than Bell's theorem actually licenses. I'd like it to end with them landing on a genuinely sharp version of the question: do they actually disagree about physics, or just about bookkeeping, once you notice the two theories agree on every prediction and only come apart in ontology?
