I spent over thirty years running a biology lab, and now that I'm retired I've finally got time to read philosophy for fun instead of just journal articles. Last week I read Schlick's 1927 essay on the meaning of life, and one thing in there surprised me: he argues meaningful activity is basically play rather than purpose-driven "seriousness," but then he's careful to add that play doesn't just mean recreation - it's any activity done as an end in itself rather then as a means to something further, so by his own definition even hard, demanding work could count as "play," as long as its not merely instrumental to some other goal.

That distinction feels pretty relevant to me right now, since my whole career was about as means-end driven as it gets - grants, results, publications, the lot. Can you lay out what you think are the most important implications of Schlick's specific argument for someone like me, coming out of a purpose-driven career, trying to figure out how to spend the years I've got left? Please skip the generic retirement-hobby advice - I want the implications that actually follow from Schlick's own reasoning, including any places where his view cuts against the obvious first reaction.
