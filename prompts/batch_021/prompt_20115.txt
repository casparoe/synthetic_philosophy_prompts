I've judged our small press's annual first-novel prize for four years now, alongside four other novelists (shortlist of six, we pick the winner), and this year it got messy. We tally with a simple points system -- each judge ranks all six books, points run 5-4-3-2-1-0, highest total wins, no discussion before ballots go in -- and it came out over drinks after the ceremony that one judge, convinced her actual favorite couldn't beat the front-runner, ranked the front-runner dead last, well below where she actually rated it, just to tank its total. Now two people on the panel aren't speaking. The steering committee's asked me to write a short memo, due before we lock in next year's rules, recommending whether we keep this system or switch (approval voting and instant-runoff have both been floated). Can you draft it? I want an actual recommendation, not a pros-and-cons list, and I want it to take the case for just leaving things alone seriously before it shoots that down -- also I definately don't want it pretending whichever system we land on will be immune to this kind of gaming, because I have a feeling none of them are. Keep it tight enough that five opinionated novelists will actually read it.
