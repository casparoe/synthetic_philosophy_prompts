In the dispositions literature everyone treats finks and masks as two genuinely different problems for the conditional analysis: Martin's electro-fink kills the simple analysis, Lewis's reformed conditional analysis is supposed to save it by requiring the causal basis to persist and stay causally operative through the stimulus, and then Bird's antidote case comes along and the reformed analysis apparently can't handle that either, so the standard move is to say finks and masks just need seperate diagnoses. Can you argue for that position - that there's no principled distinction between finkish and masking cases - and use it to show why the "reformed CA handles finks but not masks" story people tell doesn't actually track a real asymmetry in the phenomena?
