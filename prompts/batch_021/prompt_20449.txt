My younger sisters getting married in six weeks and asked me to give the toast, which is terrifying since I'm four years into a PhD on early Confucian ethics and she knows exactly how deep I'll go overthinking this. Her fiancé grew up totally secular and individualist, my parents are pretty traditional, and there's real unspoken tension at every family dinner over whether he's "marrying into" this family with actual lasting obligations (holidays, decisions, eventually caregiving) or whether he and my sister see it as just two independent people choosing each other with no strings attached to the rest of us. The annoying part is I think the Rosemont/Ames line is basically right -- that on the classical Confucian picture there's no self behind the roles that could opt in or out, you just are the sum of your relations -- so I can't write the toast where two fully autonomous people simply "find" each other, my parents would hear the falseness in it immediately. But I also can't stand up in front of his friends and tell them he's now constituted by obligations he never chose -- that lands as a lecture, or worse, like I'm taking my parents' side. Can you write me a 4-5 minute toast that's warm and actually funny, works for both crowds, and doesn't fudge the philosophy either way?
