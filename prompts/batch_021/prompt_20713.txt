Every few months, a thread on LessWrong or the EA Forum reinvents the same argument about voting reform, and it always stalls at the same impasse. Someone points out that Arrow's impossibility theorem shows no ranked voting system can simultaneously satisfy unanimity, non-dictatorship, independence of irrelevant alternatives, and unrestricted domain, and concludes that all voting systems are more or less equally compromised. Someone else then objects that this is a non-issue for cardinal methods like range voting, since Arrow's proof is explicitly restricted to social welfare functions that take purely ordinal rankings as input — so a system that lets voters express intensity of preference simply isn't the kind of thing the theorem was ever about. And there the thread usually dies, with neither side engaging the other's strongest move: the pro-Arrow side rarely brings up what actually happens to the impossibility result once you allow interpersonally comparable utility information (à la Sen and the later social-welfare-functional literature), and the range-voting side rarely grapples with whether their cardinal ballots are doing any principled work once strategic voters have an incentive to exaggerate them into de facto approval ballots.

I'd like to see that conversation actually happen. Please write a dialogue between two equally well-informed interlocutors — an Arrovian who thinks something in the spirit of Arrow's result still constrains any preference-aggregation method, cardinal ones included, and a range-voting advocate who thinks moving to cardinal utility is a genuine, principled escape rather than a trick of definitions — as they try to find the actual double crux of their disagreement. Each should engage directly with the specific point the other has just made rather than restating their opening position, and neither should simply talk the other out of their view by the end; I'd rather the dialogue end with both of them having isolated exactly which further question (empirical, mathematical, or normative) their disagreement really reduces to. Please make sure both sides are represented at full strength — I don't want either position played as a strawman.
