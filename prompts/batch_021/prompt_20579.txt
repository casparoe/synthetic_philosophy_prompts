I write the ideas column for a general-interest science magazine, and next month's profile centers on a philosopher of mind who has spent two decades defending the zombie thought experiment against nearly every physicalist objection to it, and who has, more recently, become one of the people journalists call when the question turns to whether a large language model could ever be phenomenally conscious. My editor rejected my first pass at questions as little more than the SEP entry read aloud, so I need this conversation to engage the actual joints of her argument rather than restate the intuition pump for readers who have already encountered it a dozen times.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions, each followed by a brief note on the answer she is most likely to give and a way to press further on it? I need at least one question on why idealized conceivability is supposed to track metaphysical possibility at all, given that the standard physicalist rejoinder invokes Kripke's necessary-a-posteriori cases -- water and H2O, heat and molecular motion -- as proof that something can seem perfectly conceivable and still be impossible; one on how she would handle a type-B materialist who concedes that zombies are conceivable and that the explanatory gap is genuine while denying that either fact settles anything ontological -- the move usually called the phenomenal concept strategy; one that puts Dennett's charge to her directly, that the zombie intuition survives only because nobody actually imagines a full physical duplicate in enough detail, so that the 'conceivability' on offer is really just under-description dressed up as insight; and one on whether the two-dimensional semantic apparatus she needs to block the Kripke objection is independently motivated or was constructed after the fact specifically to rescue this argument. Please end the sequence with a question that forces her to say what, if anything, the argument even means for a system with no biological substrate to be a physical duplicate of in the first place.
