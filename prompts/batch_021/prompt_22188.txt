Supererogation is usually introduced with the simple gloss "going beyond the call of duty," but stated more precisely, the concept harbors a genuine puzzle. A supererogatory act is supposed to be morally better than the alternatives available to an agent, yet also merely permissible rather than obligatory. The trouble is that if an act truly is the best one available, and agents generally ought to do what is best when they are able to, then the act seems to be required after all -- which collapses the very distinction supererogation is meant to mark. Could you give me an intuitive, gut-level sense of why philosophers treat this as a real problem rather than a definitional confusion, and of what it reveals about the relationship between an act being better and an act being required? I'm less interested in a survey of named positions than in actually feeling the force of the difficulty and seeing what generates it in the first place.
