I've read enough Less Wrong and adjacent blogs that the basic pitch of updateless decision theory feels familiar, but one part of it has never sat right with me. In cases like counterfactual mugging, an agent that conditionalizes on new evidence and maximizes expected utility relative to the resulting posterior ends up refusing to pay Omega once it has learned that the coin came up tails, since paying can no longer benefit it; UDT is supposed to avoid this by having the agent evaluate its entire policy against its original, unconditional prior instead. But the policy an updateless agent settles on still assigns a different action depending on whether it learns the coin came up heads or tails, so its behavior obviously tracks what it observes. In what sense, then, has it avoided updating on that observation, rather than simply updating on it and calling the result a "policy" instead of a posterior-guided choice? Is there a substantive difference between updating one's beliefs and updating one's policy, or does "updatelessness" just amount to ordinary Bayesian conditionalization performed in advance and relabeled?
