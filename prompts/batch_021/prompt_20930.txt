Graham Priest's Logic of Paradox (LP) is a three-valued paraconsistent logic in which true and both are the designated values, negation swaps true and false while mapping both to itself, and disjunction takes the maximum under the ordering false < both < true. On this semantics, the law of excluded middle (A ∨ ¬A) remains valid even though LP tolerates true contradictions, while disjunctive syllogism — from A ∨ B and ¬A, infer B — comes out invalid. Please work through the relevant valuations to explain this asymmetry, and then explain why disjunctive syllogism specifically, rather than some other classically valid principle such as addition (A entails A ∨ B), is the one that has to be sacrificed: once a logic retains addition and blocks the inference from a contradiction to an arbitrary conclusion by tolerating truth-value gluts rather than by restricting structural proof rules, abandoning disjunctive syllogism turns out to be close to unavoidable.
