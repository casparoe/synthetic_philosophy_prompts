My closest friend is getting married next month, and because I'm the science-and-ideas journalist in her friend group, I've been handed the maid-of-honor toast, with instructions to "make it smart." She wrote her dissertation defending Bernard Williams's internalism about reasons — the view that a reason to act must connect, through some intelligible chain of deliberation, to something already in the agent's own desires and projects, rather than to a free-standing "external" ought that binds her regardless of what she actually cares about. Her fiancé, also a philosopher, has spent their entire courtship arguing a broadly Kantian, externalist line back at her. I want the toast built around this disagreement, and around Williams's related image of the husband who, mid-rescue, pauses to check that saving his drowning wife rather than a stranger is "permissible" — one thought too many, when the fact that she's his wife should already have been reason enough. Please write the toast, about five minutes long: precise enough that the philosophers in the room recognize real Williams rather than a caricature of him, but moving in its own right for the relatives who've never heard the phrase "internal reasons" in their lives.
