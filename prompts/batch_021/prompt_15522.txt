So I went down a rabbit hole a while back after re-reading Eliezer's old "Where to Draw the Boundary?" post (and the rest of that concept-formation cluster in the Sequences), which is all about how category boundaries get drawn for pragmatic, predictive reasons rather than being purely arbitrary. Satisfying as far as it goes, but it totally sidesteps the actual logical puzzle that's been bugging me since, which is what to say about a sentence like "this is a heap" as you pull grains of sand off one at a time. I ended up on the SEP page on vagueness at like 1am and got stuck trying to actually understand the difference between epistemicism (Timothy Williamson's thing, more or less) and supervaluationism (Kit Fine, Rosanna Keefe, that whole tradition) -- on paper they're both trying to rescue classical logic from the sorites, but I can never keep straight why they're supposed to be such different projects, especially once you get into the "higher-order vagueness" stuff (vagueness about where the vague part itself starts being vague, if that makes any sense). My friend, who's a real logic nerd, keeps insisting supervaluationism is basically epistemicism wearing a trenchcoat, and I don't think that's right but I can't say exactly why not.

Could you write me a dialogue between a committed epistemicist and a committed supervaluationist, standing over a heap of sand and pulling grains off one at a time, each trying to talk the other out of their view? I want real back-and-forth rather than each of them just restating their own position past the other -- so e.g. the supervaluationist should push the epistemicist on why we're supposedly ignorant of some exact grain-count cutoff that nobody could ever locate even in principle (isn't positing unknowable sharp facts just cheating?), and the epistemicist should push back on whether "admissible precisifications" is doing any real metaphysical work or is just semantic bookkeeping dressed up as a theory. I'd especially love it if they got into the higher-order vagueness problem directly -- is "borderline heap" itself a vague category, and what does each side even say about that -- since that's the part where I always loose the thread. Neither one should be an easy win for the other; I want to come away still genuinely torn, not feeling like one side quietly strawmanned the other.
