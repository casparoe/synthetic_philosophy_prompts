So I've been on a bit of an Emilie du Chatelet kick lately (the mathematician who translated Newton's Principia into French, and yeah, was also Voltaire's longtime partner, but turns out she wrote loads of her own stuff too). I got pulled into this essay of hers on happiness, and the part that really stuck with me is where she argues that people who don't actually need to work for a living are in real danger of being miserable from boredom, and that the fix is to force yourself into a genuine taste for study, since thats basically the one source of pleasure that doesn't depend on luck or on other people. Obviously she's talking about aristocrats like herself, but it got me thinking about all the talk of AI wiping out most jobs and maybe some kind of universal basic income down the line, where nobody really has to work anymore. Do you reckon she'd look at that future and see the whole population landing in the exact trap she was warning rich people about? And what would her actual advice be for a world like that - just tell everyone to take up study as a hobby, or would she say something different once you bring in her other big points about needing illusions and passions too?
