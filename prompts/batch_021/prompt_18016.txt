Over dinner last week, a close friend and I spent nearly two hours arguing about an episode from my workplace, and neither of us managed to persuade the other, so I would value a more careful outside perspective on it.

At a team meeting, my department's director, Diane, publicly reprimanded a junior analyst, Priya, for submitting a taxi receipt on which the fare had been quietly inflated by about twenty dollars, calling it "a serious breach of trust" in front of the whole team and implying that anything similar in the future could jeopardize Priya's job. A few days later, a colleague who has been at the company far longer than either of them mentioned, almost in passing, that Diane had done something comparable herself more than a decade ago, back when she was still an analyst and the firm's expense policies were far more loosely enforced. As far as anyone can tell, she was never caught, never mentioned it to anyone above her at the time, and has not done anything like it since moving into management.

My friend's position is that none of this history changes anything: inflating an expense report is wrong no matter who objects to it, and bringing up something Diane did quietly, under looser rules, more than ten years ago, is just an irrelevant "who are you to talk" maneuver that dodges the substance of the criticism rather than answering it. When I pointed out that Diane had since reformed and never repeated the behavior, he treated this as reinforcing his view, since it shows the episode is fully behind her and therefore even less relevant to how she treated Priya. I found this fairly convincing at first, but the more I sit with it, the more I suspect there is a real difference between whether Diane's accusation against Priya was correct and whether Diane, given her history and the authority she now holds over Priya, was the right person to be delivering it so publicly and unsparingly. I cannot quite work out what that difference amounts to, or whether it should have changed anything about how the meeting went. I would like help figuring out whether my friend's argument actually disposes of the issue, or whether my hesitation is tracking something real.
