And here's what he wrote:

"Rawls's argument depends on the original position, a hypothetical situation where people design the basic structure of society before they know their race, class, talents, gender, or even their own conception of the good life. This not-knowing is the veil of ignorance, and it stops anyone from tilting the rules in their own favor. Because nobody knows what position they'll end up in, each party has to pick rules that would be acceptable no matter who they turn out to be, including the worst off person in society.

Since the parties don't know their place in society, they treat every social position as equally likely and work out which principle gives the best outcome on average across all those equally likely positions. This is what leads them to the maximin rule: because you might end up as the least advantaged person, and the stakes of landing at the bottom are so high, the rational move is to pick whichever principle makes the worst off group as well off as possible. That principle is the difference principle: inequalities in income and wealth are only justified if they work out to the benefit of the least advantaged group. Rawls treats this as basically the master principle of justice, so it takes priority over the equal basic liberties principle, since without enough resources to live on, formal liberties don't actually mean much anyway."
