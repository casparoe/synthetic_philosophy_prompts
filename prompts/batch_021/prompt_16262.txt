I spent something like 35 years as a wildlife disease ecologist, and it's only since retiring that I've actually sat down and read moral philosophy properly instead of just gesturing at "sentience" in a discussion section somewhere. A friend from my old department pointed me at the wild animal suffering literature a while back -- Yew-Kwang Ng's stuff on "welfare biology," some of Oscar Horta's papers on suffering in the wild -- and I won't pretend it hasn't gotten under my skin. It's got me re-reading my own old field data in a pretty different light.

Which is relevant because I sit on the scientific advisory panel for a small nature reserve, and we've got an actual decision in front of us. There's a herd of feral goats on one of the reserve's outer islands, no natural predators, and every four or five winters, when a bad one hits, something like a third of the herd starves over a few grim months -- basically our own slow-motion version of the Soay sheep crashes people study at St Kilda, except with goats, and its us who actually has to decide what to do about it. One camp on the panel wants an annual precautionary cull each autumn, to keep numbers safely under what the island can support. Another wants to switch to immunocontraception instead, darting the does every spring, so nothing is deliberately killed -- though it works slower, and does nothing for the goats facing this coming winter. And two of our longest-serving members want to just hold to the reserve's founding charter, which commits us to not "interfering with natural processes" on the island, culling included -- and they're not just being sentimental about it, they've got real ecological arguments for leaving well alone that I don't think should get waved off.

I have to circulate an advisory memo before next month's vote, and I'd like your help drafting it. I want an actual recommendation with a rough timeline attached, not a tour of the three options that shrugs at the end, and whatever you land on, I want it to seriously engage with the strongest case for whichever option or options it's turning down, rather than just making the loosing side sound silly.
