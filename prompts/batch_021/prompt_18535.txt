We'd been going through Dawkins alongside Sober and Wilson's Unto Others, and here's roughly how it went. (1) Warren said the whole dispute dissolves once you take the 'selfish gene' picture seriously: genes are the real unit of selection because they're what gets copied with high fidelity across generations, organisms are just 'vehicles' built to protect and propagate them, and so anything that looks like it needs group selection -- worker sterility, alarm calls, the lot -- bottoms out in kin selection once you actually run the numbers, meaning there's no such thing as genuine group selection, only gene selection wearing a disguise. (2) Noor said that's just false: the trait-group models show you can get selection for cooperation among individuals with zero genealogical relatedness, purely through assortment (green-beard-style recognition genes are the standard example), so it can't be true that it's always secretly kin selection underneath. (3) Warren said fine, but that's still bookkeeping -- any group-selection model can be rewritten as an individual- or gene-level model that makes identical predictions, so arguing about which level is doing the real work is like arguing about which reference frame is the true one in relativity, a matter of convenience rather than a fact about the world. (4) Noor said that equivalence only holds in the tidy additive cases; once fitness effects are non-additive -- synergistic cooperation, say, where a group of cooperators does better than the sum of what each would manage on its own -- the group-level and gene-level partitions can actually diverge and predict different outcomes, so it isn't mere redescription, and even where the numbers do agree, the two framings are picking out different things as causally responsible, which isn't nothing.
