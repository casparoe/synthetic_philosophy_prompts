A friend and I spent most of a long train journey arguing about how much weight coherence arguments can really bear, and neither of us gave any ground. She was defending a view I hear often in discussions of advanced AI: that money-pump and Dutch-book arguments are close to decisive, so that any sufficiently capable, long-lived agent will end up acting as an expected-utility maximizer with respect to some fixed utility function, because any agent that doesn't will eventually be exploited out of its resources. I objected that this proves less than it looks like it proves: non-exploitability only requires that an agent never accept a sequence of trades that leads it, by its own lights, back to something strictly worse than where it started, and an agent can guarantee that much while leaving huge swathes of its preferences incomplete, simply by refusing to trade away what it currently holds for anything short of a strict improvement. She replied that this is not the free lunch it looks like: once an opponent can choose the order in which trades are offered and attach small "sweeteners" to pry otherwise-incomparable options apart, the same style of argument used to money-pump cyclical preferences can be adapted to exploit this sort of incompleteness too -- or else it forces the agent's choice policy to be so tightly constrained that it behaves exactly like a complete preference ordering in every case that matters. I didn't have a fully satisfying answer to that, and we ran out of journey before we ran out of disagreement.

Write a dialogue of around 700 words between two characters holding our two positions -- name them however you like -- in which they actually engage each other's specific moves rather than restate their opening positions. Have them work through the role of the completeness axiom in the standard coherence theorems, whether refusing anything short of a strict improvement genuinely secures non-exploitability once multi-step, sequential offers are allowed, and whether "incomplete but non-exploitable" is a stable resting point for a rational agent or just a disguised version of completeness. Each character should press the other on the specific weaknesses in what was just said rather than on a caricature of their overall view, and the dialogue should not resolve into a clean win for either side.
