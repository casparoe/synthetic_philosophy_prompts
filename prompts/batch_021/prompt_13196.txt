I'm about a third of the way into a new novel and one of my side characters is an old, cranky opera critic, so lately I've been falling down research rabbit holes on philosophy-of-art stuff to get her voice right. That's how I found out that Bernard Williams -- the moral philosopher known for the "internal reasons" essay and for basically dismantling utilitarianism from the inside -- was low-key obsessed with opera his whole life, sat on the board of English National Opera for years, and left behind a whole book of essays on the subject that his wife put together after he died. Something about that combo tickles me: a guy famous for being rigorous and suspicious of neat moral theories spending decades seriously defending an art form where people fall in love in five minutes flat and sing their private thoughts out loud instead of talking like normal humans. A friend who's directing a student production of Così this spring told me Williams didn't try to explain this weirdness away though -- apparently he argued that opera's "irrationality" is bound up with its power rather than being an embarrassment to apologize for, and that he was pretty skeptical of the whole reform tradition (going back to Gluck) that keeps trying to make opera more dramatically "sensible."

Can you actually unpack what his argument was, in more depth than "he thought it was moving despite being silly"? I'm especially curious whether he had something specific to say about what the music-plus-text-plus-singing package lets opera do that a play or a novel structurally cannot -- like, does an ensemble number, where three or four characters are "saying" completely different and incompatible things to the audience at the same time, do something for how we understand the scene emotionally that sequential dialogue on a page or a stage just never could? I want the monolgue I write for this character defending her weird art form to actually have philosophical bones, not just "opera is beautiful, deal with it," so any detail you've got on how Williams really built the case would help a ton.
