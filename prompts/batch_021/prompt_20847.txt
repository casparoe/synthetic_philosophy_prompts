Since I retired from a career in particle physics I've been reading epistemology in my spare time, and it's got me second-guessing something from my lab days that never really got resolved.

For 15 years my closest collaborator Dave and I ran the same detector together. Near the end we found a bump in the count rate sitting around 2.3 sigma, and after independently combing through the data we landed on opposite calls - he was convinced it was a real signal, I was convinced it was calibration drift. We're equally good statisticians and neither of us budged even after comparing full writeups line by line. A postdoc at the time said that since we were equally qualified to judge this, we should each just shrug and downgrade to something like 50/50 confidence instead of digging in.

That never sat right with me, becuase I'd personally logged way more hours on that specific detector's quirks than Dave had, even though he's arguably the sharper physicist overall - and if it matters, his track record on these calls has historically been a bit better than mine anyway.

Was the postdoc right that we should've just split the difference, or is there a real basis for one of us to reasonably stick to his guns here?
