I have been teaching myself about AI risk from books and online writing, and one thing that keeps bothering me is how comfortably the orthogonality thesis and the instrumental convergence thesis are usually presented together, as though there is no tension between them. The orthogonality thesis says that an agent's level of intelligence is independent of its final goals, while instrumental convergence says that almost any final goal will tend to generate the same small cluster of subgoals in a sufficiently capable agent — self-preservation, resource acquisition, cognitive self-improvement, and protecting its own goals from being changed. But if goal content is genuinely independent of intelligence, it seems like it should be possible to specify a final goal for which none of those standard subgoals are actually useful, so that a highly capable agent pursuing it would have no reason to bother with them. Could you work out a detailed example of such a goal, explain carefully why an agent pursuing it would plausibly forgo the usual convergent subgoals, and then say whether the example really holds up under scrutiny, or whether convergence has a way of creeping back in even in cases that look, at first, like genuine exceptions to it?
