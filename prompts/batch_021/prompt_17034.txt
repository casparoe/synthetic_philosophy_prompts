Me and my friend Dana got into it over drinks last night about something that started as a dumb work story and spiraled into a two-hour arguement neither of us won. Dana works at a small app startup, and apparently whenever they launch in a new city, someone higher up literally flips a coin to decide the size of the invite-only beta: heads means they only let in 50 people, tails means they let in 50,000, and the beta users themselves are never told which one happened. Dana got an invite to be a tester in her city last month.

I told her that the mere fact she got the invite should already make her think it's way more likely her city got the 50,000-person rollout rather than the 50-person one -- like, before she looks at any other clue (how crowded the app feels, how fast support replies, whatever), just knowing "I'm a beta user" should shift the odds hard toward the big version, since she'd be one random person out of a much bigger pool if that's what actually happened. Dana thinks this is nuts. Her whole point is that she was always going to find herself as a beta user no matter which way the coin landed -- that's not a coincidence, its basically a precondition of her even having this argument with me -- so it can't be evidence of anything. She also kept saying "compared to who, though?", which I still don't fully get but which seemed to be doing a lot of work for her side.

Neither of us backed down, and it's been bugging me since for a bigger reason: if I'm right that you can update like that, it feels like the exact same move would let you conclude, with zero telescopes or data, that the universe almost certainly has trillions of alien civilizations rather than just a handful, purely from the fact that you exist at all. That seems way too cheap to be a legit way of learning about reality, but I can't pin down the flaw. So who actually has the stronger argument here, me or Dana? Is her "compared to who" jab pointing at something real? And if my reasoning really does lead somewhere absurd like the alien thing, where exactly does it break down?
