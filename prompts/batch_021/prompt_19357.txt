I'm a retired marine biologist -- spent most of my working life studying pain and stress responses in octopuses and cuttlefish, of all things -- and since retiring I've been slowly working my way through a pile of philosophy of mind books I never had time for before. A few of us from the old lab still meet up most Thursdays, technically for cards, but lately it's turned into a bit of an informal philosophy reading group instead.

Right now I'm stuck on David Rosenthal's higher-order thought theory of consciousness, and last week we got into a real argument about one specific wrinkle in it. As I understand it (correct me if I'm garbling this), Rosenthal says the higher-order thought that makes a state conscious doesn't actually have to correspond to any real first-order state at all -- it can misfire and represent you as being in pain when there's no genuine first-order pain signal there, or it can just fail to fire when there IS a strong first-order signal, so that "what it's like" to be you ends up hostage to a monitor that can get it wrong in either direction. One friend thinks this makes the whole theory a little absurd; another thinks its actually the most honest part of it, since real brains confabulate and misfire all the time anyway.

Anyway, I'd love to see this played out in fiction rather then just argued about over cards. Could you write me a short story built around some mind -- human, animal, alien, or engineered AI, whatever setting appeals to you -- where this exact gap between a first-order state and a higher-order monitor that can misrepresent or simply fail to represent it is the actual engine of the plot, not just background flavor? I don't want a generic "maybe the machine is secretly conscious" story; I want that specific Rosenthal-style possibility, a higher-order thought coming completely uncoupled from whatever is really going on underneath, to be doing real dramatic work, with some genuine stakes riding on getting it wrong in either direction.
