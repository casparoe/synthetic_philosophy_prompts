A friend and I spent the better part of a long dinner arguing about Rawls, and by the end he had very nearly convinced me of his view: that the argument from the veil of ignorance to the difference principle, via the maximin rule, is really just risk-aversion dressed up in technical vocabulary. On his telling, parties in the original position protect the worst-off position for the same reason anyone buys insurance — they would rather sacrifice expected gain to avoid a catastrophic loss. If that is really the mechanism, doesn't the whole argument simply fail to apply to the sizable minority of people who are risk-seeking rather than risk-averse, which would make the difference principle a far more parochial conclusion than Rawls presents it as being?
