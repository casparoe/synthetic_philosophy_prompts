Back when I still had a working lab, I spent a good chunk of the 80s and 90s measuring specific heat curves near the critical point in a couple of different magnetic salts, watching that lovely, sharp-looking divergence as you crept up on Tc. Needless to say I never had anything close to an infinite number of spins in a sample, just a few grams of material with an enormous but still very finite number of atoms in it. Now that I'm retired I've been chewing through philosophy of science books between gardening chores, and I ran into an argument, associated mostly with Robert Batterman, that goes roughly like this: a genuine phase transition requires an actual non-analyticity (a mathematical singularity) in the free energy, but the partition function of any finite system is just a finite sum of smooth exponential terms and so is perfectly analytic everywhere (something to do with the zeros of the partition function only pinching the real axis once N goes to infinity, if I'm remembering the Lee-Yang stuff right). So strictly speaking you only get a real phase transition in the thermodynamic limit, and from there Batterman and others want to conclude that phase transitions are a genuine case of emergence, something over and above what the statistical mechanics of the actual, finite, particle system delivers on its own.

This has been bugging me for weeks now, and my old book club (mostly other retired physicists, which probably tells you something) couldn't agree on it either. Something about the argument feels like its smuggling a mathematical idealization into a metaphysical conclusion, but I can't quite pin down where it goes wrong, or if it does. Could you argue against this Batterman-style conclusion for me? I mean specifically: make the strongest case you can that needing the N-to-infinity limit to rigorously define a phase transition does NOT show that phase transitions are ontologically emergent, over and above the underlying finite statistical mechanics. Bring in universality and the renormalization group flow if it helps the case, since that's usually invoked in the same breath and I never got a satisfying answer on how it bears on any of this either.
