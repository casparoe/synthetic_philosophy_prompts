For my decision theory seminar this week we did a whole session on Parfit's hitchhiker and I honestly can't stop thinking about it. Quick recap in case it's useful: you're dying of thirst in the desert, a driver comes by who's basically a perfect lie detector, and she'll only drive you into town if she's sure you'll pay her once you get there -- but once your actually standing in town safe and sound, you have zero causal reason to hand over the money, so a purely self-interested causal decision theorist just keeps their cash, which means the driver (who saw that coming) never would've picked them up to begin with and they'd have died in the desert. My professor calls this whole dynamic "rational irrationality" -- it's rational to want to be the kind of agent who'll do the locally "irrational" thing later, even though doing it in the moment looks like a total loss by ordinary self-interest. She also threw in this idea of "resolute choice" as the alternative to being merely "sophisticated" about your own future unreliability, which half the seminar is still arguing about over email.

I want something I can actually tell my study group without everyone just staring at me blankly.
