So this is going to sound pretty niche but bear with me. About eight months ago I started taking sports betting way more seriously (I know, I know) and got into bankroll management, which is how I ended up down the Kelly criterion rabbit hole — bet a fraction of your bankroll proportional to your edge, maximize long-run geometric growth, don't go bust. Fine, made sense. But then a friend of mine doing a physics PhD sent me down a much deeper rabbit hole on "ergodicity economics" (the Ole Peters stuff about ensemble averages vs. time averages), plus some LessWrong sequence on "geometric rationality" — I think it's by a guy called Scott Garrabrant — that apparently runs the same move on bargaining, and even on how you'd combine different people's utilities. Now he's convinced that all of this basically overturns expected utility theory as the foundation of rational choice: growth-rate/time-average reasoning is supposedly the "real" rational thing, and expected-value reasoning with a utility function bolted on is just people quietly using the wrong average and calling it a preference. I got kind of swept up in it too honestly, its a genuinely cool idea, but something about it smells like people rediscovering log utility and then acting like they discovered a new continent.

I vaguely remember Samuelson tore into the "maximize expected log wealth" crowd decades ago, to the point of writing a paper entirely in one-syllable words just to mock them (something like "Why We Should Not Make Mean Log of Wealth Big Though Years to Act Are Long"?), and I'd rather have that side of the argument laid out properly than half-remembered off a Twitter thread. Could you write me a real argument, one I can actually send my friend, for the position that Kelly betting and "geometric rationality" more broadly are not a genuinely new foundation for rational choice under uncertainty, just expected utility theory with log (or isoelastic) utility smuggled in and relabeled as physics? I really want it to engage directly with the ergodicity/non-ergodic-observable point rather than just wave it away, since that's the part of his argument that sounds like an actual objection and not merely rebranding — and I'd like it to be honest about which bits of the Kelly/geometric side's case survive the "it's just a utility function" charge, if any do.
