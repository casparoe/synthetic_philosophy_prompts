I'm giving the toast at the wedding of two friends from my economics PhD program. He built his dissertation on Robbins's claim that interpersonal comparisons of utility are not something science can settle, only a value judgment dressed up as one; she works in the tradition of Harsanyi and Sen, which holds that some degree of interpersonal comparability has to be readmitted before welfare economics can rank social outcomes at all, and has spent more than one conference Q&A dismantling his papers on exactly this point. About half the guests will find this very funny; the rest just need to know the two of them have been arguing productively since the day they met. Write the toast (four or five minutes): it needs one real joke about the impossibility of interpersonal utility comparisons that works for both halves of the room, and it has to arrive, honestly rather than cleverly, at why two people who each think the other's account of comparing welfare across persons is confused are nonetheless exactly suited to spend a life doing precisely that for one another.
