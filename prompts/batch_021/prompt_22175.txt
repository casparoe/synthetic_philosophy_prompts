Kierkegaard's 1846 work Two Ages, composed in the immediate aftermath of his public ridicule at the hands of the satirical newspaper The Corsair, diagnosed "the public" as a bloodless abstraction summoned into being by the press, and "leveling" as the process by which this abstraction erodes individual distinction, uniting people not through shared passion or conviction but through envy toward anyone who stands out. Against this he posed "the single individual," who resists the pull of the crowd and answers to conscience rather than to opinion.

How would Kierkegaard likely assess today's social media environment, in which individuals can publish directly to mass audiences under their own name, a curated persona, or complete anonymity, and in which algorithms rather than editors decide what circulates? Would he regard any of this as a genuine vindication of the single individual against institutional gatekeeping, or would he see it as leveling achieved more thoroughly, and more anonymously, than the nineteenth-century press he battled ever managed? Ground your answer in his specific concepts and vocabulary rather than in generalities about his thought.
