I'm entering a short-story contest for my high school's literary magazine, and I want to build my submission around acausal trade: the idea, from some decision-theory reading I've been doing, that two agents who can never communicate or causally affect one another in any way might still each act to benefit the other, simply because each is confident that a sufficiently rational counterpart would reason the same way and do likewise — the sort of reasoning behind Hofstadter's "superrationality" and, more recently, "functional decision theory." To keep this from collapsing into a generic "we'll-never-meet" story, I'd like the impossibility of contact to be a hard fact of the setting — for instance, the two parties are already beyond each other's cosmological event horizons, so the universe's own expansion guarantees no signal could ever pass between them, even in principle — and I'd like neither side to ever be fully certain the other one exists, only that it's rational to act as if it does. Could you have at least one character explicitly raise, and then talk themselves past, the objection that none of this matters, since nobody could ever detect or punish a broken promise? Something in the 800–1200 word range would be great.
