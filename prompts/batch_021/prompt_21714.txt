I'm writing a piece on AI and the future of mathematical proof, and one result I keep returning to is MIRI's 2016 "logical induction" framework. Garrabrant and his coauthors proved that a computable reasoner's credences in mathematical statements are guaranteed to converge on the correct truth value over time, often long before a conventional proof exists, all while remaining logically coherent in the limit. Given that guarantee, why do mathematicians still insist on producing formal, human-checkable proofs for open problems like the twin prime conjecture or the abc conjecture, rather than simply running a logical inductor and accepting a sufficiently stable, near-certain credence as settling the matter once enough computing time has passed?
