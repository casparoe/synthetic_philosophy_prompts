So Kelly betting says you should size bets to maximize expected log wealth (the geometric growth rate) instead of plain expected value, because with repeated compounding bets, going broke even once is way worse than a simple arithmetic average of outcomes would suggest. Some people generalize this into a whole "geometric rationality" thing -- using geometric-mean aggregation instead of expected value more generally, not just for bankroll bets (I think this is basically what Scott Garrabrant's posts on the topic are doing, though I might be misremembering the name). Argue that this same logic actually gives a principled reason to reject "fanatical" expected-value arguments in x-risk/longtermism debates -- that treating extinction as a ruin outcome, the way Kelly betting treats hitting zero wealth, justifies discounting tiny-probability astronomical-payoff arguments, rather than that being just a vague intuition that something's off with them.
