Since I retired I've gotten pretty into philosophy, and right now im down a Newton rabbit hole - not the Principia stuff everyone knows about, but his letters to Richard Bentley where he argues the solar system's whole layout (all the planets going round the same way, roughly the same plane, sizes and distances all suspiciously convenient) is too tidy to come from blind mechanical causes and needs, if I'm remembering his phrase right, a "voluntary Agent" behind it. Basically a proto fine-tuning argument, a good century before Paley's watch. Here's what I keep chewing on: if Newton somehow got to sit in on a modern physics seminar and heard how most cosmologists answer fine-tuning with a multiverse plus some anthropic selection effect instead of a designer, what do you reckon he'd say? Given his run-in with Leibniz over whether God has to keep "rewinding the clock" to stop the planetary orbits from decaying, would he dismiss the multiverse as just a fancier version of the "nature did it alone" explanation he already rejected to Bentley, or is there something in his own framework that would actually let him make peace with it? Genuinely want a reconstructed argument here, not just "he was religious so obviously he'd say no."
