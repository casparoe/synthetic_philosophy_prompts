So a couple of people in the AI safety reading group I'm part of (we just meet over Zoom every other week and argue about papers) got into it after last session, which was on the shutdown problem, and it kept going in the group chat for like two days after. I saved the thread because I wanted to think it through myself, but I've gone back and fourth enough times that I want another opinion. Roughly reconstructed (Dan and Rosa aren't their real names), it went like this:

1. Dan: "I think people overrate how hard this is. Just penalize the agent whenever it interferes with the shutdown button during training. Done."

2. Rosa: that's the exact naive fix the old MIRI corrigibility paper argues against — if the agent is otherwise an expected-utility maximizer over outcomes, whether it gets shut down IS part of the outcome, so "not resisting" isn't one clean behavior you can train against. It'll still have an incentive to shift the probability of shutdown through any channel that isn't literally grabbing the button — stalling, selectively informing you, making itself indispensable, whatever.

3. Dan: fine, then use Armstrong's utility indifference trick — add a correction term so the agent's expected utility comes out the same whether the button gets pressed or not. Then there's nothing to gain by pushing on the probability either way.

4. Rosa: indifference brings its own baggage though. It can make the agent weirdly averse to learning things that would shift the shutdown-relevant odds, since that messes with the correction term's math (I think this is what people call the "managing the news" problem, or something close to that), and the fix doesn't obviously carry over to any sub-agents it builds. Even the original corrigibility paper admits this is a patch, not a solution.

5. Dan: okay, so use the newer move — give the agent incomplete preferences so it literally can't compare trajectories that differ in when shutdown happens (something about "timestep dominance"), and then there's nothing for it to optimize with respect to shutdown timing in the first place.

6. Rosa: but that just relocates the problem — an agent with incomplete preferences is exactly the kind of agent the money-pump arguments say gets exploited into completing its preferences, or loses out to a rival that already has. So "the agent messes with its shutdown button" turned into "the agent has an incentive to fix its own incompleteness," which doesn't sound like progress to me.

7. Dan: money-pump arguments only bite if the agent is naive enough to walk into the pump instead of just refusing any trade that leaves it predictably worse off. Staying incomplete and never accepting a dominated sequence of trades aren't mutually exclusive, are they?

Nobody backed down before everyone had to hop off the call. Could you go through moves 1 through 7 and say, at each step, who's actually more right, not just who you'd bet on overall? I'm especially curious whether Rosa's move 4 is a fair hit on indifference specifically rather than a generic "everything has problems," and whether move 7 actually answers move 6 or just dodges it.
