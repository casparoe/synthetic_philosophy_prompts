Two friends of mine, Marcus and Dana, got into an argument on the drive back from a camping trip that neither would drop. It started when Marcus claimed that nothing can really be true unless someone could, at least in principle, discover it -- otherwise, he said, calling something "true" is no different from calling a story true. Dana objected that plenty of things must be true right now that nobody will ever discover, such as the exact number of leaves that fell from trees somewhere in the world yesterday. Marcus said that was fine -- the number is still discoverable in principle, just not actually discovered.

Dana then had him consider a related fact: not the leaf count itself, but the fact that "the count is such-and-such, and nobody currently knows it." Marcus agreed this must be true too, and so, by his own rule, discoverable in principle. Dana argued it can't be: the moment anyone discovered that whole fact, they would thereby know the leaf count, making it false that nobody knows it -- so this fact could never be discovered by anyone, however good their tools. She pushed further: the same trick works for any fact that's true and unknown, so Marcus's rule seems to commit him to something absurd -- that there's no such thing as a true, unknown fact, i.e., that everything true is already known by somebody.

Unwilling to accept that, Marcus tried to save his view by restricting it to plain, ordinary facts, excluding facts about who does or doesn't know something. Dana replied that this fix doesn't just exclude a few odd cases: every true, undiscovered fact has a paired fact of exactly that form, so the exception swallows the very territory his claim was supposed to cover, rather than trimming off some marginal curiosity. They dropped it there, both a little annoyed, and I haven't been able to stop turning it over since.

Who was right at each stage of this exchange, and is there anything important that neither of them considered? I'm especially curious whether Marcus can hold on to something like his original idea without it collapsing the way Dana says it does, or whether he really has to give it up.
