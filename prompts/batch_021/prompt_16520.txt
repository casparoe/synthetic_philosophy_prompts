This term I'm taking a seminar on aesthetics, and a situation from outside of class has been nagging at me in a way that feels related to some of what we've covered. I co-run my university's film society with a friend whose taste in cinema is far more refined than mine, and I've fallen into the habit of relaying her verdicts as though they were my own: when a member asks whether a film we're screening is any good, I'll say something like "the script drags in the second act, but the lead performance holds it together," even though I haven't actually seen the film myself. This doesn't trouble me at all when I'm passing on a straightforwardly factual claim of hers, such as whether the director really shot on 35mm rather than digital; I'm perfectly happy to take her word for it and repeat it to others. But doing the same thing with her evaluative judgments feels subtly dishonest, even though the structure seems identical in both cases: trusting someone whose judgment I respect and passing along her conclusion. Is there a genuine asymmetry between these two cases, or am I just being inconsistent?
