A close friend of mine was diagnosed with adult ADHD last year, after she brought her doctor a checklist of traits she had found circulating online. In the months since, she has become active in several ADHD communities on social media, adopted their vocabulary ("executive dysfunction," "time blindness," "ADHD paralysis"), and started redescribing large stretches of her childhood and work history in those terms, even though she used to tell the very same stories quite differently before her diagnosis. Her psychiatrist mentioned, almost in passing, that the formal criteria for the diagnosis have themselves loosened over the past decade or so, partly in order to capture adults and women whose presentation looks different from the hyperactive young boys the category was originally built around.

Her partner, who has known her for well over a decade, has grown quietly skeptical of all this. He does not deny that she has always struggled with focus and follow-through, but he worries that the label, the online community, and the loosening criteria are together now producing a good deal of what they claim only to describe, rather than simply naming something that was already there before any of it. My friend finds this suggestion insulting, and points out that people said equally dismissive things about other psychiatric diagnoses that are now considered entirely legitimate. Having watched this same disagreement resurface over several dinners, I cannot decide whether her partner has identified a genuine problem with her diagnosis specifically, or is simply describing something that would be true of more or less any psychiatric category, and so cannot really count against this one in particular. Is there a principled way of telling these two possibilities apart, and if not, how should my friend and her partner reason about the diagnosis in the meantime?
