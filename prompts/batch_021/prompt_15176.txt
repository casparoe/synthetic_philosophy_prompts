For my AP Psychology research project on hemispheric lateralization, I would like to close my presentation with a dialogue rather than another summary slide. Write a dialogue between two philosophers of mind sharing a coffee break at a conference on consciousness: one defends the traditional view that commissurotomy ("split-brain") patients instantiate two simultaneous, independent streams of consciousness, while the other defends Tim Bayne's "switch model," on which a single subject's consciousness merely alternates rapidly between the disconnected hemispheres. Do not let them simply restate their conclusions: have the two-streams theorist press on how a mere switch could occur within one brief chimeric-face presentation and still produce two different, simultaneous reports, and have the switch-model theorist push back on whether sustained dual-task performance -- each hand occupied with a different task at once, seemingly without either interfering with the other -- really requires two full subjects rather than one hemisphere's contribution simply going non-conscious, the way walking does while we talk. Have both sides also address cross-cueing as a confound for whatever evidence they lean on, and, if it fits naturally into what they would say, weigh in on the 2017 Pinto-group findings that some split-brain patients can still integrate information across the two visual fields to a surprising degree. Neither should concede more than their position genuinely allows, and the dialogue need not end in resolution. Aim for around 450 words.
