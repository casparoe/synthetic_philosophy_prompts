Write a dialogue between a Pudgalavadin -- the old Buddhist "personalist" school that insisted the pudgala (person) is neither identical to the five aggregates nor completely different from them -- and Vasubandhu, who thinks thats incoherent and that a person is nothing but a causal series of aggregates we slap the label "person" onto. Get them fighting over the crux the Pudgalavadins actually cared about: can pure reductionism explain memory, and who reaps the karmic fruit of an act decades later, without quietly smuggling back in something that just functions as a self? Vasubandhu already "wins" this exact fight in the appendix to his Abhidharmakosha where he refutes the pudgala theory, but that's kind of unfair since he wrote both sides -- so this time make sure the Pudgalavadin gets their strongest moves in too (like why "neither same nor different" isn't just doubletalk), and have Vasubandhu genuinely engage instead of just steamrolling them. Want a real back-and-forth between equals, not a curbstomp.
