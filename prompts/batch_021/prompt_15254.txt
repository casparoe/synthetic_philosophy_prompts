My friend and I spent an evening arguing about whether the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem is fatal to buck-passing accounts of value, and we never quite agreed on whether the standard object-given/state-given distinction actually dissolves the problem or just relabels it. I argued that a demon's threat gives you a state-given reason to admire something, not an object-given one, and that this difference does real explanatory work rather than merely gesturing at value. My friend replied that "object-given" only ever cashes out as "a reason that tracks the value we were trying to analyze in the first place," so the distinction quietly reintroduces the very evaluative facts that buck-passing was supposed to reduce to facts about reasons.
