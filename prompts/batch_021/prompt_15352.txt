Write an essay on whether Harsanyi's aggregation theorem actually gives us good reason to accept utilitarianism. People cite it a lot as basically deriving utilitarian social welfare from pretty minimal axioms -- vNM rationality for both individuals and society, plus a Pareto condition over lotteries -- but there's two classic problems that seem to undercut the "proof of utilitarianism" reading. First, Sen's point that vNM utility functions are only unique up to seperate affine transformations for each person, so nothing in the theorem actually pins down the weights in the aggregate sum. Second, Diamond's fair coin-flip case, where the theorem forces indifference between randomizing an indivisible good and just handing it to someone outright, as long as the expected utilities match -- which seems to throw out any independent value that fair process has. Weigh how damaging these really are (is there a good Harsanyi-style reply, or does one of the axioms have to go) and actually commit to a verdict instead of just landing on "it's complicated."
