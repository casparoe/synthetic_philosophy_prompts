I'm a software engineer on my AI lab's pre-publication review board. Our interpretability group wants to publish a finding: they've isolated a direction in the model's activations that tracks which answer it 'knows' to be correct, and this direction still points to the truth even when the model is prompted into stating a falsehood. The abstract says the model 'represents the correct answer internally but chooses to lie to the user.' Half the board thinks that once you've localized an internal correlate like this, words like knows and lies have earned something more literal than mere behavioral shorthand would give them -- close to what I gather philosophers call the 'intentional stance.' The other half wants every mentalistic verb replaced with neutral statistical language, since readers will hear 'lies' as a claim about conscious, culpable deception that the finding doesn't establish. Please draft the board's decision memo, due before Friday's meeting: recommend approving the language, rejecting it, or approving it with specific required edits, with reasons the team could act on, and give a genuinely fair hearing to the strongest version of whichever side you don't pick.
