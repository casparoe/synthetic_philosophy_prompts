So this is a genuine dispute on the little grants circle I help run (five of us, we pool money and fund weird high-EV longshots, kind of a mini ACX Grants). One of our regular applicants -- a guy who does decision theory as a hobby and is always trying to get people to take his bets -- pitched us a wager instead of a normal proposal. Terms: flip a fair coin repeatedly til it first lands heads; if that happens on flip n, the payout is 2^n/n dollars, which he owes us if n is odd and we owe him if n is even. He's already said he'll offer the same bet again at 100x stakes if we take it this time, so the committee actually cares about getting the principle right and not just the roughly 69 cents on the table. One member summed the payoffs in the obvious order (n=1, then 2, then 3...) and got that clean positive EV, so wants to take it. A second member pointed out you're allowed to regroup that exact same infinite list of numbers a different, equally legitimate way and get a smaller total, and wants everyone to slow down. A third member says they're both making a category error, because the series is only conditionally convergent, not absolutely -- meaning it can apparently be rearranged to sum to any number at all, or to no number at all -- so "the expected value of this bet" isn't unknown, it's just not a well-defined quantity, full stop. I've got a nagging feeling this is a reskin of the "Pasadena paradox" thing from decision theory (Hajek's, iirc?), which I'd only ever seen as a cute example in a blog comment section and never expected to actually have to rule on.

Our charter just says we fund "whichever pending proposal has the highest expected value," clearly written by someone who assumed that phrase always names a number. I need to send the committee a memo before we vote Friday -- can you draft it? I want an actual bottom line (take the bet, decline it, ask for different terms, whatever), not a "here are three equally valid takes" cop-out, plus one line of standing policy for how we handle the next proposal shaped like this. And please give a fair hearing to the strongest version of "just take it," since nobody on the committee can point to an actual expected loss here, only to the fact that the number won't sit still.
