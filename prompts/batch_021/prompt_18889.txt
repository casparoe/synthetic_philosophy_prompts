I'm a philosophy PhD student specializing in just war theory, and I sit as the outside ethics consultant on my country's Conscientious Objector Review Board. Current Board policy grants CO status only to conscripts who object to war "in any form" -- selective objectors, who reject one particular campaign as unjust while affirming they would fight in, say, a clearly defensive war, are denied automatically, whatever the sophistication of their reasoning.

The file in front of me is from a conscript who says he would have served without hesitation in a war of national self-defense, but invokes the revisionist rejection of the moral equality of combatants -- the line most associated with Jeff McMahan -- to argue that he cannot permissibly fight in the present campaign. Our blanket rule quietly relies on something closer to Walzer's traditionalist picture, on which combatants are neither competent nor entitled to judge their state's cause, and so incur no special guilt for fighting on the unjust side provided they respect jus in bello.

Draft the memo I will bring to next week's review: a ruling on this file (grant, deny, or refer it for a discretionary exception), plus a recommendation on whether the Board's categorical exclusion of selective objectors should change going forward. Engage honestly with the strongest institutional case -- unit cohesion, the unverifiability of sincerity, conscripts' limited access to the classified reasoning behind any given war -- for keeping the rule even if the revisionists turn out to be philosophically correct.
