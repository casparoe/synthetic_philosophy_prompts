In my philosophy of science seminar this week we got into structural realism, and I feel like I only half get the difference between the 'epistemic' and 'ontic' versions -- both sides seem to agree that all we're really entitled to believe is the structure of a theory (the relations, not whatever it is that's standing in them), so I can never tell if the two camps are disagreeing about something real or just using different vocab for the same idea. What made it click a little was when my professor brought up this old objection from Newman (a 1928 review of Russell's structuralism, I think?) -- the point being that just claiming 'there's some structure here' is almost trivially true once you've got enough objects to work with, since you can basically always cook up some set of relations among them that fits, so the whole structuralist claim threatens to collapse into something empty unless you add more constraints.

So can you write me a dialogue between an epistemic structural realist and an ontic structural realist arguing about whether Newman's objection actually lands on both of them equally, or whether the ontic realist's move -- saying there's no layer of 'bare' objects behind the relations in the first place, relations without relata or whatever -- actually gets them out of it in a way the epistemic realist cant match. I don't want either of them to be a pushover for the other; they should really engage with each other's specific moves (like whether "no relata" is even a coherent thing to say, and whether smuggling in modal or causal structure to dodge Newman just quietly reintroduces the kind of non-structural fact they were supposed to be doing without). It's fine if they don't fully resolve it, I just want to see them actually grapple with each other rather than talk past one another.
