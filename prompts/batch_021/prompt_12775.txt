I have been working through the political dimensions of logical empiricism, and there is one puzzle about Carnap specifically that I have not seen addressed head-on in the secondary literature I know. His official meta-ethical position, set out in places like his 1935 *Philosophy and Logical Syntax*, treats value and normative statements as expressions of attitude or disguised commands rather than as candidates for truth or falsity — they fall outside the kind of verifiable, unified science he and the rest of the Vienna Circle wanted to build. Yet Carnap was a committed socialist from his early involvement in the pre-war German Youth Movement onward, and he plainly read the "scientific world-conception" announced in the 1929 Vienna Circle manifesto as continuous with, rather than neutral between, a progressive program of social and economic planning of the kind Otto Neurath had tried to put into practice and went on to theorize about for years afterward. What I would like from you is not a general primer on non-cognitivism, but a careful working-through of this specific tension. If normative claims have no theoretical content on Carnap's own semantics, what, if anything, entitled him to argue for planning over laissez-faire rather than merely expressing a preference for it? Is there a principled way of carving off a purely technical, means-ends question — given some fixed set of social ends, does planning serve them better than the alternatives? — from the question of which ends to adopt, in a way that keeps Carnap's actual political engagement safely on the technical side of that line? Or does his practice in the end commit him to more than his stated semantics can license?
