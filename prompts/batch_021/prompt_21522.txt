My uncle Frank sat on the criminal bench for almost thirty years, and at every family dinner he'd hold forth on how nobody deserves blame or credit in some deep ultimate sense, it's all just causes stretching back before you were born, so drop the whole idea of 'desert.' Funny thing for a judge to believe for thirty years. He died Tuesday, and since I've published two novels I'm apparently the designated eulogy-writer now, the problem being that Frank was also quicker to forgive then anyone I've ever met and would well up with gratitude over the dumbest little kindnesses, and I don't want this eulogy to treat that warmth like some inconsistency we're all politely ignoring today. I've got a foggy memory of a philosopher named Strawson who supposedly explains how you keep all the resentment and gratitude and love fully intact even after you've thrown out 'desert,' no contradiction required, but I never understood it well enough to write it myself. Half the room -- his old clerks, one philosophy pal from his reading group -- will catch it if I get this right; everyone else just needs something honest that doesn't turn into a lecture. Can you write me the eulogy?
