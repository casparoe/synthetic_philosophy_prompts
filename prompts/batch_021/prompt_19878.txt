This actually happened to me last week and it's still bugging me. At poker night, a friend who trades for a living showed me that the odds I'd quoted across a few side bets could be combined into a book that guaranteed him a profit no matter which cards came up. He called it "Dutch-booking" me and said it proves my beliefs about the game were flat out irrational. That stung, so I read up on it, and turns out philosophers are actually split on this: some think being exploitable like that really does show your credences are irrational, others think that's the wrong kind of argument entirely and that the real case for having coherent credences has to rest on something else (accuracy/truth-tracking, nothing to do with betting). Honestly I can't tell wether he even earned his gloating. Could you write me a dialogue between someone defending the classic Dutch book argument and someone who thinks that whole approach is the wrong way to justify coherence and argues for the accuracy-based alternative instead -- real back and forth where they actually engage each other's strongest points, feel free to have them hash it out over my case specifically, rather than just two people restating their own view past each other.
