It started when someone brought up the standard 'acausal trade' framing: agents who can never causally interact -- different Everett branches, causally disconnected patches of an eternally inflating universe, whatever -- can still benefit each other by each doing what the other would want, conditional on the other reciprocating.

(1) Owen, who's basically been raised on Yudkowsky's old decision theory posts, said the whole framing is a category error: it's not trade, just parallel independent reasoning by correlated agents dressed up in trade-talk, since 'trade' implies an actual bargaining relationship, and each side here is just unilaterally doing the locally correct thing given its beliefs about correlation -- exactly like superrational agents 'cooperating' in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma without either one influencing the other.
(2) Mara pushed back that trade has never actually required causal influence, only correlation between one party's choice and the other's. Even an ordinary handshake deal only works because you predict the other guy will hold up his end, not because your compliance physically causes his, so if that's enough to disqualify acausal coordination from counting as trade, plenty of real-world trades made under uncertainty shouldn't count either.
(3) Owen conceded the correlation point but said theres still a real disanalogy: in ordinary trade there's always at least some possible channel, however unlikely, by which your action could become known and change what the other party does -- reputation, a witness, anything. In genuinely causally disconnected cases there's no channel even in principle, so 'trade' is the wrong word for it; better to call it 'decision-theoretic coordination via shared priors' and drop a metaphor that implies a relationship which literally cannot exist.
(4) Mara answered that 'channel' is doing sneaky work here: what actually licenses trade-talk is that each party's action is conditional on a reliable model of the other, and a physical channel is just the usual, contingent way that conditional dependence happens to get established -- logical dependence between sufficiently transparent reasoners can license it just as well with zero physical contact. She granted this makes real proposals for acausal trade (with actual aliens, not toy identical AIs) way shakier than the clean thought experiments, since you'd need real confidence in a correlation you can never check, but insisted that's a difference of degree, not of kind, and that Owen was smuggling a practical worry into what he'd presented as a conceptual objection.
