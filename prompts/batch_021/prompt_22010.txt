So a few months ago I tried one of those sensory deprivation float tanks — total darkness, the water's the same temp as your skin so eventually you lose track of where you end and it begins, dead silent — and it messed with my head in a good way. Afterward I went down an internet rabbit hole and landed on this old argument from a medieval philosopher named Avicenna (or Ibn Sina, apparently he's got two names). He imagines someone created in a single instant as a full-grown, fully rational adult, but suspended in mid-air, blindfolded, with their limbs spread apart so they never even touch each other, and with zero sensory experience of any kind to draw on before that moment. His claim is that this person would still be completely certain that they themselves exist, even while having no clue whatsoever whether they have a body, hands, a stomach, anything at all. He uses that to argue our sense of "self" doesn't come from awareness of the body, and from there builds up to the much bigger claim that the self (or soul) is a completely separate thing from the body.

What's been bugging me is this: doesn't that setup only show you can be dead certain about one thing while totally clueless about a second thing — not that the two things are actually different? Like, I can be 100% sure the guy who made my coffee this morning exists, while having zero idea whether that same guy happens to be, unbeknownst to me, my landlord's son. Being sure of one and ignorant of the other obviously doesn't make "the barista" and "my landlord's son" two seperate people, it just means I haven't connected the dots yet. So maybe the floating-guy thought experiment only proves something about what we happen to know, and then quietly slides into the bigger claim that self and body are actually distinct.

Could you come up with a fresh example — your own scenario, not the float tank or the barista — that really zeroes in on this exact gap? I want one specific enough that it's genuinely debatable whether "certain about A, clueless about B" is real evidence A and B are separate, or just evidence we haven't realized yet that they're the same thing. And say which side of that line you think your own example actually falls on.
