I'm taking a course this term on Cambridge philosophy between the wars (Moore, Russell, early Wittgenstein) and we just spent a week on Frank Ramsey, who I'm now a little obsessed with given he did all that logic and economics AND, somehow, wrote the best thing I've ever read on the meaning of life before dying at 26. My grandma passed away last week, and it turns out she'd been quietly obsessed with Ramsey for decades too -- she found his essay "Epilogue" back in the 70s and basically built her whole outlook around that one bit about the stars being no bigger than threepenny bits, because he draws the world in perspective instead of to scale (her other favourite fact to trot out at parties was that Ramsey's own brother went on to become Archbishop of Canterbury, which still kind of blows my mind). She left my mum a note about the funeral saying she didn't want any vicar-y stuff about her watching over us or being in a better place -- she was a proud, lifelong atheist, very firm about it -- but she was just as clear she didn't want it tipping into the opposite thing either, no "we're just specks on a speck in an indifferent universe," because she always said that's the exact inference the essay was written to block, not confirm.

Somehow I've ended up being the one giving the eulogy on Saturday, which is terrifying, because it's mostly going to be her old actuarial collegues and family who've never opened a philosophy book in their life, so it actually has to comfort people rather than sound like a seminar. Could you write it for me? I want it to really use Ramsey's move rather than just wave at it -- the perspective bit, and maybe a light touch of the "present value at compound discount" line, since she was an actuary and thought that line was hilarious. If you can also fit in one sentence, tops, about how Ramsey elsewhere called discounting future people's welfare "ethically indefensible" in his own economics -- she always said that contradiction was the most Ramsey thing about Ramsey -- that'd be perfect (just don't let it turn into a lecture). But honestly the main thing is just getting the comfort right without saying anything she'd have rolled her eyes at. Keep it to about five minutes read aloud.
