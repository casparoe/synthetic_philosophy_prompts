For a scene in which he drafts a private rebuttal, I need the strongest argument available to him from within Anscombe's own moral framework—not the utilitarian "it shortened the war" defense, which she explicitly treats as exemplifying the consequentialist reasoning she spent her career attacking. Drawing on the doctrine of double effect that she herself accepts in her writing on just war, construct the best possible argument that the two cities were struck as legitimate military-industrial objectives, with civilian deaths as a foreseen, proportionate, but unintended side effect rather than the direct object of the attack. Please engage directly with the historical complication that the Target Committee's own selection criteria weighed the psychological impact on Japanese leadership and world opinion alongside military value, and conclude whether a double-effect defense can really survive that fact.
