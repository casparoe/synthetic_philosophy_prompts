So this is a bit random but bear with me. I had a health scare a few weeks ago (turned out fine) that sent me spiraling about how nothing I do matters given how vast and old the universe is. A friend doing a philosophy PhD sent me this bit from Frank Ramsey — apparently in some talk he gave to a group of friends at Cambridge in the 1920s (later published as "Epilogue", I think?) he argues that thinking your life matters less because the universe is big is basically a category error, like assuming the people in the foreground of a photo are less real just because they look bigger than the mountains behind them. It actually calmed me down for like a day, but then I noticed it doesn't touch the version of the worry that actually bugs me, which isn't about size at all — it's that everything eventually gets erased (heat death, whatever), so nothing sticks around long enough to matter either. Could you write me a dialogue where a Ramsey-style defender of "scale is irrelevant to meaning" actually goes back and forth with someone pressing that impermanence version of the worry? Neither one should just restate their opening position, I want them responding to each other's actual best points, and I don't want Ramsey's side to win by default just because his line worked on me for a day.
