so this has been bugging me for a while, feels like it should have a clean answer. there's this idea that's been floating around alignment circles (i'm pretty sure DeepMind even ran an actual study on it) where you use Rawls's veil of ignorance to pick the values/fairness principles an aligned AI should implement — get people to choose distribution rules without knowing which position in society they'll end up occupying, then just go with whatever they converge on. everyone treats this like it elegantly sidesteps the whole 'whose values' problem, since your not aggregating anyone's actual stated preferences. but isn't that just aggregation with extra steps? behind the veil each person is presumably still just maximizing their own expected outcome across all the positions they might land in, so whatever pops out the other end should just be the population average — i.e. utilitarianism wearing a trenchcoat. why do people treat Rawlsian veil-of-ignorance bargaining like some principled alternative to just averaging preferences, rather than a fancier way of doing the same math?
