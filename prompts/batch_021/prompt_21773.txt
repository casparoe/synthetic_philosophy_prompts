So here's what's been bugging me for weeks now. I spent thirty-five years as an analytical chemist before I retired, so I like my numbers precise, which is exactly the problem. I was diagnosed with a slow-growing prostate cancer a couple months back, and my oncologist gave me a ten-year survival percentage drawn from a big study of "men like me." I still get journal access through my old department, so I had a former student pull a few of the actual papers, and it turns out that depending on exactly how you slice up "men like me" - by age bracket, by the precise Gleason grade, by how the PSA responded to the first round of treatment - the number shifts by fifteen or twenty points, and these aren't sloppy studies, they're all well done. Narrow the group enough to match my exact profile and there's only a handfull of men in it worldwide, and the number pretty much stops meaning anything. My son says I'm just an old scientist who can't stand not having one clean answer and should stop shopping for whichever study I like best. But it feels to me like the trouble isn't that I don't know the number yet, its that there might be no single fact about "my" true odds, since it depends on which group you decide I belong to and there's no obvious place to stop narrowing. Is my son right that I'm overthinking this, or is something real going on here? And practically, how should I reason about treating aggressively now versus watchful waiting, given how slippery "my chances" turns out to be?
