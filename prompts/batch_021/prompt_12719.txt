The question set for the assignment was this: "Two entangled photons are sent to two distant observers, Alice and Bob, who each independently choose one of several possible measurement settings and record one of two possible outcomes. Using Bell's theorem, explain why the correlations predicted by quantum mechanics, and confirmed in experiments such as Aspect's in the early 1980s and the loophole-free tests of 2015, cannot be reproduced by any local hidden-variable theory. Your answer should also address two further points: (a) does the violation of the Bell inequality mean that some influence or signal travels faster than light between Alice and Bob, and how does this square with special relativity? (b) does Bell's theorem show that the outcomes of quantum measurements cannot be determined in advance by any prior state of the universe?"

And here is the answer I've been asked to grade: "Bell's theorem grows out of the 1935 Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen paper, which argued that if two particles that have interacted are later separated, and if we assume locality (nothing done to one particle can instantly affect the other) together with the idea that each particle really has definite properties before it is measured, then quantum mechanics must be an incomplete description, since it does not assign values to those properties in advance. Bell turned this into a testable claim: any theory built on those two assumptions, local hidden variables, has to satisfy a specific inequality relating the statistics of Alice's and Bob's outcomes across different setting combinations. Quantum mechanics predicts correlations that break this inequality, and experiments from Aspect's in the early 1980s through the loophole-free tests of 2015 confirm the quantum predictions, so no local hidden-variable theory can be right.

On (a): even though Bob's particle behaves as if it 'knows' what setting Alice chose the instant she chooses it, this does not let anyone send a message faster than light. Special relativity only forbids matter or energy from crossing space faster than light, and what is passing between the particles here is a correlation rather than a physical signal, so there is no real conflict with relativity.

On (b): because Bell rules out local hidden variables, and hidden variables were the only way to keep the outcomes fixed in advance, the theorem shows that measurement outcomes are genuinely random and not determined by anything, local or not, that existed before the measurement."
