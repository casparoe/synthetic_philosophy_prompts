A few years ago I bought an electric guitar that the seller told me had once belonged to a moderately well-known blues guitarist who died in the 1990s. I've since learned that this isn't true: what I actually own is a guitar built much later by a highly skilled luthier to match the original in every visible and audible way -- same model, same wood and hardware, even the same wear marks and small modifications, aged to look exactly right. A specialist told me that short of dismantling the instrument and checking hidden stamps, there's no way to tell it apart from a genuine period guitar that really did belong to him. It plays and sounds exactly as it did the day before I found this out, yet it no longer feels the same to me -- it's just 'a guitar' now, not 'his guitar.' A friend of mine says that reaction doesn't make sense, since nothing about the object itself has changed, only what I believe about it, so nothing about its value should have changed either. I suspect she's missing something, but I can't put my finger on what. Is there something to my reaction, or is my friend right that I'm just being irrational about it?
