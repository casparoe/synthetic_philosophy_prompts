I moved from an academic background in mathematical logic into industry AI safety work three months ago, and I've just been asked to write my first recommendation for our lab's internal safety review board. The proposal we're evaluating: our verification team wants to replace manual release sign-off with an automated "reflective certification" pipeline. Every updated model must ship with a proof, carried out in the model's own logic, that whatever this same certification procedure approves is in fact safe -- a guarantee meant to hold across every future generation of the model, not just the release in front of us. A colleague from our decision-theory group is circulating a memo arguing that the whole scheme runs headlong into the Löbian obstacle and should be rejected outright; the verification team insists she's inflating a fact about idealized formal systems into a constraint that doesn't really bind their bounded, engineering-grade provers. I need to circulate the board's one-page recommendation before Thursday's vote -- approving, rejecting, or approving with specific redesign conditions -- and it has to take the verification team's pushback seriously rather than simply repeating the theorem at them.
