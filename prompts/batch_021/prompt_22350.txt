The Allais paradox is typically presented as a counterexample to the independence axiom of von Neumann–Morgenstern expected utility theory, yet the very same pattern of choices is also often said to violate Savage's sure-thing principle from subjective expected utility theory. Compare these two axioms directly: one is formulated over lotteries with stipulated objective probabilities, the other over acts defined on states of the world whose probabilities need not be objectively given. Are the two ultimately the same rationality requirement stated in different formal settings, or is there a principled reason a decision theorist could reject one while retaining the other? Justify your answer by explaining, in some detail, exactly how the standard Allais choice pattern violates each axiom.
