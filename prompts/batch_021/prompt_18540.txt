So I've fallen down a rabbit hole I can't climb out of. I'm not a philosophy person at all, I just read wikipedia way too late at night, and I ended up on this idea from decision theory called a "money pump." From what I can tell: if your preferences go in a circle -- say you like A better than B, B better than C, but then, weirdly, C better than A -- a smart enough dealer can walk you through a chain of trades that you'd each agree to on their own (trade C for B plus a small fee, then B for A plus a fee, then A for C plus a fee) and at the end of it your holding the exact same thing you started with, just lighter in the wallet. What really hooked me is that people don't treat this as "you just made one dumb trade," they treat it as being about whether you're rational over TIME, like the problem only shows up once you string the choices together in a row. And apparently there's a real fight about the fix: some people say the lesson is just "don't have circular preferences, period," but I also saw this term "resolute choice" where the idea seems to be you're allowed to keep the loopy preferences as long as you commit to a plan up front and refuse to budge later even when the next single trade looks amazing on its own -- kind of like Ulysses getting tied to the mast so the sirens can't talk him into anything.

I brought this up with my brother, who plays alot of poker, and he said it sounded exactly like guys at his table who keep "upgrading" a hand or a read through a string of side bets that each seem smart in the moment but somehow leave them broke by 3am. That's when I decided I wanted an actual story about this instead of another explainer, so here's the ask: write me a short story where a con artist or a shark (cards, sneakers, crypto, whatever setting fits) has noticed that the mark's preferences cycle like that and quietly walks them through trade after trade. I'd like the trap itself to be something a reader could piece together just from watching the trades happen, not something a narrator stops to explain, and I want a real moment near the end where the mark either catches the pattern and holds the line even though the next trade looks amazing all by itself, or doesn't -- your call which way it goes, I just want that moment to actually cost something either way.
