In my philosophy of probability seminar this week, we covered Bertrand's paradox as the standard illustration of what goes wrong with Laplace's classical principle of indifference once one moves from finite to continuous sample spaces. The version we discussed asks for the probability that a chord drawn "at random" on a circle is longer than the side of an equilateral triangle inscribed in that circle: depending on whether one (a) fixes two independent uniform points on the circumference, (b) fixes a uniform radius and then a uniform point along it, or (c) fixes a uniform point inside the disc and treats it as the chord's midpoint, one obtains 1/3, 1/2, and 1/4 respectively, and nothing in the phrase "at random" specifies which of these constructions is intended.

Our TA generalized the lesson well beyond chords, arguing that the paradox shows indifference reasoning to be globally unsalvageable as a way of generating probabilities from pure ignorance: two people who disagree about which parametrization is "natural" will each walk away with a number that looks, from the inside, equally well-motivated, with no further fact to which either could appeal in order to settle the disagreement. When a classmate raised, in the discussion section afterward, Jaynes's attempt to save the principle of indifference by requiring the answer to be invariant under the circle's rotations, translations, and rescalings, our TA replied that this move only relocates the arbitrariness by one level, since demanding invariance under exactly those transformations, rather than some other set, is itself an unmotivated choice presented as though it were a technical fix. Given this, for my statistics capstone I am estimating a scale parameter about which I have no genuine prior information, so it seems to follow that I might as well default to whatever prior is most computationally convenient (say, a flat prior on the parameter itself) instead of spending time deriving the Jeffreys prior for it, since neither option can really claim to be any more "principled" than the other. Which convenient prior would you recommend for a scale parameter like this, given that the usual "objective" alternative apparently has no defensible advantage over it?
