My partner Alex and I have been together about five months, and this Thanksgiving was the first time I met their whole extended family. Everything was fine until dessert, when Alex's uncle Gary (an engineer who's gotten really into intelligent-design arguments over the last couple years) and Alex's aunt Rosa (who teaches AP Bio) ended up in a long argument about whether evolution even counts as science, which somehow turned into a whole thing about Karl Popper specifically. I mostly just sat there eating pie because I'd met these people three hours earlier, but I havent been able to let it go since, and there's another big family thing at christmas, so I'd like to actually work out who had the better of it instead of just nodding along again.

Roughly how it went: (1) Gary opened with the claim that Popper — "the guy who basically invented the modern definition of science" — said natural selection isn't a testable theory, just a metaphysical framework, because "survival of the fittest" really just means "survival of whoever happens to survive," which is a tautology. (2) Rosa said that's out of date, because Popper recanted that years later and admitted natural selection is testable after all. (3) Gary said backing down under pressure from the whole biology establishment doesn't make the original point wrong, and the tautology problem stands unless you can define "fitness" without just pointing at who ended up surviving and reproducing more. (4) Rosa answered that biologists don't actually define it that way — they predict ahead of time that some specific trait will help survival or reproduction in a given environment, and that prediction can fail, which is exactly what makes it non-circular. (5) Gary conceded that individual claims like that are testable, but said the big theory — that natural selection explains all the complexity we see — isn't falsifiable, since no single observation could count against the whole framework, it just absorbs everything after the fact, and that this is basically what Popper still meant by "metaphysical research programme" even after his so-called recantation. (6) Rosa said that's true of every major scientific framework — nothing falsifies plate tectonics or quantum theory in one shot either — so if that disqualifies evolution it disqualifies most of science.

Nobody won, dessert just got cleared and we all migrated to the living room. Could you go through the six moves one at a time and say who came out ahead at each one (a clean point, a partial one, or a miss for both), instead of just giving an overall verdict? I'm especially curious whether Rosa's move in (4) actually defuses the tautology charge from (1)/(3) or just relocates it, whether Gary's individual-hypothesis-vs-general-theory split in (5) is a fair distinction or a goalpost move, and whether Rosa's parity move in (6) really answers that or is a dodge. Also, from some half-hearted googling afterward it sounds like Popper's "recantation" might have been a lot more partial and hedged than Rosa made it sound in (2) — if that's true I'd like to know how much it actually changes the scorecard.
