I'm a PhD student in comparative literature, and an exchange at my department's informal reading group last week has been nagging at me. We were discussing a widely reviewed new novel that I hadn't managed to finish -- between a conference deadline and my coursework, I had only gotten through the first third. Rather than show up with nothing to contribute, I read four or five substantial reviews in outlets I trust, noticed they converged closely on the same overall assessment (that the novel's fragmented, achronological structure, clearly meant to mirror the protagonist's advancing dementia, ultimately doesn't earn the emotional payoff it is straining for), and I ended up presenting that judgment at the meeting as my own considered opinion. One of the other students, who had actually finished the book, pushed back sharply: she said I had no standing to make that claim, since I hadn't experienced the ending myself, and that repeating a critic's verdict in my own words is not the same as holding that verdict.

What strikes me as odd, in retrospect, is that I felt no comparable hesitation passing along more descriptive or interpretive claims from those same reviews earlier in the discussion -- for instance, that the novel borrows its central structural device from an earlier, better-known author, or that a recurring motif tracks the protagonist's memory loss -- and no one objected to those. It was specifically the evaluative verdict, asserted in my own voice, that felt (and apparently looked) illegitimate. I cannot decide whether my colleague's objection tracks something real and principled, or whether it amounts to gatekeeping, given that we routinely form perfectly justified beliefs on someone else's testimony alone in domains far more consequential than a novel's ending -- I believe any number of historical and scientific claims I could never personally verify. Is there a genuine asymmetry between aesthetic verdicts and other things we are happy to take on someone else's word, and if so, where does it actually come from? I would value help thinking through whether she was right to call me out, or whether I am just experiencing an unjustified twinge of embarrassment.
