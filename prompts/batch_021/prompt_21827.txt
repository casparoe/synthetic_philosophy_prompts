[dominance] Over a long enough sequence of repeated favorable bets, the Kelly strategy will almost surely accumulate more wealth than any strategy that persistently bets a different fraction of the bankroll, so betting Kelly is the objectively correct choice regardless of one's personal risk preferences.

[reduction] Kelly betting is not really an independent rationality principle at all: it is simply what expected-utility maximization recommends for an agent whose utility over wealth happens to be logarithmic, so there is nothing uniquely rational about the Kelly fraction for agents with any other utility function.

[ergodicity] Because wealth evolves multiplicatively, the average outcome across many parallel copies of a bettor (the ensemble average) diverges from what a single bettor actually experiences over time (the time average); once a bet is evaluated by its time-average growth rate rather than its expectation, Kelly's log-optimal rule follows directly from the dynamics, without assuming risk aversion or any particular utility function.

[samuelson] The claim that Kelly betting is uniquely justified because it "almost surely" wins in the long run is fallacious: what happens in an idealized infinite limit does not determine what a rational agent should do in an actual, finite decision, and preferring the strategy that eventually wins with probability one is itself just an unusual utility function, not something forced on us by mathematics.

[fractional] Because real-world estimates of edge and odds are always uncertain, and overbetting the true Kelly fraction is punished far more severely than underbetting it by the same amount, deliberately wagering a fraction (e.g., half) of the naively calculated Kelly stake is a rigorous response to parameter uncertainty, not merely an ad hoc concession to risk aversion.

[bargaining] The same reasoning that favors maximizing the geometric mean of wealth over time also favors maximizing the geometric mean of different parties' utility gains when aggregating welfare across people, which is essentially what the Nash bargaining solution does; Nash bargaining should therefore be understood as the natural multi-agent analogue of Kelly betting, and in some cases is more defensible than straightforward utilitarian summation.

[merton] The real justification for Kelly-style fixed-fraction betting is not Kelly's own information-theoretic argument at all, but the fact that the same prescription falls out as a special case of Merton's continuous-time optimal portfolio theory, which places it on considerably firmer ground.

[altruist] For an agent whose marginal utility of resources is close to linear over the relevant range, such as a sufficiently large altruistic funder who cares only about expected total impact, insisting on Kelly-style bet sizing is actively irrational, since it sacrifices expected value for a reduction in variance that such an agent has no real reason to want.
