So I was at the launch party for my new novel (there's a plague in it, don't ask) and two friends of mine who're way more into philosophy than me got into this whole theodicy argument I can't stop turning over -- partly because I want to steal it for a book, but mostly because I actually want to know who's right.

Sara's opening move: a perfect god would obviously make the best possible world there is, this clearly isn't it, so no perfect god. Marcus said that's the exact move Leibniz saw coming -- his answer was never 'the suffering is secretly fine,' it's that not all goods can coexist in one world (he called this compossibility), so this could still be the best possible world despite the suffering, since you can't remove one evil without loosing whatever good it's bundled to. Sara said that's just unfalsifiable, he's asserting the evils are load-bearing with no way to check. Marcus said it was never an empirical claim -- it's supposed to follow from what a possible world even is on Leibniz's picture, since individuals come as a package deal and you can't hold everything else fixed and edit out one kid's illness. Sara said fine, but that just kicks the mystery back a step: he still needs a single best world to exist at all, and she doesn't see why there'd be a maximum instead of an endless ladder of better worlds, same as there's no biggest number. Marcus didn't have a snappy answer to that, and honestly, neither do I.

Can you go through the whole back-and-forth and say who's right at each point, not just who wins overall -- and then say what, if anything, you think both of them are missing?
