Thomas Kuhn's account of scientific revolutions distinguishes genuine paradigm shifts -- marked by incommensurability between successive frameworks, i.e., differences in standards, methods, the problems considered worth solving, and even the phenomena scientists take themselves to be studying -- from the cumulative refinement characteristic of normal science. Historians and philosophers of science disagree substantially about which actual episodes deserve to be called paradigm shifts in this strong sense, as opposed to being better described as continuous progress within a shared framework, or as driven mainly by the accumulation of new evidence rather than a genuine change of worldview.

Rank the following ten episodes from clearest case to weakest/most disputed case of a genuine Kuhnian paradigm shift involving strong incommensurability:

- Ptolemaic geocentrism giving way to Copernican-Keplerian heliocentrism
- Phlogiston chemistry giving way to Lavoisier's oxygen theory of combustion
- Caloric theory giving way to the kinetic theory of heat
- Special creationism giving way to Darwinian evolution by natural selection
- Miasma theory giving way to the germ theory of disease
- Newtonian mechanics giving way to Einsteinian relativity
- Classical determinism giving way to quantum mechanics
- Continental fixism giving way to plate tectonics
- Behaviorism giving way to cognitivism in psychology
- Steady-state cosmology giving way to Big Bang cosmology
