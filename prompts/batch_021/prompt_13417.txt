Last weekend my friend Priya and I got into a long, half-serious argument about whether the Kalai-Smorodinsky bargaining solution or the Nash bargaining solution is the better model for how two people should divide concessions in a marriage. It started because she refused to simply split the difference with her fiancé over the wedding budget, and instead sketched a diagram with a disagreement point and an ideal point for each of them, insisting that real fairness meant something more specific than meeting in the middle. I'm giving the toast at her wedding next month, and the room will be split much the same way: her entire economics PhD cohort, who will notice immediately if I get the concept wrong, and both families, who have never heard of either bargaining solution and shouldn't need to in order to enjoy the speech. Please write the toast, using what is actually distinctive about the Kalai-Smorodinsky solution — not splitting differences, but preserving each person's fair share of their own best possible gains as circumstances change — as the real backbone of it, with no equations or jargon, so that it lands as genuine affection rather than a lecture on bargaining theory.
