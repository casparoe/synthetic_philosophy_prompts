As the philosophy prof who got roped onto my university's Research Integrity & Compliance Committee, I've somehow ended up drafting our recommendation for two cases we're deciding next tuesday. Two postdocs in the same lab broke our data-security protocol the exact same way, for the exact same reason: both kept identifiable data on personal, unencrypted laptops instead of the encrypted server because the server was slow. Postdoc A's laptop sat in a drawer for a year, nothing happened. Postdoc B's laptop got stolen out of a car, and now a few thousand patients' psychiatric records are floating around online and the university's facing a lawsuit. Half the committee wants B sanctioned way harder than A since B actually caused harm; the other half says that's just punishing bad luck, since the actual violation -- the only thing within either postdoc's control -- was identical. I know its basically a moral luck case, but don't just namedrop Nagel and move on, most of this committee has never heard of him. Write the memo I'll circulate before the meeting: recommend concrete sanctions for both (probation, retraining, loss of data access -- your call, but be specific about how long), say plainly whether and why they should differ, and take the losing side's fairness worry seriously instead of just steamrolling it.
