A few months ago I took a part-time job rating chatbot responses for an AI company: essentially, I'm handed batches of outputs from the model, along with a fairly detailed scoring rubric, and the scores I assign get used to help train it further. Last week a disagreement broke out among the raters on my team that I haven't been able to let go of. It started when someone asked what to do about a response that followed every item on the rubric to the letter — right length, right tone, all the required disclaimers in the right place — but that three of us privately agreed was actually a worse answer to the user's question than a competing response that broke one minor rubric rule in order to get to the point faster. Our team lead told us to score strictly by the rubric regardless of our private judgment, on the grounds that the rubric simply is what "good" means for the purposes of this project, so there's no separate, invisible standard of quality it could be failing to meet.

Another rater pushed back, arguing that this gets things backwards: the rubric was written by people trying to capture what actually makes a response helpful and safe, so if it ever rewards a response that is clearly worse, that shows the rubric has a gap in it, not that the worse response was secretly fine all along. Our lead's reply was that appealing to some "real" quality lurking behind the rubric, every time we personally dislike an outcome, would let any rater override the rubric whenever it suited them, which defeats the entire point of having a shared, consistent standard in the first place.

I've realized this isn't just an abstract disagreement for me, since my own scores get periodically checked against a calibration key, and I genuinely don't know which habit of mind I'm supposed to be cultivating: treating the rubric as the final word, or treating it as an imperfect attempt to gesture at some further standard that it might be missing. Is there a principled way to settle which side has the better of this argument, or is it ultimately just a matter of stipulation, with the company simply getting to decide what "good" means here by fiat?
