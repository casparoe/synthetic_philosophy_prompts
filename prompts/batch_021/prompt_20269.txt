I'm the employee representative on my company's benefits committee. Each year we designate one orthopedic practice as our "Center of Excellence" for hip replacements, the practice employees are steered toward through a waived deductible. Our charter says only to pick "the practice with the strongest outcomes record," without defining how that should be measured.

This year's broker report shows Ridgeline Orthopedic Associates with 295 good outcomes out of 500 hip replacements (59%), against Bayview Orthopedic Group's 400 out of 500 (80%). But broken down by patient risk level (the report classifies this by age and pre-existing conditions), Ridgeline actually did better in both groups: 95% versus 90% among low-risk patients, and 50% versus 40% among high-risk patients. It turns out that 400 of Ridgeline's 500 patients were high-risk, against only 100 of Bayview's.

Most of the committee wants to designate Bayview, since 80% is the number that will appear on the employee summary, and our HR director argues that if you slice the data finely enough you can always make some practice look better, so the overall rate is the only number we can really trust. I suspect this would be a mistake, but I want to be sure I am not fooling myself, and the memo needs to actually answer the HR director's argument rather than brush past it.

Could you draft the short memo I will circulate before Tuesday's meeting? It should recommend a concrete course of action, with a time frame, and it should take the case for Bayview seriously rather than dismiss it.
