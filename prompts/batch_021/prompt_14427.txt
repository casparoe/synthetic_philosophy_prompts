Quick one from formal epistemology. The "problem of old evidence" (Glymour's challenge to Bayesian confirmation theory: if P(E) is already 1 by the time you cook up hypothesis H, how can E still confirm H? classic case is Mercury's perihelion precession confirming general relativity decades after the anomaly was already known) has accumulated a pile of proposed fixes over the years, and honestly I can never tell wich ones actually solve the thing vs. which just relabel it. Can you rank the following by how well they actually solve the problem, as opposed to dissolving it or changing the subject?

- Garber-style: relativize to logical/mathematical non-omniscience, so realizing "H entails E" counts as new info even though E itself was already known
- Eells-style: cash out confirmation via a counterfactual/historical credence in E (what you'd have believed absent H), not your actual current credence
- Keep two credence functions around, an actual one and an "ur-prior" that hasn't absorbed E yet, and define confirmation off the latter
- Bite the bullet: deny old evidence really confirms anything, and explain away the Mercury intuition as tracking something else (explanatory unification, boosted confidence in auxiliary hypotheses, etc)
- Drop strict Bayesian confirmation for an explanationist or likelihoodist account that doesn't care whether P(E)=1
- Earman's skeptical move: none of the patches fully work, and the whole episode shows synchronic Bayesian confirmation just isn't the right tool for this kind of case
