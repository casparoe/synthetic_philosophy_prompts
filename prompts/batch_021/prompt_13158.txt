Okay, this is a weird one, but it's actually holding up a real feature I'm trying to ship. I'm an indie dev working on a mobile idle game, and I designed this "streak gamble" bonus: the player keeps flipping a fair virtual coin (50/50), and the streak ends the first time it lands heads. If it ends on flip n, they win 2^n/n coins when n is odd and lose 2^n/n coins when n is even, so the swings get bigger the longer the streak survives, but happen less and less often. Standard geometric-distribution stuff, nothing fancy.

To balance the game I obviously needed the expected value, so I summed (probability of ending on flip n) times (payout on flip n) for n = 1, 2, 3, ... in a spreadsheet, and it converges beautifully to about 0.693 coins (which I now realize is just ln(2), lol). Great, I figured, so I set the buy-in price to 0.69 and moved on. Except then my roommate, who's a math PhD student, glanced at the sheet and said "this only converges because of the order you added the terms in," rearranged the exact same infinite list of terms, and got a completely different total. Then he did it again and made it diverge to infinity. I didn't beleive him until I tried it myself, and he's right, you can make the "expected value" of my own game be almost anything just by re-ordering terms that were already all there. I even wrote a quick script simulating a few million plays and tracking the running average payout, and it never settles down either, it just keeps jumping around forever. Googling around, it turns out this exact structure is a known thing in decision theory called the Pasadena paradox (I think from a paper by a philosopher named Hájek and a coauthor?), and the whole point is supposedly that the expected value here is "undefined," not just some huge number like in the St. Petersburg paradox.

Here's what I don't get though: when you sum it in the natural order, the order the flips actually happen in real time, n=1 then n=2 then n=3 and so on, you get a perfectly well-behaved, finite number, ln(2). That's not some weird artificial rearrangement, that's just doing the sum in the order the game is actually played. So isn't ln(2) obviously the "real" expected value here, and all the "it's undefined" talk just philosophers being needlessly precious about rearrangements no one would actually do in practice? Or is there something about why my "natural" order isn't as privileged as I think it is? Kind of need to actually understand this before I ship the thing.
