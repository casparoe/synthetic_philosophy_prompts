Write a dialogue between an experimental philosopher who defends the "restrictionist" challenge to intuition-based methodology in analytic philosophy and a traditionalist epistemologist who resists it. Center their exchange on the cross-cultural literature on philosophical intuitions — the divergent Gettier judgments reported by Weinberg, Nichols, and Stich, and the divergent reference judgments in Kripke's Gödel/Schmidt case reported by Machery, Mallon, Nichols, and Stich — and, especially, on whether an appeal to philosophical expertise can rescue the evidential use of intuitions from this variation. Have the traditionalist spell out the expertise defense precisely enough that the restrictionist can press its actual empirical and dialectical weaknesses, rather than a caricature of it. Let the two end by trying to determine whether their disagreement is ultimately empirical — hostage to how later, more equivocal replications turn out — or a philosophical dispute that no amount of further data could settle.
