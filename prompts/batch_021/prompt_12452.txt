So my cousin has properly gone down the flat-earth rabbit hole this year (long story, started with a youtube algorithm thing, now its basically his whole personality), and after one especially unproductive argument with him over Thanksgiving I went looking for some actual philosophical footing on why "the earth is round" feels like a different kind of claim than an ordinary empirical belief. That's how I ended up in the deep end of Wittgenstein's On Certainty and the whole "hinge proposition" literature that's grown up around it. What's been messing with my head is that there seem to be two pretty different camps on what a hinge commitment even is, and half the time I can't tell if theyre actually disagreeing about something or just using different words for the same intuition.

One camp (I've seen this tied to Crispin Wright's idea of "entitlement") says that even though hinges like "the earth isn't flat" aren't believed on the basis of evidence, we're still, in some thin sense, rationally entitled to accept them - so there's an actual epistemic story to tell about why the round-earther is doing better than the flat-earther, even though neither of them argued their way there. The other camp (closer to what I've seen attributed to Duncan Pritchard, who calls his version something like "quasi-fideism") says this is basically cheating: for Wittgenstein, hinge commitments are supposed to be arational bedrock, not even candidates for warrant or unwarrant, so dressing them up as "entitled beliefs" just smuggles the evidentialism On Certainty was trying to get rid of back in through the side door.

Could you write me a dialogue between one philosopher defending the entitlement view and one defending the arational/bedrock view, hashing this out specifically over my cousin's case? I want them equally sharp - the entitlement person pressing on how the bedrock view seems to leave us with no way to say what's actually wrong with the flat earther, or how "the earth is round" could ever have become a hinge in the first place if hinges sit outside rational evaluation entirely, and the bedrock person pushing back on whether "entitlement" is doing any real epistemic work or is just a fancier name for the fact that we don't ask for grounds here. I want genuine back-and-forth, each one responding to the other's actual objection instead of just restating their opening position, and by the end I'd like it to be clear what, if anything, this disagreement actually implies for how I should think about someone like my cousin - is he simply wrong, arational, or something else entirely.
