So this happened to a friend of mine a few weeks ago and I can't stop turning it over in my head. She's about the least woo-brained person I know - does actuarial work, thinks tarot and homeopathy are nonsense - and she went with her mom, half as a joke, to see a psychic medium in her mom's town. The medium apparently nailed a bunch of pretty specific details about my friend's late grandfather: his childhood nickname, an old farm accident that cost him a finger, details about how he actually died. My friend swears there's no way the woman could've looked any of this up beforehand, since the visit was totally last-minute and nobody involved has much of an online footprint. I brought it up in a group chat with some friends from a LessWrong-adjacent meetup who are way more into debunking this kind of thing than I am, and none of them could actually point to a specific mechanism (cold reading, some way of pulling the info in advance, whatever) that explains this particular case - they just kind of shrugged and said there's obviously SOME boring explanation, we just don't happen to know what it is.

This is nagging at me on two levels I can't quite untangle. First, my friend is about as credible a witness as I could ask for, and normally "someone I trust told me X happened" is decent evidence for X, so this should clearly update my priors somewhat - I just don't know how much, especially since nobody can name the specific flaw in her story. Second, and seperately: even if I fully grant that everything happened exactly like she describes, I don't think that actually gets you all the way to "so psychic powers might be real" - that feels like a different question from whether her account is accurate in the first place. Is there a principled way to think through either of these, or am I just overthinking a fun dinner party story?
