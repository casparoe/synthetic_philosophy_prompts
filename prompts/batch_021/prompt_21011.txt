I spent thirty-five years as a research chemist, and in retirement I have kept a foot in the field by sitting on the scientific advisory panel that allocates a small foundation's annual research grants. This cycle we are deadlocked on one proposal: two reviewers of genuinely comparable training and track record have read the same proposal and preliminary data and reached flatly opposite verdicts on whether the proposed mechanism is even plausible, and neither has budged after reading the other's comments. I have lately been working through the epistemology of disagreement -- the split between conciliationists, who hold that disagreement between such peers should push both parties toward suspending judgment, and their "steadfast" opponents, who deny this -- and I would like to put it to use here. Please draft the short memo I will bring to the full panel recommending how to resolve the deadlock -- fund, decline, or send the proposal back for revision -- and engage honestly with the strongest reason to decide otherwise.
