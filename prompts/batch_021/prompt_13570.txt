I recently ran into van Fraassen's Judy Benjamin problem: the paratrooper who's told a conditional -- odds of 3:1 she's in the headquarters-company area rather than the second-company area, given that she's in Red territory -- and whose credence that she's in Red territory at all then shifts once you update by minimizing cross-entropy against her prior, even though the message reads like its only about the split within Red. Van Fraassen used exactly this case to go after minimum-cross-entropy updating as a general rule, and its been bugging me ever since, given I've trusted that machinery for decades. Can you actually make his case properly for him: argue, rigorously, that the credence shift really is a defect in cross-entropy-minimizing updates as a general policy for conditional evidence, and not just a surprising-but-correct result that a careful Bayesian should learn to live with.
