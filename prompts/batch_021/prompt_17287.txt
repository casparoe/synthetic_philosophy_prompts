I'm a backend engineer, and I've spent a fair amount of my spare time over the years reading about algorithmic information theory as a hobby. Kolmogorov complexity first came up for me in the context of data compression, and I ran into Solomonoff induction later through a handful of AI-alignment-adjacent blog posts that all present it as the thing that finally makes Occam's razor mathematically rigorous, rather than just a vague aesthetic preference for simple explanations. A couple of weekends ago I decided to stop reading popularizations and work through some more careful treatments instead, and it left me less settled than when I started, because two things I thought I understood no longer fit together as neatly as I'd assumed.

The first issue is that almost every informal explanation describes the procedure as something like "weight each hypothesis by two to the power of minus its program length, then favor the simplest hypothesis that fits the data" -- but the actual construction, as far as I can tell, sums the weighted contributions of literally every program consistent with the data into a single mixture prediction, and never selects one winning hypothesis at all. That looks like a genuinely different idea from Occam's razor as it's normally stated, which is about preferring one explanation over its rivals, not averaging over all of them in a simplicity-weighted vote. The second issue is the standard response to "but complexity is relative to your choice of programming language or universal machine," which is to invoke the invariance theorem and note that switching machines only shifts complexity by some additive constant -- except that constant depends on which two machines are being compared, and nothing I've read rules out its being enormous relative to the specific strings anyone would actually care about, so the invariance seems to hold only asymptotically, which is no real guarantee for any particular finite dataset.

Could you explain how these two considerations actually bear on the claim that Solomonoff induction "formalizes" Occam's razor? I'm especially interested in whether the mixture-versus-single-hypothesis distinction is philosophically important or just cosmetic, and whether the invariance theorem genuinely rescues Kolmogorov complexity from language-dependence for the finite, practical cases anyone would actually care about, or only for a limiting case that offers little comfort otherwise. I'd like an answer that actually engages with both worries rather than the one-paragraph reassurance that simplicity has finally been made rigorous.
