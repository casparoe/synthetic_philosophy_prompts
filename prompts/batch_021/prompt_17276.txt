I spent about thirty-five years doing fieldwork on animal signalling -- stickleback courtship displays, honeybee waggle dances, that sort of thing -- before I retired a couple of years back, and since then I've properly fallen down the philosophy of mind rabbit hole. Somebody put Millikan's teleosemantics stuff in front of me and it felt like finding a whole discipline built around a question I used to ask informally out in the field: what makes it true that a signal is *about* something, rather than just being an effect with a cause. I run a little monthly reading-group-slash-podcast with some other retired academics in my town, and next week I'm interviewing a guest who is a committed defender of that view -- the idea that a state gets it's content from the proper function of whatever mechanism produced it and whatever mechanism is set up to consume it, "proper function" meaning whatever past tokens of that same mechanism did that got the mechanism reproduced down the generations. Part of the sell, as I understand it, is that this explains misrepresentation for free: the frog's snapping mechanism has the proper function of nailing flies, so when it snaps at a BB pellet instead that's a malfunction, not just a different true description of what's being tracked.

I don't want forty minutes of her just re-explaining biosemantics to an audience who mostly already read the assigned paper, so can you draft me eight or nine sharp interview questions? I want at least one that really presses the swampman case -- a molecule for molecule duplicate of a person, no selection history at all, so on this view it has zero thoughts, which strikes just about everyone as nuts -- I'd guess she'll say our intuitions here simply aren't trustworthy, so give me a follow-up ready for when she says that. One on the indeterminacy problem with that same frog example -- if flies, BB pellets and "small dark moving things" were all coextensive back when the snapping mechanism got selected, what licenses saying the content is specifically fly rather than any of the others? One on why the theory doesn't seem to stop it handing out the exact same kind of content to things like blood clotting or a house thermostat, which nobody wants to call a representation. And at least one on what happens to content once an animal, or a person, ends up in conditions nothing like the ones the mechanism was selected under -- captivity, a shifted climate, evolutionarily brand-new stuff like screens or refined sugar -- does the content just stay frozen on the ancestral referent forever, or what? Arrange them however flows best for a live back and forth, and keep the follow ups tied to what she'd actually plausibly say, not to a strawman version of the view.
