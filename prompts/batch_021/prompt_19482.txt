Two months ago I joined the incident-review team at a company that operates a fleet of autonomous sidewalk delivery robots, and last week we encountered our first real incident. One of our robots swerved around a fallen bicycle and struck an elderly pedestrian, fracturing her wrist. In the review meeting, our lead engineer explained that the robot's evasive maneuver emerged from a neural network trained on vast amounts of driving footage, and that no one on the team wrote, or could have predicted, the specific move that caused the fall. Legal's position is that the company should compensate the woman as a matter of policy, but that neither the engineers nor the company were at fault in any meaningful sense, since nobody could have foreseen or controlled that particular maneuver. This unsettles me: it feels wrong to conclude that no one is responsible for a real injury, yet I can't identify anyone who actually violated a duty of care in the way we'd normally require before assigning blame. Is Legal right that responsibility has essentially evaporated in a case like this, or is there a defensible way to hold someone accountable even without traditional negligence?
