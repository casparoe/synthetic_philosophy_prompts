Write me a dialogue between the two of them, staged as though they were sitting across from each other arguing over this exact case, in which each has to directly answer the other's strongest objection rather than simply restate his own position. I'd like it to end with the two of them grappling with a narrower question: does Ghazālī actually need to deny that fire has any real power to burn at all, or would his theological worries be satisfied just as well if God could merely override that power on special occasions like Abraham's?
