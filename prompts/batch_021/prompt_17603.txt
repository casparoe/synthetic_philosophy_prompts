Okay so this has been living in my head for like two weeks now. My partner and I binged that old Starz show Counterpart (the one where J.K. Simmons plays two versions of the same guy, Howard, who split off from each other decades ago after some Cold War-era programme opened a crossing into a parallel Earth) and it turned into a whole 1am argument about whether the two Howards are "the same guy" who just had different stuff happen to him, or whether their actually two different people who just happen to share a birth and a face. We couldn't resolve it, so I brought it up at Sunday dinner, and my sister's husband, who's doing a philosophy PhD, got way to excited and started talking my ear off about "counterpart theory" versus "transworld identity."

From what I could piece together: David Lewis apparently thought nothing ever literally exists in more than one possible world, so when we say "I could have been a doctor," what we really mean is that some similar-but-distinct "counterpart" of me is a doctor in some other world, not literally me — and which things count as counterparts of what is a matter of overall resemblance, kind of fuzzy and context-dependent, not strict sameness. Other philosophers (he mentioned Kripke) apparently think that's a weird way to talk, and that you really can be one and the same individual, strictly and numerically, across different possible worlds, no counterparts required. He said Kripke's big objection involves some guy named Humphrey who lost a US election, and how it seems wrong to cash out "Humphrey might have won" as really being about some other, similar guy winning in a different world, since Humphrey himself presumably couldn't care less about that. He also brought up an old puzzle — Chisholm's, I think? — about a chain of possible worlds that very gradually morphs one person into somebody else, property by property, which is supposed to cause real trouble for the "literally the same person across worlds" camp, kind of like the sorites paradox but for personal identity instead of heaps of sand.

Anyway, I clearly only half followed all of this, but it's stuck with me, especially because the show itself never actually takes a side — the characters just act like obviously the other Howard is a different person, which now feels like a huge unexamined assumption. Could you write me a short story that actually engages with this specific disagreement (counterpart theory vs. transworld identity) instead of doing another generic parallel-universe romp? It doesn't need literal doppelgangers, but I'd like the plot to hinge on the distinction in a way that actually matters to the characters — like someone's inheritance, guilt, marriage, or sense of self depends on whether the person from across worlds "counts as" the same individual or merely resembles them. Bonus points if you can work in something like Chisholm's gradual-change idea too, since that's the part that really broke my brain.
