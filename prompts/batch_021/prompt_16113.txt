Hi - I'm a retired physicist (spent about thirty five years doing condensed matter work before I hung up the lab coat), and this past year I've fallen down a philosophy rabbit hole, mostly on account of all the AI-risk chatter in the news these days. One thing I keep running into is the "coherence argument" for why any sufficiently advanced AI should end up acting like an expected utility maximizer - the idea, which I gather traces back to von Neumann and Morgenstern and gets invoked a lot by people like Omohundro and Yudkowsky, is that an agent whose preferences are intransitive or incomplete can be "money-pumped": cycled through a series of trades that leave it strictly poorer while it ends up right back where it started, over and over. So the thought is that rationality (or just competitive pressure) sands away any incoherence over time, and full coherence plus almost any goal at all supposedly implies some pretty nasty convergent behavior, like resisting being switched off. Honestly that part felt intuitive to me, almost thermodynamic - leaky, incoherent agents just get drained of value until whats left is coherent.

But then I stumbled on some push back (I want to say it was a post by Rohin Shah, though don't quote me on that, my memory for names isn't what it used to be) arguing that the theorem doesn't actually show what everyone thinks it shows, because you can always cook up some utility function or other that rationalizes whatever an agent happens to do, even if it just sits there like a rock - so "coherence" by itself doesn't pin down goal directed behavior unless you sneak in extra assumptions about what a utility function is allowed to depend on. And separately, I read that agents with genuinely incomplete preferences (not indifference, mind you - just no preference either way between some pair of options) might dodge the whole money pump business if they simply refuse certain trades, and that this apparently matters a lot for whether you could ever build an AI that stays stably indifferent about its own off-switch. Between those three threads I've gotten myself pretty tangled up. Could you help me sort it out - what exactly do coherence and money-pump arguments prove, where precisely do these objections land their punches, and does anything like the original scary conclusion survive by the end?
