I sit on my university's Student Media Board, which allocates student-activity-fee funding and grants official recognition to student-run publications and platforms. The Board must now decide whether to defund and de-recognize an anonymous "confessions" app, in the vein of Yik Yak, built and administered by one of our chartered clubs, after a sustained anonymous pile-on against a named teaching assistant escalated into harassment and the TA went on leave. One faction wants to treat the app exactly as we would a print publication that had abandoned editorial responsibility; another insists the app is doing nothing our own official newspaper's anonymous opinion page hasn't always done, only faster and to a much wider audience, and that singling it out is inconsistent. My Kierkegaard seminar just worked through his account of "the public" and leveling in Two Ages, and it strikes me as bearing directly on whether anonymity combined with mass, instant distribution is a difference in kind from the old anonymous opinion page, not merely one of degree or speed. Please draft the memo I will bring to the Board's vote next week: recommend one specific action with a timeframe, and take the "it's just the opinion page, only faster" objection seriously before you answer it, rather than dismiss it out of hand.
