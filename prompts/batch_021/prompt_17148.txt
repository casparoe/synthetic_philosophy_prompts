Here's roughly how it went (numbering the moves so you can reference them):
(1) Elena opened with: we have real, action-guiding reason right now to be somewhat cooperative toward hypothetical distant agents — far-future superintelligences, aliens, simulators, whoever — because if enough agents "out there" reason the way we do, being the kind of agent who cooperates is exactly what raises the correlated expected payoff, acausal-trade style, zero causal contact required.
(2) Marcus shot back that this isn't trade at all, it's unilateral niceness with a fancier label: an actual trade requires your cooperation to be conditional on theirs in some trackable way, and since you can't even identify who's supposedly on the other side of the deal, "acausal trade" just becomes a license to do whatever you already wanted and retroactively call it a bargain.
(3) Elena said that objection proves too much, since Newcomb's problem has zero causal enforcement either (the boxes are already filled by the time you choose) and everyone still treats one-boxing as the rational response to a real correlation with the Predictor — so "no causal enforcement" can't be what disqualifies something from counting as a trade.

(5) Elena countered that every use of expected value under uncertainty rests on priors that aren't "given" either — anthropic reasoning being the obvious comparison — and that you can set a non-arbitrary prior over possible trading partners by weighting simpler agents more heavily (basically what Oesterheld does), so this is a difference of degree and not of kind.
(6) Marcus's last move was that even a principled prior doesn't save you from a further problem: the same reasoning that licenses acausal cooperation seems to license worrying about acausal blackmail too (Roko's-basilisk-shaped stuff), and neither of them, in the moment, could articulate a principled reason to let in the trade while keeping out the blackmail.
