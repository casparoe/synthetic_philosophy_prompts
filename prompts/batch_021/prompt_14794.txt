My brother-in-law owns a small bakery, and something that happened there last month has been rattling around in my head ever since. Partly it's a genuine mess for his business, but mostly it's turned into exactly the kind of case I'd normally just toss at my metaphysics seminar as a fun thought experiment -- except now its not hypothetical, I don't get to enjoy watching the students squirm, and I actually have to say something useful to a real person's face. Short version: the fryer caught fire, and it did way more damage than it should have because the extinguisher had expired four months earlier and barely sprayed anything. Checking the inspection tag wasn't officially anyone's job -- it had just become an unspoken habit that whoever noticed it creeping toward its expiry date would call the fire-safety company, and for the past year that had almost always ended up being Ana, one of his two employees. Separately, the landlord's maintenance company was contractually on the hook to catch exactly this kind of thing during quarterly walkthroughs, and there running three weeks behind schedule that quarter. And my brother-in-law himself had been meaning to just assign the tag-check to somebody as an actual job since he took over the lease, and never got around to it. Ana has been quietly miserable for weeks. Theo, the other employee, had exactly as much chance to notice the tag and didn't; the landlord's people had an explicit duty and didn't; my brother-in-law could have ended the whole ambiguity and didn't -- but somehow she's the one who feels like she personally burned the place down.

So -- professor to model -- did Ana's not-checking the tag actually cause the extra damage, in any real sense, or is she just the one our attention happens to snag on out of a whole lineup of equally-placed non-preventers? If she counts as a cause, don't the other three have to count too, which seems to prove too much; and if none of them counts individually, since any one of the others could just as easily have stepped in, that seems to let everyone off the hook, which feels equally wrong. I'd like your actual best answer here, not a "well, in a sense, everyone and no one" shrug -- and I'm especially curious whether the different flavors of expectation floating around (her habit, their contract, his managerial duty) should change who we single out, or whether that's just us being swayed by irrelevant psychology. I want to walk into this weekend's family dinner with something better to offer Ana than "these things happen."
