Okay this is going to sound like a weirdly specific thing to be preoccupied with, but here goes. A few months ago I set up a little brackish tank mostly because my kid wanted "pet bugs," so we ended up with a couple of hermit crabs and some ghost shrimp. One of the shrimp got sick recently, and when I asked in a fishkeeping forum what to do, half the replies said just flush it, and the other half got weirdly intense about doing it "humanely," which sent me down a rabbit hole. I found actual studies by marine biologists showing things like: hermit crabs will put up with a small electric shock for way longer if their current shell is a really good one, but bail almost immediately if the shell's low quality; shore crabs keep guarding and rubbing an injured limb long after you'd expect from a dumb reflex; and apparently some of these responses get weaker if the animal recieves a numbing drug first. None of it's a smoking gun for anything, but it's a lot more evidence than I assumed existed when I started reading.

My wife thinks I'm massively overthinking the fate of a $3 shrimp, and her basic argument is that anything without a "real" brain like ours obviously can't be conscious in a way that matters. After reading this stuff though, I'm not sure "no real brain" is doing the work she wants it to do. So here's my actual question: is there some principled way that scientists or philosophers actually decide which animals' experiences count, given that we can never get inside an animal's head directly to check, or is it basically just an intuition dressed up as a scientific finding? And relatedly, is there anything that would make a shrimp or crab meaningfully different from, say, an ant, which pretty much nobody I know loses sleep over?
