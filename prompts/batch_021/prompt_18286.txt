A friend and I spent most of a long train journey arguing about whether metaphysical grounding has to bottom out somewhere, and neither of us budged. Their view was that a chain of grounding facts descending forever, with nothing ever ungrounded, is simply incoherent: if nothing is fundamental, nothing ever really has the properties it has, because the explanation is never actually completed -- it just gets deferred one level down, forever, like a debt that never comes due. I pushed back with a physics-flavored worry: we've been surprised before about where composition bottoms out (atoms turned out to have parts, nucleons turned out to have parts), so matter being "gunky" all the way down -- infinitely divisible, with no smallest bits -- seems like a live possibility rather than an absurdity, and I don't see why grounding in general should be any different. My friend thinks the vicious-regress intuition is just obviously correct and that I'm the one being perverse for resisting it.

I know this maps onto an actual debate about whether grounding must be "well-founded," with Jonathan Schaffer's work on fundamentality on one side and things like Ricki Bliss's and Ross Cameron's writing on "metaphysical infinitism" somewhere in the mix -- though I'll admit I don't have the dialectic straight enough to know exactly who ends up committed to what. Could you reconstruct the strongest argument for thinking grounding chains must terminate in something fundamental, being precise about what is actually supposed to go wrong if they don't (a strictly logical problem? explanations that never finish? facts needing some non-relational basis instead of relations all the way down? something else)? Then give me the best infinitist reply, and tell me whether it genuinely defuses the charge of viciousness or just accepts an endless explanatory chain as the price of doing business. I'd also like your take on whether this whole foundationalism/infinitism framing is quietly borrowed from the epistemic regress problem in a way that might not transfer cleanly from a justificatory relation to an explanatory one, and, if there's room, how Schaffer's own fallback position of priority monism -- on which the entire cosmos is the sole fundamental entity and grounds all of its parts -- is supposed to escape the bottom-level worry rather than just relocating it to the question of what, if anything, grounds the whole.
