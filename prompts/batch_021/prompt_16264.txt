I've been slowly working through the old Overcoming Bias archives, and I keep coming back to Eliezer Yudkowsky's 2007 "Torture vs. Dust Specks" post, where he argues, via straightforward aggregation of disutility across an astronomically large number of people, that a single person tortured for fifty years is the lesser evil compared to 3^^^3 people each getting a single dust speck in the eye. At a rationalist-adjacent reading group a few weeks ago, someone pointed out that Alastair Norcross had made what looks like a structurally similar move a full decade earlier, in his 1997 paper "Comparing Harms: Headaches and Human Lives," which argues against granting any harm, however severe, lexical priority over a milder harm, however trivial, regardless of how many people suffer the milder one — a conclusion he reaches by chaining together a spectrum of intermediate harms rather than comparing the two extremes head-on. Half the group insisted this was essentially the same argument in academic dress; the other half thought the differences in how each argument is actually built matter quite a bit. I said I'd look into it properly, so I'd like your help settling it.

Could you compare the two arguments in real depth, rather than just noting the family resemblance? I'm interested in the actual logical skeleton of each: where transitivity and chaining are doing the work in Norcross's spectrum of harms versus in Yudkowsky's more direct comparison, what each has to assume about the interpersonal comparability and summability of welfare, and which of the two is more exposed to a sorites-style objection that attacks the chain of comparisons itself rather than just biting the bullet on scope insensitivity. I'd also like your own verdict rather than just a list of similarities and differences: do the two arguments ultimately stand or fall together, or is one of them more resilient than the other to the standard escape routes — denying transitivity, denying that value is Archimedean in the relevant sense, or rejecting interpersonal aggregation outright?
