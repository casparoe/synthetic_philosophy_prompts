So I've been down a rabbit hole this week that started with re-reading Yudkowsky's old reductionism posts (the "Angry Atoms" / "Say Not Complexity" cluster) and ended with me reading Anderson's "More is Different" and a summary of Laughlin and Pines' "protectorate" paper on emergent physics, and now I'm thoroughly confused about whether these people are even talking about the same "emergence" that philosophers argue about. I brought it up at my ACX meetup last week and we mostly just talked past each other, so now I'm trying to get it straight in my own head instead.

As I understand it, there's the more "epistemic" notion of weak emergence that people like Bedau (and Chalmers, in his taxonomy of emergence) use — basically, a macro-property is weakly emergent if it's fully fixed by the micro-facts and micro-rules, but you can't predict it except by brute-force simulating the system, like gliders in Conway's Game of Life. Then there's the thing physicists mean when they talk about phase transitions, superconductivity, or renormalization-group universality, where wildly different microscopic setups all flow to the same macroscopic behavior — and some of them (Anderson, Laughlin) seem to want to say this is almost it's own separate ontological deal, not just "we haven't done the math yet," to the point where they talk about macro-level "organizing principles" as if they have a life of their own.

Could you help me actually compare these two notions of emergence properly? In particular I want to know: is the physicists' version just a fancier, more rigorous cousin of the same "only-predictable-by-brute-force-simulation" idea, or is something genuinely different going on philosophically (e.g. because of how idealized infinite limits work in the renormalization group, which no finite real system ever actually reaches)? And whichever way that shakes out — does either notion actually threaten the old Nagel-style picture of reduction via bridge laws, or are both of them ultimately compatible with reductionism and just cases where reduction is available in principle but a practical nightmare? I'd like an actual verdict here and not just a "well, it depends," if you can manage one.
