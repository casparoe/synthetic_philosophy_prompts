I've spent the past couple of weeks down a rabbit hole for a longer piece I'm writing on the history of astronomy, and I keep getting stuck on this Kepler-and-astrology thing. Every popular account mentions in passing that he cast horoscopes and wrote yearly almanacs to pay the bills, shrugs, and moves on with something like "well, even geniuses were men of their time" — which always felt like a cop-out to me. So I actually sat down with Tertius Interveniens (his 1610 broadside defending astrology against a physician who wanted it banned outright), the astrology sections of Harmonices Mundi, and an even earlier pamphlet where he tried to put astrology on, roughly translated, "more certain foundations." Turns out he wasn't defending the zodiac-and-houses package every jobbing astrologer of his day was selling — he'd worked out his own weird, fairly rigorous theory for why a much narrower kind of astrology should work, grounded in geometry and harmony rather then in occult rays or planetary "influence," and he was scathing about most working astrologers as superstitious hacks even while being financially dependent on doing basically that same job (he was still drawing up horoscopes for paying patrons right up until his death).

What I can't quite pin down on my own, and where I'd love your help, is exactly how Kepler's version of astrology differs from the mainstream Ptolemaic-style astrology of his day — what specifically he keeps, what he throws out, and why his reasons for keeping what he keeps are actually different in kind from the traditional justifications, and not just a trimmed-down version of the same thing. Theres also a follow-up thats been nagging me: given how far he strips it down, is "astrology" even the right word for what's left, or is he basically running an early theory of temperament-and-weather that happens to be dressed up in astrological language because that's the only vocabulary available to him at the time? Don't just give me the "he lived in a transitional period" hand-wave, I've already got that part — I want the actual mechanics of the comparison.
