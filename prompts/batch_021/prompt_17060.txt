Every time a LW post talks about whether an LLM 'really' believes something, it just gestures at Dennett's intentional stance and calls it a day, which always felt like it was skipping a step to me. Could you write me a dialogue between a Dennettian, who wants to say "the model believes X" can be literally true in the same thin 'real patterns' sense that it's true of a chess engine that it wants to trade off its bishop, and a Fodorian, who thinks real patterns aren't enough and that beliefs have to be discrete, causally efficacious symbols (language-of-thought style) or the talk is just convenient fiction. Kick things off with a hypothetical interpretability result: someone finds a single direction in a transformer's activations that reliably tracks whether it's about to assert vs. contradict some claim. I want them to hash out whether a finding like that could even in principle settle their disagreement or whether its just orthogonal to what they're really fighting about, with each actually responding to the other's specific moves rather then both just restating priors past each other. Don't strawman either side -- these are both serious, defensible positions in phil of mind.
