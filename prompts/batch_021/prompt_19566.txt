I recently reread Turing's 1950 paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" and was struck by a passage I'd previously skimmed past: in his survey of objections to the imitation game, Turing takes the statistical case for telepathy seriously enough that he proposes running the test inside what he calls a "telepathy-proof room," so that a psychically gifted human contestant couldn't use ESP to tip off the interrogator. As someone who spends a fair amount of time thinking about contamination and information leakage in machine learning benchmarks, this stuck with me. Please describe the most important implications of Turing's willingness to treat extrasensory perception as a genuine experimental confound for how much confidence we should place in purely behavioral, black-box tests of intelligence -- his imitation game as much as today's AI evaluations.
