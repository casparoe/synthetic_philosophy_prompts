"Kuhn's theory of scientific change says that most of the time, science operates as 'normal science' within a paradigm — a shared set of theories, methods and assumptions that tells scientists what counts as a real problem and what a real solution looks like. As normal science continues, anomalies that don't fit the paradigm build up until the field hits a crisis, and eventually a revolution replaces the old paradigm with a new one. Kuhn calls the old and new paradigms 'incommensurable' because there's no neutral, shared measure you can use to compare them directly — for instance, when astronomy shifted from the Ptolemaic to the Copernican system, the word 'planet' itself changed meaning, since Earth became a planet while the Sun and Moon stopped being planets. Because the two paradigms are incommensurable in this way, Kuhn thinks scientists on each side are essentially speaking different languages and literally can't understand what the other side is claiming, which is part of why he compares a paradigm shift to a religious conversion — Kuhn even came up with the famous duck-rabbit drawing to show how the switch happens in one sudden Gestalt flip rather than gradually, and he says it isn't something scientists can be argued into with reasons or evidence, only something caused by sociological pressure within the community."
