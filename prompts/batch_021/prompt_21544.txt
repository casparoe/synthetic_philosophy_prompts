Write a short story that takes seriously the trilemma forced by Bell's theorem: given the experimentally confirmed violation of the Bell inequalities, one must give up locality, give up realism (the idea that particles have definite properties independent of measurement), or give up "measurement independence" -- the assumption that an experimenter's choice of what to measure is statistically uncorrelated with the hidden variables of the system under test. Center the story on a physicist who becomes convinced that the third option, superdeterminism, is correct, and dramatize the vertigo of realizing that every "free" choice she has made in the lab was already correlated with outcomes fixed in the initial conditions of the universe. The story should not lean on the familiar, and physically incorrect, trope of entangled particles being used to send faster-than-light messages.
