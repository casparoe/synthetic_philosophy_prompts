I'm several chapters into a novel that follows an animal-rights lawyer who spars, across a string of family dinner-party scenes, with her brother-in-law, a libertarian economist who treats Robert Nozick as something close to scripture. While drafting his lines, I went back to Anarchy, State, and Utopia and got stuck on a passage I'd half-forgotten: in the course of setting up his theory of rights as inviolable "side constraints" on how persons may be treated, rather than goals to be maximized, Nozick briefly floats the idea of utilitarianism for animals and Kantianism for people — treating animal pleasures and pains as freely aggregable and tradeable against one another and against human benefit, while insisting that persons alone may never simply be used as means to others' ends. Tellingly, he seems uneasy with his own suggestion, conceding that it is unclear how the two systems are supposed to mesh whenever a single action affects both a person and an animal, and he leaves the tension essentially unresolved.

I would like my economist character to be pressed harder on this than Nozick presses himself, so that his sister-in-law can deliver a rebuttal with real teeth. Could you lay out, in full and at the level of rigor I could adapt for that scene, the strongest argument against maintaining this "utilitarianism for animals, Kantianism for people" split? I am especially keen for you to explore whether the very worry that led Nozick to reject unconstrained aggregation for persons elsewhere in the book — his "utility monster," and the broader thought that no individual should be treated as a mere receptacle for maximizing some overall sum — does not equally indict pure aggregation within the animal kingdom, and whether any non-arbitrary place remains to draw the line between beings who receive side constraints and beings who are merely weighed against one another. Please do not settle for a bare gesture at "the argument from marginal cases"; work through the strongest version of the case against Nozick's split, and address the best moves available to him, or to a defender of his position, along the way.
