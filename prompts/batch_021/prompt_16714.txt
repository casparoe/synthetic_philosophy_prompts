Random thing that's been bugging me while I prep my Moore seminar. In Principia Ethica he argues personal affection and the enjoyment of beauty are the two greatest goods there are, but crucially the value of either depends on an "organic unity" of genuine emotion plus a true belief about the object - so admiring someone for a virtue they don't actually have, or being moved by a gorgeous view that turns out to be a painted backdrop, is worth way less than the real thing, even though it feels identical from the inside. Given that, what's your best guess at what Moore would've said about people now who fall in love with AI chatbot companions, or who get genuinely moved by AI-generated art and music? Since theres no actual mind behind the "affection" or the "expression," does his view basically commit him to calling these cheap goods no matter how convincing the AI gets, or is there a more generous way to read him here?
