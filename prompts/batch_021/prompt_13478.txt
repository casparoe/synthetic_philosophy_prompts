Write a dialogue between two philosophers arguing over Thomson's lamp: a lamp toggled at times 1 - (1/2)^n (for n = 0, 1, 2, ...) within a single minute, so that it switches infinitely many times with no last switch ever occurring before the minute is up. One philosopher takes the position, associated with Thomson's original argument, that because the lamp must end up either on or off, and no switching in the sequence could be responsible for producing either outcome, the supposition that the supertask has been completed is simply contradictory. The other takes the position, associated with Benacerraf's reply, that no contradiction has actually been derived -- only that the sequence fails to fix a final state for the lamp, which is a gap in the description rather than a paradox in the world. Have them press each other on whether that gap can really be filled in without cost, and on what, if anything, their dispute settles about the more general question behind Zeno's paradoxes: whether an infinite series of tasks could ever be completed in principle. Neither side should have the easier case to make, and each should respond to what the other has actually just said rather than simply restating their own position.
