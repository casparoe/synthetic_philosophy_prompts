Ok so I live in a tiny condo building, just three units total, and because it's so small there's no property management company or bigger HOA above us -- the three of us owners literally ARE the board, we just sit around someone's kitchen table every couple months and sort things out ourselves. Last month we had to vote on a pretty big special assessment to fix our roof, and something about how that vote went down has been bugging me ever since, even though I can't quite tell if anyone actually did anything wrong.

Basically we'd all agreed ahead of time that the assessment should only go through if two seperate things were both true: (1) the roof actually needs replacing now rather than in a couple more years (we'd gotten two inspection reports and they didn't fully agree with each other), and (2) we could cover the cost without pushing anyone's monthly fees past what they could realistically afford. So at the meeting we voted on those two questions one at a time. On "does it need replacing now," two of us said yes and one said no. On "can we afford it without wrecking anyone's budget," a different two of us said yes and one said no -- not the same person who'd been outvoted on the first question. Since both questions passed by majority, we went ahead and approved the assessment.

Then a few days later one of my neighbors said, kind of as a joke, "wait, if you'd just asked each of us straight up whether we're for or against this assessment, I'd have said no, and so would you" -- and she was completely right. If instead of splitting it into those two questions we'd just polled the three of us directly on the assessment itself, two out of three would've voted it down. Nobody flip-flopped, nobody was being inconsistent, everybody reasoned the exact same way from start to finish -- we just landed on totally opposite answers depending on whether we voted on the two pieces separately or on the whole thing at once. I tried explaining this to my brother-in-law, who's an engineer, and he just said "yeah, that's why committees are the worst," which, ok, not exactly the answer I was looking for. It's honestly kind of unsettling, because it feels like whoever gets to decide HOW we vote (piece by piece vs. all at once) basically gets to decide the outcome, and that seems like it can't be right for something we all thought was just a neutral procedural choice. Was one of the two ways of voting the "correct" one and we just used the wrong one by accident, or is there something deeper going on that I'm missing? And going forward, is there any way to run votes like this that actually avoids this kind of thing, or are we just stuck picking a method and hoping it doesn't bite us again?
