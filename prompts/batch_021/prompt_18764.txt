For a while now I've been reading about metaethics purely as a hobby -- I have no formal training in philosophy, just whatever books, papers, and recorded lectures I can find -- and one argument I keep circling back to is the "evolutionary debunking" challenge to moral realism. As far as I understand it, the idea is that traits like loyalty to kin, outrage at free-riders, or disgust at certain acts were instilled in us by natural selection simply because having those particular reactions helped our ancestors survive and reproduce, and this would be true regardless of whether there are any actual, mind-independent moral facts for those reactions to be tracking. If that is the real causal story behind our moral sense, it would be a staggering coincidence for our moral beliefs to also happen to be true, so a moral realist's confidence in them starts to look unearned.

When I brought this up with a friend, the objection that stuck with me is that the very same reasoning seems to apply to elementary arithmetic and logic. Presumably our ancestors' confidence that two predators are worse than one, or that a statement and its denial cannot both be true, was also favored by selection because it made them act successfully, not because natural selection was somehow reaching out and grasping abstract mathematical or logical truths as such. If "produced by selection pressures indifferent to the truth of the belief" is enough to debunk a class of beliefs, then arithmetic and logic look just as debunked as morality -- and since almost no one is prepared to accept that, something seems wrong with running the argument against morality specifically.

Digging further, I found that Sharon Street, who this style of argument is most closely associated with, apparently anticipates exactly this move. As I understand her reply, she argues that for beliefs like basic arithmetic, being useful and being true would naturally tend to go together (I believe she calls this a "tracking" relationship), whereas for moral beliefs, usefulness and truth could easily have come apart, so morality does not get to borrow whatever protection logic and math enjoy. Please argue, as rigorously as you can, that this proposed asymmetry does not actually hold up, and that anyone who accepts the evolutionary debunking argument against moral realism is thereby committed, whether they like it or not, to the same debunking skepticism about basic arithmetic and logic.
