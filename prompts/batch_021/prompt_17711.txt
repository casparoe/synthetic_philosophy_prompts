I'm finishing a novel narrated in the first person by Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz, and my publisher wants me to close the launch event with a live Q&A conducted in character as her. In the actual Respuesta a Sor Filotea, she habitually wraps quite bold claims about her own learning in professions of humility and a well-timed scriptural citation, and for the rehearsal I want an audience member who refuses to let that move go unchallenged. Draft eight interview questions, sequenced so the humility deflection gets progressively harder to use, keyed to the real content of her argument: her claim that theology, the queen of the sciences, requires logic, physics, arithmetic, and history as its foundation; her reworking of Paul's instruction that women keep silent in church into a charge that older women teach the younger; and the childhood episodes she offers as proof that her thirst for learning was involuntary rather than chosen. Close with one question that uses the reader's hindsight against her: knowing she will, within a few years, sell her books and sign a renewal of her vows in her own blood, ask whether she can still call the path defended in the Respuesta a choice rather than the capitulation biographers such as Octavio Paz have described.
