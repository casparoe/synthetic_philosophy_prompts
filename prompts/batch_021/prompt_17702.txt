I'm a philosophy PhD student (my dissertation's on moral uncertainty, if that's relevant) and I somehow got roped onto the three-person grants subcommittee for my university's effective giving society. Every spring we write up a recommendation memo for the full membership to vote on before the ~$18k we've raised goes out the door, and this year the two finalists could not be more different: a GiveDirectly-style cash transfer charity with mountains of RCT evidence behind its near-term effects but nothing much to say about the next century, versus a small AI-safety/biosecurity outfit whose whole case for impact is a long causal chain about shaving some tiny amount off extinction risk decades or centuries out, which literally no one can verify either way. Two of us want to just default to the cash transfers, on the grounds that at least we know what we're buying. The third -- our resident longtermist, who keeps citing some Greaves paper on "cluelessness" -- says that's confused, because we're just as clueless about the far-future effects of the cash transfers too (more kids surviving changes how many people there are, how much they consume, emissions -- everything downstream of that), so if the cluelessness is genuinely symmetric it cant be a reason to favor one option over the other, and we should just bite the bullet and go with expected value.

Could you draft the actual memo the subcommittee would send? We vote next Tuesday so it needs to land on a real recommendation, not "here are some considerations, good luck" -- and I want it to actually grapple with the cluelessness worry instead of gesturing at it, e.g. is there a genuine difference between unknown effects that are basically noise and plausibly cancel out versus ones that are probably systematically one-directional even though we can't tell which direction, and does leaning on that distinction actually survive scrutiny here. Whichever way you come down, give the side you're rejecting its strongest version -- at least one of the other two is going to push back hard no matter what we recommend.
