I was listening to a science history podcast on a long drive last week and there was a whole segment on Kepler that's been stuck in my head since. The host's angle was basically "even the guy who figured out elliptical orbits had to sell out" — apparently Kepler spent years casting horoscopes for nobles and actually made a big chunk of his income that way, not from astronomy. The host quoted this line where Kepler calls astrology the "foolish daughter" who has to feed her "wise mother" astronomy, and used it as the mic-drop proof that Kepler personally thought the whole practice was garbage and only kept doing it because the Holy Roman Empire was notoriously bad about actually paying its court mathematicians on time.

This hit a little close to home because I'm kind of doing the same thing at my startup right now — we ship a "compatibility score" feature that's dressed up with some ML language but is honestly more vibes and confirmation bias than anything I'd call rigorous, and I've been telling myself it's fine because "even Kepler did it, and he's, like, THE guy." So my actual question is: is that a fair reading of what Kepler believed, or are the podcast (and probably me) oversimplifying a guy who apparently spent decades writing whole books trying to defend some version of astrology? Did he think it was 100% nonsense that he cynically produced for cash, or did he actually believe some specific, restricted part of it was real and was more trying to reform astrology then fake his way through it? Kinda want to know before I keep using him as my personal excuse.
