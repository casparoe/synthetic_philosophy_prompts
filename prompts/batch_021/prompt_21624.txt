Ok so I went down a bit of a rabbit hole last week after someone in an ACX comment thread brought up Avicenna's "flying man" argument and I havent been able to stop thinking about it since. Quick recap in case it's not fresh for you (though I'd guess it is): Avicenna asks you to imagine a man created by God in a single instant, fully grown and fully rational, but suspended in a void, blindfolded, with his limbs held apart so they don't even touch each other or the rest of his body -- so basically zero sensory input, not even proprioception. His claim is that this man, despite having no grounds whatsoever to affirm he has a body, or hands, or a world, would still immediately and certainly affirm his own existence, his bare "I am." He uses that to argue that self-awareness is direct and prior to any bodily awareness, which is doing a lot of the same work as Descartes' cogito, except like 600 years earlier and aimed more at proving the soul is a seperate substance from the body.

Anyway, I'd love it if you wrote me a short story that actually takes this scenario seriously and sits inside it, rather than just gesturing at it -- like, really try to render the phenomenology (if that's even the right word) of a mind that switches on with zero sensory anchor and has to figure out from the inside what, if anything, it can know about itself. You don't need to set it in medieval Persia or mention Avicenna by name -- I keep thinking this maps eerily well onto an upload, or an AI in the instant before it's ever hooked up to any input/output channels, or maybe some extreme sensory-deprivation-tank scenario, but honestly take it wherever feels most alive to you, those are just the first places my brain went. I'd rather have something that really earns its central conceit than something plot-heavy -- think more Egan or Ted Chiang than airport thriller. Something like 800-1200 words would be perfect.
