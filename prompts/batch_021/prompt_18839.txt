So im drafting a historical novel with a character loosely based on Hobbes (self-taught obsessive type, holed up scribbling about commonwealths during the Civil War years), and I've been reading bits of the actual Leviathan to steal some of his voice for the manuscript. Got kind of stuck on this line from Chapter 4 though:

"Seeing then that truth consisteth in the right ordering of names in our affirmations, a man that seeketh precise truth had need to remember what every name he uses stands for, and to place it accordingly; or else he will find himself entangled in words, as a bird in lime-twigs, the more he struggles, the more belimed. And therefore in geometry, which is the only science that it hath pleased God hitherto to bestow on mankind, men begin at settling the significations of their words; which settling of significations, they call definitions, and place them in the beginning of their reckoning."

I get the basic idea (loose definitions = bad, careful ones = good), but why does he zero in on geometry specifically and call it literally the ONLY science God's ever given mankind — what about astronomy, or medicine? Is that just a rhetorical flourish, or is he setting up something he actually leans on later, like building up the whole rest of the book (state of nature, sovereign, commonwealth) step by step from definitions the way a geometer builds from axioms? Trying to get my character's weird geometry obsession to feel earned instead of just "quirky guy likes triangles."
