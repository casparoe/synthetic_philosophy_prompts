A friend and I spent half of last night arguing about whether the use-mention distinction does any real moral work with slurs -- she thinks mentioning the word is never meaningfully different from using it as an insult, I kept insisting that context and intent obviously matter -- and neither of us budged. This week almost the identical dispute landed on the faculty committee I sit on that handles classroom-conduct complaints: an instructor teaching a unit on the semantics and pragmatics of slurs wrote the unredacted words on the board and had students pronounce them aloud while comparing rival theories, including prohibitionism and Camp's perspectival account, on the grounds that euphemisms like "the n-word" can't sustain the very distinctions those theories are fighting over. Two students filed a complaint insisting that the harm doesn't depend on the instructor's intentions, or on whether the word was used or merely mentioned. The committee is split and must decide before next week's meeting whether this was a policy violation and, either way, whether to recommend any change to how such material is taught. Please draft the committee's memo: a clear recommendation with reasons, including genuine engagement with the strongest version of the complaint rather than a quick appeal to academic freedom.
