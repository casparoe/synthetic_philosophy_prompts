I'm a political theory PhD student serving as one of two graduate representatives on my university's student conduct board. Two weeks ago, a student group staged a sit-in blocking the main doors of the administration building for several hours to protest a mid-year tuition increase; organizers had announced the action publicly in advance, and it remained non-violent throughout. When campus police ordered the crowd to disperse, most students left, but eleven stayed, were arrested for trespassing, and issued a joint statement saying they had chosen to remain 'to accept the consequences, as civil disobedience requires.' The board must now recommend sanctions, ranging from a warning to suspension, and has asked me to draft the discussion memo for next week's meeting, since I was the one who raised two questions the board could not resolve: first, whether a dispute over tuition, as opposed to, say, a violation of someone's basic rights, is even the kind of grievance to which the concept of civil disobedience properly applies; second, whether crediting the eleven's willingness to be arrested as a point in their favor makes sense at all, given that on the standard accounts of civil disobedience, what gives the act its point is precisely that the protester bears the cost, so that leniency might undercut the very thing they were trying to demonstrate. Please draft the memo: give a concrete recommendation on sanctions for the eleven (and, if you think it bears on the matter, for those who left when ordered to), take an actual position on both questions rather than leaving them open, and address the strongest objection to whatever you recommend.
