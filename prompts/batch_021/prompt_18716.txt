I'm a software engineer who's been doing some AI-safety and decision-theory reading on the side, and I've come across the concept of "acausal threats," along with a proposed defense called "surrogate goals": an agent commits in advance to respond to a threat against some cheap, arbitrary proxy exactly as it would respond to a threat against what it actually cares about, so that a rational threatener supposedly ends up targeting the proxy instead. I can't see how this could matter, though. If a threat is genuinely acausal, there is no causal channel between my decisions and the agent issuing it, so nothing I decide now can change whether that agent makes or carries out the threat - the outcome is fixed independently of anything I do. Doesn't that make adopting a surrogate goal, or any other precommitment, irrelevant against a threat that I have no causal power to influence in the first place?
