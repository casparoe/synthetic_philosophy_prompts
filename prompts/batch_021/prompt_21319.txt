A friend and I had a long argument last night that started with office gossip and ended up somewhere far more abstract. My company recently pushed out several managers who were blamed for a toxic, backstabbing culture, but the atmosphere was completely unchanged within weeks. My friend argued that this was inevitable, since the culture was never really made up of those particular people to begin with; it is sustained by the bonus structure and reporting lines, which would produce much the same behavior in almost anyone who occupied those roles. I argued that this gets things backwards: an organization is not anything more than what its members currently think and do, so the culture just is the pattern of their behavior, and it has to change once enough of the individuals do. Neither of us could settle whether the culture is somehow more basic than the people currently making it up, or just a convenient way of describing their behavior. Who has the better argument here, and is this even a question with a determinate answer?
