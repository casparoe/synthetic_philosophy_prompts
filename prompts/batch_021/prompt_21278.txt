I cover AI and decision theory for a science outlet, and that's somehow how I ended up as the token outside member on the internal review board of a small longtermist research shop that does acausal bargaining work -- how AI systems that can predict each other but never actually interact might still threaten or cooperate with one another (picture something Center on Long-Term Risk-shaped). Right now the boards stuck on whether to publish a staff paper on 'surrogate goals': an agent expecting this kind of threat commits in advance to treat threats against some cheap decoy value as though they were threats against its real one, so a rational threatener redirects there instead and actual harm stays small even if the threat gets carried out. Half the board wants it published as serious safety work; the other half thinks spelling out the mechanism is dangerous in itself, since a threatener who's read the paper can just threaten the surrogate and the real goal together, or make the threat conditional on 'no surrogate adopted' -- basically handing adversaries the counter to your own defense, not to mention reviving all the old Roko's basilisk anxieties. Can you draft the memo the board needs before Friday's vote? It should recommend publish, publish-with-redactions, or shelve, and whichever it picks it actually has to answer the meta-threat objection rather then wave it off as paranoia.
