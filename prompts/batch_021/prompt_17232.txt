A friend and I spent much of last night debating Turing's imitation game, and she raised a point I haven't been able to shake since: in his 1950 paper, Turing takes the statistical evidence for telepathy seriously enough to worry that a psychic contestant could tip off the interrogator, and he even proposes isolating participants in a telepathy-proof room to close the loophole. Doesn't building a safeguard against ESP into the test amount to Turing quietly conceding that some human mental powers aren't computable, which would seem to undercut his own claim that thinking is just a matter of information processing? If she's right about that, I'm puzzled why philosophers of mind still treat the imitation game as a serious benchmark for machine intelligence.
