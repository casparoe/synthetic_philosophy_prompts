Been reading about the hole argument in phil of physics (the GR determinism puzzle involving diffeomorphisms) and wrote up my own quick version of it below, mainly to test if I actually understand it properly. Can you go through it and flag anything thats off, especially the "sophisticated substantivalism" bit near the end? Also tell me honestly whether the determinism framing is the most natural way to motivate the puzzle or if there's a better angle I'm missing.

GR's field equations are generally covariant: if g solves them on a manifold M, so does any diffeomorphism-dragged version of it, φ*g. So take a spacetime with matter in it, carve out a "hole" H, some open region with no matter, and pick a diffeomorphism φ that's the identity everywhere outside H but does something nontrivial inside it. Both g and φ*g solve the equations, and they agree everywhere outside H (all the matter fields, the whole history up to the edge of the hole), but inside H they disagree about which metric value goes with which point.

If spacetime points are real individuals with identities that don't depend on what fields happen to sit on them (manifold substantivalism), this is bad news: you've got identical data everywhere outside the hole but two genuinely different possibilities for what happens inside it, so the theory just doesn't determine what happens there. And that failure of determinism has nothing to do with actual physics, its just a byproduct of the theory being generally covariant.

Earman and Norton's own move, a bit surprisingly, is to bite the bullet and say fine, give up substantivalism. The standard rescue attempt instead is "sophisticated substantivalism": keep believing spacetime points are real, but deny that the point in g is the same individual as "the corresponding" point in φ*g, i.e. deny there's a fact about cross-model identity that outruns the qualitative structure. Once you say that, g and φ*g stop being rival descriptions of the same situation and just become two descriptions of one possibility (this is the "Leibniz equivalence" move), so determinism is saved and you keep your points.

What bugs me: doesn't this just relocate the whole dispute into a claim about modality/haecceitism, rather than actually resolve anything about the physics? It reads like the sophisticated substantivalist wins by stipulation rather than argument. Curious what you think.
