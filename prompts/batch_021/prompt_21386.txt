My friend Alex and my other friend Sam got into a whole thing over dinner last week about a decision our friend Renee made a few months back, and I can't decide who actually won. Renee had two job offers: a well paid but kind of soulless data analyst job at an insurance company, and a much lower paid coordinator job at a small environmental nonprofit that she said actually felt like it mattered. She agonized over it for something like eight months and then took the nonprofit job, basically on a gut call.

Alex's opening point was that the whole eight months was pointless: theres no common scale between "more money" and "more meaning," so neither job was really better than the other, and if nothing is better than anything else there's nothing to deliberate about, she could've flipped a coin in week one. Sam said that's wrong, because if the two jobs were genuinely tied, even a small sweetener to the insurance offer should've flipped her preference -- and the insurance company actually did bump the offer by a few thousand bucks in negotiation, and it still didn't make Renee want it more. So they weren't equal either, which means the coin-flip take is wrong too. Sam said she'd read about this exact situation having a name, being "on a par," as some third option besides better, worse, or tied.

Alex's comeback was that "on a par" is just a fancy way of saying "we can't measure it precisely enough," not proof some new category actually exists out there. Sam's response was that the point isn't that Renee was trying to detect a hidden fact about which job was objectively better -- deliberating that hard, in a case like this, is literally how someone decides who they want to be, and picking the nonprofit is what made it the right choice for her, so the eight months weren't wasted, they were the choosing. Alex wasn't having it: said that sounds like a nice thing you tell yourself after a scary leap so it doesnt feel random, and that it would work no matter which job she'd taken, which makes it sound less like insight and more like a story that can't lose.

Neither of them backed down. Can you go through their argument move by move and say who's got the better of it at each point, and point out anything you think they're both missing?
