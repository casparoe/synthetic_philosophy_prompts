I'm on the student council at my high school, and every winter we run a week-long fundraiser called Penny Wars to benefit a local food pantry. Every grade gets its own jar: dropping in pennies or dollar bills adds points to your own grade's total, while dropping in silver coins subtracts points from a rival grade's jar. Whichever grade finishes Friday with the highest net point total gets to pick the theme for the spring pep rally and a catered lunch, but every single cent collected during the week goes to the food pantry no matter which grade ends up "winning" — nobody gets any of it back, regardless of the outcome.

By Wednesday of this year's drive, it had turned into a two-grade race between us (juniors) and the sophomores, with everyone else far behind. We'd built up close to an $80 lead just from spare change people tossed in casually over the first two days. Then the sophomore officers dumped in about $110 in silver coins in one afternoon and jumped ahead of us. Several people in our class group chat immediately pushed to pool extra money — well beyond what any of us would normally put toward this kind of thing — specifically to retake the lead before Friday. The argument that kept coming up was that if we didn't respond, everything we'd already put in that week "wouldn't have counted for anything." Someone did point out that the sophomores could just top our new total again before Friday, but that got waved off pretty quickly and the conversation moved on to logistics. I understood the impulse to respond, but the reasoning behind it bothered me, especially since barely anyone actually cares who picks the pep rally theme, and the money was headed to the food pantry either way.

I brought this up with a couple of friends afterward, and we spent almost half an hour going back and forth without really agreeing on anything, so I'd like an outside perspective. Is there actually something wrong with the "it wouldn't have counted for anything" reasoning for chipping in more, or is my discomfort with it misplaced? And more generally, how should a group in our position think through whether it's worth continuing to add money just to retake the lead?
