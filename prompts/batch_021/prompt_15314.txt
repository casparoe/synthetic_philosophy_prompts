I spent my career as a marine physiologist and only got into philosophy after retiring; lately I've been reading Descartes' letters to Henry More, where he argues animals are just automata -- machines with nothing going on inside, since they don't really use language and their behaviour is fixed rather than flexible like ours. Turns out it's not just an old debate for me: I sit on my university's animal ethics committee, and we've got a protocol from a postdoc wanting to run electric-shock avoidance trials on shore crabs and cuttlefish with no analgesia, since our rules only require pain relief for animals with backbones. Half the committee want full vertebrate-style protections given recent nociception and avoidance-learning evidence in decapods and cephalopods; the other half think that's projecting feelings onto reflexes with nobody home, and don't want to block good research for no solid reason. Could you draft the short decision memo I'll circulate before next weeks vote -- an actual recommendation with conditions and a timeframe, not just "more research needed" -- one that takes the automaton-style sceptical side seriously rather than strawmanning it?
