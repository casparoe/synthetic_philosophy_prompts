"PAP says a person is only morally responsible for an act if they could have done otherwise. Frankfurt's case is supposed to break this: Black is a neuroscientist who's wired a chip into Jones's brain that will kick in and make Jones vote for the Democrat if Jones starts to show signs of deciding to vote Republican instead. As it happens Jones decides all on his own to vote Democrat, so the chip never has to fire. Frankfurt says Jones is still responsible for voting Democrat even though, because of the chip, he literally couldn't have done otherwise - which is supposed to show PAP is false.

Defenders of PAP weren't ready to give up, and came back with the 'flicker of freedom' reply - a move originally floated by Frankfurt himself as a concession - that even in the Jones case, Jones has a thin alternative possibility: he could've shown the prior sign that would've triggered the chip, so in a technical sense he could have done otherwise. Fischer's response is that this flicker is too flimsy to ground responsibility, since showing the sign wouldn't really be a free action anyway.

So basically, because the chip never actually has to fire, Jones's vote is fully determined either way - and that's exactly what shows determinism and moral responsibility are compatible."
