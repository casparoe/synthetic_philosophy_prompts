So this happened at a reading group two people from my program run on philosophy of religion (I mostly just go for the wine, my actual work is on modality/counterfactuals, which is the only reason I could even follow the argument). They'd just read the Molina chapter and ended up going several rounds on the grounding objection to middle knowledge that never really got resolved, and its been bugging me since.

(1) Marco opened with the standard objection: counterfactuals of creaturely freedom, like "if Curley were offered a bribe he'd take it," are supposed to be contingently true prior to any divine decree -- not necessary, and not fixed by God's will either -- but there's nothing about Curley that could make that true rather than false or indeterminate. No truthmaker, so middle knowledge is empty.

(2) Ilana came back with the Craig line: that objection just assumes a truthmaker principle nobody's actually defended. Plenty of truths get treated as brute all the time -- e.g. facts about what a merely possible person's uninstantiated dispositions would be -- and nobody thinks that's incoherent, so why is this case special.

(3) Marco said the disanalogy is that any brute truth we're comfortable with concerns something actual, whose actual properties could at least in principle do the grounding work, whereas a merely possible Curley-in-circumstance-C isn't a thing that exists to have any properties, actual or otherwise, for the truth to depend on.

(4) Ilana replied that this proves too much: even for an actual person, what "grounds" a counterfactual about a choice they've never actually faced is just further facts about their dispositions, and dispositions just are further counterfactuals, not some deeper categorical fact -- so anyone who accepts ordinary counterfactuals about real people's untested choices is already committed to the same kind of fact Molinism needs, they just don't notice because there's a person there for their intuitions to latch onto.

(5) Marco felt like something was off with that but couldn't pin down what, and the conversation just kind of fizzled into someone changing the subject.

Can you go through this move by move instead of just handing out an overall winner? For each numbered move say who's got the better of it (or if it's a wash), and then tell me specifically whether Marco actually has a good reply to Ilana's move in (4) available to him -- some real disanalogy between the actual-person case and the merely-possible one -- or whether her point genuinely generalizes and Molinists are worse off than they usually let on.
