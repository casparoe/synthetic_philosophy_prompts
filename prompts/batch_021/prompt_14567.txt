I am several drafts into a novel whose central figure is a sociolinguist who becomes entangled in a public controversy after using a slur in a scholarly context — quoting it, not using it to insult anyone — and I want the philosophical stakes of her situation to be right rather than merely plausible-sounding. In researching her field, I have been reading through the technical literature on the semantics and pragmatics of slurs, and I keep getting stuck between two theories that seem to disagree about almost everything except the data they are both trying to explain. The first is Christopher Hom's combinatorial externalism, later extended with Robert May, on which a slur's derogatory force is built directly into its truth-conditional content: the word is taken to semantically encode a complex, socially constructed property tied to some racist institution's practices and beliefs, and so thoroughly that Hom and May end up arguing every sentence predicating a slur of someone is systematically false, since no such institution's presumed properties ever actually obtain. The second is Luvell Anderson and Ernie Lepore's prohibitionism, on which none of this talk of content is doing the real work: a slur is offensive simply because there is a standing social prohibition on the word-form itself, rather like a taboo, which is why even quoting or mentioning the word, with no content asserted about anyone, still seems to give offense, and why a colorless placeholder like "the N-word" can refer to the very same term without triggering the same reaction.

I would like your help thinking through what is really at stake between these two views, partly because I do not fully trust my own grasp of the material and partly because I want my character's self-defense, later in the book, to track a genuine philosophical position rather than a straw man I have invented for the plot's convenience. Concretely: which theory gives the better account of the fact that mere mention of a slur, as opposed to its use in predicating something of a target, still seems to carry offense? And separately, how well do you think either theory handles reclamation, where members of the targeted group appropriate the very same word-form for in-group use — or does reclamation instead expose a real weakness in both accounts? I am not looking for a verdict handed down from nowhere; please argue for whichever position, synthesis, or rejection of both you think the evidence actually supports, and flag anywhere you think I have oversimplified the debate in setting it up this way.
