A friend and I spent much of last night arguing about disjunctivism in the philosophy of perception, and she got the better of me on a point I would like to revisit. I was defending the disjunctivist's claim that a veridical perception and a subjectively indistinguishable hallucination share no common underlying mental state. Her objection, which I now think is the strongest one available, was that the same proximate cause in the brain can produce the experience whether or not a matching external object is present, and that it seems wildly implausible for one type of cause to yield experiences of fundamentally different natures depending only on facts about what is happening outside the head. Please help me construct the strongest disjunctivist response to this objection, ideally one that does not simply stipulate, without further defense, that hallucination can only be characterized negatively, as whatever is introspectively indiscriminable from a veridical perception.
