I'm a cognitive science PhD student -- my lab works on metacognition, basically confidence judgments and the "feeling of rightness" people get about their own reasoning -- and I've fallen down a rabbit hole into the philosophy side of this, specifically the epistemology literature on higher-order evidence and epistemic akrasia (Christensen, Elga's level-splitting stuff, Worsnip, Horowitz, that whole cluster). I think Ive got the basic shape of the puzzle: sometimes your first-order evidence points to P, but you also pick up evidence that your reasoning about P is somehow compromised, and then theres this genuinely weird question of whether rationality demands you actually lower your credence in P, or whether its fine to just sit there believing P while also believing "my evidence doesn't support P" -- the akratic state that sounds crazy on its face but that some pretty serious epistemologists think is sometimes fine, or even required.

This isn't just armchair stuff for me because it maps embarrassingly well onto my actual life: I do most of my real analysis and writing between like 11pm and 3am, and there's a recurring pattern where I'm completely certain some regression spec or an argument in a draft is airtight in the moment, and then the next morning I spot the dumb error immediately. My advisor's running joke is that nothing I conclude after midnight counts, which is basically her handing me higher-order evidence about my own reasoning ("sleep deprived people reason worse, you're sleep deprived, discount accordingly") that I then have to weigh against however confident I feel about the actual argument sitting in front of me.

Could you walk me through the most important implications of the higher-order evidence / epistemic akrasia debate for how I should actually handle situations like this? One thing I especially want to know is whether the standard conciliationist pressure -- to knock your credence down whenever you get solid evidence that you're impaired -- runs into a regress problem, since if I can't trust my 1am object-level reasoning, why would I trust my 1am reasoning about how much to discount it? And the other is whether the literature gives me any principled grounds for thinking it's not irrational to just flag "I'm probably compromised right now" and keep the belief anyway, instead of treating that flag as something I'm rationally required to update on.
