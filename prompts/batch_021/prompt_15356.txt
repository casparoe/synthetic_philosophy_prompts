I have to decide by Friday between two summer internships, and the more I try to compare them, the less I trust any method I use. One is a well-paid software engineering internship at a large tech company; the other is an unpaid research assistantship doing fieldwork abroad with a professor whose work genuinely excites me. In my seminar on practical reason, we've been taught that for any two options, one must be better than the other, worse than the other, or exactly as good as the other. But when I built a spreadsheet scoring each internship on pay, résumé value, and personal fulfillment, every weighting scheme I tried felt arbitrary, and none of the resulting numbers seemed trustworthy. My roommate insists this just means I haven't introspected carefully enough, and that a sufficiently careful comparison would reveal either a genuine winner or an exact tie. But here's what puzzles me. If I imagine the tech internship throwing in an extra $1,000 signing bonus, that doesn't seem to make it the better option overall — yet I also don't think the two internships would still be exactly tied once that bonus is added. What's actually going on here, and how should I go about making this decision?
