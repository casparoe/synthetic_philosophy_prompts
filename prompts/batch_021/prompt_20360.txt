Okay, this is a real thing that happened to me about two weeks ago and I can't stop chewing on it, and I'm pretty sure it's going to end up in the novel I'm drafting (my two leads are estranged siblings, so at this point I'm calling it research). My younger brother and I split the cost of a scratch-off ticket fifty-fifty at a gas station on the way home from our mom's birthday dinner, as a joke, and it actually won $4,000. He immediately said he should get something like 70% instead of half, because he's been out of work since the spring and that money would genuinely change his month, whereas for me it would just get parked in savings and basically not register. And the annoying thing is, I don't think he's wrong about that part -- the same chunk of money probably would matter more to him than it would to me, if I'm honest. But we put in the exact same amount, on the exact same coin-flip of luck, and giving up on the 50/50 split still feels off to me in a way I can't quite articulate. My partner thinks I'm being stingy, my brother says I'm "technically correct but a bad person," and I brought it up at my writers' group half-joking last week and it turned into a forty-minute argument, so apparently this isn't just a me problem.

What I actually want to know is whether theres a principled way to settle something like this, or if its just vibes all the way down. Is my brother allowed to lean on "it would mean more to me" as basically a knockdown argument, or is there something sneaky about that move? Like, is there any real way for either of us to verify a claim like that, or does it just collapse into whoever states their own need most convincingly wins? I'd love an actual answer I could use to either hold my ground or fold with a clear conscience next time it comes up -- and yeah, some version of this fight is absolutely getting written into a scene at some point.
