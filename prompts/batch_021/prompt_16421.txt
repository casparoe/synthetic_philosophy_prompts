For my high school's philosophy club, I've been writing about paradoxes of infinity, comparing Thomson's lamp with the Ross-Littlewood 'ball and vase' paradox. Benacerraf's classic reply to Thomson grants that the lamp's setup is not contradictory, only that its final state is left undetermined by the switching rule -- though this still leaves something unsatisfying about the case. The vase paradox, meanwhile, is usually treated as fully solved: if the lowest-numbered ball is always the one removed, exactly zero balls remain after the supertask even though ten balls go in for every one taken out at each step; yet if the highest-numbered ball present is removed instead, infinitely many balls remain forever, from an equally natural rule. Please argue that once this rule-sensitivity is taken seriously, the vase's 'determinate' answer is no real advance on the lamp's indeterminacy, and that the two puzzles deserve the same philosophical verdict rather than one being treated as solved and the other as merely unresolved.
