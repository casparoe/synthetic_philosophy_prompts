I'm outlining a novel with a bored signals-intercept officer as a POV character, and I've been down a rabbit hole of old probability puzzles for texture. Told two friends about one, the Judy Benjamin problem, at dinner last week and instead of enjoying it as trivia they argued the actual math for like 40 min. Setup: a soldier parachutes into a region split into four equal quadrants, two in Blue territory (call them I and II), two in Red (III and IV); with zero info she puts 25% on each. HQ's garbled reply only gets through as "if you're in Red, you're three times more likely to be in III than IV" -- nothing about red vs blue overall, nothing comparing I to II.

1. Renata: the update is obvious from what ISN'T mentioned -- I and II stay equal to each other, and by the same logic red-vs-blue can't move either, since "if red, then X" is true regardless of what P(red) is. So you just redistribute inside red 3:1 and leave the rest alone: III to 37.5%, IV to 12.5%, I and II stay at 25% each.

2. Josh (does actuarial-ish work) said he actually ran it using the standard move for this kind of update -- minimizing relative entropy/cross-entropy against the prior subject to the new constraint -- and that gives P(red) dropping to something like 46-47%, even though the message never mentions blue at all.

3. Renata said that's got to be a misuse of the method, not a real result -- you cant wring information about a disjunction out of a conditional thats silent on its own antecedent's probability. If the math says otherwise, its being fed the wrong question.

4. Josh said the I/II symmetry holds for a real reason: swap the labels "I" and "II" everywhere, message included, and nothing about the problem changes. But there's no equivalent swap for red/blue once you've bolted on a constraint that singles out red's internal ratio -- the flat prior alone was propping up "P(red)=50%," and the new info gives a real, non-arbitrary reason to move off it.

5. Renata said fine, maybe no symmetry forces it to stay put, but that just shows this "disturb the prior as little as possible" method has no business calling itself minimal or conservative. If the least committal way of digesting a fact about red's insides quietly reshuffles red vs blue anyway, it isn't "adding only what the evidence forces," whatever it calls itself.
