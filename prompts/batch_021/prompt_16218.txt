So I somehow ended up on my county's little all-volunteer land trust board a few years back (I run a landscaping business, definately not a science background), and we've got a genuinely tough call due at next Tuesday's meeting. A local herpetologist came to our last meeting and asked us to put around $40k of this year's habitat budget - which is a big chunk of what we have - toward buying and protecting a strip of land connecting two ponds where a certain population of salamanders lives.

Here's the part that's been bugging me ever since, so I went and read up on it myself a bit. Apparently these salamanders are part of a whole chain of populations that loops most of the way around the valley - each one can mate fine with its immediate neighbors up and down the chain, but by the time you follow the chain all the way around, the two ends can't produce babies together anymore, even though there's an unbroken line of successful mating connecting them the whole way round. The population we'd actually be protecting sits somewhere in the middle, not at either end. Two of our members (one of them's my cousin, so it's gotten a little tense) think this obviously isn't worth the money, since "it's not even it's own species - it mates fine with the next population over, and that one's common as dirt and already protected two towns over." The herpetologist and another member say that logic proves too much, because you could run the exact same argument on the two end populations and conclude they're "the same species" too, which is the opposite of what the mating evidence actually shows. Nobody on the board can agree on what would even count as a good reason to fund this or not, so we just keep talking past each other about it.

We vote either way at that meeting, so could you write the short memo I'd actually bring in? I need it to land on a real recommendation with a concrete timeframe attached, not just "it's complicated," and I need it to take the "why fund something that's arguably not even its own species" side seriously instead of just explaining it away.
