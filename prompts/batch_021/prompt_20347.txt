After a long career in experimental physics, I have spent my retirement reading the philosophy of science I never had time for, starting with a stalemate I watched play out for decades in my own field: Copenhagen, Bohmian mechanics, and Many-Worlds, all still standing, all still compatible with every measurement anyone knows how to make. I now understand that 'the underdetermination of theory by evidence' is invoked as though it names a single argument, when it clearly does not. Please compare the classical Duhem-Quine holist argument for underdetermination with Kyle Stanford's argument from unconceived alternatives, developed in his book Exceeding Our Grasp, being precise about where their premises actually diverge. Which of the two, in your judgment, poses the more serious threat to scientific realism? Justify your answer.
