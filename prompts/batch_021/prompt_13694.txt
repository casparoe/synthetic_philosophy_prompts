A senior colleague of mine — I'll call him Marcus, since he'd disown me if this ever got forwarded to him — is retiring after nearly thirty years as the resident "Kelly guy" at the trading desk where I work. He's the reason most of the desk sizes positions as a fraction of bankroll rather than by naive expected value, a habit that apparently caused real arguments at his previous firm about whether he was just smuggling in log utility by the back door. About a decade ago, after (I'm fairly sure) falling into some LessWrong-adjacent corner of the internet, he decided Kelly betting was really just the sequential-gambling special case of something bigger he now calls "geometric rationality": the idea that a whole family of problems people solve by taking an arithmetic mean — splitting a windfall with a spouse, pooling two forecasters' probability estimates, dividing the gains in a negotiation — are quietly asking for a geometric mean instead, and that most "obviously rational" behavior in those settings is really arithmetic-mean thinking smuggled in where it doesn't belong. I've lurked on those same forums for over a decade myself, which is presumably why I've been handed the toast at his retirement dinner next month. Roughly half the room will be traders who will catch every reference to fractional Kelly and to time-average versus ensemble-average growth; the other half will be his wife, his kids, and old friends from outside finance, for whom none of that will mean anything on its own.

There's one further wrinkle. Marcus has said, more than once and not entirely joking, that the evening will be ruined for him if anyone frames tonight, or his career, as him having "won" or "come out ahead," since on his own view no single outcome is ever a win or a loss — only the shape of the long-run compounding trajectory is — so treating a retirement dinner as a triumphant final score is exactly the arithmetic-mean mistake he has spent his career warning the rest of us against. Could you write the toast: five to seven minutes, built around one genuine Kelly-betting or geometric-rationality joke or analogy solid enough that the traders in the room will recognize it as actually correct rather than dumbed down, but that still works as real warmth for everyone else, and that manages to celebrate him generously without ever quite letting the room call it a win?
