Random question from my quals reading – I work on philosophy of action, not metaethics, but I keep running into people talking about the "wrong kind of reasons problem" like its this special new puzzle for buck-passing accounts of value. Isn't it basically just Kant's shopkeeper case dressed up in new vocabulary? The shopkeeper has a reason to give correct change (to keep customers happy) that's totally disconnected from whether honesty is actually good, which seems like exactly a wrong-kind-of-reason situation to me. So why does everyone treat this as its own distinctive problem instead of just an old point about motives and moral worth with a fancier label?
