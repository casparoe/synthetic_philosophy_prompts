For a political philosophy elective at my high school, I have been working through the parts of Robert Nozick's *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* where he grounds individual rights in what he calls "side constraints"—the broadly Kantian idea that a person may never be sacrificed or used merely as a means to some greater aggregate benefit. Tucked into a brief, almost offhand aside, Nozick admits he is not fully satisfied that these constraints should stop at the edge of our species, yet he tentatively settles on something like a split: animal suffering matters and can be weighed against benefits in a roughly utilitarian way, while only beings with some unspecified rational capacity get the full, non-negotiable Kantian protection. The trouble is that the capacity he gestures at—something like being able to shape one's life according to an overall plan—seems to come in degrees, which makes a clean species line feel arbitrary even on his own terms.

Could you write me a short story, roughly 800 to 1200 words, that dramatizes this specific problem rather than animal ethics in general? I would like it built around one particular creature—real, hypothetical, or invented—whose place relative to Nozick's line is genuinely unsettled, and around a character who must act on some decision about which side it falls on without actually being sure. Please do not let the ending quietly resolve the puzzle; I would rather sit with the discomfort than be handed a tidy answer.
