My uncle Walt died last week at 84. He was an evolutionary biologist and a genuine crank about it -- a lifelong gene's-eye-view die-hard who'd literally pause nature documentaries to complain that the narrator just said something evolved "for the good of the species." I'm writing his eulogy for Saturday. A few of his old colleagues from the department will be there so it can't be flat-out wrong, but it's mostly cousins and grandkids, and everyone keeps sending me drafts full of exactly the lines he spent forty years arguing against -- "he gave everything of himself for the good of this family," "it's just human nature to look after our own kind," that sort of thing. Being the novelist in the family I'm apparently the one who's supposed to make it sound nice, but I don't want to either lecture forty crying people about inclusive fitness or put sentiments in his mouth he'd have dismantled at a dinner party. Can you write the actual eulogy -- genuinely warm, doesn't shy away from how much he adored his kids and grandkids, but grounds that devotion in whatever the real hard-nosed mechanism is (kin selection, inclusive fitness, whatever he'd actually have said) rather then the "good of the group" language he hated. Keep it to eulogy length, not an essay, and don't let it turn into a biology lecture.
