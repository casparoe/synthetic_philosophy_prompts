A friend and I have been working through Wittgenstein's *On Certainty* together for the past few weeks, and after our reading group last night we ended up in a nearly two-hour argument over coffee that neither of us really won. The sticking point was hinge propositions—things like "I have two hands" or "the earth has existed for a long time before I was born"—which Wittgenstein treats not as empirical claims that we know on the basis of evidence, but as the groundless hinges on which our whole practice of doubting, giving reasons, and being certain turns. My friend takes this to be a genuine dissolution of external-world skepticism: once you see that hinges are not the kind of thing that could be justified or unjustified in the first place, the skeptic's demand that we justify them is exposed as confused rather than simply unmet, rather like demanding proof that the rules of chess are true. I kept pressing the same objection all night: if hinges are not known, then strictly speaking we do not know we have hands, and relabeling this as "groundless trust" rather than "absence of knowledge" looks like conceding the skeptic's substantive point while quietly changing the subject. My friend replied that this objection smuggles back in exactly the evidentialist picture that Wittgenstein was trying to dismantle, and that Moore was simply asking the wrong kind of question when he claimed to know that he had a hand.

Neither of us managed to dislodge the other, so I would like to see the argument conducted properly by two people who have thought about it far more rigorously than we had that evening. Could you write a dialogue between a committed Wittgensteinian hinge epistemologist and an epistemologist sympathetic to Moore's common-sense response to skepticism, in which they try to work out whether hinge epistemology actually defeats radical external-world skepticism or merely relocates it under a more comfortable description? I would like the Moorean side to be genuinely difficult to satisfy: they should press on whether treating hinges as arational rather than justified is a stable position once one also wants to insist, with Moore, that we really do know we have hands, and on whether moves like Crispin Wright's idea of a non-evidential "entitlement" to hinges collapse back into the very evidentialist picture that hinge epistemology was supposed to avoid. I would like the Wittgensteinian side to have equally serious resources to draw on, including the distinction between hinges that are universal to all inquiry and hinges that are local to a particular practice or context, and the thought that not every acceptance must be either grounded in evidence or held arbitrarily. Please have the two characters respond to each other's specific moves as the conversation develops, rather than simply restating their opening positions, and feel free to let the exchange end in a sharply clarified disagreement rather than a tidy resolution.
