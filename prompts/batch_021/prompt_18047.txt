ok so i've fallen down a rabbit hole reading about "acausal trade" - the idea that two minds that can never causally touch each other in any way (different timelines, different bubbles of the multiverse, one of them might not even exist yet or might just be a simulation) can still strike a deal, because each one works out what the other would do and acts on that, kinda like superrationality but way more extreme since there's not even a shared history or a coin flip to correlate on, just pure "i know that you'd know that i'd know" all the way down. everyone always jumps straight to roko's basilisk when this comes up and honestly im kind of sick of that version, it's always a threat/blackmail story. i want the other kind, the cooperative kind - two agents with zero causal contact who each independently figure out they can spend a bit of their own resources on the other's goals, because they've both reasoned the other is rational enough to actually hold up its end, even though neither one can ever check up on the other or punish it after the fact if it doesn't. can you write me a short story built around that exact gap - the moment an agent has to go through with its half of a deal that literally can't be verified or enforced by anything, and has to decide for itself whether that's genuinely rational or just an elaborate way of fooling itself. dont give it a neat "and it worked out great" ending, that kind of certainty feels wrong for a topic this shaky.
