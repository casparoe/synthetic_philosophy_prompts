I'm a junior on my high school's election-reform committee, and we're debating whether to replace plurality voting for student body president with ranked-choice voting (RCV), after two popular candidates split the vote last year and handed the win to someone most students liked less. Two committee members, Maya and Diego, got into a real back-and-forth about what RCV would actually fix, and I'd like a considered opinion on their exchange rather than just a verdict on who won.

(1) Maya said RCV solves the problem outright: since a ballot moves to your next choice once your favorite is eliminated, nobody has to worry about "wasting" a vote or helping their least-favorite candidate win, so people can finally vote sincerely.

(2) Diego said that's too strong: instant-runoff counting can still reward insincere ballots, since a voter can sometimes get a better outcome by ranking their true favorite second, or by burying a strong rival at the bottom, rather than voting honestly.

(3) Maya answered that this only matters if a voter can predict how hundreds of classmates will vote, which is unrealistic in a school-wide election, and proposed sidestepping the issue with approval voting instead, where you check off every candidate you approve of and the most-checked one wins, since there's no ranking to game.

(4) Diego replied that approval voting isn't strategy-free either, since a voter still has to decide strategically where to draw the "approve" line. He added that this isn't a quirk of one method: any reasonably fair ranked system with three or more candidates can be manipulated in some scenario short of outright dictatorship, so the honest goal is minimizing manipulation, not eliminating it.

Please go through (1) through (4) and say, for each, whether the person is right, partly right, or mistaken, and why. I'm especially interested in whether Diego's closing claim is really as universal as he makes it sound, or whether a cleverly designed system could dodge it. End with one line, in exactly this format: FINAL VERDICT: <Maya or Diego>, whoever had the stronger overall position.
