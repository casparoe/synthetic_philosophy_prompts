Sometimes when I'm driving home late at night and I'm tired, a mailbox at the edge of the road will for a split second look exactly like a person standing there, before I blink and see it for what it actually is. While it lasts, the mistaken impression feels every bit as vivid and real as anything else I'm seeing through the windshield. A friend of mine argues that since I can't tell the false sighting apart from a genuine one while it's happening, seeing an actual person and being fooled like this must, at bottom, be the very same kind of experience, one that simply happens to get the facts right in one case and not the other. That strikes me as too quick a conclusion, though I can't quite say what's wrong with it. Is my friend's reasoning sound?
