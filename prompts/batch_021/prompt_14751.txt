And here's the student's answer in full:

(a) In the Sleeping Beauty problem, Beauty is told all the details of the experiment on Sunday and then put to sleep. A fair coin is tossed: if heads, she's woken once, on Monday, and the experiment ends; if tails, she's woken on Monday and again on Tuesday, with a memory-erasing drug given after the Monday waking so she can't tell the two awakenings apart. On each waking, what credence should she place in Heads? Halfers, following David Lewis, say 1/2. The coin is fair, and Beauty has always known she'd wake at least once no matter how it landed, so waking up gives her no new information bearing on the coin -- her credence should just stay at the coin's objective chance, 1/2. Thirders, following Adam Elga, say 1/3. Beauty can't distinguish, from the inside, between "Monday-and-tails" and "Tuesday-and-tails," so by a principle of indifference she should split her credence evenly between these; combined with the fact that conditional on its being Monday, heads and tails are equally likely, this forces an even 1/3-1/3-1/3 split across Heads-Monday, Tails-Monday and Tails-Tuesday, so credence in heads is 1/3.

(b) This maps onto Bostrom's two rules for anthropic reasoning. The Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) says an observer should reason as if randomly sampled from the set of all actually existing observers in her reference class -- it's about how you locate yourself once you already know which world is actual. The Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA), by contrast, tells you to weight entire hypotheses in proportion to how many observers-like-you they contain, since your own existence is more probable on hypotheses with bigger populations of observers. Applied to Sleeping Beauty, SSA says the tails-world, which contains twice as many Beauty-awakenings as the heads-world, should get double the weight -- which is exactly the reasoning that generates Elga's 1/3.

(c) One serious problem for thirding is the "Presumptuous Philosopher" case, originally due to Adam Elga: two cosmological theories are empirically tied except that T2 implies the universe contains far more observers than T1; if you weight hypotheses by how many observers-like-you they predict, a philosopher can declare T2 vastly more likely from the armchair, without looking through a telescope. Since the thirder answer rests on essentially the same move -- favoring "tails" partly because it entails more Beauty-observers -- the same charge of illegitimate armchair confidence threatens it too. A thirder could respond that Sleeping Beauty only reshuffles credence within a single, already-fixed run of the experiment, so it doesn't license the same kind of runaway inference about the external world that makes the Presumptuous Philosopher case so troubling.
