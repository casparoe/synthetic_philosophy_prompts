So I've been chewing on this since a tangent in an old LW thread about Yudkowsky's meta-honesty post, and I want to see it actually fought out properly instead of everyone just nodding at both sides like usually happens in those comment sections. There's this long running "norms of assertion" debate in epistemology about what actually licenses you to flatly assert something — that it's true, that you believe it, or that you know it (Williamson's the guy who made the knowledge norm famous: assert p only if you know p). The test case people always trot out against any norm stated purely in terms of credence/probability is the lottery one: you hold 1 ticket in a fair 1,000,000 ticket lottery, the draw already happened but hasn't been announced yet, so your credence that you lost should be like 0.999999 — and yet it still feels off to just flatly say "you lost," you have to hedge it. Now compare that to something like "my bike's still locked up outside," based on nothing but half remembering where you left it that morning, which, given how common bike theft actually is, arguably has a *worse* chance of being true then the lottery claim, and yet asserting that flatly feels completely fine, zero hedge needed. The puzzle is that no norm defined purely over your credence seems able to explain that asymmetry.

What I'd like is a dialogue between two committed Bayesians — the type who'd both sign onto "beliefs are probabilities, not binary states" without blinking — who nonetheless end up on opposite sides of this. One of them looks at the lottery/bike contrast and basically concedes Williamson's right, that assertion has to be tracking something knowledge-shaped (safety, or whatever) rather than raw credence. The other wants to bite the bullet and defend a straight credence-threshold norm — assert p iff your credence clears some contextually set bar, full stop — even for the lottery case, and actually has to do something with the contrast rather than wave it away: maybe cash the weirdness out as conversational implicature, maybe lean on resilience/stability of credence under new evidence, maybe something else. I want real back and forth here, not two people talking past each other or one side just folding — have them keep poking at each other's specific move and forcing refinements, the way it actually goes when both people know the literature cold.
