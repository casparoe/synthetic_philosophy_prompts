I have no academic background in philosophy, but over the past year I have taught myself a fair amount of aesthetics by working through Bernard Williams's posthumous essay collection *On Opera*, and I host a small podcast for opera lovers. Next month's guest is a philosopher who has just published a book defending and extending Williams's account of opera as an art form. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the book's thesis. I want at least one question on how his essay on Don Giovanni can claim that the music makes Giovanni's predatory vitality vivid, even seductive, without the opera thereby endorsing him -- especially given what happens to Zerlina and Donna Anna; one on his claim that opera's artificial, sung mode of address is precisely what lets it handle seduction and betrayal more directly than spoken drama can, when through-composed verismo, and even Bizet's originally spoken-dialogue Carmen, seem to achieve comparable directness without that artifice; and one on whether this writing on opera is genuinely continuous with Williams's attack on a single "morality system" and his insistence on irresolvable tragic conflict, or whether it is really a separate enthusiasm with no deep tie to that philosophical program. For each, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and end with a question asking what kind of operatic case would actually count as evidence against Williams's picture, rather than simply serving as one more illustration of it.
