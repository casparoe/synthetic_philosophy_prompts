Working on a piece about stadium concerts and "immersive theatre" and I keep coming back to Rousseau's weird beef with the stage in the Letter to d'Alembert. He's totally fine with public festivals -- gather the whole town in the square, no stage, no actor/audience split, everyone's simultaneously performer and spectator -- but he thinks actual theatre (professional actors performing a scripted illusion for a seated, passive crowd) rots a republic, partly because it trains citizens to admire virtue from a distance instead of practicing it themselves. Can you give me one modern example of an event or format that would genuinely count as a "fête" in his sense, and one that looks similar on the surface but is really just "theatre" in his condemned sense -- and explain exactly which features earn each one that verdict, by his own criteria?
