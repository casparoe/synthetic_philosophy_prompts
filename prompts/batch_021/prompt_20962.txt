About three weeks ago, my house was broken into, and the aftermath has left me stuck on a question I can't quite resolve. My roommate, Daniel, and I share a small rental house, and months ago we agreed that whoever leaves last in the evening is responsible for locking the front door, since the lock doesn't engage automatically and there had been a string of break-ins in the neighborhood. On the night in question, Daniel left last and simply forgot to lock it. Someone walked in a few hours later and took my laptop, a bike, and a few pieces of jewelry that had belonged to my grandmother.

Daniel feels terrible about it, and we've already settled the practical side of things (he's covering the cost of the laptop). But when I told him, in the heat of the moment, that this was basically his fault, he pushed back in a way that has stuck with me since. His argument was that he didn't do anything to anyone: he didn't let a stranger in, didn't prop the door open, didn't advertise that the house was empty — he just failed to perform an action he had agreed to perform. He also pointed out, correctly, that the lock on our front door is an old, cheap deadbolt that a moderately determined person could probably have forced or picked regardless, so he isn't even convinced that remembering would have changed anything. In his view, there's a real difference between causing a break-in and merely failing to prevent one, and he thinks I'm holding him responsible for the burglar's actions rather than his own.

I've stopped being angry about it, but I can't let go of the underlying question, and it turned into a whole debate over dinner with friends a few nights ago, without much resolution. On one hand, it seems obviously relevant that if Daniel had simply done the one thing he'd agreed to do, this particular break-in, on this particular night, almost certainly wouldn't have happened — that has to count for something. On the other hand, "forgetting to lock a door" doesn't intuitively feel like the kind of thing that causes a robbery, the way actively letting someone in would; it feels more like it merely allowed something else — the burglar's own decision — to do the causing. And the business with the cheap lock complicates things further, since I'm not even sure the outcome depended on what Daniel did. I'd like a clearer way of thinking through this than my friends and I managed: did Daniel's forgetting actually cause the loss of my things, or only fail to prevent it, and is there a principled basis for deciding how much responsibility someone in his position should bear?
