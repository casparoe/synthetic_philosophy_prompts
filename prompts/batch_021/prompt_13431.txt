Over the past few years, my father has drifted into a media ecosystem that treats nearly every mainstream institution—journalism, medicine, higher education—as fundamentally compromised, and no amount of patient, evidence-based conversation on my part has made even a dent. Thanksgiving before last ended with him leaving the table; this past year I resolved to just listen instead of arguing, which kept the peace but left me feeling like I'd conceded something I shouldn't have. My sister, who is midway through a philosophy PhD, listened to me vent about all this and told me, not entirely gently, that I was making a category mistake: I kept treating his core commitments as ordinary empirical beliefs that a sufficiently good article or fact-check could dislodge, when they function more like what Wittgenstein calls "hinge propositions" in On Certainty.

I've since read most of that book, or at least the parts of it a non-philosopher can follow, and I think I have the basic shape of the idea: some propositions aren't held on the basis of evidence at all, because they're what make gathering and weighing evidence possible in the first place, the way a door needs its hinges to stay fixed before it can swing at all. I was struck by the passage where he compares these commitments to a riverbed that can shift, but only slowly, and never by being argued with directly; I was struck even more by a fairly bleak stretch near the end of the book where he seems to concede that once two people's hinges genuinely conflict, "giving grounds" runs out and what's left is something closer to persuasion or conversion than argument—he even reaches, a little uncomfortably, for the analogy of missionaries converting native populations.

Please describe the most important implications of hinge epistemology, as Wittgenstein develops it in On Certainty, for how I should actually handle this situation with my father going forward. In particular, I'd like you to address whether the framework implies that continuing to argue with him is a kind of category error rather than just an uphill battle; whether it cuts both ways, so that my own trust in the institutions and experts he's abandoned is just as much a hinge commitment as his distrust of them is, leaving the two of us in a genuinely symmetrical standoff rather than one of us simply being wrong; and whether the riverbed image gives any real grounds for hope that his hinges could shift over time through something other than confrontation.
