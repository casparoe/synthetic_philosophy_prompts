Two-dimensional semantics is often taught as though it were a single, unified framework, with Robert Stalnaker and David Chalmers cited in the same breath as its architects; in fact, the two put the two-dimensional matrix to markedly different philosophical work. Please compare Stalnaker's diagonalization, developed out of his account of assertion and common ground under contextual uncertainty, with Chalmers's epistemic two-dimensionalism, on which primary intensions are meant to track an autonomous space of a priori-accessible epistemic possibilities. Address, in particular, whether each theorist's apparatus is well suited to underwriting modal rationalism—the inference from ideal conceivability to metaphysical possibility—and use the necessary a posteriori status of "water is H2O" to illustrate the contrast. Whose version of two-dimensionalism does more philosophical work while incurring fewer metaphysical commitments, and why?
