I've been doing alot of work lately moving some of our backend services from mutating rows in place to an immutable, event-sourced model, and a coworker joked that this is basically the same fight philosophers have over "endurantism vs perdurantism." Never heard of either term, fell down a rabbit hole, now I can't stop thinking about it. As I understand it: endurantists say a persisting thing is the same whole object, wholly present, at every moment it exists — it just has different properties at different times — while perdurantists say a persisting thing is really more like a bunch of time-slices stitched together, and "the object" just IS that whole series, with no slice being any more "it" than another.

Can you write me a dialogue between a sharp endurantist and a sharp perdurantist hashing this out? Have the perdurantist open with the puzzle of how one and the same thing can have flat out incompatible properties at different times without that being a contradiction — skip the classic bent poker / green banana examples though, come up with a fresher case. I don't want the endurantist to just fold either: I gather there's more than one move available to them, something about indexing properties to times instead of treating them as flatly true or false, versus making the having of a property itself a relation to a time — so make the endurantist actually use those and force the perdurantist to find the real weak spot in each. Then flip it: have the endurantist press the perdurantist on whether anything really changes or persists on their view at all, versus there just being a static heap of disconnected slices sitting there. Keep it a genuine back and forth, not one side quietly winning by the end.
