I've been a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, but a recent disagreement with my partner has left me stuck on a question I can't resolve alone. For months I've told her, and myself, that I want to leave work by six and be home for dinner, yet when six arrives I almost always stay late "to finish one more thing," and an hour later I regret it every time. She argues, in fairly economic terms, that whichever version of me makes the six o'clock choice is the one whose preference counts: that repeated choice, night after night, is the only honest evidence of what I want. The version of me who shows up later regretting it, she says, doesn't get a veto just for complaining loudest. Is she right that my preferences are simply whatever my choices consistently reveal, or can a person's own choices fail to track what they want, or what's good for them, at all?
