I've been asked to give the opening remarks — five minutes, no more — at a small retreat for people who work on s-risk reduction: the effort to prevent worst-case, astronomically bad suffering outcomes in the far future. I've followed rationalist and EA writing for long enough to know exactly how fraught this crowd is, but I've never had to write for it myself. Of the dozen or so attendees, a third are old hands who've been doing this work for the better part of a decade and are, frankly, a little burnt out; the rest are newer and still earnest. There's a fault line running through the room that I can't paper over: several of the veterans are committed negative utilitarians who hold that suffering has lexical priority over everything else and think the group's priorities should follow from that without apology, while others are classical-utilitarian longtermists who treat s-risk as one grim tail alongside extinction risk and would bristle at remarks that quietly assume the stronger view is correct. I want to open the day honestly, without minimizing how bad the scenarios we study actually are, but also without taking sides in that dispute or leaving the room too demoralized to work for the rest of the day. Could you draft the remarks?
