So a friend of mine and I got into it the other night about whether identical twins are ever truly "the same" in some deep sense, and I ended up bringing up Max Black's old two-spheres thought experiment to make a point, and now I can't stop thinking it'd make a good short story premise. The setup (Black wrote it up as a dialogue back in 1952, its kind of fun to read actually) is: imagine a universe that contains nothing at all except two solid spheres, same size, same material, same age, hanging in space some fixed distance apart, with nothing else around to break the symmetry. Every property one has, the other has too — even the relational ones, since the whole setup is perfectly symmetric (whatever's true of sphere A's distance/relation to the other sphere is equally true of B's relation to A). Black's whole point was to needle Leibniz's principle that no two distinct things can be complete qualitative duplicates of each other — here you supposedly have two things that share literally every property except numerical identity itself. There's also a rejoinder I vaguely remember (Hacking?) that if you set the same scenario in curved/elliptical space instead of flat space, the "two" spheres could just be one sphere you're running into twice from opposite directions around the curve of the universe, which messes with the thought experiment even more.

Anyway — could you write me a short story built around this? I don't need Black or Leibniz mentioned by name or the philosophy explained out loud, I'd rather the premise just quietly drive the plot. I'm picturing something told from inside the situation, like from the POV of a mind that is one of the two spheres (or that inhabits one), slowly figuring out there's something else out there identical to it in every respect it can check, and having to sit with the fact that it can't name a single fact about itself that makes it "this one" and not "that one" — while still somehow just knowing, from the inside, that it is. Toss in the curved-space wrinkle too if it's useful (maybe it's ambiguous the whole time whether there are really two of them). Keep it shortish — flash fiction to short story length — and it doesn't need a neat ending, I'm mostly curious whether the weirdness of the idea can actually land as a feeling and not just a puzzle.
