So I got nerd-sniped this week re-reading about the absent-minded driver problem -- the Piccione & Rubinstein highway example where there are two identical intersections, exiting at the first one pays 0, exiting at the second pays 4, and sailing past both nets you 1. I brought it up with my rationalist reading group over dinner and we spent an hour arguing about it without landing anywhere, which is probably on me for bringing up decision theory during dessert. Here's the part that bugs me: the plan you'd commit to in advance is "continue with probability 2/3," which is what maximizes it's expected payoff at 4/3 -- but the second you're actually standing at an intersection, unable to tell whether it's the first one or the second, and you try to update on the mere fact that you've been asked to decide at all, it starts to feel like you should second-guess that 2/3 and do something else instead. Aumann, Hart and Perry wrote a whole reply to Piccione and Rubinstein arguing this isn't a real inconsistency once you carefully distinguish what they call "planning-optimal" from "action-optimal" strategies, but half my reading group thinks that distinction just relabels the paradox rather than dissolving it. Someone else pointed out that this is basically a decision-theoretic cousin of Sleeping Beauty, and that it gets invoked a lot in arguments for updateless decision theory over causal or evidential decision theory, since the driver seems to get into trouble precisely by updating on where they are.

Could you write me an essay on this that actually picks a side instead of doing the diplomatic "reasonable people disagree" thing? Walk through why naive updating-at-the-node seems to pull against the ex-ante optimum, engage seriously with whether the Aumann-Hart-Perry planning/action distinction genuinely resolves the tension or just renames it, and take a stance on whether this example is best read as evidence for something like updateless decision theory once recall is imperfect. I want an actual verdict at the end -- what the driver should do, and whether "updating" even makes sense as a description of what's happening to them inside the problem -- not a shrug.
