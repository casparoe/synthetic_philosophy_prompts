I've been auditing a graduate seminar on self-knowledge this term, and after last week's session on Timothy Williamson's anti-luminosity argument, a friend from the class and I ended up debating it over dinner for the better part of two hours without reaching any resolution. The argument, as I understand it, runs roughly as follows: no non-trivial condition is "luminous" — that is, no condition is such that whenever you're in it, you're thereby in a position to know that you're in it — because our discriminatory powers are always coarse-grained. Consider sitting in a room as the temperature drifts downward by imperceptible degrees from warm to freezing: at some point you really are cold, yet you don't yet know it, because the belief you would form at that point is indistinguishable from the belief you would form at an adjacent moment when it would still be false, so the belief isn't safe, and unsafe belief doesn't amount to knowledge. My friend thought this was basically decisive, and that the same reasoning generalizes to sink the KK principle too (the thesis that knowing that P puts one in a position to know that one knows it), since "knowing that P" is itself just another condition subject to the same coarse-grained recognition. I argued that the case for luminosity is stronger than this makes it look: that the argument illegitimately imports a margin-for-error requirement suited to perceiving a world-involving, continuously varying quantity like temperature into the very different territory of occurrent phenomenal awareness, and that an internalist about justification has principled resources to resist the whole setup. Neither of us budged, and afterward I felt that neither of us had really done justice to the other's strongest moves.

I'd like your help seeing the disagreement more clearly by staging it properly. Please write a dialogue between two contemporary epistemologists of equal sophistication — one a defender of luminosity and of a suitably restricted KK principle, the other a committed Williamsonian anti-luminosity theorist — who work through the gradually-cooling-room scenario as their shared example. Each should respond directly to the other's actual arguments rather than talking past them; in particular, I would like the Williamsonian to have to answer the objection that purely phenomenal seemings, as opposed to judgments about external, continuously varying quantities, might be a genuine exception to the argument, and I would like the luminosity-defender to have to answer whether jettisoning the safety condition on knowledge doesn't cost more than it saves elsewhere in epistemology. The dialogue need not end in agreement, but it should end with the two of them having isolated, as precisely as possible, exactly what is doing the work in their disagreement.
