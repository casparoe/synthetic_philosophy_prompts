I'm part of my school's Ethics Bowl team, and a disagreement came up during practice this week that I still haven't been able to shake, so I wanted an outside opinion. Back in the fall, my teammate Priya spent an entire weekend helping me finish a robotics project I was completely absorbed in at the time, even though she has no interest in robotics herself. In exchange, I agreed to help her canvass door-to-door most Saturdays this spring for a city council candidate she cares about a great deal, since at the time I was genuinely curious about local politics too. Two months in, though, that curiosity has completely dried up: I don't care who wins the race, I get nothing out of standing on porches in the cold, and if I'm honest, I don't feel even a flicker of guilt at the thought of texting Priya to cancel for good.

When I brought this up at practice, one of my teammates argued that I don't actually have any reason left to keep showing up, since the only things that ever gave me a reason to canvass were my old interest in politics and my old wish to stay close with Priya — and now that both have faded, there is simply nothing left for the commitment to be "for." On his view, whatever sense of obligation I still feel toward Priya is just habit or leftover guilt dressed up as a reason, not an actual one, since a reason has to be anchored in something I currently want, and none of the relevant wants survive. That conclusion feels wrong to me, especially considering how much Priya helped me, but during the debate I couldn't pin down exactly where his reasoning breaks down. Is he right that my reason to help her disappeared the moment my desire to do so did, or is there some sense in which I still have a genuine reason to follow through, even though nothing I currently want or care about is served by it?
