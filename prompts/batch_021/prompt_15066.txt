I've been reading around moral luck to get the guilt right and jotted down some notes toward an essay on it, wanted your eyes on it before i try to turn it into something longer.

My angle: everyone always cites Williams' agent-regret as this special emotion that's supposedly rational even though the unlucky driver didn't do anything wrong that the lucky one didn't also do. But I think agent-regret only really makes sense from INSIDE a story — once an event has happened it retroactively organizes the causal chain into "the thing that led to this," and the driver's guilt is really just them being unable to stop reading their own life as a plot with that death as its ending. The twitch of the wheel becomes "the cause," even though nagel's whole point is that from outside, before the fact, it was just one more meaningless twitch identical to a thousand others that came to nothing.

So my actual claim is that nagel's "problem" of moral luck (the bit where it conflicts with the control principle) isn't really a paradox about morality at all, its a byproduct of us being creatures who can't help emplotting our own lives after the fact, and williams basically already saw this but didn't put it in those terms.

Does this hold up as a reading of williams and nagel, or am I mangling agent-regret?
