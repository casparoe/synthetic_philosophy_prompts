I'm a novelist and I sit on the board of a smallish literary nonprofit that runs an annual indie fiction prize. Alongside the jury shortlist there's always been a 'Readers' Choice' pick decided by open online voting, and this year it went sideways: a coordinated campaign stuffed the ballot for one author's book, and a different shortlisted memoir got dogpiled by a wave of anonymous accounts who obviously hadn't read it. The board's split -- half want to scrap the readers vote as unworkable, half think cutting it is elitist and undemocratic. I've been rereading Kierkegaard's stuff on 'the public' and the press lately (the leveling material in Two Ages / The Present Age, whatever the official title is), and it feels wierdly on the nose -- his point was never just that individual voters are dumb, its about what 'the public' even is, as opposed to actual readers relating to actual books. Can you draft the memo I'll bring to Tuesday's meeting: one clear recommendation (keep, cut, or reform the vote, in time for next year's prize) plus reasoning that actually engages the best case for an open public vote, rather than just waving it off as mob rule.
