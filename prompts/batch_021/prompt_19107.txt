For a seminar paper on contractarian ethics, I have been reading David Gauthier's Morals by Agreement alongside the axiomatic bargaining theory it draws on, and I keep running into what looks like more than a family resemblance. Gauthier argues that rational bargainers converge on the principle of "minimax relative concession": among the feasible, Pareto-optimal divisions of a cooperative surplus, they settle on the one that minimizes the largest concession any party makes, where a party's concession at a given outcome is the fraction of the distance between her disagreement payoff and her best feasible ("claim") payoff that she gives up in accepting it. Set out that way, the principle looks very close to the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution to the two-person bargaining problem, which is standardly presented not as a moral principle at all but as a technical rival to Nash's bargaining solution, justified purely by an appeal to a monotonicity axiom that Nash's solution violates.

I'd like you to compare the two properly rather than just note the resemblance. First, show as precisely as you can whether minimizing the maximum relative concession really does pick out the same point, in the two-person case, as the Kalai–Smorodinsky construction, and flag any place where Gauthier's informal notion of a "claim" and the bargaining theorists' "ideal point" might fail to coincide. Second, make something of the contrast with Nash's solution: Gauthier defends his principle over alternatives like Nash's on the intuitively moral ground that no bargainer should have to make a proportionally larger sacrifice than anyone else, whereas Kalai and Smorodinsky arrive at what appears to be the very same solution purely on the formal ground that it satisfies monotonicity. What should we make of the same mathematical object having both an apparently moral defense and an entirely non-moral one?

Finally, I want an actual verdict rather than a survey of considerations. Does the existence of an independent, purely mathematical axiomatization of "minimax relative concession" give Gauthier's attempt to derive morality from rational self-interest any support it would not otherwise have, or does it instead sharpen the standard complaint that bargaining-based contractarianism simply formalizes whatever distribution of bargaining power the parties bring to the table and dresses it up as impartial? Please defend one answer.
