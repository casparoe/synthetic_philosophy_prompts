At my company we use ranked-choice voting for stuff like picking next quarter's roadmap feature or the offsite location - everyone ranks the options, then we run instant-runoff (eliminate the lowest vote-getter, redistribute their ballots, repeat). Last round a teammate admitted afterward that he'd ranked his actual second choice as #1, since he knew his real favorite had zero shot and wanted to make sure it beat the other frontrunner - and it worked, flipping the outcome for the rest of us.

Now someone wants to switch to Borda count (points based on where you rank each option) since it uses more info and seems "fairer," but my gut says that just moves the gaming somewhere else instead of fixing it. I vaguely remember some theorem from a college stats elective - Arrow's maybe? - about no voting system being "perfect," but I thought that was about weird paradoxes in how you combine everyone's rankings, not people being able to flat out lie about their preferences to get a better result. Is that the same theorem or a different issue? Bottom line: is there an actual ranking method thats immune to this kind of dishonest voting, or are we just picking our poison no matter what we switch to?
