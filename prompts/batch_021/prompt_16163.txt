quick one for the science-and-ideas newsletter i write. i'm doing a piece on Kripke's rigid designators, and everyone explains them with the same two examples -- Hesperus/Phosphorus and water=H2O -- which i think just lets people (and models) recite the conclusion without ever really grappling with the argument. so can you write me a dialogue set around a case that isn't already worn smooth: jade. jadeite and nephrite are two chemically and structurally distinct minerals that both got called 'jade' for basically all of human history, until 19th-century mineralogists figured out they weren't the same stuff.

i want two philosophers of language arguing about what the jade case does to the necessary a posteriori: one who thinks jade shows the Kripke/Putnam picture -- natural-kind terms rigidly designate a hidden essence, so 'water is H2O'-style necessities are the norm -- survives just fine once you're careful about what 'jade' actually refers to, and one who thinks jade is a genuine counterexample: that ordinary natural-kind terms are more like clusters of surface properties, and the tidy necessary-a-posteriori story doesn't generalize the way Kripke and Putnam wanted. neither should be a pushover -- i want them actually engaging with each others strongest move, back-and-forth, not two speeches stapled together. somewhere around 8-12 exchanges, and no need to explain who Kripke is or ease into it, just dive straight into the actual arguement.
