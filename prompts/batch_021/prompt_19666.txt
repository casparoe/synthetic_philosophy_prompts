A friend of mine recently applied for life insurance and ran into difficulty on the question of whether he counts as a smoker: he has an occasional cigar three or four times a year, and when he pressed the insurer for a clear answer, he was told only that it depends on "how much and how often" — which became the occasion for a long argument between us. I argued that if the category genuinely admits of borderline cases like his, then there simply is no fact of the matter about whether he qualifies, and the insurer is mistaken to assume that some precise cutoff exists in the first place. My friend disagreed, insisting that there must be an exact fact about whether he counts, even if no one, including the underwriters, can ever know exactly where it falls. Neither of us persuaded the other. Whose position is better supported, and does it actually make any difference to what my friend should write on the form?
