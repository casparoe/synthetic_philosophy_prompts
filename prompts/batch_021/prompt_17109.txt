I've served two years on my city council's rules and ethics committee. I'm an accountant by trade, with no background in law or philosophy, but I've spent a fair amount of time reading up on this on my own since we had a real blowup over it. During debate on a nondiscrimination ordinance, one member said only that "the Bible is clear on this" and voted no; when a colleague asked her to explain further, she replied that this was reason enough and she had nothing to add. Half the committee wants a standing rule that anyone invoking a religious or other deeply held conviction on the floor must also offer a reason that a colleague who doesn't share it could still accept as grounds for the vote; the other half says that rule is neutral in name only, since it tells religious colleagues their real grounds for voting don't count unless repackaged in someone else's terms. I don't think this is a First Amendment question — no one is being made to pray, and no one's belief is being penalized — it's about what members can fairly ask of each other as reasons for a vote. Please draft the memo I'll bring to our next meeting: recommend a specific rule or recommend against one, say how it would have applied to last month's incident, and take on the second objection directly rather than treating it as obviously mistaken.
