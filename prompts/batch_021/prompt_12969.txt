So a few of us have this little friend-group side thing where we put small amounts of actual cash on a prediction market app for dumb stuff—local rec league games, who's gonna be late to trivia night, whether our office picnic gets rained out, that kind of thing. Last week my friend Priya, who does actuarial work and is way more careful with numbers than the rest of us, sat down with my full betting history across like six different overlapping questions and worked out that the specific combination of positions I'd locked in was set up so I'd loose money no matter which outcomes actually happened. Not just probably lose, guaranteed, every single combination of results ends with me down money. She says this proves my underlying beliefs about all this stuff were flat out contradicting each other, not just a run of bad luck, and that a genuinely rational person would never end up boxed in like that in the first place.

Something about her conclusion is bugging me though. Sure, in hindsight the contradiction is right there in black and white once you write out all the numbers side by side, but it took her a solid twenty minutes with a spreadsheet to actually dig it out across those six bets, its not like I could've just noticed it in my head while clicking through the app one bet at a time over a few days. Nobody manually cross checks every possible combination of every position they hold before confirming each new one. So is she right that failing to spot a hidden inconsistency the moment you create it makes you irrational in some deep sense, or is "irrational" just the wrong word for something that's really more about the limits on how much math a person can run in their head at once? I feel like there's an actual open question buried in here about what we're holding people to when we call beliefs "rational" in the first place, and I'd like to actually get my head around it properly instead of just conceding the argument because Priya's better with spreadsheets than I am.
