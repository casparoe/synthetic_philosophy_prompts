I'm sketching a scene for a novel in which my protagonist -- a rare-manuscripts dealer who has spent half his life absorbed in Maimonides -- is cornered at a Jerusalem dinner party by a young volunteer from the Temple Institute, the organization that sews priestly garments, breeds red heifers, and rehearses the sacrificial rites against the day the Temple Mount becomes available again. Half joking, she asks him whether the Rambam would have volunteered alongside her. My protagonist can't dodge the question, and I'd rather not either: Maimonides treats the sacrificial cult in the Guide as a concession to a people not yet capable of pure, non-physical worship, yet the Mishneh Torah lays out that same cult, in granular legal detail, as something a restored Temple remains obligated to resume, and he was, in his own lifetime, notably unsentimental toward premature messianic enthusiasm. Working from what he actually wrote rather than a generic "religion evolves toward spirituality" gloss, what do you think Maimonides would really have said to her?
