Two months ago my sister walked away from a car accident that, by any reasonable estimate, should have killed her, and I have spent the weeks since compulsively re-running the counterfactuals -- what if she'd left the house five minutes later, what if the other driver had glanced at his phone a second sooner. That habit is what pulled me into reading about Molinism, since the whole doctrine rests on the claim that God has settled knowledge of exactly these "would have" truths prior to any decision to create anything at all. I host a small podcast on philosophy of religion, and next month's guest is a philosopher who defends the Molinist account of middle knowledge. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the theory of scientia media for listeners.

I want at least one question on the grounding objection -- what, if anything, makes a counterfactual of creaturely freedom true, given that it must be settled prior to both the divine decree and the creature's own existence -- together with a follow-up keyed to whichever way she resolves it. I want at least one question on whether appealing to a menu of "feasible worlds" really affords God any more providential control than simple foreknowledge would, since which counterfactuals happen to be true isn't itself up to Him, with a follow-up that presses the worst-case version of that worry. And I want one question that comes back to my own situation: if God's middle knowledge of how I would have prayed was already accounted for in whichever world He actualized, what work is left for the praying itself to do?
