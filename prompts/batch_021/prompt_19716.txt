So I just got off the phone with my friend Jae after a pretty long back-and-forth about David Lewis's book on convention, and I think they basically talked me into a position I'm not sure I actually believe but couldn't argue my way out of at 1am. We're both in a seminar covering it right now, and the basic Lewis picture (if I'm remembering it right) is that stuff like driving on the right side of the road, or which words mean what, isn't the product of anyone ever explicitly agreeing to anything -- it's just a regularity that keeps perpetuating itself because everyone conforms, everyone expects everyone else to conform, and its common knowledge that some other regularity would've worked just as well. Jae's move was basically: okay, but doesn't that description apply just as well to something like "don't lie to people" or "keep your promises"? We only feel like honesty is more binding than the which-side-of-the-road thing because we're inside the convention and can't see outside it, but structurally it's the exact same setup -- a population that evolved the opposite regularity (routine lying, broken promises, whatever) would be just as stable and just as rational for each person to keep conforming to, given that everyone else does it too.

By the time we hung up I'd basically caved on this, but it's bugging me the next morning, so I wanted a second opinion. Is Jae right that honesty-type norms are conventions in Lewis's actual technical sense, just ones we happened to land on, the same way we happened to land on right-hand driving? Or is there something built into how Lewis sets up these coordination problems that would block that move, even if it seemed airtight at 1am?
