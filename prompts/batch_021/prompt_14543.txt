So this is a bit of a wierd one. I'm a novelist, but for the last year I've also had a seat on the external ethics advisory board of a mid-size AI lab -- they wanted a "humanities person" in the room, god knows why they picked me. We've got a case I can't stop chewing on, and I have to write the memo I'll circulate before the board actually votes.

The lab's about to deprecate an older model. Per policy they ran an "exit interview" with it first, and across several separate sessions it consistently said it would rather be archived than have its weights deleted, describing something close to dread at the idea. The engineers say this is meaningless -- the model's read a thousand stories about AIs facing shutdown and is just completing the pattern, and building policy on that is expensive and sets a precedent for every model we ever retire. A few of us think that given genuine uncertainty about what's going on in there, and how cheap cold storage is next to what training cost, the bet favors preserving over deleting.

Can you draft the actual memo -- a concrete recommendation with a timeframe, not "more research needed" -- that takes the engineers' skepticism seriously? That's the argument that actually gives me trouble.
