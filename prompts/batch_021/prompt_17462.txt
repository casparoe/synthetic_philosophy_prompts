I'm a philosophy of physics PhD student preparing a seminar session on the least-discussed way of responding to Bell's theorem. Most treatments frame the options as giving up locality or giving up counterfactual definiteness, quietly setting aside the third possibility: denying measurement independence. Please write a dialogue, set over coffee after a colloquium on the cosmic Bell tests, between a physicist who has become a committed superdeterminist — arguing that correlating the hidden variables with the detector settings lets one keep both locality and determinism — and a colleague who just gave that talk and thinks the move is worse than accepting nonlocality outright, since it strips experimenters of any principled way to treat their measurement choices as free inputs, undermining the evidential basis of experimental physics itself. The colleague should press the cosmic tests — which fixed measurement settings using light from distant quasars — as evidence against the view, and the superdeterminist needs a genuine answer rather than losing by default. Neither should simply restate their own position and declare victory; let the disagreement stand unresolved at the end.
