I chair my philosophy department's search committee for a tenure-track position, and we have just finished campus visits for our two finalists. By long-standing custom, the seven of us announce our verdicts aloud in turn, with the most senior members speaking first. Looking back over our last three searches, I have noticed a pattern: once the first two or three senior members converge on a candidate, every vote that follows agrees with them, however mixed people's private impressions of the visit seemed beforehand. No one is being timid or falling into line — each subsequent vote is plausibly a rational inference from the votes already declared, which is exactly why it is hard to say anyone is doing anything wrong, and exactly why I doubt we are pooling seven independent judgments rather than one or two. We reconvene this Friday to decide between the two finalists. Please draft the short memo I'll circulate to the committee beforehand: it should recommend one specific change to how we take that vote, not a general exhortation to greater independence, and it should take seriously the colleague who will insist that speaking in turn, most senior first, is simply what letting real deliberation happen looks like.
