Ok, this is a dumb example but its been bugging me more than it has any right to. I want to run it by someone who actually thinks about this stuff for a living, even though it's honestly not my corner of philosophy (I mostly work on ethics and political philosophy). Six of us have run the same fantasy football league since grad school, so eleven years now, and pretty early on we agreed on a rule for breaking ties in the final standings. It's only ever come up twice before, and both times the two "obvious" ways of extending our original rule happened to give the same answer, so nobody ever noticed it could be read more than one way.

This year we got a three-way tie with a genuine wrinkle nobody had seen before (don't worry about the specifics, it involves head-to-head record plus a game that got rescheduled for weather), and it turns out this is exactly the kind of case where the two most natural extensions of the old rule disagree with each other. Two guys are dead certain it obviously works one way, two others are equally certain it obviously works the other way, and everybody is very confident this is "what we always meant" -- even though, as far as I can tell, all that ever existed in writing is one paragraph in a group text from 2013 that just doesn't decide the new case either way. There's $600 in the pot plus a whole year of bragging rights riding on this, so nobodys being especially chill about it.

I tried playing peacemaker by asking everyone what we "really" intended back in 2013, but that just produces more confident, contradictory answers -- everyone's memory conveniently lines up with whichever reading saves their own team. Someone suggested asking what "a reasonable commissioner" would rule, but we've never had one, just six equally invested guys arguing in a group chat. What I can't shake is a bigger question sitting underneath the silly fantasy-football one: is there actually some fact about what we collectively meant in 2013 -- one that better evidence (clearer memories, some old email we forgot about, whatever) could in principle turn up, even if none of us can produce it right now? Or is the whole question somehow confused, such that there was never really a fact of that kind to begin with? And if it's the second one, does that mean whatever we decide now is just arbitrary, or is there still some principled way to settle it that doesn't just amount to picking a winner and calling it a ruling?
