So I've been going through Frederick Douglass's old speeches on my own lately, not just the famous July 4th one everyone quotes. What suprised me is that he didnt always think the Constitution was secretly anti-slavery - for a good while he was firmly on Garrison's side, that the thing was rotten and pro-slavery to the core and decent people shouldnt even vote under it. Then he broke from Garrison and totally flipped, arguing that if you just read the actual words on the page instead of guessing at what the framers privately intended (which was often pretty pro-slavery, lets be honest), theres nothing in the text itself that requires slavery. So basically he picked text-over-intent, and he picked it specifically because the framers' intent was the problem. That feels like the exact fight "originalism" is having right now, except modern originalism usually means going WITH the original intent/understanding of the framers, not against it. If Douglass saw how originalism actually works today, do you reckon he'd be for it or dead against it? Don't just tell me "he'd have supported civil rights," I want your real take on the interpretive question, not the moral one.
