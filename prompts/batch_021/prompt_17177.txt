been going down a Kepler rabbit hole for my newsletter and wrote up the core of the argument below to sanity-check before I build the rest of the piece around it. Its rougher than what I'd publish - more like notes with full sentences - but the claim is real and I want it stress-tested, not complimented.

Everyone knows the trivia that Kepler cast horoscopes for money while doing "real" astronomy on the side. Less appreciated: he spent decades running an internal audit of astrology, not rejecting it and not swallowing it whole, but asking which parts could survive being held to the same standards he used everywhere else in his physics.

By his own account, in a 1606 letter to Thomas Harriot, he'd decided a decade earlier to throw out the zodiac signs, the houses, the planetary rulerships, the triplicities - basically the whole bookkeeping apparatus of ordinary horoscope-casting - as arbitrary conventions with no basis in nature. What he kept were the aspects, the angular relationships between planets (conjunction, trine, square, etc), because he thought those were doing real causal work. Tertius Interveniens (1610) - written as a warning to the physician Feselius and others not to throw the baby out with the bathwater while they scrapped "star-gazing superstition" - and later book 4 of Harmonices Mundi (1619), are both attempts to explain why the aspects specifically, and nothing else, should count as real.

His answer plugs the aspects into the same geometric theory that grounds musical consonance and the nested Platonic solids elsewhere in his cosmology: an angle only counts as a genuine aspect if it corresponds to a regular polygon that's constructible with straightedge and compass (iirc this is why he'll defend the quintile, 72 degrees, off the pentagon, but there's no aspect off the heptagon - happy to be corrected if I'm misremembering that part). He even added a few new aspects of his own, quintile/biquintile/sesquiquadrate, partly on the strength of years of his own weather diaries. And critically, the causal mechanism isn't rays or occult sympathy - he grounds it in a "soul" (anima), of the earth for weather effects, and of the individual person, imprinted with the sky's geometric layout at birth and later "resonant" with similar layouts.

The argument I want to make is that this is Kepler trying to naturalize astrology from the inside, using the explanatory currency of his own physics - geometry, parsimony, fit with observation - instead of just discarding astrology or leaving it alone. What I'm not sure holds up: does grounding influence in a soul that's "sensitive" to angular ratios actually explain anything, or does it just relocate the unexplained affinity - swap "Mars in the seventh house means X" for "the soul resonates with 72 degree geometry" - dressed in better math but no less mysterious underneath?

So: does the naturalizing framing survive that objection, or am I giving Kepler too much credit? Is there a cleaner way to describe what he's actually doing? And separately - anything here that's just wrong, historically.
