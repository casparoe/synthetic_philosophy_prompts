I work as a freelance translator, and every few months I face a version of the same choice: two clients want to schedule work with me, one offering a small, quick translation I could start almost immediately, the other a longer, better-paying contract that would start about a month later. When I first sit down and plan out my schedule, well before either job is due to start, I always decide to prioritize the longer contract once the time comes: it pays far better for the time involved, and the decision feels obviously correct when I make it. But when the small translation's start date actually arrives, with the longer contract still weeks off, I quite often say yes to the small job anyway and ask to have the longer contract pushed back, even though nothing relevant has changed in the meantime. What strikes me is that, in that second moment, I feel no tension and no sense of giving in to anything, unlike how I feel when I skip a workout despite knowing better. Taking the small job just seems like the obviously correct call right then, even though a month earlier the obviously correct call was the opposite one. Is there a good way of understanding what has really happened to my judgment in between, and does it point to anything I could do differently?
