I'm drafting a first-contact novel in which a field linguist has to work out an alien language from scratch, using nothing but her observations of how its speakers respond to what is happening around them, and I've been reading Davidson's work on radical interpretation to keep the decoding scenes intellectually honest. As I understand his Principle of Charity, it directs her to treat the aliens as correct about virtually everything they assert, using that presumed correctness as the fixed point from which she reads off, sentence by sentence, what each utterance means. On this approach, whenever a candidate translation makes an alien sound mistaken, that counts as evidence that she has mistranslated rather than evidence that the alien has actually gotten something wrong. Have I understood the principle correctly, and is this a sound basis for how she should reason her way into their language?
