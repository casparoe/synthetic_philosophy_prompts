I got stuck owning the slur/toxicity filter for the chat feature at work, and while trying to make it less dumb I fell into a philosophy-of-language rabbit hole. Turns out theres real disagreement about why slurs are offensive in the first place: some say the derogatory content is baked right into the word's meaning, some say its more pragmatic (using the word implicates that you endorse some ideology, without changing the sentence's literal truth conditions), and apparently theres a whole "prohibitionist" camp arguing it's not about content at all, just that the word itself is taboo -- which is supposed to explain why even neutrally quoting a slur to explain why its bad can still land badly.

What are the most important implications of this debate for how an actual filter should be built? I mainly care about mention/quote cases, in-group reclaimed use (feels like prohibitionism has an easier time here since the taboo could just be indexed to the speaker, but i'm not sure the content-based views do), and dogwhistle terms that aren't slurs and would never show up on a wordlist to begin with. Bonus question: does any of these theories actually predict anything about how new dogwhistles get invented, or is that just a different phenomenon none of them were ever meant to explain?
