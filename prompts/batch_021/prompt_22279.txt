I've been falling down a total rabbit hole this week for a piece on precommitment devices — the whole "Ulysses contract" thing — and the more I read the more I think people (me included, historically) throw that phrase around way to loosely. Elster's own position actually shifts across his two books on it: in "Ulysses and the Sirens" he's pretty into the metaphor, but by the time of "Ulysses Unbound" years later he's practically taking it back apart, pointing out that Odysseus binding Odysseus — same guy, continuous memory, knows exactly what temptation he's protecting against — is a very different animal from, say, one generation of voters entrenching a rule against a totally different set of people fifty years on. That distinction, one continuous agent using foresight to restrain its own predictable future self versus something else wearing the same costume, is what I want to actually test out on you rather than just take on faith from the secondary literature.

So: below are nine things that get called (or plausibly could be called) "Ulysses contracts."
