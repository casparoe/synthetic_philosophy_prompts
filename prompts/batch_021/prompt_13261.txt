A friend and I spent most of a dinner last week arguing about art forgery, after the conversation drifted from a gallery visit into the old story of Han van Meegeren, the Dutch painter whose forged Vermeers were convincing enough to end up in Hermann Göring's collection during the war. He later had to prove in court that his own "masterpiece" was in fact a fake, in order to escape a treason conviction. My friend's position was that once a forgery is perceptually indistinguishable from the original, any extra value we place on the original is just historical snobbery dressed up as aesthetics. I pushed back with a version of Nelson Goodman's argument about autographic art: that indistinguishability today doesn't settle the matter, because a sufficiently trained eye might, with time and instruction, learn to pick out features that separate the original from the copy, so the original can count as aesthetically superior right now even though no one can currently tell them apart. My friend thought this move was suspiciously convenient, since it lets you defend the original's superiority against any forgery whatsoever by gesturing at a future discrimination nobody has actually made. Things got even messier when I brought up Alfred Lessing's rival claim that the wrongness of forgery has nothing to do with aesthetic properties at all, and everything to do with originality as an achievement, and we never really reached a resolution.

I'd like you to write me a joke that turns on this specific dispute, rather than on forgery in general. Ideally the humor should depend on the "you'll eventually be able to tell them apart" move itself, or on how conveniently unfalsifiable that move is, such that the joke would fall flat for someone who didn't already understand what was being argued. It needn't follow a classic setup/punchline structure — a short absurd scene, a one-liner, or a mock exchange between two collectors would all work — as long as the actual philosophical content is doing the comedic work, rather than a generic joke about fakes simply being fake.
