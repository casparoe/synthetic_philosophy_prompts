so our comp neuro journal club covered the free energy principle a few weeks back and it kicked off this whole argument between two people in my cohort that i still can't stop turning over. quick background: Naomi's the resident FEP skeptic, Theo's writing his whole thesis on active inference, so neither of them exactly came in neutral.

(1) Naomi opened with: the expected free energy story is a shell game. you decompose it into an extrinsic/pragmatic term and an epistemic term and present that as deriving the explore-exploit tradeoff from first principles, but that's just reward plus a curiosity bonus, which every RL person already has, wearing a variational costume.

(2) Theo said that's not fair, because under EFE those two terms aren't seperately-tunable knobs the way a reward function and an exploration bonus are in RL - they both fall out of minimizing one functional under one generative model, so the theory forces a specific relationship between how precise/confident your beliefs are and how much you should explore vs exploit, instead of just hand-picking whatever exploration schedule happens to work.

(3) Naomi's comeback: sure, once you've already picked the generative model. but nothing stops anyone from picking one after the fact that reproduces whatever tradeoff was actually observed - there's no experiment that could come out and force you to abandon the framework, only your particular guess about the model. so the 'constraint' binds the modeler, not the theory.

(4) Theo conceded a chunk of that - said fine, the principle itself is basically unfalsifiable, Friston's said as much himself, comparing it to Hamilton's principle of least action: it's a 'state theory' not a process theory, closer to a bookkeeping identity than an empirical claim. but that's exactly why your supposed to test the process theories built underneath it, like predictive coding and precision-weighting, not the principle itself, and those have generated real, checked predictions. Naomi wasn't having it: predictive coding and the Bayesian brain stuff predate Friston and are testable on their own terms, no variational free energy required, so either the FEP math adds falsifiable content beyond 'pick a generative model that fits after the fact,' or the state-theory/process-theory move is just a way of always relocating the substance one level away from wherever you're pressing.
