We'd had a bad prod incident, and Noah, who wrote the doc, put "Root cause: memory leak in the billing service" right at the top. Farah flagged it in review: all we've actually got is that billing's memory graph looked weird in 4 of the last 5 similar incidents, nobody pulled a heap dump or found the actual leak this time, so its really just a strong correlation. Farah countered that theres a real difference between being confident because of a statistical pattern and actually knowing what happened this specific time, and that other teams will read "root cause: X" and treat it as settled, maybe not dig any further. Noah shot back that basically every causal claim anyone makes at work, like "the deploy caused the outage," is really just a strong correlation dressed up as fact, so if that standard disqualifies his sentence it disqualifies almost everything anyone writes in a doc. Farah said fine, but there's still a difference between writing "likely cause: X, unconfirmed" and flatly asserting X as the cause, and that Noah's doc did the latter when only the former was actually earned.
