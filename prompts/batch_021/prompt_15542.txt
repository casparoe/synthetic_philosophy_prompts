So over the summer I've been slowly working through Tim Maudlin's book on the philosophy of space and time (basically prepping because I want to double major in physics and philosophy in college, don't judge me), and I hit the chapter on Einstein's "hole argument" and I feel like I'm missing something huge about why anyone cares. The setup is: you take a solution to the field equations of GR, pick some little region with no matter in it (the "hole"), and smoothly reshuffle the metric inside that region using a diffeomorphism thats just the identity everywhere outside it. Since GR is generally covariant, the reshuffled version is ALSO a totally valid solution, matching the original one exactly everywhere outside the hole (including the whole past), but disagreeing inside it. Apparently this exact idea is what stumped Einstein for like two years before he landed on general relativity, which is a cool fact, but I don't get what philosophical point people are actually making with it now.

Like, if all you're doing is relabeling which point gets which metric value, why would that ever count as a real physical difference to anyone? My gut reaction is that its just an empty bookkeeping move that shouldn't bother anybody. But apparently this relabeling trick is supposed to show that "substantivalism" about spacetime (the view that points of spacetime are real things, not just a useful way of talking about relations between objects) leads straight into a radical indeterminism, where identical pasts allow for totally different futures with nothing to explain the difference, while "relationists," who deny spacetime points are real in that way, apparently just don't have this problem show up at all. Can someone actually explain WHY the relabeling counts as metaphysically loaded instead of an obviously empty formal trick once you're a substantivalist, and also what "sophisticated substantivalism" is supposed to be doing to dodge the indeterminism without basically just turning into relationism wearing a substantivalist costume? My friend says this is all just people arguing over word games, but that feels like too easy of an answer to me.
