So in this elective at my school called AI & Society (its new this year, sort of a mashup of intro CS and philosophy), we actually had to read Turing's whole 1950 paper instead of just getting the imitation game explained to us, which meant we also got to the objections section, including the weird one near the end called the Argument from Extrasensory Perception, where Turing says the statistical evidence for telepathy is "overwhelming" and then proposes you'd need a "telepathy-proof room" to keep the imitation game fair if ESP turns out to be real. Our teacher kind of breezed past it to get to the next objection, but two kids in my class, Malik and Sasha, got into a whole argument about it after class that I haven't been able to stop thinking about.

Malik's take is that Turing genuinely took ESP seriously as a live empirical threat, not just as a hypothetical he's knocking down for show -- his main evidence is that Turing never debunks the evidence the way he does for basically every other objection (he doesn't say the card-guessing experiments are flawed or the stats are cherry-picked), he just accepts telepathy is probably real and redesigns the thought experiment around it, and Malik's line was "you don't build an elaborate experimental control for something you think is made up." Sasha's comeback was that the whole essay is Turing steelmanning every objection as hard as possible before moving past most of them, so engaging with ESP "seriously" in the text is just thoroughness, not a personal confession that he believed in it. Malik replied that real thoroughness would at least gesture at counterevidence, the way Turing does for the theological objection right before it, and the fact that he doesn't do that here -- that he just folds ESP into his framework and patches around it -- means he's treating it differently in kind, not just being extra fair. Sasha's response was that Turing might've simply felt unqualified to critique parapsychology data specifically, since it wasnt his field, so going quiet on the evidence could just be caution about a subject he's not in a position to referee, not agreement with it.

Can you go through this move by move and say who's got the stronger point at each stage? Also if you think there's some other way of reading that whole section that neither Malik nor Sasha is considering, I'd want to hear that too. We don't really need Turing's whole biography or anything, just want an actual read on what's going on in that specific part of the paper.
