So a few of us were at my friend Dan's place last night, a couple beers in, and the conversation took one of those pointless-but-weirdly-gripping turns around 2am. It started because I mentioned we'd already seen that new heist movie together back in March, Dan insisted we hadn't, and when I said I clearly remembered the ending he went "yeah but how do you actually know your memory isn't just misfiring." Fair enough, I guess, except he didn't stop there — from there he moved on to "how do you know your senses are reliable in general," then to the old "how do you even know you have hands" bit, and eventually landed on: literally nothing can be proven, so my belief that we saw the movie is exactly as justified as his belief that we didn't, we're just running on different unproven assumptions. I didn't have a good comeback in the moment and it's definately been bugging me since.

Here's what bugs me most: Dan doesn't actually live like a guy who doubts he has hands or that his memory basically works — he drove himself home after, and he'll show up to his job tomorrow assuming the building he works in still exists. So his big skeptical move feels like its cheating somehow, or at least inconsistent with itself, but "come on, obviously you have hands" isn't much of a comeback either, and just repeating my original claim louder felt like it'd just be assuming the very thing he was questioning. Is Dan's position actually coherent — is everything we believe really just equally "unproven" once you push back far enough, or is he confusing two different kinds of doubt somehow? And is there any way to actually argue someone out of a position like that, or once someone's committed to it, does normal evidence and argument just stop working?

I only took one intro philosophy class years ago, so I feel like there's probably some standard answer to this that I'm just not aware of. For context, this isn't even the first time Dan's pulled this move — he loves being the annoying skeptic at parties — but this was the first time I walked away actually unsettled instead of just annoyed, so I'd love an actual answer instead of just a better comeback for next time.
