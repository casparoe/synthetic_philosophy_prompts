I sit on my university's student-run Honor Committee, and a seminar on Sartre I'm taking this term has made this week's case hard to shake. A first-year was found responsible for bringing prohibited notes into a final exam, and at the sanctioning hearing she presented testimony from professors calling her scrupulously honest, arguing that the act reflected panic, not who she really is. Our sanctioning guidelines list "character and reputation for integrity" as something the panel "may weigh" in setting the penalty, without saying how much. Half the committee wants to treat the testimony as an ordinary mitigating factor, the way we would for anyone with a clean record; the other half keeps returning to Sartre's point that facticity -- a person's past and traits -- never adds up to a fixed character that could make an act genuinely "out of character," and worries that crediting the plea just rewards the kind of self-flattering excuse he calls bad faith. Write the short memo I'll bring to Friday's meeting: recommend how much weight, if any, the testimony should get, with reasoning the rest of the committee could apply to later cases, and take the strongest version of the other side's argument seriously rather than dismiss it.
