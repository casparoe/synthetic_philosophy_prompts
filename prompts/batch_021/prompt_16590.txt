I'm giving the toast at my friend Theo's wedding this weekend, and last night at the rehearsal dinner I got into this whole argument with him and his fiancée Mira that's kind of wrecked my plans for tomorrow. Theo's been a serious meditator for years now (I tried it with him for a summer, mostly just fell asleep) and is fully committed to the anatta thing, no fixed self, just this changing bundle of aggregates that we conventionally call "Theo." Mira's finishing a philosophy PhD and, coming at it from a totally different angle (some 20th century British guy, not Buddhist at all), has landed in basically the same spot: she says theres no further fact about personal identity over time, just degrees of psychological connectedness, and that realizing this is supposed to be freeing rather than sad. Cute, they found each other. But then I pointed out that their vows have this line about loving each other "for the rest of our lives," and asked: if neither of you thinks theres a continuous person here, who exactly is making that promise, and why should Theo-in-thirty-years or Mira-in-thirty-years feel bound by words said by two totally different bundles tonight? Theo came back with this image of a flame lighting another flame off it, not the same flame, not a different one either, and said that's a fine enough basis for a vow, but by the end even he admitted it doesn't really explain why anyone should feel bound by anything.

So now I actually have to write this toast and I'm stuck between two bad options. I can lean on normal wedding-toast lines like "two souls becoming one, forever," which is exactly the view they'd both say is false, and which Theo has literally asked me not to say. Or I get so careful about the metaphysics that it stops sounding like a toast and starts sounding like a seminar. Half the guests are Theo's sangha and Mira's philosophy cohort, who will absolutely notice if I quietly smuggle in a persisting soul; the other half is just family who want to cry happy tears and eat cake. Can you actually write the toast for me, around four minutes? I want it to take their view seriously enough to actually use it, maybe build on that flame image since it's the one thing they both seemed to agree actually helped, rather than dodge the whole problem or bury it in disclaimers.
