"Max Black's dialogue asks us to imagine a universe containing nothing but two exactly similar iron spheres, a couple of miles apart, with no other objects around to serve as a frame of reference. (a) State the Leibnizian principle this thought experiment is meant to challenge, and explain why the scenario counts as a counterexample to it. (b) A critic objects that the spheres aren't really indiscernible after all, since each one stands in a spatial relation to the other that it doesn't stand in to itself. Explain the objection, and say whether it succeeds. (15 points)"

Student's answer:

"(a) The principle at stake is the Identity of Indiscernibles (PII): if x and y share all their properties, x and y are the same thing. This is the converse of Leibniz's Law, the Indiscernibility of Identicals, which says that if x = y then x and y share all their properties -- Black's thought experiment is really aimed at both principles at once, since it shows that sameness of properties doesn't guarantee sameness of object, and vice versa. The two spheres are meant to be a counterexample to PII because they share every property there is to share -- same size, mass, composition, age, even the same distance from one another -- and yet there are clearly two of them, not one, since we could stand next to first one and then the other. Black's version is stronger than older versions of this kind of counterexample because it builds in relational properties from the start, not just intrinsic ones like colour or shape, so you can't save PII just by pointing out that the spheres share a relation.

(b) The objection says sphere A has the property of being two miles from B, while B has the property of being two miles from A, and since one relation involves B and the other involves A, these are actually two different properties -- so the spheres are discernible after all. I think this objection succeeds: each sphere really does bear a relation to the other one that it doesn't bear to itself (zero distance vs. two miles), so there's a genuine qualitative difference between them, which means Black's counterexample to PII doesn't actually go through."
