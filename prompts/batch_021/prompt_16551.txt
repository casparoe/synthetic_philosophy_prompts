I chair our department's colloquium series, and next month's invited speaker is a philosopher whose recent work argues that evidential cooperation in large worlds (ECL) gives us practical reason to act cooperatively toward agents who hold value systems other than our own — even though we cannot causally affect them — provided the universe or multiverse is large enough to contain many other agents reasoning in relevantly similar ways. Pressed on why any of this should matter absent a causal channel, she tends to reply that what needs to correlate across a large world is the decision procedure, not the object-level values, so it does not matter who is actually "out there." Draft eight to ten questions for the Q&A that go beyond having her restate the view: I want at least one question on what actually licenses confidence that our reasoning correlates with these agents rather than the correlation being merely stipulated, one on whether the superrationality machinery is doing any work that a simpler heuristic such as a moral parliament could not do on its own, and one on how, in practice, we are supposed to identify what "cooperating" with agents whose values we cannot observe even amounts to. For each, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give.
