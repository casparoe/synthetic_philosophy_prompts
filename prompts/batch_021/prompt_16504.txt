Since retiring after a long career in physics, I have been working through the philosophy of mind, and I keep returning to Roger Penrose's Gödelian case against computationalism in *The Emperor's New Mind* and *Shadows of the Mind*. As I understand the argument, which I gather traces back to J. R. Lucas in the 1960s, it runs as follows: for any consistent formal system powerful enough to encode arithmetic, there is a Gödel sentence that the system itself cannot prove, yet a human reasoner standing outside the system can recognize that very sentence as true; since this holds for every such system, human mathematical understanding cannot be captured by any single algorithm, however large or sophisticated. This looks to me like a genuine mathematical proof rather than a piece of contestable philosophy, so I am puzzled as to why so many logicians, philosophers of mind, and AI researchers still treat mechanism about the mind as a serious, open possibility. What am I missing?
