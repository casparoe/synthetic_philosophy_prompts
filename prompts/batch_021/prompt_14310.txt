I'm a physics PhD student auditing a philosophy of physics seminar, and we've just moved from Newton's bucket to Earman and Norton's hole argument. As I understand it, the argument shows that spacetime substantivalism -- the view that spacetime points are individuals with identities of their own, over and above the metric field defined on them -- is simply incompatible with general relativity: because the theory is diffeomorphism invariant, a substantivalist has to treat two models that agree everywhere outside some bounded region but disagree inside it as describing distinct physical possibilities, which saddles GR with a completely unphysical indeterminism. Since no working physicist thinks GR is indeterministic in that sense, this looks like a clean reductio, on a par with a genuine no-go theorem, that should have closed off substantivalism the moment Earman and Norton published it in 1987. Yet I keep running into contemporary philosophers of physics who still defend substantivalist positions without seeming to think they owe anyone a response to this. Am I missing some way the argument is actually inconclusive, or is this just a case of a philosophical position surviving well past the point where it was refuted?
