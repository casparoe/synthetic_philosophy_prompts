While reporting on questions of legal and moral accountability for AI-related harms — a self-driving car's split-second decision in a crash, say, or an autonomous weapons system misidentifying a target — I keep running into Andreas Matthias's notion of a "responsibility gap": his claim that sufficiently autonomous, self-learning systems can behave in ways that no designer, manufacturer, or operator had enough control or foreknowledge over to be fairly blamed for, while the system itself is no more a plausible target of blame than a falling rock would be. This strikes me as closely related to the older "problem of many hands" from theories of organizational and political responsibility, where a harmful outcome is produced by so many small, individually blameless contributions within a bureaucracy or corporation that no single person can justly be held responsible for it. Is the responsibility gap actually a new problem, or is it just the problem of many hands with a machine added to the chain of contributors? Justify your answer, being specific about where the analogy between the two holds and where it breaks down.
