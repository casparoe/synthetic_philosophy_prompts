I've spent a lot of my free time over the past year teaching myself statistics, and the idea that has stuck with me the most is Simpson's Paradox: cases where a trend that clearly holds in the combined data disappears, or even reverses, once the same figures are broken down into subgroups. Every explanation of it I have come across frames the lesson the same way: the combined number is treated as a kind of statistical illusion, and the subgroup breakdown is presented as the version you should trust, since some other factor was distorting the overall picture. That has left me with a general rule of thumb: whenever someone hands me an aggregate statistic, I now assume it is probably concealing something, and that the real relationship will only show up once I split the data into the right subgroups. Is that actually the correct lesson to take from Simpson's Paradox, or would it be a mistake to treat "always trust the disaggregated version over the combined one" as a rule that holds in general?
