I get the family resemblance: both are about two parties landing on a jointly good outcome despite there being no causal channel between them -- no communication, no enforcement, no shared history -- and both lean on the idea that sufficiently similar reasoners will reason their way to the same policy. Could you compare superrationality, in Hofstadter's original sense, to acausal trade as it's used in that more recent decision-theoretic literature?
