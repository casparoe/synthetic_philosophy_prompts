I'm a retired marine biologist who's gotten pretty into philosophy since retiring, so bear with me if this turns out to be a dumb question. Something happened in my neighborhood recently that's been bugging me. A teenager down the street totaled a parked car and the court hit him with a $600 fine plus 30 hours of community service. His dad paid off the fine right away and nobody thought twice about it, but when the dad offered to serve the 30 hours himself instead (he's retired too, and has alot more free time than his kid), the judge said absolutely not, the boy has to do it himself. Everybody I've mentioned this to agrees the judge was obviously right, but nobody can tell me why. If money can be handed from one person to another to settle a debt, why can't time, or suffering, or punishment more generally, get handed over the same way? Is there an actual principled line here, or are we just being inconsistent?
