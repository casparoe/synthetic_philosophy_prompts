Ok this has been bugging me for a while: Yudkowsky's CEV basically asks us to defer to the preferences of a smarter, fully coherent, "grown up farther together" version of ourselves -- which is pretty much Railton's old "ideal advisor" move from theories of well-being, and it inherits the classic Sobel-style objection to that move: why does the hypothetical extrapolated version's volition have authority over actual, un-extrapolated me, when its arguably not even the same person anymore? CEV also has to handle the case where a single person's extrapolation just doesn't converge, or where different people's extrapolated wishes point in different directions (IIRC he calls these the "muddle" and "spread" problems), and his fix is basically to just stay silent on those parts rather than force an answer. Write me a dialogue between a committed CEV defender and someone pressing the authority objection hard -- I want real engagement with the muddle/spread machinery, not just trading "what if the AI gets it wrong" back and forth, and both sides need to be sharp enough to land real hits, no strawmen.
