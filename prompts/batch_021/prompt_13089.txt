I'm a few years into a phil of mind PhD, and this happened after our reading group last week -- we'd just gone through the section of Kim's Mind in a Physical World on the causal exclusion arguement, and two people in the group (not me, I mostly just ate the snacks) ended up going at it over coffee afterward about whether Kim's own preferred fix -- functional reduction -- actually rescues non-reductive physicalism or secretly just gives up on it. Jonah opened by saying it solves the exclusion problem cleanly: if you define a mental property by its causal role, then in any given system that property just IS whatever physical state occupies the role there, so there's no separate mental cause competing with the physical cause for the same effect. Multiple realizability survives at the species/kind level (pain-in-humans vs. pain-in-octopuses), while each token case gets a clean local identity, so no exclusion and no epiphenomenalism. Mara said that's not a solution, it's a surrender -- species-specific identity is reductionism with a fig leaf, and it throws out the whole reason people became non-reductive physicalists to begin with, namely Fodor's point that psychology states real causal generalizations (about beliefs, desires, etc.) that hold across wildly different realizers, with no unifying physical description at all. If the causal work is always done locally by the realizer, she said, the higher-level generalization isn't tracking one causal kind, it's just a convenient summary of many different ones.

Jonah shot back that Mara's own example defeats her: if a "kind" is realized by arbitrarily disjunctive physical states with nothing in common, that's exactly the kind of case Kim says shows it was never a single causally unified property to start with, so biting that bullet is totally fine. Mara replied that this concedes way too much to the metaphysics -- it means a fairly abstract argument about causal closure and overdetermination gets to tell working cognitive scientists that the kinds they quantify over every day (belief, pain, desire) aren't fully real, or aren't doing any causal work, which seems backwards. Neither budged, and I had to head home before it went anywhere further -- I've been flip-flopping on who's right ever since.
