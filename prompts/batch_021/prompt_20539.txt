I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole this week with David Lewis's modal realism for a class, specifically the fight between counterpart theory and transworld identity, and I think it'd make a genuinely good short story if someone actually took the metaphysics seriously instead of doing the usual lazy "parallel universe" thing where it's just assumed the other-you is you. Here's the premise I want you to run with: possible worlds are real, concrete, causally sealed-off places (not branches of our timeline, actual seperate universes, Lewis-style), and someone invents a viewer that can find and observe a person's counterparts in nearby worlds -- people picked out purely by resemblance, who the device's own operating theory insists are not numerically the same person as you, just very similar strangers. Have your protagonist get shown a counterpart of themselves living a way better (or worse, your call) life, and really sit with the Kripke-style discomfort that if nothing that happens to that person is literally happening to them, it's not obvious why they should care about it at all -- and then complicate things by having the machine turn up two equally good counterparts of the protagonist in that very same neighboring world, which a mere similarity relation allows but strict identity never could.

I'd also like some tension in the story between that counterpart-theory framing and a rival view floating around in-world -- a scientist, a cult, whatever fits the tone you want to go for -- that insists genuine transworld identity is possible, that you can actually become or merge with the other-world self instead of just watching a lookalike, and that resemblance was never the point since you can just stipulate which person out there counts as you. It doesn't have to resolve the metaphysical dispute, but I want the plot and especially the ending to actually turn on the difference between those two pictures, rather than just hand-waving at "alternate timelines" the way most sci-fi does.
