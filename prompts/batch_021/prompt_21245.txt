Two coworkers of mine, Derek and Naomi, got into an argument after a team lunch about something our teammate Sam had done. Sam had stopped buying anything from a clothing brand the moment a friend mentioned, in passing, that the company mistreats its overseas factory workers. Sam hadn't looked into it himself — he just took the friend's word for it and switched brands that same week.

(1) Derek said there's nothing wrong with this: it's no different from trusting a doctor about cholesterol, or trusting a well-tested library instead of re-deriving the algorithm yourself. Nobody has the bandwidth to independently verify every ethical claim before acting on it, so taking a trusted person's word for something is just ordinary division of labor, not a special failure.

(2) Naomi disagreed: cholesterol levels and code correctness are just questions of fact, where getting the right answer is all that matters. Sam, she said, hasn't actually done anything commendable by boycotting the brand, because he still has no idea why it's supposedly wrong — if the friend turns out to be mistaken next month about some other company, Sam will just as blindly switch back, since he never had any grip on the reasons himself.

(3) Derek replied that this proves too much: most people can't defend, from first principles, why child labor is wrong either — they absorbed that from parents and teachers — yet nobody says their opposition to child labor is somehow lesser or not genuinely their own.

(4) Naomi answered that upbringing is different from a one-off adult decision: something absorbed over years of moral upbringing gets internalized into a person's own outlook, and even a kid can usually gesture at some reason ("because it hurts people"), whereas Sam couldn't articulate even a rough reason right now — he'd just point back at his friend.

Neither of them budged. Could you go through the exchange point by point and say who has the better of it at each step, not just who wins overall, and flag anything you think both of them are missing?
