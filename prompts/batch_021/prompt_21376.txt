I spent almost forty years as a condensed matter physicist before I retired, and these days I fill my mornings with philosophy books instead of journal articles. Something that's been nagging at me lately, and which actually connects back to old arguments from my own field, is this whole business in metaphysics about grounding and what counts as truly fundamental. In condensed matter we used to argue endlessly about whether things like superconductivity or magnetism (think of Philip Anderson's old essay "More is Different") are genuinely new phenomena in their own right, or just complicated rearrangements of the same old electrons and lattice sites, and it turns out philosophers have a much more general and much older version of that same argument.

A friend from my new philosophy discusion group pointed me toward Jonathan Schaffer's idea of "priority monism" - the claim that the cosmos as a single whole is the one truly fundamental entity, and that everything else, including all the particles and objects we normally think of as basic building blocks, is actually derivative, grounded in that whole rather than the whole being built up out of them. This struck me as a pretty radical reversal of what I'd always just assumed metaphysicians believed, namely something more like priority pluralism or atomism - the Democritus-style picture where the smallest, simplest parts are what's fundamental, and everything bigger is grounded in, or built out of, those parts.

Could you walk me through a comparison of these two positions for me? I'm especially curious about the actual arguments given for each side (I have a vague memory that quantum entanglement gets used as ammunition for the monist side, which as an old physicist obviously grabs my attention, though I'm sure its not quite that simple), and about where the real crux of the disagreement lies - is it basically just a clash of intuitions about parts versus wholes, or is there a deeper disagreement about what "grounding" itself is even supposed to mean? And if you had to bet, do you think one of these two views is in noticeably better shape today, argument-wise, than the other, or is it more of a genuine standoff?
