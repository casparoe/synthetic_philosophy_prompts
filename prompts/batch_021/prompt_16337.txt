As I've been working through the literature on mathematical constructivism, I keep encountering the claim that constructive mathematics is simply a more cautious fragment of classical mathematics: if a statement has a constructive proof, that proof is automatically a classical proof as well, so a constructivist never asserts anything a classical mathematician would deny; they merely decline to assert some things that classical mathematicians do assert. But I've also read that Brouwer proved, within intuitionism, that every total function from the reals to the reals is continuous -- which is classically false, given the existence of step functions. Doesn't the existence of a theorem like that refute the reassurance that constructive mathematics never proves anything a classical mathematician would deny?
