So I went down a rabbit hole this week on Herbert Simon's idea of "satisficing" -- the theory that people mostly just grab the first option that clears some "good enough" bar instead of grinding through every alternative to find the actual best one. It hit close to home because its basically what we do at work constantly: you stop tuning a query or stop benchmarking configs once perf is "good enough" and ship, instead of searching forever for the global optimum. I brought it up with a buddy of mine over lunch (we're both engineers, so this was a weird lunch topic) who's way more econ-brained than me, and he immediately went "that's not actually a different theory of rationality, that's just normal expected-utility maximizing once you price in the cost of searching and deliberating -- satisficing is a special case of optimizing, not an alternative to it." Which, fair, but apparently people have had this exact fight for decades. I guess Simon had some kind of comeback about that move basically making rational choice theory untestable, since you can rescue ANY behavior that way if you're allowed to invent costs after the fact -- but I don't really follow the details of his counter-arguement or how the maximizer side is supposed to answer back.

Could you write me a dialogue between two people hashing this out -- one a committed rational-choice/expected-utility maximizer, the other a bounded-rationality person in Simon's tradition -- who are watching a mutual friend decide when to stop apartment hunting (she's got a "first place above my threshold, done" rule) and start arguing about whether that rule is secretly just optimization with search costs folded in, or something genuinely different. Neither of them should be the obvious pushover -- I want a real back and forth where they're both clearly sharp and actually responding to each other's specific points, not restating their opening position in fancier words each round. So have the maximizer push the "search cost" redescription as hard as it goes, give the satisficer an actual comeback (not just "well I still don't buy it"), and then let the maximizer reply to THAT. Extra credit if someone brings up the issue where, to optimize how much you search, you'd apparently need to already know the value of searching more -- which seems like it either needs its own optimization (regress forever) or has to get resolved some other way -- that's the part that's been bugging me most and I honestly can't tell whose side it's supposed to help.
