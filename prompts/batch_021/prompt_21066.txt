I'm finalizing lecture notes that set Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against classical hard determinism, and I want to check my framing before class. As I have it, the argument runs: an agent's actions issue from her character; that character was itself formed by antecedent causes -- genes, upbringing, formative experience -- reaching back to a time before she existed; determinism then guarantees that she never authored the character from which her actions flow, so she cannot be truly, desert-entailingly responsible for what she does. On this reading, Strawson's real contribution is just to spell out, more carefully than earlier hard determinists, why a deterministic causal history undermines ultimate responsibility. If that's right, the obvious libertarian escape route is to deny determinism and insist that the crucial choices in a life are undetermined -- perhaps via some form of agent causation -- so that the regress of "responsible for the way you are" halts at an act that is genuinely the agent's own, rather than the product of prior causes. Have I set up the dispute between Strawson and his libertarian critics correctly, and would establishing indeterminism at the relevant junctures be enough to meet his challenge?
