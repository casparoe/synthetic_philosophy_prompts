For my school's philosophy club, we've spent the last several meetings working through the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's entry on metaphysical grounding, and one assumption keeps getting treated as almost too obvious to need defending: that grounding must be "well-founded," meaning that every chain of grounded facts eventually has to bottom out in something fundamental that isn't grounded in anything further. The usual justification offered is that if a chain never terminated, nothing along it would ever really get explained — a metaphysical version of the "turtles all the way down" worry. Digging a little further, though, I found that a handful of contemporary metaphysicians (I believe Ricki Bliss is one of the names most associated with this) take the opposite view seriously, holding that an infinitely descending structure of grounding, with no fundamental level at all, might be a perfectly coherent way for reality to be — a position sometimes called "metaphysical infinitism," by analogy with the infinitist option in the more familiar epistemic regress problem about justification.

I'd like you to actually make the case for infinitism rather than presenting both sides evenhandedly. Please argue that grounding chains need not terminate in something fundamental, and that a world which is derivative "all the way down" is not only conceivable but not obviously incoherent.

In making this argument, I'd especially like you to confront the "vicious regress" charge head-on — the objection that an endless chain of grounding could never discharge any explanatory debt, so that even the facts we started with at the "top" would end up just as unexplained as if there were no grounds at all — rather than simply asserting that infinite regresses are sometimes benign elsewhere in philosophy. Feel free to draw on the parallel (and any disanalogies) with the classic three-way foundationalism/infinitism/coherentism split regarding epistemic justification, since noticing that structure is what got me curious about this in the first place. I'm less interested here in circular or "looping" grounding structures, so you can set coherentism itself aside and keep the argument focused on infinitism versus the standard foundationalist picture.
