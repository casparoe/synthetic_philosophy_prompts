For the past several months I have shared an apartment with two roommates, Jonah and Priya, and a minor dispute between them has stayed with me longer than it probably should have. Priya has a habit of leaving dishes in the sink for days and skipping her turns on our cleaning schedule, and last week Jonah confronted her about it, calling it selfish and disrespectful to the rest of us and telling her she needed to grow up and start pulling her weight. The complication is that Jonah was just as bad about this himself until a few months ago, when our fourth roommate, who has since moved out, got fed up and called him out directly. He cleaned up his act after that, but he has never once mentioned his own history to Priya, and he speaks now as though he has always held these standards. When Priya shot back that he was the last person who should be lecturing her, he waved this off as a deflection from the real issue, namely that she is not doing her chores.

I keep going back and forth on who has the better of this. On one hand, Priya's retort looks like a way of dodging fair criticism rather than addressing her own behavior, and the fact that Jonah used to be lazy about dishes does not make it any less true that she is currently being inconsiderate; the chores either get done or they do not, regardless of who points that out. On the other hand, something about the specific combination of Jonah's past and his silence about it seems to genuinely disqualify him from being the one to deliver this particular lecture, in a way that would not be true if our former roommate, or some friend from outside the apartment, had said exactly the same words to Priya. Is Priya right to think Jonah has no business coming at her like this, or is that just a convenient way of avoiding the point he is making? And would your answer change if Jonah were still secretly skipping his own chores while lecturing her, rather than having genuinely reformed two months ago?
