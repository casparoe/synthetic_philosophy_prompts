Fitch's paradox of knowability starts from a thesis that anti-realists about truth — most famously Michael Dummett and those who have followed his lead — find almost irresistible: that truth cannot outrun our capacity to know it, so that every truth, whatever it concerns, must at least be knowable in principle, even if it happens never actually to be known. Frederic Fitch showed that this "knowability principle," together with the modest assumption that not every truth is in fact known by anyone, entails the wildly implausible conclusion that every truth is known after all. The proof works by taking some actual truth p that no one ever comes to know, forming the proposition "p is true and it is not known that p," and showing, first, that it is impossible even in principle for anyone ever to know that particular proposition, and, second, that this very impossibility contradicts what the knowability principle demands, given that the proposition itself is true. Anti-realists have proposed several ways of resisting the slide into omniscience: restricting the knowability principle so that it no longer applies to propositions of exactly this self-undermining kind, denying that the notion of "knowledge" used in the proof really has all the properties — such as distributing over conjunction, or guaranteeing the truth of what is known — that the argument needs, or revising the background logic so that a key step in the derivation no longer goes through.

Write a dialogue between an anti-realist who wants to hold on to the knowability principle at any cost, and a realist who takes Fitch's result to be a decisive argument that truth can indeed outrun what is or ever could be known. The anti-realist should commit to one specific strategy for blocking the paradox and defend it in real technical detail, rather than merely gesturing at the existence of some fix, while the realist should press hard on whether the chosen fix is principled or just an ad hoc patch stitched on to save a thesis that ought to have been abandoned, and on whether anything recognizable as the original anti-realist doctrine survives once the patch is in place. The two should respond to each other's actual moves rather than restating their opening positions, and the dialogue need not end in agreement or in a clean victory for either side.
