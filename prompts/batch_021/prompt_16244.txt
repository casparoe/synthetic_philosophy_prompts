I cover consciousness research for a science magazine, and I've been asked to give the opening remarks — no more than seven minutes — at a small, invitation-only workshop that will, for the first time, put Integrated Information Theory (IIT) researchers in the same room as several signatories of the 2023 open letter that branded the theory "pseudoscience." Half the room still feels ambushed by that letter; the other half thinks IIT finally got the scrutiny it had coming. A few funders and uncommitted neuroscientists, who know little of this history, will also be present. Please write these opening remarks. They need to do more than plead for civility: they should use IIT's exclusion postulate specifically, explained in terms the funders could follow, to show why this is a genuine disagreement about what would even count as evidence for a theory of consciousness rather than a turf war — and the remarks themselves must not take a side on whether "pseudoscience" was the right word.
