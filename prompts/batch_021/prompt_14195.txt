I have a philosophy background from before I became a science journalist, and I'm currently reporting a piece on how Karl Popper's old charge that natural selection is little more than a tautology -- and therefore, by his own criterion of demarcation, not a testable scientific theory -- keeps resurfacing in arguments about evolution's scientific credentials, almost always without mention of the fact that Popper later revised the judgment. A former professor of mine, mid-way through grading finals on exactly this material, asked me to sanity-check her marking on one answer she's on the fence about. I'd rather form my own view before seeing hers, so I haven't asked what's bothering her about it.

Question: Popper argued that natural selection, as usually formulated, is little more than a tautology and, on his own criterion, not a strictly testable scientific theory but a "metaphysical research programme." He later described himself as having revised that judgment.
(a) Reconstruct the tautology objection as precisely as you can: what exactly threatens to make the principle unfalsifiable?
(b) Explain what work the label "metaphysical research programme" is doing -- why didn't Popper simply dismiss Darwinism as unscientific nonsense?
(c) What, on your understanding, changed his mind, and does his revised position fully satisfy the falsifiability criterion, or does some tension remain?

Student's answer: Popper's worry starts from how "fitness" gets defined. If the fittest are simply whichever organisms survive and reproduce more, then "the fittest survive" collapses into "those that survive, survive" -- a claim no observation could contradict, since any outcome can be redescribed afterward as the fit one winning out. On Popper's criterion, a claim no evidence could in principle refute isn't doing scientific work, however true it might be. This is why Popper concluded that Darwinism, and evolutionary theory generally, falls short of the standard he set for physics or chemistry.

Yet Popper didn't want to throw Darwinism out with the pseudosciences. He called it a "metaphysical research programme": not itself falsifiable, but a framework that organizes inquiry and can be filled in with testable content by more specific claims -- about particular adaptations, particular fossils, particular biogeographical patterns -- even though the master principle behind them isn't itself at risk from any single observation. In that sense it sits above astrology and below Newtonian mechanics.

Popper eventually said he'd changed his mind. Once biologists pointed out that fitness needn't be defined circularly -- it can be tied to independently identifiable traits whose survival value is predicted in advance and checked afterward -- the tautology threat dissolves, because the theory is now risking something. Once that move is available, natural selection turns out to be testable in essentially the same sense as any theory in the experimental sciences: it generates predictions that could, in principle, come out false, and a single well-documented counterexample would refute it, exactly as one black swan refutes "all swans are white." Popper's recantation effectively puts Darwinism on the same epistemic footing as physics, which is presumably why he was willing to make it so publicly.
