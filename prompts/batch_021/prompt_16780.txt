I'm taking a metaethics seminar this term, and it's made me look differently at a situation from my own life that I still can't fully resolve. A close friend recently won a research fellowship I'd also applied for, and my roommate keeps telling me I should work on genuinely admiring her instead of feeling envious, since people who feel admiration rather than envy toward successful peers tend to be happier and more motivated themselves. On this reasoning, the psychological benefit to me is itself a good reason to cultivate real admiration for her, whether or not her work is actually admirable. I don't doubt admiring her would be good for me, but it seems strange to treat that as a reason to actually admire her, rather than just a reason to want to feel that way. Is my roommate right that this gives me a real reason to admire her, or is something wrong with that argument?
