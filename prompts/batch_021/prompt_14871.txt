The owners' association in my apartment building is preparing to vote on a proposal to prohibit short-term rentals such as Airbnb listings. Under the building's bylaws, only unit owners are entitled to vote, which excludes the many long-term tenants who would be directly affected by the decision: a ban could lead some owners to raise rents to recoup lost income, while its defeat could mean continued noise and turnover from short-term guests. When a group of tenants asked to be given a voice in the vote, the board replied that voting rights have always belonged to owners alone, since owners are the ones who bear the building's financial and legal liabilities. This response strikes me as too convenient, though I cannot quite articulate what is wrong with it. Do the tenants have a legitimate claim to a vote, or is the board right that this is a decision for owners alone?
