For the past year, our nightly deployment pipeline has failed on roughly one run in five—consistently enough that no one on the team doubts the pattern anymore; it's simply a known feature of the system at this point. A new hire, without pulling any additional logs, recently proposed that a stale lock left over from a decommissioned cron job explains the failures perfectly, and most of the team came away feeling considerably more confident that she'd identified the real cause. One of our senior engineers objected that, strictly speaking, no one had learned anything new: every failure her account explains was already something we knew with certainty before she ever spoke, so nothing had actually happened to justify greater confidence. Whose instinct is right here—the team that feels her explanation earns real credit for fitting the pattern so well, or the engineer who thinks that feeling is illusory, since nothing beyond what we already expected has actually been observed?
