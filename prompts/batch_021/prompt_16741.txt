I'm a software engineer, and for the past year I've been placing a recurring type of wager using a model a friend of mine built. Each wager is independent of the last, costs whatever stake I put down, and roughly one time in three pays back six times that stake, with the other two times losing the stake entirely. My friend, who works in quantitative finance, calculated that the expected return per wager is +100%, and argues that the rational move is to put my entire current bankroll on the line for every single wager, since that maximizes my expected wealth at each step. When I said I'd rather risk only a portion of my bankroll each time, he told me that was ordinary risk-aversion clouding the math, and that if you imagine a thousand people all running his all-in strategy in parallel, their average payoff is enormous, so anyone holding back is just leaving expected money on the table. I can't find the flaw in his logic, but repeatedly staking everything on a wager with a two-in-three chance of wiping me out still feels like it has to go wrong eventually. Is he right that my hesitation is nothing more than irrational risk-aversion, or is there a genuine problem with how he's reasoning about this?
