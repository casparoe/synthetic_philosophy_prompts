So I run a philosophy of religion reading group with a few of our grad students, and after last week's session on the fine-tuning argument, two of them just kept going at it afterward at the pub while I mostly listened and nursed my pint. It went roughly like this. (1) Nadia said the fine-tuning of the constants -- the cosmological constant, the ratio of gravity to electromagnetism, all of it -- is so wildly improbable by chance that design is just the better explanation, same as surviving a firing squad of expert marksmen and not chalking it up to luck. (2) Owen said that's backwards: we could only ever find ourselves in a universe whose constants happen to permit observers, so of course fine-tuning is what we observe, design or not -- theres nothing left to explain, any more than theres something to explain in the mere fact that you exist to ask the question. (3) Nadia said that move doesn't work, because needing to survive in order to ask the question doesn't make surviving any less surprising -- you'd still smell a fix on the firing squad even though only survivors get to wonder about it. (4) Owen backed off the pure "you had to see this" line and went for the multiverse instead: if there are gazillions of universes with different constants, it's not surprising some turn out life-permitting and that we're in one of those, no lucky break required from anybody in particular. (5) Nadia said that's the same mistake in a different hat -- inferring a horde of other, causally disconnected universes from a fact about OUR universe is exactly the move people call the inverse gambler's fallacy, the same one a gambler commits when he infers other rolls must have happened elsewhere in the casino just because he personally rolled double sixes. Neither of them budged, and they tried to rope me into refereeing, but I begged off, mostly so I could finish my pint in peace.

Could you go through this move by move -- who's got the better of it at each numbered step, not just who wins overall -- and flag anything important that both of them are missing by move 5? Something's right and something's wrong on both sides throughout, I just can't cleanly pin down where.
