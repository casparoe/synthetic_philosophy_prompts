Been meaning to write this up as a shortform for a while, figured I'd sanity check it with you first instead of just posting it.

So there's this idea from Oesterheld & Conitzer (I think that's the right paper — "safe Pareto improvements" for delegated game-playing) about situations where two parties are stuck in an inefficient equilibrium because of private information, and you want to modify how they play in a way that's a Pareto improvement for basically every possible type/realization of private info, without either side ever having to reveal what they actually know. The "safe" part is that you're not relying on anyone's honesty or on a mediator correctly eliciting private info like in standard mechanism design — the improvement has to hold up no matter what the other guy's type turns out to be, using only common knowledge about the type distribution, not the realization.

I've been trying to map this onto the Hicks paradox of strikes — the old puzzle that strikes are costly to both sides, and standard bargaining-under-incomplete-info models say both sides can roughly predict the eventual settlement, so a "rational" strike shouldn't happen any more than a Fearon-style rational war should (yet both do, presumably bc of private info about resolve/costs plus incentives to misrepresent it).

My proposed "safe Pareto improvement" for union/management bargaining: both sides pre-commit that the first ~5 days of any dispute get resolved via a toothless public arbitrator whose ruling is a mechanical function of publicly available stuff only (industry settlement stats, inflation, etc — NOT this specific firm's finances or the union's actual reservation wage). Only if either side rejects that ruling does an actual strike start. My hand-wavy case that this is "safe": even the union type that wouldve struck immediately anyway isnt made worse off (they just eat a short delay), while types that wouldve had a long costly strike sometimes settle at the arbitrator's number instead — so it's an improvement in expectation for every type, and it never asks anyone to reveal their reservation wage.

Things I'd love your take on: (1) does this actually satisfy the formal "safety" property, or does the accept/reject decision itself leak info about type and just relocate the signaling problem one step earlier? (2) is the strikes/Hicks-paradox mapping even a good fit for what SPIs are meant to address, or am I forcing an analogy that doesn't really work? (3) worth cleaning up into an actual post, or is this a known dead end?
