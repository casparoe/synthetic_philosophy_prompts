This semester I am taking a course in philosophy of religion, and this week's unit covered the two standard forms of the cosmological argument: the kalam argument and the version from contingency associated with Leibniz. Both, as I understand them, are ultimately trying to establish the same conclusion, that the universe cannot account for its own existence and therefore requires an external, uncaused cause, which is presumably why they tend to be taught as a pair. Given that shared aim, it seems to me that both arguments should be equally undermined by any cosmological model in which the universe, or some larger multiverse it belongs to, turns out to be past-eternal, since neither argument would then have a beginning event left to explain. When I raised this point in office hours, however, my professor maintained that the two arguments are not actually vulnerable to this worry in the same way, and despite going back through my notes, I have not been able to reconstruct why that would be true. Could you help me see what I am missing?
