I spent almost forty years as a condensed-matter physicist before I retired, and these days I've been filling the gap with philosophy of mind, which is a very different kind of rigor than what I'm used to. Right now I'm stuck on something about Giulio Tononi's Integrated Information Theory that I can't decide is the theory's best feature or a fatal flaw. As I understand it, IITs whole approach ties a system's level of consciousness (its "phi") to the intrinsic causal structure of the actual physical substrate, not to the function being computed, and one consequence is that a purely feedforward network -- no loops, information only ever flowing forward -- comes out with phi at or near zero, i.e. not conscious at all, no matter how rich it's input-output behavior is. My grandson, who works in ML, points out that most of what a large language model does inside a single forward pass is exactly this kind of feedforward computation (the token-by-token loop happens at a much coarser grain), so IIT seems to hand you a clean, principled "no" to whether an LLM could ever be conscious, regardless of how it behaves. That's the part that bugs me: it feels like you could take any garden-variety conscious recurrent system, mathematically "unfold" it in time into a feedforward system computing the identical input-output function, and now IIT is forced to say the unfolded twin lost consciousness somewhere in the unfolding even though nothing about what it actually does changed. That smells like it's proving too much, but I can also see the reply that phi was never supposed to track behavior in the first place, so of course two behaviorally identical systems can differ in it.

Rather than keep arguing with myself over coffee, could you write me a dialogue that actually stages this fight properly? One character should be a sharp, unapologetic IIT defender who's happy to bite the bullet on the feedforward/unfolding case; the other should be an equally sharp functionalist (or global-workspace type, whichever fits better) who thinks this is exactly where IIT goes off the rails. I don't want either of them to be a pushover -- they should be responding to each other's actual best moves on the unfolding trick specifically, rather than just trading the standard "IIT implies panpsychism / thermostats are conscious" talking points, and honestly it's fine, maybe even more realistic, if it doesn't end in a tidy resolution.
