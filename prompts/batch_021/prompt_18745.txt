My friend Walt died last week, 84 years old -- we'd been in the same physics department for thirty-odd years. About five years ago, right after he retired, he fell hard for this David Lewis stuff about laws of nature and dragged me into it too, which is honestly how I ended up down this philosophy rabbit hole myself. Walt was dead serious about it: the laws we spent our careers chasing, he'd say, aren't some hidden hand pushing particles around, just the best summary of everything that happens, nothing behind it holding things together. He even left a half-joking note titled "banned phrases for my funeral," and topping the list was stuff like "he's obeying higher laws now" or "it was all meant to be." I'm giving the eulogy Saturday, to a room thats half old physics colleagues who'll clock it instantly if I get this wrong, and half grandkids who just remember he loved model trains. Can you write it so its warm and genuinely comforting, doesn't sneak in any "everything happens for a reason" talk he'd have hated, and if you can, work in one joke about patterns or his beloved "mosaic" that lands for the physicists without losing the grandkids?
