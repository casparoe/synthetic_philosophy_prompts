The Doomsday Argument reasons from your own birth rank — your position in the sequence of all humans who will ever live — to the conclusion that, absent other evidence, a smaller total human population is more probable than a larger one, making imminent extinction more likely than we might otherwise think. The Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) is often presented as the natural corrective: it favors hypotheses that imply more observers in exactly your situation, on the grounds that your own existence is itself evidence for them, and is claimed to cancel the Doomsday update entirely. Explain precisely how this cancellation is supposed to work, and then assess whether adopting SIA is really the more defensible resolution, given difficulties — notably Bostrom's "presumptuous philosopher" problem — that SIA is widely thought to generate elsewhere. On balance, does the Self-Sampling Assumption behind the original argument, or SIA, better withstand scrutiny? Justify your answer.
