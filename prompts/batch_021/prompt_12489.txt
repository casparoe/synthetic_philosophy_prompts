Okay so this is a very specific relationship problem, bear with me. The part I keep getting stuck on, which I found after our second or third fight about this, is the grounding objection: before any creature exists or does anything, what actually makes it true that "if Sarah were in circumstance C she'd freely choose X" rather than false, or just not true at all yet? When I press him on it he agrees it's a real puzzle but says it isn't fatal, whereas I think it either leaves the counterfactuals floating with nothing to ground them, or it quietly imports the exact same fixity my own Calvinism just admits to upfront - in which case I genuinely don't know what libertarian free will is buying him. He, understandably, does not think either of those is what's actually going on.

We never get to finish these arguments because somebody's mom calls us in for dinner, so I'd love to see the whole thing played out properly. Could you write a dialogue between the two of us - or really, between a careful Molinist and an equally careful Reformed theological determinist - that pushes past the point where we always get stuck? If they can actually locate where the disagreement bottoms out, versus it just being two incompatible sets of intuitions that never really meet, that would be amazing - but please don't force a neat resolution if there isn't one to find.
