Ok so weird personal dilemma that's been eating at me. Back in January I was so worried about not finishing my novel that I gave my brother $3,000 in cash and told him to burn it -- actually burn it -- if I hadn't hit 80k words by June 1st. The one rule was that he could grant a single extension, but only if he was convinced I had a genuinely new reason and not just an excuse, and I told him upfront to assume by default that any reason I gave in the moment was BS, since that's exactly what someone trying to weasel out would say too. Well, its May 28th, I'm at 61k words, and honestly the book is better for going slower: I ended up restructuring the whole back half and cutting a subplot that wasn't working, which isn't something you can rush. I explained all this to my brother and he just said "that's exactly what you'd say if you were lying to yourself," and won't budge. Part of me thinks he's right to hold the line, since the whole point was that I wouldn't trust my own judgment in the moment. But part of me also thinks a rule that can never tell a real reason apart from a fake one isn't much of a safeguard, it's just a coin flip wearing a disguise. Who's actually right here, him or me?
