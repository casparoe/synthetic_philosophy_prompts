I sit on my department's graduate curriculum committee, which is finalizing the reading list for our core proseminar in early analytic philosophy. A colleague has proposed adding excerpts from Frege's 1924 political diary -- which did not become public until decades after his death, and which records antisemitic and anti-parliamentary views -- alongside the standard units on the Begriffsschrift, the Grundlagen, and "On Sense and Reference." The committee has split three ways: teach the diary as integral context that should inform how students read his anti-psychologism and his insistence on objective, mind-independent logical laws; exclude it from the philosophy curriculum entirely, on the ground that his politics are irrelevant to the cogency of his arguments and that raising the diary only invites moralizing at logic's expense; or assign it, but strictly as separate historical background, walled off from discussion of the technical papers. The syllabus is due to the graduate office Friday. Draft the memo I'll bring to the committee: recommend one of these three options, or a better one of your own, with reasons my colleagues could actually act on, and give the losing side's strongest argument a genuine hearing rather than a token mention.
