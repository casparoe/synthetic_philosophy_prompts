I sit on my state's tax appeals board. Last year the legislature recodified the tax code as a pure reorganization -- no substantive changes, per the committee report -- and somewhere in the shuffle the surcharge exemption for disabled veterans lost the word "not," so the enacted text now literally says the surcharge "shall apply" to them, the opposite of every earlier version of the code. A disabled vet in front of us got hit with the surcharge and wants the exemption honored anyway, since the error seems obvious; the revenue department's lawyer is making the textualist case that we have to apply the text as actually enacted, that fixing typos is the legislatures job and not ours, and that once a board starts correcting "obvious" mistakes there's no principled place to stop. Both sides have a real point. Write the short opinion I'd circulate recommending how the board should rule and why -- and seriously engage with whichever side's argument you end up rejecting rather than waving it off.
