Reading my way through Eliezer Yudkowsky's sequence on free will years ago left me fairly convinced that the whole debate dissolves once you accept that a choice just is a deterministic (or stochastic) physical computation, and that praise and blame survive as useful social practices rather than as trackers of some deeper metaphysical fact. Next month, for a small philosophy podcast, I'm interviewing a philosopher who defends Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: the claim that true moral responsibility is impossible because it would require you to have chosen the mental character that produces your choices, which would in turn require having already chosen the character that produced that choice, and so on in a regress that never bottoms out. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond a restatement of the regress: include at least one question on what the regress adds beyond an ordinary hard-determinist argument, given that Strawson insists it holds even if indeterminism is true; one asking how she would handle P. F. Strawson's reactive-attitudes naturalism in "Freedom and Resentment," the classic route to making peace with responsibility-talk regardless of how the determinism question is settled; and one putting Yudkowsky-style "dissolve the question, keep praise and blame as useful fictions" compatibilism to her directly, asking whether that actually answers the regress or just changes the subject. For each question, add a short follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give.
