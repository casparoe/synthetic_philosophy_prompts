So this happened at a friend's birthday last weekend and it's been bugging me way more than it probably should. Eight of us were trying to pick a restaurant and someone pulled out one of those ranked-choice voting apps instead of letting everyone just yell over eachother — you rank your top three, it runs an instant runoff behind the scenes, eliminating whoever's in last place each round and redistributing their ballots, the whole pitch being that nobody has any reason to vote for anything but their honest favorite. Two of the options were a sushi place and a burger spot that are basically rivals in our group, plus a pizza place everyone was lukewarm on. Sushi was genuinely my favorite so that's what I ranked first, and the pizza place ended up winning. Out of curiosity I reconstructed the rounds afterward and realized that if I'd insincerely ranked pizza first instead of sushi, sushi actually would have won. So voting honestly handed the win to my second choice, while lying about my favorite would've gotten me my actual favorite. Which feels like exactly the opposite of what the app was supposed to guarantee.

I've written a couple of explainers on cities switching to ranked-choice voting specifically as a cure for this kind of strategic-voting problem, so now I'm wondering if I got something wrong. Is what happened at dinner just a quirk of instant-runoff in particular, something a cleverer counting method could design away, or is there some deeper reason why any voting system whatsoever — however carefully constructed — has to leave an opening for someone to benefit from voting dishonestly, the moment you've got three or more options and more than one voter? And if it really is unavoidable, is that something settled and well understood in some field already, or still genuinely contested?
