My aunt died a couple of weeks ago after 40 years as a large-animal vet, and she was also, weirdly, a total Kant die-hard -- she'd cheerfully tell you over dinner that her patients werent "ends in themselves" and that cruelty to animals is only wrong because of what it does to us, not because it wrongs the animal itself. And yet she was the softest touch I've ever known: drove an hour through a blizzard for a lame goat, grieved for weeks over the ones she lost. I cover science and ideas for a living, so I've naturally been elected to write the eulogy, for a room that'll be half farmers and old clients, half her philosophy reading group. Please write it so it actually reckons with the Kant honestly -- not fudging her view to make her sound warmer than she was, but also not leaving people thinking she saw animals as means to an end, because she plainly never treated them that way.
