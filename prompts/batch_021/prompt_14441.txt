So this argument keeps coming up at our weekly poker game and nobody will let it die. One of the guys is really into gold and basically thinks the whole monetary system's been a scam since we went off any kind of gold standard — to him, a gold coin is "real" money because your holding actual value in your hand, and everything else, dollars but especially crypto, is just people agreeing to pretend numbers mean something. Another guy, who got into crypto a few years back, says that's a weirdly arbitrary place to draw the line: gold is only worth anything because people collectively treat it as valuable, which is the exact same mechanism that makes a dollar bill or a bitcoin worth something, so if fiat and crypto are "fake" for that reason then gold should count as fake too, and the gold guy is just sentimentally attached to shiny rocks he can't even do anything with.

I can't fully agree with either side, but I can't quite articulate why. It feels like they've both accepted the same background assumption — that whether money is "real" comes down to either (a) is it a physical thing with intrinsic value, or (b) is it just a social agreement with nothing behind it — and I have this nagging suspicion that the framing itself is the problem, rather than one guy being right and the other wrong within it. Like, shouldn't the question be less about what the money is made of (or not made of) and more about what it actually lets people do — get food grown, get houses built, get stuff moved around — and whether it's doing that job well? Is there a more basic way to think about what makes something count as money that cuts through this whole argument, or is it genuinely just down to personal taste which of gold, dollars, or crypto you happen to trust?
