Okay, this is going to sound like a niche craft complaint but bear with me. I've been serializing a fantasy-romance on one of those episodic web-fiction apps for about a year and a half now — the kind where your pay, and how hard the app's algorithm pushes your book to new readers, is tied directly to a "completion rate": basically what percentage of people who start a chapter actually make it to the end before bailing.

My numbers were pretty mediocre for the first few months, so a friend who's done a lot better than me on the platform sat down and basically reverse-engineered the metric with me: keep chapters under 1,500 words, end literally every single one on a cliffhanger (fake ones included, just cut the scene off mid-sentence if you have to), bring the love-triangle guy back every three chapters because the data shows a spike whenever he shows up, that sort of thing. I tried it for my last dozen chapters and it worked, no question — completion numbers jumped something like 40%, way more algorithm placement, actual money for once.

Here's what's bugging me though. My reviews have gotten weirdly split — a bunch of readers are now saying the constant cliffhangers feel exhausting or even kind of manipulative, and yet according to the app's own numbers, those same readers are "completing" chapters at a higher rate then they ever did before. So the thing the platform is measuring keeps climbing, but I've got this nagging feeling that the actual thing the measurement was supposed to stand in for — people genuinely loving the book — might be drifting the opposite way, or has at least come loose from the number entirely. My partner's whole take is "the numbers are the numbers, and numbers are money, quit overthinking it," and I don't really have a comeback for that, but it still doesn't sit right. Am I just being precious about my art here, or is there an actual reason not to trust a number that's going up?
