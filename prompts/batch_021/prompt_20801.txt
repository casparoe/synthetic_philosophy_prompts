I'm a retired physicist who's fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole since retiring, and lately its been Leibniz. He dreamed of a calculus ratiocinator - get the right formal notation and even moral or metaphysical disputes get settled by calculation instead of argument, lets calculate and be done with it. Separately there's his theodicy, God surveying every possible world and picking the optimal one.

Here's what's bugging me: Leibniz never had P vs NP or NP-hardness to contend with, but plenty of optimization problems have exactly that shape - cheap to check a candidate, brutal to find the best one, and "nothing anywhere in the whole space beats this" seems even nastier than a garden-variety NP-hard problem. Would he see that as a real crack in the rationalist project, or does he get a clean theological escape hatch (God isn't resource-bounded like us)? Does that same find-vs-check gap quietly sink the calculemus dream even with God left out of it?
