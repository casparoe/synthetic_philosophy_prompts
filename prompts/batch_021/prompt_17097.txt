Carnap's relationship to politics is usually flattened into the vague claim that "the Vienna Circle leaned left." His actual commitments were more specific. He was a close collaborator of Otto Neurath, who argued that a planned economy could be rationally administered through direct calculation in physical quantities rather than through money prices, and who defended this claim against Mises's and Hayek's argument that planning without price signals faces an insurmountable knowledge problem. At the same time, Carnap's own logical empiricism denied that value and "ought" statements carry the kind of cognitive content that empirical statements do, so that no planning body could simply discover the correct social objective the way it discovers an empirical fact. After emigrating to the United States, Carnap and most of his colleagues largely stopped making this sort of argument in public, recasting logical empiricism as a neutral, purely technical philosophy of science.

Suppose Carnap were confronted today with proposals to use large-scale machine learning to finally deliver what Neurath promised and Hayek denied was possible: planning without prices, backed by enough data and compute that the old knowledge problem is said to dissolve. Reasoning from Carnap's specific, actual commitments rather than from a generic "logical positivist" stereotype, what do you think he would have said, both about the technical claim that computation can substitute for prices and about the deeper question of where such a system's objective function could derive its normative authority, given his own account of value statements?
