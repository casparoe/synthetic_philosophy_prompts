I'm a backend software engineer, not a philosopher, but over the past year I've gotten pretty absorbed in AI safety material in my spare time, partly because my team has started building products on top of large language models and I wanted to understand the debates informing that work. One idea that keeps coming up, in slightly different forms, is the "coherence argument" for expected utility maximization: the claim that any agent whose preferences violate certain consistency conditions—say, preferring A to B, B to C, but C to A—can be exploited by a sequence of trades that extracts resources from it while leaving it no better off, a so-called money pump. Several explainers I've read walk through this alongside the analogous Dutch book argument for why credences have to obey the probability axioms, and then generalize the pattern into a proof that any agent sophisticated enough to avoid this kind of exploitation will necessarily end up behaving exactly as if it is maximizing the expectation of some fixed, real-valued utility function over outcomes.

I brought this up with a couple of coworkers who work more directly on ML research, expecting them to shrug and say "yeah, that's the standard result," but instead they seemed oddly reluctant to grant how much weight the argument can bear, which surprised me given how airtight the money-pump reasoning seemed on first read. If avoiding exploitability really does force any sufficiently capable optimizer—human, firm, or AI system—into the expected-utility mold, it seems like this should have settled the ongoing disagreement in the AI safety community over whether advanced AI systems will necessarily be "goal-directed" in the strong, utility-maximizing sense that motivates worries about instrumental convergence and power-seeking; the only question left would be which utility function a given system happens to end up with, not whether it has one at all. So why do people who clearly understand the argument still treat AI goal-directedness as an open question rather than as something the coherence theorems have already settled?
