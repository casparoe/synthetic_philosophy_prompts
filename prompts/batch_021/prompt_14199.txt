In 1690, Manuel Fernández de Santa Cruz, the Bishop of Puebla, published Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz's closely argued critique of a sermon by the Portuguese Jesuit Antônio Vieira, prefacing it with a letter of his own — signed under the pseudonym "Sor Filotea de la Cruz" — that praised her gifts but urged her to set aside secular study for pursuits more fitting a nun. Her reply, the Respuesta a Sor Filotea de la Cruz (1691), remains the fullest statement of her conviction that women's claim on the sciences and letters is equal to men's.

Write a dialogue between Sor Juana and "Sor Filotea" in which each presses her strongest case and answers the other's actual objections rather than talking past one another. "Sor Filotea" should argue in earnest, from within Catholic tradition, for the priority of sacred over secular learning and for the virtue of humility — not from crude prejudice. Sor Juana should deploy her real arguments: her reading of St. Paul's injunction on women's silence, her claim that secular knowledge is a "handmaid" without which theology cannot be properly understood, and her appeal to precedents of learned women in sacred history. Let neither side simply concede.
