In my philosophy of mind seminar, we're covering Chalmers's zombie argument against physicalism: since it's conceivable that there could be a being physically identical to me in every respect but lacking any conscious experience, Chalmers infers that such a zombie is metaphysically possible, and therefore that physical facts don't necessitate phenomenal facts. But earlier in the term we covered Kripke's point that "water is H2O" is necessarily true even though it's perfectly conceivable that water could have turned out to be something other than H2O, which is usually presented as showing that conceivability doesn't entail possibility. Doesn't this Kripkean point immediately undercut Chalmers's whole argument, since the zombie scenario seems to be exactly the same kind of case—something conceivable that we have independent reason to think isn't really possible? I'm confused why so much of the literature treats the zombie argument as a serious, still-open challenge to physicalism if it can be dismissed this easily via Kripke.
