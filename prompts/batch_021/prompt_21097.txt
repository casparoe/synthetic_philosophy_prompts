I'm putting together a lecture on medieval resistance theory and want to confirm a point before presenting it to my graduate seminar. As I understand Aquinas's position, once a ruler governs for his own advantage rather than the common good, he thereby forfeits his authority altogether and is reduced to the status of a private criminal, so that any individual subject may licitly kill him in defense of the community -- in effect, the same general permission that Juan de Mariana would later defend explicitly. Could you reconstruct the natural-law argument behind this license as Aquinas actually gives it, and specify what conditions, if any, he places on a private citizen's exercise of it?
