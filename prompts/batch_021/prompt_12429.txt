I'm taking a seminar this term on the philosophical foundations of AI alignment, and we recently covered inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) as a method for inferring an agent's reward function from its observed behavior rather than specifying that function by hand. One point that has stuck with me is that IRL is underdetermined in a fairly deep way: not only is a constant reward function trivially consistent with any observed policy, but even once that degenerate case is set aside, a given body of behavioral data can usually still be explained equally well by very different combinations of what the agent values and how it is biased or mistaken in pursuing what it values, so that behavior alone never pins down a unique reward function. Could you describe the most important implications of this identifiability problem for projects that treat IRL, or similar behavior-based methods, as a way of learning people's genuine underlying preferences, whether for AI alignment specifically or for adjacent uses such as recommender systems? I am especially interested in how much normative or interpretive work ends up being done by the modeler's background assumptions rather than by the behavioral data itself.
