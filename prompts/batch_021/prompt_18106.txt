"Epistemic akrasia is a state where someone believes P but also believes that they shouldn't believe P, given their evidence — its basically a purely epistemic version of the classic akrasia where you judge you should do one thing but do another anyway. A good illustration is Christensen's case: you carefully work out how to split a dinner bill and feel very confident in your answer, then you're told you were secretly given a drug that impairs arithmetic ability in a large percentage of people who take it. When you check back over your calculation it still looks perfect, so you have first-order evidence that the answer is right, but you now also have higher-order evidence — evidence about how reliable your reasoning is likely to be — suggesting you shouldn't trust that answer.

Cases like this put pressure on the Enkratic Principle, which says that rationality requires believing whatever your total evidence supports. The problem is that your first-order evidence (the calculation) and your higher-order evidence (the drug) seem to support different doxastic states, so it looks like the rational thing to do is to end up akratic: fully confident in your answer, while also believing, on the basis of the drug evidence, that this confidence isn't justified. If that's right, the Enkratic Principle is simply false, since the Principle says being in that state is irrational, and yet the case shows a rational agent's total evidence can force them into exactly that state.

A defender of the Enkratic Principle would resist this by saying the case is misdescribed: the higher-order evidence about the drug doesn't just sit alongside the first-order evidence, it's additional evidence that folds into your total evidence bearing on the arithmetic conclusion itself, so a rational agent lowers her confidence in the answer directly, instead of staying fully confident and just tacking on a separate belief that she shouldn't be so confident. Once you do that there's no more akrasia, because the object-level belief and the higher-order belief line back up."
