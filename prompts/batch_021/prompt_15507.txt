In the centipede game, backward induction implies that the first player should defect at the earliest opportunity, yet experimental subjects reliably cooperate for several rounds before finally taking the pot. Since this mismatch disappears once we grant that real players care about fairness and reciprocity rather than pure monetary self-interest, doesn't building such motives into the payoffs fully resolve the paradox of backward induction, leaving no further philosophical puzzle about the rationality of this solution concept?
