As part of my dissertation in philosophy of psychiatry, I have spent the past year working through Ian Hacking's account of the looping effect: his observation that classifying a kind of person, unlike classifying a kind of mineral or subatomic particle, can change the people so classified, and that this change can in turn revise the classification itself. His own illustrations of the mechanism — multiple personality disorder, autism, the "discovery" of child abuse — are familiar to the point of cliché by now, so I would like to see the idea tested on less familiar ground. Please write a short story, set wherever you find fitting, built around a different psychiatric classification (real, historical, or plausibly invented) in which a diagnosis reshapes how a character understands and enacts their condition, and that reshaping feeds back to unsettle the category itself among the clinicians, institutions, or communities that sustain it. Let the loop emerge through plot and character rather than through anyone explaining the theory outright.
