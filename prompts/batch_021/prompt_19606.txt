I am a PhD student working on the philosophical foundations of social convention, and I would like to see a specific tension in this literature worked out as a dialogue rather than left as two papers talking past each other. David Lewis analyzes a convention as a regularity in a recurring coordination problem that persists only because it is common knowledge among the parties that everyone expects everyone else to conform, and because some other regularity would have served their interests just as well. Brian Skyrms later modeled Lewis's own signaling (sender-receiver) games using replicator dynamics and simple reinforcement learning, and found that populations can settle into these same arbitrary, self-perpetuating conventions even though none of the agents involved ever represents the alternatives, let alone achieves common knowledge of them. Write a short dialogue between a committed Lewisian, who insists that whatever has evolved in Skyrms's models, it is not a convention absent that cognitive structure, and a Skyrmsian, who maintains that Lewis's epistemic conditions were never doing the explanatory work he thought they were doing. The two should engage each other's strongest arguments rather than talk past one another, and neither should concede more than their position can actually afford to.
