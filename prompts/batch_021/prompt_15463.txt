Ok, I need a sanity check on something from my kid's school raffle last month -- I cover science for a living so I usually trust my own instincts here, but not this time.

There were two seperate raffles that night. In the first, you could take a guaranteed $200 gift card, or a ticket with an 80% shot at that same $200 card, a 15% shot at a $1,000 voucher, and a 5% shot at nothing -- nearly the whole line, me included, took the guaranteed card. In the second raffle, you chose between a ticket with a 20% shot at the $200 card (80% nothing) or one with a 15% shot at the $1,000 voucher (85% nothing) -- and this time almost everyone, me included, went for the voucher instead.

My brother-in-law, an actuary who cant help himself, worked out afterward that these two picks don't actually hang together: whatever makes someone favor the guaranteed card so strongly in raffle one should make them favor the safer ticket again in raffle two, and there's no consistent way of valuing the prizes that gets you both of my actual picks at once.

So did a whole line of normal adults just mess up the math, or is he missing something about why "guaranteed" feels different in kind from "pretty likely," even though the prizes themselves never changed? And if nearly everyone falls into the same pattern, is that a fact about people, or about the theory grading us?
