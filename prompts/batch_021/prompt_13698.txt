I'm a teaching assistant for a graduate course that covers Zorn's Lemma and its standard uses in algebra, and I'm currently grading the take-home portion of this term's qualifying-exam practice set. One of the problems is the following: Let R be a commutative ring with 1 ≠ 0, and let I be a proper ideal of R (i.e., I ≠ R). Using Zorn's Lemma, prove that there exists a maximal ideal M of R such that I ⊆ M.

Below is the solution one student submitted. A couple of other students in the class took a visibly similar approach, so before I record any grades I'd like to pin down exactly where the argument does and doesn't succeed, rather than rely on a holistic impression.

"Let F be the set of all proper ideals of R, partially ordered by inclusion. Since I ≠ R, we have I ∈ F, so F is nonempty. Let C be a chain in F, and put J = ⋃_{A∈C} A. If a, b ∈ J, then a ∈ A and b ∈ A′ for some A, A′ ∈ C; since C is totally ordered, one of A, A′ contains the other, say A ⊆ A′, so a, b ∈ A′ and hence a + b ∈ A′ ⊆ J. Similarly, for r ∈ R and a ∈ J, a lies in some A ∈ C, so ra ∈ A ⊆ J. Thus J is an ideal. Moreover, since each A ∈ C is proper, 1 ∉ A for every A ∈ C, so 1 ∉ J; hence J ≠ R, i.e., J ∈ F, and J is clearly an upper bound for C. Since every chain in F has an upper bound in F, Zorn's Lemma guarantees that F has a maximal element M. Now, M is a maximal ideal of R: if N is an ideal with M ⊊ N ⊊ R, then N is itself a proper ideal properly containing M, so N ∈ F, contradicting the maximality of M in F. Hence there is no ideal strictly between M and R, so M is a maximal ideal. Finally, I is a proper ideal of R, so I ∈ F, and since M is the maximal element of F produced above, we conclude I ⊆ M. Moreover, since the argument never used any special feature of I beyond its membership in F, this M is the unique maximal ideal of R containing I. This completes the proof."
