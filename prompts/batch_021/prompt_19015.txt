The question on the pset was: "Let R be a commutative ring with 1 ≠ 0, and let I be a proper ideal of R. Prove that I is contained in a maximal ideal of R. You may use Zorn's Lemma, but you must explicitly verify its hypotheses."

Here's the student's answer, more or less word for word:

"Proof. Let Σ be the set of all proper ideals of R that contain I, partially ordered by inclusion. Since I is itself proper, I ∈ Σ, so Σ is nonempty.

Zorn's Lemma says that if every chain in a poset has an upper bound in the poset, then the poset has a maximal element. So let {J_i} be a chain in Σ, meaning a collection of members of Σ that are pairwise comparable under inclusion. Because the J_i are totally ordered by inclusion, they can't just keep growing forever without bound within Σ: being a chain means precisely that for any two of them, one contains the other, so among the J_i there is a largest ideal J*, and J* is automatically an upper bound for the whole chain. J* is one of the J_i, so it's still proper and still contains I, meaning J* ∈ Σ.

Since every chain in Σ has an upper bound in Σ, the hypotheses of Zorn's Lemma are satisfied, so Σ has a maximal element M.

Finally, M is not just maximal in Σ but a genuinely maximal ideal of R: suppose M ⊆ N for some ideal N ⊊ R. Then N is proper and N ⊇ M ⊇ I, so N ∈ Σ, and maximality of M in Σ forces N = M. So no proper ideal strictly contains M, and M is the maximal ideal of R containing I."
