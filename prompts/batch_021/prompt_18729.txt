My next novel follows a dealer who fleeces collectors with a classic money pump: a cycle of swaps among three pieces the mark ranks A over B, B over C, and C over A -- the dealer skimming a small fee at every trade -- until the mark is broke and holding exactly what he started with. To mark the book's publication, I'm hosting a public conversation with a decision theorist who has spent her career defending 'resolute choice' -- the view that rationality is a property of a whole plan pursued across time rather than of each choice taken in isolation, so that escaping this kind of pump requires not transitive preferences but a standing disposition to see a chosen plan through even when deviating would look better in the moment. Draft seven or eight interview questions that don't let her simply restate the position. I want one question on whether a merely 'sophisticated' chooser -- who predicts the cycle and declines the last trade without ever revising his ranking -- already escapes the pump without any resolute commitment at all; one on whether her resolve just is a further, transitive preference for plan-keeping under another name; one on whether a sufficiently clever operator could build a second pump that targets the resolution itself rather than the original cycle; and, to close, one on what outcome would make her abandon the view. Give each a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to offer.
