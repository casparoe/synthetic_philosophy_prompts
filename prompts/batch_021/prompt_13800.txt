I take Yudkowsky's Sequences argument about "emergence" as basically settling the matter: calling a phenomenon an emergent property instead of naming its actual mechanism is a curiosity-stopper dressed up as an explanation, no different in kind from invoking phlogiston, and it buys no more predictive power than you had before you heard the word. A friend who works in condensed-matter theory pushed back on this recently, describing how renormalization-group methods explain why microscopically different systems converge on identical critical exponents near a phase transition, and calling the resulting universality genuinely "emergent." I assumed she was just running the same move in fancier notation, but she insists the two claims aren't remotely comparable. Is there actually a principled distinction here, or is "emergent" doing the same explanatory-placeholder work no matter what it's dressed in?
