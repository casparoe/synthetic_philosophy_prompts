At the software company I work for, a critical security patch went undeployed over a long holiday weekend, and an attacker exploited the resulting vulnerability, causing significant financial damage. Five engineers had equal access and equal ability to deploy the patch that weekend, and none of them did; the only relevant difference between them is that one happened to be the officially designated on-call engineer for that week. Management is now treating that person as the one who caused the breach and is holding them accountable for it, while the other four argue this is arbitrary, since their own failure to act was just as complete. Did the on-call engineer's failure to deploy the patch cause the breach in some way that the others' failure did not, or is the company wrong to single out one person as uniquely responsible?
