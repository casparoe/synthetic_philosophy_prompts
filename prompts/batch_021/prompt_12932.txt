Write me a dialogue between a Bayesian epistemologist who defends Dutch Book arguments and a skeptic who thinks the whole thing is a pragmatic trick dressed up as an epistemic one. The Bayesian wants to use the fact that a clever bookie could hand an incoherent agent a guaranteed loss to show that having credences that violate the probablity axioms is just irrational, full stop. The skeptic keeps coming back with: all that's been shown is that a certain package of bets is a bad idea, not that the beliefs behind them are defective, and that getting from "exploitable by a bookie" to "irrational belief" takes a further bridge premise that's doing a lot of unacknowledged work. Push them to actually engage with each other's objections instead of just restating their positions, and end it on whether the argument even needs a bookie and real bets at all, or whether that's just theater dressing up a plain logical inconsistency.
