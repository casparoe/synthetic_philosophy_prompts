I was reporting a piece on why ultra-rich people buy these ludicrous superyachts (one had its own submarine garage, I am not joking) and I dragged two friends to a yacht-show cocktail thing with me for colour. My friend Alina, who's mid-PhD in behavioural ecology, and her boyfriend Ravi, who's more of a econ/game-theory guy, ended up in a genuinely heated argument about the handicap principle that I havent been able to stop turning over since.

It went something like this. (1) Ravi said the yacht is basically a peacock's tail: a Zahavi-style handicap signal, and the wastefulness is the whole point, since only someone truly loaded could set that much money on fire and still be fine. (2) Alina said that's a common misreading — Grafen's actual formal version of the handicap principle doesn't require the signal to be wasteful in some absolute sense, just that the marginal cost of signalling falls off faster for low-quality individuals than high-quality ones; a peacock's tail is a handicap because it also directly hurts survival odds (harder to fly, easier to catch), which the yacht mostly doesn't do to a billionaire's fitness, financial or biological. (3) Ravi said okay, but the yacht clearly is differentially costly in exactly her sense too: a poor person can't fake owning one at any price, so what's actually doing different work here? (4) Alina answered that "literally can't fake it" is the signature of a different mechanism entirely — what Maynard Smith and Harper call an index, which stays reliable for structural reasons (like how a roar's pitch is physically tied to body size) and doesn't need any cost differential to stay honest; she said the yacht is closer to an index, or maybe just an ordinary Spence-style costly signal, than to a Zahavian handicap. (5) Ravi said that distinction doesn't actually buy you anything, since any index can be redescribed as "a trait that's maximally costly to fake," so the categories just collapse into each other. (6) Alina said no — the reason biologists keep them separate is that an index stays honest even if the cost of cheating dropped to zero, because the constraint is physical, whereas a handicap only stays honest because of the cost itself; she admitted she wasn't 100% sure the yacht actually passes that test.

Neither of them would give an inch, and honestly I could see both sides.
