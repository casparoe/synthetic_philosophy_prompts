I was just named one of three co-trustees of my grandmother's living trust, alongside two cousins, after she died unexpectedly this spring. Her trust leaves 'my emerald ring' specifically to our cousin Elena, but two years before her death, my grandmother had the ring's gold band melted down and the same emerald reset into a pendant because arthritis had made rings uncomfortable to wear; she never amended the trust. Our attorney says neither the trust's language nor our state's case law settles whether the pendant fulfills that gift or the gift has failed, leaving Elena only a residuary share, and the trust requires the three of us to agree unanimously on the point. One co-trustee insists a pendant is plainly not a ring, whatever it is made of; the other thinks denying Elena the very stone and gold grandmother wore for sixty years, merely because a jeweler reshaped it, is pure formalism. We must finalize distributions within the month, so please draft the memo I will circulate recommending how to resolve this and why, one that takes whichever position we end up rejecting seriously enough to actually engage it rather than just asserting it is wrong.
