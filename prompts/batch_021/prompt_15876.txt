And here's the student's answer in full:

"(a) The Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem says that once there are at least three voters, any social choice function that is (i) defined on the full domain of possible preference profiles, (ii) onto, meaning every alternative can be the winner for some profile, and (iii) strategy-proof, meaning no voter can ever secure a better outcome for themselves by misreporting their preferences, must be dictatorial: there is some fixed voter whose top choice is chosen no matter what everyone else reports. Contrapositively, any non-dictatorial rule satisfying (i) and (ii) must be manipulable by somebody at some profile.

(b) The usual proof goes through Arrow's theorem. Given a strategy-proof, onto social choice function, you build a full social ranking of the alternatives out of it by looking at what the function does on each pairwise restriction of the domain, and one shows the derived ranking is transitive and satisfies both the Pareto condition and independence of irrelevant alternatives. Arrow's theorem then forces that ranking to be dictatorial, and the dictator for the ranking turns out to be a dictator for the original choice function too. So the two theorems are really the same phenomenon in different clothes.

(c) A standard example is the median voter rule for picking a point on a line (say, a budget level, or the site of a public facility): if every voter has single-peaked preferences and reports their ideal point honestly, the rule outputs the median of the reported points. It's strategy-proof, since misreporting your peak either leaves the median where it is or drags it away from your true ideal, never closer, and it's the textbook illustration of how restricting the domain to single-peaked preferences lets you get around Gibbard–Satterthwaite. Though honestly, I think this example is a little oversold as a real counterexample: whoever happens to be the median voter for a given profile gets exactly their ideal point implemented, so there's still always exactly one person dictating the outcome -- it's just that the identity of that person can shift from profile to profile."
