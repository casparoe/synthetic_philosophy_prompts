Ok, this is going to sound like a very "extremely online rationalist" question, because that's exactly what it is. I've been rereading old threads on Bostrom's simulation argument lately -- the trilemma where either (1) almost no civilizations reach a posthuman stage capable of running ancestor-simulations, (2) almost none of the ones that do bother running them, or (3) we're almost certainly living in one right now -- and I got nerd-sniped by a side issue about the anthropic reasoning holding the whole thing up. In the Doomsday Argument literature everyone brings up how switching from the Self-Sampling Assumption to the Self-Indication Assumption basically kills the argument, since SIA is biased toward hypotheses that imply more total observers, which cancels out the doom-y update you get from SSA. I have a vague memory, source long since lost, that the exact same move does NOT save you from the simulation argument and might even make things worse instead of better, but I brought it up in a rationalist-adjacent discord a while back and just got hand-wavy answers, and honestly on reflection I can't tell if that's a real result or just a vibes-based analogy that doesnt actually survive contact with the math.

So here's what I want: please actually build the argument, carefully, for the claim that moving from SSA to SIA does not let us escape into disjunct 1 or 2 the way it supposedly rescues the Doomsday Argument from its pessimistic conclusion, and that if anything it pushes credence further toward disjunct 3. I'd especially like you to address whether this whole line of reasoning secretly collapses into a repackaged version of Bostrom's own Presumptuous Philosopher case (the one where a philosopher uses SIA from the armchair to declare that the universe must contain way more observers than physicists think, purely because that hypothesis implies more observers existing). If it does collapse into that, it seems like it should count against trusting this style of reasoning rather than for it, so I'd like to know if there's a principled way to keep the two cases apart, or if we're just stuck biting a weird bullet either way.
