Two of my favorite former grad students, Mira and Dan, are getting married in three weeks and asked me to do the wedding toast. They actually got together by spending a whole semester in my phil of science seminar going at each other -- Mira's spent her career since defending strong emergence and downward causation (Anderson's "more is different," the works), Dan's a diehard Nagel-style reductionist who thinks "emergent" mostly means "haven't found the bridge laws yet." Half the room is our phil of science crowd who'll clock it instantly if I fudge the disagreement, the other half is family who've never heard the word "supervenience." I definately don't want to pretend the debate's secretly fake, or that love "transcends" it in some cheap emergentist hand-wave -- Dan would never forgive me, and its not even honest. Can you write me a five-minute toast that treats their disagreement as real, uses it structurally rather than as a garnish, and still lands warmly for the people who have no idea what any of this means?
