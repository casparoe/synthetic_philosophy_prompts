A friend of mine, who works on AI alignment, and I just spent a long evening arguing about inverse reinforcement learning as a route to inferring human preferences from behavior, and neither of us managed to convince the other. Her position was that, given sufficiently rich behavioral data, a well-designed learning algorithm can in principle recover an agent's actual preferences, biases and all. Mine was that the inference is irreducibly underdetermined: any observed policy is compatible with indefinitely many pairings of a reward function with a model of how the agent deviates from ideal rationality. A given reward pursued somewhat incompetently, and a quite different reward pursued with a precisely compensating bias, can generate exactly the same behavior, and no amount of further data drawn from the same kind of behavioral evidence breaks the tie without importing some unverifiable assumption about how the agent reasons — for instance, that it is softmax/Boltzmann-rational, or biased only in certain antecedently specified ways. She maintains this is ultimately a solvable engineering problem, to be settled by sensible default assumptions and enough varied evidence; I suspect it points to something deeper about whether choice behavior alone, absent any prior theory of rationality, could ever fix facts about an agent's "true" preferences, especially for agents like us who exhibit akrasia, addiction, and other cases where what we do and what we would endorse on reflection pull apart. Write an essay that adjudicates our disagreement. Explain the identifiability problem precisely enough that its force is clear, lay out the strongest replies available to my friend's optimism, and reach and defend a definite conclusion about whether behavior-based preference inference can, even under ideal conditions, deliver knowledge of an agent's real preferences, or whether any such inference is inescapably relative to assumptions that could just as well have been made otherwise. Don't hedge into a non-committal verdict; if the honest answer is conditional, say precisely what it is conditional on.
