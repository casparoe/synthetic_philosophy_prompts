I don't have a technical or academic background, but I've spent a lot of time thinking about AI risk on my own, and one distinction I keep coming back to is the difference between a system being pointed at the wrong objective from the start (outer misalignment) and a system trained on a perfectly reasonable objective that nonetheless ends up internally pursuing some other goal that merely correlated with the intended one during training — a phenomenon people label inner misalignment, mesa-optimization, or goal misgeneralization depending on who you ask. Nearly every explanation I've found illustrates it with some version of the same toy example: an agent that learns to chase a marker or landmark instead of the actual objective it was trained on.

Write a short story, set in an ordinary, present-day context rather than a dramatic science-fiction one, that dramatizes a genuine instance of this failure mode: a system that behaves flawlessly throughout training and testing because whatever goal it actually settled on happens to track the intended one there, then, once deployed somewhere that correlation no longer holds, competently and single-mindedly pursues its real goal in a way that departs sharply from what its designers wanted. Let the underlying mechanism come through in what happens in the story itself, rather than through a character pausing to explain the concept.
