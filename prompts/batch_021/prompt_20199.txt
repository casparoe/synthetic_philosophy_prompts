This term I am taking a political philosophy seminar that has spent several sessions on twentieth-century debates about truth, propaganda, and public life, which has left me unexpectedly attentive to something going on in my university's student government, where I have served as a general council member for the past year. A situation there has been bothering me for a few months, and I would value an outside perspective on what is actually happening and why it might matter more than it first appears to.

Last fall, when club funding was cut noticeably compared to previous years, the executive board's response was fairly unremarkable: they simply avoided drawing attention to the decrease in the weekly newsletter, while in closed session everyone continued to plan around the real, smaller figures. That struck me as ordinary, if slightly cynical, political management. Since the start of this semester, though, something has shifted. The president has taken to telling council, apparently quite sincerely, that funding is "more flexible than it has ever been" and that event turnout is "at an all-time high," claims that do not match the spreadsheet I help maintain, which shows both figures essentially flat or declining. Committee budgets and event plans are now being drawn up around this rosier picture rather than around the actual numbers, and when I have pointed out the discrepancy, the president has not reacted the way I would expect from someone caught in a lie: she seems genuinely puzzled that anyone would bring the spreadsheet into it, as though it were beside the point.

What I cannot work out is whether this second phase is really different in kind from the first, or simply the same low-grade dishonesty grown more brazen. Is there something specifically more worrying about a leader who seems to have talked herself into believing a story the numbers contradict, as opposed to one who is knowingly managing what people are told? And should that distinction change how the rest of us on council respond, given that the usual advice to "just tell the truth" seems to presuppose a kind of deliberate deception that may no longer be quite what is happening?
