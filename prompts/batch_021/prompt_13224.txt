I'm taking a graduate seminar on fundamentality and metaphysical structure this term, and over the last few weeks we've worked through Jonathan Schaffer's "On What Grounds What" alongside Kit Fine's "Guide to Ground" and the more formal treatment in his paper "The Pure Logic of Ground." Both are routinely cited together as founding texts of the contemporary "grounding orthodoxy," but reading them side by side, it seems to me that the two authors are building the notion on quite different foundations. Fine treats ground as a primitive, hyperintensional connective relating truths directly (something like "Q because P"), governed by its own proof theory with distinctions such as full versus partial ground and mediate versus immediate ground. Schaffer, by contrast, treats ground as a relation holding among worldly entities or facts, and effectively defines fundamentality through it, by identifying "the fundamental" with whatever sits ungrounded at the very bottom of the hierarchy.

This difference seems to bite hardest on so-called logical grounding claims. On Fine's framework, a conjunction is fully grounded jointly by its two conjuncts (each of which counts as only a partial ground), while a true disjunction is already fully grounded by a single true disjunct on its own; these distinctions fall out naturally once ground is understood as a relation between truths of matching logical structure. I have trouble seeing how Schaffer's picture, on which grounding relates coarser-grained worldly facts rather than syntactically structured truths, could recover the same distinctions without covertly relying on something operator-like after all. My professor also mentioned, almost in passing, that Fine does not want to simply equate the fundamental with the ungrounded in the way Schaffer does, on the grounds that ungroundedness alone may be too thin a notion to track what is metaphysically basic — but we did not have time to work through the details in class.

Overall, which of the two frameworks offers the more defensible package of views on the relation between grounding and fundamentality, and is the disagreement between them substantive, or could it be dissolved through some translation between the two idioms? Please justify your answer.
