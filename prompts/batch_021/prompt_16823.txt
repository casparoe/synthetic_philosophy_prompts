Ok, weird thing happened at my writing group last night. I read out the chapter where my main character finally dies (car crash, been building to it the whole book) and I actually choked up reading it out loud, even though I wrote the death myself, could undo it with one keystroke, and the guy never existed in the first place. Afterwards, this one guy in the group, an engineer, said that doesn't add up -- grief's supposed to track an actual loss, and there's no loss here since nothing happened to anyone, ever. He compared it to getting upset that 2+2 doesn't equal 5. I couldnt explain where his argument goes wrong, and it got worse when he pointed out that I apparently teared up more over this guy than over my actual second cousin, who died last year. So who's right here -- is he onto something and I'm just fooling myself, or is there something real going on when we grieve people we made up?
