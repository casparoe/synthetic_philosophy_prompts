At a family thing this weekend, my aunt (Danny's mom) and my uncle got into it about Danny, who's deep in a gambling addiction and has been lying to her and taking money out of her account to cover his debts.

My aunt said she's done making excuses for him: every time he picks up the phone to place a bet, or lies to her about where the money went, that's a choice, and being furious at him is exactly the right reaction, same as she'd be furious at any random person who stole from her.

My uncle said that's not fair anymore. once someone's this deep into an addiction, the craving's basically running the show, and staying mad at him for it is like staying mad at someone for a seizure — you don't resent people for compulsions, you get them help.

My aunt said fine, but Danny wasn't compelled to do anything the first bunch of times, years ago, before it spiraled. He knew exactly what he was risking and kept going anyway, so even if he's not fully "choosing" now, he's the one who set the whole thing in motion, and he doesn't get to hide behind that later.

My uncle said by that logic you'd have to stay furious forever at anyone whose problem started with one bad call, like blaming a guy for a pill addiction that came out of a legit injury and prescription. He said the real question isn't whether Danny's a good person, or even whether he's responsible in some sense — its whether being angry AT him, personally, right now, is the right response versus just being sad and worried, and he thinks the anger stopped making sense a while back even if the responsibility never fully disappeared.

Neither of them budged and I've been turning it over since, feels like they're each onto something but I can't fully square it. Can you go through the exchange and say who's got the better argument at each step, not just who wins overall, and point out anything you think they're both missing?
