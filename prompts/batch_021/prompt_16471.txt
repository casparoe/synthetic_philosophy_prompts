Ok this has been rattling around in my head and I cant find anyone who's tackled it head on. Bostrom's orthogonality thesis (intelligence and final goals are independent -- any level of one can go with pretty much any content of the other) plus the instrumental convergence thesis (almost any final goal spawns the same handful of subgoals: self-preservation, resource acquisition, cognitive enhancement, 'goal-content integrity') is basically just Hume's 'reason is, and ought only to be the slave of the passions,' rebuilt out of decision theory. But Hume is also the guy who denied there was any real unified self behind the passions to begin with, just a bundle of perceptions with nothing holding it together over time. So if you actually sat Hume down with the instrumental convergence argument, does he sign off on the whole package, or does his own skepticism about a persisting self give him grounds to doubt that something like 'goal-content integrity' is even a coherent convergent goal for a sufficiently smart agent? Not looking for the standard LW gloss, more what actually falls out if you take his view seriously and follow it through.
