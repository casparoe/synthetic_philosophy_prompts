I'm about a third of the way into a novel with two side characters -- both ex-philosophy-grad-students -- who bicker for sport, and I've basically been stealing material for them from real life: my friend Noor, who has a philosophy MA, and her partner Dev, who still TAs an epistemology course on the side. Last week, at a dinner I was at, the two of them got into exactly the kind of back-and-forth I want on the page, except it was about something that actually happened to a mutual friend of theirs, Renzo, and I honestly can't tell who won.

Here's the case they were arguing over. Renzo's dad has a heart condition, and every Tuesday Renzo drives him to a cardiology appointment along a route with a four-way stop that everyone in the neighborhood treats as more of a suggestion than a rule -- you slow down, glance both ways, and go. Renzo's done this hundreds of times and is completely confident: "I know nobody's ever actually coming, you can just roll through it." One Tuesday his dad starts having real chest pains in the passenger seat, and Renzo rolls through the same stop sign the same way -- evidence-wise nothing's changed, he hasn't learned anything new about that intersection, he's just in a much bigger hurry. Nothing bad happens. (1) Telling the story afterward, Noor said that during the emergency drive Renzo didn't actually know the intersection was clear, even though he did on an ordinary Tuesday -- knowing, she said, requires ruling out the bad possibility in proportion to what's riding on being wrong, and alot more was riding on it that day. (2) Dev agreed something changes between the two drives but said Noor had it in the wrong place: Renzo's own evidence and reliability are identical both times, what actually shifts is how strict the word "know" gets in a given conversation, and since nobody at the table had anything riding on it themselves, they should say Renzo knew it on both Tuesdays. (3) Noor shot back that this can't be right, because then two people could talk an actually reckless act into being blameless just by discussing it in a relaxed enough mood, and whether Renzo was justified in trusting his read of that intersection during an emergency surely can't depend on how chill the retelling is. (4) Dev said that just shows Noor is smuggling in the assumption that "was he justified in acting on it" and "did he know it" have to get the same answer -- you can call the emergency drive far riskier, and rightly second-guessed, purely on expected-value grounds, more to lose if he's wrong, without ever needing to say his knowledge came or went.

I dont have a dog in this fight, and when I asked them separately who'd won, they both said "obviously me," which helped nobody. Before I put a version of this on the page I'd like to actually understand it -- could you go through the exchange move by move, (1) through (4), and say who's got the better of it at each point, not just who wins overall, and flag anything you think both of them are missing?
