For the better part of four decades I worked as a field ecologist, and since retiring I have, rather to my own surprise, fallen into philosophy -- currently working through H.L.A. Hart's Law, Liberty, and Morality and Patrick Devlin's The Enforcement of Morals, on the recommendation of an old colleague who thought I would enjoy watching an Oxford don and a law lord disagree so civilly about whether a community may rightly punish conduct simply for offending its shared morality, harm or no harm -- legal moralism, I now know to call it. I did enjoy it rather more than I expected, and then discovered, to my considerable dismay, that the Fellowship and Conduct Committee I now sit on at our natural history society is re-running the Hart-Devlin debate almost line for line, except that the statute book has become an internal bylaw, and the person facing censure is one of our most distinguished Fellows.

The facts, briefly: last winter this Fellow took part in a legally licensed hunt abroad and killed a large mammal that is endangered across most of its range but for which the host government issues a small number of permits each year specifically to fund the anti-poaching patrols that protect the rest of the population. She broke no law, and nothing about the episode touches her research record or her conduct toward students, staff, or colleagues. All the same, the story reached the international press, our membership has been in uproar for weeks, and a motion will come before Council on Thursday to strip her of her Fellowship under a bylaw forbidding "conduct unbecoming a Fellow" -- a phrase our governing documents have never defined beyond those three words. Roughly half the Committee argues that tolerating this within our ranks corrodes the shared commitment to conservation that gives the Society any standing to speak on these questions at all: Devlin's point about a shared morality being the cement holding a body together, dressed up in our own parochial terms. The other half, including me on my more Hart-ish days, thinks this is simply punishing a Fellow for a lawful private choice that the majority happens to find distasteful, which is no business of ours at all.

Could you draft the short written opinion I have been asked to circulate to Council before Thursday? I need it to come down on one side rather than surveying both, to propose a standing reading of "conduct unbecoming a Fellow" that the Committee could apply consistently in future cases instead of deciding each one from scratch, and to state the strongest form of whichever side's argument it ultimately rejects, rather than dispatching it in a single sentence.
