I'm about a third of the way into a novel where my narrator has spent fifteen years quietly rehearsing a betrayal by an old friend, and I want his resentment to be philosophically load-bearing rather than just "guy is bitter." Two research rabbit holes have collided on me though. In "Freedom and Resentment," Strawson makes resentment (and its cousins, gratitude, indignation) basically the whole foundation of moral responsibility -- we hold people responsible because we're the kind of creatures who react to good or ill will with these attitudes, and thats about all the justification the practice needs or gets. Nietzsche's ressentiment, in the first essay of the Genealogy, seems like a totally different animal -- festering, self-deceiving, born out of powerlessness, and its literally what invents slave morality's whole vocabulary of good and evil in the first place. They can't both just be "resentment," can they? Compare the two accounts properly for me: what job is each concept actually doing in its own argument, and then tell me plainly -- does Nietzsche's genealogy threaten Strawson's naturalistic case for moral responsibility, or are they simply not talking about the same phenomenon at all? Don't hedge, land somewhere and justify it.
