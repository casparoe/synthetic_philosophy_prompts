Aristotle's sea-battle passage in De Interpretatione 9 is usually read as posing a puzzle about whether statements about future contingents are already either true or false before the relevant event occurs. Building on this puzzle, Arthur Prior's branching-time tense logic produced two classic rival semantics for future-tensed statements: the Ockhamist solution and the Peircean solution. Please explain the core difference between these two semantics, including how each treats the truth-value of a genuinely contingent future-tensed statement. Then explain why the Ockhamist strategy of appealing to a privileged "thin red line" -- a distinguished actual history running through the branching structure -- to fix such truth-values is widely regarded as difficult to reconcile with the indeterminism that the branching-time framework is meant to capture.
