Had a long, slightly too heated argument with my friend Priya last night (over pretty mediocre pad thai) about deflationary theories of truth. She's basically finished with a phil of logic PhD and I'm more of an interested amateur, so I definitely got out-argued on the technical stuff in the moment, but I still don't think I was wrong. What set us off was the "conservativeness argument" against deflationism -- I think this goes back to Shapiro and Ketland, something from the late 90s, though don't quote me on who said what first. The basic idea, as I understood it: deflationists say truth isn't a substantive, explanatory property, it's just a device for disquotation and generalization -- and one natural way to cash that out is to insist that a good theory of truth, bolted onto some base theory like Peano arithmetic, shouldn't let you prove any NEW theorems in the base theory's own vocabulary that you couldn't already prove without it, i.e. it should be conservative over the base theory. Except apparently if you add the obvious compositional truth axioms to PA and let induction apply to formulas that contain the new truth predicate, the resulting theory ISN'T conservative -- you can suddenly prove reflection-principle type statements (stuff in the neighborhood of PA's own consistency) that PA alone can't touch. Priya's move was basically: fine, don't extend induction to formulas with the truth predicate then, use the restricted theory, which is conservative, and the objection just evaporates. I said that felt like cheating, like you're picking exactly the restriction that saves the result with no independently motivated reason for treating the truth predicate differently from every other predicate PA's induction schema applies to.

I want the strongest possible version of my side of this, since I clearly didn't argue it well enough at the time. Can you make the case that this conservativeness/non-conservativeness business really is a serious problem for deflationism -- that restricting induction just to preserve conservativeness is an ad hoc patch rather then a principled deflationary move, and that deflationists end up stuck between a truth predicate with real deductive bite and an arbitrary, gerrymandered restriction? Please actually engage with the best version of Priya's reply instead of a strawman of it (is there some independent, non-question-begging rationale for the restriction I'm just not seeing?), and don't be afraid to get into the logical weeds -- I'd much rather have something precise than something that stays comfortable at the "truth is/isn't substantive" level of abstraction.
