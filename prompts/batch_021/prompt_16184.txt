Our department has this dumb-but-fun tradition where whoever's teaching a given unit that week has to put a "philosophy joke of the week" on the whiteboard outside the seminar room. Its been going for years now and everyone is way more invested in it then the premise really deserves. I've got modal metaphysics this week -- counterpart theory vs. transworld identity, so Lewis's On the Plurality of Worlds versus the Kripke of Naming and Necessity -- and I'm stuck. Every possible-worlds joke I can find online is some version of "somewhere out there I'm rich and have hair," which bugs me, because that would work just as well for literally any view that has possible worlds in it at all. It doesn't show that whoever wrote it knows the first thing about the actual dispute.

What I want instead is a joke that depends on the specific disagreement between the two views -- one that wouldn't be funny, or wouldn't even really parse, to someone who didn't know that counterpart theory replaces strict cross-world identity with a similarity-based counterpart relation between distinct, worldbound individuals, whereas the transworld identity camp has the very same individual literally showing up in more than one world (however you want to ground that -- stipulation, haecceities, whatever you like). Bonus points if it works in the Humphrey objection somehow, or the fact that on Lewis's view one person can have several equally good counterparts in a single other world, which is its own mess. It doesn't have to be a real knee-slapper, it just has to land with people who've actually sat through the relevant lectures, and not just with my nephew.
