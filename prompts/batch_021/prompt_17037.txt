Carnap's Principle of Tolerance, developed in The Logical Syntax of Language, holds that no logical or linguistic framework can be shown to be uniquely correct: frameworks are to be freely proposed and are to be judged only by their fruitfulness for the purposes at hand, with disputes about foundations replaced by convention. Yet in his Intellectual Autobiography, Carnap expresses real sympathy for a rationally planned reorganization of economic and social life as a natural extension of the scientific world-conception he shared with the rest of the Vienna Circle. Please argue that these two commitments are in genuine tension: a consistent extension of the anti-authoritarian, pluralistic spirit that makes tolerance compelling for language and logic should have pushed Carnap toward decentralized, experimental competition among social and economic arrangements, rather than toward centralized planning.
