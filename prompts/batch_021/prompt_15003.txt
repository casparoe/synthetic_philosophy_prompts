So this has been rattling around in my head for weeks now and I don't really have anyone in my life to talk it through with. I never finished any kind of science degree (community college dropout, no shame) but I went down a physics YouTube and pop-sci book rabbit hole a while back and got stuck on the question of why time even has a direction in the first place. The standard answer everyone gives is basically "entropy always increases, that's the second law of thermodynamics, that's why you remember the past and not the future and why eggs don't un-scramble themselves," and for a good while that actually satisfied me.

Then I read about this old objection, I think the guy's name was Loschmidt, that basically says: if the underlying laws of physics are time-symmetric, meaning they run just as fine backwards as forwards, then there's no reason entropy should be any more likely to increase toward the future than toward the past starting from right now. So the only way you actually get a real one-directional arrow is if you also just assume the universe happened to start out in some incredibly low entropy state at the very beginning, I've seen this called the "past hypothesis," and that assumption isn't something the second law explains, its just stapled on from outside. There's apparently an even weirder problem called "Boltzmann brains" that falls out of this same issue, where if you don't stipulate that special starting condition, statistically almost all observers with memories like mine should just be random fluctuations rather than the product of an actual history, which sounds insane but I guess follows from the same logic.

What I'm hoping you can do is argue FOR the position I've basically talked myself into over the last few weeks: that the "entropy increase explains the arrow of time" story people tell is incomplete to the point of being kind of a bait and switch, and that all the real explanatory weight is quietly being carried by this unexplained past hypothesis rather than by the second law or the dynamics themselves. Specifically I want you to deal with the obvious comeback, something like "its totally fine to just treat the low entropy past as a basic law of nature, that's not cheating," and make the strongest case for why that comeback doesn't actually get people off the hook, rather than just repeating the textbook version of the second law at me again.
