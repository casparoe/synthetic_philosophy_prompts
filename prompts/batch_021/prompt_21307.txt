My closest friend from university, now a decision theorist, is getting married next month, and I've been asked to give the best man's toast. He met his fiancée at a workshop on cooperative AI, and among his colleagues there's a running joke that the two of them are the first real-world instance of program equilibrium, having decided early on to be radically transparent with each other instead of building trust the slow, repeated-game way most couples do. Roughly half the guests will catch a reference to Löb's theorem or FairBot without blinking; the other half is family who just want to feel something. The complication is that the groom is himself known for insisting that two people are nothing like two idealized agents with common knowledge of each other's source code, so a toast that flattens this into a tidy point about trust would embarrass him in front of exactly the people I'm hoping to land it with. Could you write me a five-minute toast that uses program equilibrium as its structural spine rather than a name-drop, takes that objection seriously instead of glossing over it, and still lands as a sincere tribute for the half of the room that has never heard of decision theory?
