I spent thirty-six years running an experimental low-temperature physics lab, and like most people in my field I got through an entire career on "shut up and calculate," treating questions about what quantum mechanics actually says about the world as somebody else's problem. Retirement has finally given me time to read the foundational literature properly -- Bell's original papers, Maudlin's work on nonlocality, Wallace's defence of Everett -- and a running disagreement has broken out in the small philosophy-of-physics reading group I joined at the local university, between the Bohmians and the Everettians among us, over something I hadn't appreciated before: whether many-worlds actually earns the right to call itself local, or whether it just relocates the nonlocality that Bell's theorem forces on us somewhere less visible.

The Bohmians in the group argue that however you dress it up, something still has to guarantee that Alice's and Bob's branches stay correlated across the experiment, and that this is nonlocality by another name, whatever the Everettians want to call it; the Everettians reply that once you take seriously that there is no single, observer-independent outcome at either wing, Bell's argument -- which presupposes exactly that -- simply gets no grip, and they point to results on locally-flowing information in the Heisenberg picture, of the kind associated with Deutsch and Hayden, as evidence that nothing actually crosses the light cone.

Could you write me a dialogue between a convinced Bohmian and a convinced Everettian, set at the two wings of an EPR-Bohm experiment just as the correlated results come in, in which each tries to talk the other out of their interpretation rather than simply restating it? I want the Bohmian to press the Everettian specifically on how cross-wing correlation is secured in the absence of a single determinate outcome to correlate, and I want the Everettian to press back using the local-information-flow point while forcing the Bohmian to own up to what an explicit nonlocal guiding equation costs once you take relativity seriously. Please have the exchange end not in a tidy resolution but with each side naming, precisely, what it has to give up to keep its own account consistent.
