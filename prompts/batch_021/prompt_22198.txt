I'm teaching a graduate seminar on philosophy of space and time this term, and we just spent two classes on the hole argument. The basic Earman-Norton indeterminism result landed fine with students -- take a diffeomorphism that's the identity outside some matter-free "hole" region and smeared around differently inside it, and you get two models that agree on absolutely everything outside the hole but disagree about which point has which metric inside it. What's landing less well is the "sophisticated substantivalist" escape hatch, where you deny haecceitism about spacetime points and just say the two hole-diffeomorphic models describe the exact same possibility. I think that move traces back to Butterfield and Brighouse in the late 80s/early 90s but don't quote me on that, its been a while since I read the primary sources closely.

What I'd like is a dialogue between a sophisticated substantivalist and a relationist who end up cornering each other at the wine reception after a colloquium talk on exactly this topic, hashing out whether the "sophisticated" move actually rescues substantivalism or is just relationism wearing a substantivalist's coat. I want the relationist to press hard on the obvious follow-up: once you strip out primitive identity, a spacetime point just is its bundle of metrical relations to everything else, so what is the "substance" even supposed to be doing anymore that the relationist wasn't already committed to?

And I want the substantivalist to have a genuine comeback rather than just insisting points are real -- something that actually puts the manifold to work in a way relationism can't easily match, e.g. making sense of vacuum solutions and gravitational waves propagating through matter-free regions, or of two qualitatively identical universes that differ only in their overall rotation (which is really just the bucket argument again, dressed up in modern clothes). I don't want either side to be a pushover; they should respond to each other's actual points as things develop, not just restate their starting position, and I'd like it to end in a genuine standoff rather than a tidy resolution, since that's honestly where I think the literature still is.
