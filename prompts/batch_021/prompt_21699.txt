So I've been down a rabbit hole on underdetermination of theory by evidence lately, started from the old grue puzzle and ended up back at Duhem-Quine. Thing that bugs me is that whenever this comes up in the LW-sphere someone always says "yeah but a simplicity prior basically kills the problem, just use Solomonoff induction" and everyone nods along. I don't buy that it's actually a knockdown move, but I can't articulate why as well as I'd like to.

Could you write me a dialogue between two people who both genuinely know thier stuff: one a committed Duhemian/Quinean holist who thinks underdetermination is basically inescapable (you can always save a theory by tweaking some auxiliary hypothesis, and empirically equivalent rivals can always be cooked up), the other a Bayesian who thinks a universal simplicity prior (Solomonoff/Kolmogorov-style) dissolves most of the practical bite of the problem. Have the Quinean push on the actual weak spots of the Solomonoff move -- it's uncomputable, and the choice of universal Turing machine isn't itself handed to you by the evidence, so maybe the arbitrariness just gets relocated instead of removed. Give the Bayesian real comebacks though, not just "priors solve everything" restated. Neither one should be a pushover, and they need to actually engage with each other's specific points instead of just talking past each other.
