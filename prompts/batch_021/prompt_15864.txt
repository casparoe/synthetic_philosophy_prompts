So I've been re-reading that infamous Frege diary lately -- the political one from 1924, the one that got dug up and published back in the 90s (Gabriel and Kienzler, I think) full of genuinely ugly antisemitic, anti-Weimar, pro-dictatorship rants, the kind of stuff that reportedly left even Dummett kind of shaken when he first read it. I keep going back and forth on what, if anything, this should do to how we read Frege's actual philosophy, and rather than just gesture at "well you can seperate the man from the work" I want a dialogue for my seminar that actually digs into it.

Can you write me a dialogue between two Frege scholars chatting at the coffee break after a talk on the Grundgesetze, who disagree about this. One thinks the diary is biographically interesting but totally irrelevant to assessing the logic and the philosophy of language -- judging Begriffsschrift by the diary is a straightforward genetic fallacy. The other thinks there's a real, defensible case that it isn't just incidental: that Frege's contempt for psychologism, his insistence on a single objective realm of thought (Gedanken) that brooks no relativism or plurality of perspectives, and his general hostility to anything "merely subjective," have some kind of elective affinity with the political intolerance on display in the diary. Both of them should be sharp and fair to the other side, actually responding to each other's best points instead of talking past one another, and don't let it resolve too neatly -- leave it a bit open, the way these things actually go.
