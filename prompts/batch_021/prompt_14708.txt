A few of us in my social psychology PhD program run an informal forecasting exercise each year: before we submit to our field's flagship conference, everyone privately assigns a probability that each of our key findings will survive a high-powered direct replication, and once the actual replication evidence comes in, we rank ourselves using the log score, where your loss on a given claim is the negative log of the probability you assigned to what actually happened. One labmate now argues the whole scheme is unfair. Last cycle she put 0.95 on a striking result that went on to fail, finished the year ranked last, and insists it's unreasonable that one confident miss can outweigh several cautious, hedged calls that turned out fine; she wants us to switch to a rule that simply credits whoever was on the correct side of 50 percent. Something about her proposed fix strikes me as a mistake, but I can't quite articulate what we would be giving up by adopting it. Am I right to resist the change, and if so, what's actually at stake in the asymmetry she wants to eliminate?
