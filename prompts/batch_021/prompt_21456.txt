I'm a PhD student in philosophy of physics. At a recent workshop in our department's philosophy of cosmology series, two audience members got into an exchange during the Q&A on the fine-tuning argument that the speaker never quite settled, and it has stayed with me since.

Nadia, a physics postdoc, restated the argument in probabilistic terms: the life-permitting range for constants like the strength of gravity or the cosmological constant is minute relative to their possible range, so a randomly-set universe was very unlikely to land there by chance. Either something tuned the dials, or there are enough universes with different constants that some were bound to be life-permitting -- and unsurprisingly, we find ourselves in one of those.

Owen, a philosophy postdoc, objected that the whole framing is confused: we could not possibly have observed anything other than life-permitting constants, so once that selection effect is factored in, there is no residual fact left for design or a multiverse to explain.

Nadia answered with a version of the firing-squad case: if a firing squad fires at you and every shot misses, "you wouldn't be here to wonder about it otherwise" is true, but it doesn't make your survival any less in need of explanation -- you'd still reasonably suspect bribery or faulty rifles. The selection effect explains why you never observe your own death, not why you survived; likewise, it explains why we never observe a dead universe, not why this one turned out to be alive.

Owen conceded the point but denied that it favors design specifically: a multiverse discharges the same explanatory debt more cheaply, since physics already has some motivation for multiplying universes -- inflationary cosmology, the string landscape -- that is independent of the fine-tuning problem, whereas God is posited for no reason beyond explaining this one fact.

Neither of them budged, and I couldn't tell who actually came out ahead. Could you go through the exchange move by move, saying who has the better of it at each point rather than just declaring an overall winner, and flag anything significant that both of them are missing?
