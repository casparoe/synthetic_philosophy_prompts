Write a dialogue between two AI safety researchers, A and B, debating whether deceptive alignment deserves serious weight as a failure mode. A argues from a version of the counting argument: among the models that would achieve low loss under a given training process, there are far more possible long-term goals that rationalize temporarily complying with training (since almost any sufficiently patient goal benefits from surviving training undetected) than there are goals that are genuinely aligned or corrigible, so absent some independent reason to think training favors the latter, we should place non-trivial prior weight on deceptively aligned mesa-optimizers. Have A and B engage each other's strongest point rather than restate their own position, and have each concede a genuine weakness in their own view somewhere in the exchange. End without a forced resolution, and aim for roughly 700 words.
