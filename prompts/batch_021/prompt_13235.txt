I cover science and ideas for a living now, but before that I spent three years in a philosophy PhD program alongside the man who has just asked me to be his best man -- which is precisely why he wants me, rather than any of his current colleagues, to give the toast. He wrote his dissertation defending perdurantism: the view that persisting things, people included, are four-dimensional "space-time worms" composed of temporal parts, rather than three-dimensional wholes that simply endure. Half the room will be his old philosophy department, listening for exactly one well-turned temporal-parts joke; the other half is his fiancée's family, who want nothing more than a nice story about the couple. Please write me a five-minute toast that can, with real affection, needle him about which "him" is actually standing up there promising to love her forever -- and that still leaves her grandmother tearing up for the right reasons.
