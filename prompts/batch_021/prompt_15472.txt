I don't have any background in philosophy, but something that has come up in my family over the last few weeks won't leave me alone, and I have a feeling there is a genuine puzzle in it rather than just an ordinary family disagreement. My older sister was recently offered a permanent position abroad in exactly the field she spent years saying she would drop everything for if the chance ever came. Now that the offer has actually arrived and she has a month to decide, she says she is frightened of starting over in a new country, unwilling to leave her partner and the life she has built here, and that she has firmly decided to turn it down. She has asked the family to accept that this is her considered decision and to stop raising reasons she might change her mind. I even brought the whole thing up with some friends over dinner, and we spent an hour arguing about it without agreeing on anything.

My mother's position is simple: whatever our sister is telling us today, calmly and consistently, counts as what she wants, and none of us has any standing to decide otherwise on her behalf. My younger sister disagrees strongly. She points out that a month ago, before any of this was a real possibility, our sister talked about that exact kind of position as the whole point of everything she had worked toward, and argues that the frightened, sleep-deprived person turning it down now is no more of an authority on our sister's own good than the person who wanted it for a decade. She thinks we owe it to our sister to act on behalf of the calmer person she will have become in a year, not the frightened person she is today. I keep going back and forth. It seems presumptuous for any of us to claim we know what our sister "really" wants better than she does. But it also seems too convenient to treat whatever she says while exhausted and frightened as automatically the last word on her own good, especially given that she herself has wanted different things at different times.

What I would like help with is not whether she should take the job, but the disagreement underneath that. When my sister tells us clearly and repeatedly what she wants, is there ever a legitimate basis for the rest of us to act instead on what we believe she would want if she weren't afraid or exhausted, or is that just a more flattering way of describing our own opinion of what she should do? And if such a basis could ever exist, what would separate someone who has actually identified it from someone who is simply better than average at mistaking their own preferences for hers? I would rather have a serious answer to that than one that settles for telling me to respect her autonomy and leave it there.
