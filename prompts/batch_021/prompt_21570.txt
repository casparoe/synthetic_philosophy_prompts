A close friend has taken to interrupting our conversations to ask how I actually know certain very basic things: that my memory of this morning isn't an elaborate fabrication, that the people around me have real inner experiences rather than merely behaving as if they do, that the world didn't spring into existence five minutes ago complete with a ready-made history. Whenever I try to answer, I end up simply restating the claim or appealing to further memories and impressions that depend on exactly the kind of trust he is questioning, and he takes my failure to escape that circle as proof that none of it is genuine knowledge, just unexamined habit mistaken for certainty. I don't think he's right, but I can't quite say what's wrong with the way he keeps pressing the question. Is he correct that I don't actually know these things, or is there something mistaken about demanding justification for beliefs like these in the first place?
