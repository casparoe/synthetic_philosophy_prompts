Aumann's agreement theorem is a recurring touchstone on rationalist blogs and forums, particularly among people influenced by Robin Hanson's writing on disagreement: two honest, rational agents who trust each other's reasoning cannot end up agreeing to disagree once they have compared notes. This seems to me to settle a question I keep seeing debated in AI safety circles. If a superintelligent system is more rational than any human it talks to and explains its reasoning honestly, the theorem guarantees that our beliefs will converge with its own, and a human who still resists afterward isn't exercising appropriate caution, just irrationality dressed up as skepticism. So why do so many people writing about AI persuasion treat a maximally persuasive superintelligence as a danger to be contained, rather than as the single most epistemically valuable interlocutor humanity could ever hope to have?
