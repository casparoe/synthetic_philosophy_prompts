I am a retired experimental physicist who has taken up philosophy in earnest since retiring, and it has left me re-examining some old memories in a new light. For four decades, my closest friend was a former colleague, R. What I admired in him above all, and what I often held up to younger colleagues as a model, was something from early in his career: as a young researcher, he had discovered that his supervisor was quietly suppressing an inconvenient result, and had confronted him over it at real cost to his own prospects.

R. died last year. While going through his old papers, I found strong evidence that this confrontation never actually took place: by every other account, the supervisor in question was scrupulously honest, and none of R.'s contemporaries recall any such episode. Nothing else about the friendship has changed in the slightest — my memory of his company and the trust between us feel exactly as they always did. Was the friendship actually less valuable than I took it to be, given that the very quality I prized most in him turns out not to have been real? Or is that thought confused, since nothing about how the friendship was actually lived and felt has been altered at all?
