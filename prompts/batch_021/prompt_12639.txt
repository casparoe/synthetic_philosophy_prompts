I've been intermittently following discussions of evidential cooperation in large worlds (ECL) on LessWrong and the EA Forum ever since I first ran into Caspar Oesterheld's write-up on what he called multiverse-wide superrationality: the idea that if the universe or multiverse is large or infinite, it plausibly contains many causally disconnected agents whose decision-making correlates with mine, so that my choosing to act cooperatively toward value systems other than my own is itself evidence that relevantly similar agents elsewhere, including ones who care about very different things, are doing the same for values like mine. It's the sort of argument that is easy to state in outline and much harder to cash out into an actual recommendation, which is exactly where I keep getting stuck.

This came to a head recently when two people at my local meetup got into a real argument about it while trying to decide how to split a small shared donation pool. She wanted to shift some of the money toward a cause she personally assigns almost no credence to being valuable, purely on ECL grounds, arguing that correlation doesn't require the relevant distant agents to share your values or even be copies of you, just to share the decision-relevant disposition to cooperate, so you can have principled reason to help a cause you are confident is mistaken. He thought this was moral-uncertainty hedging wearing a multiverse costume, and pushed the objection that once you admit you have no real handle on what fraction of correlated agents out there hold which values, there is no way to compute what "cooperating" should actually change about your behavior, so the whole apparatus is either empty or a fancy restatement of ordinary worldview diversification. It got more technical from there: someone raised whether any of this has force once you are not already assuming evidential rather than causal or updateless decision theory, and, as far as I could tell, nobody present actually resolved that before the conversation moved on.

Could you write the dialogue this pair might have had if they had kept going, with the two speakers cast as the ECL-optimist and the ECL-skeptic? I would like the optimist to meet the "multiverse costume" charge head-on, saying something substantive about what ECL recommends that ordinary hedging over your own moral uncertainty would not, rather than just restating the correlation-without-copies point. I would like the skeptic to press the epistemic-access problem as hard as it deserves and also to force the decision-theory-dependency worry, so that the optimist has to say whether ECL is redundant for an updateless agent and toothless for a causal one. Please make it a real back-and-forth of roughly eight to twelve exchanges in which both sides are equally sharp, each lands at least one genuine point against the other, and neither simply wins by the end.
