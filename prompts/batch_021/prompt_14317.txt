So I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole on Simpsons paradox ever since it came up in an ACX comments thread. The example everyone uses is the classic Berkeley grad admissions case: the university looked biased against female applicants in the aggregate numbers, but once you broke it down department by department the bias basically vanished, because women were disproportionately applying to the more competetive depts. Since that's the canonical example, doesn't it basically prove that the disaggregated, subgroup-level numbers are always the ones tracking what's actually causally going on, and the pooled stat is just an artifact of not slicing the data finely enough? If so, shouldn't "just disaggregate as far as your sample size lets you" be the correct general rule any time you're worried about confounding, instead of something you actually have to reason about case by case?
