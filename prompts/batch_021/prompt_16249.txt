I've spent a lot of time on the rationalist end of the blogosphere -- Overcoming Bias, the old Less Wrong sequences, Astral Codex Ten -- where the "reference class problem" keeps getting invoked as the hidden engine behind the Doomsday Argument, the Sleeping Beauty problem, and the dispute between Bostrom's Self-Sampling Assumption and the rival Self-Indication Assumption. What none of these treatments has ever given me is a satisfying gut-level sense of why the problem is genuinely hard, as opposed to just a label for "statisticians disagree about which subgroup to use." Most write-ups repeat Reichenbach's line about frequentism needing the narrowest reference class for which reliable statistics can be compiled, note that any individual belongs to indefinitely many classes with different base rates, and then move straight on to the anthropic debates as though the underlying issue had been resolved rather than merely renamed.

What I'd like from you is an intuitive explanation -- not another restatement of the frequentist version of the puzzle -- of two connected things. First, why the problem is supposed to survive the move from frequencies to Bayesian credences: I recall an argument, I believe due to Alan Hájek, to the effect that even a committed subjectivist who denies that probabilities reduce to frequencies at all still can't dodge the underlying issue, because assigning a credence to a single case requires choosing a description of that case, and that choice is exactly as unprincipled as choosing a reference class ever was. Second, I'd like that abstract point made concrete through the Sleeping Beauty problem specifically: what would it mean to say that halfers and thirders are implicitly committed to different reference classes of "awakenings" or "trials," and why doesn't further stipulation about the experimental protocol ever force a unique answer?

I already know the halfer/thirder split itself, so I'd rather you not spend much time re-litigating that; what I'm after is the layer underneath it -- the sort of explanation that would let someone who has followed the anthropics debates for years stop merely nodding along at the phrase "reference class problem" and actually see, viscerally, why no amount of further cleverness about the experimental setup makes the indeterminacy go away.
