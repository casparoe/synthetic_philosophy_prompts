I've been reading rationalist discussions of mind uploading for years, and the standard argument for substrate independence holds that an emulation reproducing the same causal and functional organization as a brain must yield the same conscious experience, since it is organization rather than matter that counts. Integrated Information Theory struck me as exactly the rigorous, physicalist vindication this argument had been waiting for, since it identifies experience with Φ (phi), a precise, computable measure of a system's integrated causal structure -- meaning a sufficiently fine-grained emulation that preserves the brain's causal relations should generate the same Φ, and hence the same experience, as the original. Given that prominent IIT theorists, including Tononi and Koch, have nonetheless expressed real skepticism about whether an uploaded or simulated mind would actually be conscious, could you help me understand why they would be hesitant about a conclusion their own theory seems to guarantee?
