I'm a philosophy PhD student working on theories of moral responsibility, but a situation in my own family has left me at a loss. When I was seven, my aunt was murdered during a home invasion by a sixteen-year-old, who was given a life sentence that only became eligible for parole decades later, once the law changed for people sentenced as minors. He is now nearly sixty, and by every outside account—prison psychologists and chaplains who have worked with him for years—he is almost unrecognizable from the boy who committed the crime. He took up Buddhist meditation in his thirties, has taught it to other inmates for two decades, and reportedly no longer talks about the killing as something "he" did, in any straightforward sense. My family wants to formally oppose his release, insisting that the man who killed her and the man now asking for freedom are simply the same person and owe the same debt. That doesn't sit right with me, but neither does the opposite view, that enough change just dissolves what's owed. I can't work out which is correct, or what it should mean for how we treat him now. How should I be thinking about this?
