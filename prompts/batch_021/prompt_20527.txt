So my partner is doing a philosophy MA and dragged me into her reading group this week for a session on Bernard Williams' internal/external reasons paper, and it's been rattling around in my head since even tho I have zero formal background in this stuff (just a swe who reads too much philosophy-adjacent stuff online). I think I've got the basic setup down: an internal reason is relative to some "subjective motivational set" S that the agent actually has, and you only count as having a reason to do X if theres some sound deliberative route from your current S that would get you to be motivated to do X. External reasons are supposed to be reasons that hold regardless of S — like you'd still have a reason to keep a promise even if literally nothing in you, on reflection, cares about it.

The bit that's actually bugging me is Williams' argument for why external reasons don't really make sense. From what I can tell the move is basically a dilemma: if coming to be motivated by the "external" reason happens through some rational process, that process has to bottom out in something already in S, in which case it was an internal reason all along and we just hadn't noticed it; but if it doesn't bottom out in S at all, then whatever produces the new motivation isn't really "reasoning," it's more like conversion or getting a new desire implanted in you, so it's not clear why we'd even call it a reason. Ok fine, I buy that as an argument. But Williams is also super insistent that deliberation from S isn't just narrow means-end Humean instrumentalism — it can involve imagination, realizing new things about your situation, noticing that some existing concern can be pursued in a totally different way than you thought, etc. So if "sound deliberation from S" is allowed to be that rich and open-ended, what stops literally any external-looking reason from getting smuggled back in as "well if you deliberated hard/imaginatively enough from your actual S you'd end up caring about this too"? It feels like the dilemma only bites if S-plus-deliberation is fairly constrained, but the richness he wants elsewhere seems to blow that constraint wide open, which would make internal vs external reasons collapse into each other or at least become untestable. Is that an actual acknowledged soft spot in the argument, or is there some principled line — maybe something Williams says explicitly, or something people added afterward — that keeps deliberation-from-S rich enough to handle the normal cases without letting it stretch to cover literally anything? I haven't gotten to the responses/secondary lit yet so feel free to bring those in if useful, but mostly I just want someone to actually walk through the mechanics of where this tension does or doesn't bite, rather than re-explaining the basic S / no-S setup, since I've already got that part down.
