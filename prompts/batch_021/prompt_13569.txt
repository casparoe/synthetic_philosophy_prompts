I've followed the LessWrong-adjacent blogosphere for years, and Scott Garrabrant's writing on "geometric rationality" is one of the few sequences that actually changed how I make decisions, so when a version of its central question came up on my own giving circle's allocation committee, I recognized the shape of the disagreement immediately without being able to settle it myself. Our circle pools around $80,000 a year in undesignated donations from roughly thirty members, and every quarter our small in-house research team ranks a handful of speculative, hard-to-evaluate interventions -- early-stage biosecurity advocacy, an unproven de-worming delivery mechanism, that sort of thing -- by estimated expected impact per dollar. For three years running, our standing rule has been to route the entire quarter's pool into whichever single option ranks highest, on the reasoning that expected value is the quantity we should be maximizing and that across enough quarters the law of large numbers will vindicate us. One committee member, who trades derivatives for a living, wants to scrap this in favor of a Kelly-style rule instead: split each quarter's pool across our top few options in whatever proportions maximize the pool's expected long-run geometric growth rate rather than its expected value, on the grounds that we are effectively compounding one shared pot round after round, and that naive expected-value maximization applied to a compounding process is, in his words, "a great way to go almost surely broke despite having the best model on the desk." Another member, who has read her Samuelson, thinks this is a category error: the Kelly rule is only justified for an agent who actually has log utility over the quantity being grown, our circle's currency is impact rather than money, and nobody on the committee has shown that impact compounds the way a reinvested trading account does, so importing a gambler's caution here may just smuggle in intuitions that don't transfer.

Could you draft the short memo I'll circulate to the committee ahead of our year-end meeting? I need it to land on one concrete allocation rule for next year -- full Kelly-style fractional splitting, some tempered fraction of it, strict winner-take-all expected-value maximization, or some other rule entirely -- rather than a balanced-sounding list of considerations, and the reasoning needs to be sharp enough that the losing side would recognize their actual argument in it rather than a caricature of it. I'd especially like the memo to be explicit about which specific features of our situation -- the fact that we reinvest the same pool instead of making one-off bets, the open question of whether "impact" compounds at all, and the size of any single quarter's pool relative to our total future giving -- are doing the real work in whichever direction the recommendation goes, since that is exactly the part my committee keeps talking past.
