I'm drafting a literary novel that includes one speculative subplot, and I would like the underlying physics to be defensible enough that it doesn't fall apart if a scientifically literate reader, or an interviewer, decides to push on it. The scene I have in mind is modeled on the passage in Vonnegut's Slaughterhouse-Five where Billy Pilgrim watches a late-night war film run backward and narrates the reversed footage as though it were the true, redemptive order of events, with the wreckage of a bombing run drawn back up into the planes and the city made whole again. I want to take that conceit a step further than Vonnegut did and literalize it within the story itself: my protagonist, a researcher, builds a sealed chamber that locally drives entropy down, and actually steps inside it, rather than merely watching a reversed film of someone else's actions. A friend of mine with a physics background, now working in an unrelated field, calls the idea "basically Benjamin Button with extra steps" and seems unconvinced, but couldn't quite tell me what he thought was wrong with it, which has only made me more determined to work out the details properly rather than wave my hands at them.

Since entropy increase is what gives time its forward direction, it seems to follow that inside the chamber, time itself would run backward for my protagonist: she would experience effects before their causes, and would be able to tell the other characters, the moment she steps out, things that from her point of view have already happened but that from theirs have not happened yet, predictions that would then unfold exactly as she describes. I would like help thinking through the mechanics of the scene on that basis. From the point of view of someone watching her through a window the entire time, should her movements and speech appear to run in reverse, like a film played backward, even while she experiences everything as perfectly ordinary and forward-flowing from the inside? And once she steps out and rejoins the rest of the timeline, is there a clean instant of resynchronization, or would there need to be some transitional stretch during which her arrow of time and everyone else's are still, in effect, catching up with one another? I want the internal logic of the scene to be airtight before I commit several chapters to it.
