Last spring I was diagnosed with ADHD at thirty-four, and it has changed how I talk about my own behavior: I now describe my chronic lateness and disorganization as "just how my brain is wired" rather than as personal failings. This came up at dinner with two friends of mine, Elena and Marcus, who both did graduate work in philosophy, and it turned into a real argument about Sartre that neither would drop.

(1) Elena said this is a textbook case of bad faith. Sartre's discussion of cowardice in Existentialism Is a Humanism targets exactly this move: there is no "cowardly nature" that excuses the coward's acts in advance, since existence precedes essence. Converting a diagnosis into "this is just how I'm wired," she said, treats me as a finished thing instead of owning that what I do with that fact today is still up to me.

(2) Marcus objected that Elena was collapsing denying a fact with denying that the fact settles anything. Sartre is explicit that freedom is always freedom-in-situation, not freedom from one's body or neurology -- facticity isn't bad faith's target, it's what transcendence has to work with. Treating every appeal to the diagnosis as an excuse risks the mirror-image error: denying real facticity in the name of a will that isn't answerable to how attention actually works for me.

(3) Elena granted the point about facticity but said bad faith is seductive precisely because it always borrows a real fact to dress up an evasion, so the test isn't whether the fact is real but what work it's doing. If "my brain is wired this way" is quietly doing the work of "so there's nothing worth trying," that's the exact move Sartre keeps catching people making with their class or their upbringing.

(4) Marcus replied that Elena's actual complaint is about a further claim I haven't made, not about citing facticity at all -- and that treating any mention of a diagnosis as bad faith, absent a disclaimer of full responsibility, would empty out Sartre's own notion of "situation," since he allows that some situations make certain projects genuinely harder without every acknowledgment of that being bad faith.

Could you go through this move by move and say who has the stronger Sartrean case at each point, rather than just naming an overall winner? I'd also like to know whether there's something about bad faith or facticity that you think both of them are missing.
