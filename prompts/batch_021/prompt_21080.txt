So in my experimental philosophy seminar this week we spent basically the whole class on Knobe's chairman/environment case (the one where the CEO says "I don't care about the environment, I just want profits" and then greenlights a program that either helps or harms it as a side effect), and my prof kept hammering on how wild it is that like 80-something percent of people say he harmed the environment "intentionally" but only 20-something percent say he helped it "on purpose," even though his stated attitude is literally identical in both versions. A few of us were riffing after class about how this basically means a boss can never get credit for anything but will get blamed for everything, and I think theres a genuinely funny joke buried in there that none of us could quite land.

Could you write me a short joke (like a two-to-four line bit, it doesn't need to be a classic setup/punchline thing) about the Knobe effect that people who've actually studied this would find funny because it turns on the real asymmetry, not just a generic "philosophers are dorks" or "corporations are evil" joke?
