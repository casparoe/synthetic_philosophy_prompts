I teach a graduate seminar on the philosophy of space and time, and this week we have been working through Earman and Norton's canonical presentation of the hole argument. Once the students see how a diffeomorphism that acts as the identity everywhere outside some source-free "hole" region, but differs smoothly from the identity within it, can be used to generate two distinct metric fields that both solve Einstein's equations, agree everywhere on the distribution of matter, and yet disagree on the geometry inside the hole, most of them reach the same quick verdict: manifold substantivalism is straightforwardly refuted by the resulting indeterminism, and only some form of relationalism is left standing. I would like you to push back on that consensus.

Specifically, please argue that "sophisticated substantivalism" — the strategy, developed in somewhat different forms by Butterfield, Brighouse, and Hoefer, of retaining spacetime points as fundamental, non-derivative entities while denying haecceitism about them, so that diffeomorphic models are treated as representing one and the same physical possibility (Leibniz equivalence) — is a metaphysically substantive and well-motivated position, rather than relationalism wearing substantivalist clothing. Your argument should directly confront the natural objection that once trans-model identity of points is given up, there is nothing left for the substantivalist's additional ontological commitment to spacetime points to do, so that the dispute between the sophisticated substantivalist and the relationalist collapses into a merely verbal one. I am less interested in a balanced survey of the whole debate than in a sustained defense of sophisticated substantivalism against exactly this charge of emptiness.
