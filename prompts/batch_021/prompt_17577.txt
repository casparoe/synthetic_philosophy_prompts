Quick bit of context: I'm on the alumni advisory board for the engineering school I went to, and every spring we vote on the "Distinguished Alumnus" award that gets handed out at commencement. This year's frontrunner is a guy who co-founded a defense-tech startup that got big building targeting software a couple of foreign militaries ended up using -- it came out in some reporting last year that the algorithm was deliberately tuned to greenlight a strike on a target's house even when family was known to be home, on the logic that wiping out the whole household means the target can't just get quietly replaced by a cousin, plus it scares off future recruits. The university loves this guy (huge donor, great "our grads change the world" pitch) and most of the board wants to just wave the award through, but two people are blocking it, and one of them -- she's the only one of us who ever actually took a philosophy class -- keeps bringing up how Oxford had basically this exact fight over giving Truman an honorary degree back in the 50s, and that a philosopher named Anscombe wrote a whole pamphlet trying to stop it because of the bomb, using some argument that apparently wasn't just "war is bad" but something more specific about intention and what counts as murder. None of the rest of us actually know what that argument was or whether it even holds up when you swap in our guy, we just kind of nodded along and tabled it till next month.

Could you write the memo I'd send round to the board before that meeting? I want it to actually explain what Anscombe's real argument against Truman was (not the flattened "she was a pacifist" version, since I get the impression it's more precise than that), and then genuinely work through whether that argument transfers to our nominee or whether theres a difference that matters -- e.g. does it matter that he's an engineer several rungs down from whoever actually ordered the strikes, not the guy with his finger on the button. End with an actual recommendation (give the award, don't, give it with some kind of statement attached, whatever you land on), and don't let whichever side loses off the hook -- give their best argument its due instead of just gesturing at it.
