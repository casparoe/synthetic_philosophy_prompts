I'm drafting a piece on why forecasters and the online rationalist community keep invoking Aumann's agreement theorem — the 1976 result showing that two Bayesian reasoners who share the same priors cannot rationally go on disagreeing once their probability estimates for some event are common knowledge between them. For a sidebar, I'd like a short story that dramatizes the theorem's actual mechanics, not just the popular slogan that "rational people can't agree to disagree." Please write one, roughly 1,000–1,500 words, in which two characters attempt to settle a genuine disagreement solely by exchanging successive rounds of their bottom-line probability estimates, never disclosing the evidence behind them, and in which the outcome — convergence, or its conspicuous failure — hinges on some specific, identifiable feature of the theorem, such as a hidden divergence in their priors or a gap between mutual and common knowledge. Set it wherever you like, but make sure a reader familiar with the theorem could point to the exact moment in the story where it is honored or violated.
