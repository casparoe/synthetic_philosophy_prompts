So I'm working on a piece about AI alignment and keep circling back to this "embedded agency" framework out of MIRI (the Garrabrant/Demski sequence from a few years back). Their whole pitch is that classical rational agent models - game theory, AIXI, those boxes-and-arrows RL diagrams - are secretly "Cartesian": the agent sits outside the enviroment and only touches it through a clean sensor/actuator channel, basically a ghost reading dials. A real embedded agent is made of the same stuff as its environment, can be damaged or edited, can't fit a full copy of itself inside its own head, and that wrecks a bunch of the old theory - trusting your own future reasoning, logical uncertainty, "naturalized" induction where the learner has to model a world that contains the learner, etc.

Given they basically stuck his name on the model they're rejecting, I can't stop wondering how Descartes would actually respond if someone laid this critique out for him. Would he take it as proof of something he already half suspected, given he never really nailed the interaction problem (pineal gland and all)? Or would he dig in and defend some updated version of the boundary? Can you work through a genuinely reasoned answer here, grounded in his actual metaphysics, not just the usual "ghost in the machine" one liner?
