had a whole two-hour argument with a friend last night about higher-order theories of consciousness and neither of us actually won. she was hammering on the old objection that Rosenthal-style HOT theory is committed to something absurd -- if the higher-order thought is what actually generates the phenomenal feel, then a totally targetless HOT (one that misfires and represents you as being in pain when there's no pain state there at all) should still make it feel like something to be you, which seems nuts. I tried to defend the higher-order approach by switching to Kriegel's self-representational version, where its one state doing both jobs instead of two seperate ones, but she said that's not a fix, its just a relabeling, since you still have to explain how a state can represent itself without either circularity or a hidden second state getting smuggled back in. neither of us could settle it. can you write a dialogue between a committed Rosenthal-type higher-order thought theorist and a Kriegel-type self-representationalist actually hashing this out? i want them directly engaging each other's strongest move rather than talking past each other, and i want the self-rep side to have a real comeback to the relabeling charge instead of just asserting it away.
