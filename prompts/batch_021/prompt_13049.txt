I'm a novelist, and I also serve as the community representative on my hospital's clinical ethics committee. We've been asked to recommend how the hospital should respond to an unusual case: a documented lab error caused the wrong embryo to be transferred during an IVF cycle — not the one cleared during screening, but a different embryo from the same round, known to carry a serious, non-lethal hereditary condition. The resulting child is now three, has the condition, and will need lifelong care; her parents want the hospital to acknowledge that she was wronged and to offer some form of redress. Half the committee treats this as straightforward negligence that harmed a child. The other half objects that, had the error not occurred, this particular child would never have existed at all — a different embryo, and so a different child, would have been born instead — so there may be no scenario in which she herself ends up better off. Everyone agrees her life, condition included, is genuinely worth living. Please draft the committee's short written opinion for the hospital board: it should recommend a concrete response to the family, and it needs to take the second group's objection seriously on its own terms rather than dismissing it as a technicality that lets the hospital off the hook.
