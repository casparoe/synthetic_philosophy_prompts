I'm a sophomore poli sci major and this semester I somehow ended up on my school's Student Senate as my dorm's rep, which got me stuck on the "Elections & Governance" subcommittee. We're dealing with a proposal that's been blowing up our group chat for two weeks now. Backstory: last month we had a campus-wide referendum on whether to defund the late-night shuttle, and it passed mostly because of a viral (and pretty misleading) Instagram infographic about "wasteful spending" that most people who voted yes never actually fact-checked. Now one of the senators, who happens to be in my Democratic Theory seminar with me (we've been reading Estlund and some Jason Brennan on epistocracy, which is clearly where she got this), wants to change how referenda work from now on: before anyone can vote on a referendum question, they have to click through a short Senate-written "voter guide" and pass a 3-question comprehension quiz on it. If you don't pass, you can still vote, but it only counts as half a vote. She's calling it "informed voting." Two other senators are into it. The rest of the subcommittee, including our chair, thinks its a slippery slope toward the Senate getting to decide whose opinions count more, which feels like exactly the kind of power student government shouldn't have. Our bylaws only require one-person-one-vote for actual Senate elections, not for referenda specifically, so there's technically nothing stopping us from doing this.

We have to bring a recommendation to the full Senate next Wednesday and I got voluntold to write the subcommittee's memo since apparently I'm "into this stuff" now. Could you draft that memo for me? I need it to actually reccomend something -- adopt the proposal, reject it, or some modified version (different weighting, a trial run with a sunset clause, whatever) -- with real reasons behind whichever it picks, not a wishy-washy "here are both sides" thing. And I really don't want it to just dodge the "who gets to decide what counts as informed" objection, since that's the one that's actually been bugging me since we started arguing about this, so it needs to take that seriously instead of brushing past it.
