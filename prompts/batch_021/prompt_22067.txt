I've been prepping my 19th-century aesthetics seminar and got kind of stuck this week on something that always bugs me when we hit the Birth of Tragedy: young Nietzsche basically lifts Schopenhauer's whole metaphysics of music wholesale (music as the one art that copies the Will directly instead of the Platonic Ideas, Dionysian mapping onto Will, Apollonian onto Representation) and then uses that exact framework to argue for more or less the opposite conclusion Schopenhauer draws from it. By the time of the 1886 "Attempt at Self-Criticism" preface, Nietzsche is openly embarrassed about how Schopenhauerian the whole book sounds, and insists that what tragic music-drama actually gives us is a Dionysian yes-saying to life, joy right through and because of destruction and suffering -- not the quieting-of-the-will, resignation-flavored relief Schopenhauer thinks aesthetic contemplation (music above all, tragedy most intensely of all) offers us.

Could you write me a dialogue between the two of them on exactly this point? Premise: never mind how, Schopenhauer was already five years dead when Nietzsche found WWR in that Leipzig bookshop, so just wave your hands and let old Schopenhauer sit down with the post-self-critique Nietzsche and hash it out directly -- maybe have them keep coming back to one concrete piece (a late Beethoven quartet, a Wagner music-drama, whatever you think works) so its not all floating at the level of abstraction. I don't want two monologues stapled together, I want them actually landing punches on each others specific commitments -- e.g. Schopenhauer should get to press Nietzsche on whether "Dionysian affirmation" isn't just resignation with better PR, and Nietzsche should get to press Schopenhauer on whether his whole picture of redemption-through-will-negation is decadence dressed up as metaphysics. Keep both of them sharp; I don't want it to read like whoever gets the last word obviously wins. Something like 15-25 lines back and forth is probably right, and it's totally fine if it just ends mid-argument rather than wrapping up neatly.
