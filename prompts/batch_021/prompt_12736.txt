I was recently elected to the board of a small community theater company. Its founding charter borrows Du Bois's own criteria for the Krigwa Players -- that Black theater should be about, by, for, and near Black people -- and cites his 1926 claim, from "Criteria of Negro Art," that all art is propaganda. That charter is now being tested: our current production, a formally daring play about a Black family's unraveling through addiction and violence, has just been offered a transfer to a major regional festival whose subscriber base is overwhelmingly white, and the board has split down the middle. Some members argue that accepting would do exactly what Du Bois accused Claude McKay and Carl Van Vechten of doing -- feeding white audiences' appetite for Black degradation -- while others argue that refusing on those grounds is precisely the respectability policing that younger Harlem Renaissance writers, Langston Hughes chief among them, rejected. Please draft the memo I'll bring to next week's board vote: it should recommend whether we accept the transfer, decline it, or accept it with specific conditions, engage Du Bois's actual argument rather than a flattened version of it, and take seriously whichever side's position you don't adopt.
