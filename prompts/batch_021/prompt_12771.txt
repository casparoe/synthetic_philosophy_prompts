Hey, I'm giving a talk next month to the formal epistemology reading group I run for our grad students, and I want a sanity check on an argument before I try it out on people who actually do this stuff for a living. Rough notes below, not full prose yet.

Setup is standard Sleeping Beauty: coin flip Sunday night, she's put to sleep, memory wiped between Monday and Tuesday if it lands Tails, so there are three subjectively indistinguishable awakening-moments -- Heads&Monday, Tails&Monday, Tails&Tuesday. Thirders (team Elga) say her credence in Heads on waking should be 1/3, halfers (team Lewis) say it stays 1/2.

The thing that's bugging me is the standard betting defense of thirdism, which usually goes: run the experiment a huge number of times, offer Beauty the identical bet at every single awakening, and look at how she does across ALL those awakenings pooled together. Since 1/3 of all awakenings (across trials) are Heads-awakenings and 2/3 are Tails-awakenings, betting according to credence 1/3 is exactly calibrated to that pool, and betting according to 1/2 will loose her money on average -- so 1/3 is "the" credence a rational bettor converges on, thirdism wins.

My worry: that argument only shows 1/3 is calibrated to the frequency of Heads among awakenings. If you pool by trial instead -- i.e. you only care whether each individual coin flip was Heads or Tails, and don't care that Tails-trials happen to generate two occasions on which the "same" bet gets re-offered to an amnesiac Beauty -- then 1/2 is exactly the calibrated credence for THAT pooling, and 1/3 is the one that's off. Both poolings seem like perfectly real reference classes, and nothing in the setup tells you which one betting behavior is supposed to track. So it looks like the standard argument just picks the awakening-pooling by fiat and calls the result "rationality," when really the choice of pooling is doing all the work credence was supposed to do.

If that's right, it seems like a big chunk of the pro-thirder betting literature quietly begs the question, which would surprise me given how often it gets treated as close to decisive. So: (1) is this reasoning actually sound, or is there a standard reason to prefer the awakening-pooling that I'm missing; (2) is this a known objection under some other name that I'm just forgetting, so I don't stand up and "discover" something that got dealt with ages ago; and (3) even if it's correct, is it too in-the-weeds to be worth five minutes in a talk that's mostly about self-locating belief more generally, or does it actually cut to the heart of the matter?
