Out of curiosity I looked up what else this guy wrote, and apparently in 2001 he put out a short book called "On Immigration and Refugees" arguing that the burden of proof should always be on a state to justify keeping someone out, never on the migrant or refugee to justify getting in, and that most of the reasons Britain actually gave for restricting entry back then were just racism wearing a policy costume. Total whiplash going from Frege and non-classical logic to that, so now I'm curious whether the actual argument holds up on its own, once you get past "well, obviously don't be racist."

Here's what I want: an essay that treats this as a live, unresolved controversy rather than a foregone conclusion. I also read that Wellman has this move where a state can basically buy its way out of the obligation -- do enough for outsiders some other way (foreign aid, whatever) and that counts as having discharged the duty, so you still get to shut the border on the single neediest person on earth. I genuinely can't tell if that's a clever point or a total dodge, which is partly why I want your take.
