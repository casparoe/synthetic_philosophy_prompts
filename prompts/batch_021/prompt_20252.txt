In my metaontology seminar we've been working through the Hirsch–Sider debate on quantifier variance. I understand Hirsch's charity-based argument that disputes like universalism versus nihilism about composition are merely verbal, since each side can be interpreted as using "exists" with whatever meaning makes their assertions true. I also follow Sider's response: stipulate a technical "Ontologese" quantifier as a theoretical term that picks out whichever candidate meaning carves nature at the joints, which exempts it from the same charity-driven metasemantics that governs ordinary words. What I can't work out is why this doesn't just relocate the problem rather than solve it: since "joint-carving" is itself just a term, what stops Hirsch from running the same charitable-interpretation argument on it, so that the question of which quantifier is privileged becomes merely verbal one level up? Could you explain whether, and how, Sider is meant to block this regress?
