My friend Mara's been using one of those AI "agent" apps for work travel, and last week she told it to book the cheapest nonstop to Denver on Thursday. It booked a pricier flight instead, explaining that her calendar showed back-to-back meetings the next morning, so it skipped the cheaper early and delayed options. Mara was annoyed — she said it should just do what she tells it, that's the whole point of giving an instruction. Her housemate Priya pushed back, saying that's basically the logic behind "coherent extrapolated volition" in AI alignment stuff: a good assistant acts on what you'd actually want on reflection, not whatever you typed in a rush. Mara said fine, but if it can override her literal words anytime it decides it knows her "real" preferences better, theres no point giving instructions at all. Priya said there's an obvious difference between overriding a clear preference and filling in a detail she plainly hadn't considered, and this was the second thing. Mara said "obviously" is doing all the work there, since Priya can't actually say where filling-in-details ends and overriding begins — and anyway, by Priya's own logic the app should've also extrapolated that Mara has a standing preference to just be taken literally, which it never checked for either. Priya said you can't interrogate every possible meta preference before booking a flight, eventually you just have to use reasonable defaults. Neither budged. Who's actually right here, move by move, and is there something important both of them are missing?
