So I've been falling down an anthropic-reasoning rabbit hole again this week (blame a slow week at work and way too many old LessWrong/Overcoming Bias tabs open), and something's been bugging me that I can't quite untangle on my own. People throw around "the Doomsday Argument" like it's one thing, but as far as I can tell there are at least two genuinely different arguments hiding under that label. There's the Carter/Leslie/Bostrom version, built on the Self-Sampling Assumption, where you treat your own birth rank as basically a random draw from the set of all humans who will ever be born, which pushes probability mass toward "we're closer to the end than intuition suggests." And then there's Gott's version with the famous Berlin Wall story, where he's not sampling from a population of observers at all—he's treating his own moment in time as a random draw from the total lifespan of the wall (and then, by extension, humanity), which spits out a similar "don't expect this to last too much longer" conclusion through completely different math involving confidence intervals on remaining duration given current age.

What I want is an actual careful comparison, not just "well they're both about humanity maybe ending sooner than we'd like," since that's roughly the level of understanding I'm trying to get past. Are these two arguments doing the same logical work in different clothes, or do they rest on genuinely different assumptions and fail (or succeed) for different reasons? My vague memory is that Gott's version gets accused of smuggling in an arbitrary prior over durations, while the SSA version gets hit with the whole reference-class problem (who even counts as "an observer like me," anyway). So: which one is more defensible, or at least less obviously broken? And as a bonus round, since I know this is the standard counter-move: does the Self-Indication Assumption undercut both versions equally, or does it only really bite one of them while leaving the other standing? Curious to hear this actually reasoned through rather then just asserted.
