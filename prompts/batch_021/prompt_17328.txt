So I've been messing around with interpretability tools at work lately (we fine-tune open source LLMs for a b2b product and I got curious how the things actually work under the hood), and I fell down a rabbit hole reading stuff like Anthropic's "toy models of superposition" paper and some of the transformer-circuits writeups. Everyone in that world talks pretty casually about models "representing" concepts - like theres a direction in activation space that represents refusal, or sentiment, or curvature, whatever, and if you can train a linear probe to decode it from the hidden states, thats treated as evidence the concept is genuinely "in there." That started to bug me though, because I've also seen people point out (something about control tasks for probes, I think it was a Hewitt & Liang paper?) that a probe can get decent accuracy even on stuff that seems like it shouldn't really be encoded, which makes me think decodability by itself can't be the bar for something counting as a real representation instead of just an artifact of how flexible the probe is.

A buddy of mine who did a philosophy minor in undergrad said this is basically an old fight from philosophy of mind about mental representation, just wearing a new outfit, and mentioned something called the "job description challenge" - like you have to show a posited representation is doing actual explanatory/causal work, not just correlating with something, before it earns the label. I don't have the background to know how that's even supposed to map onto the neural net case, or whether people who study this stuff agree it does.

Can you walk me through what the real crux of the "do neural nets have representations" debate actually is? I don't need the generic "neural nets are black boxes" spiel, I've read plenty of that already. I'm more interested in, concretely, what would separate a network that "really" has a representation of some feature from one that just has useful structure we're layering representational language on top of, and whether there's any principled way people think you could settle that, or if it basically bottoms out in how you choose to define "representation" in the first place.
