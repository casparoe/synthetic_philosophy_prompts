Quine's gavagai example asks us to imagine a field linguist dropped into a community whose language has never been translated, sharing no language with the natives. A rabbit runs past, and the native says "Gavagai." The linguist's first guess is "Rabbit," since that's the obvious cue. Quine's point is that the very same behavioral evidence — natives assent to "Gavagai" exactly when a rabbit-ish stimulation is present, and dissent otherwise — is equally compatible with translating "gavagai" as "undetached rabbit part," "rabbit-stage," or "rabbithood," since wherever a whole rabbit is present, a rabbit-stage and undetached rabbit parts are present too. All these candidates agree on stimulus meaning — the class of stimulations that would prompt assent or dissent to the sentence — so stimulus meaning alone cannot decide among them.

Quine insists this goes beyond ordinary underdetermination of theory by evidence, but the underlying structure is the same: with only a finite stretch of fieldwork behind her, the linguist hasn't yet tried the tests that would come apart in their predictions for the rival hypotheses — for instance, eliciting the natives' identity judgments about whether two rabbit-sightings involve "the same gavagai," or seeing how they count gavagais compared to how we'd count rabbits, parts, or stages. Once she runs those tests, the field of viable translation manuals narrows, just as further observations narrow the field of viable theories under Quine's own holism about theory-testing. So translating "gavagai" is underdetermined by the linguist's data in exactly the way any theoretical claim is underdetermined by a finite run of observations: the indeterminacy marks how much fieldwork remains to be done, not a permanent feature of the case.

Because of this, indeterminacy of translation and inscrutability of reference really come to the same thing in the end: both just amount to the observation that "rabbit," "rabbit-stage," and "undetached rabbit part" pick out different objects, so which object the native refers to is inscrutable — and that inscrutability is exactly what makes translating "gavagai" indeterminate. If reference were scrutable, translation would not be indeterminate, so the two theses rise and fall together.
