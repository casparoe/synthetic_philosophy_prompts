For the past few years I've co-written a multi-volume fantasy series with a collaborator, and we've hit an impasse that has proven surprisingly hard to resolve. Across the earlier books we established, through a handful of scenes rather than any explicit statement, that a certain binding spell works whenever a mage learns a creature's true name, and that it succeeds on animals and humans but fails outright on ghosts. The volume we're drafting now hinges on whether that spell can bind a golem: a body of animated clay that was never alive and has no pulse, yet is very much physical. My collaborator is certain the spell must fail, on the grounds that it has only ever worked on things that are alive; I'm equally certain it must succeed, on the grounds that it has only ever failed on things without a physical body. Both readings fit every scene we've actually written, and even our old outlines and notes turn out to be silent on the question, so neither rereading the text nor comparing our original intentions has settled anything. Is one of us actually right about what our rule already implied, or is there something confused about assuming the earlier books settled this at all?
