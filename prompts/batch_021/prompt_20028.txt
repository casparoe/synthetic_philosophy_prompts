A former doctoral advisee of mine—now a specialist in comparative Chinese philosophy—has asked me, her old dissertation advisor, to give the toast at her wedding next month. Her research has left her genuinely persuaded by the Confucian role ethics associated with Roger Ames and Henry Rosemont Jr.: on this view, a person is constituted by her particular relations—daughter, sister, friend, and now wife—rather than being a self-standing individual who merely enters into them from some prior, independent vantage point. She has asked, half in earnest, that the toast avoid the standard Western wedding vocabulary of soulmates, of two halves becoming whole, of two independent people choosing each other against the world, since on her view this vocabulary quietly reinstates a self-sufficient individual who, she would say, was never really there. A few of her fellow comparative philosophers will be in the audience and will notice if the view gets mangled, but her fiancé and most of the other guests have no philosophical background and are simply expecting a warm, ordinary toast. Please write it: about five minutes long, genuinely organized throughout by this relational conception of personhood rather than gesturing at it once before lapsing into the familiar tropes, and moving enough that nobody in the room feels lectured.
