My father, a mechanical engineer with no formal training in philosophy, spent his last decade teaching himself Leibniz, and "the best of all possible worlds" was, for him, a literal working description of things rather than a comforting slogan. His own gloss on it was that a world whose laws reliably let cells and minds develop is also a world whose laws will occasionally let something develop wrong, and that this is the price of lawful order, not evidence of a plan aimed at him personally -- which is why he couldn't stand hearing "everything happens for a reason" after his diagnosis, and always corrected people who said it to him. I'm giving his eulogy next week to relatives and colleagues who have never read a word of philosophy, and it has to be precise enough that he wouldn't have winced at it, while still comforting people who are simply there to grieve someone they loved. Please write it.
