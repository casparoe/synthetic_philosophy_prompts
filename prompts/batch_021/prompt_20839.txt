Ok, this has been rattling around in my head for weeks and I can't fully resolve it on my own, so figured I'd ask. Back in 2019 I was part of a small tenants committee in my apartment building (maybe 30-ish people total) that organized against a massive rent hike our landlord tried to push through — we did petitions, packed a city council meeting, got a tiny mention in the local paper, and the landlord eventually backed down. I've told this story for years as basically "we organized and we won." But a friend of mine, who's kind of obsessed with those history books that ask 'what if this one event had gone differently,' pointed out over drinks recently that our city was already tightening its rent stabilization rules that same year, and two other buildings nearby got very similar rollbacks around the same time without any tenant group doing anything at all. So maybe our landlord folds regardless of what we specifically did, just a bit later or on slightly worse terms for us.

Now I keep going back and forth on wether that means our little campaign didn't actually matter. It feels like a shrunk-down version of the argument historians have about whether WWI happens without the assassination in Sarajevo — like, if the tensions in Europe were going to blow up eventually no matter what, does Gavrilo Princip pulling the trigger even count as 'causing' the war in any meaningful sense? Same question, just at the scale of my dumb apartment building instead of a continent. My gut says our committee still did something real, but I can't articulate why that isn't just me being defensive about like 200 hours of my life I sunk into this. I've been lurking on LW and ACX for years and feel like there should be a more rigorous way to think about this than just vibes — is there an actual principled answer here, or does the question just dissolve once you look at it closely enough?
