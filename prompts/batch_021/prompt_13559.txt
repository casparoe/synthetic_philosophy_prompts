After a talk in our philosophy of physics reading group, two colleagues of mine fell into a disagreement about the hole argument that has outlasted the seminar itself, and neither will let it drop.

Marcus's opening claim was that the argument refutes substantivalism about spacetime points outright: if points are individuals whose identity is fixed independently of the metric, then an active diffeomorphism that is the identity outside some matter-free 'hole' region but smoothly redistributes the metric inside it yields a second, equally admissible solution of Einstein's field equations -- one that agrees with the original everywhere outside the hole and diverges inside it, though no physical fact could ever mark the difference. That, he said, saddles substantivalism with an indeterminism so gratuitous that only relationalism escapes it.

Elena answered that a substantivalist can block this without turning relationalist, by denying that spacetime points have primitive, haecceitistic identity across models: hold that diffeomorphic models depict one and the same possibility, so there is no indeterminism to explain, while still insisting that within a single model the points are basic, substantival individuals rather than constructs out of relations among fields -- a position that gives up only cross-model identity, not individuality.

Marcus objected that it gives up everything that made the position interesting: once sameness of possibility tracks isomorphism of qualitative structure, the structure is doing all the metaphysical work, and calling the points 'individuals' adds nothing beyond a label -- which is relationalism wearing a substantivalist costume.

Elena replied that the label is not idle, since her substantivalist, unlike a relationalist, can still say of a given world that its points would have existed unoccupied by any field value, there being nothing for the points to reduce to; a relationalist has no subject left for that counterfactual.

Marcus's rejoinder was that this just relocates the problem: securing a determinate 'this very point, unoccupied' counterfactual requires exactly the sort of cross-model identity that Elena had already given up in order to block the original argument, so she cannot consistently help herself to both.

They shelved the dispute only because the department happy hour intervened, and asked me to rule on it, which I've been avoiding. Could you go through the exchange move by move, saying who has the better of each individual point rather than just declaring an overall winner, and flag anything relevant that neither of them thought to raise?
