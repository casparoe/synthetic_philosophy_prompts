My aunt Ruth ran our small-town paper for almost 40 years until it got bought out and shut down in 2019 - she died this Tuesday and Im giving the eulogy Saturday. She was a total news obsessive but also, weirdly, a lifelong Kierkegaard nerd - I write about science and ideas for a living, so I blame her for that. She always said his stuff on "the public" and the press was basically a diagnosis of her own trade: an anonymous, leveling "phantom" that lets everyone have opinions and nobody answer for them, versus what she thought real journalism should be, named people saying named things they'd stand behind. The room Saturday is maybe 10% people who'd catch a Kierkegaard reference and 90% family and old subscribers who just want to remember Ruth. Write it so it actually sits with that tension instead of turning into either a generic "she informed her community" speech or a philosophy seminar that loses grandma in the second row. Keep it to about four minutes.
