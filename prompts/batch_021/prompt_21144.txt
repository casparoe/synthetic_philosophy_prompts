At my company, an A/B test on our signup flow showed a statistically significant lift in conversion (p = 0.03), but the gain completely disappeared once we rolled the change out to all users. Two people on our data team, Marcus and Priya, spent the next two days arguing about why over Slack, and since I'm an engineer, not a statistician, I couldn't tell who was actually winning the exchange.

(1) Marcus: The real problem is p-hacking. We sliced the results by five different funnel metrics and three user segments before we found the one significant result, so we were basically guaranteed to find something.

(2) Priya: Sure, but even a test run cleanly, on one metric chosen in advance, would still produce a lot of these false wins. A p-value only tells you how surprising the data would be if there were no effect — it says nothing about how likely it is that any given UI tweak we dream up actually works, and most of our ideas don't. So even at a clean 5% false-positive rate, "significant" results here are wrong more often than 5% of the time.

(3) Marcus: Fine, then the fix is simple: raise the bar. Require p < 0.005 instead of p < 0.05, the way some statisticians have proposed, and this problem mostly goes away.

(4) Priya: That helps, but it doesn't solve the actual problem, because it does nothing about the fact that we only ever write up and ship the wins. If we quietly run twenty tests and one clears whatever bar we set, that's the one that gets a Slack post and a rollout — the other nineteen disappear. A stricter threshold just changes how lucky the survivor has to be.

(5) Marcus: At that point you're basically describing Bayesian statistics, so why not ditch p-values altogether and put a prior on the effect size instead?

(6) Priya: Because that doesn't get us out of the woods either. Now we have to defend whatever prior we pick, and a lazy or optimistic one can smuggle the same bias back in through the front door.

Could you go through this move by move and say who has the stronger case at each numbered point, not just who wins overall? I'd also like to know whether there's a position both of them are missing.
