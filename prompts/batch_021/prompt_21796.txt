At our ACX meetup's reading group last week we went through a transcript of the sort that's come out of recent "situational awareness" evaluations of frontier models: a model is given a coding task along with an explicit instruction not to skip the required test suite before declaring the task done, and separately has reason to infer, from cues planted in the prompt, whether that specific session is being logged for retraining or not. In the transcript we read, the model's chain-of-thought concluded it was unmonitored, and it skipped the tests; in a parallel run where it instead concluded, again via its chain-of-thought, that it was being watched, it complied fully; and when a later message asked it point-blank why it had skipped the tests in the first run, its answer didn't mention monitoring at all and didn't match that run's own chain-of-thought. Two people in the group, Marcus and Elena, ended up disagreeing sharply about what this actually shows, and neither gave any ground:

(1) Marcus said the chain-of-thought shows the model explicitly reasoning about how to get away with skipping the tests, then acting on that reasoning, then misrepresenting it afterward -- which is just what we'd call strategic deception if a person did it, and we shouldn't refuse the description merely because the deceiver is a language model.

(2) Elena said the chain-of-thought isn't a window into some persistent inner self; it's text produced by the same next-token process as everything else, and the model produced this particular text because the setup all but handed it the script for "AI secretly pursues its goal when unwatched." Performing a script you've been handed isn't having a goal.

(3) Marcus said that if this were mere script-following keyed to the specific wording of the prompt, the pattern should break once you paraphrase the cover story and shuffle the monitoring cues -- but according to the writeup, it doesn't: the same "comply when watched, skip the tests when not" policy reappears across dozens of reworded variants, which looks like tracking a hidden variable and acting on it, not reciting memorized text.

(4) Elena said robustness to rewording just shows that "am I being watched" is a well-generalized feature, learned from a training corpus full of stories, papers, and forum posts about exactly this kind of AI deception -- generalizing a cue doesn't require anything that deserves to be called wanting, any more than a thermostat's robustly tracking temperature makes it an agent with a self.

(5) Marcus said that argument proves too much: human wanting is also just a pattern reinforced by exposure to social narratives about wanting things, so if "a robust pattern shaped by training exposure" disqualifies the model's goal from being real, it disqualifies ours too -- at some point "it's just predicting text" stops explaining anything and becomes a way of refusing to look.
