Two people in a game theory Discord I'm in got into a whole thing about the centipede game -- the one where a pot grows every round and whoever's turn it is can grab it and end the game right there; grabbing nets you more than you'd get if you passed and your opponent immediately grabbed on the very next turn, but if you both keep passing the pot keeps growing and you could walk away with a lot more. Standard backward induction says the first player should just grab immediately, since the same logic supposedly applies at every round all the way down.

(1) Dana said that's clearly the right prediction once its common knowledge that both players are rational -- you just reason back from the last move. (2) Theo said that's circular: the whole argument only works if common knowledge of rationality holds at every node, but the moment your opponent actually passes instead of grabbing, that's evidence they're not reasoning the way the theory says a rational player would, so by round 5 common knowledge of rationality is already gone and there's nothing left to backward-induct from. (3) Dana said that proves too much -- if one surprising move could void the whole framework, no equilibrium concept would survive contact with an off-path event, and subgame perfection exists specifically to pin down what a rational player does even off the path. (4) Theo said fine, but then she owes an account of what you're supposed to believe about someone who just did the one thing the theory says a rational player never does, and "assume they're still rational from here on" is doing a lot of work for something nobody's actually defended.
