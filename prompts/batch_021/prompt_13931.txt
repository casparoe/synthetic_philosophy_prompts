Me and my friend Priya got into it for like an hour last night over whether you're rationally required to just adopt whatever belief you already know you'll end up having later on. Her take was: if you're dead certain future-you will believe X, present-you should basically already believe X now too, otherwise you're being inconsistent with your own future self. I pushed back with stuff like — what if future-you only believes X because you'll have forgotten the counterarguments by then, or gotten worn down by everyone else, or you're just older and more cynical? None of that seems like a good reason for present-you to budge. We never sorted it out, but this morning I looked into it and apparently theres an actual principle for the position Priya was defending, van Fraassen's Reflection principle. Can you walk through the most important implications of the Reflection principle for disagreements like ours, especially the cases where the anticipated future belief change isn't obviously coming from better evidence or clearer thinking?
