So after I retired (35 years as a research chemist, nothing to do with finance) I fell down the Kelly criterion rabbit hole through low-stakes poker, of all things -- bet a fraction of your bankroll each time so as to maximise the expected log of your wealth rather than its expected value -- and that's dragged me into reading decision theory and philosophy of rationality for the first time in my life. Now I keep running into this "geometric rationality" / ergodicity economics stuff (Ole Peters and that crowd), which frames it as a genuinely different notion of rationality under uncertainty: optimise the time-average growth rate, not the ensemble expected value. Apparently it drove Samuelson up the wall for decades -- IIRC he even wrote a whole paper against the "mean-log" camp using only one-syllable words, just to mock how simple-minded he found the arguement.

Can you actually compare these two notions of rationality for me -- the geometric/time-average one versus plain expected utility theory? I get that they agree whenever utility happens to be logarithmic. What I can't work out is whether Peters has a real arguement that the log-growth criterion is forced on you by the dynamics alone, independent of whatever utility function you hold -- or whether "time-average" is just log utility sneaking back in under a new name. Who's actually got the better of it, him or Samuelson?
