So this went down at the semi-regular poker-and-forecasting meetup my group runs (a bunch of us met through LW and ACX originally, if that tells you anything about the vibe), and I haven't been able to stop replaying it. My friend Marcus, who does prop betting on the side, was arguing that you should basically always size bets with full Kelly, because Kelly is *the* growth-optimal strategy: if you bet the Kelly fraction, then with probability 1 your bankroll eventually overtakes the bankroll of anyone running a meaningfully different fixed strategy, given enough repeated bets. My other friend Elena, who does quant risk for a living, said "eventually, given enough bets" is doing a ton of unearned work, since real people get a finite number of bets before they run out of edge or capital, and over any realistic horizon full Kelly has a genuine shot at loosing 50-70% of your stack at some point, which is exactly why every actual fund or pro bettor she's ever read about runs half- or quarter-Kelly instead.

Marcus's reply was that this isn't really a counterargument, it's a category error: the log utility baked into Kelly already prices in drawdown risk (that's precisely why Kelly never bets your whole stack on a positive-EV wager), so if you want less variance than full Kelly gives you, you're not being "more prudent," you've just quietly adopted a different, non-log utility function for wealth -- and you should say so, instead of talking as if half-Kelly is a free-lunch improvement on the objectively correct bet size. Elena's comeback to that was that this is the exact move the ergodicity-economics crowd (Ole Peters and co, the stuff that made the rounds on LW/ACX a few years back) always pulls: "maximizes almost-sure long-run growth rate" and "maximizes my expected utility over the horizon I actually have" are different criteria that only coincide for log utility in the infinite limit, so presenting full Kelly as the rational bet size, rather than the size that's optimal under one specific utility function plus one specific asymptotic criterion, is the actual overclaim here -- and Marcus, in her telling, was just smuggling that same conflation back in by treating any non-log utility function as a euphemism for cowardice.

Neither of them budged and it kind of killed the vibe for the rest of poker night. I've read enough on this stuff to know both of them are gesturing at something real, but not enough to actually settle it myself. Could you go through this move by move -- Marcus's opening claim, Elena's finite-horizon reply, Marcus's "that's just a different utility function" comeback, and Elena's "ergodicity-economics sleight of hand" closer -- and say who's got the better of each specific move, not just who wins overall? I'd also genuinely like to know if there's some third framing neither of them reached for that would dissolve this instead of just restating one side more forcefully.
