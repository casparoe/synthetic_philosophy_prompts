Hi - so after 30-odd years running a low-temperature condensed matter lab I retired a couple years back and I've been reading my way through philosophy of social science for fun. I keep bumping into the individualism vs holism argument (Weber/Popper/Hayek insisting a real explanation of social stuff has to bottom out in individuals and their beliefs and actions; Durkheim insisting some "social facts" are sui generis and just don't survive being cashed out person by person), and I scribbled some notes trying to connect it to my own field. Would love your honest comments before I embarrass myself showing this to an actual philosopher freind.

Rough idea: bulk magnetization below the Curie point is, no argument, "nothing but" a huge pile of interacting spins. But right near the critical point the measurable behaviour - the critical exponents, the scaling laws - is universal: wildly different microscopic systems (a magnet, a fluid going critical near its boiling point) land on exactly the same numbers. Wilson's renormalization group is basically the proof that the microscopic details wash out and only a few coarse features (dimension, symmetry) survive into the macro law. So you get a macro regularity that's ontologically nothing but the micro facts, but you'd be a fool to try and predict it by simulating every spin - the individual-level story doesn't even make the relevant pattern (universality) visible.

My tentative, probably-not-original claim: the individualists are right ontologically, there's nothing "over and above" individuals, but wrong to also insist thats how you have to explain things - that's like insisting thermodynamics isn't a real explanation until it's rewritten in statistical mechanics. Meanwhile the Durkheim-style holists sometimes seem to want more than my physics case gives them license for, something like downward causation of the whole onto its parts, and bulk magnetization doesn't push around individual spins in any way I can see an analogy for.

Things I'd like you to actually push back on rather than just being nice:
1. Is "ontological vs explanatory individualism" already a standard move in this literature, or have I badly reinvented something that's already been shot down?
2. Is there a specific, well-known case in the social sciences that this analogy just can't handle - somewhere you genuinely need downward causation, not just multiple-realizability dressed up?
3. Am I overselling the physics - is the whole universality-class business actually a fair disanalogy generator here, given how idealized (infinite, homogeneous, symmetric) those systems are compared to messy, historically contingent social ones?
