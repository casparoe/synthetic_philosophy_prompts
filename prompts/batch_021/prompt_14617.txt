I've been down a Vienna Circle rabbit hole for a piece I'm working on, and here's what got me: Carnap flat-out called himself a socialist in his intellectual autobiography, and he was genuinely close with Otto Neurath, who spent years arguing against Mises that a planned economy could run on "calculation in kind" instead of money and prices. At the same time, Carnap insisted logic and the "logic of science" are neutral, totally seperate from anyone's politics. Now there's basically a rematch of that old fight -- economists, and weirdly tech billionaires like Alibaba's Jack Ma, claiming AI and big data finally crack the computation problem that was supposed to have sunk Neurath's side for good. Given his actual politics and that neutrality thesis, how do you think Carnap would size up that crowd -- an engineering question unified science could eventually settle, or just another case where his own thesis means planning-vs-markets stays a clash of values no amount of compute touches? And is there an irony in the fact that Neurath wanted to elevate broad social welfare over strict utility-maximization, which is basically the opposite of what these AI optimizers do?
