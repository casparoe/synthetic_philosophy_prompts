I'm drafting a novel centered on a fictional scientific revolution: an aging astronomer, still devoted to the cosmological model that made her career, is confronted by her former student, who has spent a decade advancing the theory meant to replace it. I want their climactic argument to dramatize Kuhnian incommensurability with some real precision, rather than treating "paradigm shift" as a loose synonym for "big change." Since Kuhn's point is that rival paradigms are incommensurable — the two scientists share no common vocabulary or standard by which either could even recognize the other's claims, let alone judge them — I'm planning to write the confrontation as a genuine non-conversation, where nothing either woman says ever registers to the other as a counterargument. Before I commit to that structure: what's the most historically grounded way to stage that kind of total mutual incomprehension?
