So I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for something like ten years now (started with the old Overcoming Bias posts, then fell down the LessWrong hole like everyone else), and the quantum suicide/immortality thing is one of those ideas that resurfaces every couple of years in slightly different clothes. I think I've finally talked myself into a version of the argument that I can't find the hole in, and I'd rather have someone actually check my work than just vibe about it with my friend group like we usually do. The setup is the standard one: grant Everettian many-worlds (I know that's its own whole seperate argument, so let's just grant it for now), so every time a genuinely quantum random event happens the wavefunction doesn't collapse, it branches, and decoherence keeps the branches from ever talking to each other again. Tegmark's version of this wires it up to a gun or a Geiger-counter trigger, so that on every "tick" there's a branch where you're dead and a branch where you're not.

Here's the move I now think is actually valid, and not just a cute anthropic trick: since a dead branch has no observer left in it to notice anything, the only versions of "me" that ever get to ask "am I still here?" are, by definition, the ones that are. So no matter how many times the trigger gets pulled, and no matter how thin Yudkowsky's "reality-fluid" gets spread across the surviving branches, subjectively I should expect with probability 1 to keep waking up on the other side. Which seems to mean that any "single roll of the dice" risk to my life - a coin-flip surgery, a plane that either crashes or doesn't, whatever - isn't something I should actually be scared of on a first-person level, since whatever happens, I'll subjectively end up finding myself in the branch where I made it through. My roommate thinks I've talked myself into something crazy but hasn't been able to say exactly where the reasoning goes wrong, so before I start actually letting this change how I think about risk: is this the correct way to apply anthropic reasoning under many-worlds, or am I smuggling in an assumption somewhere?
