My closest friend from undergrad is hosting her daughter Elena's first birthday party next month and has asked me -- I'm now a bioethics PhD student -- to say a few words. Some context that needs to shape what I write: she and her husband deliberately delayed trying to conceive for three years after grad school, partly over money and partly because she genuinely wasn't sure it was right to bring a child into a warming world. She's since confessed to me, quietly, that she still feels an odd guilt about those years, as if she'd made Elena wait to be born -- as if three years had been taken from this specific child. I'd like the toast to work through that thought using the actual logic of Parfit's nonidentity problem: Elena isn't a delayed version of some earlier child, because a different pregnancy would have produced a different person entirely, so there's no one those years were taken from. But I don't want the toast to pretend that settles everything, since it leaves untouched the harder question of whether it was right to time a child's existence around one's own fears in the first place. A few people from my cohort will be there and will catch it if I mangle the argument, but everyone else will be family who've never heard of Parfit. Could you write it -- two to three minutes, spoken aloud, warm rather than clever?
