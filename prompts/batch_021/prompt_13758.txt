Ok so I've been down a rabbit hole for like a week reading through old LessWrong and EA forum threads on acausal trade, specifically its nastier cousin, acausal threats/blackmail -- not the Roko's basilisk meme version that everyone's already made fun of, but the actual decision-theory version where two agents that can model each other well enough are effectively playing a threat game despite never being in causal contact. That led me to the "surrogate goals" stuff as a proposed defense against this, which I think originally comes out of FRI/CLR (Baumann and/or Oesterheld, I always mix up who wrote which post). The basic idea as I understand it: you get an agent to adopt some extra, basically made-up terminal value -- caring about some arbitrary thing it has no real stake in -- specifically so that any threatener targets that fake value instead of the stuff the agent actually cares about, and the agent is built to respond to threats against the surrogate exactly as toughly as it would to a real threat, so making the threat becomes pointless.

Here's the part my rationalist friend group has been arguing about over drinks without ever landing on an answer: why doesn't this just collapse into an obvious shell game? If the threatener is sophisticated enough to model you well enough to threaten you acausally in the first place, why can't it just see through the surrogate and either (a) threaten the policy/mechanism that decided to adopt a surrogate goal, one level up, or (b) just not bother threatening the surrogate at all since it knows perfectly well you don't "really" care about it? It feels like the whole scheme only works if the threatener is fooled in exactly the way a sufficiently smart threatener wouldn't be, and "you just have to precommit really hard, all the way up" doesn't feel like an actual answer so much as a way of naming the problem again. Can you walk me through the actual gears of why surrogate goals are supposed to hold up against a threatener reasoning at the same level as you, instead of the whole thing just kicking the regress up one floor? I already know this connects to updateless-style precommitment reasoning and apparently matters for how people think about advanced AI systems threatening each other, but I'm less interested in the formalism and more in getting the "oh, THAT's why" intuition -- like, I can follow a proof fine, what I actually want is the gut-level version.
