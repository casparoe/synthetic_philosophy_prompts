For my philosophy elective's final project on Kantian ethics, we have to write a dialogue that applies the universalizability test (the Formula of Universal Law) to a case of our own choosing, and our teacher has specifically banned the shopkeeper and false-promising examples because she says everyone just recites the textbook answer for those instead of actually thinking. My friend Lena and I picked the case of using AI to write school essays, and we have been stuck in a real disagreement for weeks that I do not think either of us can resolve alone. Lena argues this is structurally identical to the false-promising case: if everyone submitted AI-written work as their own whenever it was convenient, the whole point of assigning essays (to give evidence of a student's own understanding) would collapse, so the maxim fails by a strict contradiction in conception and is wrong even to act on once. I think it is closer to the duties to develop one's talents or help others in need — you can perfectly well conceive of a world where most people lean on AI, since schools would simply adapt with oral exams or in-class writing, so there is no contradiction in conception, only at most a contradiction in the will, which would make it an imperfect duty you can weigh against other things rather than an absolute prohibition. Neither of us can even agree on how to word the maxim in the first place, which I am starting to suspect is the actual source of the disagreement.

Could you write this dialogue for us? I would like it to be a genuine back-and-forth between two equally sharp Kantians, one arguing Lena's side and one arguing mine, where each character responds directly to the other's actual argument rather than repeating their own point, and where the disagreement over how narrowly or broadly to formulate the maxim gets addressed head-on instead of glossed over. If possible, I would like the two characters to end by pinning down exactly where their disagreement really lies — whether it is a factual dispute about what would actually happen if the maxim were universalized, or a prior disagreement about which formulation of the maxim is even the legitimate one to test — even if they cannot fully settle it by the end.
