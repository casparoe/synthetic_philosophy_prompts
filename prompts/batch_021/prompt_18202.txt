Descartes usually gets flattened into "he thought animals are just unfeeling machines," but that's actually a live interpretive fight -- his letters to Newcastle and to More, plus the language and action tests he lays out in Part V of the Discourse, leave real room to argue he thought animals lack reason but still feel pain in some lesser sense, rather then that there's nothing it's like to be a dog getting kicked at all. Write an essay adjudicating this using the actual texts, not the standard beast-machine caricature -- weigh the evidence on both readings and commit to a verdict: did Descartes think animals genuinely suffer, or not?
