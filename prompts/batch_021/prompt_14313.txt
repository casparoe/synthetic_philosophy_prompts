Lately, whenever I mention that I spent an afternoon sketching in a notebook nobody else will see, working out a guitar solo I have no intention of performing, or just shooting hoops with no score being kept, my parents and my college counselor ask the same question: what's it for? I've started asking myself that too, and it unsettles me, because if I follow the logic all the way through, everything turns out to be a means to something else — the club is for the résumé, the résumé is for college, college is for the job, and presumably the job is for retirement — so that nothing along the way is ever valuable for its own sake. Meanwhile, the one friend who genuinely seems happy despite her brutal robotics-competition schedule treats it like play rather than a stepping stone to a scholarship, while the rest of us grind through similar hours mainly to have something to show for them afterward. Is my sense that those unscored, seemingly pointless hours might be the most worthwhile part of my week actually defensible, or am I underrating how much a life needs to be building toward something in order to count as well spent?
