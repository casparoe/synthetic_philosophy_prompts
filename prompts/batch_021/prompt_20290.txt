I'm working on a longer feature about how economists put a present-day price tag on climate damage that won't hit for another 50-100 years (the whole "social cost of carbon" fight), and I've gone down a rabbit hole on discounting that I can't climb back out of. Everyone talks about "discounting the future" like its one move, but the more I dig into the postmortems on the Stern Review and the Nordhaus rebuttals, the clearer it gets that the standard discount rate is actually smuggling together two pretty different ideas: theres "pure time preference," which just means caring less about a unit of someone's wellbeing purely because it happens later, full stop, and then there's the separate move of discounting future consumption because people down the line will probably be richer, so an extra dollar (or a ton of avoided carbon damage) is worth less to them at the margin than it is to us now. Stern basically zeroed out the first one and got these huge, scary damage estimates; Nordhaus thought that was borderline dishonest and wanted something closer to observed market interest rates.

My economist friend swears these two things are worlds apart morally - one is basically arbitrary discrimination against people who don't exist yet, the other is just ordinary diminishing-marginal-utility reasoning that any decent utilitarian should already accept - but when I pushed her on why philosophers get so exercised about pure time preference specifically while mostly shrugging at the growth-adjusted kind, given that both spit out the exact same practical result (future welfare counts for less in the sum), she kind of hand-waved. I gather Frank Ramsey is the guy who kicked this off almost a century ago by basically calling pure time discounting indefensible. So could you walk me through the actual distinction properly: is there a real principled case for treating these two forms of discounting so differently, or is the growth-based one just pure time preference wearing a lab coat? Genuinely want a verdict here, not just a "well, philosophers disagree" shrug.
