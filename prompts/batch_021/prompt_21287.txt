I've been dipping back into old Overcoming Bias and LessWrong threads on anthropic reasoning, which reignited a running argument I've had with a friend from grad school about how to handle self-locating uncertainty. He's squarely in the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) camp: once you grant that your own existence is itself a piece of evidence, he thinks ordinary Bayesian conditionalization forces you to weight competing hypotheses by how many observers-like-you each one implies. I keep pushing back with Bostrom's presumptuous philosopher: two cosmological theories are tied on all the evidence physicists have gathered so far, except that one entails a vastly larger population of observers than the other, and SIA alone — no telescope, no new data, just headcounts — drives credence in the larger-universe theory to near certainty, enough, on the philosopher's telling, that the physicists needn't bother running their decisive experiment at all. That still strikes me as a reductio. But I'll grant that the rival view, the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA), doesn't come free either: it's what generates the Doomsday Argument's leap from "I am roughly the Nth human born" to a raised credence in near-term human extinction, its verdicts are hostage to how you draw the "reference class" of relevantly similar observers, and it's often paired with the "halfer" side of the Sleeping Beauty debate, which plenty of competent Bayesians find no less counterintuitive than anything SIA implies.

Could you write an essay that actually settles this rather than just laying out both sides? I'd like the strongest available case for SIA (including why the presumptuous philosopher is supposed to be devastating for it), the strongest available case for SSA (including how well or badly it copes with the Doomsday Argument, the reference-class problem, and Sleeping Beauty), and then a real verdict — either a clean choice between the two, or an explicitly stated and defended third option (reference-class relativism, a hybrid view that takes the "thirder" side on Sleeping Beauty without buying into full SIA, treating anthropic "probabilities" as decision-theoretic bookkeeping rather than genuine credences, or whatever you think actually works). A conditional verdict is fine ("SIA is correct once the reference class is fixed this way, otherwise SSA"), but please state the condition explicitly rather than hedging your way out of the question.
