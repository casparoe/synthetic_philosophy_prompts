I'm a philosophy major taking a seminar on duty and obligation this term, and I sit on my university's Student Petitions Committee, which rules on requests for retroactive excused absences. A case on Thursday's agenda seems to turn on exactly the distinction between required duty and going beyond it that we've been discussing in class, and I need to draft the committee's decision memo.

A resident assistant's job description obliges her, during a crisis, to contact campus safety and stay with a resident only until professional responders arrive. During a mental-health crisis last month, officers told her she was free to leave once they arrived, but she stayed with the resident through the night anyway. She missed her 9 a.m. final as a result and is petitioning for a make-up exam under our "extenuating circumstances" policy, which doesn't say whether going beyond a job's formal duties counts as such a circumstance.

The committee is split three ways. Some say this was just an extension of her existing duty of care, so no special claim follows. Others say everything she did after the officers arrived went clearly beyond anything her role required, and penalizing it would be perverse. Others worry that rewarding this sets an unfair precedent for students whose hardships gave them no chance to be "heroic," and that formalizing such rewards quietly turns admirable extras into new expectations.

Please draft the memo: a specific recommendation on the petition, reasoning the committee could act on, and a serious, fair treatment of the strongest objection to whatever you recommend.
