Write a dialogue between a rational-choice economist and a bounded-rationality theorist working in the tradition of Herbert Simon, set at a hiring committee meeting where the two disagree about whether to keep interviewing once a candidate clears the position's aspiration level. The economist argues that satisficing is not a genuine alternative to optimization, since any stopping rule can be redescribed as optimal once the costs of search and computation are folded into the objective function; the bounded-rationality theorist replies that this redescription explains nothing unless the relevant costs can be specified independently of the behavior they are meant to explain. Have the two characters engage directly with the specific argument the other has just made, rather than simply restating their own position.
