Okay so a few weeks ago I went down a Wikipedia rabbit hole on something called Moore's paradox — the thing where a sentence like "it's raining but I don't believe it's raining" sounds totally insane to say out loud even though, if you actually think about it, there's nothing technically contradictory about it (it could just be a true fact that it's raining and that some particular person doesn't believe it). I brought this up at a bbq because it reminded me of something my cousin actually said about her boyfriend a while back, pretty much word for word: "I know he's cheating on me, but I don't believe it." That set off a whole argument between two of my friends, Deb and Marcus, and I honestly couldn't tell who was winning, so I want someone smarter than me to referee it properly.

Roughly how it went, in order:

1. Deb said the sentence is just contradictory, plain and simple — if you actually know something you believe it, that's basically part of what knowing even means, so my cousin can't "know" and "not believe" the exact same thing at the exact same time.
2. Marcus said that's way too strict, because people say stuff exactly like this all the time when they're in denial, and it obviously describes something real that happens in people's heads, so if a definition says it's flat out impossible, maybe the definition needs work, not my cousin.
3. Deb said fine, then she doesn't technically "know" it, she just has a ton of evidence and is refusing to accept it — swap out the word "know" and the puzzle disapears completely.
4. Marcus said that's a bit of a dodge, since we use the word "know" loosely like that constantly in everyday talk ("I know I put my phone somewhere in this room"), so it's weird to suddenly demand strict precision only in this one case, just to make the weirdness go away.
5. Deb said okay, forget "know" then — even just "I believe he's cheating, but I don't believe it" sounds completely unhinged, so somethings wrong with the sentence itself, not just with sloppy word choice.
6. Marcus said sure, it sounds unhinged to say out loud, but that's a fact about how strange it is to say something about yourself, right now, in the moment — not proof that the state of mind behind it is actually impossible, since plenty of true stuff about people sounds like nonsense the second you try to say it about yourself.

Nobody backed down, we just ran out of chips.
