I'm a musicology PhD student currently drafting a dissertation chapter on 19th-century "absolute music" aesthetics (Hanslick, the early Wagnerians, that whole debate), and my advisor keeps pushing me to actually engage with the Schopenhauer metaphysics behind it instead of just gesturing at "he thought music was special." Problem is I never took a proper German idealism course, so I'm reading Book 3 of *The World as Will and Representation* (and the later supplement on music) mostly on my own, and I keep running into what feels like a flat contradiction in the space of a couple pages that none of the secondary lit I've found actually addresses head-on.

Early in §52 he says pretty unambiguously:

"Music is thus by no means like the other arts, the copy of the Ideas, but the copy of the will itself, whose objectivity the Ideas are. This is why the effect of music is so much more powerful and penetrating than that of the other arts, for they speak only of shadows, but it speaks of the thing itself."

Fine — so music, unlike sculpture or painting or poetry, supposedly bypasses the whole apparatus of the Platonic Ideas entirely and goes straight for the will. But then literally a page or two later he writes this (trimming a bit for length):

"I recognise in the deepest tones of harmony, in the bass, the lowest grades of the objectification of will, unorganised nature, the mass of the planet... Bass is thus, for us, in harmony what unorganised nature, the crudest mass, upon which all rests, and from which everything originates and develops, is in the world. Now, further, in the whole of the complemental parts which make up the harmony between the bass and the leading voice singing the melody, I recognise the whole gradation of the Ideas in which the will objectifies itself. Those nearer to the bass are the lower of these grades, the still unorganised, but yet manifold phenomenal things; the higher represent to me the world of plants and beasts. The definite intervals of the scale are parallel to the definite grades of the objectification of will, the definite species in nature... Lastly, in the melody, in the high, singing, principal voice leading the whole and progressing with unrestrained freedom, in the unbroken significant connection of one thought from beginning to end representing a whole, I recognise the highest grade of the objectification of will, the intellectual life and effort of man."

So which is it? He spends this whole stretch of text mapping very specific musical elements (bass, inner voices, melody) onto the very specific hierarchy of Ideas he laid out earlier (minerals, plants, animals, man) — which sure sounds like music representing the Ideas after all, just through a different sense. Its not obvious to me how "no resemblance to the Ideas, only a direct copy of the will" is supposed to be compatible with then drawing out a point-by-point correspondence between musical structure and the Idea-hierarchy. Is there some real technical distinction in his system between "parallelism/analogy" and "copy/resemblance" that does actual work here, or is he just quietly having it both ways and hoping nobody notices? I'd really appreciate a careful walk-through of how (if at all) this hangs together, ideally tied back to how will, representation, and Idea relate for him more generally, since that's honestly the part of the system I'm still shakiest on.
