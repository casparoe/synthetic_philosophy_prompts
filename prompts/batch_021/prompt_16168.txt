This term I am taking a seminar on the metaphysics of persistence, and we just spent a week on how special relativity is supposed to bear on the endurantism/perdurantism dispute. The textbook argument, which roughly follows Ted Sider's version of it, runs like this: for an enduring object to be "wholly present" at a time, there has to be some determinate fact about which of its spatially scattered parts count as existing simultaneously with one another; but the relativity of simultaneity for spacelike-separated events seems to leave no room for any such frame-independent fact, whereas the perdurantist can carve the four-dimensional "worm" up in whatever way is convenient without ever needing a privileged "now" to unite an object's parts in the first place. A couple of classmates and I got into an argument about this after class, over whether it actually sinks endurantism or is more of a "so what" than the textbook lets on, and neither of us felt our own back-and-forth did justice to the strongest version of either side.

Could you write me a dialogue between a philosopher defending endurantism and one defending perdurantism who run into each other after a colloquium talk on exactly this argument? I would like enough back-and-forth — at least two or three rounds on each side — that it does not just end after a single objection and a single reply. Please give the endurantist genuine resources to draw on rather than having them simply concede: for instance, biting the bullet by relativizing "wholly present" to a reference frame, or questioning whether endurance really requires the kind of determinate, frame-independent simultaneity among an object's parts that the argument assumes — though feel free to have them reach for a stronger move instead if you think of one. The perdurantist, in turn, should not just restate the original argument but should actually respond to whatever specific move the endurantist makes. Finally, please do not resolve the dispute for me by having either side clearly win by the end; I would rather see the strongest form of the disagreement play out and reach my own verdict.
