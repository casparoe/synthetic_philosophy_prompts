Ok so I've been stuck on this for weeks: what does it even mean to say a body part has a "function"? Everyone agrees the heart's function is to pump blood, not to make that thump-thump sound, even though it does both just as reliably. I went down a wikipedia rabbit hole and found out there's actually two rival philosophical answers to this. One (the etiological view, Wright's I think) says a trait's function is whatever effect it was selected for in the past - hearts pump blood because ancestor-hearts that pumped blood well got favored by selection, and the thumping was never what got selected for, so it doesnt count. The other one (causal role, Cummins?) drops the history angle completely - a part's function is just whatever it contributes to some capacity of whatever system you happen to be analyzing right now, so under a doctor's stethoscope-diagnosis "system," the thump is a perfectly real function too.

Can you actually compare these two properly instead of just laying them out side by side like a textbook would? Mainly want to know: is one of them basically a special/restricted case of the other, or are they honestly answering two different questions and only look like rivals because they use the same word? And which one copes better with vestigial traits (like ostrich wings) and traits that got fully repurposed over time (feathers: warmth first, flight later)? If you had to bet, which view is closer to being right overall?
