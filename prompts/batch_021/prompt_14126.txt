A friend of mine who works in machine learning keeps making an argument that nags at me, even though I can't quite locate the flaw in it. He points out that in the 1930s, several logicians, each working independently from quite different technical starting points, set out to formalize the informal idea of a "mechanical, step-by-step procedure," and their formalisms were later proven to define exactly the same class of functions. He treats that convergence as settling, for all practical purposes, that whatever the brain does is just this kind of procedure as well, so a sufficiently sophisticated program wouldn't merely imitate a mind but would have to be one in every sense that matters. This is part of why he no longer feels any qualms about how casually his team spins up and kills model instances at work. Is he entitled to lean on that convergence for this much, and does it really carry him all the way to his conclusion about minds?
