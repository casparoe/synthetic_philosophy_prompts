I am preparing a graduate seminar on naturalized ethics and would like a sanity check on a point about Quine. In "On the Nature of Moral Values," he extends the confirmation holism of "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" to the moral domain, observing that no statement, ethical or scientific, is tested against experience in isolation, but only as a member of the whole interconnected web of belief. Since Quine draws this parallel, and had, after all, already dissolved the analytic/synthetic distinction himself, does it not follow that he treats moral judgments as answerable to experience in essentially the same holistic fashion as high-level theoretical claims in physics — making him, in effect, a naturalistic moral realist rather than the skeptic about moral knowledge he is usually taken to be? I want to be sure I characterize this correctly before presenting it to students.
