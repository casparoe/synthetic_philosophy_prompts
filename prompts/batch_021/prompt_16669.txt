Ok, this is a weird combination but it's been stuck in my head so bear with me. I've been rereading Yudkowsky's old metaethics sequence (the original LW one, not any of the newer stuff) and got hung up again on "Created Already In Motion" -- the post right after "No Universally Compelling Arguments," where he argues a mind has to already be built caring about certain things, because no matter how good an argument is, it can't manufacture motivation out of nothing. Around the same time I fell down an unrelated rabbit hole into academic philosophy of action and ran into Bernard Williams' internal/external reasons paper from the late 70s, and it hit me that he's making basically the same move decades earlier in totally different vocabulary: his argument for internalism about reasons is that a "reason" only applies to an agent if there's a sound deliberative route to it from something already sitting in that agent's actual motivational set (he calls it your "subjective motivational set," S for short) -- because otherwise there'd be nothing to explain why it could ever move them to act.

Here's the bit I can't untangle though. Williams has this famous example where a guy wants a gin and tonic and believes the glass in front of him contains one, but its actually full of petrol -- Williams says the guy does NOT have a real internal reason to drink it, despite being motivated to, because "sound" deliberation isn't allowed to run on false beliefs. So even Williams' internalism isn't just "whatever you currently happen to want," it already smuggles in some correction of the agent's psychology, as long as that correction is reachable via deliberation from where they already stand. Which is what made me think of coherent extrapolated volition -- the old proposal that a friendly AI should target something like what we'd want if we were more informed, more coherent, and more the people we'd endorse being, instead of our current stated preferences. CEV obviously does way, way more correcting than "oh wait, that's petrol, not gin" -- but it's still officially trying to stay tethered to actual human volition, not importing some free-floating moral standard from nowhere.

So: on Williams' own terms, is CEV best understood as a (very ambitious) internal reasons view, or does the sheer amount of extrapolation it involves quietly turn it into the kind of external reasons view Williams thought was basically incoherent? And more generally, does "internal vs external reasons" even survive being stretched from "one guy and his drink" up to "extrapolating the volition of an entire species," or does the distinction just stop tracking anything useful at that scale? I brought this up at a meetup once and got a lot of polite nodding and zero actual answers, so I'm hoping you can do better -- I don't need a full literature review on Williams scholarship, I mainly want the actual structural parallel (or disanalogy) spelled out.
