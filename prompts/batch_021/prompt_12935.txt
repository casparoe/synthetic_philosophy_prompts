Two friends of mine got into a disagreement after one of them brought up a case she'd read about: a woman kept taking a long-term medication into the early weeks of a pregnancy, even though her doctor had told her that pausing it for three months before conceiving would eliminate a small risk of a mild but permanent condition in the child. She didn't pause it, and the child was later diagnosed with the condition. Here's roughly how the conversation went:

(1) Aisha said the mother wronged the child: she knew about the risk, knew exactly how to avoid it, and didn't bother.

(2) Ben objected that this gets the case backwards. "Avoiding" the risk would have meant conceiving three months later with a different egg and sperm — a genetically distinct child. The child who actually exists couldn't have existed at all without that exposure, so waiting wouldn't have made him better off; it would have replaced him with somebody else. Since his life is still clearly worth living, he hasn't been made worse off than the alternative in which he, specifically, exists — so there's no one here who was wronged.

(3) Aisha replied that this can't be the whole story: it seems to excuse almost any foreseeable carelessness in conception, no matter how serious, as long as the resulting child's life still clears the bar of "worth living." That's too permissive a conclusion for something the rest of us clearly want to call irresponsible.

(4) Ben conceded that some negative verdict on the mother seems right, but said the fix is to stop describing it as a wrong done to the child and call it impersonally bad instead: she brought about a world with more suffering in it than was necessary, even though no particular person is worse off as a result.

(5) Aisha said that move looks like a retreat rather than a solution — if no one was wronged, it's strange to still insist this was a wrong at all, let alone one serious enough to justify blaming the mother the way we ordinarily do when we say someone wronged their child.

Neither of them budged, and honestly I could see both sides. Could you go through this move by move and say who has the better argument at each step, rather than just declaring an overall winner? I'd also be curious whether there's some standard move in this kind of debate that they're both missing.
