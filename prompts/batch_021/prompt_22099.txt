I've been working through Gautama's Nyāya Sūtras for a reading group and got kind of stuck on the definition of perception in NS 1.1.4. Standard translations render it roughly as: perception is that cognition, produced by contact between a sense organ and its object, which is non-verbal (avyapadeśya), non-erroneous (avyabhicāri), and of the nature of determinate/certain cognition (vyavasāyātmaka).

Vātsyāyana's commentary insists 'avyapadeśya' doesn't stop you from perceiving something as a cow -- its only there to rule out cognitions that come from testimony rather than the senses. But then what work is 'vyavasāyātmaka' doing as a separate clause? Calling a cognition determinate seems to already require the sort of conceptual, nameable content (this-is-a-cow, not just an undifferentiated patch) that avyapadeśya was supposed to be excluding. Is there an actual difference in the job each qualifier does, or is this where the nirvikalpaka/savikalpaka distinction has to come in as a fix -- and if so, is that split actually in the sutra, or are commentators just patching over a tension that was already there?
