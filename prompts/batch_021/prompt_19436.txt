Fell down a rabbit hole this week: turns out Carnap wasnt just the logic-and-syntax guy -- he co-wrote the Vienna Circle's 1929 manifesto (with Neurath and Hahn), and later admitted, in his own words, that he and most of his Circle colleagues were socialists, even though he insisted on keeping his actual philosophical work strictly seperate from politics. Neurath, meanwhile, spent decades arguing with Mises and then Hayek over whether you could plan a whole economy "in kind," without market prices at all. Fast forward to now and there's a whole genre of "big data/AI finally solved Hayek's knowledge problem, so planning is back on the table" takes (see: the People's Republic of Walmart crowd pointing at Amazon's logistics as proof). Genuinely curious what you think Carnap specifically -- not Neurath -- would've made of that argument. Given his whole fact/value split, would he treat "AI can plan the economy" as a purely empirical claim he'd be happy to referee, or would he suspect a value judgment sneaking in under a lab coat?
