Write a dialogue between a halfer and a thirder about the Sleeping Beauty problem. Both characters already know the setup in full detail -- a fair coin determines whether Beauty is woken once, on Monday, or twice, on Monday and Tuesday with her memory of the first waking erased -- so neither should spend time re-explaining the experiment or rehearsing the standard indifference and betting-frequency arguments for their credence on an ordinary waking. Instead, have them argue about a narrower question: what happens to Beauty's credence in Heads once she is additionally, and truthfully, told that today is Monday? Each character should state and derive their own answer, then respond directly to whatever tension the other identifies between that answer and the credence they defended before the day was revealed, rather than simply restating their opening position.
