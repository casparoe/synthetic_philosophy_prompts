A close friend from our old LessWrong meetup is marrying a fellow alignment researcher next month, and I've been asked to give the best man speech. The two of them had their first real conversation at that meetup arguing over whether Stuart Armstrong's utility indifference trick actually solves the shutdown problem or just relocates it into whatever subagents a sufficiently capable system would be motivated to build; neither has ever conceded the point. About half the guests are meetup regulars who'll know exactly what I mean by 'the off-switch'; the rest are relatives who've never heard the word 'corrigibility' and shouldn't have to. I want five minutes built around that old argument as a running metaphor for the marriage. It can't collapse into a joke about one of them 'controlling' the other, though -- that's precisely the misreading of corrigibility that would make the alignment crowd wince, since the whole concept is about staying correctable without being manipulated or dominated. Please write the speech.
