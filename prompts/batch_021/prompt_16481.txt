ok so this happened to a friend of mine and i cant stop turning it over in my head. she does analytics for a sales team, and her company rolled out a new training program for reps. company-wide, the reps who went through training closed a noticeably higher fraction of their deals than the reps who skipped it -- looked like a clear win. but then someone split the same numbers by deal size (small deals vs enterprise deals), and within each bucket, trained reps actually closed a slightly lower fraction than untrained reps did. turns out the training also taught people to blow through small, quick deals way more efficiently, so trained reps started chasing a lot more small deals as a share of their pipeline, while untrained reps mostly kept doing what they'd always done, mainly big accounts. her boss's take is that the split-out numbers are the "real" ones, since they're not muddied by deal type, and that by that measure the training barely helped and maybe even hurt a little. something about that conclusion bugs both of us but neither of us can say exactly why. feels like the kind of thing scott alexander has definately written a post about at some point, but i can't dig it up. is her boss right that you should trust the split-out numbers over the company-wide one here, or is there a problem with that move in this particular case?
