So this has been rattling around in my head since a totally routine physical a few weeks ago. I'm 29, run three times a week, feel completely fine, and my blood pressure came back 132/84. The nurse practitioner flagged it as "Stage 1 hypertension" right on the after-visit summary, handed me a pamphlet about sodium, and mentioned I might need medication eventually if it doesnt come down. Out of curiosity I looked into it that night and found out that the exact same reading would've been unremarkable "prehypertension" — not an actual diagnosis, just a watch-it category — under the guidelines that were standard before 2017. Nothing about my arteries changed in the meantime; a committee just moved the line on where "normal" stops. To make it weirder, apparently not every major medical body even signed onto the new cutoff — some kept recommending doctors hold off treating until much higher numbers, especially for older patients.

My roommate, who's in his second year of med school and treats every conversation like an oral exam, says none of this matters: these categories get set using long-term risk data, so if the data shows this range predicts real strokes and heart attacks down the road, I actually do have the condition now, the same way I "have" a fever at 100.4°F whether or not anyone's around with a thermometer — redrawing the number doesn't make the underlying risk less real. I said that feels like a dodge, because a fever is either present or it isn't, while risk is a smooth continuum with no natural place to cut it into "healthy" and "diseased" — someone still has to choose the cutoff, and a different panel, arguing in equally good faith, could've chosen a different one. So "disease," at least here, looks less like something doctors discover in my arteries and more like a decision about how much risk we're willing to tolerate, wearing a lab coat. He thinks I'm overthinking a blood pressure cuff. I don't know this literature at all and can't tell whose framing is actually right, or if we're both missing something bigger. Is my new "diagnosis" a plain fact about my body, a value judgment dressed up as one, or somehow both at once — and if it's both, what does that actually mean for whether I should believe it?
