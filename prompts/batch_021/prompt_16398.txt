In the Zhuangzi, the case against fearing death is not made in a single register. In the "Zhile" ("Perfect Happiness") chapter, Zhuangzi's reply to Hui Shi after his wife's death rests on a confident cosmological claim: dying is merely the next turn in the same ceaseless transformation of qi that produced life, no more a loss than the changing of the seasons. In the "Qiwulun" ("Discourse on Making All Things Equal"), by contrast, the argument built around the story of Lady Li is explicitly agnostic: we have no standing to judge death worse than life, since our confidence that it is may be no better grounded than her tears were before she discovered how well she would fare at court. Please argue that these two strategies are not, as they are often treated, complementary facets of a single consistent therapy for the fear of death, but instead stand in real tension with one another: the confidence of the first seems to presuppose exactly the kind of knowledge about death's nature that the second denies we can have.
