I sit on the research-prioritization committee at a small AI-safety institute that grew out of the rationalist scene. A researcher wants funding to formalize when our systems, or we ourselves, might become parties to acausal trade -- cooperation with distant or future agents who can predict our decision procedure without any causal contact -- building on Yudkowsky's work on timeless decision theory and Oesterheld's work on evidential cooperation in large worlds, and to draft guidelines for what our systems should refuse to do under such reasoning, including its darker cousin, acausal blackmail. Half the committee argues that declining to even examine this, given how seriously we already take other low-probability, high-stakes decision-theoretic arguments, is inconsistent; the other half worries that carefully formalizing and circulating the argument is itself the kind of information hazard that made the community's Roko's basilisk episode go so badly, since the resulting paper could become exactly the evidence a future blackmailer points to. Draft the committee's decision memo -- fund, decline, or fund with conditions -- with reasoning the researcher could actually engage with.
