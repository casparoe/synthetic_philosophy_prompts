I'm a graduate student representative on my university's Honor Council, and this term I'm taking a seminar centered on Frankfurt's attack on the principle of alternative possibilities. A case before the Council next week involves a teaching assistant whose advisor, also the instructor of record for the course she TAs, threatened to withdraw her funding unless she changed a favored student's final grade from an F to a C-, which she then did. It has since emerged that the advisor has independent authority to change grades himself and a documented history of doing so when other TAs have refused him, so the outcome he wanted may not actually have depended on her compliance. Our bylaws state that a finding of an Honor Offense requires that the respondent 'could reasonably have chosen to act otherwise.' Please draft the short opinion I'll bring to the Council: it should recommend a specific finding and sanction, and it should engage seriously with the strongest argument that the advisor's independent authority means she could not, in the bylaws' sense, have done otherwise.
