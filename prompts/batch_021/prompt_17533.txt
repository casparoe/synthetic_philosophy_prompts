For thirty-five years I worked as a biochemist, and I have spent much of my retirement finally reading the philosophy I never had time for during my career. A small puzzle has been occupying me lately, and I have started drafting an essay around it. I would value your comments before I take the draft any further.

In the lab, I trusted colleagues' testimony as a matter of course: if a co-author whose work I trusted told me a compound's melting point came out at 158°C, I simply believed her, and no one would have said the resulting belief was any less genuine knowledge than if I had measured it myself. Yet when I told a friend recently that a well-regarded critic finds a certain Rothko painting profoundly moving, and that I therefore believed this of the painting too, she said that was a strange way to arrive at such a belief — stranger, she thought, than taking a colleague's word for a melting point — even though she granted that the critic was perfectly sincere and knew the painter's work intimately.

My tentative diagnosis, which I would like pressure-tested, is that an aesthetic verdict is partly constituted by one's own response to the object, so that believing a painting is moving purely on someone else's authority is a bit like believing you find a joke funny because a trustworthy friend assures you that you do: the belief and the state it is supposed to report have come apart. But I am bothered by a complication. Some scientific judgments — reading an ambiguous medical scan, say, or recognizing by ear that an engine is misfiring — also seem to require the trained observer's own encounter with the thing, and we do not usually conclude that expert testimony about scans or engines is therefore epistemically second-rate. So perhaps the asymmetry I started with is not quite the one I think it is.

I would be grateful for your thoughts on whether this diagnosis holds up, whether the scan-and-engine complication is as damaging to it as it looks to me, whether there is existing philosophical work on precisely this question that I ought to be reading, and, more generally, whether this looks like a promising thread to pull on or a dead end.
