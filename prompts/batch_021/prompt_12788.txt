A friend of mine, who does formal verification for a living, and I spent most of Tuesday night arguing about the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents; neither of us budged an inch. His position was that the obstacle is permanent, not merely inconvenient: Löb's theorem guarantees that a formal system can prove the statement "if I can prove P, then P is true" only for those P it can already prove outright, so any self-modifying agent hoping to certify, once and for all, that each successor it builds can be trusted to reason correctly — this is essentially what MIRI researchers mean when they talk about wanting an agent to "tile" — is chasing a guarantee that is structurally unavailable to it, however carefully the system is engineered. I pushed back with the usual counter, that probabilistic reflection and logical-induction-style approaches at least let an agent extend a reasonable, if not ironclad, form of trust to its successors; he replied that this just quietly changes the question, trading a hard guarantee for a soft one, rather than actually closing the gap between provability and truth that Löb's theorem exposes. We got sidetracked for a while arguing over whether Gödel's second incompleteness theorem is basically just Löb's theorem applied to the case where P is an outright falsehood, or whether that's too quick a way of putting it, and by midnight we had resolved precisely nothing.

Since neither of us is going to concede in conversation, I would like to send him a joke about the Löbian obstacle as a kind of peace offering. I don't want a generic joke about self-referential AI with the word "Löb" bolted onto it; I want the punchline to actually depend on the mechanics of the theorem — on the gap between a system proving that its own proofs are reliable and the system actually being reliable, or on the way a naive policy of "trust whatever my future self proves" collapses the moment you try to formalize it. If someone who has never heard of Löb's theorem hears the joke, they shouldn't really get it; if someone who has, they should wince a little before they laugh. Could you write me one?
