My grandma died on Monday and I'm giving the eulogy at the funeral this weekend. She'd been a pretty hardcore panpsychist for like thirty years -- not the crystals-and-vibes kind, the Galen Strawson/Philip Goff "experience is a fundamental feature of matter, all the way down to electrons" kind, and she'd get genuinely annoyed when people conflated the two. A few weeks before she died she made me promise, twice, that nobody would use her death as an excuse for a "she's merging back into the universal consciousness now" line as comfort. Her reasoning, as best I can reconstruct it (she wasn't always this articulate near the end), was that the combination problem cuts both ways: if nobody has a real account of how a bunch of micro-experiences combine into one unified subject like her in the first place, that's not a license to get poetic about what happens when that combination comes apart either -- we just don't know, and reaching for comfort there is exactly the sloppy thinking she spent her life annoyed by. So no dust-to-dust-but-make-it-panpsychist. Some of her old reading group will absolutely clock it if I get this wrong, but most of the room is aunts and cousins who've never heard the word "qualia" in their life. Can you write me a eulogy that's warm, actually works for that room, is true to what she really believed rather than the comforting misreading of it, and doesn't turn into a philosophy lecture?
