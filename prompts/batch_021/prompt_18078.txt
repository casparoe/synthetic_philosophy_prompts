Over the last year I've been doing a lot better with my anxiety, and I can't quite figure out why, which has turned into a whole arguement with my roommate. I've been in weekly therapy this whole time, and about six months ago I also started an SSRI on my psychiatrist's suggestion. Both seem to have helped, but when I try to pin down what's actually responsible for the improvement I get two pretty different stories depending on who I ask. My therapist talks about it in terms of me learning to catch certain thought patterns as they happen and talk myself down before they spiral - like I've genuinely gotten better at reasoning my way out of catastrophizing, and that reasoning is what's driving the change. My roommate, who's doing a neuroscience PhD (and I think this is basically what my psychiatrist believes too, she's just more polite about it), says the real story is that the SSRI changed my serotonin levels and rewired some circuits, full stop, and that whatever "insight" I feel like I'm having in therapy is just a side effect riding along on top of that, not something that's actually doing anything on its own. Her question to me was: if you could in principle explain the whole change just by pointing to what's happening in my neurons and the drug, what's actually left over for my thoughts and reasoning to be doing?

We argued about this for close to an hour at dinner last week and got nowhere, and it's been bugging me since. Saying "both matter" feels like a cop-out, since it dodges the actual question of whether my reasoning is actually doing anything or if it's just along for the ride while the chemistry does the heavy lifting. Is there an actual way to think clearly about who's got the better of this, or is that a false choice to begin with? I'd like to understand this better than we managed to over dinner.
