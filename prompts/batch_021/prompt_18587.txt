Ok this has been bugging me for a while. Yudkowsky's whole 'concepts are clusters in thingspace, not defined by necessary and sufficient conditions' thing treats arguing over exactly where to draw a category boundary (is Pluto a planet, is a tomato a vegetable) as a confused fight over definitions rather than a real disagreement, since the word was never meant to carve reality at a sharp joint to begin with. But the actual philosophical literature on the sorites paradox -- epistemicism, supervaluationism, all that -- takes the existence (or non-existence) of a sharp cutoff dead seriously, as the very thing that needs explaining. Are these actually rival answers to the same puzzle, or is the thingspace picture just quietly changing the subject on us? And if 'dissolving' the question this way is supposed to be better than just answering it, does it actually make the logical problem go away (the inductive premise, higher order vagueness, all that) or does it just relocate the vagueness into 'how similar is similar enough' without admitting thats what its doing?
