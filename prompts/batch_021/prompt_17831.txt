I've been following the discussion of evidential cooperation in large worlds (ECL) on Less Wrong and the Effective Altruism Forum: the proposal that, in a sufficiently large or infinite universe, we should weight the values of agents we can never causally reach, because our decision-making is correlated with theirs, and acting cooperatively is thereby good evidence that they will act cooperatively toward us. What is supposed to make this more than a rehash of Hofstadter-style superrationality is that it secures cooperation between agents with genuinely different terminal values, not merely near-copies of one another. Please argue that this generalization fails: that whatever grounds the required correlation in decision procedure will, in any realistic population of agents, also correlate their values, so ECL collapses into either cooperation among near-copies (unsurprising) or an unmotivated assumption that decision procedure and values vary independently of one another.
