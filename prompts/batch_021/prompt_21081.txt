I've been messing around with a little side project — a tool that splits shared expenses or chores between roommates based on how much each person actually cares about the different outcomes, instead of just doing a boring 50/50 — and that sent me down a rabbit hole into bargaining theory. Everyone talks about the Nash bargaining solution (the "maximize the product of the gains over the disagreement point" one), but then I found out theres this other one, the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution, that basically got invented because Kalai and Smorodinsky didn't like Nash's independence of irrelevant alternatives axiom and swapped in some kind of monotonicity condition instead, and apparently the two solutions don't always agree on what the "fair" split even is. A friend of mine who did game theory in grad school tried explaining over coffee why this actually matters, but it didn't fully click for me.

I get the axioms fine on a technical level, but I don't have any real gut feeling for when the choice between them matters in practice. Could you make up a concrete two-person bargaining scenario — any domain, splitting rent, dividing up a startup, whatever's easiest to make vivid — where the Nash solution and the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution clearly recommend different splits, and where, if you lined the two splits up side by side, most people's gut sense of "fair" would pretty obviously land on one over the other? I want to actually see what intuition is supposedly baked into monotonicity vs IIA instead of just staring at the two definitions, since that's the part that isn't clicking yet.
