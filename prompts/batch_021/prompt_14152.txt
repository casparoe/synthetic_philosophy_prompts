I teach a graduate seminar that's half formal epistemology, half AI-alignment adjacent, and this week we finally got to Löb's theorem and the "Löbian obstacle" to self-trust: the reason an agent can't just run on the policy "I'll do X whenever I can prove I'll do X," since if it could prove that schema in general it would thereby be able to prove X outright, wich for arbitrary X is obviously bad news. I put the whole derivation on the board, provability predicate, diagonal lemma and all, and by the end half the room had the look of people who'd just been personally attacked by a modal operator.
