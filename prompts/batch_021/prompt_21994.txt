              L            C            R
      T    (3, 2)       (0, 2)       (0, 0)
      M    (1, 0)       (1, 5)      (10, -5)
      B    (0, 2)       (3, 2)       (0, 0)

Perform iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies. At each round, a strategy may be eliminated if it is strictly dominated by another strategy available to that player, where "another strategy" includes mixed strategies (probability distributions over that player's own remaining pure strategies), not just pure ones. Show your work at each round and state the fully reduced game you end up with.

And here's the student's submitted answer, copied exactly:

"Round 1: Compare Player 2's columns first. For every row, L gives Player 2 a strictly higher payoff than R does (2>0 for T, 0>-5 for M, 2>0 for B), so R is strictly dominated by L and can be eliminated. Column C is not dominated — at row M it gives Player 2 a payoff of 5, which beats both L (0) and R (-5) there — so C survives into the next round.

Round 2: Now compare Player 1's rows using only the surviving columns L and C. T gives (3,0), M gives (1,1), and B gives (0,3). T does not dominate M, since T beats M at L (3>1) but loses to M at C (0<1). B does not dominate M either, since B loses to M at L (0<1) even though it beats M at C (3>1). So neither T nor B individually dominates M — and since we're eliminating strategies rather than solving for an equilibrium, only pure-strategy comparisons are relevant here; mixed strategies come in later, when you're looking for a mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium of the reduced game. So M is not dominated and survives.

No further eliminations are available: T and B each do better than the other in one of the two remaining columns, and L and C give Player 2 identical payoffs (2,2) against both T and B. So the process terminates.

Final reduced game: Player 1 chooses from {T, M, B}, Player 2 chooses from {L, C}."
