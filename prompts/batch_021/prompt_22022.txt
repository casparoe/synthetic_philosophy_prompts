So I've been chewing on something from a decision theory seminar and can't shake the feeling that two canonical results just flatly contradict each other. Good's theorem says a Bayesian expected-utility maximizer should always (weakly) want free information before acting, since worst case you just ignore it — so the value of information can never be negative. But then theres the Hirshleifer effect, where publicly revealing information ahead of time can wreck everyone's ability to write insurance/risk-sharing contracts, since the whole point of those contracts depended on nobody knowing the outcome yet — so releasing the info early leaves literally every party worse off in expectation. Isn't that a straight up contradiction, info can't hurt you except when it makes everyone strictly worse off? Someone in my cohort said "they're just talking about different things" but couldn't actually explain what that meant, and its been bugging me since. What's the real resolution here?
