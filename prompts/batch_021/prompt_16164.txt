As a science-and-ideas journalist working on a piece about the history of animal-sentience debates, I keep encountering the claim that Descartes regarded animals as unfeeling automata, incapable of any real pain or pleasure. My own reading of his correspondence leaves me suspecting that this fully "no feeling whatsoever" doctrine is more properly the contribution of his later follower Nicolas Malebranche than of Descartes himself. Could you compare exactly what each philosopher denies animals, thought and language on the one hand versus sensation itself on the other, and explain why Malebranche's occasionalism, unlike Descartes' own (famously awkward) mind-body interactionism, commits him to the harder conclusion that animal suffering is illusory, even though neither grants animals a rational soul? Please flag any point at which Descartes' own texts are genuinely ambiguous or contested among scholars, rather than presenting a tidier picture than the sources actually support.
