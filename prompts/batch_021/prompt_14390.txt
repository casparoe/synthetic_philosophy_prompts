I'm putting together a lecture on Kripke's reading of Wittgenstein for my graduate seminar in philosophy of language, and a conversation with a colleague in cognitive science has left me wanting a sanity check before I commit to how I present the material. My understanding of the rule-following paradox is this: any finite stretch of my past use of "+" — say, additions confined to numbers smaller than 57 — is equally compatible with my having meant the plus function and with my having meant some deviant "quus" function, one that agrees with plus on every case I've actually computed but diverges on a novel case such as 68+57. My colleague's reaction was that this is, at bottom, an evidential problem: at any given moment, we simply don't have enough behavioral data to know which function a person means. But since dispositions to respond are presumably realized in the brain, it seems to follow that sufficiently advanced neuroscience should in principle be able to settle the matter directly: scan the relevant neural mechanisms, characterize their complete dispositional profile, and read off whether they compute plus or quus for an input like 68+57. On this way of looking at things, the rule-following paradox turns out to be a merely practical limitation on our current evidence, one that would dissolve as our access to the brain's dispositional facts improved, rather than a genuine, standing metaphysical puzzle.

I would like to use this as a teaching example of how apparent philosophical puzzles can sometimes rest on empirical bottlenecks rather than anything deeper, but I want to make sure I'm not building a lecture around a mistake. Could you help me pressure-test the idea? In particular, I'd be grateful if you could address whether a complete, idealized specification of an agent's dispositions — the kind neuroscience might eventually deliver — would really settle which rule the agent is following, and whether Kripke's own distinction between a "straight" and a "sceptical" solution suggests he would have anticipated and rejected this sort of response in advance.
