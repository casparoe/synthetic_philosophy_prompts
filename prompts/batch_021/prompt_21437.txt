Epistemicism about vagueness holds that predicates like "heap" or "tall" have a perfectly precise, if unknowable, extension, so that some exact number of grains marks the transition from non-heap to heap. Supervaluationism is usually presented as the more modest alternative, since it lets borderline sentences be neither true nor false instead of positing a hidden fact about exactly where the predicate's application ends. Given that supervaluationism thereby dispenses with epistemicism's most implausible commitment, shouldn't it simply be preferred on grounds of ontological economy?
