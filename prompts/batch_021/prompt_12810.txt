my cousin and her husband started IVF a few months back and are doing genetic testing on the embryos because Huntington's (HD) runs in his family — his dad has it, so each of his kids had a 50/50 shot of carrying the gene. they're only going to transfer embryos that test negative. i never thought about any of this stuff before, but now i can't stop reading about the ethics of it.

at her baby shower last weekend, two people who are NOT my cousin got into an actual argument about it and its been stuck in my head since. one is my uncle's partner Dana, who's about to finish a philosophy PhD, the other is Marcus, an old family friend whose brother has HD. roughly how it went:

Dana said the embryo that doesn't get chosen isn't wronged by that, because it's a classic "nonidentity problem" case: if they'd implanted that embryo instead, a totally different kid would exist than the one who's actually going to be born, so there's no single person who ends up worse off. the HD embryo's only alternative to nonexistence was never becoming a person at all, not becoming a person with a better life.

Marcus came back with two things. first, that this can't be the whole story or you could screen for literally any trait — eye color, whatever — and it'd always come out morally fine by the same logic, since its always "a different individual" who doesn't get picked. second, that the textbook version of this problem is about a kid who doesn't exist yet at all when the choice gets made, whereas here both embryos are already sitting in the dish at the same time, so maybe the whole framework doesn't even transfer cleanly — picking between two things that already exist might just be a different kind of choice than deciding whether to conceive later.

Dana said being a "victim" requires being a subject who could've had a better life than the one you actually got, and an embryo that's never implanted never becomes that kind of subject, so the fact that its physically sitting in a dish right now doesn't change the logical structure — it's not a person either way. and on the eye-color thing, she said proving there's no victim doesn't mean every selection is equally fine; selecting for eye color and selecting against HD could still come apart for other reasons, it just means "somebody got wronged" isn't going to be that reason.

Marcus said sure, but that's exactly his point — nobody at that party was claiming there's a specific victim, they were saying the practice is troubling for other reasons, and he brought up that Dana's own favorite philosopher, Parfit, thought you needed a whole extra principle to explain why bringing about more suffering is worse even in cases where nonidentity means nobody's worse off. so if Dana wants credit for the framework, she doesn't get to also act like "no victim" settled anything, since that was never actually the part in dispute.

nobody backed down and my cousin just stood there holding a diaper cake looking like she regretted bringing it up. can you go through their exchange move by move and say who actually has the better of each point? and if there's some real question underneath all this that you think they're both talking past, i'd like to know what that is too.
