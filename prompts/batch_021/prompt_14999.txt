been down a bit of a youtube rabbit hole on epistemology and i think i finally worked out why plato's old road-to-larissa puzzle isn't really a live puzzle for modern philosphy anymore. his question was why knowing the way to larissa is better than just having a correct opinion about it, since both get the traveler there just fine either way. but that's basically the same puzzle as the gettier problem, right -- once you patch up 'justified true belief' so accidental correctness (like glancing at a stopped clock that happens to show the right time) doesnt count as knowing anymore, that patched up definition already explains why knowledge beats mere true belief, since it just filters the lucky guesses out. so why do epistemologists still write whole papers on 'the value problem of knowledge' like its some open question?
