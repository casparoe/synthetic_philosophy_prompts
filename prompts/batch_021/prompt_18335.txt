Random one for you. I spent forty years as a chemist before retiring, and I've picked up philosophy as a hobby since then, which is probably why this is bugging me more than it should. Our Thursday bridge club has an automatic dealing machine, so the odds on any given hand are about as close to a genuinely known, fixed probability as you'll find outside a lab. Lately one of the guys, Walt, keeps calling his hand before its dealt ('feels like a big one coming'), and over maybe fifteen sessions now he's been eerily right, way more often than chance should allow. A retired statistics professor in our group insists this is pure noise and that we should keep betting purely off the known odds, end of story - the machine's specs don't care about anybody's hunches. I'm not so sure though; Walt being right that often feels like real information sitting right there in front of us, not nothing. Am I being dumb to let that shift my bets at all, or is she right to just ignore it?
