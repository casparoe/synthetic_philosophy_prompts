I'm a junior on my school's Science Bowl team, and something that happened at practice a couple of weeks ago has been bugging me ever since. To drill facts before tournaments, our coach reads us long lists of true-or-false statements pulled from the study packet, and normally whoever is serving as team captain that day calls out "true" or "false" almost instantly, with the rest of us just nodding along unless we're pretty sure she's wrong. It's worked fine for two seasons, so nobody thought much of it.

For one practice set, though, our coach had all four of us write "T" or "F" on our own personal whiteboards at the same time, before anyone said anything out loud, and then had us reveal together and go with whatever three or more of us agreed on, only talking it out on the rare two-two split. When we compared scores afterward on sets of matched difficulty, the simultaneous-whiteboard round clearly beat our usual captain-calls-it-first round, and on several statements where the old method's answer had been wrong, it turned out that at least three of us had privately written down the correct answer. This wasn't just our captain having an off day, either, since the sets were randomized from the same master packet and she didn't do noticeably worse on the questions she called individually elsewhere in practice.

Our coach thinks it's probably a small-sample fluke, and honestly it might be, but I keep feeling like there's a real, general reason lurking here: some fact about how a group should share individual opinions before combining them into one answer, that would apply just as much to a class vote or a panel of judges scoring a competition as it does to us. I can't quite articulate what that reason is, though, and I don't know how confident I should be that it's real rather than coincidence. Is my coach right to write this off as noise, or is there something real going on that we should actually plan around before regionals in a few weeks?
