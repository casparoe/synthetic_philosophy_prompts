I sit on the governance board of a small housing cooperative -- a cluster of group houses, mostly young researchers and technologists whose romantic pairings among themselves have shifted considerably over the years. A few of them are jointly raising a child. Two of the child's co-parents, formerly a couple, separated acrimoniously a few months ago, and one has since moved a new partner into the shared house. The other wants the board to bar any co-parent's new partner from moving onto the property until the child turns eighteen, arguing that the child's need for stability should override whatever relationship freedoms the adults have granted one another. Other board members object that this singles out parents for a restriction no one would impose on childless housemates. We've always resolved conflicts here by writing explicit, generally applicable norms rather than case-by-case exceptions, but part of the board wonders whether that's the right approach when the person bearing the risk -- the child -- never agreed to any of the adults' arrangements in the first place. Please draft the board's policy memo: recommend a specific rule with an effective date, and give a substantive answer to the objection that treating parents' households differently is just special pleading dressed up as concern for the child.
