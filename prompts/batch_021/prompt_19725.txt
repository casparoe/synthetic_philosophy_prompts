and here's their answer:

"(a) On the classic JTB view, S knows p iff p is true, S believes p, and S is justified in believing p. In Gettier's case, Smith has loads of evidence that Jones owns a Ford (Jones has driven one for years, has just offered him a lift in it, etc), so Smith is justified in believing 'Jones owns a Ford.' From this he deduces 'Jones owns a Ford or Brown is in Barcelona,' picking Barcelona basically at random. It turns out Jones doesn't actually own a Ford anymore, but by pure coincidence Brown really is in Barcelona. So Smith's belief in the disjunction is true and justified, but nobody wants to say he knows it, since it's true for a reason totally disconnected from why he believed it. So JTB isn't sufficient for knowledge.

(b) The false lemma here is 'Jones owns a Ford,' since that's the false premise the whole inference goes through. So if a belief can't count as knowledge when it's inferred from something false, that rules this case out, which is the result we want. And this generalises nicely: take Gettier's other case, the fake barn county, where a guy is driving through a county full of barn facades and happens to be looking right at the one real barn in the county. His belief 'that's a barn' isn't inferred from anything false, so the no-false-lemmas rule correctly tells us that he does know it's a barn. So the fix handles both of Gettier's cases and basically solves the problem."
