I'm a fifth-year PhD student in economics, and with job-market season approaching again, a disagreement that started half-jokingly at our department's placement workshop has turned into a genuine point of contention between two close friends in my cohort. Every autumn our placement office circulates a statistic along the lines of "about 12% of graduates from programs outside the top thirty place into tenure-track research positions," and the three of us cannot agree on what, if anything, that number tells any one of us about our own prospects. One friend argues that the figure is nearly useless for an individual, since it averages over people with very different advisors, subfields, publication records, and letters; the only intellectually honest move, she thinks, is to condition on a narrower comparison group instead — say, students of our own advisor working in applied micro who already have a paper under review at a good journal. When I pointed out that this kind of narrowing never obviously stops — you can keep adding qualifiers like specific coauthors, conference presentations, undergraduate pedigree, even the exact year one goes on the market — until the "comparison group" just is you, a population of exactly one, our other friend took this as proof that we should give up on narrowing altogether and simply anchor on the broadest well-measured number, treating all "but my case is different" reasoning as flattering self-deception.

Neither position fully satisfies me, though I have trouble articulating why. The first view seems to imply that almost no published statistic is ever really about any particular person, since one can always find some further distinguishing detail; the second view seems to throw away information that is obviously relevant to my actual odds, since advisor and subfield surely matter to placement outcomes. Is there a principled way of deciding how narrow or broad a comparison group should be when estimating the odds facing one specific individual, or is this something statistics is simply unequipped to settle? And if there is genuinely no fact of the matter about which comparison group is the "right" one, what should that do to my confidence in any particular number I attach to my own chances next year?
