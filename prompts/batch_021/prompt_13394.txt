For almost ten years I have kept a private notebook that no one else has ever read. Whenever I am deep in a scene and feel a very particular, hard-to-describe inward sensation — a kind of quiet click that tells me the scene has finally found its true shape — I mark the page with a small invented symbol, always the same one, though I have never translated what it names into words, even to myself. I simply trust that I will recognize the feeling when it returns, and use the symbol to note it down. Over the years this private ledger of instinct has become one of the chief ways I judge my own progress as a writer, and I had always assumed that whatever else was uncertain about the habit, I at least knew, better than anyone else could, what I meant by my own mark.

I described the whole habit recently to an old university friend who now teaches philosophy, half joking that I had invented my own secret code. She was less charmed by it than I had hoped. Her worry was that I have no way of checking whether today's "click" is really the same feeling I marked down last year, since the only thing I can compare my present impression to is another impression of my own; and comparing one of my own impressions to another, she pointed out, doesn't establish that either one is accurate — it is no better than reassuring yourself that you remember something correctly by simply picturing it again. Once she had said this, I went back through several old notebooks and found the symbol sitting beside passages that, on reflection, do not seem to belong together at all, which has left the whole habit feeling far less solid than it used to. I would genuinely like to know whether she has identified a real problem with any purely private way of tracking an inner state like mine, or whether there is a good reply on my behalf that would let me trust my own notebook again. I ask partly for my own peace of mind, and partly because I am tempted to hand a milder version of the same doubt to a character in the novel I am currently drafting, and would like to understand how deep the problem really goes before deciding how she comes out on the other side of it.
