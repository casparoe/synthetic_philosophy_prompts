I write about science and ideas for a living, and lately I've been collecting real examples of how institutions reason through ugly trade-offs. The best one yet came out of a school board budget meeting I sat in on a few weeks back, and it's been stuck in my head since. The district had to choose between two cuts: (a) shave 5 minutes off recess for every kid in the district, all 3,200 of them, every school day for the rest of the year, or (b) cut the two aides who give one-on-one support to two severely autistic kids so they can stay in mainstream classrooms -- their parents say losing that support would set the kids back badly and could get them moved to a different school entirely. The board picked option (b), and one member defended it by literally doing the math out loud: 3,200 kids times 5 minutes times 175 days is a way bigger number than whatever happens to two kids, so cutting recess is actually the "bigger harm." That logic rubbed me the wrong way but I couldnt put my finger on why. Did the board actually reason well here, or is something wrong with adding up harms like that?
