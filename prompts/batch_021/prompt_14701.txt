I've been reading Overcoming Bias and LessWrong since back when Overcoming Bias was still the joint Hanson–Yudkowsky blog, and the centipede game is one of those examples that keeps resurfacing whenever a thread turns to the gap between formal rationality and actual human behavior. The setup, for reference: two players alternate turns in a game built around a single growing pot, where at each turn the player to move can either take a slightly larger share of the current pot and end the game immediately, or pass and let the pot grow further for the other player to face the same choice, up to some fixed, commonly known, finite number of rounds. Standard backward-induction reasoning starts at the final node, where taking strictly dominates passing, and unravels that conclusion back through every earlier node, yielding the well-known result that both players should defect on the very first move, even though many rounds of mutual passing would have left both of them substantially better off. I'm aware that the experimental literature, going back to the McKelvey and Palfrey studies, consistently finds that actual subjects pass for several rounds on average before anyone defects.

I've been drafting a post that uses this as my central illustration of a case where game theory has rigorously settled exactly what a rational agent is required to do, so that the empirical failure to defect immediately is simply a bias to be explained away (fairness norms, altruism, bounded lookahead, and so on) rather than evidence of any genuine remaining difficulty in the theory itself. A friend from my local meetup objected that this framing glosses over something important but couldn't quite put his finger on what it was. Before I commit to that framing in the post: is it accurate to treat the backward-induction result as an uncontroversial normative benchmark that human cooperation simply fails to meet, or is there a more serious game-theoretic reason to doubt that backward induction actually settles what a rational player should do in this game?
