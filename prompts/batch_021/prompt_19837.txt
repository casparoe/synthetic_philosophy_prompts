So two people in my rationalist-adjacent friend group (we all met through ACX meetups years back) have had this running argument for like a year about whether our mutual friend Dave's startup is still gonna exist in 2027 -- Mara's consistently said something like 20%, Theo's consistently said something like 80%, and neither has moved an inch no matter how many times it comes up. A couple weeks ago, at the pub after our meetup, it stopped being about Dave entirely and turned into a fight about Aumann's agreement theorem instead, which I honestly found way more interesting then the original argument. Roughly how it went, in order:

1. Theo: "We've each said our actual number to the other like a dozen times now and neither of us has budged, and it's not like we started from wildly different priors, we're both just normal reasonable people. So by Aumann's theorem this basically already is common knowledge, and if we still disagree, one of us is either lying about their real number or being irrational."

2. Mara: "Saying a number out loud a dozen times isn't 'common knowledge' in the sense Aumann means -- that needs each of us to know the other's exact number, know that they know we know it, and so on forever. Also, I worked there for a year, I have a bunch of private information you just don't have, and that's a big chunk of why my number's lower."

3. Theo: "That doesn't actually get you out of it though. The whole point of the theorem is that I don't need to see your evidence, just knowing your posterior should already move mine, assuming we share a prior. So by your own logic I should just adopt your 20%, not throw out the theorem."

4. Mara: "Except we don't share a prior, that's the part you keep skipping past. My number was already going to be lower than yours before I ever worked there -- I've just never trusted founder promises as much as you do. Aumann's theorem doesn't say anything about two people who started from genuinely different places."

5. Theo: "A 'genuinely different starting point' that never once moves no matter what gets said is a little convenient though. Isn't that basically the thing Robin Hanson's always going on about -- that when two people who each think the other is basically rational keep disagreeing anyway, it usually means at least one of them isn't being fully honest about updating?"

Nobody at the table could settle it, obviously. Can you go through this move by move and say who's got the better of it at each step (not just who wins overall), and point out anything about how Aumann's theorem actually works that you think both of them are getting wrong or skating past?
