I'm a philosophy PhD student giving the toast at the wedding of two close friends from my cohort, both of whom wrote animal-ethics dissertations and ended up in opposite camps. Priya is a Singer-style utilitarian: she accepts the replaceability argument — that painlessly killing an animal and replacing it with an equally well-off one isn't wrong in itself — and has a side project arguing that we may eventually be obligated to intervene against wild-animal predation. Ben works in the Regan/Korsgaard tradition, where any subject-of-a-life is an end in itself that cannot be traded off against aggregate welfare; for him, replaceability is a non-starter and the predation-intervention idea looks like plain moral hubris. What makes their situation interesting for a toast is that they agree completely on rejecting speciesism and disagree about nearly everything built on top of that shared premise. Roughly half the guests are philosophers who will catch every reference; the rest are family who think of the couple simply as "the vegan ones" and have no idea there's a live dispute underneath it. Could you write me a four-to-five-minute toast that uses their disagreement as its spine, represents both positions as ones a serious person could hold, includes one moment that actually lands as a joke for the people who know the literature without losing the family, and never tips over into declaring a winner?
