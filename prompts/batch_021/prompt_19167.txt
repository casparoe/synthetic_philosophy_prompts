I'm a novelist finishing a book about a family that spends three generations restoring the same wooden sailboat, plank by plank, until nothing of the original timber survives; I also host a small podcast where I interview specialists whose work touches on the questions my fiction keeps raising. Next week's guest defends van Inwagen's organicist answer to the special composition question -- the view that some things compose a further thing only when they are caught up in a life, so that strictly speaking there is no boat, no hull, no plank, only particles arranged boat-wise, hull-wise, plank-wise. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the position: I want at least one question on the argument from vagueness against any restricted answer to the composition question and how accepting indeterminate composition is supposed to avoid collapsing into vague existence, one on what her view implies happens, metaphysically, at the instant an organism dies, and one on whether the "arranged X-wise" paraphrase of ordinary object-talk quietly reintroduces the very objects it was meant to do without -- for each, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give. Close with a question about what she thinks would actually count as evidence against organicism, rather than just another case to be paraphrased away.
