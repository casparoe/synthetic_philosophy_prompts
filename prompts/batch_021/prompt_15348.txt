So for AP Euro we had to read excerpts of Rousseau's Lettre à d'Alembert sur les spectacles (the pamphlet where he basically goes off on d'Alembert for suggesting in his Encyclopédie article that Geneva should get a theatre), and I actually found it way more interesting than I expected going in. Like his whole argument that actors are basically professional liars who train themselves to fake emotions on command for money, and that watching a play just makes you cry over made-up people's suffering while doing zero for real suffering, and then that whole bit near the end where he says Geneva doesn't need imported spectacles bc real civic life should look more like everyone gathering in the town square around some flower-covered pole and being their OWN show instead of just sitting there watching professionals perform — that stuff felt oddly relevant still.

Me and my friend Priya got into it arguing about what he'd say about stuff today. She thinks he'd hate Netflix/streaming specifically the most, since it's even more passive than 18th century theatre (you literally don't even leave your house or sit with other townspeople anymore). I think the better target is actually influencer culture on instagram/tiktok, bc those are people who quite literally perform a curated, fake version of themselves for an audience as their whole job, kinda like actors except now the audience is basically everyone all the time and the "performance" pretends to be real life instead of admitting its fiction like a play does.

Can you actually use his real arguments from the Lettre (not just vibes of "old dead guy hates fun") to work out whose modern example he'd have gone after harder, or if he'd have picked some totally different third thing neither of us are even thinking of? I guess what I'm stuck on is whether his core objection is really about spectators being passive vs whether it's more about performed/fake emotion corrupting the performer's (and audience's) character, bc those two readings seem to point at pretty different modern villains and I don't wanna just guess.
