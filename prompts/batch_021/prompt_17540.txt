I am a second-year philosophy PhD student currently drafting a dissertation chapter on physicalism and the mind, and I have run into a difficulty in the functionalism/type-identity debate that my advisor keeps telling me to think through on my own rather than resolving for me. Putnam's classic multiple realizability argument is supposed to be fatal to mind-brain type-identity theory: since pain, say, is realized by C-fiber firing in humans, by some very different neural arrangement in octopuses, and in principle could be realized by silicon-based hardware in a robot, there is no single physical kind with which pain can be identified, so "pain" must instead name a functional or higher-order kind that these diverse substrates merely realize. Kim's response, sometimes described as a "local" or "structure-specific" reduction strategy, grants that no single species-independent identity is available, but denies that this refutes type-identity theory -- it only shows that the reduction must be relativized, so that pain-in-humans is identified with C-fiber firing, pain-in-octopuses with whatever its own realizer turns out to be, and so on for every structure type.

What I cannot work out to my own satisfaction is why this move is not simply a relabeling of functionalism rather than a genuine rival to it. If saving type-identity theory requires conceding that "pain" itself picks out no unified physical kind, and if the real work of unifying humans, octopuses, and the hypothetical robot under one predicate is done by whatever the structure-relative reductions have in common -- which sounds exactly like a functional role -- then it is unclear what local reduction has purchased for the identity theorist beyond the word "reduction" itself. I would appreciate a careful reconstruction of how Kim and other defenders of local reduction try to resist this collapse -- for instance, by denying that the cross-structural commonality picked out by the functionalist is itself a genuine causal-explanatory kind, or by some other route I have not considered -- together with your own assessment of whether that resistance actually succeeds, or whether the critics who say local reduction quietly concedes the functionalist's central point are right.
