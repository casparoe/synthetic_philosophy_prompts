I spent thirty-five years as a research chemist before I retired, and these days I'm finally getting round to reading philosophy properly instead of just picking up scraps of it at dinner parties. Lately I've been on a bit of a logic kick - Frege, Russell, that whole crowd - partly because formal systems were always lurking around the edges of my own work. So I was reading Dummett's book on Frege's philosophy of mathematics, and in the preface he mentions, almost in passing, that Frege kept a diary in the last year or so of his life that wasn't published until decades after he died, and its full of genuinely ugly antisemitic and anti-democratic material - from what I gather, stuff about Jews not deserving full political rights, contempt for parliamentary politics and the various parties, hostility to Catholics and the French, that sort of thing. Dummett, who more or less built a career on revering the guy, says it left him badly shaken. What really got me is that this is the same Frege who spent his whole life trying to purge anything subjective, psychological or "merely human" from logic, insisting logical laws are objective residents of what he called a "third realm" (drittes Reich, if I've got the German right) - which is a genuinely creepy coincidence of terminology given what came a decade or two later, even though I know that's probably just a coincidence and not evidence of anything.

Anyway, here's what I'd like from you. Every discussion of this I've dug up takes the tidy way out: his politics and his logic are separate compartments, so judge the Begriffsschrift and the Grundlagen on their own merits and set the diary aside. Fine, but that feels like it's dodging the actually interesting question, so instead I want you to argue the harder, more contrarian thesis: that there's a real, non-accidental connection between Frege's authoritarian, anti-pluralist politics and his core philosophical commitments - his war on psychologism, his platonism about that "third realm," and his general horror of relativism about truth. Don't just gesture at a vague family resemblance along the lines of "likes hierarchy and absolutes" - actually trace the connection through specific doctrines. Then turn around and give me the single strongest objection to your own argument (my guess is it'll have something to do with all the politically unobjectionable logical objectivists out there) and be honest about how much damage it does to your case.
