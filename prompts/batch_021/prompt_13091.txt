Write a dialogue between two philosophers of mind arguing about octopuses. One of them thinks the octopus's weird, super distributed nervous system (most of it's neurons are out in the arms, not centralized like ours) is a great real-world case of multiple realizability, and a genuine problem for reductive type-identity theories of mind. The other isn't buying it -- they run something like the Bechtel & Mundale line, that once you actually look at how neuroscientists individuate neural states (partly in functional terms, not just raw physical structure), the apparent multiple realizability mostly dissolves, and the octopus case doesn't show what the first philosopher wants it to show. Have them really engage with each other's specific arguments rather than just talking past each other, and get into the weeds on what would even count as "the same" mental state being realized differently in the first place.
