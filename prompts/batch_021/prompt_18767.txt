I'm several chapters into a novel whose protagonist — a mathematician who abandoned a promising academic career after what she calls a "metaphysical accident" — has become privately convinced that David Lewis's modal realism is true: that literally every way a world could be is a way some world is, each such world just as concrete and actual, from its own perspective, as ours, merely cut off from us in space and time. I'm drafting a scene in which her former doctoral advisor, worried about her, tries to talk her out of it not with the usual "incredulous stare" but with a more technical objection that I gather actually gave Lewis some trouble: a worry about how many individuals a possible world is allowed to contain.

As I understand the worry, it runs roughly as follows. Lewis's principle of recombination is supposed to guarantee plenitude: patch together duplicates of parts of any worlds you like, in whatever size and shape the receiving spacetime allows, and you get a further genuine possibility. But nothing in that principle obviously puts a ceiling on how large a world's population of individuals can be, and it seems arbitrary to insist afterward that there must be some maximum. Yet if, for every cardinal number, there has to be a possible world containing at least that many individuals, then the sizes of worlds cannot be bounded by any cardinal whatsoever, since Cantor's theorem guarantees there is no largest cardinal to begin with. That seems to leave all the possible worlds, taken together, too big and unruly to form the sort of determinate totality that Lewis's own semantics needs when it says things like "possibly P" is true just in case P holds at some world — quantifying, apparently, over absolutely all of them at once.

I'd like my advisor character's challenge to be a genuine philosophical challenge, not a strawman my protagonist gets to wave away too easily, but I also don't want to put a defeat into her mouth if the objection actually has a good answer. So could you construct, as rigorously as you can, the strongest available argument that a Lewis-style modal realist can block this cardinality problem through some principled restriction on recombination — one that isn't just an ad hoc stipulation adopted solely to dodge the paradox? I'd rather give her a genuinely defensible position to retreat to than a hand-wave about proper classes.
