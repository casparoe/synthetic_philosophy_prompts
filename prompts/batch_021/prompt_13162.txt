Ok so I got into one of those sprawling comment-section arguments (this time on a rationalist-adjacent subreddit, not even LW proper) about the Sleeping Beauty problem, and it went in a direction I haven't seen hashed out as much as the usual thirder/halfer back-and-forth. Someone brought up Dutch book arguments as the tiebreaker -- like "obviously you just check which credence keeps you from getting exploited by a bookie and that settles it" -- and then someone else pointed out that the standard betting case for thirding only clearly works if you score Beauty's bets seperately at each awakening; if you instead treat the whole tails-branch as one bet (since she's arguably making the "same" wager twice under tails, with no way to introspectively tell the two occassions apart), the sure-loss argument doesn't obviously go through anymore, and you can apparently rig a package-style Dutch book pointing the other way. Nobody in the thread actually settled it, and it made me want to go back to whoever basically invented this whole way of thinking about credence in the first place.

So: what do you think Frank Ramsey would have said about the Sleeping Beauty problem? He's obviously long dead before Elga's paper, the Doomsday Argument, or any of Bostrom's self-sampling stuff existed, but "Truth and Probability" is where we get degrees of belief as betting quotients and coherence-via-Dutch-book in the first place, so it seems fair to ask how his own framework, on its own terms, would shake out here. Would he land as a thirder or a halfer, or would he think the per-awakening-vs-per-trial betting mess I described above shows his method just doesn't return a determinate verdict in cases like this? I'm especially curious whether his whole operationalization of credence even survives contact with a case where the subject can't individuate the occasions she's supposedly betting on -- Beauty can't tell a Monday-tails wager from a Tuesday-tails wager, which seems like exactly the kind of thing his betting-quotient method quietly assumes away. Feel free to also guess whether he'd have had any patience for the SSA/SIA-style reference-class talk people use now, or whether he'd have waved the whole apparatus off as not really his problem.
