I'm on the curriculum committee for my rabbinical school's required first-year Rambam course, and we're stuck on how to handle the sacrifices unit. In the Guide (III:32) Maimonides basically argues korbanot were a concession to wean the Israelites off the ambient idol-worship of the ancient world -- not God's ideal, just the best pedagogically realistic option given where people actually were -- but the Mishneh Torah codifies the sacrificial laws in loving detail as permanent halakha and has the messianic king rebuilding the Temple so they resume, with zero hint that any of it was a workaround. One member keeps bringing up Nachmanides' old complaint that the theory can't be the whole truth anyway, since Abel and Noah were already sacrificing long before there was any idolatry around to wean people off of. Half the committee wants to just teach the Mishneh Torah's straight legal picture to first-years and save the Guide's account for an elective, worried it'll quietly undercut these future rabbis' ability to preach the restoration of sacrifice with a straight face down the road; the other half says that's exactly the kind of protective curating of truth that Rambam's own views on esoteric vs. exoteric teaching would seem to endorse, which is either a point in it's favor or deeply ironic depending how you look at it. Can you draft the memo the committee can actually vote on -- keep the unit, cut it, or teach it in some specific way -- with real reasons, including the strongest version of whichever option you end up rejecting.
