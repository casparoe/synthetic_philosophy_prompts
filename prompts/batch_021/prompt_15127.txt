I'm a student representative on my high school's grant committee, deciding how to spend a one-time $40,000 gift earmarked for "improving academic outcomes"; our only guidance is a bylaw requiring that funds "serve the interests of the student body as a whole" — a phrase nobody has ever defined further. One faction wants to fund a selective STEM accelerator (extra mentorship, competition coaching, new equipment) for whichever twenty students have the highest GPA, arguing that the resulting scholarships and recognition will attract more resources to the school over time, benefiting everyone eventually. The other wants the same money split evenly across tutoring hours open to any student who signs up. Since none of us on the committee knows whether we'll personally land in that top twenty, draft the memo I'll bring to Monday's vote: recommend one option using Rawls's veil of ignorance and difference principle rather than generic appeals to fairness, and give the accelerator's "rising tide" argument the strongest version you can before weighing it against the alternative.
