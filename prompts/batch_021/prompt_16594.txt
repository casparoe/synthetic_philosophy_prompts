So I fell down a rabbit hole this weekend reading about "accuracy-first" epistemology / epistemic utility theory (I think the classic paper is by some philosopher named Jim Joyce?), and now I can't stop connecting it to my day job. The basic argument seems to be: if your credences dont follow the probability axioms, there's always some coherent set of credences that beats yours on accuracy no matter how the world turns out, and this holds for any "legitimate" scoring rule, like the Brier score or the log score. But the log score is literally just negative cross-entropy, which is THE loss function almost every classifier I've ever trained uses. So doesn't that mean epistemology has basically already proven cross-entropy is the objectively correct, rational loss function, and that training a model with hinge loss or plain old MSE is technically irrational? Feels like every ML paper that skips log-loss for classification is committing a philosophical error and nobody told them. Am I missing something, or is this an actual gap between the two fields that somebody should write up?
