My undergraduate logic professor is retiring after thirty-two years, and a few dozen former students spanning four decades are throwing her a literal surprise party. This feels like it demands acknowledgment, since her signature first-day lecture, every year, was the surprise exam paradox, and she spent the rest of the semester administering genuinely unannounced pop quizzes, as though daring her own argument to make good on its conclusion. I've been asked to give the toast. For weeks, the organizing committee half-expected her to reason backward from "a party is obviously coming" to the exact date, by the same backward-induction move that's supposed to rule out any guaranteed-but-unannounced event -- the kind of argument that gets periodically rehashed on Less Wrong and its ancestor blogs without anyone, starting with Quine, quite closing the case -- and yet by all accounts she looked genuinely stunned when we walked in. Please write it: it should take that tension seriously rather than merely gesture at it, use it to say something true about her, and still work as a warm four- or five-minute toast for a room that's maybe a third logicians and two-thirds people who took her class purely to fulfill a requirement and remember her mainly for her kindness.
