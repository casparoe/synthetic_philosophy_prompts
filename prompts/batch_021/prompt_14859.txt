I've been down a bit of a Popper rabbit hole lately. Everyone on LW/ACX loves citing his falsifiability criterion with the standard examples (Freud, astrology, Marx's theory of history, etc), but this time I actually went and read some of the primary source stuff instead of just the secondhand summaries, and stumbled on this whole other thread about Darwinism that I don't think i've ever seen mentioned on any rationalist blog. Basically, in "Unended Quest" (or maybe it was one of the essay collections, going off memory a bit here) Popper says something like "survival of the fittest" is almost a tautology, since "fitness" just gets defined circularly as whatever happens to survive and reproduce, so the theory can't really be falsified in his sense, and he ends up calling the whole Darwinian theory of evolution a "metaphysical research programme" rather than a proper testable scientific theory.

This kind of threw me? I'd always just internalized evolution as basically the textbook example of a maximally falsifiable theory (wrong fossils turning up in the wrong strata, wrong biogeography, etc, would sink it immediately) and here's Popper, one of the actual founding fathers of the falsificationist epistemology this community treats as bedrock, apparently saying it doesnt clear his own bar and is closer in kind to psychoanalysis than to general relativity. I mentioned this to a friend and he said creationists apparently love quoting this exact bit of Popper, which is bizarre given Popper obviously wasn't a creationist and clearly thought evolution was true.

So my actual question is: does this reveal some real flaw in falsifiability as a demarcation criterion (like, if it can't even cleanly place evolution on the "science" side of the line, maybe the criterion is less useful than the rationalist canon gives it credit for), or was Popper just being sloppy/wrong here in a way that's basically a footnote nobody needs to care about, or is there context i'm missing that dissolves the apparent tension entirely? Would appreciate the actual nuance rather than a quick "eh, Popper was just wrong lol."
