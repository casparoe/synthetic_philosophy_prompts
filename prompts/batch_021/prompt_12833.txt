Explain (a) the analogy he draws between testing courage against fear and testing self-restraint against pleasure, (b) why he thinks the Spartan and Cretan systems are incomplete even by their own standards, and (c) what role the person presiding over the drinking party is meant to play. And here's the answer I got assigned to grade, copied exactly as my classmate submitted it:

"In Book 1, Megillus and Cleinias are proud that Sparta and Crete have laws that train young men to resist pain and fear through military drills, endurance tests, and the common messes. Socrates points out that this is clever but only half the story: a citizen also has to be tested against pleasure, not just pain, since someone who's brave in battle can still end up enslaved to his own appetites. Wine is useful for this because it lowers people's guard and sense of shame in basically the same way fear does, so a properly supervised drinking party works as a kind of safe laboratory for finding out who actually has self-control -- exactly what Sparta and Crete are already doing with their fear-drills, just redirected at pleasure instead of pain. That's why he doesn't think Athens's habit of holding symposia is just self-indulgence, as long as whoever runs the party is experienced and stays sober, the same way a ship's captain has to stay clear-headed even while everyone else on board is being tossed around.
