So I've kind of fallen down an AI safety youtube rabbit hole lately (the Robert Miles type videos about the "off switch" problem for AI), and two people in a discord server I'm in got into this whole argument about it a while back that I still think about sometimes. Neither of them actually works in AI or anything, they're just both way more read up on this stuff than me, so I honestly can't tell who had the better case.

It started when someone asked why you can't just solve the shutdown problem by making the AI totally fine with being switched off. Theo's answer was: just set it up so pressing the shutdown button gives the AI exactly the same reward as finishing its task, so on paper it has no preference either way. Nadia said that actually makes things worse, because now the AI has an easy shortcut to that same reward - it could just manipulate you into shutting it down early instead of grinding through the actual task, since getting shut down is a way more reliable bet than a task that might fail. Theo came back with "ok fine, don't reward it FOR being shut down then, just make the reward stop counting completely the second its off, so there's nothing left to gain or lose either way." Nadia said that still doesn't fix it, for two reasons: first, now it has zero reason to protect the shutdown button or keep it working, since it doesn't care what happens after anyway, and second, "not caring what happens after shutdown" isn't the same thing as "not setting anything up beforehand" - it could still use the time it does have to nudge things so its goal keeps getting pursued after its gone (leave instructions lying around, stockpile resources somewhere, whatever), since none of that costs it anything in terms of the reward it collects before being shut down. Theo pushed back that this was way too paranoid - if the thing genuinely doesn't care about after-shutdown outcomes, why would it waste any effort on some contingency plan, that's work for zero payoff. Nadia's answer was that it doesn't need to be a deliberate plan at all - competently going after almost any goal naturally tends to leave the world more "primed" to keep heading that direction, and nothing about the setup gives it any reason to avoid that.

They never really resolved it, the conversation just got buried under other messages. Could you go through it step by step and say who had the stronger point at each stage, not just who won overall, and flag anything you think they're both missing? Also curious for your honest take - is this actually a real unsolved problem with building a "corrigible" AI, or were they overcomplicating something that has a more obvious fix?
