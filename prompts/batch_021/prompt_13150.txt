I'm a retired geophysicist, and this last year of retirement I've fallen hard into philosophy of economics. Right now I'm stuck on the no-trade theorem -- the Milgrom-Stokey result, building on Aumann's old agreeing-to-disagree paper, which says that if two rational people share a prior and its common knowledge they both still want to make a trade, that mutual willingness is itself information, and once you factor it in neither of you should want the trade anymore (I probably don't have every detail exactly right, but that's the gist). There's even a nickname for the corollary, the Groucho Marx theorem, after the joke about not wanting to join any club that would have you. My sister just retired after 33 years as an options market maker, and I'm giving her toast next month -- half the room is her old trading-floor friends who'll catch every bit of that, the other half is cousins and her book club who wont. I don't want two speeches stapled together. I want one real joke built out of the theorem, something about how her whole career is basically a rebuttal to a result that says nobody should've been able to make a living the way she did, and I want it to land for both halves of the room as love, not a lecture on rational expectations. Can you write me the toast, about five minutes spoken?
