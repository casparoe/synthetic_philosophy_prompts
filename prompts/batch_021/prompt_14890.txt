My sister's degenerative condition became personally urgent last year, when our national health service refused, on cost-per-QALY grounds, to fund a treatment for her; I was then invited onto its technology-appraisal committee as a patient representative. Ahead of next month's funding round, the committee must weigh two proposals against one fixed budget. One would extend life for an otherwise healthy population, yielding large gains on standard population-derived health-state valuations. The other funds an unrelated treatment for people with a long-standing severe disability, whose additional years of life are scored lower on those same valuations, even though such patients do not themselves report correspondingly lower day-to-day well-being. Some committee members treat these population-derived weights as the only workable common currency for comparing benefit across such different patients; others argue that using them here effectively penalizes people for their disability a second time. Please draft the committee's decision memo recommending how we should weigh the two proposals, and engage honestly with the strongest objection to whichever recommendation you adopt.
