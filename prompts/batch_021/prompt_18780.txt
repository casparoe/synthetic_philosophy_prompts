Write a dialogue between a total utilitarian and a critical-level utilitarian at a population-ethics workshop, each trying to convince the other that the counterintuitive implication their own theory accepts is the lesser evil. The total utilitarian accepts Parfit's Repugnant Conclusion (that a sufficiently large population of lives barely worth living can be better than a much smaller population of excellent lives) as the cost of holding onto what Arrhenius calls the Non-Sadism Condition: never ranking the addition of a suffering life above the addition of one with ordinary but positive well-being. The critical-level utilitarian avoids the Repugnant Conclusion by holding that a life adds positive value only once its welfare clears some threshold above neutral, but must then answer for the resulting Sadistic Conclusion, on which their theory can rank the addition of a few thoroughly miserable lives above the addition of many lives that are good but fall just short of that threshold. Have each invoke Arrhenius's impossibility theorem to argue that some such cost is unavoidable, while still pressing each other on concrete cases rather than simply restating their own theory's appeal.
