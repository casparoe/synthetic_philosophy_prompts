My father moved into a memory-care facility last month, and the staff recently introduced a companion robot that they say learns each resident's preferences through inverse reinforcement learning: rather than being told what someone wants, it infers a reward function from what that person actually does. Reading about how the method works, what unsettles me is that the same behavior it watches -- him refusing food, pacing at odd hours, repeating a question -- is equally consistent with "this is what he wants" and with "this is what his illness is doing to what he wants," and nothing in the behavior alone can tell those two apart unless you already assume he is choosing rationally. I'd like you to write a short story about a caregiving robot that has built a resident's preference model this way, and that ends up faithfully optimizing for his illness rather than for him. Don't state the problem outright; show it through what the robot does, how it justifies its choices, and how the people around it slowly come to notice. Somewhere in the story, have someone ask what he would have wanted before he got sick, and let the story sit with how unanswerable that question has become.
