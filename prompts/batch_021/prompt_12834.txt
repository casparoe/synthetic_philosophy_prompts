Ok so I got into it with my roommate last night about capitalism vs socialism (we'd both had a few drinks, as per usual), and he pulled out this line from John Stuart Mill — like, THE guy I always thought was basically the founding father of let-the-market-decide liberalism — saying communism would basically be better than what we've got now. I looked it up after and he wasnt lying, it's a real passage from the start of one of the chapters in Mill's "Chapters on Socialism":

"First, among existing social evils, may be mentioned the evil of Poverty, The institution of Property is upheld and commended principally as being the means by which labor and frugality are insured their reward, and mankind enabled to emerge from indigence. It may be so; most Socialists allow that it has been so in earlier periods of history. But if the institution can do nothing more or better in this respect than it has hitherto done, its capabilities, they affirm, are very insignificant. What proportion of the population, in the most civilized countries of Europe, enjoy, in their own persons, anything worth naming of the benefits of property?"

And right next to it, set up like some kind of epigraph or heading quote for the chapter: "The restraints of Communism would be freedom in comparison with the present condition of the majority of the human race." Which is apparently a line from his OWN Principles of Political Economy, not him quoting some socialist just to knock it down later.

So did Mill genuinely come around to thinking communism might be preferable to private property, or is something else going on with how this passage actually works? It doesnt match up at all with the guy I thought I knew from On Liberty.
