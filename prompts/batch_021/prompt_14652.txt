I'm about a third of the way into a new novel and the central image of the book is a pair of rooms on opposite ends of an old house that are, as far as anyone can tell, completely identical -- same dust on the same shelves, same crack in the same window. I built the whole thing around Max Black's old two-spheres thought experiment about the identity of indiscernibles, because that picture of two things being qualitatively identical but still stubbornly two things and not one is exactly the itch I want the novel to scratch. The problem is my writing group clocked the reference immediately, and now the whole chapter reads like I lifted a philosophy 101 example instead of actually thinking it through, which, fair enough.

So what I need is a different example that gets at the same tension but comes at it from a different angle than Black did -- not the two spheres, and please not just "they're identical twins," that's too easy an out. I'd love something that engages a little with how philosophers of physics talk about indistinguishable particles and "weak" discernibiliy, rather than leaning entirely on spatial relations the way Black's spheres do, since I haven't seen that angle dramatized anywhere and it might get me out of my plagiarism-by-implication problem. Could you actually spell the example out in some detail -- what the two things are, in what sense they're meant to be qualitatively identical, and exactly where a stubborn Leibnizian would insist a hidden difference has to be lurking -- so I've got enough to build an actual scene around it rather than just a factoid? Extra credit if it's a case that working metaphysicians still genuinely disagree about, since "smart people still argue about this one" is pretty much the mood I want for this part of the book.
