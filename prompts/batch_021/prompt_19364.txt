So I've been kind of obsessed with this thing called Aumann's agreement theorem ever since I stumbled on it in a blog post — the idea that two honest, rational people supposedly can't just agree to disagree forever. My brother Danny and his wife Ren got into it about this exact idea over dinner last week and I honestly can't stop turning it over in my head, trying to figure out who actually had the better of it.

Some backstory: they've had a running bet for months on whether their neighbors business is gonna fold by the end of the year, and neither one has budged an inch even though its basically all they talk about anymore. Danny brought up the theorem and said "see, this actually proves one of us isnt being honest about what we really think, because if we're both rational this should force us to agree." Ren said no, the theorem needs both people to start out with the same prior — like the same starting beliefs before either of you saw any evidence — and there's no way two different people with two totally different lives actually have that, so it doesnt even apply to the two of them. Danny said fine, but if it only kicks in when two people have the exact same starting point, then it can never apply to any two actual humans, so why does anyone act like it means something for real arguments. Ren said it's not totally useless even then, because the fact that they keep disagreeing after talking about it so much is still evidence that either their priors really are different or one of them isn't being fully straight about how confident they actually are. Danny came back with: but they've gone through literally every reason either of them has, out loud, more than once, so by her own logic they should've landed in the same place by now, and they haven't, so somethings still not adding up. Ren said going back and forth on reasons over dinner isn't the same as the setup in the theorem, which is about both people having common knowledge of the actual number the other one landed on, not just trading arguments, and that's a way higher bar than "we argued about it a lot."

Could you go through their exchange move by move and say who's got the stronger position at each point, not just who wins overall? And if you think there's some bigger thing both of them are missing here, I'd want to hear that too.
