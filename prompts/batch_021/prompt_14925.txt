I am writing a novel whose central chapter is narrated from inside a character's grief hallucination: she perceives her recently deceased mother sitting in the armchair, an experience that, from the inside, is seamless with any ordinary act of perception she has ever had. Rather than write the scene on the strength of the vague and possibly incoherent idea that hallucination is simply "perception gone wrong," I want to ground it in an actual philosophical position. Please compare disjunctivism's response to the argument from illusion with the intentionalist (representationalist) response. Both positions reject the classical sense-datum conclusion that we are only ever directly aware of private mental objects rather than the world itself, so I'd like you to locate precisely where they part ways -- and then say which view gives the more defensible account of a case like my character's, where the non-veridical experience cannot be told apart from veridical perception at the time it occurs. Justify your answer.
