I'm putting together a session for a graduate seminar on the metaphysics of science, and I'd like to pressure-test an idea before I commit to it. Rather than rehearsing the usual, well-worn contrast between "laws as mere regularities" and "laws as relations among universals," I want to pit David Lewis's Humean Best System Account (BSA) against David Armstrong's anti-Humean necessitarian account (the Dretske–Tooley–Armstrong, or DTA, theory) on the specific point where each is most vulnerable, and see whether those two vulnerabilities are really distinct problems or the same problem in different metaphysical costume.

On the anti-Humean side, Armstrong's theory faces what is sometimes called the inference problem: the second-order relation of nomic necessitation N(F,G) is supposed to necessitate the corresponding first-order regularity that all Fs are Gs, yet it is unclear how positing a further, sui generis relation between universals — rather than simply stipulating the regularity outright — does any genuine explanatory work, especially once N is denied the status of logical or broadly logical necessity. On the Humean side, Lewis's own account faces a structurally similar charge, pressed most forcefully by Maudlin: since the best system is just whichever deductive systematization of the Humean mosaic wins out on simplicity, strength, and (for probabilistic laws) fit, critics argue that whatever gets selected this way is at most a good summary of the mosaic, and cannot explain the very pattern of particular facts it summarizes, or ground the asymmetric counterfactual dependence we ordinarily expect laws to underwrite.

Please compare these two accounts specifically with respect to how damaging their corresponding objections are. Is the inference problem for Armstrong's necessitation relation ultimately more or less serious than the explanatory-circularity problem for Lewis's Best System Account, and why? I am especially interested in whether one of the two problems can be dissolved by a fairly modest amendment to its theory (for instance, by simply giving up the demand that laws explain their own instances) while the other cannot be dissolved so cheaply — or whether, on reflection, the two problems are ultimately the same worry wearing different clothes.
