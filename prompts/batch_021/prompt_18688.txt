I'm assembling a collection of short stories, each built around a single argument from analytic philosophy, and I'd like a draft for the entry on induction. The concept is Nelson Goodman's grue: an object is grue if it is examined before some future time t and is green, or not examined before t and is blue, with bleen as the mirror-image predicate. Every emerald examined so far counts as both green and grue, so the evidence collected up to now confirms "all emeralds are green" and "all emeralds are grue" equally well, even though the two hypotheses make opposite predictions about emeralds first examined at or after t. What I want the story to preserve is Goodman's own twist: nothing in the evidence itself favors green over grue, since a language that took grue and bleen as its primitive color terms could just as easily define green and blue as the artificial, time-indexed predicates built out of them. Please write me a short story — around 800 words, well under 1000 — set in the hours around such a t, following one character for whom this is not an abstract puzzle but a real question about what to expect when they open their eyes tomorrow. Leave it open which color they see; Goodman's own proposed fix, entrenchment, only explains which prediction we are in the habit of making, not which one is correct.
