hey, im a phil PhD student (pragmatism/Putnam is my main area) and I'm trying to turn some seminar notes into an actual paper on his late religion stuff — the "Jewish Philosophy as a Guide to Life" book and the negative theology essay, not the Twin Earth/functionalism stuff everyone already knows him for. Can you poke holes in the core argument before I take it to my advisor? Still rough, sorry for the note-form.

The setup: Putnam wants to reject two things at once. First, classical theism, where "God exists" is a straight metaphysical claim you could in principle check from what he elsewhere calls the God's-eye view. Second, Wittgensteinian non-cognitivism about religion (basically D.Z. Phillips's reading), where religious sentences don't have factual content at all, they just express a form of life — Putnam thinks this actually insults believers by draining away the seriousness they themselves attach to their own claims.

His escape route leans on Rosenzweig, Buber, and Levinas. From Rosenzweig: drop the "old thinking" that surveys God/World/Man from outside in one totalizing Hegelian system, for a "new thinking" done from inside lived relation — creation, revelation, redemption. From Buber: the basic religious relation is I-Thou not I-It, so "does God exist" is a bit of a category error, more like asking mid-conversation whether your interlocutor exists than a hypothesis you test from outside. From Levinas: the infinite/transcendent shows up first as the unconditional ethical demand of the face of the other, prior to any ontology — so God-talk is mostly a way of marking how non-negotiable that demand is, not a claim about a being "out there." Put together, religious language keeps real bite (contra Phillips) without needing "God exists" to be realist-true in the God's eye sense Putnam rejects even for chairs and numbers.

My worry, which is what I actually want pushed on: I don't think there's a stable spot between Phillips and classical realism here, just two ways of collapsing dressed up in nicer vocabulary. Either (a) "God is real" just means "I stand in, or should stand in, this I-Thou relation and answer to this demand" — which is Phillips with better furniture, not an escape from him — or (b) Putnam wants it to be really, mind-independently true that the other places this claim on me whether I recognize it or not, which just relocates the view-from-nowhere from ontology to ethics. Borrowing three thinkers who each arguably sit on one horn or the other doesnt obviously buy a genuine third option, it just lets the argument equivocate between them paragraph by paragraph.

Is that fair to Putnam, or is there a real third reading I'm missing? And is the Levinas move doing less work than I think it is — I've seen the objection that Levinas wants more ontological weight for "the face" than Putnam can actually afford him, and that might be the cleanest place to press. Be harsh, I'd rather find the hole now than in front of my committee.
