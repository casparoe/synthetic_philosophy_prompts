I've been down a rabbit hole reading about "safe Pareto improvements" — this idea from recent game theory/AI-agent research where two parties stuck in a mediocre equilibrium can tweak how their delegates play so that neither side is ever worse off than the status quo, and theres a decent chance both end up better off, without either side having to trust the other's private information. Basically you get some of the gains from cooperation while staying immune to exploitation if the other party doesn't reciprocate. It made me think of Thomas Schelling, who spent his career on exactly this kind of problem — commitment devices, credible threats, focal points. If Schelling were alive today and saw AI agents using tricks like this to bargain on behalf of people, companies, even countries, what do you reckon he'd make of it? Would he see it as basically a cleaner, more rigorous version of what he was already doing in The Strategy of Conflict, or is there something about outsourcing commitment to an algorithm that breaks his whole framework?
