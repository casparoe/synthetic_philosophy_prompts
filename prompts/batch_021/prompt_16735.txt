A couple years back I wrote a piece on the decision-theory obsessions of the AI-safety/rationalist crowd (Newcomb's problem, counterfactual mugging, updateless decision theory, all of it), and the one example that actually stuck with me was Parfit's hitchhiker: you're dying of thirst in the desert, a driver will only pick you up if she's confident you'll pay her $1000 once your safely in town, and the updateless answer is that the rational thing is to actually pay once your safe, even though by then you have nothing left to causally gain from it, because being the sort of agent who pays is what got you the ride in the first place. I filed it away as a nice illustration for the piece and moved on.

Now I'm on the volunteer board of a small nonprofit newsroom, and we've basically got a live version of it. A ransomware crew has locked our archive and is threatening to publish unredacted source material, including stuff that would out a whistleblower we spent a year protecting, unless we pay by Monday. Our security consultant and about half the board want to pay: it's a modest sum against a person's actual safety, and whatever effect our paying has on the next outlet's odds of getting targeted is, causally speaking, not a cost we're the ones bearing. The other half say that's exactly backwards -- outlets get targeted because attackers expect them to be soft touches, so the fact that we're the kind of newsroom that pays travels ahead of us into every future negotiation, and we should refuse even now, mid-crisis, with no pre-signed pledge forcing our hand. I get the updateless logic under the second view, but the driver in Parfit's story is basically a perfect predictor of you, and our attackers are just some guys working off a spreadsheet of past victims -- so I don't know how much of that logic survives the trip from thought experiment to actual extortion email.

Could you draft the short memo I'd bring to Friday's emergency board call? I need it to land on one actual recommendation with a concrete deadline attached, not "reasonable people disagree," and it has to take the pay-up camp seriously on its own terms -- given what's actually at stake for our source -- rather than just telling them they're reasoning locally instead of updatelessly.
