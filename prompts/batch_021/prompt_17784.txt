Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have recently taken up philosophy, and Graham Priest's case for dialetheism and paraconsistent logic has caught my attention. It seems to me that quantum mechanics settled Priest's central claim long before he gave it a name: a spin-1/2 particle prepared in a superposition of 'up' and 'down' along a given axis genuinely has both properties at once prior to measurement, so that the statement 'the particle has spin-up' and its negation are simultaneously true of the same system. Doesn't this mean physics has already furnished an empirically confirmed dialetheia, and shouldn't physicists therefore concede that quantum theory presupposes a paraconsistent logic rather than the classical logic they were trained in?
