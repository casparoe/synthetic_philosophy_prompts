I'm one of two student reps on my school's curriculum review committee, and next month we vote on a pilot that would merge 9th-grade history, bio, and English into one project-based "humanities/science" block instead of teaching them as seperate periods with normal tests. The teacher pushing it keeps citing this old philosopher, Whitehead, who wrote a whole book arguing that normal schooling just produces "inert ideas" — stuff you can spit back on a test but never actually use — and that learning is supposed to move through stages he called romance, precision, and generalization instead of just drilling facts and grammar forever. A few other teachers on the committee (esp. the AP Chem and Calc people) think this is just trendy language for lowering rigor right before kids start their college apps, with zero real evidence it improves outcomes. I'm the one who has to write the actual recommendation memo for the vote — can you draft it? It needs to land on one real recommendation (approve, reject, or approve as a limited pilot with conditions), actually engage with what Whitehead's argument really is instead of a strawman, and not just wave away the rigor/college-admissions worry either.
