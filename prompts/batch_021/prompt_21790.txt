I'm a philosophy PhD student completing a bioethics practicum, and this term's seminar on the ethics of aggregation has centered on Taurek's "Should the Numbers Count?" and the replies to it. Through the practicum I sit on my university hospital's Crisis Standards of Care committee, which is finalizing protocol language for a scenario the members keep arguing about: a scarce medication whose entire remaining supply could either fully treat one critically ill patient, or, split into smaller doses, fully treat five other patients who are equally critical and equally likely to recover with treatment — never both. The current draft simply tells staff to allocate to whichever option saves more patients. One member, invoking Taurek, objects that this treats five separate losses as though their sum were itself a further loss that outweighs the one patient's claim, and wants such ties broken by a coin flip between the two options rather than by headcount; others say any rule short of "save the greater number" is indefensible to a family, a regulator, or a jury. Please draft the short memo I need to bring to next week's committee vote: recommend one specific rule for the protocol, and give reasoning the losing side could actually engage with rather than dismiss.
