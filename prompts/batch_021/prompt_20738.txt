I'm on my philosophy department's graduate admissions committee this year, and something from our last meeting has been troubling me. I read one applicant's file first, before any of the others, and was struck by how strong it was, ranking her well above the rest of the pool. Partway through our discussion, a colleague brought up research showing that materials reviewed first in a stack tend to get rated more favorably than equally strong materials reviewed later, simply because reviewers are more attentive to whatever they see first - and our files, it turned out, had been stacked in a fairly arbitrary order. That evening I reread her file slowly, side by side with two others, specifically watching for signs that its early position had swayed me. I found none: on its own merits, it still seemed clearly the strongest.

Should learning about this effect lower my confidence that hers really is the best file, even though my careful second look turned up nothing to fault in my reasoning? And if it should, what am I supposed to say the next time someone on the committee asks me, point-blank, whether I still think she's our top candidate? I'd like your sense of what I owe my own judgment here, and why.
