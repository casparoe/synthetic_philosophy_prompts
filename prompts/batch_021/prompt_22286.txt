One proposed criterion for legitimate persuasion by an AI system holds that a persuasive act is legitimate just in case it consists entirely of true, relevant claims and valid or inductively strong inferences, addressed to the listener's capacity for reasoning rather than bypassing it (e.g., via exploiting cognitive biases or emotional pressure). The philosophical literature on manipulation already shows this criterion to be too permissive even for ordinary human persuaders, since someone can be manipulated using nothing but true claims and valid arguments if the persuader merely exploits foreseeable facts about how the target will respond to them. Give an example showing how the criterion could fail in a way that is distinctive to a superintelligent persuader specifically: a case in which the communication is wholly truthful and logically impeccable throughout, would raise no manipulation worry if produced by an equally scrupulous human, but should count as illegitimate precisely because of some capability that only a superintelligence possesses. Explain exactly what that capability is, and why it undermines purely content-based accounts of legitimate persuasion once a persuader's abilities exceed the human level.
