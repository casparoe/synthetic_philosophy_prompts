I've followed the periodic threads on Astral Codex Ten and LessWrong about polygenic embryo screening for years, and now the question isn't academic: my partner and I are deciding whether to use it for our next IVF cycle. A friend who does philosophy for a living pointed out that our dilemma is a clean instance of Parfit's non-identity problem, and that the problem's bite here depends on something like Kripke's view of the necessity of origin -- that facts about which particular sperm and egg a person developed from are, if true at all, necessarily true of them, so that no one could have originated from different gametes and still been that same, numerically identical person. If that's right, then "selecting for a healthier child" isn't a case of making some existing possible child better off; it's a case of choosing which of several distinct possible children gets to exist at all. Please describe the most important implications of rigid designators and the necessary a posteriori, applied to personal origin in this way, for how my partner and I should think about the ethics of embryo selection -- and specifically for whether it should change how much weight we put on claims like "the selected embryo will have a better life than the alternative would have had."
