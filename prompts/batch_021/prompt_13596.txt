One of the two works on epistemology, the other on classical Chinese philosophy, and here's roughly how it went:

(2) The Zhuangzi scholar said that's an anachronistic reading: Descartes treats not-knowing as a problem to be defeated, while Zhuangzi ends the passage completely serene, using the unresolvability to make a point about how identity and perspective shift, not to mount a challenge to knowledge.

(4) The Zhuangzi scholar said a "skeptical argument" isn't just the scenario, it's the scenario plus treating the resulting gap in knowledge as troubling, and Zhuangzi does neither, so calling it skepticism smuggles in a pretty Cartesian idea of what the passage is even for.
(5) The epistemologist then pointed out that the "must be some distinction" line cuts against reading the whole passage as pure "nothing is really fixed" relativism, so maybe the Zhuangzi scholar's reading proves too much.
