Last spring I spent two weeks on a homestay in New Zealand. Before I left, my host sister described a local bird to me over video call so that I would recognize it: a weka, she said, is basically a scrawny, brown, flightless bird about the size of a chicken, except much bolder, since it will walk right up to someone and take a chip off their plate. I had never seen an actual weka, only her description of one by comparison to a chicken. On my first afternoon there, an unfamiliar bird emerged from the hedge, took a chip from my plate, and I said, "that's a weka," before anyone told me. A friend back home insists this wasn't real knowledge on my part, just a lucky guess dressed up as reasoning, since I had effectively solved a logic puzzle from her description and actually seeing the bird taught me nothing new. That doesn't sit right with me: it felt like seeing the bird taught me something the description alone hadn't, though I can't say what. Is my friend right that I merely deduced this in advance, or is something else going on when someone first recognizes a real thing based only on a comparison they were told about beforehand?
