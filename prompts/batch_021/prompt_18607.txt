I recently traded a doctorate in ancient philosophy for a research position on AI "scalable oversight," where my team trains two models to argue opposing answers to the same question before a judge -- sometimes a human, sometimes a weaker model -- who cannot verify either answer directly and must decide who is right based on the exchange alone. The method's central wager is that honesty carries a structural advantage: a false claim should be harder to sustain under cross-examination than a true one. I keep returning to my dissertation chapter on the Gorgias, where Socrates argues that rhetoric aims only at persuading the ignorant rather than at discovering the truth, and where Gorgias himself boasts that a skilled speaker could out-argue a physician about medical treatment before an audience with no medical training. Reconstructing Socrates' actual arguments against Gorgias, Polus, and Callicles as faithfully as you can, what do you think his verdict on debate between artificial reasoners would be: a technological descendant of his own elenchus, or the very sophistry he diagnosed in that dialogue, now automated?
