So I just turned in my master's thesis, and in the acknowledgments I wrote the standard hedge: "despite careful checking, some errors almost certainly remain, and they are my own." My housemate and her boyfriend got into a whole argument about that one sentence over dinner and I still cant decide who won.

(1) She said the sentence is incoherent: I'm asserting every single claim in the thesis, but also asserting that the conjunction of them all is false -- a contradiction no matter how humbly it's phrased.

(2) He said that's literally the textbook "preface paradox," and it's totally rational: my confidence in each claim comes from actually checking that specific claim, while my confidence that "something's probably wrong somewhere" comes from base-rate knowledge about how theses go -- different evidential sources, so thats no real inconsistency, just two beliefs formed two different ways.

(3) She said that proves too much, because you could say the exact same thing about a lottery (the classic lottery-paradox setup): "ticket 1 won't win, ticket 2 won't win," each individually well supported, while also believing one of them wins -- and everyone agrees that's fishy. So why does "different evidential sources" save the thesis case but not the lottery case?

(4) He said the lottery is disanalogous because the "won't win" belief there is grounded in nothing but raw statistics with zero claim-specific evidence, whereas every sentence of the thesis was individually checked -- not the same kind of belief at all.

Neither of them budged, and since it's literally my thesis on the line, I'd love an actual answer. Can you go through moves 1-4 and say who's got the better of it at each one, not just an overall winner, and then settle the real question -- is there a principled difference between the thesis case and the lottery case, or is move 4 just special pleading dressed up as epistemology?
