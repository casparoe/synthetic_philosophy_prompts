Ok, this has been eating at me for weeks and I trust you more than my actual writing group right now, who are, lets say, not neutral parties here. There's a woman from my old MFA cohort, I'll call her R, who I got on totally fine with back then - we swapped drafts, grabbed beers, all that. Her debut came out last spring and just exploded: six-figure deal, blurbs from people whose blurbs actually mean something, and now apparently there's a streaming adaptation in development. My second novel came out that same season with a small press and is doing, generously, fine.

Here's the part I actually want your take on. Over the last few months I've become the person at parties who has Opinions about her book, unprompted. I call it emotionally manipulative, say it hits its beats like a formula, "competent but hollow" is a phrase I've used more than once. And in the same breath I've started implying I'd never write something so crowd-pleasing myself, that I care more about the sentence than the hook - basically treating my own so-so sales as proof of integrity instead of, you know, a lack of readers. My partner finally said, "you didn't talk about her writing like this when you were actually reading her drafts, you only started once the deal happened," and timeline-wise she's not wrong.

I know the boring answer is "sour grapes, you're jealous, get over it," and fine, maybe that's basically whats going on underneath. But it feels a bit more specific than just deciding the book is bad because I can't have what she has. It's more like I've started treating the commercial success itself as faintly damning - like the sheer size of it basically proves the book must be a little dishonest, while quieter, harder-to-love books (mine, for instance) get to count as virtuous purely for not doing that. Is that actually a different move than ordinary sour grapes, or the same one with extra steps? And either way: if a harsh judgment like mine only shows up once someone succeeds, does the timing tell you anything at all about whether the judgment is any good, or can jealousy and being correct just coexist with no way to pry them apart from the inside?
