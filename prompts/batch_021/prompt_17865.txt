Been down a Thomas Hobbes rabbit hole for a piece on history's great intellectual own-goals, and I want to sanity check something. The guy apparently spent something like the last 25-ish years of his life insisting he'd squared the circle, getting picked apart in print again and again by John Wallis and the rest of the Oxford math crowd, and never once backed down even after being shown pretty clearly where he'd gone wrong. But the whole premise of Leviathan was that Hobbes was doing political philosophy "geometrically" -- deriving the social contract and sovereignty from first principles the same way Euclid derives theorems. So if he demonstrably couldn't hack it as an actual geometer, doesnt that mean the method the whole book rests on is basically discredited too? Curious why that's not, like, the headline every time somebody teaches Leviathan.
