Over the thirty-five years I spent running an evolutionary ecology lab, one methodological quarrel kept resurfacing at conferences: the charge, revived periodically since Darwin's day, that "fitness" is an empty concept because it is simply defined as whatever traits happen to survive and reproduce, so that "survival of the fittest" collapses into a tautology unless fitness can be specified independently of the outcomes it is meant to explain. Since retiring I have been working through the philosophy of economics, half as a hobby and half as penance for having ignored the field for so long, and I was struck to meet what looks like the very same worry wearing a different coat. Samuelson's revealed-preference program, as I understand it, tried to strip "utility" of any psychological content and define an agent's preferences purely in terms of the consistency of their observed choices, so that x counts as preferred to y just when x was chosen while y was available. That move seems to invite the biologist's tautology problem all over again: if "preferred" just means "chosen," doesn't the claim that agents maximize utility become true by construction, a bookkeeping device rather than a substantive claim about them?

I would like you to compare the conception of utility built into the revealed-preference tradition with the older, mentalistic conception inherited from Bentham and Jevons, on which utility names a real psychological magnitude that choice merely provides evidence for, and can on occasion misrepresent. I gather that Amartya Sen made something close to this contrast central to his attack on revealed-preference theory, arguing that economists cannot have it both ways: either the theory stays purely behavioral and forfeits any claim to underwrite judgments about a person's welfare, or it is pressed into welfare work and thereby quietly reintroduces the very mentalistic commitments it was invented to avoid. Please assess how much of that charge sticks, specifying where revealed preference genuinely succeeds in avoiding the older tradition's commitments and where, if anywhere, it covertly smuggles them back in. I would also value your judgment on a possible disanalogy: biologists have mostly defused their own tautology problem by treating fitness as a propensity, an expected reproductive output rather than an achieved one, which gives the concept content independent of what actually survives. Is there an analogous move available that could rescue revealed-preference utility from circularity, or does the economic case fail for reasons the biological one does not?
