I don't work in ethics myself (philosophy of language is more my thing), so I've been chewing on this without really having the tools to sort it out, and it's been bugging me since a faculty meeting last week. We have a colleague — call her Meera, not her real name — who for years has done a ton of stuff for our job-market students that was never part of anyone's actual job: mock interviews on weekends, marathon calls about cover letters, checking in individually with every student every week each fall. None of it was ever asked of her, and nobody else does anything close to it. The problem is it's quietly become the expectation. Our newest hire, who just does the ordinary required advising and nothing more, is getting side-eyed by students and even a little by the chair, like he's shirking something, even though there's nothing beyond standard advising in his contract or anyone else's. Meanwhile Meera is completely burnt out and would love to scale back, but feels like she can't without everyone concluding she's stopped caring.

Something about this feels philosophically off in a way I can't quite pin down. The whole point of doing more than duty strictly requires is supposed to be that no one else is on the hook for not doing it themselves — thats what's supposed to make it praiseworthy rather than merely expected. But here, doing it seems to have generated a new hook for everybody, including, weirdly, future-Meera. When I brought this up, a friend in ethics just said "yeah, that's basically why people fight over whether moral saints are even a coherent ideal to hold up," which didn't fully clarify things for me. Could you actually walk me through what's going on here in careful terms — is this a recognized problem, does it suggest that the whole category of act I'm gesturing at is less stable than it looks, and is there a principled way for Meera to pull back that doesn't just amount to "stop being so nice"? And please don't just tell me "people have different role-obligations" — that's true but it's clearly not what's actually bothering me about the case.
