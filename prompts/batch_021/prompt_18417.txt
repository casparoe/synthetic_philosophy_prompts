I'm a PhD student in philosophy and TA for our department's large introductory ethics lecture, which is how I ended up as the graduate-student representative on our undergraduate teaching committee. Every December, about fifteen of us grade the common final exam together in one long session, and the professor's routine check of our marks against her own benchmark grades has found, three years running, that agreement drops sharply and reliably after about hour five. This year, several of us whose grading fell in that window re-marked our own hour-five-plus exams blind to our original scores; almost all of us reproduced them and still find them well-supported by the rubric on direct inspection. I'm taking a graduate seminar this term on higher-order evidence and epistemic akrasia, and the committee is split on exactly that question: some members want to mandate a fresh second grader for every hour-five-plus exam from now on regardless of how confident the original grader remains after re-checking, while others think a documented, item-by-item reconfirmation should be allowed to stand, since automatic override treats the graders' own reflective judgment as worthless. Please draft the committee's short policy memo: recommend a concrete rule for handling future exams, and engage honestly with the strongest argument for trusting a grader's post-audit reconfirmation rather than overriding it automatically.
