On the rationalist blogs and forums I've followed for years, the standard case for thinking a sufficiently detailed whole-brain emulation would preserve one's mental life rests heavily on multiple realizability: since a mental state such as pain is supposedly individuated by its causal-functional role rather than by its physical medium, a silicon emulation with the right computational organization should realize the same mental states as the biological brain it replaces. I recently came across the critique, associated chiefly with Bechtel and Mundale, that when neuroscientists compare cognitive functions across species they typically individuate brain states at a coarser grain that reveals real structural commonality, rather than the rampant multiple realizability Putnam's original examples assumed. On this view, many textbook cases of multiple realizability owe more to the philosopher's choice of description-level than to any real discovery about the mind. Having signed up for cryonics partly on the strength of the standard functionalist story, please describe the most important implications of this critique for how much confidence that story can still license, should I ever be revived through emulation rather than biological repair.
