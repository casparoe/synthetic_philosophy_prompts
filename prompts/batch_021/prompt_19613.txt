Been slowly working my way through Rawls this year—strange retirement hobby for an old geophysicist, but here we are. Got to the original position and the difference principle, and fell down a rabbit hole into Harsanyi's old critique, where he says that if the parties behind the veil actually reasoned like proper expected-utility maximizers (treating "I don't know who I'll be" as "I'm equally likely to be anyone"), they'd land on average utilitarianism, not maximin and the difference principle. Rawls of course has his own reasons for saying you can't, or shouldn't, assign probabilities like that behind a veil that thick.

Could you write me a dialogue between two contract-designers who are still stuck behind the veil, about to vote on the principles for there society before either one knows who they'll turn out to be—one reasoning their way to the difference principle, the other to average utilitarianism? I want both of them sharp, actually going after each other's weakest points (the equiprobability assumption, how much risk-aversion is even rational when it's your one and only life on the line, etc) rather than just trading slogans about fairness vs. efficiency.
