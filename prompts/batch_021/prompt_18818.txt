I'm teaching a grad seminar on conditionals this term, and we just spent two sessions on Lewis's 1976 triviality result -- the proof that you can't have the "Equation" (P(if A, B) = P(B | A), the idea that your credence in a conditional should just be your conditional credence) hold across a whole family of credence functions closed under updating without the probability space collapsing into something trivial. Half the room walked away thinking the moral is just "well, I guess conditionals don't have truth conditions then," like that's the only place the argument could possibly land, and its been bugging me ever since, because most of them couldn't actually reconstruct the proof well enough to say why it's specifically the demand that the Equation survive conditionalizing -- not the Equation on its own -- that generates the collapse. My office mate thinks I'm overcomplicating a settled question; I think the seminar got the CliffsNotes version of a much messier debate, and honestly I want a model answer sharp enough to embarrass their response papers a little.

So here's the actual ask.
