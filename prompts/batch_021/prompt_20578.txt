"Priest claims the Liar sentence ('This sentence is false') is a genuine dialetheia -- true and false. Explain (a) why the classical Principle of Explosion makes this dangerous for classical logic, (b) how adopting a paraconsistent logic like Priest's LP (the Logic of Paradox) is supposed to defuse the danger, and (c) whether 'dialetheism' and 'paraconsistent logic' just name the same position. Use the Liar sentence as your running example throughout."

Here's the student's answer:

"The Liar sentence L says of itself that it's false. Suppose L is true -- then, since that's what it says, L is false. Suppose L is false -- then what it says is the case, so L is true. Priest's response is to stop trying to avoid this and just accept that L is both true and false: a dialetheia.

The trouble is that classical logic can't tolerate even one true contradiction. From A and ¬A you can validly derive an arbitrary B: add B to A by disjunction-introduction to get A∨B, then use disjunctive syllogism with ¬A to strip away the A disjunct, leaving B. Since B was arbitrary, literally everything becomes provable -- this is the Principle of Explosion (ex contradictione quodlibet). If L is a genuine dialetheia and our logic is classical, we could derive that the moon is made of cheese. So something has to give.

This is why Priest reaches for a non-classical logic: LP is a three-valued system that adds a status 'Both' alongside True and False, and treats 'Both' as designated so L can be assigned this status instead of forcing a choice between the two horns. In this respect LP is really in the same family as Kripke's fixed-point construction, where the Liar also drops out of the classical true/false partition into a third category the theory was built to accommodate -- both are ways of giving the paradoxical sentence a stable home outside bivalence rather than banning it as meaningless. Because LP keeps the classical truth tables as much as possible and just lets designated values do less work, disjunctive syllogism stops being valid, and the explosion argument above no longer goes through.

So overall I'd say 'dialetheism' and 'paraconsistent logic' are basically two labels for one package: dialetheism just is the thesis that some sentences need the kind of logic paraconsistent systems provide, and a logic counts as paraconsistent precisely because it's been built to let dialetheias like L sit true-and-false without exploding. You can't really have one without the other."
