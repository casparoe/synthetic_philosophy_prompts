I sit on the board of a mid-sized research foundation and recently finished my first stint as an ordinary panelist on one of our grant-review panels. The board is about to vote on our executive director's proposal to triple every panel, from four reviewers to twelve, on the theory that more independent judgment converges on better funding decisions, especially on our closest calls. However, what I saw during calibration calls made me skeptical: reviewers are drawn from a small, overlapping set of subfields, scores drift toward whoever speaks first or most confidently, and on the genuinely contested proposals -- the ones this reform is meant to help with -- I doubt any single reviewer is even reliably right more than half the time. Please draft the memo I'll circulate to the board before the vote: take a clear position on the proposal, engage seriously with the "more independent judgment" reasoning rather than dismissing it outright, and say concretely what, if anything, would actually fix the problem it's meant to solve.
