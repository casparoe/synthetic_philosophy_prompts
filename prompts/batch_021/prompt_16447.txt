I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher, but a disagreement between two people on my team has been bugging me, and I'd like an outside adjudication.

We maintain a model that scores loan applicants for default risk; anyone above a threshold gets routed to manual underwriting. A routine fairness audit found that among applicants who never actually defaulted, those in Group B were flagged for manual review at nearly twice the rate of those in Group A. Owen and Naomi, two people on the team, got into an argument about what this means.

(1) Owen said the flag-rate gap is itself the injustice: a model that makes non-defaulters in Group B jump through hoops far more often than equally non-defaulting people in Group A is treating them worse for no good reason, and we should retune the model — even using different thresholds per group — until that gap closes.

(2) Naomi said that's the wrong fix, because the model is calibrated: applicants who get, say, a 70% risk score default at essentially the same rate whether they're in Group A or Group B. Forcing equal flag rates among non-defaulters would mean giving Group B applicants a stricter effective threshold than Group A applicants with the identical predicted risk — deciding by group membership in all but name.

(3) Owen replied that calibration is exactly what you'd expect to see even if the model is disadvantaging Group B, provided Group B's base default rate in the historical data is higher than Group A's — and that gap in base rates might itself be an artifact of biased lending history rather than a real difference in creditworthiness, in which case pointing to calibration isn't a defense at all.

(4) Naomi answered that if the base rate is the contaminated part, the fix is to audit and correct the base rate, the labels, or the features, not to force equal flag rates downstream while leaving the contamination in place, because that move just relocates the unfairness. It makes the system treat a marginal, lower-risk Group A applicant and a marginal, higher-risk Group B applicant identically, which is unfair to whichever one of them is worse off, and it can't even be implemented without conditioning the decision on group membership.

Could you adjudicate this move by move?
