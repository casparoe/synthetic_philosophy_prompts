For the past few months I have been working through Pascal's Pensées on my own, in a secondhand paperback translation, and one passage has stayed with me more than anything else in the book: the one where Pascal writes that justice without force is powerless, that force without justice is tyrannical, and that since we have never managed to make what is just strong enough to prevail, we have instead made whatever is strong count as just. I mentioned this to a friend who took a couple of philosophy courses years ago, and she told me this is essentially Thrasymachus's position from Book I of Plato's Republic, restated: that justice has no content of its own beyond the advantage of whoever currently holds power, and that Pascal, like Hobbes after him, is really just conceding this once you strip away the theology. That explanation made sense to me at the time, and I have more or less accepted it since, because it seems to fit the tone of world-weary realism that runs through so much of the rest of the book.

What puzzles me now is that Pascal does not write like someone who has actually accepted that conclusion. He keeps referring to a "true justice" that we fail to reach, as though there really were a standard beyond whoever happens to be strong, and elsewhere he describes distinct "orders" - of the body, the mind, and charity - where he specifically uses the word tyranny for the case of someone trying to obtain, through force, something that properly belongs to a different order altogether, such as love or belief. If Pascal's real view is that justice simply is whatever force decides, then this talk of a "true" justice, and of tyranny as its own particular kind of mistake, both seem like things that should not be in the book at all, or at least like loose ends he never got around to tying up. I have no formal training in philosophy, so I do not know whether this is a recognized inconsistency in Pascal's thought, an artifact of translation, or something I am simply misunderstanding. Could you help me work out whether these two sides of the text are really in tension with each other, and if they are not, where exactly my reasoning has gone wrong?
