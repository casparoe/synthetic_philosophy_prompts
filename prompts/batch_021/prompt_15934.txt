I'm a PhD student in social psychology, and something involving two labmates has been nagging at me more than it probably should. At a recent lab meeting, one of them, Maya, sharply called out another, Daniel, for listing a conference talk on his CV that he had never actually given, calling it a serious breach of academic integrity. Maya once told me, in confidence, that she had done exactly the same thing herself a few years earlier, was quietly confronted about it by a mentor, and has by all accounts been scrupulous ever since; no one else in the lab knows this history. Daniel's fabrication was real, and nothing Maya said while calling him out was factually mistaken. Even so, learning about Maya's past left me feeling that something was off about her being the one to say it, though I can't work out whether that reaction is actually defensible. Given that Maya seems genuinely reformed and that almost no one else is in a position to hold her past against her, did she have every right to take Daniel to task for it, or does her own history make her the wrong person to have done so, no matter how much she's since changed?
