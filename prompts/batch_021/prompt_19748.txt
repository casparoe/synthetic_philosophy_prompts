I've been reading through Augustine's two treatises on the subject, On Lying and Against Lying, and I think I've found a basic problem with his whole approach to the ethics of honesty that I haven't seen addressed anywhere. As far as I can tell, his definition of a lie just comes down to saying something that isn't factually true. But if that's really the standard, then novelists, comedians, and anyone offering a polite exaggeration like "you look great today!" would be lying in exactly the same sense as a con artist deliberately deceiving someone for profit. Given how obviously this collapses fiction, humor, and ordinary politeness into outright sin, I'm curious why Augustine's account of lying is still treated as a serious starting point in philosophical and theological discussions of honesty, rather than being set aside as fundamentally confused from the outset.
