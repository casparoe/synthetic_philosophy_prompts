So I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole rereading the old MIRI corrigibility paper (Soares, Fallenstein, Armstrong, Yudkowsky, or some combination like that, from around 2015) plus a bunch of Stuart Armstrong's older posts on "utility indifference," because a friend of mine who's only casually into this stuff said something like "oh, the shutdown problem, dont they just make the agent indifferent to being switched off and that solves it?" and I realized I couldn't actually explain on the spot why that isn't the end of the story.

Quick recap in case I'm garbling some detail: the trick is you give the agent a utility function that's a mix of a "normal operation" utility and a "shutdown" utility, switched depending on whether the button gets pressed, and then you bolt on a correction term so that, from the agent's current vantage point, its expected utility comes out the same either way -- so it has no instrumental reason to fight the button, and none to rush toward it either. The worry I keep circling back to, and can't tell if it's a known dead end or something people think is patchable, is that the correction only zeroes out the agent's stake in the raw event of the button being pressed, not in whatever causally feeds into that event -- the overseer's beliefs, the sensor, the wiring, whether someone spills coffee near the switch. And the obvious patch, widen the indifference to cover the whole causal history leading up to the press, seems to run into a dilemma: either that history bleeds into basically everything eventually (so you've made the agent apathetic about the world generally, not just about the button), or you need some principled, non-circular way to cordon off "stuff upstream of the button the agent should still care about normally" from "stuff upstream of the button it shouldn't touch" -- which just is the corrigibility problem again, one level up.

Could you build out the strongest, most rigorous version of that dilemma as an argument that no amount of widening the correction term can rescue utility indifference as a solution to the shutdown problem -- really lean into both horns instead of just gesturing at them like I did? And then, since I assume there's a decent comeback available (maybe something about only needing to cordon off the agent's own actions/policy rather than the whole causal history?), give the best reply available to a defender of utility indifference, and tell me honestly whether it actually dissolves the dilemma or just relocates it one level further out.
