I recently began IVF treatment, and my clinic quoted a 40 percent per-cycle success rate, drawn from national outcome data for women in my age bracket. Trying to make sense of what that number should mean for me, I have been reading about David Lewis's Principal Principle in the philosophy of probability, which holds that one's credence in an outcome should defer to its known objective chance. My reproductive endocrinologist, however, pointed to several factors particular to my case, such as my ovarian reserve markers and how I responded to the stimulation protocol, that are not reflected in that headline 40 percent figure. Given the Principal Principle, am I not rationally obligated to treat 40 percent as my personal credence that this cycle will succeed, and to regard any adjustment based on my doctor's case-specific observations as an unprincipled departure from the objective chance?
