Random one from my Sidgwick seminar prep. Everyone remembers him for the utilitarian stuff in the Methods of Ethics, but way fewer people clock that he also co-founded (and was the first president of) the Society for Psychical Research, and spent the better part of two decades personally chasing down mediums, telepathy claims, apparitions, all of it -- and by most accounts never let himself declare victory even though he clearly wanted to. Can you explain the actual philosphical logic connecting this side pursuit to the dualism of practical reason stuff from the Methods -- i.e. why he thought duty and self-interest needed some further fact about the universe to fully cohere, and why evidence of that sort, rather than, say, just picking theism on faith, or dropping the benevolence axiom, was supposed to do the job? And relatedly, why do you reckon his famously brutal epistemic standards never bent given how badly he apparently needed a positive result?
