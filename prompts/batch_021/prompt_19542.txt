I'm a graduate student in philosophy of language, and a few of us run a small podcast aimed at an audience of fellow grad students and the occasional interested faculty member. Next week's guest has built her reputation defending the Russellian analysis of definite descriptions -- the claim that phrases like "the F" are not referring expressions but incomplete symbols that, in context, contribute a claim of existence and uniqueness to the proposition a sentence expresses -- against the objections from Strawson and Donnellan that have shaped most of the subsequent literature. I want to walk into the recording better prepared than I was two nights ago, when a friend who is writing his dissertation on Frege more or less talked me into a corner: his view is that once you take the sense/reference distinction seriously, Russell's quantificational machinery is unnecessary, and that a sentence like "the present King of France is bald" is better explained by the subject term's simply failing to secure a referent -- leaving the thought expressed without a truth value -- than by treating the whole sentence as false.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions for this guest that go beyond a restatement of the Russellian line? One should make her confront, without letting her settle immediately for the wide-scope/narrow-scope-under-negation reply, why that technical distinction should explain an intuition of truth-value gappiness that people report even for unnegated sentences. Another should press on Donnellan's referential/attributive distinction and specifically on misdescription cases -- someone picked out as "the man drinking a martini" who, it turns out, has water in his glass -- asking what entitles a Russellian to separate semantic reference from speaker's reference rather than simply stipulating that distinction to save the theory. A third should target incomplete descriptions like "the table," where the uniqueness clause is never literally satisfied, and ask whether patching this with a contextually supplied domain restriction is still compatible with calling the resulting view purely quantificational rather than covertly referential. For each of these three, please add a short, pointed follow-up question keyed to the answer she is most likely to give.
