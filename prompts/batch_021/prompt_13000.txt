So I'm drafting a scene in my new novel where two characters -- a semanticist and her old grad-school frenemy who went the pragmatics route -- run into each other at a conference bar and end up arguing about slurs, and I want to actually get the philosophy right before I write it. Can you give me their dialogue? She holds something like Hom's combinatorial externalism: the ugly stuff isn't connotation, its literally part of the word's truth-conditional content, so a slur asserts a whole racist ideology and comes out false (or gappy) whenever anyone uses it, because that ideology doesnt legitimately apply to anyone. He's a prohibitionist, more in the Anderson & Lepore vein -- same descriptive content as the neutral counterpart, and all the damage comes from a social taboo on the word-form itself, not from any special meaning. Get them to really fight over one specific case: someone snaps "he's not a kraut, he's just German, quit it" to shut down a bigot, and I want each of them to explain, from inside their own theory, why that sentence still feels dicey to say out loud even though it's meant kindly and (on one theory) literally just negates a falsehood. I want them to poke real holes in each other's explanations, not just restate their own view louder -- nine or ten exchanges, real back and forth, neither one should be the strawman here.
