I'm a PhD student in oral history working on migrant narratives, so a disagreement that broke out at a family reunion this summer landed closer to my own research than I expected. My family has a story, retold across four generations, that my great-great-grandfather Vittorio stowed away out of Genoa on a ship bound for New York, traveling under a false name to dodge conscription, with an uncle said to have bribed a sailor to hide him below decks. Nothing survives to confirm any of it -- no manifest, no letter -- just an unbroken line of people telling their children.

(1) My cousin Marco, a law student, said the story doesn't deserve much confidence: we're three or four tellers removed from anyone who was there, and even perfectly honest tellers introduce small distortions at each hand-off, so there's no real way to know how close we are to the truth. He called it hearsay stacked on hearsay.

(2) My aunt Rosa, who has spent a decade building out the family tree, answered that Marco's standard would disqualify nearly everything anyone knows about family history from before there was any paperwork, including things he doesn't doubt -- and that the story hasn't gone generic the way garbled stories usually do; the odd specifics (the false name, the bribed sailor, the amount he was said to have paid) have held steady in a way accidental corruption doesn't typically produce.

(3) Marco replied that the specifics holding steady shows nothing about independence: once the family settled on one version, every later telling just copies it rather than checking it, so consistency across retellings is exactly what you'd see whether or not the original story was true.

(4) Rosa asked what would ever satisfy him, then, given that he won't credit consistency and there's no document to be had -- and asked whether it would change his mind if our long-lost Argentine relatives, descended from a brother of Vittorio's who emigrated separately, turned out to tell the same story with the same odd details. Marco said that would count, since it would be a second, independent line rather than one lineage repeating itself -- but that isn't the situation we're actually in.

Neither of them budged. Could you go through the four moves and say who has the better of it at each one, rather than just declaring an overall winner, and flag anything important you think both of them are missing?
