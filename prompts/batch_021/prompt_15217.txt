So my best friend from my philosophy MA is getting married in three weeks and asked me to do the toast. Last night a few of us ended up in a two hour arguement about "hard choices" -- I was defending Ruth Chang's idea that some choices are hard not because we're ignorant of which option is better, and not because the options are secretly equal, but because there's a whole fourth relation, "parity," where neither option beats the other and yet they're still not equal, so nothing out there is forcing a verdict either way. Her fiancé (an engineer, zero patience for this stuff) kept insisting thats just a fancy label for "we don't have enough information," and two bottles of wine in, neither of us budged.

Anyway it's relevant because the bride turned down a fully-funded postdoc in Berlin to stay here and build a life with him, and every single person at this wedding is going to expect me to tell that story in the toast. I really don't want to tell it in a way that implies she quietly figured out he was the better option, because that's not how she's ever described it to me, and I think it would flatten the whole thing (and honestly embarrass both of them).

Could you write me the toast? Aiming for about five minutes, 600-800 words, warm and funny, not lecture-y. Roughly a third of the guests are people from our old cohort who will absolutely notice if I gesture at "parity" and will silently judge whether I got it right; the other two thirds are family and hometown friends who've never heard the word "incommensurable" in their life and will glaze over at anything that sounds like a seminar. The idea that the choice was genuinely hard -- not because she was indecisive, and not because the two options were secretly tied -- needs to actually shape the toast, so that by the end people get why picking him over Berlin wasn't her "settling," without me ever having to spell that out in so many words.
