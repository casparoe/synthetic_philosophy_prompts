So I'm writing a seminar paper on Benatar and think I've finally got a handle on why the antinatalist argument is supposed to work: life just contains way more suffering than we like to admit -- chronic low-grade stress, boredom, disease, the fear of death, etc -- so once you actually tally it up, never existing avoids a bad deal you never had to take. His whole "asymmetry" framing (absence of pain is good even with nobody around to enjoy that good; absence of pleasure isnt bad unless somebody's actually deprived of it) just seems like a fancier way of dressing up that same pessimistic accounting. Given that, what's the strongest existing rebuttal to the empirical premise that suffering outweighs happiness in a typical life, since that's really the load-bearing assumption antinatalists need for the argument to actually go through?
