So a friend and I got into a whole argument this weekend about the weirdest part of Turing's 1950 paper -- not the imitation game itself, but section 6, where he runs through objections to it. Everyone remembers the theological objection or the Lady Lovelace bit, but almost nobody talks about the fact that he takes the "argument from extrasensory perception" completely seriously: he basically says the statistical evidence for telepathy (citing that Rhine card-guessing research) is overwhelming, and then has to patch his own thought experiment so a telepathic human contestant couldn't just win by guessing face-down cards while the machine can't -- his fix being to stick everyone in some kind of telepathy-proof room. My friend's read was that Turing was just being cheeky, granting a premise he didnt believe to show the test survives it anyway; I think if you actually read the tone there he sounds like a guy who's genuinely a little spooked by the data, not just being a good sport about it. Neither of us could settle it and its been bugging me since.

So here's what I actually want from you, since we couldn't work it out ourselves: imagine Turing somehow got to see everything that's happened since, in both psychology and AI, before he had to answer for that section. The replication crisis basically gutted the kind of parapsychology evidence he leaned on (Rhine's work included, plus stuff like Bem's 2011 "precognition" studies that blew up and then couldn't be replicated), while on the other side, language models now pass loose versions of his own test constantly, with nothing psychic about it at all -- just fluent next-word prediction. Would he treat the collapse of the ESP evidence as an honest retraction, or would he insist he was reasoning fine given what was known at the time? And does the telepathy-proof room idea end up looking totally quaint to him now, or would he say the same instinct -- shielding the test from whatever the interrogator can exploit that has nothing to do with actual thinking -- was right all along, just aimed at the wrong threat back then? I want an actual argued answer here, not a "we can't really know what a dead guy would think" cop-out.
