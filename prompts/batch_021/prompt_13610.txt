Last weekend, a friend of mine who's finishing a postdoc in evolutionary biology and I spent an entire dinner arguing over whether Karl Popper was right to worry that natural selection isn't genuinely falsifiable, and neither of us budged. The question turns out to matter beyond the dinner table: I sit on the exhibits advisory committee of our city's natural history museum, and the curators have circulated final panel text for the new Origins gallery describing evolution by natural selection as "a proven fact, tested and confirmed beyond doubt, on the same footing as the theory of gravity." One committee member, a retired philosopher of science and no friend of creationism, has formally objected before the text goes to the fabricator: she cites Popper's own remark that natural selection is better understood as a "metaphysical research programme" than a strictly testable theory, and wants the panel reworded accordingly. The curators want her objection overruled outright, worried that any hedge will be quoted out of context by evolution-deniers for years to come. Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Friday's sign-off meeting: recommend specific replacement wording (or none), and engage her Popperian objection on its philosophical merits rather than dismissing it purely for its optics.
