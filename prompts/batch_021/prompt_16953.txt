Last week a friend of mine, who is finishing a doctorate in clinical psychology, and I ended up arguing for the better part of an evening about whether psychiatric categories describe a reality that exists independently of how people are classified, or whether classifying people in a given way can itself help bring about what it purports to describe. I raised Ian Hacking's notion of the "looping effect": his idea that once people are sorted into a psychiatric kind, their self-understanding and behavior change in ways that feed back into the classification itself, reshaping its criteria, its subtypes, or the population thought to fall under it, so that the kind and the people classified continually remake one another over time.

My friend was willing to grant that something like this happens with Hacking's own showcase case, multiple personality disorder (the subject of his book *Rewriting the Soul*), and perhaps with the more recent history of autism diagnosis, but she insisted these are outliers — unusually self-fulfilling classifications — and that most categories in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM) are comparatively stable, "indifferent" kinds that do not loop in any philosophically interesting way. I wasn't convinced, but I couldn't produce a clean example on the spot to prove her wrong.

So: give a detailed example of a psychiatric or closely adjacent psychological classification — other than multiple personality disorder/dissociative identity disorder and autism — that plausibly exhibits Hacking's looping effect. Reconstruct the loop concretely and in Hacking's own terms: explain what makes the kind "interactive" rather than "indifferent," describe how being classified changed how the people so classified understood, presented, or performed their condition, and trace how that change then fed back into revising the diagnostic category itself (its criteria, its subtypes, its presumed prevalence, or its presumed causes). Finally, since I suspect my friend would try to dismiss any example I bring back as "mere" historical fashion in diagnosis rather than genuine looping, briefly explain what would distinguish the two — that is, what makes a case count as Hacking-style looping as opposed to just a classification changing over time for ordinary sociological reasons.
