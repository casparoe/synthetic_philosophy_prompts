Okay so I've been chewing on the free will thing for months now, just me, a stack of library books, and way too many 3am wikipedia rabbit holes (never went to college so I'm doing this the slow way). I think I've got the basic map down: compatibilists say free will and determinism can both be true at the same time, incompatibilists say nope, can't have both. Which by my logic means the incompatibilists are basically betting everything on the universe having some genuine randomness in it somewhere, like quantum noise in the brain or whatever, since thats their only way of keeping free will alive if strict cause-and-effect turns out to go all the way down. So it seemed to follow that if physicists ever nailed down that the brain runs on fully deterministic rails with no meaningful randomness anywhere, the incompatibilists would basically have to throw in the towel and quietly become compatibilists, since determinism would just be a settled fact at that point and there'd be nothing left to argue.

But then I brought this up with a friend who took exactly one philosophy course in undergrad, and she said that's not really how it works -- apparently there's a whole group of incompatibilists (she called them "hard incompatibilists" or something like that) who don't think free will exists even if the universe turns out to be totally random and indeterministic, which just confused me even more. Like, if you don't even think indeterminism would save free will, why would determinism specifically be the enemy? Isn't it a contradiction to say free will is impossible no matter what the physics turns out to be, while still treating determinism as this special threat that defines your whole camp? I feel like I'm missing some pretty basic piece of the puzzle about what "incompatibilism" actually means as a label, so I was hoping you could help me figure out where exactly my reasoning goes off the rails.
