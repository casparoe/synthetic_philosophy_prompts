Teleosemantics says a mental state's content is fixed by the biological function it was selected for -- but that seems to have a weird upshot for Davidson's Swampman: a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a person that congeals by pure chance in a swamp, with zero evolutionary history, comes out with no thoughts and no meaningful words at all, just noises and behavior that happen to look like the real thing. And its not just the mental stuff -- none of Swampman's organs would have a "proper function" either on this view, so his heart couldn't even be said to malfunction if it stopped working. Write an essay on wether this is a genuine, fatal problem for teleosemantic (selected-effects) theories of content, or a bullet defenders like Millikan can happily bite. Weigh the strongest arguments on both sides, but actually land on a verdict instead of just saying "it's complicated."
