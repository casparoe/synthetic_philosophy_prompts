I think I've got the basic geometric picture down - Nash maximizes the product of the two side's utility gains over the disagreement point, KS instead picks the point on the Pareto frontier that sits on the straight line to the "ideal point" (the max each side could conceivably get). Nobody's actually walked me through a real defense of IIA though, so I wanna put the question to you directly: can you make the strongest case you can FOR sticking with independence of irrelevant alternatives, or at least against treating monotonicity as the more obviously rational requirement? I'm not looking for "well, Nash's solution is more popular in econ departments" as an answer - I want something that actually engages with why IIA might capture something genuinely important about rational bargaining that the standard KS counterexample doesn't really kill.
