My aunt Marion died on Sunday and I've been asked to give the eulogy at her memorial this weekend, which is freaking me out a bit. She was the one who got me into engineering as a kid, but for like 50 years her actual passion was being an active member of the Society for Psychical Research -- the real old British one, founded in the 1880s by a philosopher named Sidgwick, not some ouija-board club. She spent her life reading case files on hauntings and near-death stuff with this total "show me the evidence" attitude, and died just as unconvinced there's an afterlife as she was at 25. But she never once told me it was all nonsense either, and she admitted to me once, pretty quietly, that the hardest part of the whole thing was never getting one case solid enough to let her stop wondering. If I get up there and say "she's watching over us now," shed never forgive me. Problem is, some of her old research friends will be in the room and will absolutely notice if I overclaim, while her bridge club and my very Catholic uncle just need something to hold onto. Can you write me a eulogy that's actually honest to how she thought about this stuff, but still lands as comforting instead of like a lecture?
