I spent my career in gravitational physics, and in retirement I have been working through the philosophy of spacetime literature, where I keep running into the same sticking point. Whenever I raised Earman and Norton's hole argument with former colleagues, their reflexive response was that diffeomorphism invariance is "just gauge freedom," so there is nothing physically or metaphysically significant about being able to construct two models that agree everywhere outside some small region but disagree within it. Yet philosophers treat this construction as generating genuine indeterminism for anyone who holds that spacetime points are substantival individuals whose identity does not depend on the metric field defined over them. Could you explain, precisely, why the "mere gauge" response fails to dissolve the argument once substantivalism is assumed, and how "sophisticated substantivalist" replies try to block the resulting indeterminism while still remaining substantivalist rather than collapsing into relationalism?
