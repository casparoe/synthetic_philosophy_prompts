In discussions of embedded agency, a recurring theme is the contrast between a "Cartesian" agent -- one that can be modeled as standing outside its environment and evaluating hypothetical actions from a neutral vantage point through an idealized input/output channel -- and an agent that is embedded within, and no larger than, the very environment it must act in and reason about. Give a specific example of a decision problem in which this embeddedness creates a genuine difficulty for standard expected-utility reasoning that would not arise for a Cartesian agent, and explain exactly why the trouble depends on treating the agent's own reasoning process as an object within the environment it is reasoning about. Finally, say whether, in your view, this difficulty is merely a solvable technical wrinkle or reflects something deeper about the limits of formal rationality.
