For my IB Extended Essay in computer science, I have been reading about the Church-Turing thesis. As I understand it, several different formal systems for capturing the intuitive notion of an algorithm were proposed independently in the 1930s by Church, Turing, Post, and Kleene—lambda calculus, Turing machines, general recursive functions, and so on—and these were all rigorously proven to be mathematically equivalent to one another. Given that this equivalence is a settled mathematical result, doesn't it follow that the Church-Turing thesis itself is thereby established as a theorem rather than remaining an open philosophical or empirical question, and that it is simply a mathematical fact that no physical process, however exotic, could ever compute a function outside this class? Several of my sources still describe the thesis's status as unsettled, or as something no proof could ever fully establish, and I'm having trouble seeing what would be left open once the equivalence results are already in hand.
