While reading about AI alignment theory on my own, I came across the concept of "embedded agency," developed by researchers at MIRI. Unlike an idealized agent such as AIXI, which is modeled as sitting cleanly outside the environment it computes about, any real agent is physically embedded in the very world it has to reason about, and so can never hold a fully accurate model of that world — including itself — inside itself. One particular consequence of this has stuck with me: the problem of "robust delegation." Owing to an obstacle connected to Löb's theorem in mathematical logic, an agent generally cannot prove in advance that a successor at least as capable as itself — whether a future version of itself or something it builds — will keep pursuing its current goals; forcing such a proof runs into genuine logical trouble, not merely practical uncertainty. Could you write me a short story, about a human or an artificial mind, in which a character must hand off a mission, an institution, or their own life's purpose to a successor they cannot fully verify in advance, and in which this precise difficulty, rather than some vaguer notion of "trust," is what the plot turns on?
