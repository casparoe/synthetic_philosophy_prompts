Ok so I went down a rabbit hole this week that I need help getting to the bottom of. It started because a friend and I were arguing about why people do these absurdly over-the-top wedding proposals (flash mobs, hiring a plane to write in the sky, whatever), and I made the classic Hansonian move of saying the expense IS the point — if it were cheap it wouldn't prove anything about how much he's willing to sacrifice. That sent me back to Robin Hanson's old Overcoming Bias posts and the Elephant in the Brain book, and then further back into the actual evolutionary biology behind the "handicap principle" — Zahavi's original idea, the fact that Maynard Smith was apparently pretty skeptical for a while that costly honest signalling could even be an evolutionarily stable strategy, and Grafen eventually rescuing it with an actual formal model.

Here's the bit that's bugging me. The popular version of the story (the one Hanson uses, and most pop-science writeups) says the signal has to be a genuine waste — fitness or resources thrown away — and that the waste itself is what makes it credible, since only a high-quality signaler could afford to torch that much value. But when I poke at the more technical stuff, it looks like Grafen's actual condition is just that the marginal cost of signalling falls of faster with quality for good signalers than bad ones (a "differential cost" thing, which as far as I can tell is basically the same underlying shape as Spence's job-market-signalling model in econ) — and that doesn't obviously require the signal to be wasteful in any absolute sense. There's also apparently a whole separate bucket of "index" signals that are honest without being costly at all, just because they physically can't be faked. If I'm getting this right, a lot of the "waste is the point, that's what makes it work" framing might be pop-science overreach relative to what the theory actually requires, even if the broader signalling logic survives. But I'm not confident I'm getting this right, which is exactly why I want to see it fought out rather than just asserted by me.
