I've been a lurker on the EA Forum and LessWrong for the better part of a decade, and I now find myself facing a problem no sequence ever prepared me for: I'm giving the toast at the wedding of two close friends from my old population-ethics reading group, and their entire relationship has effectively been organized around one unresolved disagreement. They met arguing about Parfit's Repugnant Conclusion, and eight years later neither has budged an inch. He is a committed total-view bullet-biter who will tell you, cheerfully and at length, that Z — the vast population whose lives are barely worth living — really is better than A, our small and wonderful world, and that anyone who feels otherwise is simply exhibiting scope insensitivity toward very large numbers. She has spent roughly the same eight years trying to build a critical-level view precise enough to block that conclusion without sliding into Arrhenius's Sadistic Conclusion instead, and regards his position as not merely mistaken but faintly obscene. Both of them have separately asked me to "settle it" during the toast, which I have no intention of doing, since I suspect the disagreement is one of the load-bearing walls of their marriage and declaring a winner would be a small betrayal of both of them.

The complication is the guest list: roughly half the room is philosophers and EA Forum regulars who will catch every allusion, and the other half is family who have never heard of Parfit and would be quietly alarmed if I explained the Repugnant Conclusion to them in so many words. What I need is a three-to-four-minute toast that uses their running argument as its actual spine rather than as a joke grafted onto an otherwise ordinary toast — something that rewards the people who recognize "mere addition" and "the critical level" while still landing as a warm, coherent story for the people who don't, that refuses to adjudicate who is actually right about population axiology, and that never once implies anything callous about actual children, given that the two of them have been trying for a while now and the last thing I want is for anyone in that room to draw the connection themselves. Could you write it for me?
