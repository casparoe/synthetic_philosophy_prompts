Two years ago I discovered that a bestselling novel bore what still strikes me as an unmistakable resemblance to a manuscript of mine — three years' work, then unpublished — that had circulated among a small circle of literary agents shortly before the other book's publication. I brought a copyright claim over what was, in financial terms, a fairly modest sum, yet the legal fees on both sides have long since dwarfed that sum several times over, and neither my lawyer nor, from what I can gather, my opponent's, shows any inclination to settle. Each time I have seriously considered dropping the case, I've been stopped by the thought that doing so would make everything already spent count for nothing, and I have little doubt that my opponent reasons the same way about her own costs. Is there a rational point at which one of us should simply stop, or does the very logic that has kept us going make that question unanswerable from where we now stand?
