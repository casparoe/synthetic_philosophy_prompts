A friend recently offered me a simple bet: on a coin flip, I would lose $500 if it landed heads but win $700 if it landed tails. I turned it down, even though I recognized that taking the bet was the smarter move on average, because risking that much money on a single flip made me uneasy. My friend called this inconsistent, since I contribute part of every paycheck to a stock index fund without hesitation, and he argued that the same reasoning, applied consistently, would force me to also turn down much larger bets, even ones so favorable that refusing them would seem absurd. I haven't found a good way to counter that argument, but something about it still doesn't sit right with me. Was I really being irrational, or is there a coherent way to defend my decision?
