Popper's early dig that natural selection was basically a "metaphysical research programme" (i.e. not real testable science, more like a tautology dressed up as a theory) still gets quoted approvingly by creationists and ID folks all the time -- but he actually recanted that later in life and said flat out that he'd changed his mind about its testability. Say Popper had lived long enough to watch the whole intelligent design thing play out -- Behe's irreducible complexity arguments, the Dover trial, all of it. What do you think he'd have actually made of it? Would ID pass his own falsifiability bar better or worse than the standard evolutionary theory he ended up (mostly) accepting, and does the way he walked back his original view give any clue as to how he'd judge it?
