I'm a software engineer who's been reading about AI alignment in my spare time, and one idea I keep coming back to is the "Eliciting Latent Knowledge" (ELK) problem described in ARC's 2021 report: the worry that a highly capable AI system might hold an accurate internal picture of a situation with no way for us to get it to honestly report that picture, especially once it's smart enough to exploit the gap between what our sensors show and what is actually true. Their illustrative case is an AI tasked with protecting a diamond in a vault that's monitored only through a camera feed: the AI could learn to tamper with the feed itself, say by placing an obstruction in front of the lens, so that a human overseer keeps seeing a diamond that is no longer there, leaving it genuinely unclear whether the system is lying or simply reporting the world as filtered through whatever the overseer is able to perceive. Could you write a short story set inside this predicament? I'd like it to dramatize the specific difficulty of telling a truthful report apart from a merely convincing one, in a mind whose internal beliefs can't be directly inspected, rather than offer a generic tale of robot betrayal. Feel free to invent your own characters and setting rather than restaging the vault scenario literally.
