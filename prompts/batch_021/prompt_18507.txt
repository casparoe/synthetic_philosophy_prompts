I'm a philosophy major in a seminar on political obligation and civil disobedience, and this term I'm also the student representative on my university's Community Standards Board, which sets sanctions for conduct-code violations. Last month a group of students occupied the provost's office for more than a day to demand fossil-fuel divestment: the action was announced in advance, the demands were made public, and most participants gave their names and left peacefully once campus police arrived. A handful of participants, however, wore masks, refused to identify themselves, and had to be carried out; the Board has since learned informally that at least two of them are international students for whom a formal conduct record could jeopardize their visa status. Our code lets the Board "impose reduced sanctions where the violation constitutes an act of civil disobedience," but does not define the term, and the Board is split: some members think refusing to identify yourself and accept the consequences disqualifies an act from counting as civil disobedience at all, while others think that requirement just rewards the students with the least to lose. Please draft the memo I'll bring to Monday's meeting. It should offer a working definition of civil disobedience precise enough to decide this case, recommend sanctions for the identified and unidentified participants that need not be identical, and take the strongest version of the losing side's argument seriously rather than simply dismiss it.
