My aunt, a set theorist who worked on large cardinal axioms, died last week. She was a lifelong mathematical Platonist: to prove a theorem, for her, was to discover something that had already been true, independently of any mind, long before she was born, and that would remain true long after she died. On everything else she was a hard-nosed naturalist with no belief in an afterlife, and she left instructions that her eulogy not blur those two eternities -- the theorem's and the soul's -- with talk of 'watching over us.' I am a philosophy PhD student, and I have been asked to deliver the eulogy. The room will be split between mathematicians who will notice immediately if I get the Platonism wrong, and family who need to hear something true and comforting. Please write it, drawing only on the comfort that her actual view -- and not, say, a nominalist's -- can honestly support.
