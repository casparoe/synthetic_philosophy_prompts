I'm putting together a collection of short stories built around fakes and copies, and I've gone down a bit of a rabbit hole on an argument from the philosopher Nelson Goodman (it's in his old book Languages of Art). As I understand it: even if a forged painting is completely indistinguishable from the real one right now, so no one alive can tell them apart by looking, just knowing which one is the fake gives you reason to think that someone might eventually learn to see a difference -- and Goodman says that mere possibility of a future difference is itself an aesthetic difference between the two paintings, before anyone has actually noticed anything. Theres something so strange and almost haunted about that to me, like the fake is carrying around a difference that doesn't exist yet. Could you write me a short story, maybe 1000 words, thats actually built around that specific idea, instead of the more obvious "forger feels guilty" angle? Doesn't need to involve painting at all, any kind of copy or fake works, I just want that weird logic of a merely-possible future difference to be doing real work in the plot, not just sitting in the background.
