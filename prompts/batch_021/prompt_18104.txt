The CoC says it applies to "official project spaces: our GitHub repo, our Discord server, our mailing list, and in-person meetups" -- and for what it's worth, the CoC's list of bad behaviors uses "including but not limited to" before its examples, but that jurisdiction sentence doesn't.

Here's the mess: one maintainer sent another some genuinely nasty personal insults over Discord DM's, growing out of a fight over a rewrite PR. The recipient filed a complaint. The sender's defense: a DM isn't one of the four listed spaces, it's just two people talking privately, and if we claim jurisdiction over that there's no limiting principle -- we'd basically be regulating anything maintainers say to each other, forever. Half the committee wants to dismiss on those grounds; the other half thinks that guts the whole point of having a CoC, since the fight only happened because of shared project access and all the worst behavior will obviously just move to DMs. Someone on the thread called this "basically the textualism vs purposivism fight," which, sure, i have zero background in.
