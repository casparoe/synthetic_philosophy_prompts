Been rereading some Popper and can't shake this question. His whole epistemology is anti-inductivist -- knowledge doesn't come from generalizing out of observations, it comes from bold conjectures getting winnowed by criticism, a process he explicitly modeled on natural selection, plus his "World 3" idea, where objective knowledge exists independent of any knower, sort of like a book sitting in a library whether or not anyone's opened it. He took the Darwinism analogy seriously enough that he'd earlier worried the theory was basically tautologous and called it "a metaphysical research programme" rather than a testable science, before recanting in a 1978 piece (iirc called "Natural Selection and the Emergence of Mind") once he'd worked out how it could actually be tested.

So: what would Popper say about LLM's? On the surface they're induction machines par excellence, just extracting statistical regularities from oceans of text -- exactly the picture of knowledge-growth he spent his career arguing was a myth. Is that a straightforward refutation of his epistemology, or does he have a move available, like relocating the real conjecture-and-refutation action to the researchers iterating on architectures and training runs, and downgrading the trained model itself to a World 3 object rather than a knowing subject?
