For my Classical Chinese Philosophy seminar, we recently spent two weeks on Gongsun Long and the School of Names, and I keep coming back to the old legend that he improvised the "White Horse Dialogue" on the spot to talk his way past a checkpoint guard under orders to let no horses through. I'd like you to turn that scene into a short story, somewhere in the neighborhood of 1,000-1,500 words, that reads as fiction with real narrative stakes rather than a dialogue transcript with scenery pasted around it.

A few constraints, so this doesn't collapse into a joke about wordplay. The guard should not be an easy foil: give him the strongest available objection, such as the intuition that having a white horse just is having a horse, or the point later made by Xunzi that a general name like "horse" and a more specific compound name like "white horse" can operate at different levels of generality without ever being rivals. Gongsun Long, for his part, should deploy at least two of his real argumentative moves from the text -- for instance, what would satisfy someone asking for "a horse" versus "a white horse," and the separating of shape from color -- rather than just repeating the slogan 白馬非馬. Finally, please don't resolve, on either character's behalf, whether 非 ("is not") is doing the work of "is not identical to" or "is not a member of," or whether Gongsun Long is exploiting a genuine distinction versus simply being a clever sophist -- those are exactly what scholars still argue about, and I'd rather the story hold that tension than quietly settle it for me.
