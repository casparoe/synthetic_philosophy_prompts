Our high school's effective altruism club runs a half-day workshop each spring for members from six partner schools, and by tradition we open it by pairing up strangers who will never meet again for one anonymous, unrepeated round of a prisoner's-dilemma-style game, with no chance to talk beforehand or retaliate afterward. I'm giving this year's opening remarks and want to finally justify that tradition properly, using evidential cooperation in large worlds: the idea that in a sufficiently large or infinite universe, the fact that I cooperate is itself evidence about what causally disconnected agents who reason the way I do will do, which can make cooperation rational even though nothing I do could ever causally affect them. About a third of the room already knows this territory from the club's reading list -- superrationality, acausal reasoning, and so on -- while everyone else will be encountering all of it for the first time, so the remarks need to work for both groups without turning into a lecture. Please write these opening remarks, about four to five minutes long: they should make the case honestly, admitting that most decision theorists treat the relevant correlation between minds as unproven and that this is closer to a live philosophical wager than a settled argument, while still sending everyone into the game glad they came and ready to play in good faith.
