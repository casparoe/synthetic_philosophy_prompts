I'm taking a seminar on formal epistemology this semester, and for my term paper I want to press on a tension between van Fraassen's Reflection Principle and the thirder position (associated with Adam Elga) on the Sleeping Beauty problem. Before I commit to this as my thesis, I'd like your comments on whether the argument below actually works.

Reflection says, roughly, that for any t1 < t2, my credence at t1 in some proposition A, conditional on the proposition that my credence in A at t2 will be x, should itself equal x -- provided, as van Fraassen requires, that I have no reason to expect that future credence to result from something other than ordinary rational updating, such as memory loss or manipulation. The usual motivation is a diachronic Dutch Book: an agent who violates Reflection can be offered a combination of bets now and at t2 that guarantees a loss no matter how things turn out.

Now consider a Beauty who is a committed thirder. She is certain in advance that, however the coin lands, her credence in Heads will be 1/3 at every waking. So on Sunday she is already certain that her credence upon waking will be 1/3. Feed that into Reflection: her Sunday credence in Heads, conditional on that certainty, should be 1/3 -- but since she's certain the condition holds, that conditional credence just is her unconditional Sunday credence. Reflection therefore seems to force a Sunday credence of 1/3, not the 1/2 that thirders themselves want to assign her on Sunday, before she's learned anything.

The obvious rescue is that waking delivers new evidence ("I am now awake"), so the shift from 1/2 to 1/3 is ordinary updating rather than a Reflection violation. But that rescue presupposes that being-awake is genuine evidence -- exactly what halfers like Lewis deny, since Beauty was already certain on Sunday that she would wake up at least once regardless of the outcome.

Does this actually show thirdism is inconsistent with Reflection, or is there a gap in the argument I'm not seeing? Is the trouble specific to thirders, or can a parallel argument be run against halfers? And if the argument does succeed, is the right diagnosis to reject Reflection, reject thirdism, or deny that Reflection was ever meant to apply to self-locating credences like this one?
