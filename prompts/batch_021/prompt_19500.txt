So I'm putting together a coursepack dialogue for my seminar on hinge propositions and could use a draft -- write me a dialogue between two characters who both take Wittgenstein's On Certainty as their starting point but end up in pretty different places.

One is a quasi-fideist, roughly in Duncan Pritchard's sense: hinge commitments like "there is an external world" or "my memory is basically reliable" are arational -- not known, not evidentially justified, not really inside the space of reasons, just the groundless stuff that has to be in place before ordinary reason-giving can get started at all. The other is an entitlement theorist, in Crispin Wright's mold: hinges aren't evidentially grounded either, but we're still rationally entitled to them, so they're inside the space of reasons after all, just warranted non-evidentially.

Get them arguing about this: what does each view say about someone whose hinges are just different from ours -- a sincere flat-earther, say, or a moon-landing denier? If hinges are merely arational reactions, on what basis do we get to say we're doing better than they are, rather than just reacting differently? And if hinges come with rational entitlement, doesn't Wright owe us a story about why we're entitled to ours and they're not to theirs -- one that doesn't just smuggle back in the evidential justification entitlement was suppose to let us avoid?

Both characters should be genuinely sharp -- no strawmanning -- and I want real back and forth, each pressing the other on the objection above and actually responding, not just restating their own view. It doesn't have to resolve neatly, but each should have to concede some real cost of their own position by the end.
