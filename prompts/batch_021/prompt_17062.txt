A friend from my old grad school reading group -- she went into philosophy of physics, I ended up teaching ethics -- died a few weeks ago out of nowhere, a brain aneurysm. She was a committed Everettian who loved bringing up Tegmark's quantum suicide setup at parties, half-joking, to argue that from the inside you never actually experience the branch where you died, so in some sense shes still around somewhere. She left a note for whoever spoke at the memorial: don't make her a ghost story, but don't let some smart-ass colleague smugly debunk it either -- just get the reasoning right. That's me, no idea why they didn't ask one of her actual physicist friends, but here we are. Half the room is her physics people, who'll absolutely notice if I botch the measure/anthropic stuff; the other half is her mom and old college friends who's never even heard of Everett and just want to grieve. Write the eulogy -- it has to be honest that on her own view, nobody left in this room is obviously getting any comfort out of any of this, while still landing as a eulogy and not a colloquium talk.
