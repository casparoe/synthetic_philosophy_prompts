ok so I've fallen down a rabbit hole on intuitionistic logic / mathematical constructivism lately -- the whole "a statement doesn't get to count as true or false until someone actually has a construction/proof for it" thing, where excluded middle isn't just automatically true for every P the way it is in classical logic.

it's messing with how I think about "logical uncertainty," which is a thing I picked up from years of lurking on lesswrong -- the idea (which MIRI's old logical induction paper tries to formalize) that a reasoner should be able to put well-calibrated credences on open conjectures, like whether the riemann hypothesis is true, even before anyone's proved them one way or the other.

but if the intuitionists are right that an unproven statement isn't secretly true-or-false-already-we-just-dont-know-which, but genuinely has no determinate truth value until its constructed, that seems like it should mess with the whole "assign a probability that riemann is true" project pretty badly -- like, a probability of *what*, exactly, if theres no fact of the matter yet for your credence to be tracking?

so: what are the most important implications of taking constructivism seriously here for how someone into logical uncertainty should think about putting credences on open math conjectures? does it actually break the project, does it just mean we need to be more careful about what the credence is supposed to represent, or does something else give way instead? not looking for a primer on what intuitionism is, I already know the basics -- more interested in where it actually bites.
