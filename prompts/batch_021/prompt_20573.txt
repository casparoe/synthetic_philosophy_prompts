My identical twin daughters, Nora and Ellie, are doing a joint wedding next month — one ceremony, two couples — and I'm the one giving the single toast for both of them. I only got into philosophy after I retired — forty years as an x-ray crystallographer, so symmetry was always my day job, just never the metaphysics of it — and last year I read Max Black's old paper where the whole universe is just two spheres, exactly alike in every quality and even in how they relate to each other, and yet still stubbornly two things and not one. I want to build the toast around that argument, seriously, because "indistinguishable in every describable way, and still two" is exactly what people have been implying about my girls their whole lives, usually not as a compliment. Two rules: no jokes about mixing up their names or finally telling them apart, they've both banned that in writing; and Nora's fiancé is finishing a metaphysics PhD with a chapter on this exact paper, so I can't just wave at the spheres — I actually have to get the identity-of-indiscernibles stuff right or he'll definitly notice. Can you write me a four-minute toast that works for him and for my sister's bridge club two tables over?
