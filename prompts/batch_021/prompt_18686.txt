I'm a student member of my university's academic integrity board, and a seminar I'm taking this term on free will and moral responsibility has complicated how I think about an upcoming case. The facts aren't disputed: on a problem set marked strictly individual work, a student texted a friend for two answers and copied them in. Investigation records show the friend had already decided to send the answers unprompted at 9pm if she hadn't asked by then; she asked at 8:40, so his plan never triggered. Some board members call this irrelevant, since she sought the answers out on her own. Others think it matters: if she was going to end up with those answers regardless of her choice, judging her as harshly as a student for whom that wasn't true seems unfair. Our guidelines move cases between three sanction tiers -- warning, probation, and suspension -- based on "the student's degree of culpability," but say nothing about how to weigh a fact like this. Could you draft the short memo I'll circulate recommending a tier, and engage with the mitigation argument on its merits rather than waving it away?
