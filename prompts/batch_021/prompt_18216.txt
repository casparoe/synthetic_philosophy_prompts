In my seminar on decision theory, we have spent the last few sessions on acausal trade: the idea that two agents who cannot causally influence one another in any way might nonetheless strike a genuine bargain, each doing what the other wants because it has modeled the other's reasoning accurately enough to predict that it will respond in kind. I would like a short story I can use as a discussion prompt in class, one that gives this idea some dramatic substance rather than leaving it as a bare decision-theoretic puzzle. Please write a piece of roughly 900–1200 words featuring two agents — not necessarily human, and never permitted to meet, communicate, or share a timeline — who nonetheless coordinate through this kind of reasoning alone. The plot should turn on a real temptation for one party to defect once it is certain the other cannot detect or retaliate against the betrayal, and it should dramatize why the agent honors its side of the bargain regardless. I would rather you not reach for Roko's Basilisk, telepathy, or backward causation as your mechanism: the cooperation should rest entirely on each agent's model of the other's decision procedure, in the spirit of superrationality and logical/functional decision theory.
