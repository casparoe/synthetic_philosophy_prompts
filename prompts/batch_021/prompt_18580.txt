I'm doing my PhD in linguistics (semantics/pragmatics side of things — nothing to do with epistemology), so this isn't really my area, but I can't stop poking at it, so bear with me.

My friend Priya and I both run small home-baking side hustles, and a while back we each bought the exact same cheap oven thermometer off the same website. Last week she was throwing together cupcakes for her kid's birthday party, glanced at the thermometer, saw it read 350, and texted me "yep I know its preheated, cupcakes are in!" without a second thought. Two days later I was making a three-tier wedding cake for a client paying me $600 for it, glanced at the exact same model of thermometer, saw the exact same 350 reading — and I just froze. It didnt feel like I actually knew the oven was up to temperature anymore, so I ran out and bought a second thermometer to double-check before putting a very expensive batch of batter into the oven. When I told Priya about this later (we were actually texting about something totally unrelated, a trip we're planning) she said I was being irrational, since literally nothing about the evidence had changed — same reading, same device, same everything. But it honestly didn't feel irrational in the moment, it felt like the stakes had actually changed what it took for me to count as "knowing" the oven was ready. So who's actually right here, is there something genuinely rational going on, or was I just being neurotic, and either way, what does this whole thing say about what "knowing" something requires in the first place?
