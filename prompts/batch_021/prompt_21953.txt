I'm on the selection committee for an early-career research prize open to "anyone within about five years of finishing their PhD, with reasonable allowances for career breaks." Our applicant is 5 years 8 months post-PhD but took roughly a year of parental and medical leave in that stretch, so depending how you count the "effective" clock she's either just inside the window or just outside it. Half the committee wants to pin down her real effective time-since-PhD more precisely, like there's a fact buried in there if we do the leave-adjusted math right; the other half thinks "effective years since PhD" was never precise enough to have a determinate answer in a case like this, and we should just decide whats fair and set a workable precedent instead of pretending to discover something. Draft the committee's decision memo -- eligible, not eligible, or refer it to the funder for a policy fix -- and make it actually engage the loosing side instead of just splitting the difference.
