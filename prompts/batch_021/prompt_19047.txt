This term I'm taking a seminar on theories of practical reason, which is perhaps why a recent disagreement with a friend has stayed with me more than it otherwise might have. He borrowed a substantial sum of money from me last year and promised to pay it back within a few months, but when I raised the issue again recently, he told me plainly that he no longer cares about the promise or about our friendship, so as far as he's concerned none of the usual reasons to repay a debt actually apply to him. His reasoning was that a consideration only counts as someone's reason for acting if it connects to something that person actually cares about, and by that standard, he insists, he has none. My instinct is that he still has a reason to repay me regardless of how he currently feels, simply because he promised, but I haven't been able to work out why that intuition should win out over his. Who has the more defensible position, and why?
