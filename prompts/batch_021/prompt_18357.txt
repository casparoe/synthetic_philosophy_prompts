Forty years staring down a microscope and now, in retirement, I've gone and started reading philosophy of mind. Currently I'm stuck on Karl Friston's free energy principle. What bugs me is this: the principle itself -- that any self-organizing system with a "Markov blanket", right down to a single cell, is just minimizing free energy -- gets accused of being basically unfalsifiable, since you can redescribe almost any persisting system that way after the fact. But predictive coding, which is supposedly just the neural "process theory" version of the same idea, makes pretty specific, checkable claims: seperate neuron populations for predictions vs prediction errors, precision handled through neural gain, mismatch negativity as a signature of error signals, and so on. So which is it -- is the grand principle doing explanatory work that the neuroscience wouldn't already give you on its own, or is it dressing up a bunch of independently testable process theories in langauge borrowed from statistical mechanics so the whole package sounds more unified than it really is? Give me an honest, in-the-weeds walkthrough of how Friston and his defenders answer the unfalsifiability charge -- not just a restatement of the neuroscience, which I already buy.
