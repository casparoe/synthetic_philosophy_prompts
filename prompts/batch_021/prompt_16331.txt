I spent my career as an experimental physicist, and now that I'm retired I've gotten a bit obsessed with philosophy of physics on the side. Lately I keep chewing on Newton's two-globes thought experiment from the Scholium -- the less famous cousin of his rotating bucket -- where two globes tied together by a cord are spinning in an otherwise completely empty universe, and he claims you can tell they're truly (absolutely) rotating just from the tension in the cord, with no other bodies around to even compare them to. It's basically Newton's cleanest shot against relationism, and Mach's reply to it never fully satisfied me. Could you write me a short story built around this setup? I don't want a dry allegory dressed up as fiction -- give it real characters and stakes -- but I do want whoever's at the center of it to actually grapple with wether that cord tension could still be explained relationally somehow, or whether it really does force you toward substantivalism about space. Bonus points if the ending doesn't let the metaphysics off the hook too easy.
