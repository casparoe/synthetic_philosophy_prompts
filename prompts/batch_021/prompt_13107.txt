Been down a rabbit hole on the philosophy of conspiracy theories lately, and there's a real fault line between "generalists" (Keeley's original 1999 paper, and more recently Cassam) who think conspiracy theories deserve extra suspicion as a category because of stuff like their self-sealing quality -- any contrary evidence just gets absorbed as more proof of the cover-up -- and "particularists" (Pigden, Dentith, Basham) who say thats basically prejudice dressed up as epistemology, since real conspiracies do happen (Watergate!) and every theory has to be judged on its own evidence. Can you write me a dialogue between a generalist and a particularist stuck next to each other at a conference bar, really hashing it out? Want them engaging each others actual best arguments instead of talking past one another -- the generalist needs a real answer to the Watergate problem, and the particularist needs a real answer to "so flat-earthers deserve a fair hearing too?" Neither should be a pushover, both need to be genuinely sharp.
