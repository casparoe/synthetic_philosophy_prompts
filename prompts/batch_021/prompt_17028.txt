So my roommate is doing a linguistics PhD and she absolutely nerd-sniped me last week with this pair of sentences: "If Oswald didn't kill Kennedy, someone else did" versus "If Oswald hadn't killed Kennedy, someone else would have." The first one sounds obviously true (somebody shot the guy, so if it wasn't Oswald it was whoever else did it), and the second one sounds obviously false (there's no reason to think some backup assassin was waiting in the wings). Same basic words, you just swap "didn't/did" for "hadn't/would have," and the truth value flips completely. Apparently this is a whole subfield of philosophy of language — the difference between "indicative" conditionals (if A is true, B is true) and "counterfactual" or subjunctive ones (if A had been true, B would've been true) — and there's a real disagreement about whether they're secretly the same logical thing wearing different grammar, or two genuinely different beasts that need two seperate theories.

As a programmer this bugs me, because an "if" in code is just one dumb boolean check that doesn't know or care about tense or mood, so it sounds like natural-language "if" is quietly doing something way more complicated than any if-statement I've ever written. My roommate mentioned some guy named Adams who apparently thinks indicative conditionals track conditional probability rather than just being true or false outright, and something about Lewis and Stalnaker doing "possible worlds" semantics for the counterfactual ones, plus a "triviality result" that's supposed to prove you can't just mash both cases into one unified truth-conditional theory without the whole thing collapsing. I only half followed any of this, honestly.

Actually walk through the strongest arguments on each side — not just a list of famous names, explain why each camp believes what it believes — and then commit to a real answer at the end, the way a senior engineer would actually make a design decision instead of writing "it depends" in a doc and calling it done. If the honest answer really is "it depends," that's fine, but then tell me exactly what it depends on.
