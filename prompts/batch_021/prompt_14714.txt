There's this idea from economics called hyperbolic discounting - basically that our preferences don't just fade evenly the further away something is, they actually flip depending on how close the reward is. Like I'll happily commit to going to the gym next monday, but the second monday actually shows up I suddenly "prefer" skipping it. People use this to explain akrasia (weakness of will), that old philosophical puzzle about doing something you yourself think is worse.

But here's what bugs me: if my preferences really do just flip like that, and I'm always acting on whatever I prefer most in the moment, isnt that just me being rational at every point in time, given weird/inconsistent preferences? There's no real battle between reason and desire, just two different valuations at two different times. That seems to dissolve weakness of will as an actual phenomenon - its just relabeled preference change. Except it still feels like betrayal in the moment, not like I changed my mind about anything.
