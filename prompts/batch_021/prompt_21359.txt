Quick one — I've been down a rabbit hole on Newton's secret theology and want you to make an argument I've only seen gestured at, never spelled out properly. Newton was privately antitrinitarian (basically Arian), and his real theological hangup wasn't the standard "necessary being" stuff but God's dominion — he's obsessed with God as sovereign Lord rather then remote first cause, which is all over the General Scholium and resurfaces when he calls space the "sensorium" of God in the Opticks. Argue that this specific heresy is actually load-bearing for his doctrine of absolute space and time, not just something that happens to sit next to the physics — i.e., that a fully orthodox Trinitarian Newton couldn't have landed on the same metaphysics of space. Use Leibniz's complaints in the Clarke correspondence as your foil, and don't let yourself fall back on the boring "Newton was religious and also did science" point.
