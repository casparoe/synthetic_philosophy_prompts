I'm working on a feature about the rapid growth of industrial insect farming for animal feed and novel foods, which has sharpened scientific and philosophical debate over insect sentience — especially now that several jurisdictions have extended legal recognition of sentience to cephalopods and decapod crustaceans while leaving insects in a far greyer zone. For a section unpacking the underlying theory-of-mind question, could you describe the most important implications of higher-order theories of consciousness for whether insects are plausible candidates for phenomenal consciousness? I'm especially interested in how the verdict shifts depending on which variant of the theory one adopts — Rosenthal's requirement that an actual, occurrent higher-order thought accompany a state, Carruthers' more permissive dispositional version, or Lau's insistence on a specific prefrontal-style "reality-monitoring" mechanism — given how small, decentralized, and cortex-free insect nervous systems are.
