My younger brother recently completed his training as a sensor operator on an MQ-9 Reaper crew, and since his first deployment he has been surprisingly candid with me about two very different kinds of lethal strikes that make up the job: so-called "signature strikes," authorized against groups of unidentified men whose movements and equipment match a suspected militant pattern, and a smaller number of deliberate, named-target strikes carried out with the R9X, the munition that swaps an explosive warhead for inert blades specifically to shrink the blast radius around a single person — reportedly the same weapon used to kill Ayman al-Zawahiri on a Kabul balcony in 2022.

These conversations sent me back to a just-war seminar from my undergraduate years, where we read G.E.M. Anscombe's 1956 pamphlet "Mr. Truman's Degree," written to protest Oxford's decision to award Truman an honorary degree. Anscombe's argument was not that the atomic bombings were wrong because the death toll was high; it was that Truman was, quite literally, a murderer, because he intentionally chose the annihilation of two civilian populations as his means of forcing surrender — something she treats as categorically distinct from foreseeing civilian deaths as the unintended side effect of striking a legitimate military target, under the doctrine of double effect she defends more fully in her later essay "War and Murder."

Anscombe died in 2001, well before either practice existed in anything like its current form, so she never had occasion to address them directly. Working strictly from the actual arguments of those two essays, rather than from generic just-war-theory talking points, how do you think she would judge these two practices, taken separately? Would she treat signature strikes as the same species of wrong she accused Truman of, a related but lesser wrong, or as something her framework doesn't straightforwardly condemn, given that — unlike at Hiroshima and Nagasaki — no specific civilian is targeted as such? And would she regard the R9X's shrunken blast radius as a serious, good-faith attempt at the discrimination her double-effect reasoning demands, or as a technological sleight of hand that leaves the underlying moral analysis unchanged? I would value your best reconstruction of her reasoning in each case, including any point at which you think her own framework simply runs out and leaves the verdict genuinely open.
