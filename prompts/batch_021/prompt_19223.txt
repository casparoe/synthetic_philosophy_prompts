My partner and I have been doing IVF for over a year now, and last week we finally got the genetic testing back (PGT-M, I think its called) on our five embryos. There's a genetic condition that runs in her family - Charcot-Marie-Tooth, it affects nerves and muscles, definitely not life-threatening and people who have it live completely full lives, but it does mean real chronic pain and mobility problems down the line. Three of the five embryos came back clear and two came back positive for the mutation, and the "obvious" plan is to transfer one of the clear ones first. But I've gotten weirdly stuck on why that's obviously right, to the point where its been keeping me up more than I'd like to admit.

Here's what's bugging me: embryo A (clear) and embryo B (positive) are, if either gets transferred, going to grow into two totally different people - different egg-sperm combo, different person, full stop. So if we go with A, we're not "sparing" some fixed kid from the condition - there's no kid who exists in both scenarios who's better off in one than the other. Whoever B would become, their only actual alternative was never existing, period, and their life would still be, by any reasonable measure, worth living. My sister, who's doing a postdoc in bioethics, brought up Parfit's non-identity problem when I mentioned this, basically to reassure me there's nothing to feel bad about either way. It didn't reassure me at all, because it seems to prove too much: it seems to say there's no reason tied to "protecting my kid from harm" to prefer A over B, and that any preference I have has to come from somewhere else entirely (or nowhere at all) - which feels wrong to me but I can't say exactly why.

So here's what I want from you: write me an essay that actually commits to an answer. Given the non-identity problem, is there a real, defensible reason to prefer transferring the unaffected embryo - and if so, what kind of reason is it (something still about harm to a person, some impersonal "one outcome is just worse" idea, something else)? Or does the logic of these cases really mean it's a moral wash and my unease isn't tracking anything real? I want you to actually walk through the main ways philosophers try to rescue a non-trivial answer here rather than just listing them side by side, and then land on a real verdict - I've gotten enough "reasonable people disagree" from the actual humans I've asked.
