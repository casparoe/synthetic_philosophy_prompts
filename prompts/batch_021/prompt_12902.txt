Compare Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility with his dad P.F. Strawson's view in "Freedom and Resentment." Galen's argument is basically that true responsibility would require you to have chosen the mental character that produced your choices, and chasing that back leads to an impossible regress (you'd have to be causa sui, self-caused). His dad's paper, on the other hand, says the whole determinism vs free will debate is kind of a red herring, and that what actually justifies our responsibility practices is just whether reactive attitudes like resentment and gratitude are appropriate given how human relationships work. Do these two views actually conflict, or is Galen just answering a totally different question then his dad was? Could you consistently accept both -- e.g. buy into the reactive-attitudes story for ordinary moral life, while still denying that anyone is "truly" or "ultimately" responsible in the deeper sense Galen cares about? Say which Strawson you think has the stronger position, or how you'd reconcile the two.
