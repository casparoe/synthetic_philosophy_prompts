so for my seminar on punishment theory (we're doing retributivism vs consequentialism this unit) i sketched out an argument for my response paper and i want to sanity check it before i hand it in, rather than just handing in something that falls apart under five seconds of scrutiny.

the basic idea: Rawls's "Two Concepts of Rules" paper distinguishes the "summary" view of rules (each act of punishing is directly justified case-by-case by weighing utility) from the "practice" view (the *practice* of punishment as a whole is justified on utilitarian grounds, but a particular act only counts as punishment -- and is justified -- by conforming to the rules that define the practice, which require guilt). he uses this to block the classic objection that utilitarianism recommends punishing an innocent person whenever its socially useful: on the practice view that's not punishment at all, its what he calls "telishment," a different practice, so a case-by-case utility calculation never even gets a shot at it once you're inside the practice of punishment proper.

my worry is that this just relocates the bullet instead of dodging it. someone still has to decide, on utilitarian grounds, whether to adopt a punishment practice with a strict guilt-rule vs one that allows telishment in extreme cases (say, only to prevent a big riot) -- and that decision is itself a summary-view-style calculation, just made at the level of institutional design instead of the individual case. so the practice view seems to just rename the "should we ever sacrifice an innocent person for the greater good" question as a question about which practice to pick, without giving a principled utilitarian reason the guilt-rule cant be relaxed at the design stage.

could you give me some comments -- specifically (1) am i being fair to Rawls or missing the actual reason the move is supposed to work, (2) is my "you've just pushed it up a level" complaint the standard objection people make against him, or is there a well-known reply i'm not thinking of, and (3) is this even an interesting angle for a short paper, or is it kind of a well worn point. not looking for a rewrite, just want to know where the argument's weak.
