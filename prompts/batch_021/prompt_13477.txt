I was appointed to my university's Honorary Degrees Committee at the start of this term, and the first nomination on our docket has turned out to be the hardest kind of case. The provost has proposed an honorary doctorate for a retired general, now celebrated for three decades of disaster-relief and reconstruction work, who as theatre commander ordered the firebombing of a rebel-held provincial capital, killing an estimated eleven thousand civilians, in order to break an insurgency that had already cost far more lives than that. A group of faculty opposing the nomination has circulated Anscombe's pamphlet 'Mr Truman's Degree,' arguing that the parallel to Oxford's 1956 honoring of Truman is exact, and that no tally of lives saved can turn what she called the deliberate killing of the innocent into an honorable act. Our bylaws require only that honorees have shown 'extraordinary service to humanity,' with no guidance on weighing a single wartime decision against decades of subsequent good. Please draft the two-page memo I will circulate before the vote: it should come down clearly on proceeding with, deferring, or withdrawing the nomination, and it needs to meet Anscombe's argument about intention on its own terms rather than simply re-running the utilitarian calculation she rejected.
