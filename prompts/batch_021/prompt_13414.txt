Before I retired, I spent much of my career as a physicist working on nonlinear dynamical systems, and I distinctly remember relying on Brouwer's fixed-point theorem whenever I needed to guarantee that one of my models possessed an equilibrium. It was only much later, once I began reading in philosophy of mathematics, that I discovered the irony: the very same Brouwer went on to found intuitionism, a movement deeply suspicious of exactly the sort of pure existence proof his own theorem exemplifies. Since then I have been working through Heyting's introductory book on intuitionism and some of Dummett's writing on the subject, and in a recent conversation a mathematician friend of mine used the phrases "intuitionistic logic" and "mathematical constructivism" almost interchangeably. This struck me as too quick, though at the time I could not quite articulate why.

I would therefore like you to compare intuitionistic logic with mathematical constructivism, understood as the broader philosophical and methodological movement of which it is usually taken to be a part. In particular, in which ways is intuitionistic logic a special, and perhaps even a somewhat contingent or non-obligatory, member of that broader family, rather than simply the canonical logic of constructivism as such? I would be grateful if your answer engaged with at least two things: first, Brouwer's own apparent ambivalence about granting any formal logic, including the calculus that Heyting later distilled from his ideas, a foundational role at all, given his insistence that mathematics is fundamentally a languageless activity of the mind that logic can only describe after the fact; and second, the existence of rival constructive programs, such as the Russian school of recursive constructivism founded by Markov, which accepts principles going beyond bare intuitionistic logic (I have Markov's Principle in mind), and Errett Bishop's constructive analysis, which is often read as deliberately neutral on which underlying logic one adopts. I would rather not simply be told that intuitionism "denies the law of excluded middle" -- I am hoping for a discussion that actually grapples with these finer-grained tensions.
