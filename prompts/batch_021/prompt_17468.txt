A friend and I spent a long dinner arguing about the AI shutdown problem. He thinks we still have no real idea how to build an advanced system that won't resist being switched off. I think that worry was largely put to rest once researchers like Stuart Russell reframed human-AI interaction as an "assistance game": an agent that is kept genuinely uncertain about its true objective, rather than handed a fixed reward function, finds it rational to defer to a human's decision to hit the off-switch, since that decision is itself evidence about what the uncertain objective actually favors. Given that this piece of the puzzle is basically settled, what's the strongest remaining argument for why building a genuinely corrigible advanced AI might still be hard?
