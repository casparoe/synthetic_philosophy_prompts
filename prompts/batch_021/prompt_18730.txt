So a bit of background - I was a research chemist for about 35 years and only got serious about philosophy after I retired. This one's a bit personal but here goes. Last winter I had bad pneumonia and ended up in the ICU, and my heart apparantly stopped for almost a minute before they got it going again. While I was "out" I had this incredibly vivid experience - floating up near the ceiling watching them work on my body, then a warm dark tunnel, then my late brother telling me it wasn't my time yet. It felt more detailed and real than most of my actual memories, if that makes sense.

I've been a committed materialist my whole life, the sort of guy who always figured if you can't run it through a mass spec it probably isn't real. Nothing about the actual evidence has changed since - I know a dying, oxygen-starved brain can produce all sorts of hallucinations - but my confidence got shaken a bit anyway, even though I definately can't point to any new fact I learned. My niece insists this proves "something" survives the body and that I'm just being stubborn by not updating. What bugs me isn't only that her case seems weak - it's that I'm not even sure I know what "surviving the body" would even mean, or how you'd ever tell that apart from a brain just doing its last bit of firing. Can you help me untangle this?
