I'm on my school's math team, and something that happened during a recent practice session for our state competition has been bothering me ever since. We were working through a set of timed problems, and on one multi-step algebra question, I solved it, checked my work by plugging the answer back into the original equation, and felt completely confident that I had it right. Afterward, our coach mentioned that this exact problem had been used in a past competition, and that the organizers had since noticed, from confidence ratings students submit alongside their answers, that students who described themselves as very confident about this particular problem were wrong more often than not, apparently because of one sign error that is unusually easy to make and unusually hard to catch even on a careful re-check. I went back through my solution a third time afterward, line by line, and it still looked entirely correct to me; I couldn't find anything resembling the mistake our coach described.

I brought this up with a couple of teammates afterward, and we ended up disagreeing about what to make of it. One of them argued that I should now treat my answer as probably wrong and scale back my confidence substantially, since the statistic amounts to real evidence about how people in exactly my position tend to fare, whatever my own impression might be. That advice struck me as strange to follow, though, since nobody has pointed out what is actually wrong with any specific line of my work; I am only being told that people who feel the way I feel about this kind of problem are usually mistaken. Yet if I did lower my confidence the way she suggested, I would apparently end up believing, at the same time, that every individual step of my solution is sound and that the solution as a whole is probably wrong, which feels like an oddly unstable place to land, even though the statistic does seem like genuine evidence rather than something I can simply dismiss.

I would like some help thinking through this more carefully than my teammates and I managed on our own. Was my teammate right that the sensible response is to defer to the statistic and substantially lower my confidence, even without being able to identify any actual error in my work? Or can it be reasonable to stick with my own step-by-step assessment precisely because it still seems flawless every time I check it — and if so, how should I make sense of holding full confidence in my reasoning and serious doubt about its conclusion at the same time, without one of those attitudes simply being a mistake?
