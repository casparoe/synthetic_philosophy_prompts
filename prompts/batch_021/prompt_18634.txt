I spent almost 40 years as a research chemist before I retired, and for the last couple of years I've fallen down a philosophy rabbit hole, mostly working through the Stanford Encyclopedia and a couple of intro books at whatever pace an old lab rat can manage. Right now I'm stuck on this literature about "standing to blame" and hypocrisy, and a real argument broke out at a family bbq last weekend that I think is basically a perfect test case, so I'm hoping you can referee it, because I honestly can't tell who's right.

My brother in law Dave spent half of dinner telling his 16 year old son to get off his phone at the table, while Dave himself was checking his own phone every few minutes. The kid shot back "look whos talking," and that set off a completely separate side argument between two friends of ours, Marcus and Lena, neither of whom even has kids, they just would not let it drop. Roughly it went like this. (1) Marcus said the kid was dodging the point: Dave being a hypocrite doesn't make the advice wrong, too much screen time really is bad for a teenager, and "you do it too" is a textbook tu quoque that never actually answers the criticism. (2) Lena said Marcus was answering a question nobody asked, since the kid never disputed that screens are bad, he was disputing whether Dave specifically has any business being the one to say it, and you can think the content is completely correct while still thinking this particular messenger has no standing to deliver it. (3) Marcus asked what "standing" could even add here beyond "you're wrong": if the message is true and the kid needs to hear it, why should it matter who says it, and isn't "you have no standing" suspiciously just a permanent excuse for hypocrites to keep doing the thing? (4) Lena said blaming someone isn't just handing over a true fact, it's making a demand and implicitly claiming you hold yourself to that same rule, so a hypocrite is helping himself to an exemption he hasn't earned, and that's what's objectionable, separately from whether the content is true. (5) Marcus said that proves too much, since by that logic nobody could ever criticize anyone for anything unless they were personally flawless on that exact point, which can't be right. (6) Lena answered that the fix isn't perfection, it's not running a double standard: Dave sharing the flaw wouldn't cost him anything if he owned it out loud ("I know, I'm just as bad, we both need to cut down"), it's specifically demanding something of his kid while quietly exempting himself that costs him the standing to scold.

Could you go through Marcus and Lena's back and forth move by move and say who has the better of it at each numbered step, not just declare an overall winner? I'm especially curious whether "standing to blame" is really doing philosophical work here or whether Marcus is onto something with move (3), and it's mostly just a polite label for changing the subject. And if there's something both of them are missing that would actually settle it, I'd love to hear that too.
