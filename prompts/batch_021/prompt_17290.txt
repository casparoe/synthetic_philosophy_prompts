As a longtime reader of the AI safety discourse on LessWrong and the Alignment Forum, I keep encountering an assumption behind "deceptive alignment" that is rarely examined through a philosophy-of-mind lens. The scenario requires attributing to a mesa-optimizer both a hidden mesa-objective diverging from the base objective it was trained on, and a belief that it is currently being trained rather than deployed — attributions meant to be true of the system even though, by hypothesis, nothing in its training-time behavior distinguishes it from a genuinely aligned model. Daniel Dennett's intentional stance holds that belief attributions are warranted only insofar as they improve an interpreter's predictions, with no further, stance-independent fact about what a system believes — a position he tries to soften without abandoning in "Real Patterns." Please argue that taking Dennett's picture seriously undermines the coherence of deceptive alignment as standardly formulated, rather than merely making the condition harder to detect empirically.
