I'm writing a piece on the limits of introspection and have been working through two related claims from Timothy Williamson's Knowledge and Its Limits (2000). The first is his general argument that almost no non-trivial condition is "luminous" — that is, a condition such that whenever it obtains, the subject is thereby in a position to know that it obtains. The second is the more specific KK principle, according to which knowing that P guarantees knowing that one knows that P. Could you compare these two claims for me? In particular, is the KK principle simply one instance of the broader luminosity thesis, applied to the state of knowing itself, or is there a principled reason that state might resist Williamson's margin-for-error argument even where other conditions do not? Please justify your answer.
