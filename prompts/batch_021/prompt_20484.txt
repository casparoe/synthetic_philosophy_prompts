Nobody outside the club is ever touched. Eventually the local paper runs a story on it, the town is horrified, and the parish council tries to push through a bylaw to shut the place down even though, strictly speaking, no existing law is being broken.

I did jurisprudence at uni years ago and the one lecture that actually stuck with me was on the Hart-Devlin debate, so I want to use it for a dinner-table scene between two side characters: Mireille, a civil liberties solicitor, and her late father's old friend Judge Alsop, retired now but still sharp as anything. I want Mireille arguing something like Hart's side — that the state has no business banning something just because most people find it disgusting, if the only person plausibly harmed is the one who consented to it — and Alsop taking Devlin's side, that a shared morality is the glue holding society together and the law's entitled to protect that even at some cost to individual freedom. Could you write the actual dialogue for me (700 words or so is fine)? I don't want either of them just reciting talking points past each other — get Mireille to really press him on whether "most people are disgusted by it" is evidence of anything at all, and give Alsop a genuine comeback instead of just folding, and somewhere in there I'd like them to get into whether anyone's ever actually shown a society falling apart from tolerating this sort of private stuff, the way Devlin's argument seems to assume it will. Don't wrap it up neatly at the end — I'd rather it just stop with both of them having landed real hits on each other.
