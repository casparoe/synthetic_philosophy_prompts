On the standard possible-worlds analysis of knowledge, an agent automatically knows every logical truth, and their knowledge is closed under logical consequence -- the notorious "problem of logical omniscience." Give an example of this problem that turns on a piece of genuine mathematical or logical reasoning, rather than a bare tautology or the stock case of an agent who does not know whether Goldbach's conjecture is true. Your example should show that the idealization does not merely simplify but actually distorts some specific epistemic phenomenon -- for instance, the experience of proof, of mathematical surprise, or of coming to recognize, only after real work, that two formulations of a problem are equivalent. State exactly which feature of the possible-worlds semantics is responsible for the problem in your example, and briefly indicate how either awareness logic or impossible-worlds semantics would have to revise that feature to accommodate it.
