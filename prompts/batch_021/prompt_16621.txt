I'm putting together a seminar on moral responsibility and keep getting stuck on a specific puzzle in the "standing to blame" literature that I can't quite untangle in my own head, so I wanted to sanity-check my intuitions with you before I try to lecture on it. The classic case is the tu quoque one: a politician rails against infidelity despite having had an affair himself, and everyone feels like his hypocrisy somehow disqualifies him from making the complaint -- but on paper this looks like a textbook ad hominem, since his own conduct is totally irrelevant to whether infidelity is actually wrong. So what exactly is the hypocrisy supposed to be undermining, if not the truth of the criticism?

As I understand it there are roughly two families of answer floating around. One (I think this is basically Wallace's "equal standing" move, with echoes in Cohen's stuff on who gets to condemn terrorists) says blame presupposes something like a fair, symmetric moral relationship between blamer and blamed, and the hypocrite violates this by exempting himself from a norm hes actively enforcing on someone else -- so the real objection is unfairness, not falsity. The other treats blame as more like issuing a demand or holding someone to a standard, and says theres something almost self-undermining, Moore's-paradox-ish, about demanding compliance with a rule you yourself flout (this is closer to the Todd-style "unified account," if I'm remembering right, which also wants to explain non-hypocritical standing problems like complicity or plain none-of-your-business-ness under the same umbrella).

The bit that really trips me up is the asymmetry with praise: a wildly undisciplined coach can still sincerely and appropriately praise an athlete's discipline, no standing problem at all, even though by parity you'd expect the "equal standing" story to generate the same violation there too. Can you walk me through, at a pretty gut level, why hypocrisy specifically is supposed to undercut standing to blame in a way that survives the ad hominem worry, and whether either of these two stories (or some third one) actually explains the praise/blame asymmetry rather than just stipulating it?
