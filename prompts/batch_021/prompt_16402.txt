Was arguing with a coworker about whether our translation model actually "understands" meaning or just pattern-matches, and they brought up this old philosophy thing that kinda broke my brain: Quine's "gavagai" example. Setup is a linguist visits some isolated tribe, a rabbit runs by, and the local shouts "gavagai." Obvious guess is "rabbit," but apparently Quine's whole point is that "undetached rabbit part," "rabbit-stage," and a bunch of other totally different meanings fit the exact same behavioral evidence forever, no matter how much more data you collect -- not cause we're bad at translating but cause theres supposedly no fact of the matter at all, even in principle.

Can you write me a short story built around this? Something first-contact-y, kinda like Arrival but bleaker, where a linguist keeps designing cleverer and cleverer tests to pin down what an alien word refers to and it just never resolves -- not from lack of trying, but cause the indeterminacy is baked into the problem itself. Don't let it collapse into a generic "language barrier is hard" story though, I want the actual underdetermination thing to be the plot engine, and honestly I'd take an unresolved, unsettling ending over a tidy one.
