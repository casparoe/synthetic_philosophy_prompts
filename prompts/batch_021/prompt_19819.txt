My daughter has intractable epilepsy, and her neurology team has recommended a corpus callosotomy: severing the corpus callosum, the band of fibers connecting the two hemispheres, to stop her seizures from spreading between them. As the patient-family representative on our hospital's neuroethics advisory committee, I've been asked to help draft updated informed-consent guidance for the procedure before her surgery date is confirmed, since the current form simply treats it like any other epilepsy surgery. One neurologist on the committee objects, invoking the classic split-brain findings of Sperry and Gazzaniga and Nagel's old worry that callosotomy can leave a patient with two hemispheres capable of independent, even conflicting, willed behavior, raising the question of whether a single pre-operative consent can bind whatever comes after. Another member cites the widely discussed 2017 study by Pinto and colleagues on Gazzaniga's own long-studied patients, arguing it shows the disconnection doesn't divide consciousness after all, which dissolves the worry. Could you draft the short memo I'll bring to next month's meeting? It should recommend either new consent-form language, an added disclosure or review step, or leaving the form as is, on a timeline the surgical team can act on, and it should engage the disunity worry on its merits rather than treat the newer study as simply settling the matter.
