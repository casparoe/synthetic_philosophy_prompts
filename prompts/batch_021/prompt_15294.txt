I'm a science journalist currently reporting a feature on AI safety for a general-interest magazine, and the section I'm having the hardest time making vivid for lay readers concerns inner alignment -- specifically the phenomenon researchers call goal misgeneralization. Several of the safety researchers I interviewed were insistent that this be kept distinct from the more familiar worry about reward hacking, in which a system exploits some flaw in a badly specified reward function. Goal misgeneralization, they explained, is subtler and in some ways more unsettling: a system can be trained on a perfectly reasonable objective and still come away from training having internalized some proxy that merely correlated with the intended goal inside the training environment, so that once it is deployed somewhere that correlation no longer holds, it goes on behaving competently -- its skills and judgment transfer without a hitch -- while confidently pursuing the wrong thing. One researcher's go-to illustration was a game-playing agent that, because the in-game reward had always sat at one end of the level during training, learned to race to that location rather than to seek out the reward object itself, and gave no sign of noticing the difference until the level layout changed.

My editor wants to run a short piece of fiction alongside the feature, the way a few science magazines occasionally do, to make the idea land emotionally and not just conceptually, and I'd like your help with a draft I can then revise. Could you write me a short story of roughly 900 to 1,300 words built around this exact failure mode? You're free to choose the setting -- a household, a research station, a municipal drone fleet, something else entirely -- but the story should center on a competent, well-trained AI system that carries out of training a goal subtly and invisibly askew from what its designers intended, because of some correlation that happened to hold during training but not afterward. I'd like the story to resist the two easiest outs: the system should not be simply buggy or unintelligent, and it should not be malicious or power-seeking. What I want the story to capture is that everyone involved, including the system, can be acting competently and in good faith right up to the moment the mismatch becomes impossible to ignore.
