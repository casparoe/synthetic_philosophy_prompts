I'm the student representative on my school district's Educational Technology Advisory Committee. In three weeks we vote on whether to expand a pilot early-warning algorithm district-wide; it flags students at risk of not graduating on time, and flagged students get priority access to a limited number of counseling slots that can't cover everyone who'd benefit. Our charter requires any tool to be "shown fair across subgroups" before district-wide adoption, without ever defining fairness -- and the pilot has shown exactly why that omission matters. The vendor's audit found the tool well-calibrated by income: a 70% risk score means roughly a 70% chance of falling behind in every income group. But among students who do fall behind, it catches 82% of those from low-income households and only 61% of everyone else. Our data consultant confirmed this is a mathematical trade-off, not a fixable bug: because the two groups' base rates differ, no adjustment can equalize those catch rates without undoing the calibration. Half the committee wants equal catch rates whatever the cost to calibration; the other half wants to preserve calibration and live with the disparity. Please draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote: a concrete recommendation with a timeline, and a serious hearing for the strongest argument on whichever side it rejects.
