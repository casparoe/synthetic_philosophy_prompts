Okay, this is going to sound like a made-up textbook example but I swear it happened at an actual meeting this week. I'm taking a seminar this semester on collective decision-making (lots of Condorcet, then List & Pettit on judgment aggregation and the discursive dilemma), and I also somehow got roped into my school's Honor Council this year, and the two collided in a way thats honestly kind of stressing me out. We've got a case where a student is accused of using an unauthorized solutions manual on a take-home final. Per our bylaws, she's only "responsible" for a violation if (a) she actually used the manual, AND (b) she knew using it wasn't allowed — if it was a genuine accident, it's not a violation even though she technically used it. We're a 5-person panel. On (a), it's 3-2 that she used it. On (b), it's also 3-2 that she knew, but not the same three people both times. Tally issue-by-issue and apply the rule, and both premises clear a majority, so she comes out responsible. But ask each of us "responsible, yes or no" straight up, and it flips to 3-2 against, because the three of us voting "not responsible" don't even agree on why — one thinks she used it but didn't know, another thinks she'd obviously have known but doesn't believe she used it, and the third just doubts the whole story.

Our chair wants a short memo before Wednesday's meeting that actually commits to how we should resolve this specific case (the issue-by-issue result stands, the direct vote stands, or something else) instead of just describing the deadlock, and I volunteered to draft it since I'm apparently the only one on the panel who finds this fascinating rather than infuriating. Could you write that memo for me? I want it to pick an answer and defend it with reasons the rest of the council would actually find persuasive, but treat the option it rejects fairly rather than strawmanning it — "just go with the direct vote" isn't a crazy position, since the alternative means sanctioning someone when a majority of us, asked point-blank, don't think she should be sanctioned. If you can squeeze in a quick suggestion for how we might amend the bylaws so we're not back in this mess next semester, great, but nailing Wednesday's decision is the main thing. Oh, and please don't just namedrop List & Pettit and move on — I've done that enough for both of us already.
