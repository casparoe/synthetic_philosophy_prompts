I cover science and ideas for a living, and I also sit on the small advisory board of a civic-tech nonprofit that pitches "evidence-based governance" tools to mid-size cities. Our newest vendor wants us to endorse a pilot where a city would hand its discretionary infrastructure budget to a cost-per-benefit optimization model instead of having the council debate and vote on it line by line -- which reminded me of a piece I've been reporting on Carnap, who genuinely thought the same rigor that cleaned up logic could clean up politics too (the Vienna Circle's 1929 manifesto literally ties a "scientific world-conception" to social planning), even though he also held that value judgments aren't the kind of thing science can verify. Half the board honestly just loves this because it finally takes the politics out of budgeting; the other half says it just launders value choices -- which metric, who's welfare, what discount rate -- through a veneer of neutrality, which is basically what Popper and Hayek warned about when they went after "scientific" planning. We vote Friday. Write me the memo I'll circulate beforehand -- a clear recommendation (endorse, reject, or endorse with real conditions and a review date) plus an honest, serious treatment of the strongest argument on whichever side you don't take.
