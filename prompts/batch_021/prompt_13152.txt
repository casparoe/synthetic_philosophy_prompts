A few of the old guard are throwing me a little leaving-do on Friday and asked if I'd say a few words about why I'm going. I dont want it to land as either a boring list of policy grievances or as me telling a room full of genuinely decent people that they're basically fascists, because they're not, and a couple of them are just as fed up as I am, they just haven't jumped yet.

Here's the complication: a friend doing a philosophy PhD got me reading Simone Weil's little essay on abolishing political parties a few months back, and it's completely reorganised how I think about what happened at that meeting. It's not just "the leadership got a bit pushy" -- her actual argument is that any party, purely by being a machine for manufacturing a collective passion, for leaning on individual members' minds until they fall into line with it, and existing mainly to grow itself without any natural stopping point, ends up totalitarian in embryo even when it's a small, harmless-looking, perfectly mainstream local branch like mine. I want the speech to actually use that argument rather than flatten it into generic "partisanship bad" stuff, because that same PhD friend is going to be standing right there and will absolutely notice if I get Weil wrong. Could you write me something like a four-minute speech that does three things at once: lands for people who've never heard of Weil and would be a bit stung to hear their branch described as proto-totalitarian, survives my friend fact-checking it in real time, and deals somewhere in there with the obvious comeback -- if parties are really this bad, how is anyone supposed to organise a mass election without them -- ideally by gesturing at what Weil actually thought should replace them, rather than just leaving everyone with a very eloquent complaint. I'd rather it come off warm than clever, and it should leave the door open for the two or three people in the room who I know are wavering too.
