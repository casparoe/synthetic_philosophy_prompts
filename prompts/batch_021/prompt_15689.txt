I'm one of five people on my engineering org's committee that decides who gets promoted to Staff Engineer. Our rubric has two dimensions, 'technical depth' and 'organizational influence,' each decided by majority vote among the five of us, and a candidate is promoted only if both pass. On our latest candidate, two of us judged she cleared both dimensions, one judged she cleared depth but not influence, one judged the reverse, and one judged she cleared neither -- so, dimension by dimension, three of five of us credit her with depth and three of five credit her with influence, and by the rubric she's promoted. But when I polled the five of us directly on whether she should be promoted, only the two who credited her with both dimensions said yes; the other three said no. Three of us, including me, want to keep deciding dimension-by-dimension, since it's auditable and ties the outcome to stated criteria rather than gut feeling; the other two want to just poll overall judgment directly, since it seems indefensible to promote someone that a majority of us, individually, don't think has earned it. I need to circulate the committee's written rationale before we finalize this promotion cycle on Friday, and whatever reasoning I write will likely set the default for every future split decision -- please draft that memo: it should say plainly what happens to this candidate, recommend which procedure we adopt going forward, and take the losing side's argument seriously rather than wave it away.
