I cover science and ideas for a living, and outside of the actual writing I sit on my outlet's small internal standards committee -- three of us who get pulled in whenever theres a real fight about how a story got worded. We've got a case this week that's exposed a genuine fault line on the committee, and even though nobody would put it this way, I'm pretty sure we're actually arguing about epistemology without realizing it.

Two incidents, both reporters who stated something flatly as fact in a published piece rather than hedging it as an allegation. Reporter A had two well-placed independent sources plus internal documents that all lined up -- did everything you'd want a careful reporter to do -- and the claim still turned out to be false, for reasons nobody could have caught in advance. Reporter B ran with a single tip she barely had grounds to trust, wrote it up as settled fact anyway, and it turned out to be exactly right. Half the committee wants to clear A and discipline B, on the theory that you can only be judged on what you had good reason to believe at the time, truth be damned. The other half wants it reversed, since isn't getting it right the whole point of stating something as fact instead of hedging it? And our editor-in-chief, who is not helping, wants a rule like "state it as fact only if you'd bet your job on it," which is really a rule about how sure you feel, not about your evidence or the truth at all.

I interviewed an epistemologist a few years back for an unrelated story, and I have this nagging memory that "what has to be true of you before you're entitled to just flatly assert something" is an actual, named debate in philosophy, not something our committee is inventing from scratch -- I just never got the terms straight and don't want to fake my way through Thursday's meeting. Could you write the memo I need to bring? I want an actual recommendation, not a split-the-difference shrug: either one standard or an explicit ranked set of them if you think no single one can do the job, applied directly enough to A and B that it's clear whether either of them should face consequences, plus the strongest honest case for whichever standard you end up rejecting, since I will absolutely get challenged on it in the room.
