I host a small philosophy podcast aimed mostly at graduate students, and next week's guest defends a Bernard Williams-style internalist theory of reasons for action: the view that a statement of the form "A has a reason to φ" is true only if A could arrive at being motivated to φ by a sound deliberative route from something already in her actual subjective motivational set, and that "external" reasons statements -- claims that someone has a reason to act quite apart from anything in her psychology -- are either false or turn out, on inspection, to be elliptical claims about what such deliberation would yield. In interviews she likes to compress the whole position into one line -- "the question is always whether sound deliberation could get you there, not whether it already has" -- and reaches for that formula whenever a counterexample comes up. Since my audience already knows the bare internalism/externalism distinction, I don't want the episode to just restate it.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond that restatement. I want at least one question pressing on whether "sound deliberation" is elastic enough that the internal/external distinction becomes unfalsifiable in practice, since deliberation is allowed to draw on imaginative and factual considerations that were never explicitly present in the agent's motivational set beforehand; one on the amoralist case -- someone with no other-regarding element anywhere in his psychology -- asking whether internalism is committed to saying he strictly has no reason at all to refrain from cruelty, as opposed to merely being unpersuadable; one built around Williams's own case of the man who wants what he takes to be gin but is actually about to drink petrol, asking whether tying reasons to what deliberation from true beliefs would recommend quietly reintroduces a standard of correctness that isn't itself sourced in the agent's prior motivations; and one on the rationalist rejoinder, pressed by critics such as Korsgaard, that even the demand to deliberate soundly looks like an unconditional requirement on the agent rather than something grounded in her actual motivational set -- and whether resisting that regress forces internalism to help itself to exactly the kind of agent-independent standard it was designed to avoid. For each of these four, add a short follow-up question keyed to the answer she is most likely to give.
