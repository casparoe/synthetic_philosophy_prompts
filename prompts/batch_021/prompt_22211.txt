This whole "moral equality of combatants" question stopped being abstract for me pretty recently. My cousin just got his mobilization papers — he's a reservist, and given everything going on where he lives he's probably getting sent to the front sometime in the next few months. He's about as apolitical as a person can be and mostly just seems resigned about it, but I'm the one who can't stop turning it over in my head, partly because he and I both think the war he's about to go fight in is unjust in the first place, whatever that's worth. That sent me down a rabbit hole reading about just war theory, where I ran into Walzer's argument for what's apparently called the moral equality of combatants — basically that jus in bello (how you fight) is independent of jus ad bellum (whether your side should be fighting at all), so a soldier who follows the actual rules of war is in the clear no matter which side he's on. But then I found out there's a whole revisionist camp, McMahan mainly, who think that's wrong and that soldiers fighting for an unjust cause don't really have the moral right to kill the other side even in "clean" combat.

What I'd actually find useful is if you could walk me through the strongest version of the revisionist argument from the ground up — like, how it's supposed to fall out of more basic ideas about self-defense and liability to lethal force, not just assert the conclusion — and contrast it properly with the traditionalist case for equality (I gather there's more to that than "rules are rules"). And then, the part I actually care about: McMahan apparently wants to say the act of an unjust combatant killing a just one is still wrong even when the guy doing it is fully excused (duress, propaganda, no real choice about being there, whatever). Is that wrong/blameless distinction doing real philosophical work, or is it basically a way to have the conclusion without having to bite the bullet on calling ordinary conscripts bad people? I keep going back and forth on whether that move is legitimate or just a dodge, and I'd like to actually understand it instead of just picking whichever answer makes me feel better about my cousin.
