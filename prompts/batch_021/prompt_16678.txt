I'm a philosophy PhD student working toward a qualifying paper in metaethics, and something from this term's seminar on non-cognitivism has been nagging at me. We spent several weeks on Simon Blackburn's quasi-realist program from Spreading the Word, and I came away thinking his "logic of attitudes" essentially settles the matter: by treating a disapproving attitude toward an act, combined with an approving attitude toward something that obviously entails it, as a species of practical inconsistency on a par with believing two incompatible propositions, he seems to explain perfectly well why an inference like "if cruelty is wrong, then teaching a child to be cruel is wrong; cruelty is wrong; therefore teaching a child to be cruel is wrong" comes out valid, even though "wrong" never describes anything and is never actually asserted in the first premise. Gibbard's apparatus in Wise Choices, Apt Feelings, built around the acceptance of norms or plans rather than beliefs, struck me as reaching much the same destination by a different road.

Given that both of these were worked out by the early 1990s, I don't understand why our syllabus, along with every metaethics survey chapter I've been assigned this year, still lists the Frege-Geach problem as one of expressivism's two or three most damaging live objections, rather than treating it as a mid-century difficulty that quasi-realism and norm-expressivism have already put to rest. Is this mostly disciplinary inertia — a tidy problem-and-solution pair that keeps getting recycled in teaching because it's pedagogically convenient — or is there some real, technical respect in which what Blackburn and Gibbard deliver falls short of fully retiring the problem? Since I'll need to characterize the current state of the dialectic accurately in my own paper, rather than either overstating expressivism's remaining troubles or dismissing a worry that specialists apparently still take seriously, I'd be grateful for help pinning down exactly what — if anything — is genuinely still open, and why the problem keeps resurfacing in the more recent literature on hybrid and dynamic-semantic versions of expressivism.
