I'm a philosophy PhD student working mostly in formal epistemology, and this term our seminar spent two sessions on Richard Jeffrey's generalization of conditionalization to evidence that isn't learned with certainty. On Jeffrey's "probability kinematics," if new information shifts your credences across a partition {E_1,...,E_n} to new values P'(E_i), while your conditional credences P(A|E_i) stay fixed for every proposition A — the "rigidity" condition — then your new unconditional credence in A is P'(A) = Σ_i P'(E_i)·P(A|E_i). Everyone agreed this elegantly fixes the obvious limitation of strict conditionalization, which can only handle evidence that arrives as a proposition you become fully certain of. What sparked an actual argument afterward, over coffee, was a homework problem showing that, unlike strict conditionalization, Jeffrey's rule is not commutative: if you apply two uncertain, rigidity-respecting updates in sequence to a pair of correlated partitions {E_i} and {F_j}, you generally land on a different final credence distribution depending on which partition you update on first, even though the total evidence incorporated is the same either way.

Half of us concluded that this order-sensitivity shows Jeffrey conditionalization isn't really a generalization of rational belief updating at all, but more of an ad hoc bookkeeping device that happens to coincide with conditionalization in the easy cases. The other half insisted the order-sensitivity is no more troubling than the banal fact that conditionalization is "sensitive" to whatever evidence you happen to receive, and that no live diachronic Dutch book or long-run coherence argument actually forces symmetry across orderings once you look closely at what the rigidity condition holds fixed at each step. I'd like you to help settle this. Please construct the strongest argument you can that the failure of commutativity under sequential updates on interacting partitions is not a defect in Jeffrey conditionalization's rational credentials, engaging directly with what a proper diachronic Dutch book or coherence argument would need to show in order to indict order-dependent updating, and with whether the fact that rigidity is relativized to your credences at the time of each update lets Jeffrey conditionalization off a hook that strict conditionalization was never on in the first place. Please close with the single strongest objection a committed critic could raise against the argument you've just given.
