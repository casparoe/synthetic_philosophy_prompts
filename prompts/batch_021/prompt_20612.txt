I have spent the past several months working through Nietzsche's "On the Genealogy of Morality" on my own, largely because I grew tired of seeing it reduced online to slogans about master and slave morality. The passage I keep returning to is near the beginning of the second essay, where Nietzsche describes the "sovereign individual" as the ripest fruit at the end of the long, often cruel process he calls the "morality of custom": someone who has finally earned the right to make promises, who is autonomous and has left mere custom behind, and who has genuine mastery over his own will, unlike the great mass of people who are merely obedient out of habit or fear of punishment. Nietzsche even says that this person's proud awareness of his own power and responsibility is what he calls his "conscience."

Since this is clearly Nietzsche's own positive ideal for what a human being can become once the long disciplinary work of custom and morality has finished its job and can be discarded, I would like to work out the practical upshot. What concrete habits or practices, in your view, would actually help someone cultivate that kind of autonomous, self-mastering, promise-keeping selfhood in their own life today?
