So I went down an AI-safety rabbit hole last week and found this idea called the "commitment races problem" on one of those rationalist blogs. The gist as I got it: when two sufficiently sophisticated agents are negotiating, whoever locks in an irreversible commitment first gets the upper hand (like ripping your own steering wheel out in a game of chicken so the other guy has to swerve), so every agent is incentivized to commit as early as possible, before it's actually had time to think the commitment through properly. And if everyone races to commit like that, you can end up with agents locked into aggressive, incompatible positions with no way to back down, which is worse for everyone then if nobody had rushed to commit at all.

This actually matters for something we're prototyping at work: a system of autonomous LLM agents that negotiate contract terms on behalf of different clients, where one proposed feature is letting an agent publish a signed "final, binding offer" mid-negotiation, and the other agent can verify the exact logic behind the number since the code's open. That way it can't just get haggled down further.

What are the most important implications of the commitment races problem for a setup like that? Skip the philosophy-101 stuff, i just wanna know what could actually go wrong and what design choices would make it more or less dangerous.
