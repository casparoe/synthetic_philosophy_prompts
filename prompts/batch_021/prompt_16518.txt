I'm working on a historical novel set in Mexico City in the early 1690s, and one of the central characters is a fictionalized version of Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz — I got obsessed with her after reading "Hombres necios" years ago, and then even more after a trip to Mexico City where I saw the old convent she lived in (I think its part of a university now). Anyway, I've hit a scene I keep avoiding: right around when she'd have been composing her response to the Bishop of Puebla — the one who published her critique of that Vieira sermon alongside his own letter under the "Sor Filotea" pen name, telling her she really ought to spend less time on secular learning and more on devotional reading — I want a scene set in the convent's locutorio where she's confronted face to face by a churchman pressing that exact point. It could be the Bishop himself if you think it's plausible he'd be in the room, or her confessor Núñez de Miranda, or some composite of the two — whoever gives the stronger scene, honestly, I don't mind taking some liberties there.

Could you write that out as an actual dialogue, maybe 400-700 words, no narration or stage directions, just the two of them talking? The thing I most want is real back-and-forth: both of them actually rebutting the specific thing the other just said, not two speeches running in parallel. I don't want the churchman to be a strawman who just says women are dumb — my understanding is that the actual objections back then were dressed up in real scripture and real doctrine about a nun's vocation, so I'd like his side of it to feel like it's drawing on that, genuinely, not just gesturing at it. And I don't want Sor Juana's side to read like a modern feminist talking point stuffed into a habit — I want it to sound like its coming from someone arguing within the actual terms of a 17th-century convent, even if she's pushing hard against the edges of them.
