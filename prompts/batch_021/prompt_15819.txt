My grandpa died last week and I'm doing his eulogy this weekend. He never studied any of this stuff (drove a forklift his whole life), but for like 30 years his party trick at holiday dinners was ranting about the grandfather paradox -- firmly on the Novikov self-consistency side of things, zero patience for anyone who said it "just branches into a new timeline." His whole point was that if you actually changed anything upstream, you'd unmake the exact chain of events that produced you, so wishing you could redo the past is quietly wishing yourself out of existence.

Half the room has heard this rant a hundred times and will clock exactly what I'm doing, the other half wont have a clue why I'm even bringing up "grandfather" anything. I dont want it to just be a cute pun -- I want to use his own logic on the relatives who keep saying "I wish we'd had more time with him," without it sounding like I'm telling them their grief is wrong. Can you write it?
