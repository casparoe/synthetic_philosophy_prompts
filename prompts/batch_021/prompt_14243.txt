So this became weirdly personal for me over the last couple weeks. I'm an engineer on a small team that builds support chatbots, and we've been having this argument that will not die: is it fine for our internal docs to say stuff like "the model believes the user already got a refund" or "it thinks this is a duplicate ticket"? Half the team treats that as totally harmless shorthand, but my teammate Priya, who did a philosophy minor, keeps insisting we're smuggling in a real philosophical claim we can't actually justify. I went down a rabbit hole re-reading Dennett on the intentional stance, which is basically the view my other teammate Marcus is unknowingly channeling: you're entitled to say a system "believes" X whenever treating it as a rational believer of X is the strategy that best predicts its behaviour, full stop, no further fact about "real" inner states required (same reasoning Dennett uses to say a thermostat sort of "wants" the room to be 68 degrees). Priya isn't buying that this scales up to our case though -- her point is that the "beliefs" our model reports flip completely depending on how you phrase the prompt, never get updated by anything resembling evidence, and don't persist between queries, so calling that a belief stretches the word past the point of being usefull.

I'd rather see this actually fought out than pick a side myself from vibes. Could you write a dialogue between Marcus and Priya where they go back and forth for real, like 15-20 exchanges, each one responding to what the other just said rather than restating their opening position? I specifically want Marcus to have to face the instability objection head-on instead of dodging it, and I want Priya to have to reckon with the fact that Dennett's whole move was to deflate the real-belief-vs-fake-belief distinction in the first place -- so it's not obvious she gets to just help herself to "these aren't real beliefs" as a knockdown objection. End it somewhere concrete, even if that's just them pinning down exactly where the disagreement actually lives, rather than trailing off into "well, we agree to disagree."
