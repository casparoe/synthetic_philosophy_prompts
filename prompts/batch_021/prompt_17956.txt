Okay, so this happened at a meeting for my grad program's student association last week and I genuinely haven't stopped thinking about it since. I'm a third-year PhD student in political science, and somehow I ended up on the five-person committee that decides which student proposals get funding each semester. Last week's proposal was for a new peer-mentoring program, and per our bylaws we're only allowed to approve something if BOTH (a) it doesn't duplicate a program we already fund, and (b) the organizers already have a faculty sponsor lined up. So we did it the proper way: everyone went around and gave a yes/no on each of those two conditions seperately, and then, as a totally separate question, everyone also gave a plain up-or-down vote on whether the proposal should be approved.

Here's the part that's been bugging me. Two of us thought there's no duplication but no sponsor secured yet; one person thought the exact reverse (sponsor's fine, but it IS a duplicate); and two of us thought both conditions were satisfied. So on the straight up-or-down question, only those last two voted yes, and the proposal lost, 2 to 3. But then our treasurer, who's kind of a bylaws stickler in a good way, pointed out afterward that if you just take a majority vote on each condition on its own, "not a duplicate" wins 4-1 and "sponsor secured" wins 3-2 -- meaning if you mechanically apply our OWN bylaw to those two majority answers, the proposal should actually count as approved! We double checked the tallies twice because it felt like someone must have miscounted, but nope. Nobody was voting strategically, everyone reasoned in good faith, and yet the condition-by-condition tally and the plain bottom-line vote flatly contradict each other. I brought it up with a friend in my cohort who does some formal modeling and she got weirdly excited and said it was "a known thing" in her area, but couldn't remember what it's called or why it happens.

Now half the committe thinks we should obviously just honor the bottom-line vote, because that's what a vote even is, and the other half thinks that's crazy and we should go with the itemized breakdown since checking those two conditions is literally what the bylaw tells us to do. Is there an actual principled reason to prefer one of these over the other, or did we just stumble into a situation where any answer is basically arbitrary? Would love something more substantive than 'it depends' if you've got it.
