I'm assembling a collection of short fiction that dramatizes unsettling results from mathematical logic, in the spirit of Borges's "Library of Babel" or Greg Egan's mathematically minded science fiction, and I'd like the next piece built around Skolem's paradox. The paradox, briefly: because ZFC has only countably many axioms, the Löwenheim–Skolem theorem guarantees that if it has any infinite model at all, it has a countable one — yet that same model still satisfies ZFC's own theorem that uncountable sets exist, since "uncountable" inside the model only means that no bijection between that set and the naturals belongs to the model's own domain; the very bijection that would betray the model's countability from the outside is simply absent from what the model contains. I'd like a story of around 1,500 words that makes a reader feel the disorientation of this relativity, ideally through a character — mathematician or otherwise — who is confronted with the possibility that some totality they had always treated as absolute is, from a vantage point they cannot themselves occupy, merely one countable structure among others. Please have the story dramatize this discovery rather than simply staging a scene in which someone explains the paradox aloud.
