I'm a second-year philosophy PhD student working on distributive justice, and this term I'm auditing a social choice theory sequence in the economics department to shore up my formal background before I start drafting my dissertation. We recently went through the proof of Harsanyi's aggregation theorem, and unlike most "utilitarianism is what rationality demands" arguments I've encountered before, this one looks like an actual theorem rather than an intuition pump: once every individual's preferences over uncertain prospects satisfy the von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms, and the social ordering itself is required to satisfy those same axioms while respecting the Pareto principle, the math forces social welfare to be computed as a sum of individual utilities -- Bentham's felicific calculus, essentially, derived rather than merely asserted. That looks to me like it settles, far more decisively than any purely philosophical argument could, the old dispute between utilitarians and Rawlsians: since maximin can't be the output of that aggregation procedure, anyone who still wants to defend it is either quietly rejecting the Pareto principle or is simply confused about what rationality under uncertainty requires.

Next week I'm presenting a short paper to our reading group defending more or less this line -- that Harsanyi's theorem converts the utilitarian/Rawlsian dispute from a live philosophical disagreement into something closer to a solved problem, with Rawlsians on the losing end of a proof rather than in possession of a genuine alternative. My advisor, who works on Kantian ethics and has no patience for formal methods, waved this away in about ten seconds without actually engaging with the derivation, which wasn't much help. So before I present, I would like help pressure-testing the argument properly: what is the strongest version of a Rawlsian, or otherwise non-utilitarian, reply to Harsanyi here, and does it actually hold up against the theorem, or is it ultimately just a way of refusing to accept an inconvenient mathematical result?
