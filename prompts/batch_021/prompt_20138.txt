Integrated Information Theory (IIT) identifies the degree of consciousness of a physical system with Φ, a formal measure of how much integrated information its causal structure generates beyond what its parts would generate on their own; on this view, whichever substrate realizes a local maximum of Φ just is a conscious subject, and the specific quality of its experience is given by the geometry of that maximally irreducible cause-effect structure. The theory has always been contested, and most recently, in 2023, a large group of consciousness researchers publicly argued that its central claims are unfalsifiable enough to count as pseudoscience. But one of the sharpest and most concrete objections to IIT actually predates that controversy by almost a decade, and turns not on IIT's overall testability but on a single counterexample.

In 2014, the computer scientist Scott Aaronson pointed out, in an exchange with Giulio Tononi carried out on his blog, that nothing in IIT's formalism confines high Φ to brain-like systems. A large grid of simple logic gates, sparsely but very evenly wired together and performing nothing more sophisticated than a simple error-correcting computation, can be constructed so that its Φ vastly exceeds any serious estimate of Φ for the human cortex — which would mean, on IIT's own terms, that the grid is more conscious than a person. Tononi's response was not to revise the theory but to accept the consequence: he argued that intuitions about which systems seem intelligent or human-like are simply not a reliable guide to which systems have experience, so that if the postulates entail a very high Φ for the grid, the right conclusion is that the grid really does have a correspondingly rich, if utterly alien, form of experience.

Write an essay that takes a side on this exchange. Weigh the view that Tononi's bullet-biting is exactly the kind of theoretical honesty that, elsewhere in the history of science, has forced us to accept deeply counterintuitive conclusions (as with relativity or quantum mechanics) against the view that it instead shows IIT has become unfalsifiable in practice, willing to absorb any inconvenient result rather than ever count as refuted. Engage with the strongest form of each position, and close with a definite verdict on whether the grid case should be treated as a fatal problem for IIT, rather than a non-committal summary of the disagreement.
