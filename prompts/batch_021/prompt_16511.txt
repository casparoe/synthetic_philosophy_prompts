I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums long enough that Eliezer Yudkowsky's old quantum mechanics sequence was formative for how I think about interpretations of QM: it argues, quite confidently, that Many-Worlds simply falls out once you refuse to bolt an extra collapse postulate onto the formalism, and it makes that case almost entirely on parsimony grounds. What it doesn't dwell on much is the question I actually care about here, which is locality specifically. Separately, I've followed the recurring disagreement between Scott Aaronson (on Shtetl-Optimized) and Sabine Hossenfelder (on Backreaction) over superdeterminism, which is one of the few places I've seen "which assumption of Bell's theorem are you actually willing to give up" argued out explicitly rather than smuggled in via whichever textbook someone happened to read first. I'd like to force myself to do that comparison properly across the whole field of interpretations, rather than picking a favorite by vibes or by which one my favorite blogger endorses.

For context on what I mean by "assumption": Bell's theorem is usually reconstructed from something like three ingredients, though the exact carving varies by author (Bell's own emphasis differs from Shimony and Jarrett's parameter-independence/outcome-independence split, which differs again from how Maudlin frames "local causality"), so treat the following as a rough working map rather than a fixed taxonomy: (i) locality/local causality, (ii) measurement independence (no correlation between hidden variables and measurement settings, sometimes called the "free choice" or "no-conspiracy" assumption), and (iii) the tacit assumption that a measurement has one single, determinate outcome. Every response to the theorem has to give up at least one of these.

With that framing, please rank the following seven responses to Bell's theorem by how well they preserve a plausible, non-question-begging notion of locality -- not by which is most parsimonious, most deterministic, or most popular among working physicists: Bohmian mechanics; GRW/spontaneous collapse theories; Everettian many-worlds; Copenhagen (orthodox/instrumentalist reading); QBism/relational quantum mechanics; superdeterminism; and retrocausal (time-symmetric) approaches. For each entry, give one sentence naming the specific assumption it rejects and saying whether that rejection genuinely buys back locality or only relocates the problem.
