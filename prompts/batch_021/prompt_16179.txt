I'm a PhD student in formal semantics, and I've been asked to toast my closest friend Priya -- a philosopher of logic -- at her wedding to Sam, also a philosopher of logic. They met arguing about the Law of Non-Contradiction and never really stopped: Priya is a dialetheist who holds that a small, disciplined class of sentences (the Liar sentence, certain claims about the exact instant of change) are genuine truth-value gluts, both true and false, and has built her career on the paraconsistent logics that keep this from collapsing into absurdity; Sam holds the classical line and finds the position barely coherent. About half the guests are logicians who will know exactly what's at stake in that disagreement; the rest are family who know only that the two of them have never agreed on anything and are obviously right for each other anyway. Please write a five-minute toast that uses their disagreement as its spine without implying the marriage resolves it, without letting Priya's view get flattened into "she thinks everything is true" (that's trivialism, a position she's spent her career distinguishing herself from), and without making Sam sound like the closed-minded one. One joke about how their two "I do"s are, for once, a perfectly unhedged truth that neither of them needs to argue about would be welcome, and a second about how, unlike a real contradiction under classical logic, their decades of arguing have never once "exploded" into relitigating every other fight would be a nice bonus -- but it has to work as a toast first and a logic seminar never.
