So I've had this idea bugging me for a while and think it'd make a good short story. Frogs have this hardwired reflex where they snap at any small dark shape that zips across their vision -- usually a fly, but they'll snap at a BB pellet flicked past them just as reliably. That's got me stuck on a question: when the frog snaps at the pellet, is that a mistake, like its brain got the wrong answer about whats out there? Or is snapping at pellets just as much what that reflex does as catching flies, since it fires the same way either time -- meaning maybe we're the ones wrong to call it a "fly detector" to begin with. I can't shake the feeling this is the same question people ask about AI now, where a model trained on some proxy goal starts firing, once its out in the real world, on stuff that has nothing to do with what we thought we trained it for. Is that malfunctioning, or was that its actual goal the whole time and we just misdescribed it? Could you write me a short story, with a creature or an AI character or whatever fits best, that sits right inside this problem, where by the end you genuinely can't tell if the thing at the center is malfunctioning or if we just had the wrong idea of what it was for all along -- and it was never actually "wrong" at all.
