My best recurring source from about fifteen years of monetary-policy stories, a central bank economist, is retiring next month, and somehow I got roped into giving the toast. Half the room is fellow economists who'll catch every reference; the other half is her family, who have never once cared what M2 is. Her whole career kept circling back to one Hume essay, "Of Money" (1752): currency is just a veil, printing more of it cant make a country richer, prices catch up eventually -- except she always loved that Hume himself admits theres a real, if temporary, kick to output and employment in the interval before that happens, and that's basically her whole case for why central banking is worth doing at all. Write me a five-minute toast that uses that veil / short-run-kick / long-run-neutrality tension as the actual backbone, not a name-drop. One rule: no "her influence just keeps multiplying" line -- that's the exact quantity-theory mistake she spent forty years correcting people on.
