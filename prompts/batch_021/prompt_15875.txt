Been down an interpretability rabbit hole lately and something's been bugging me. The Olah-style circuits stuff (curve detectors, "features are the fundamental unit of neural networks," all that) talks about representations like they're objectively *there* in the weights, just waiting to be discovered the way you'd discover a new species. Then polysemanticity/superposition shows up as a wrinkle, and instead of backing off the realism, everyone doubles down with sparse autoencoders to dig out the "true" monosemantic features supposedly hiding underneath the mess. This reminds me a lot of Dennett's old real patterns move (the gliders-in-Conway's-Life essay), where a pattern counts as real just in case describing things that way buys you compression/prediction you wouldnt get from the raw pixel-level story, no extra glider-stuff required. So compare the two for me: is interpretability-realism basically just Dennett's real-patterns move applied to neurons, or is it secretly committed to something more full-blooded (more Fodor, actual sentences etched into the weights)? And does your answer change whether we should treat a feature an SAE finds as a discovered fact about the model, or just a useful fiction we're projecting onto it?
