ok this has been rattling around in my head for weeks so bear with me. i started using habitica (the app where you level up a little avatar by checking off habits, and it dings your health if you skip) back in january, then read a blog post pointing out the whole format is basically lifted from ben franklin's virtue chart in his autobiography. so i went and actually read that section instead of the blog's summary of it - the 13 virtues, the part where his quaker friend informs him hes widely considered proud so he tacks Humility onto the list as damage control, and the bit where he admits Order is the one virtue he could never lick (the "speckled axe" line).

whats bugging me: franklins whole justification for the project was pretty cold and practical, not religious - vice is harmful in itself, not just "forbidden," so training yourself out of it is basically enlightened self-interest, a skill you build through deliberate daily practice. but habitica (and every app like it) is built by people whose business model runs on the exact same streaks-and-dings mechanics that make junk apps addictive, aimed at my lizard brain instead of my judgment, in service of supposedly building virtues like temperance and order. reasoning it out on his own terms, would franklin count something like this as a legit descendant of his project, or would he think the delivery mechanism defeats the purpose before you've even opened the app? i want your actual best reconstruction of his reasoning, not a generic "wise founding father" answer.
