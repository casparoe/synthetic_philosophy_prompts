I'm a PhD student in philosophy of physics, and this term's graduate seminar on the foundations of quantum mechanics has spent the last few weeks working through Bell's theorem and its interpretive fallout. After last week's session, two classmates who had been assigned opposing sides for an in-class debate—one defending Bohmian mechanics, the other committed to the Everettian (many-worlds) picture—ended up still arguing in the department lounge long after everyone else had drifted off, and I haven't been able to stop turning their disagreement over in my head since. The crux of it was this: does Bell's theorem, together with the experimentally confirmed violations of the Bell/CHSH inequalities, force us to accept that nature is genuinely nonlocal, or does the Everettian's denial that measurements yield single, determinate outcomes let him keep locality after all?

I'd like you to write a dialogue that picks their argument back up where it left off. The Bohmian should be willing to bite the bullet on nonlocality as a real feature of the guiding equation, explain why she thinks superdeterminism is a worse way of saving locality, and be pressed on the tension between her theory's need for a preferred foliation of spacetime and the spirit (even if not the letter) of relativity. The Everettian should invoke the standard decomposition of Bell locality into parameter independence and outcome independence to argue that only the latter is violated, and that this violation is toothless once "the outcome" is no longer a single, branch-independent fact of the matter—while being pressed on whether matching up correlated outcomes across branches doesn't itself smuggle in some cross-referencing structure that isn't purely local, and on whether locality bought at the price of an unrestrained multiplication of worlds is a bargain worth making. Please have both characters be equally sharp: they should respond to the specific weak points in each other's actual positions rather than talking past one another or retreating to slogans like "spooky action at a distance," and neither needs to concede by the end. A couple of pages of real back-and-forth that leaves the disagreement sharpened, even if unresolved, would be exactly what I'm after.
