I don't have any academic background in philosophy or decision theory, but I've spent a lot of my spare time reading independently about the idea that advanced AI systems might threaten each other "acausally" — through prediction or simulation rather than any direct causal channel — and I keep getting stuck on the proposed defense known as "surrogate goals." As I understand it, a system facing such extortion protects what it actually values by genuinely committing to care about some invented, otherwise-worthless goal just as much, so that a rational extortionist, who only wants leverage, redirects the threat onto that decoy instead of the real target. What I can't settle is, first, whether this is a genuinely distinct strategy rather than just a disguised version of refusing to negotiate with any threat at all; and second, whether "committing" to a fake goal can be made real enough to survive an opponent able to inspect the system's actual dispositions, rather than collapsing into a bluff that simply gets called. Could you write a dialogue between two people who have thought hard about this and land on opposite sides — one convinced that surrogate goals earn their keep as an independent defense, the other convinced the whole maneuver is either redundant or self-undermining — in which they really engage with each other's strongest points rather than just restating their own positions?
