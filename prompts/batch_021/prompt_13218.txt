In a history-of-science reading group, we have been working through Newton's unpublished theological manuscripts alongside more familiar material like the General Scholium to the Principia, and one thing that keeps coming up is how selectively Newton chose to reveal his views. He kept his anti-Trinitarian convictions hidden for his entire life, even though his own successor in the Lucasian chair, William Whiston, was expelled from Cambridge in 1710 for saying similar things openly. That contrast made me want a clearer sense of how heterodox, and therefore how personally risky, the different strands of Newton's private theology actually were relative to one another, since it is tempting to lump them all together as "Newton's secret religious eccentricities" when in fact some of it was genuinely dangerous and some of it was fairly unremarkable by the standards of his time.
