So I've been re-reading Ecce Homo with my graduate seminar this week - specifically the "Why I Am So Clever" section, where Nietzsche goes on this long, weirdly obsessive riff about nutrition, climate, and digestion, insisting that basically every error of "spirit" traces back to bad instincts about the body: what you eat, where you live, how you recover. He rants about German cuisine ruining the stomach and the spirit alike, praises the lighter Piedmontese cooking he'd taken up in Turin, and has this real hostility toward beer and spirits that he ties to the heaviness of German idealism itself. One of my students half-joked that Nietzsche would've had strong opinions about seed oils and probably run a wellness Substack.

That got me curious about something more substantive though. If Nietzsche somehow encountered the current landscape of dietary science and food culture - gut microbiome research linking digestion to mood and cognition, intermittent fasting as a discipline/self-mastery movement, veganism as a mass ethical-slash-lifestyle movement, the moral panic over ultra-processed foods, keto and carnivore diets marketed on strength and mental clarity grounds - what do you think his actual position would be? I don't want the lazy "Nietzsche = eat steak, be strong" take that gets thrown around online; I want you to reason from what we actually know of his physiological approach to philosophy (which was more about instinct and physiology then about sheer willpower) and, frankly, from his own lifelong digestive misery, about which of these movements he'd have found kindred and which he'd have mocked as just another form of ressentiment or ascetic self-denial dressed up as science. Curious where you land, and why.
