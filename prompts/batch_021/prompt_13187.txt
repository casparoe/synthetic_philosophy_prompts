I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and next week I'm interviewing a decision theorist who defends superrationality: the view that in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma between two ideally rational, identically-reasoning agents, both should cooperate, since each can reason that whatever he does, an identically-reasoning counterpart does too -- making mutual cooperation and mutual defection the only self-consistent outcomes, and cooperation the better one. Draft seven or eight interview questions that push past a bare restatement of the position: I want at least one question each on how this cooperative outcome is supposed to survive the standard dominance objection (that defecting does better no matter what the opponent does), on how much work 'identically reasoning' is doing once we move from exact twins to ordinary humans who merely think alike, and on whether the whole argument just smuggles in Kantian universalizability under a game-theoretic label -- and for each, a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
