Ok so I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole on imprecise/interval-valued credences as the "honest" alternative to forcing a single Bayesian number out of yourself when you're deeply uncertain about something (partly trying to figure out if the old LW hostility to "Knightian uncertainty" as a cop-out was ever fair). Anyway, I hit a worked example where conditioning a credal set on new evidence made the interval WIDER instead of narrower, no matter which way the evidence actually came out. That seems completely backwards to me — isnt the whole point of updating on evidence that you become less uncertain, not more? Feels like this has to be a bug in how the framework handles conditionalization rather then something an actually rational agent should do. Am I missing something, or is this just a real flaw that makes imprecise probabilities less useful than advertised?
