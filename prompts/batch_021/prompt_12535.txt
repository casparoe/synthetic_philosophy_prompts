My partner and I just went through our first failed IVF cycle, and I can't stop turning over the odds we were given. Our consent form cited a 38 percent per-cycle success rate for women in my age bracket, and I assumed that this number simply represented our chance of success. But when I pressed our reproductive endocrinologist on what it actually meant, she told me the 38 percent describes patients "like me" only loosely, and that her own considered estimate for our next cycle -- based on my hormone panel and embryo grading -- is somewhat higher than that. A close friend who works in biostatistics dismissed both numbers, insisting there is some fixed underlying chance belonging to a given embryo in a given cycle, and that the clinic's statistic and the doctor's estimate are each just imperfect attempts to track that hidden quantity. I don't know whether the three of them are actually disagreeing about something or just describing the same situation in different vocabularies. More importantly, I don't know whether it matters for the question now in front of us: does a failed cycle give us any real reason to expect the next one will go differently? Could you help me untangle what's really at stake between these three ways of talking about our odds, and whether resolving it should change how we reason about trying again?
