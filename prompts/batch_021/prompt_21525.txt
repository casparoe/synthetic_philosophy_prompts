ok so a lot of LW-sphere discourse about mind uploading and "substrate independence" (Age of Em, Yudkowsky's GAZP vs GLUT post, all that) basically just takes Putnam-style multiple realizability for granted -- if the causal/functional organization is preserved, it genuinely shouldn't matter whether its neurons or transistors doing the realizing. but i went down a rabbit hole a while ago and found out theres a whole strand of philosophy of science (Bechtel & Mundale, i think, though don't quote me on that) arguing multiple realizability is way overstated -- that once neuroscientists get specific about how they actually individuate neural states across species, the "different" realizers of "the same" psychological state converge at the right grain of description, so the classic textbook examples (silicon martians, octopus pain, whatever) are kind of cheating on the grain issue.

can you write me an essay that (1) lays out the classic Putnam/Fodor multiple-realizability case for functionalism and against reductive type-identity theory, (2) gives the neuroscience-based pushback a fair hearing, and (3) actually commits to a verdict -- does MR survive in strong enough form to keep grounding the standard rationalist assumptions about substrate-independent minds and uploads, or should the pushback push us toward taking some form of type-identity (or at least a much more restricted functionalism) seriously? no "both sides have a point" cop-outs, pick a side and defend it.
