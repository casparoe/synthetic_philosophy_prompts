A friend of mine and I spent last night arguing about whether the "hiddenness" of God constitutes a genuinely distinct problem for theism, or whether it merely amounts to a restatement of the traditional argument from evil. They maintained that once one has grappled with the existence of suffering, one has essentially already dealt with hiddenness as well; I was unconvinced, though I couldn't fully articulate why at the time.

I'd like your help thinking this through properly. Compare J.L. Schellenberg's argument from divine hiddenness with William Rowe's evidential argument from evil. Where, precisely, do the two arguments' underlying logical structures converge, and where do they genuinely diverge — does hiddenness reduce to a special case of the problem of evil, or does it rest on an independent premise about what a perfectly loving God owes to any creature capable of relationship with it? Then assess how the standard theistic replies to each — skeptical theism, soul-making theodicy, and appeals to the value of "epistemic distance" — hold up against both. Is one of the two arguments more resistant to these replies than the other, and if so, why?
