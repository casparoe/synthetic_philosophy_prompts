Got into it with my friend last night for like two hours about Molinism and neither of us actually convinced the other. He's Reformed and kept saying middle knowledge is just Calvinism with extra steps: if there's already a true counterfactual about exactly what I'd freely do in every possible circumstance, fixed before God even decides to create anything, then it was never really up to me to begin with. I tried arguing the counterfactual is grounded in me, in what I'd actually freely choose, but he kept hammering the grounding objection and honestly i didn't have a clean answer. Can you write a dialogue between a Molinist and a Calvinist theological determinist hashing this out properly? I want the determinist to press both the grounding objection and the "fixity" problem (if it's already true what you'll do, how could you ever have done otherwise) as hard as possible, and I want the Molinist to have real substantive comebacks, not just "God's ways are mysterious." Bonus if they also get into whether feasible worlds and transworld depravity actually rescue the free will defense against evil, or just push the problem back a step. Neither one should be a pushover - they need to be equally sharp and actually engage with each other's points, not talk past each other.
