During a stand-up meeting last week, I told the team there was roughly a 20 percent chance our upcoming database migration would cause a customer-facing outage, basing that figure on the fact that one of our last five comparable migrations had gone badly. A colleague pushed back, arguing that the number was essentially meaningless: this particular migration will happen exactly once, so there is no long run for "20 percent" to describe, and he added that treating "the last five migrations" as the relevant group to compare against was itself arbitrary, since slicing our migration history a different way would have produced a different percentage. I still believe the figure was doing real work, since we used it to decide whether to schedule extra on-call coverage that night, but once he pressed me on it I found I couldn't say clearly what the 20 percent actually referred to. Was he right that it makes no sense to assign a probability to a one-time event like this, or is there a defensible way to understand what that number meant?
