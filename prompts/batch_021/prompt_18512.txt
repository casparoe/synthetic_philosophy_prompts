There's a minor genre of rationalist-blogosphere writing about the "hidden" Newton: the man who spent more hours on biblical chronology and prophecy than on the Principia. Less often discussed is what that research actually led him to conclude. Newton privately held that the Trinity was a post-biblical corruption engineered largely by Athanasius at and after Nicaea, and that worshipping Christ as co-equal with the Father was therefore a species of idolatry; he folded this corruption into the "apostasy" foretold in Daniel and Revelation, used the historicist "day-for-a-year" principle to convert the relevant prophetic periods into a chronology of the church's decline, and on that basis privately arrived at 2060 as the earliest possible date for the Apocalypse. Tellingly, he framed that figure as a rebuke to the "fanciful men" who were "frequently predicting the time of the end" and thereby brought prophecy into disrepute, rather than as a prediction of his own.

Reasoning from these specific commitments rather than generic piety, how do you think Newton would size up today's secular counterpart -- AI-risk researchers who likewise produce specific-sounding timelines for transformative or catastrophic outcomes, and who worry, in nearly his own terms, that a failed forecast will discredit the whole field? Would he recognize a legitimate secular heir to the prophetic-historical reasoning he thought he was purifying of corruption, or exactly the presumption he spent his life condemning? And given his account of how doctrine gets corrupted once entangled with institutional power, what would he expect from the AI labs and governments now positioned as stewards of this risk?
