I'm one of about four people who maintain a small internal Lean library at my company -- basically a tiny in-house mathlib we use to verify parts of a job scheduler. Someone opened a PR with a lemma that says any partial schedule can be extended to a maximal one, proved via Zorn's Lemma (treat partial schedules as a poset ordered by extension, chains get an upper bound by just unioning them, so a maximal element exists). Because that proof bottoms out in choice, Lean forces the definition to be marked noncomputable. Half the maintainers are fine merging it since the theorem's true and we actually need it, the other half think noncomputable lemmas shouldn't live in the same library as our executable scheduler code, since it blurs the line between "this exists" and "here's how to actually build it," and once that norm slides people stop noticing the difference. Our contribution guide says nothing about this case. I've gotta send a short memo before next weeks review recommending one of: merge as-is, quarantine anything noncomputable into its own module, or reject and require a constructive version. Can you draft that memo -- actually pick one, give the reasoning, and don't just wave away the quarantine side as being precious about purity, there's something real going on there so take it seriously.
