Two people in my graduate reading group on free will argued for most of an evening after a session on Galen Strawson's "Basic Argument" against moral responsibility, and I couldn't settle who came out ahead. Mateo's opening move: we act on the character and motives we have; we never chose that character, since any deliberate attempt to shape it would itself flow from some earlier, equally unchosen character; so no one is ever ultimately responsible for anything they do -- and this holds even where choices are undetermined, since a random neural swerve is luck, not agency. Sasha's reply: this makes literal self-creation from nothing the price of responsibility, a standard not even a self-made, omnipotent being could meet, and an unsatisfiable criterion counts against the criterion, not in favor of calling responsibility a myth; what responsibility actually tracks is the ordinary difference between reasons-responsive deliberation and acting under compulsion or delusion. Mateo's rejoinder: this concedes rather than refutes his point -- he never denied that ordinary difference, only that it secures the kind of desert that would make retributive blame or praise deserved for its own sake, and that even a self-made god would fail to clear that bar, which only confirms such desert was never on offer. Sasha's close: if the "true" responsibility Mateo means is one nobody invokes in blaming a friend for breaking a promise, then his argument, however valid, hasn't shown what its title advertises -- it has only retired a notion of desert ordinary moral life never needed. Go through the exchange move by move, say who has the better of it at each step and why, flag anything important both of them are missing, and end with a one-line verdict: \verdict{Mateo}, \verdict{Sasha}, or \verdict{Draw}.
