I write novels for a living, and for the last couple years I've also sat on the small board that runs the Kestrel Fellowship -- we give eight emerging novelists a stipend and a year of mentorship, kind of like a mini writers' colony except longer and with actual money attached. Renewal into year two has always been decided by the four of us re-reading each fellow's new pages every quarter and just talking it out, which two of my fellow board members think is basically a popularity contest wearing a literary judgment costume. So this year the director proposed we track two numbers automatically going forward: net monthly word count pulled straight from each fellow's shared drive, and a monthly 'buzz score,' where a rotating panel of thirty volunteer beta readers rates each fellow's new pages 1 to 5 through an app and we average it.

Here's the case thats splitting us (and honestly eating up time I should be spending on my own draft, which is already late). Dana is crushing both numbers -- 20k+ words most months, buzz score sitting around 4.6 -- but her new chapters read to me like she's reverse-engineering what the panel wants: a cliffhanger every three pages, a whole subplot that appeared the same week her score dipped, sentences getting shorter and punchier by the month. Marcus deleted 200 pages in month four after realizing his structure didn't work, so his word count is negative for the quarter and his buzz score is a so-so 3.1, but the twenty pages he kept are the best thing anyone on the board has read all year. Two board members want the numbers to just settle renewal from now on, since our old 'gut feeling' system let personal taste (and, lets be honest, a bit of favoritism) creep in for years, and a number can't play favorites. I'm not so sure, but I don't want to wave that bias worry away either just because I happen to like Marcus's pages more. We meet in twelve days to lock in the new policy -- could you draft the memo I'll circulate beforehand? I need an actual recommendation (keep the numbers, drop them, reweight them, whatever you land on) with a timeline for phasing it in, a concrete read on how it plays out for Dana and Marcus specifically under whatever you propose, and a real engagement with the 'a number can't play favorites' argument instead of a quick dismissal of it.
