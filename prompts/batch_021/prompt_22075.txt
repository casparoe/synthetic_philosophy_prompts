Ok this has been rattling around in my head for a while. In inverse reinforcement learning / preference learning for alignment (think Stuart Russell's "assistance games" pitch), you try to recover someone's reward function just from watching what they do. Thing is, behavior alone massively underdetermines the reward - for literally any policy, there's a perfectly rational agent pursuing some reward R that would produce it, but also a biased/noisy "planner" pursuing a totally different reward R' that produces that exact same policy, and you can't tell them apart from the actions alone. I'm pretty sure theres even a no-free-lunch-ish result out there (Armstrong and Mindermann? i think) showing no simplicity prior over "how humans are biased" reliably breaks that symmetry either.

Could you write me a dialogue between two alignment researchers hashing this out at a whiteboard after a seminar, each trying to convince the other they've got the right read on the situation? One's a reward-learning optimist who thinks a reasonable prior over human biases, plus tons of varied behavioral data, still gets preference learning far enough for practical alignment purposes. The other's an identifiability skeptic who thinks it's hopeless in principle, since any prior you pick over "bias" is arbitrary and just smuggles in its own answer to the underdetermined mess. I want them actually responding to each other's strongest counterarguments, not just restating their own positions, maybe having them fight it out over a concrete case like whether a daily smoker "wants" to smoke or is just being steered by something like hyperbolic discounting, so it doesn't float off into pure abstraction.
