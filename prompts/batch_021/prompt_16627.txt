Question: Explain why, for Schopenhauer, music has a fundamentally different relationship to the Will than the other arts do. Your answer should cover how the other arts relate to the Platonic Ideas, how music differs from them, and what the bass/harmony/melody structure of music is supposed to correspond to.

Student answer: For Schopenhauer, the phenomenal world is just the Will objectifying itself at different grades, and the Platonic Ideas are the direct expression of those grades - the lowest grades (gravity, rigidity, fluidity) show up in architecture, the plant and animal kingdoms in landscape painting and animal sculpture, and the Idea of the human being, in all his suffering and striving, is expressed most completely in tragedy and the other narrative arts. Music is the highest of the arts because it expresses these same Ideas in an even purer, more universal way, without needing any particular material form to carry them - it gives us the Idea itself rather than one more copy of it. This is essentially the distinction Schopenhauer draws, which Nietzsche later labels Apollonian and Dionysian: the other arts stay bound to the principle of individuation, while music dissolves it and lets the will's underlying, formless striving through directly. It's also why he analyses music into three parts: the bass corresponds to the lowest, inorganic grades of the will's objectification, the inner voices (harmony) to the plant and animal kingdoms, and the melody - the single connected voice carrying the piece from beginning to end - to the intellectual life and conscious striving of human beings, so a piece of music mirrors the whole hierarchy of nature in miniature.
