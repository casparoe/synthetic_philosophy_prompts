I write a column on the philosophical underpinnings of AI research for a magazine that covers science and ideas, and I've booked a recorded conversation with a researcher who has spent years developing the "embedded agency" framework: the claim that real decision-makers, unlike the idealized agents of classical game and decision theory, are physically part of the world they are modeling and acting on, rather than sitting outside it and exchanging clean inputs and outputs across a well-defined boundary. In the handful of prior interviews of hers I've read, she reaches for the same tidy image whenever things get technical -- something like "the map has to be smaller than the territory it sits inside of" -- and then moves on before anyone can press her on what actually follows from that. I'd like questions that don't let her stop at the slogan.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions, each keyed to a specific technical commitment of the framework rather than the general picture. I want at least one question on why the realizability assumption behind ordinary Bayesian sequence prediction breaks down once an agent has to be smaller than the environment it is modeling, and what she thinks should replace it; one on the tiling-agents problem, and specifically the Löbian obstacle to an agent ever fully trusting a future or more capable version of itself; one on what a "counterfactual" over one's own action can even mean when that action is just the deterministic output of a piece of source code embedded in the same universe it is reasoning about (the old "5-and-10" example seems relevant here); and one on how current worries about mesa-optimizers and inner misalignment in trained models relate back to the original subsystem-alignment cluster, or whether that connection has been oversold in alignment circles. For each question, add a short follow-up keyed to the deflection she is likeliest to reach for -- especially any retreat back to the map/territory line -- and close the set with one question asking what result, from ordinary computer science or mathematical logic, would have to come out differently before she would admit that embedded agency isn't really a distinct cluster of problems, just classical decision theory with better branding.
