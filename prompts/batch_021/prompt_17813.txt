Ok so this is something I've been chewing on for a while and I can't find anyone who actually argues it out instead of giving some wishy-washy "well it's complicated" non-answer. It started because my aunt got told by her doctor that she's "prediabetic" - her blood sugar is a bit over some cutoff number but she feels completely fine, no symptoms at all - which got me wondering if that's a real disease or just a line someone decided to draw on a chart. Then i went down a wikipedia rabbit hole and found out about a doctor in the 1800s (Cartwright, I think his name was) who diagnosed enslaved people who kept trying to escape with an actual "disease" called drapetomania, complete with causes and treatments, totally straight-faced. Obviously that's just racism dressed up as medicine, but it made me actually try to work out what separates a real disease from a made-up one like that. And then I thought of a case that messes with my head even more: menopause. Your body's reproductive stuff basically stops working, which sounds exactly like the kind of "part not doing its job" thing that should count as a disease, except it's also completely normal and universal for that age, so is it one or isn't it?

Turns out there's a real split in how people answer the bigger question of what disease even is, and I'd love to see it actually fought out properly. One side says its basically an objective, value-free biological/statistical fact - something counts as diseased when it's functioning abnormally compared to what bodies like yours (same age, same sex, same species) are statistically "supposed" to do, roughly meaning "helps you survive and reproduce the way most bodies like yours do," no opinions needed anywhere. The other side says that move never actually works, that "normal functioning" always ends up smuggling in some judgment about what we value or what counts as a harm, so you can never fully wash the values out of it. Could you write me a dialogue between two people who each hold one of these views and are genuinely good at defending it - I don't want either one to be a pushover, I want the strongest version of both sides actually going back and forth, responding to each other's specific points (menopause, drapetomania, my aunt's blood sugar, or whatever else you think fits) instead of just each giving a monologue.

One more thing that's been nagging me: I wonder if part of this fight is really just two people using the word "disease" to mean different things - like maybe one of them is talking about some pure biological fact and the other is really talking about "illness," in the sense of whether you deserve sympathy, or sick leave, or treatment. If that seems like a real possibility to you, I'd like the dialogue to poke at it a bit near the end, but don't force it if you think the disagreement is genuinely substantive and not just verbal.
