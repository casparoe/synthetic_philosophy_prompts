My friend Dave is throwing a little get-together this weekend to mark the fact that he officially quit his job for good — he hit his "number" a few months back (FIRE-community jargon for the net worth where you never technically have to work again) and finally pulled the trigger at 39. Half the people coming are fellow personal-finance/FIRE nerds like me, the other half is his family, especially his parents, who I don't think have come around to this at all — I get the vibe they think he's thrown something away, or is being a little irresponsible, even though the math clearly works out fine. He asked me to say a few words at the party, no pressure.

Here's my problem: Dave has spent like three years obsessed with this old Keynes essay, "Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren," to the point where its basically his personal scripture, and he's made all of us read it too. Everyone in our group chat can recite "Three-hour shifts or a fifteen-hour week" from memory at this point, it's a running joke. So if I get up there and do the obvious toast — congrats Dave, no more grind, even Keynes saw this coming back in 1930 — it's going to land completely flat for the FIRE crowd because they've heard him say it a hundred times already, and it's not going to do anything for his parents either, who have no idea who Keynes is and would just hear "some old economist thinks not working is fine," which doesn't actually touch what's bothering them.

What I'd actually love is a short toast (3-4 minutes, spoken out loud) that uses the parts of that essay Dave never quotes — the parts where Keynes sounds way more nervous about all this than the meme version suggests, like his bit about people with money and free time often handling it badly, or about how hard it might be to lose the exact thing that's organized your whole effort up to now. I want it to actually say something real to Dave about the harder task ahead, not just congratulate him, but it still needs to work as a toast at a party — warm, a little funny, done in a few minutes — and it can't depend on his parents having read a word of Keynes to still land for them.
