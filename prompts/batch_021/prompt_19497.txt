I don't have any formal background in economics or philosophy, but something happened last year in a small trading group I'm part of, and I still haven't been able to make full sense of it. A handful of us collect vintage board games and get together every couple of months to trade pieces from our collections, sometimes adding cash on top when one person's game feels like it's worth noticeably more than the other's. Last spring I traded a game I'd owned for years to a friend, David, for one of his, and threw in twenty dollars because his felt like the clearly better game to me at the time. A couple of months later I traded that new game to another friend, Priya, for one of hers, again adding cash because hers seemed like the upgrade. Then, at our most recent meetup, David showed up with a copy of the very game I'd started with, and once again it felt like the better deal, so I traded for it and paid a bit more on top. It was only afterward, laying the whole year out on paper, that I realized I owned exactly the collection I'd started with, just about seventy dollars lighter.

Nobody tricked me, and at no individual moment did I feel like I was making a bad call — each trade genuinely felt like an upgrade given how I was weighing the games against each other at that particular time. A friend who reads a lot about game theory and economics heard the story and told me it proves that the way I compare and rank things must be inconsistent in some deeper sense, even though he couldn't point to any single trade where I actually went wrong. That explanation doesn't fully satisfy me: if every choice was reasonable on its own terms at the moment I made it, it seems strange to call the whole episode irrational, and yet I can't pretend I didn't end up worse off through nothing but my own decisions. I've also been wondering whether it would have mattered if I had somehow seen the whole pattern coming right after the first trade — would recognizing the loop in advance have made it irrational to keep trading at each step, in a way that it apparently wasn't for me at the time, or does foresight not really change anything here? I'd like to actually understand what was going on, rather than just being told I was being silly.
