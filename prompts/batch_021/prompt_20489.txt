Something has been nagging at me. Whenever I read about how logic or philosophy formally model belief or knowledge — in modal logic, say, or in descriptions of "common knowledge" in game theory — the agent involved is treated as automatically believing every logical consequence of what they believe. So if I believe a handful of axioms, then on paper I already believe every theorem that follows from them, including ones nobody has ever worked out. I've seen this called the problem of "logical omniscience," and it's obviously not how real thinking works, but apparently it's surprisingly hard to build a formal system that avoids it without breaking something else. Could you write me a joke that turns on exactly this gap, between an "ideal believer" who is stuck believing every logical consequence of their beliefs and how actual people believe things? I'd like the humor to depend on genuinely understanding what the problem is, rather than just being a generic joke about logicians or nerds.
