Been having an argument with a friend of mine who works in AI safety about what it even means for a model to "lie" or be "deceptive," and I can't shake the feeling that the term gets used way too loosely. Like, on one hand you've got all this talk about "deceptive alignment" — a future system hiding its true goals during training so it can do something else once deployed — and that sounds like real, intentional deception, the kind that requires the system to actually have goals and beliefs and to be tracking the gap between what it wants you to think and what's actually true. But then on the other hand, when current chatbots confidently make up a fake citation, or tell you what you want to hear instead of what's true (sycophancy), people call that "lying" or "hallucinating" too, even though there's no obvious sense in which the model believes anything or is trying to pull one over on you. I vaguely remember some paper, maybe called "ChatGPT is Bullshit" or something like that, arguing this stuff is closer to Frankfurt-style bullshit — text produced with zero regard for truth either way — than to lying, since lying requires tracking the truth well enough to hide it.

So here's what I actually want: an essay that settles on a definition of honesty/deception that's actually usable for evaluating AI systems — one that either unifies both cases above under a single concept, or explains why they need to be kept seperate (e.g. "deception" gets reserved for the strategic, goal-directed case, and something else — bullshit, confabulation, whatever — covers the rest). Walk through the main candidate approaches — purely behavioral/functional definitions that don't care what's going on inside the system, versus ones that require something belief-like in there — and the problems with each. But don't just land on "both sides have a point" — actually pick a definition, defend it against the obvious objections, and spell out what it implies in practice: does a sycophantic model count as dishonest? What about one that fabricates a source with total confidence? Would either of these count as "lying" on your view, or does that word only really belong to the deceptive-alignment scenario?
