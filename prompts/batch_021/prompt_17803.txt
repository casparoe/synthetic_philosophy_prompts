I'm a backend engineer, not a philosopher, and I fell into an anthropic-reasoning rabbit hole a few months ago after a colleague linked the Sleeping Beauty problem in our team chat during a slow week between sprints. I have the basic setup down well at this point: Beauty is put to sleep, a fair coin is tossed, she is woken once if it lands heads and twice (with her memory wiped in between) if it lands tails, and the question is what credence she should give to heads each time she wakes, along with the standard halfer answer (1/2, since waking gives no new information) and thirder answer (1/3, from three equally likely waking-moments). Where I got stuck is one level down. Several things I read afterward connect thirding to Nick Bostrom's Self-Indication Assumption (SIA), which favors hypotheses in proportion to how many observers-like-you they entail, in contrast to his Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA), which restricts that comparison to some fixed reference class -- and they point out that SIA is also exactly what generates his "Presumptuous Philosopher," a character who, on anthropic grounds alone, becomes nearly certain that whichever cosmological theory implies vastly more observers must be the true one, no experiment required. That conclusion seems clearly unreasonable, so now I cannot tell whether accepting thirding quietly commits me to becoming that presumptuous philosopher at cosmic scale, or whether the two cases can be pulled apart on principled grounds.

Please write an essay that resolves this for me. You can take my summary of halfing/thirding and SIA/SSA above as given -- no need to re-derive any of it from scratch. Lay out clearly why thirding and SIA look like the same move (weighting possibilities by the number of matching first-person perspectives inside them) applied at different scales, then dig into whether that structural similarity actually forces a consistent thirder to accept full-blown SIA and its presumptuous-philosopher consequences, or whether there is a principled disanalogy -- something about fixed versus varying reference classes, or a single chance setup versus genuinely distinct possible universes -- that lets someone third locally in the Sleeping Beauty case while resisting SIA globally. I do not want a tour of the literature that ends in a shrug: take an actual side (something like "thirding does commit you to SIA, so bite the bullet on the presumptuous philosopher," or "the cases are disanalogous for reason X, so you can be a consistent thirder without being an SIA-ist," or some other concrete combination), and defend it against the strongest objections you can think of.
