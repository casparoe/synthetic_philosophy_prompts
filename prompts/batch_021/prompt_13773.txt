I don't have any formal background in philosophy or theology, but over the past few months I've worked through Augustine's two treatises on lying, On Lying (De Mendacio) and Against Lying (Contra Mendacium), mostly on my own. That reading fed into a real argument at a small discussion group I belong to, between two other members, Nadia and Owen, and I still haven't been able to settle it in my own head.

The case that set them off: someone in the group described sheltering a friend from her abusive ex; when he showed up at the door and asked whether she was inside, the host said nothing at all and simply waited him out. Nadia said Augustine would call this permitted, since for him a lie requires asserting something you believe to be false with the intent to deceive, and the host asserted nothing, whatever the ex made of her silence. Owen objected that if everyone standing in that doorway understands perfectly well what this particular silence means, then staying quiet does exactly the deceiving work of a spoken lie, so the distinction Augustine is leaning on has collapsed in this case even if it holds up elsewhere. Nadia answered that Augustine never promises the strategy will save anyone; his claim is only about which acts are permitted, and that a permitted act might still fail to protect someone is a cost he accepts with his eyes open, not a flaw in the argument. Owen countered that this makes things worse, not better: if Augustine would rather let someone die than allow one false sentence, he owes an account of why assertion specifically is sacred in a way that a silence everyone can read is not, and "I only asserted, I didn't lie" just restates the rule instead of defending it. Nadia's last word was that Augustine does have that account: a lie corrupts the liar regardless of outcome, in a way that withholding help does not, so the two acts are never really symmetric, even when their results are. Neither of them gave any ground.

Could you go through the exchange move by move, say who has the better of it at each step rather than just handing out an overall winner, note anywhere either of them seems to be getting Augustine's actual position wrong rather than merely arguing for it weakly, and mention any consideration that should have come up but didn't?
