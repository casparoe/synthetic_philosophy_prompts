I'm a junior on my high school's Honor Council, the elected student body that hears academic-integrity cases and recommends a sanction to the Assistant Principal, who almost always adopts whatever we send up. This fall I'm also taking a semester-long elective called Existentialism and Ethics, and the unit on Sartre landed right in the middle of a case the council is about to decide, which has made it hard for me to think about the case in any terms other than the ones from class.

Here's the case. A sophomore was caught turning in a chemistry lab report copied almost line for line from a friend's. When the council asked her about it, she said copying labs has quietly been "just what our group does" since September and that she "wasn't going to be the one to break that." When a council member pushed further and asked whether she could, in that moment, simply have refused, she said that in the abstract, sure, but "that's not a version of me that exists in that friend group, so no, not really." Our honor code says the council "may weigh the student's degree of individual agency in the violation" when recommending a sanction, but it has never been written down what that means, and the council is stuck. About half of us keep coming back to the café waiter from Sartre's account of bad faith, the one who plays at being a waiter a little too perfectly, as though he simply were his role rather than someone freely sustaining it moment to moment, and think her whole defense is a version of the same move, so it shouldn't lighten the sanction at all. The other half worries that refusing to weigh her situation at all smuggles in an equally one-sided picture of freedom, since even Sartre insists we are only ever free in situation and never free from one, and since actual research on adolescent conformity suggests the friend-group dynamic she is describing is a real, if partial, constraint rather than just a story she is telling us.

Could you draft the memo I will send to the rest of the council before we vote on Thursday? I need it to recommend one specific sanction from the range our code allows — a formal warning, a grade penalty on the assignment, or the assignment grade plus a probation period — and to propose a short, usable standard the council could apply to "individual agency" in future cases, so we are not relitigating the same disagreement every time it comes up. Please take the mitigation side's argument on its own terms rather than resolving the case by simply declaring her "really" free and moving past it.
