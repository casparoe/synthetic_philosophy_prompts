I spent 35 years as a chemist (reaction kinetics, mostly), and only in retirement did I start reading philosophy of physics properly, which is how I ended up down a rabbit hole on Boltzmann's old fight with Zermelo over the recurrence objection. What strikes me is that Boltzmann's own way out of it, suggesting our whole observable universe is just a rare statistical fluctuation out of a mostly-equilibrium background, is basically the seed of what cosmologists now call the Boltzmann brain problem: since small fluctuations are wildly more probable then big ones, that same logic should predict a lone deluded brain with false memories far more often than a whole coherent universe like ours. So if you sat Boltzmann down today and walked him through the modern version, eternal inflation, de Sitter vacuum, freak brains outnumbering real observers and all, do you reckon he'd recognize it as just his own argument against Zermelo pushed to its logical end, or would something about the modern cosmological picture genuinely have caught him off guard? Talk me through how you think he'd reason it out, and where his 19th century statistical instincts would help or mislead him compared to how physicists handle it today.
