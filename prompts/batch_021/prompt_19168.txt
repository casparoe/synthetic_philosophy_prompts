I'm a PhD student in philosophy of language, and this year I've ended up, mostly by accident, as secretary of my department's graduate committee, which among its duller duties administers the Frege Prize: a small annual award, endowed a few decades ago, for the best graduate essay in logic or philosophy of language. The trouble started during a reading group on the Grundgesetze, when someone raised Frege's 1924 diary -- the one that surfaced among his papers only after his death and, alongside an old man's ordinary complaints about the Weimar Republic, sets out a genuinely virulent antisemitism, including the view that Jews should be stripped of political rights. Even Michael Dummett, who did more than any other twentieth-century philosopher to place Frege at the center of the analytic canon, has said he found the diary distressing to read.

The committee is now split, and not just on what to do but on what the diary is even evidence of. Two members want the prize quietly renamed, on the grounds that continuing to award it in Frege's name is itself a kind of endorsement, whatever we conclude about the logic. Two others call that a category error: the diary, they say, is biography with no demonstrated bearing on the actual content of the Begriffsschrift or the theory of sense and reference, and they worry about the precedent of judging a name by the worst thing its bearer ever privately wrote, especially given how many other departmental namesakes would fail that test. Nobody on the committee has cleanly separated the empirical question -- whether the diary tells us anything about the philosophy itself, as opposed to the man who happened to write both -- from the normative one, which is whether the answer to that question even settles what we should do about the prize.

I've been asked to draft the recommendation the full committee will vote on before nominations reopen this spring, and I'd rather not just hand them a summary of the disagreement they already have. Could you write that memo? It should come down on one concrete option -- keep the name as is, retire it, or keep it while adding some standing acknowledgment of the diary -- and it needs to represent the strongest version of whichever side it doesn't recommend, rather than the version that makes that side look like it hasn't reckoned with Frege's politics.
