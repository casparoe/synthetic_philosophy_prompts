I've been sitting in on a graduate seminar on non-classical logic this term, largely at the urging of a friend finishing her PhD in the department, who promised the unit on dialetheism would be the most disorienting thing I'd encounter all year. She was right. For the first few weeks I found the case for accepting true contradictions genuinely compelling: given fairly minimal assumptions about truth and self-reference, the Liar sentence seems to force a choice between ad hoc-looking restrictions — a Tarskian hierarchy, a context-sensitive truth predicate — and simply admitting that it is both true and false, and Priest's paraconsistent line started to look like the least costly way out. What has unsettled that confidence is the week we spent on Curry's paradox, where triviality follows from nothing but a conditional, self-reference, and the structural rule of contraction — no negation, and so no contradiction, ever has to appear on stage. Our instructor mentioned that Priest treats giving structurally similar paradoxes a uniform solution as close to a non-negotiable constraint on any adequate theory of truth, and usually presents this as one of dialetheism's chief advantages over rival, more piecemeal treatments; a classmate countered that Curry is exactly where that constraint turns against the dialetheist, since blocking it seems to require revising contraction rather than tolerating any new glut, so whatever is doing the real work against Curry has nothing to do with contradictions being acceptable after all.

I'd like to bring a more carefully worked-out version of that objection back to the seminar next week. Could you construct the strongest argument you can for the claim that Curry's paradox shows dialetheism cannot deliver the uniform solution to the paradoxes of self-reference that Priest promises, because whatever independently blocks Curry — most likely some restriction on contraction — is a resource just as available to a paracomplete or substructural theorist who never accepts a single true contradiction? Please also engage directly with the most natural dialetheist reply, that a properly paraconsistent, non-contracting conditional was always part of the original package and that restricting contraction is therefore a development of the dialetheist diagnosis rather than a quiet retreat from it, and say plainly whether you find that reply successful.
