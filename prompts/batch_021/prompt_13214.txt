So this is a case of my two jobs colliding in a way I didn't expect. I teach a graduate seminar on shared agency (lots of Bratman, some Gilbert, a bit of Tuomela) and I'm also doing my mandatory stint on the university's Academic Integrity Board, and this week's case is basically a seminar reading come to life. Four students in a research methods course submitted a joint lab report with two fabricated data columns. Two of them cooked the numbers up together over text, which isn't really in dispute - max sanction, no argument from me there. A third found the thread two days before the deadline, said nothing, and let her name go on the final report; the board's mostly settled on something serious but a notch below the first two. The real fight is over the fourth student, who wrote his own section using his own honestly-collected data, was never added to the thread, and says his group leader told him "just do your part, we've got the rest handled," which he says he took at face value and never questioned further.

Our code says all members of a joint submission are "jointly and severally responsible for its integrity, absent clear and convincing evidence of non-participation," which was obviously written with copy-paste plagiarism rings in mind and gives us zero guidance for a case like this one. Half the board wants to hold student four to the same standard as the other three, on the grounds that "I never asked what my groupmates were doing" cant become a free pass - it just teaches everyone to cultivate convenient ignorance; the other half wants him cleared outright, on the grounds that he never shared an intention to submit fabricated data with anyone - whatever the other three did, it was never jointly his to begin with. I keep thinking this is exactly the fault line between Gilbert, where a joint commitment binds you to a venture in ways you didn't fully sign up for once you've knowingly thrown in your lot with the others, and Bratman, where nothing is genuinely shared unless there's real mutual responsiveness to that specific part of the plan - and the two pictures pull the case in opposite directions. Could you draft the memo I'm supposed to circulate before we vote Thursday? I need one clear recommendation per student with a proportionate sanction, and whichever way you land on student four, it has to actually engage with the strongest version of the losing side's argument instead of just waving it away. And since two of my collegues on the board are scientists who will tune out the second a philosopher's name comes up, it needs to make its case in plain terms rather than leaning on the reader having read either guy.
