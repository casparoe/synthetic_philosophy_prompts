Over the past few weeks I've been working through the foundations-of-quantum-mechanics material that circulates constantly on rationalist blogs and forums — Yudkowsky's old quantum mechanics sequence, a stretch of Scott Aaronson's Shtetl-Optimized archives on Bell inequalities and the CHSH game, and the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's entry on Bell's theorem, which is where I eventually ended up. Almost everything pitched at that level says some version of "Bell's theorem proves the world is nonlocal" and leaves it there. But I eventually ran into Jarrett and Shimony's decomposition of Bell's factorizability condition into Parameter Independence (my choice of measurement setting can't shift the statistics of your distant outcome) and Outcome Independence (once we fix the hidden state and both settings, our outcomes are still correlated with each other) — and the fact that ordinary quantum mechanics, and even explicitly nonlocal hidden-variable theories like Bohmian mechanics, satisfy the former while violating the latter. That distinction seems like it should change how alarmed we're entitled to be about "nonlocality," yet almost none of the popular treatments mention it.

I'd like you to argue for the following position: that the Bell experiments only force us to give up Outcome Independence, never Parameter Independence, and that this is enough to show quantum mechanics involves no superluminal causal influence between spacelike-separated events. On this view, the residual correlations — Shimony's "passion at a distance" — are better explained by treating the entangled pair's joint state as an irreducibly holistic, non-separable property of the composite system than by any story in which a measurement on one side reaches out and affects the other. Please make the strongest possible case for this reading, engaging seriously with the best objection to it: that relabeling the phenomenon "holism" instead of "causal influence" doesn't actually dissolve the tension with relativity, since there is still no Lorentz-invariant fact about which measurement event is doing the influencing — only a correlation that seems to be imposed on the pair from outside spacetime altogether. I want an actual defense of the position, not a survey that politely concludes both readings are equally reasonable.
