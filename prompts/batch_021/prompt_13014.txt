A colleague of mine in the economics department and I have a standing wager on whether a new drug currently in a large clinical trial will outperform the existing standard treatment. We have each examined the same interim data, explained our reasoning to the other at length, and stated exactly how confident we are — yet neither of us has budged, even though we each regard the other as a careful, level-headed judge of this kind of evidence. This puzzles me: shouldn't the mere fact that each of us knows the other still disagrees, after hearing all of this, be a reason for our views to converge? Since it isn't, I wonder whether one of us must be quietly irrational, or whether this sort of stable, fully informed disagreement is perfectly compatible with both of us reasoning correctly. What is really going on here, philosophically?
