So the surgeon isn't giving her patient a new reason, she's leaning on a known quirk in how people process risk — that's exactly Sunstein's bypass test. The one wrinkle is that there's no framing-free way to state a statistic, every phrasing emphasizes something, so if exploiting a frame is enough on its own to fail the test, basically all risk communication turns out manipulative, which seems too strong. Given that, I'd lean towards calling this legitimate persuasion overall, mainly because the surgeon's aim lines up with the patient's own interest rather than her own. If a car salesman used the identical framing to sell an extended warranty nobody needs, the transparency test would come back positive for manipulation, since the buyer wouldn't want to be nudged toward a worse deal.
