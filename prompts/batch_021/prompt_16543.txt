I keep running into the idea of "superrationality" -- Hofstadter's claim that two agents facing a one-shot prisoner's dilemma should cooperate purely because they are both perfectly rational and reasoning through the identical problem, so that whatever conclusion one reaches, the other is bound to reach as well. I follow the basic pitch, but I can't get past the objection that at the moment of choosing, nothing either player does can causally affect the other's choice -- each has, in effect, already decided independently, so defection still looks like it strictly dominates. Could you lay out the strongest version of the superrationality argument, respond to that causal objection directly, and clarify whether superrationality counts as a genuinely distinct decision theory or is really just evidential decision theory operating under a different name? I'd also like to understand how much the whole argument leans on the two agents being exact copies of one another, since real people -- or even two running instances of the same AI model -- are never quite identical.
