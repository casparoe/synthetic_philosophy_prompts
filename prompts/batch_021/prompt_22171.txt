Okay, this is going to sound like a boring homeowner problem, but it's genuinely been rattling around in my head for weeks. We closed on our first house back in March, and ever since then I've been getting hit from every direction—two different insurance agents, our home warranty company, and my dad, who is weirdly invested in this—all pitching different "protect yourself" add-ons: flood insurance (we're technically not even in a flood zone), earthquake coverage, sewer/water backup coverage, an appliance home warranty, and something called "service line coverage" for if a tree root ever cracks a pipe underground somewhere. Every single pitch has basically the same shape: "sure, the odds of this happening in any given year are under 1%, but if it did happen it'd cost you $40-80k that you dont have sitting around, so isnt $20-30 a month obviously worth it?"

Taken one at a time, I can't actually poke a hole in any single one of these pitches — each one really does seem to check out on its own terms. But if I said yes to every policy that's been pitched to me this way (and there's always one more obscure disaster somebody can dream up), I'd be paying more a month in premiums than our actual mortgage. So something about stacking all these individually-reasonable "the downside is way bigger than the cost, so just be safe" arguments feels off once you zoom out and look at the whole picture, except I can't locate exactly where the reasoning goes wrong, since no individual step looks broken to me. My partner thinks I'm majorly overanalyzing what's supposed to be a two-hour paperwork afternoon, but it's genuinely stuck with me now. Is there something actually flawed with this style of argument, or do I just have to accept that "rationally" I should be insuring against everything and I simply can't afford to be fully rational here? I care less about which specific policies we end up buying and more about figuring out where, if anywhere, this whole line of reasoning breaks down.
