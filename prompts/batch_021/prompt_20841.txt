Something happened on a small grant panel I'm on (there's three of us, and we award reporting fellowships to science journalists) that's been bugging me for weeks. We only fund a pitch if it clears two bars: the underlying science checks out, and the reporter can realistically deliver given their track record. On the pitch that caused all the trouble, I thought it cleared both bars, one panelist thought the science checked out but the reporter couldn't deliver, and the third thought the reporter was solid but the science was shaky. Straight up-or-down vote: it lost, 2-1. But then our chair reran the numbers issue by issue and pointed out that a majority of us actually did think the science checked out, and a seperate majority thought the reporter could deliver -- so by our own two-bars rule it should've passed. Half the panel now wants issue-by-issue voting to be the official method going forward; the other half says thats just gaming the outcome to get whatever answer you want. Is there an actual principled reason to trust one voting method over the other, or did we just stumble into a situation where any fix is equally arbitrary?
