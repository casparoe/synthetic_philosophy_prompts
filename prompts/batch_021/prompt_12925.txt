So a few months back I got into a pretty dumb argument with my brother-in-law over Thanksgiving about how nobody on any side of anything seems to actually think for themselves anymore, they just pick a team and defend it to the death no matter what the actual argument is. Later that night I went down an internet rabbit hole and stumbled on this old essay by Simone Weil called "On the Abolition of All Political Parties," which she apparently wrote in London right near the end of her life, before she died in 1943, and it wasn't even published until years after she was gone. What really got me is how specific she is about WHY parties are bad, its not just "they're corrupt" or some vague complaint, she lays out this three part definition: a party is basically a machine built to manufacture collective passion, its designed to put pressure on the minds of its own members until they fall in line, and underneath whatever platform it claims to have, its real goal is just to keep growing with no limit, forever. Because of those three things she says every party, even in a democracy and even if it's never done anything violent, is "totalitarian" in embryo. She's also surprisingly lukewarm on democracy and majority rule themselves, saying those only matter to the extent they get you closer to truth and justice, not because the majority is automatically right. She even throws in this random but interesting bit about how the party system wasn't some natural feature of self-government at all, she traces it back to the Terror in revolutionary France and British influence, like it was a specific historical accident rather than something democracies just need. Her actual fix isn't "reform parties," its full abolition, no parties, period.

Here's what I can't stop chewing on though. Everything Weil is describing is a real, official organization, something with a name and a structure you could actually pass a law to abolish. What do you think she would say about the way political "sides" mostly work now through informal online tribes, like on twitter/x, where nobody signs a membership card but the group still does all three things on her list: it runs almost entirely on manufactured outrage, it absolutely pressures individual members to fall in line through pile-ons and cancellation, and the platforms hosting the whole thing are quite literally optimizing, as a business model, to make the tribe grow without any limit. Would Weil see that as basically the exact same disease she was diagnosing just wearing a new costume, or would she think it's a genuinely different and maybe scarier problem, seeing as you can pass a law abolishing a party but you can't exactly pass a law abolishing an algorithm or a hashtag? I'd like an answer that actually works through her specific reasoning on this rather than just a generic take on internet tribalism.
