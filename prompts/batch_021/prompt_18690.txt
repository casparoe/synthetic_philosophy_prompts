So this happened at a dinner thing last week and it's still bugging me. Someone brought up our friend Marcus, who's basically turned his whole life around over the past few years -- quit drinking, got in shape, built up a little business from nothing, all through what everyone agrees is insane discipline. Everyone at the table was praising him for it, and then Owen (who's apparently been on a philosophy kick, reading some guy named Galen Strawson i think) said something like "sure, but he doesn't actually deserve credit for the discipline itself, since that's just a trait he happens to have, and having it isn't really his doing any more than his height is." That kicked off a whole back-and-forth with Lucia that went roughly like this. (1) Owen: Marcus doesn't deserve real credit for being disciplined, because the discipline itself is just a product of stuff outside his control -- genes, upbringing, whatever lucky run of circumstances made him the kind of person who sticks to things. Praising him for it is really just praising his luck. (2) Lucia: that's nuts -- we praise and blame people's characters constantly, and if his reasoning were right then literally nobody could ever deserve credit or blame for anything, full stop, which is too big a bullet to bite. (3) Owen: that IS the point, nobody has "ultimate" responsibility for anything. To really deserve credit for becoming disciplined, Marcus would've had to freely choose to become that way, but that choice would flow from whatever character he already had at the time, which he also didn't choose, and so on backwards forever -- it's either an infinite regress or it bottoms out in something that's just luck. Either way there's no point where his own free contribution shows up that isn't itself just another product of luck. (4) Lucia: but that argument eats itself -- Owen's own confidence that the argument is correct is ALSO just a product of stuff he didn't choose, his temperament, whatever he happened to read, and so on, so if the regress destroys everyone's claim to responsibility it should destroy his standing to smugly insist he's right about it too. (5) Owen: no, that mixes up two different questions, whether a belief is true or well-supported versus whether the person freely chose to hold it -- determinism (or whatever) can fully explain why he believes the argument without that making the argument false, so it's not actually self-refuting, it just looks that way for a second. (6) Lucia: fine, but then he owes her an explanation of why the same move doesn't also save Marcus -- if being caused by stuff outside your control doesn't disqualify a belief from being true or justified, why does being caused by stuff outside your control disqualify a character trait from being praiseworthy? Either both survive the "outside causes" problem or neither does, he can't just apply it to Marcus and not to himself.

Nobody backed down and honestly I couldn't tell you who came out ahead.
