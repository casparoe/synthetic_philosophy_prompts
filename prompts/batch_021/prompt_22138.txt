I'm on the committee revising my high school's student government constitution ahead of next year's elections. One proposal on the table would let candidates officially run as organized "slates": members would share a platform, campaign together, and once elected be expected to vote as a bloc in the senate rather than case by case. Supporters say this just formalizes the coordination that already happens informally and gives voters a clearer choice; a couple of committee members who've read Simone Weil's wartime essay "On the Abolition of All Political Parties" want it rejected outright, since her objection wasn't really about one-party states -- she argued that any party, whatever its size or ideology, tends to make its own growth and internal discipline the real goal, crowding out members' independent judgment about what's actually true or good for the school. Please draft the short recommendation memo I'll bring to the committee: come down on adopting the slate system, rejecting it, or adopting some modified version, and make sure your reasoning genuinely engages Weil's argument and takes seriously whichever side's concern you end up rejecting.
