So I've been re-reading Mill's Chapters on Socialism, plus the socialism-adjacent stuff he added to later editions of the Principles (the Fourierism discussion, the "Probable Futurity of the Labouring Classes" chapter) for a seminar, and honestly its kind of wild how relevant it still is. Mill ends up sympathetic to a gradual, voluntary transition toward cooperative ownership -- worker co-ops outcompeting capitalist firms on the open market -- while still holding onto private property, capped inheritance, and a real horror of anything achieved by revolution or state fiat. Given all that, what do you think he'd make of the gig economy -- Uber, DoorDash, the whole "independent contractor" model? Would he read it as basically an intensified version of the wage-relationship he wanted co-ops to gradually displace, or does the contractor framing actually change his analysis? And would he be excited about the small worker-owned platform co-ops that have popped up as alternatives (driver-owned rideshare apps, delivery co-ops), or would his worries about capital-intensiveness and scale make him doubt they could outcompete the incumbents the way he expected 19th century producer co-ops to? Give me your best reconstruction of his actual reasoning here, not just vibes.
