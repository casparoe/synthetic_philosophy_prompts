So a while back I went down a rabbit hole on the Sleeping Beauty problem -- the puzzle where she's put to sleep sunday, a fair coin gets flipped, and depending on heads or tails she's woken up once or twice with her memory wiped after each waking, and each time she wakes up she has to say how confident she is that it landed heads. I brought it up with two friends, Tessa and Marco, and they ended up arguing about it for like an hour, neither one backing down.

1. Tessa said that when Beauty wakes up with no idea what day it is, there's three equally likely situations she could be in: heads+monday, tails+monday, tails+tuesday. Only one of those has heads, so her confidence in heads should be 1/3, not 1/2.

2. Marco said that sneaks something in: the coin was flipped before she ever woke up, and she was guaranteed to wake up at least once either way, so waking up on its own isn't news about the coin. Treating the three wakings as equally likely assumes that being confused about the day works just like ordinary evidence -- which is exactly what's in dispute.

3. Tessa came back with this: say tails gives her a thousand wakings instead of two. If it's really always 50/50 no matter what, then someone who bets even money on heads every time she's woken will loose almost every single bet, since 999 out of 1000 wakings are tails-wakings. A way of thinking that makes you loose nearly every bet you'd actually place can't just be a harmless quirk.

4. Marco said that only shows something about betting when you get paid separately per waking, which literally pays out more on tails -- of course the best strategy leans that way. It doesn't prove her credence has to be 1/3 though; if she were paid once for the whole experiment no matter how many times she woke up, the betting argument doesn't obviously favor 1/3 anymore, and what she believes about the coin shouldn't flip depending on how the payout is structured.
