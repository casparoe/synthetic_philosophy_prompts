ok i've been chewing on the nonidentity problem again -- parfit's case, or one of the classic versions of it: a woman's told that if she conceives now the kid will have some disability, but if she just waits a while she'll conceive a totally different (and healthy) kid instead. so the kid who actually gets born literally couldn't have existed as the "better-off" version, since waiting means a different egg/sperm combo and a different kid entirely. feels very adjacent to all the population-ethics/longtermism stuff EAs argue about, so im kinda surprised i dont see it dissected more on here.

anyway, every proposed fix for explaining what's wrong here (if anything is) seems to either smuggle the conclusion in the back door or just bite a bullet somewhere. can you rank the major responses by how well they actually earn their conclusion, rather than just relabeling the intuition? the ones i've got in my head:

- narrow person-affecting views (only harms to people who exist in both compared outcomes count)

- shiffrin-ish consent/risk-imposition accounts (the wrong is about imposing serious risk without possible consent, not comparative harm)
- just biting the bullet a la boonin and saying nothing's actually wrong in the original case
