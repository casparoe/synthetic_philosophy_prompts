Over Thanksgiving dinner, my uncle Frank criticized my cousin Julia at the table for having an affair -- something Frank himself did decades ago, in a fling that nearly ended his own marriage. The remark set off a side argument between my aunt Renata and my cousin's husband, Colin, that I haven't been able to stop thinking about since.

(1) Renata said Frank had no business saying anything to Julia, because you forfeit the right to condemn someone for a wrong you've committed yourself.
(2) Colin said that's beside the point: whether the affair was wrong has nothing to do with who's pointing it out, and going after Frank's own history instead of his claim is just an ad hominem.
(3) Renata said she wasn't disputing that the affair was wrong, only that Frank personally had no standing to be the one saying so -- which she took to be a separate question from whether the criticism was accurate.
(4) Colin said that if hypocrisy alone forfeited standing, almost no one could ever criticize almost anything, since nearly everyone has failed by their own standards somewhere; that seems like too strong a conclusion to accept.
(5) Renata said it only costs you standing on the specific failing in question -- Frank could still fault Julia for, say, being careless with money, just not for cheating -- which is exactly why calling someone a hypocrite is a narrower charge than just saying they've done something wrong before.
(6) Colin said that still leaves open whether the decades since, and the fact that Frank had long ago owned up to what he'd done, should matter: an unrepentant repeat offender seems worse placed to lecture than someone who slipped once, regretted it, and rebuilt things -- so maybe what really disqualifies a critic is a lack of remorse, not hypocrisy as such.

Neither of them budged, and the more I turn it over on my own the less sure I am that either had the whole picture. Could you go through the exchange move by move, say who has the better of it at each step, flag anything you think both of them are missing, and close with your own verdict on whether Frank actually had the standing to say what he said?
