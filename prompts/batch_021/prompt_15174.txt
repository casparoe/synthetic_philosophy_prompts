I'm a student member of my university's Honor Council, which decides academic-integrity cases through three-member panels that vote by majority; the bylaws say nothing about panel size in cases that could end in expulsion. Right after we covered the Condorcet Jury Theorem in my social choice seminar, a council member proposed enlarging panels to seven, on the grounds that adding more independent voters to a binary verdict drives the probability of a correct decision toward certainty, provided each panelist is more likely than not to be right. Skeptics counter that our panelists deliberate openly and tend to converge on the chair's reading of the evidence rather than voting independently, and that in the hardest cases -- where the evidence of intent is thin -- nothing guarantees any panelist clears 50 percent reliability, below which the theorem favors a smaller panel, not a larger one. Please draft the short decision memo I'll circulate before our next meeting: recommend a specific panel size and procedure for these cases, or an alternative to enlarging panels altogether, with reasoning solid enough that the skeptics could actually respond to it.
