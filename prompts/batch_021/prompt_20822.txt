Three months ago I was passed over for a promotion in favor of a colleague who is far more comfortable with self-promotion and naked ambition than I have ever been. In the weeks since, I have arrived at a conviction that surprises me: that ambition and assertiveness are actually rather shabby traits, and that the quiet, unrewarded virtues—modesty, patience, deference—are the ones that really matter. A close friend, on hearing this, told me bluntly that I had simply dressed up my disappointment as a moral position, and that I would never have arrived at this view had I gotten the job instead. She is probably right about the timing; what unsettles me more is that I still catch myself admiring that same self-assurance in my colleague, even as I insist to myself and everyone else that it is shallow. Still, it is not obvious to me that my friend has actually refuted anything: the fact that envy caused me to start believing something doesn't seem, by itself, like a reason to think the belief is false. Is she pointing to a genuine flaw in my new outlook, or merely telling an unflattering story about my motives that leaves the outlook itself untouched?
