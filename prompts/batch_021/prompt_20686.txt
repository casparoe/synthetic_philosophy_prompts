So here's a situation that's actually happening to me right now, and I think there's something philosophically interesting buried in it even though day-to-day it just feels like a headache. Couple years back I signed with a small press for one of my novels. Recently it came out that they maybe lifted a decent chunk of my back-cover copy and a few structural beats for another author they represent, without ever asking me (some people say its just "genre convention," honestly its murky). Cue lawyers. We sent a demand letter, they got their own lawyer, and now something like eight months and roughly $22k of my own money later, my lawyer keeps saying stuff like "well, we've already put this much in, we can't fold now, that money's gone either way, so we might as well see it through." Turns out, through the grapevine (via the other author, who I'm still weirdly friendly with), their lawyer is telling them almost the identical thing. The kicker is the underlying dispute is probably only "worth" about $15k if I won everything I could plausibly win, so between the two of us we've already blown past the value of the actual prize, and neither side is backing down.

I was venting about all this to a friend at a dinner party last month, who happens to be a mediator, and she got this look on her face like she'd seen this movie before, but wouldn't just tell me what to do, very "on principle" of her. What I've noticed myself is that the thing pulling both sides forward at every single step is the exact same reasoning -- "too much is sunk to stop now" -- and yet that reasoning is precisely what keeps making things worse for everyone, step by step. My partner says cut your losses and walk away, my lawyer says that's basically handing the other side a win for free. I keep flip-flopping. Rather than just telling me what my gut says, could you actually help me reason through this properly: why do situations shaped like this spiral the way they do, is there some principled point at which continuing stops making sense, and is there anything either side could've done differently earlier on to avoid ending up stuck in a trap like this in the first place?
