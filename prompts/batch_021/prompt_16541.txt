In my philosophy of social science seminar, we have been examining the dispute between methodological individualism and holism, and my professor recently proposed an analogy that I would like help evaluating: the question of whether macro-level social properties (such as "an institution" or "a social class") perform any genuine explanatory or causal work over and above the individual-level facts on which they supervene looks structurally similar to Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument against non-reductive physicalism in the philosophy of mind. In both cases, if the "lower" level (individuals and their properties, or physical states) is already doing all the causal work, the "higher" level threatens to become epiphenomenal unless it turns out, after all, to reduce to the lower level. I am fairly familiar with Kim's argument in its original philosophy-of-mind setting, but I am unsure how far the parallel to social holism actually extends. Could you reconstruct the exclusion-style argument as it would apply to social structures and individual-level facts, and explain whether there are genuine disanalogies between the mental and social cases that would allow a holist to avoid a conclusion that non-reductive physicalists are often said to be unable to escape?
