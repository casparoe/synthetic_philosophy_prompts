My grandma passed away a couple months ago, and it kicked off a family debate I can't stop turning over in my head. A week before she died, she made my uncle Neal promise out loud, holding his hand, that he'd never sell the lake house and would always keep it in the family. He said yes, obviously, she was dying. Now Neal's drowning in medical bills from her last year and wants to sell, and my cousins Priya and Deshawn have been arguing over whether he's still bound.

Priya says a promise is a promise no matter what: the point of giving your word is that it holds regardless of whether grandma's around to know if you kept it, otherwise it's not really a promise at all. Deshawn says that's backwards - promises matter because breaking them lets down the person who trusted you, and grandma can't be let down anymore since she doesn't exist to be disappointed, so most of the obligation went with her. Priya shot back that this logic would make it fine to promise a dying person anything just to soothe them, since "they'll never know," which is obviously monstrous, so tying the obligation to detection can't be right. Deshawn said detection isn't his point, its that a living person's ongoing expectation is what gives a promise its force, and once that's gone what's left is more like a duty to honor her memory, which can lose out to Neal's actual need for the money. Priya thinks that's just a convenient way to let Neal off the hook.

I've stayed out of it but honestly can't decide who's right. Can you go through their exchange round by round, say who's got the stronger argument at each point, and flag anything important both of them are missing?
