"Both cases start the same way: Hannah and her husband are driving home and need to deposit a paycheck before the bank closes. In the low-stakes version nothing much depends on it being deposited right away, Hannah remembers being at the bank two weeks ago on a Saturday and it was open, and she says 'I know the bank will be open tomorrow' — this sounds true. In the high-stakes version a big loan payment is due Monday and the consequences of the bank being closed would be serious; when her husband points out that banks sometimes change their hours, we're suddenly much less willing to say Hannah knows the bank will be open, even though her evidence (the earlier visit) is identical in both stories.

Contextualists like DeRose, Cohen, and Lewis explain this by treating 'knows' as a context-sensitive term, similar to gradable adjectives like 'flat' or 'tall,' whose truth conditions shift with the standards salient in a conversation. When a lot is riding on the outcome, far-fetched error possibilities (the bank changed its hours) become worth raising, which raises the standard 'knows' invokes in that conversation; so the very same sentence 'Hannah knows the bank is open' expresses a stricter, harder to satisfy claim in the second conversation than the first, even though nothing about Hannah's evidence has changed. On this view the shift happens at the level of what 'knows' means in context, not at the level of Hannah's own epistemic situation.

Defenders of pragmatic encroachment, like Fantl and McGrath, deny that 'knows' has any such context-sensitive meaning. Instead they say the word has one fixed meaning across every conversation, and what changes between the two cases is a non-traditional, practical factor — namely how much Hannah has riding on being right — which is itself part of what determines whether she meets that one fixed standard. Because more is at stake for her in the second version, she needs a stronger epistemic position to count as knowing, so the same evidence that gave her knowledge in the first case doesn't in the second. This is exactly why the view is labeled a form of invariantism: it holds that whether a subject knows is never an invariant, fixed matter but instead varies from case to case with her practical circumstances, in contrast with the contextualist's claim that the real variation is located in the conversation rather than in the subject.

It's worth situating this in the bigger picture: the 'pragmatic' in pragmatic encroachment links the view to the classical American pragmatism of James and Dewey, for whom a belief's truth just consisted in its practical success. Encroachment theorists can be read as extending that same insight from truth to knowledge, holding that practical payoff isn't just a byproduct of knowing but partly constitutive of it."
