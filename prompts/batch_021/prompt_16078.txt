I am a retired biologist who came to philosophy of mind only after I retired. My closest friend of thirty years, a color-vision researcher, died last week. She had complete congenital achromatopsia, so her whole career was spent on the physiology of a sense she had never personally used, and she liked to joke that this made her a living rebuttal to Frank Jackson's Mary. She was a committed physicalist of the ability-hypothesis persuasion: to the end, she held that what she lacked was a set of recognitional skills, not some further fact about color that her own science had left out. She told her family plainly that no one was to stand up at the funeral and say she can finally see red now, since that would concede exactly what she had spent her career denying. I am delivering that eulogy myself, in a chapel that will seat her family, who have never heard the word "qualia," beside several of her former philosophy-of-mind collaborators, who will notice at once if I soften the metaphysics to make the ending prettier. Please write it: warm enough for the family, and careful enough that her old collaborators do not wince.
