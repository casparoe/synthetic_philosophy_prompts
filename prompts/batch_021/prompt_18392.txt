My older brother had a genuinely rough time growing up. Our parents' divorce was messy, and he basically ended up raising himself from around age eleven onward with nobody really checking whether he was okay. As an adult now he's the type who goes quiet for months at a time, forgets basically every birthday, and cancels plans last minute without much of an explanation. Whenever I bring it up he says, and lately my friends back him up on this too, that he just never learned how to let people in because of how he grew up (one of them even said it sounds like textbook "avoidant attachment" stuff she read about), and that it isn't really fair of me to expect the same kind of closeness or effort from him that I'd expect from someone who actually had people around growing up.

Here's what's bugging me though. I don't doubt the explanation at all, i think it's probably true. But I can't shake this feeling that the conclusion everyone draws from it is off somehow. Like, if I fully accept it, does that mean I'm just supposed to stop feeling hurt when he disappears on me? Am I supposed to treat him kind of like the weather, something you route around instead of somebody you actually hold to anything? That feels wrong, like it quietly turns him from someone I'm in an actual relationship with into more of a situation I manage. But also, if "he can't help it because of how he was raised" is a good enough reason to let it go, doesn't that apply to basically everyone? Everybody's personality got shaped by stuff that happened to them that they didn't choose, so by that logic it feels like nobody could ever really be blamed or resented for anything, which also seems like a crazy place to end up. Me and my friends got into a whole arguement about this over dinner last week and nobody landed anywhere solid. Is there some actual clear way of thinking through when an explanation like his should change how much hurt or resentment I'm entitled to feel, and when it shouldn't?
