At my company, engineers become eligible for the Staff Engineer title once they have shown "sustained, significant technical impact across the organization," but no one has ever pinned down what that phrase actually requires. During a recent leveling discussion, one committee member pointed out that no single additional project or design doc ever seems to be the one that turns a "not yet" into a "yes" — and concluded from this that the whole criterion must be a fiction, since a genuine standard would have some contribution, however small, at which the verdict actually flips. I disagreed, but when he pressed me on whether I thought there was a precise cutoff that we simply cannot identify, or no cutoff at all, I did not have a good answer. Does a standard like this have to rest on some exact, even if hidden, line in order to be meaningful and fairly applied, or can "significant impact" function as a real standard even though no such line exists?
