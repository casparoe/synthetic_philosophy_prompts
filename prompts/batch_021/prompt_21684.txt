A close friend of mine is getting married this spring, and she has asked me to give the toast at the reception. She and her fiancé met in a graduate seminar on Being and Nothingness and have gone on arguing happily about Sartre ever since; bad faith is genuinely how the two of them talk about their own lives, which — to someone who has spent years reading rationalist blogs on motivated reasoning and self-deception — looks like an independent rediscovery of the same phenomenon under a different name. They have set me one hard constraint: the toast may not use "meant to be," "soulmate," "fate," or "everything happens for a reason," since to them this is textbook mauvaise foi — dressing up a contingent, continually re-made choice as a fixed fact about the world, which conveniently excuses you from ever having to choose it again. I don't think that's actually the hard part, though. The hard part is that Sartre's own account of romantic love, in the "Concrete Relations with Others" chapter of Being and Nothingness, is not exactly wedding material: the lover wants to be freely chosen by a freedom while also wanting that freedom's choice guaranteed in advance, a demand Sartre thinks is self-defeating, and one he uses to explain why love keeps collapsing into what he calls sadism (appropriating the other's freedom by force) or masochism (surrendering your own freedom to become an object for the other). Sartre himself, for what it's worth, spent decades with Beauvoir under an openly non-monogamous pact of "necessary" and "contingent" loves, and never married her. I'd like you to write the toast: three or four minutes, for a room split between guests who will notice if I botch the in-itself/for-itself distinction and guests who have never heard of Sartre and are only there for their cousin's wedding. It should be genuinely moving, it should respect the ban on fate-talk without smuggling the same comfort back in under a different name, and it should not quietly pretend that Sartre's actual views on love are warmer than they are.
