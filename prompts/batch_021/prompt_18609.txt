Two people in my philosophy of mind reading group got into it over the symbol grounding problem and it's been bugging me since. (1) Leah said Harnad basically solved it: ground your elementary symbols in categorical representations built out of sensorimotor feature detectors (so "horse" bottoms out in whatever lets the system reliably sort horses from non-horses), let combinatorial symbols inherit that grounding from thier grounded parts, and you've escaped the "just more symbols" merry go round for good. (2) Omar said that's not a fix, just a fancier version of the same problem -- all you get is a system whose internal categories cohere with each other, but nothing guarantees those categories actually latch onto real external kinds rather than something else, or nothing at all. Feed the exact same sensorimotor patterns from a simulation instead of the world and by Harnad's own lights it counts as equally grounded, so really he's grounded symbols in appearances, not things. (3) Leah shot back that this is an unfair standard since it applies just as much to actual brains -- our concepts are also just fixed by internal processing on sensory input, and nobody says human concepts are ungrounded just because a brain-in-a-vat scenario is conceivable. If in-principle envatment defeats grounding then nothing is ever grounded, so the objection proves too much to be useful. (4) Omar said that's backwards: the fact that the worry generalizes to brains isn't a defense of Harnad, it just means brains might not be grounded in the strong sense either, and anyway plenty of respectable theories of content (causal theories of reference, e.g.) build in exactly the world-involving individuation Harnad's story leaves out, so demanding it isn't some exotic ask.
