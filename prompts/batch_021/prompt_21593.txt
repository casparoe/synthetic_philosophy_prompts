This term I'm taking a seminar on moral responsibility, and a situation from an unrelated group project has been nagging at me ever since. I'd been assigned to proofread our team's final report before submission, but skipped my read-through, assuming the two teammates who had already gone over it would catch anything serious. A data error that one of them had introduced through a careless copy-paste slipped past all three of us, and it ended up costing our group a full letter grade. My teammates insist that I bear the most blame, since catching exactly this kind of error was officially my job. That verdict feels intuitively right, yet something about it bothers me: the other two read the report just as carefully and missed the same error, and I would have had no better a chance of catching it than either of them did. If everyone's failure to notice is equally tied to how things turned out, what actually justifies treating my inaction as more responsible for the result than theirs, or than the original mistake itself? Is there a principled way to single me out here, or is the blame my teammates are assigning ultimately arbitrary?
