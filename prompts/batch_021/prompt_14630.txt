So I started therapy about four months ago for anxiety (work stuff mostly, the racing heart before meetings kind of thing), and my therapist has this framing she keeps coming back to that's been stuck in my head. She says anxiety is basically an ancient alarm system doing exactly what it evolved to do, it kept our ancestors alive by making them jumpy around rustling bushes, and it's just badly calibrated for modern life so now it goes off over slack messages instead of predators. Honestly I found that pretty comforting at first, like ok, nothing's "wrong" with me, I just have outdated software running.

Then over the holidays I fell into a rabbit hole of psych and biology podcasts where people were absolutely dragging this kind of explanation, calling it an unfalsifiable just-so story, saying you can invent some ancestral "reason" for literally any trait after the fact if you try hard enough, and that it doesn't even matter for treatment purposes what anxiety was for back then, what matters is what it's doing in my body and thoughts right now, i.e. the whole avoidance-feeds-the-fear-loop thing my therapist also brings up constantly, without ever connecting it back to the evolution stuff. My partner says I'm overthinking a throwaway metaphor, but it feels like an actual disagreement to me and not just two ways of describing the same thing.

So which is it: is there a real tension between explaining something like anxiety by what it was "for" way back in our evolutionary past versus by what it's causally doing in someone right now, or are these just different questions that both happen to be true? And practically, does it even matter which framing you use when it comes to something like deciding whether a given persons anxiety is functioning normally versus is an actual disorder?
