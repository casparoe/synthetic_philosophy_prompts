Ok this has been bugging me for a bit. Every pop-psych explanation of akrasia (like, you know you should be working but you doomscroll instead) just says its hyperbolic discounting -- you overweight the near-term reward, unlike the exponential discounting (gamma^t) we use for reward functions in RL, which is time-consistent and never flips. But if at the actual moment you cave, your discounted-value calc genuinely ranks doomscrolling above working right then, aren't you just doing what you currently want most? That doesn't sound like "acting against your own better judgement" to me, it sounds like your judgment just changed out from under you. So is there an actual principled difference between plain old time-inconsistent preferences reversing, and "real" akrasia in the classical philosophical sense (acting against a judgment you still hold at the very moment of acting) -- or is the classical concept basically just a confused way of redescribing the same discounting phenomenon? Genuinely curious if philosophers have an actual answer to this, not the standard behavioral-econ hand-wave.
