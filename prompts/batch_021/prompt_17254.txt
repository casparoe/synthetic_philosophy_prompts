A friend of mine and I had a long, unresolved argument last week after a session of a cognitive science reading group I'm part of this term, sparked by a paper that used "the free energy principle" and "predictive processing" almost interchangeably throughout. My friend maintained that this conflation is basically harmless: predictive processing, on her view, just is the free energy principle worked out for the special case of the brain, so the two frameworks are bound to stand or fall together. I pushed back that this glosses over a real difference in scope: the free energy principle is pitched as a maximally general claim about any system that manages to maintain itself away from thermodynamic equilibrium, from single cells to whole organisms, formulated in terms of variational free energy, generative models, and Markov blankets separating internal from external states, whereas predictive processing is a much more specific, empirically loaded hypothesis about hierarchical prediction-error minimization and precision-weighting in cortical circuits.

We went several rounds without making progress. She pointed out that Friston himself describes predictive coding and active inference as the "process theories" that the free energy principle predicts the brain, in particular, should implement, so there is no real daylight between the two once you get down to cases. I countered that this is exactly the worry: a framework general enough to apply to almost any self-organizing system, living or not, and that seems compatible with essentially any pattern of self-maintaining behavior, risks being closer to a mathematical truism than a substantive empirical theory, in a way that plainly does not transfer to predictive processing's specific and checkable claims about neural architecture. Neither of us budged, and, on reflection, I don't think either of us was simply being obtuse.

I'd like your help doing what we couldn't. Could you compare the free energy principle and predictive processing in more depth and rigor than we managed? I'm especially interested in whether predictive processing is best understood as simply a special case, or "process-level" instantiation, of the free energy principle, or whether it carries independent commitments that could survive even if the free energy principle itself turned out to be unfalsifiable; and, relatedly, whether the generality that supposedly makes the free energy principle so unifying comes at a real cost in empirical content, or whether that worry is overstated. If you had to adjudicate, who do you think had the better of this particular argument?
