Last night, a friend and I spent hours arguing about Bradley's regress and never really settled the disagreement. She defended David Armstrong's view that instantiation is a fundamental, non-relational 'tie' binding particulars to universals, and that because this tie is not itself a further relation, it needs no additional connector to attach it to its terms, so the regress stops at the first step. I argued that this move only relabels the problem: if the tie manages to unify a particular and a universal into a single fact, we still owe an account of how it achieves that unity without a further connector, and any such account will end up either smuggling in something like Frege's claim that concepts are inherently 'unsaturated' and gap-like, or collapsing into Russell's insistence that a relation must actually relate its terms rather than merely sit alongside them in a list. Please argue for my side of the dispute: that no tie which is merely stipulated to be non-relational can explain propositional or factual unity without covertly relying on one of these two moves, and that if it relies on neither, it has not actually solved Bradley's regress but merely renamed it.
