So I'm about a third of the way into a historical novel set in a small made-up Italian principality (fifteenth-century-ish vibe, but I'm not tied to any real events), and I've hit a scene I can't quite crack. A handful of minor nobles are plotting to kill their duke. Nobody disputes he's the rightful ruler, he inherited the title clean, but he's turned genuinely vicious over the years, crushing dissent, seizing land, the works. There's no pope or emperor or any other overlord in this setting for them to appeal to, its just the one small independent state. Before they move, the ringleader goes to his confessor, a Dominican friar, basically expecting the friar to wave the plan through, because "Aquinas says its fine to kill tyrants," which is the version of Aquinas that everyone in my writing group seems to have absorbed secondhand from somewhere.

Except from what I've actually gone and read, Aquinas is way more careful than that. In De Regimine Principum especially he seems genuinely worried about letting private individuals decide for themselves that someone counts as a tyrant and act on it, and wants removal to go through some kind of public or communal authority instead. But then there's that other, much more permissive-sounding line in his commentary on the Sentences about how someone who kills a tyrant to free their country deserves praise when there's no one higher to appeal to, which seems to cut the opposite way. I don't want my friar to just cite whichever bit is convenient for the plot. What I actually want is the strongest version of the argument that this specific group, a few nobles acting purely on their own say-so, not a council, not any body that represents the wider community, do not have standing to kill the duke under Aquinas's own principles, even granting he really is tyrannical in how he governs, and it needs to actually deal with that tyrant-slayer line from the Sentences as the obvious objection rather than just quietly ignoring it. Could you write that argument out for me properly, basically the case the friar would lay out to the conspirators for why he won't give the plan his blessing?
