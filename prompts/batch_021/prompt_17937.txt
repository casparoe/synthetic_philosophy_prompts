I'm teaching a grad seminar on philosophy of mind this term and we just spent a week on the Lucas-Penrose argument. Half my students now think Gödel's theorem straightforwardly refutes computationalism, and the other half think "oh but Putnam refuted that ages ago" is a satisfying place to stop -- neither is engaging with the actual moving parts of the argument, which is way more subtle then either camp realizes. I want to hand them something that models real engagement, so could you write me a dialogue between a Penrose-style Gödelian anti-mechanist and a computationalist who's read Penrose closely (not someone who just recites the one-line Putnam rebuttal and declares victory)?

I want the anti-mechanist to run the strongest version of the argument, and to be explicit about why it has to be run in terms of soundness rather than mere consistency (Penrose's actual move in Shadows of the Mind, as I understand it, after people pointed out the problem with the original consistency-based version). I want the computationalist to press hard on where our supposed insight into our own soundness is meant to come from, given that we can't even get consistency for free post-second-incompleteness-theorem, and to raise the "moving target" issue -- if a machine can just adopt its own Gödel sentence as a new axiom and carry on, why isn't that structurally identical to what we do when we reflect and revise, and doesn't the fact that neither of us has a surveyable specification of "the" formal system underlying our reasoning undercut the anti-mechanist's confidence that they've located a real asymmetry rather than an artifact of comparing an idealized human to a needlessly static machine? Let the anti-mechanist have real comebacks too, not just eventually concede -- I want two people who've actually thought hard about this, going back and forth and responding to each other's specific moves, ending wherever it honestly ends (a clean resolution isn't necessary, and might not be realistic). Something in the 700-1000 word range would be great.
