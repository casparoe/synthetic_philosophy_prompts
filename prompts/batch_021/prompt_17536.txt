So this happened at a dinner party over the weekend, nothing to do with me, I was just topping up glasses and trying not to laugh. Owen mentioned he'd started going to christmas/easter mass again with his mom, half-joking that it's basically Pascal's wager -- infinite upside if he's wrong to skip it, costs him nothing. Leah said that doesn't even work as hedging, because if you're only going through the motions for the payoff, a God worth worrying about would see through the insincerity and he'd get nothing for it anyway. Owen said fine, but that's literally what Pascal himself recommended -- go through the motions, holy water, the whole ritual, and let the habit wear the doubt down until real belief shows up on its own, so the insincerity is a bootstrapping problem, not a fatal one. Leah switched tack: even granting that, why catholicism specifically -- the same logic commits him to hedging on every possible god with an infinite payoff, including ones that specifically damn people for hedging, and there's no principled way to priviledge his mom's religion over that whole field of alternatives. Owen said that's too fast, actual historical religions have accumulated real evidential weight from tradition and testimony that made-up alternatives haven't, so it isn't a fair fight between equally live options. Leah said that's a real point, but it quietly gives up what made the wager work in the first place -- the whole appeal was that it goes through no matter how tiny the probability is, so long as it isn't literally zero, because the infinite payoff swamps it; he can't lean on "infinity swamps tiny probabilities" for the first move and then lean on "the probabilities aren't actually comparable" for the second, that's playing both games at once. They got cut off by dessert before Owen could answer.
