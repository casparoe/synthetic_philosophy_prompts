So I got into a pretty long argument with a friend who's doing a physics PhD about what a "law of nature" actually is, and afterward I wrote up my side of it more carefully so I could send it back to her. I'm a software engineer with zero formal philosophy background, so I'm sure I'm just badly reinventing some wheel that already has a proper name, but I'd like a sanity check before I send it and get destroyed.

My basic take: there seem to be two ways to think about what makes something a law of nature rather than just a pattern that happens to hold every single time. One way treats a law like a physics engine in a video game - some real thing sitting "underneath" events that actively generates or enforces them, so the pattern holds BECAUSE the law is pushing it that way. The other way treats a law as more like the best possible compression of one giant log file containing every particle's state at every instant since the beginning of the universe: "best" meaning the shortest program (simplicity) that still lets you reconstruct or predict the whole log accurately (strength), and a "law" is just whatever regularity ends up being part of that optimal trade-off. On this second view there's no separate mechanism making the pattern happen, the law just IS the most efficient true description of the pattern, full stop. I find the second one way more intuitive - I don't get what extra ingredient a law would even be adding on top of the pattern just holding - but then I hit a snag: this compression framing runs into the exact same issue as Kolmogorov complexity in CS, where "shortest program" is only defined relative to a choice of language or instruction set. That choice stops mattering in the limit (up to a fixed additive constant), but for one specific, finite thing like our actual universe, a different choice of vocabulary could plausibly crown a totally different system as "simplest." Which seems to mean whether something counts as a law becomes partly a fact about which description language we happened to pick, not a fact about the world - which feels like a real problem for the whole "law = best compression" idea, but I don't know if its fatal, well known with some standard fix, or if I'm just confusing it with a totally different problem.

Does the overall argument hold together, and is that language-dependence thing an actual known objection to this kind of view (with some standard answer to it), or am I muddling two separate issues? Also curious whether the video-game/physics-engine framing is even a fair way to describe the other camp, or if its a strawman. Trying to find the holes myself before she does.
