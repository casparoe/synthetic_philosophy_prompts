I'm assembling a collection of short fiction in which each story stages a specific philosophical problem through character and incident rather than exposition, and I'd like your help with the entry on mental causation. The problem I want dramatized is Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument: if the physical domain is causally closed, so that every physical effect already has a sufficient physical cause, and mental properties merely supervene on physical properties without being identical to them, then a person's reasons and intentions threaten to be causally idle—mere shadows cast by the neural events doing the actual work, rather than what made them act. Write a self-contained short story, built around one character and a single consequential decision or confession, in which this worry stops being an abstract puzzle and becomes something the character has to live with. Dramatize the dilemma through plot and voice rather than having anyone explain it outright, resist resolving it too neatly, and avoid the mind-uploading or brain-simulation settings that usually carry this kind of question in science fiction.
