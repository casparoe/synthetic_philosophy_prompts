Question 3 (20 points). The simple conditional analysis (SCA) of dispositions holds that x is disposed to give response r to stimulus s just in case it is true that x would give r if x underwent s.
(a) [6 pts] Explain, using an example, how a finkish disposition constitutes a counterexample to the SCA.
(b) [7 pts] State David Lewis's Reformed Conditional Analysis (RCA) and explain how it is meant to block the finkish counterexample.
(c) [7 pts] Does the RCA also succeed against masking (or "antidote") cases? Explain why or why not, using an example.

Student answer:

(a) The SCA claims that having a disposition is equivalent to a counterfactual's holding, but C. B. Martin showed that this breaks down wherever a "fink" is present. Consider a dead wire hooked up to an electro-fink: the fink instantly charges the wire the moment a conductor touches it, so the wire lacks the disposition to conduct (it is dead) even though the counterfactual "if touched, it would conduct" is true. There is also a mirror-image version: a live wire wired to a fink that kills the current at the very instant of contact, so the disposition is present but the matching counterfactual is false. Either way, the fink switches the wire's causal basis exactly when the stimulus condition is realized, which the SCA has no resources to accommodate.

(b) Lewis's RCA repairs this by writing the causal basis directly into the analysis: x is disposed at t to give response r to stimulus s iff x has some intrinsic property B at t such that, if x were to undergo s and retain B for a short while afterward, x's undergoing s and its having B would together be an x-complete cause of x's giving r. Since the analysis is now a counterfactual about what would happen if B were retained through the stimulus, a fink that destroys or installs B at the very moment of the stimulus no longer generates a counterexample: we are explicitly holding fixed the very thing the fink otherwise tampers with.

(c) The RCA handles masking in exactly the same way. Johnston's antidote cases include things like a fragile glass that has been carefully bubble-wrapped, so that striking it will not break it, or a dose of poison neutralized by an antidote taken at the same time. In both cases the object plainly keeps its disposition throughout the episode: the glass is still fragile, the poison is still poisonous. Since the causal basis B is retained rather than destroyed, Lewis's requirement that B persist through the stimulus is satisfied, so the RCA correctly predicts that the disposition is still present even though no manifestation occurs. So the very fix that disarms the finkish cases disarms the antidote cases too: once you're tracking the persistence of B instead of the bare truth of the naive conditional, both kinds of counterexample come out the same way.
