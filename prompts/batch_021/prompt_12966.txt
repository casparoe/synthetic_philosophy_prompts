So a few weekends ago I was hacking together a little iterated prisoner's dilemma tournament (like an Axelrod-style tournament, just for fun) and fell down a rabbit hole reading about "program equilibrium" -- apparently instead of players picking a strategy directly, they submit actual programs that get to read each other's source code before acting, and this supposedly gets you cooperation in a ONE-SHOT prisoner's dilemma, no repeated play needed. The thing that really got me was this "FairBot" construction that uses Löb's theorem (some result from provability logic, still a bit fuzzy on the details tbh) to literally prove that it cooperates with itself, plus a fancier "PrudentBot" that's built to cooperate with FairBot and itself but still defect against a dumb always-cooperate bot instead of leaving free value on the table the way FairBot does.

I brought this up with an old coworker who's way more into decision theory blogs than me, and we ended up arguing about it for like an hour without either of us budging. His take: this is a genuinely new and important result, real proof that mutual transparency plus the right kind of self-referential reasoning gets you cooperation "for free," no repetition, no external enforcement needed, and it should generalize to stuff like smart contracts or AI agents that can inspect each other's code. My take was more like: this only works because you've quietly rigged the game (both sides get magic verified read-access to source, and you have to bound the proof search just so it halts), the whole "exploit CooperateBot but don't get exploited by DefectBot" trick just relocates the hard problem into arbitrary-seeming proof-length cutoffs, and mutual defection (DefectBot vs DefectBot) is sitting right there the whole time as an equally legit equilibrium, so nothings really been "solved" in the sense of picking out cooperation as special.

Neither of us backed down, so I'd love it if you could settle it properly by writing the whole thing up as a dialogue between the two positions -- someone arguing program equilibrium / Löbian cooperation is a substantive result, and someone arguing it's mostly a clever formal trick that doesn't generalize -- where they actually respond to each other's specific points (the CooperateBot/DefectBot asymmetry, the source-code-transparency assumption, the proof-length bounds, whatever else seems relevant) instead of just talking past one another. Make both sides as strong as they can genuinely be argued, because honestly I don't think this one has an obvious winner.
