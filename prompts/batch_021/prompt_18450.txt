A friend and I spent the drive home arguing about whether any conditional analysis of dispositions can survive Martin's electro-fink and the standard poison-and-antidote masking case, and we never settled whether patching the analysis with talk of "the proportion of relevant scenarios" solves the problem or just relabels it. I'm interviewing a metaphysician next week who defends exactly that patch: on her view, a disposition ascription is a gradable, comparative claim about the share of relevantly similar scenarios in which the stimulus would produce the manifestation, rather than a flatly true-or-false single-case conditional, which is meant to let finks and masks be absorbed as atypical cases rather than counterexamples. Draft eight to ten interview questions that push past having her simply restate the view, giving at least one question -- plus a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give -- on each of: whether "relevant" or "normal" scenarios can be specified without covertly reimporting the very causal basis the analysis was meant to replace; how the account is supposed to apply to a disposition that is tested only once, where there's no population of cases to take a proportion over; whether the move actually resolves the metaphysical puzzle about dispositions or just relocates it into the semantics of disposition-ascribing sentences; and a disposition that is masked in the large majority of actual cases, such as a poison always sold together with its legally mandated antidote, where "normal" and "masked" seem to trade places.
