I'm one of two student members on my high school's Honor Council, the body that hears academic integrity cases and recommends sanctions to the Dean of Students. I ran for the seat last spring because I enjoy this kind of case-based reasoning, but the case in front of us now has split the Council in a way none of us knows how to resolve. A sophomore, Maya, reported a classmate, Ethan, for submitting an AI-generated essay as his own work. Two days before the scheduled hearing, another Council member came across an old group chat showing that Maya herself had copied several paragraphs of a lab report from a friend the previous year -- a violation for which she was never caught or disciplined.

Ethan's family has since sent a letter arguing that Maya's own undisclosed violation means she has no standing to bring this accusation at all, and that the Council should either dismiss the complaint or at least pause it until her conduct has been reviewed on its own. The Council is now roughly split: some members think the standing objection is just a distraction, since Ethan's essay is either his own work or it isn't, no matter who reported it, while others think proceeding against Ethan while quietly letting Maya's conduct slide would make the Council complicit in a double standard. Our honor code only requires that proceedings be "conducted fairly," and says nothing about what fairness requires when the complaining student's own hands are unclean.

I got curious about the deeper question after our ethics elective mentioned, almost in passing, that philosophers actually disagree about whether hypocrisy merely makes the hypocrite separately blameworthy or can genuinely revoke her standing to blame someone else for the very same kind of thing. Could you write the short memo I'll circulate to the Council before Tuesday's hearing? It should recommend one of three courses of action -- hearing Ethan's case as scheduled regardless of Maya's conduct, postponing it until Maya's conduct has been reviewed, or dismissing the complaint outright for lack of standing -- and justify that recommendation on its merits. Whatever you recommend, please engage honestly with the strongest reasoning behind the options you set aside, rather than treating the standing objection as an obvious distraction or an obvious disqualifier.
