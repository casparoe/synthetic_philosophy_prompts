Ok this is going to be a weird one. I fell down a rabbit hole reading old lesswrong/alignment-forum posts about multi-agent game theory for AI systems and got stuck on this idea called a "commitment race" -- the gist is that if agents can make binding commitments, whoever commits first and hardest wins the bargaining game (like ripping the steering wheel out in a game of chicken), so every agent is incentivized to lock in a commitment as early and irreversibly as possible, before its even figured out what's actually worth committing to. Kind of reminds me of a race condition, except instead of corrupting some shared state you might lock in something catastrophic forever.

Across a bunch of these posts people throw out different proposed fixes, but I can't tell which ones actually attack the "race to commit early" incentive itself vs which just treat a symptom (or quietly make things worse). Here's the list I've collected:

- surrogate goals (adopt a decoy value for threats to target instead of your real one)
- "safe pareto improvements" -- renegotiate/rewrite the game itself before anyone locks anything in
- radical transparency about your own commitments so others can verify them instead of guessing
- deliberately staying bad at self-modification, i.e. keeping your own commitment-power weak
- going full updateless decision theory and just committing to an entire policy upfront
- a hard taboo/norm against ever making threats to begin with
- pre-committing to use some symmetric, order-independent bargaining solution instead of committing to a specific outcome (I think this one's called "ROSE values"?)
