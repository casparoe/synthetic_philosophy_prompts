I'm a novelist, and I've been asked to toast two friends who spent eleven years breaking up and reconciling before finally agreeing, this year, to marry. The groom's sister, who is finishing a doctorate on money-pump arguments, will be sitting at the head table, so I can't just wave at "they finally grew up" -- though everyone else at the wedding has no idea what a money pump is and just wants to laugh and cry. I keep sensing that their eleven years only resolves as a story once you see it as a standing money pump: a cycle of reversed preferences bleeding them of time and heartbreak, until the vow itself becomes the resolute choice that closes the loop, rather than one more sophisticated dodge of the same old temptation. Please write the toast so a decision theorist would recognize the mechanics as sound, while everyone else just hears a love story.
