I've read enough Solomonoff induction explainers on rationalist blogs to know the standard reply to the objection that Kolmogorov complexity depends on your choice of universal Turing machine: any two such machines' complexity measures differ by at most a fixed constant (the invariance theorem), so the dependence supposedly doesn't matter much. What I can't square is that this constant is fixed once the two machines are fixed — it doesn't shrink as the strings in question grow longer or as more evidence accumulates — so for any two hypotheses whose complexity differs by less than that constant, some universal machine will rank them the other way round, regardless of how much data has piled up. The standard rejoinder is that any two "reasonable" encodings only differ by some modest constant anyway. Could you give me a gut-level account of exactly what the invariance theorem does and doesn't establish here, and whether that rejoinder is doing real philosophical work or is just smuggling the problem back in through the word "reasonable"?
