was at a bar after an ai safety workshop last week (covering it for a piece i'm writing) and ended up wedged between two researchers having a proper argument about whether "debate" actually works as a scalable oversight scheme -- you know, two models argue opposite sides of a question and a judge who can't verify the answer on their own just picks whichever debater is more convincing, the hope being that this lets you supervise a model that's smarter than the judge.

(1) Dan's claim: it's way harder to build a convincing false argument that survives cross-examination than it is to point out the flaw in one, so as the debaters get more capable, even a judge who's much weaker than both of them should reliably end up backing the honest side.

(2) Mira said that's exactly what the "obfuscated arguments" problem breaks: a dishonest debater doesn't need one clean lie, it can bury the flaw inside a huge pile of individually plausible-looking sub-claims, so the honest debater cant cheaply point to the one spot that's wrong within whatever budget the judge has, and the judge has no way to tell "this is genuinely complicated" from "this is being made complicated on purpose."

(3) Dan's comeback: that's literally what cross-examination is for -- each debater can interrogate the other's commitments from earlier branches of the argument, so a liar eventually contradicts themselves somewhere and gets caught. that's basically the whole point of the newer "doubly efficient" debate protocols people have worked out, so obfuscation isnt some unsolved fatal flaw, it's a solved problem now.

(4) Mira wasn't having it: catching contradictions only works when there's a fixed fact of the matter underneath to be consistent with, like a proof or a program trace. but the stuff we actually want oversight for -- is this plan safe, does this chain-of-thought reflect what the model's really doing -- doesn't have that kind of ground truth sitting under it, so the fix quietly saves the easy, well-defined cases and leaves exactly the messy ones the whole scheme was supposed to handle just as exposed as before.
