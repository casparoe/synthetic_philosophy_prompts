I sit on the joint steering committee for a coalition of five safety-focused AI labs that spent the past year negotiating a mutual restraint pledge: each lab would withhold deployment of a specific class of autonomous coding agents until an independent review clears them, even though there is no regulator, no contract, and no way to verify or punish noncompliance, and whichever lab deploys first captures a real first-mover advantage in enterprise sales. Two of the five now want to sign the pledge unconditionally, on the grounds that everyone in the coalition trained under the same handful of mentors and runs nearly identical internal risk models, so any line of reasoning that led one lab to defect would lead the others to the identical conclusion -- they are calling this superrational cooperation rather than naive trust. The other three think this dresses up wishful thinking in decision-theoretic language: the labs differ enough in runway, investor pressure, and risk tolerance to break the symmetry the argument depends on, and an unconditional pledge would simply invite exploitation by whichever lab turns out to be the least symmetric. Please draft the brief memo I will circulate before next month's steering-committee vote: it should recommend one specific course of action with a concrete timeline, and it should take the skeptics' objection seriously rather than waving it off as mere risk-aversion.
