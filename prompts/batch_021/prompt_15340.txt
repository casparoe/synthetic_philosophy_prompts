Among followers of the debate over symbolic versus connectionist theories of mind -- a topic that resurfaces on rationalist blogs every time neural networks are in the news -- Paul Smolensky's tensor-product representations are usually treated as the move that finally saves connectionism from Fodor and Pylyshyn's systematicity argument, by showing that distributed representations can be compositional without a classical architecture. I've never found this fully convincing. Please argue that any tensor-product (or comparable role-filler binding) scheme powerful enough to guarantee that a system's cognitive capacities are systematic must thereby implement the very kind of classically combinatorial constituent structure that, according to Fodor and Pylyshyn, is required for systematicity in the first place -- so that Smolensky's proposal, instead of refuting their conclusion, ends up conceding it.
