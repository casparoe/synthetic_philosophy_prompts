After twenty years of writing fiction, I've developed an instinct for spotting the load-bearing inconsistency in any account I'm given, which is not the most comfortable trait to bring to my father's death. He had a cardiac arrest three weeks ago and never regained any brain activity, so my family withdrew life support; he was also a registered organ donor. The transplant coordinator explained that once his heart stopped and the mandated waiting period had passed, surgeons would clamp the arteries feeding his brain and then use a pump to restart circulation through the rest of his body, so that his organs would stay usable until they could be retrieved. She was adamant that he would already be dead by that point, and that restarting his heart afterward was a technicality rather than a resuscitation. I don't doubt her sincerity, but pronouncing someone dead because his heart stopped, and then deliberately restarting that same heart, seems to be in tension with itself in a way I can't quite name. Is there a genuine inconsistency here, or am I mistaking a technical detail for a metaphysical one?
