I'm a PhD student in social psychology, and something happening in my own department has been nagging at me in a way my work on induced attitude change doesn't resolve. Our program runs an anonymous annual survey in which students rate their satisfaction with their advisor, and advisors who score well receive discretionary funds they typically pass on to their students as stipend top-ups. Knowing this, I spent the weeks before this year's survey deliberately rereading old emails and cataloging my advisor's genuinely admirable qualities, until my low-grade irritation with him gave way to real, unforced admiration — not merely a favorable rating I felt pressured to give. One labmate insists this admiration is now perfectly legitimate, since it's sincere and grounded in qualities I can point to; another insists something is still off, since I only went looking for those qualities because my stipend was on the line. Which of them has the better argument, and why?
