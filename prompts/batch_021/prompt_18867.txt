For my dissertation in the philosophy of cognitive science, I keep encountering the assumption -- in both popular and some academic treatments -- that Karl Friston's free energy principle (FEP) and "predictive processing" (PP) are essentially the same theory, merely pitched at different levels of abstraction. I am not convinced this is right, and I would like your help thinking it through. Please compare PP -- a hierarchical, prediction-error-minimizing account of perception, action, and learning in the brain -- with the FEP, a more general variational principle, framed in terms of non-equilibrium thermodynamics and Markov blankets, that purports to describe any self-organizing system that persists over time. Specifically, address whether the FEP actually entails or motivates PP's particular computational architecture, or whether the two are conceptually and empirically separable, such that one could be true while the other is false. Relatedly, consider whether the FEP's sheer generality renders it an unfalsifiable mathematical tautology, leaving PP as the only part of the package with genuine empirical bite, or whether that charge is unfair to the FEP. Defend a definite answer rather than simply cataloguing considerations on both sides.
