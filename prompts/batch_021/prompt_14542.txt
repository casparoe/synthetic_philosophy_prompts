As a software engineer who dabbles in quantitative trading on the side, I recently came across the no-trade theorem while reading about market microstructure. As I understand it, the theorem proves that if traders are rational, share the same prior beliefs, and would only want to trade in order to profit from private information, then no trade should occur at all, since the mere fact that one party is willing to trade at a given price reveals information that a rational counterparty would immediately incorporate into their own valuation. Given that this is a rigorously proven mathematical result, doesn't the enormous daily trading volume on exchanges like the NYSE, or on prediction-market platforms like Polymarket, amount to direct empirical proof that most market participants are behaving irrationally? I'd like to know whether I should start treating high trading volume itself as a red flag for collective irrationality, rather than as a sign of a healthy, informative market.
