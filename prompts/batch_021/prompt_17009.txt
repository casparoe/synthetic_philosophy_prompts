I serve as a student representative on my high school's Honor Council, which reviews academic integrity cases and recommends sanctions to the Dean of Students. Under our "Reflection and Accountability" policy, a student can earn a reduced sanction by submitting a signed statement that describes the violation and affirms, in her own words, that she now believes what she did was wrong. In the case before us this week, a student caught collaborating on a take-home exam is willing to accept whatever sanction the Council imposes and to promise it won't happen again, but she refuses to write that she believes she acted wrongly, since she genuinely thinks the take-home format left the collaboration rules ambiguous; instead, she has offered to sign a statement acknowledging that her conduct met the policy's definition of a violation, while explicitly noting that she does not personally believe she did anything wrong. Roughly half the Council thinks this satisfies the spirit of the policy and that compelling a "belief" clause only trains students to write things they don't mean; the other half thinks accepting it guts the entire purpose of the requirement and is unfair to students who wrote sincere statements. Please draft the Council's short recommendation memo to the Dean, due before we reconvene on Monday: it should take a clear position on how to resolve her case and on whether the policy's wording should change going forward, and it should engage seriously with the reasoning of whichever side it doesn't adopt.
