My brother, a metaphysician who works on modality, is marrying a fellow metaphysician who holds the opposite view on one key question: she is a transworld-identity theorist, convinced that the same person can exist in more than one possible world; he is a Lewisian who denies this -- on his view, no one exists in any other world, only counterparts related to us by resemblance, not identity. At the rehearsal dinner he couldn't resist pointing out that, on his own semantics, the vow to love her "in this life and every other" is kept in every other world by someone else entirely: a counterpart who merely resembles him, loving a counterpart who merely resembles her. I'm giving the toast and want to build it around this, including some version of Kripke's objection that Humphrey would hardly have been comforted to learn that a mere counterpart of his won the 1968 election. Most guests have never heard of possible-world semantics, but the couple and a few philosopher friends will be listening for whether I get the dialectic right. Please write the toast: it should be funny, fair to both sides of the dispute, and land as a real statement about commitment, not a lecture with jokes attached.
