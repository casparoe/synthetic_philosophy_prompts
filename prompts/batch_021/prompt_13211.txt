I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and next week I'm interviewing a metaphysician who has spent her career defending David Lewis's counterpart theory against the rival view -- sometimes called transworld identity -- that one and the same individual can be literally present in more than one possible world. She tends to deflect Kripke's charge that talk of a mere counterpart changes the subject -- the thought being that Humphrey himself wouldn't care whether some other fellow, however similar, would win in another world -- by replying that this only works if you've already assumed there's a fact about Humphrey's identity across worlds over and above resemblance, which is exactly what's in dispute. Draft nine or ten interview questions that don't let her simply repeat that move. I want at least one question on that exchange itself, one on why the problem of accidental intrinsics is supposed to make her view less awkward than saying a person's shape is somehow relative to a world, one on Chisholm's paradox and whether a non-transitive counterpart relation genuinely escapes the underlying sorites-style arbitrariness or just relocates it, and one on what follows from Lewis's own admission that a single person can have several counterparts in one world who disagree about some property. For each, add a follow-up tuned to the answer she's most likely to give, and close with a question about what would actually count as evidence against the theory, rather than just another place to bite the bullet.
