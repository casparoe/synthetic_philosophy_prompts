I'm on my school's Ethics Bowl team, and during practice this week we discussed the philosophical idea that a hypocrite forfeits their standing to blame someone else for a fault they themselves share — the stock example being a chronically speeding parent who scolds their teenager for speeding. If the parent's act of blaming is genuinely illegitimate in a case like that, it seems to follow that the teenager hasn't actually done anything blameworthy in that exchange, since the only person positioned to call it out has been disqualified from doing so. And because virtually everyone is a hypocrite about something, doesn't it follow that virtually no one is ever truly in a position to call any action wrong, so that the entire "standing to blame" framework quietly commits its defenders to a kind of practical moral nihilism? I'd like to understand whether philosophers who work on this actually accept that conclusion, or whether I'm running two separate ideas together.
