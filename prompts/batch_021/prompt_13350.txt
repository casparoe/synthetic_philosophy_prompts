"The hard problem of consciousness is the puzzle of why physical processes in the brain are accompanied by subjective experience at all, instead of just happening 'in the dark.' The easy problems, by contrast, are things like explaining how we discriminate stimuli, integrate information, or report on our own internal states - in principle these can all be explained just by describing the right mechanism. Chalmers's meta-problem is the question of why we think there's a hard problem in the first place: why do we judge that experience is ineffable, intrinsic, and hard to explain, even before we've settled whether it really is any of those things. Since the meta-problem just asks us to explain a pattern of judgments and behavior, it's actually one of the easy problems already on the original list Chalmers gave in his 1995 paper, just applied reflexively to our own philosophizing. A good example of a proposed meta-problem solution is the illusionist idea that introspection systematically misrepresents our internal states as simple and ineffable when they're really complex and physical - and that's the solution Chalmers himself favors, which is why he thinks that once the meta-problem is fully solved, the hard problem automatically goes away too, since there'd be nothing left over to explain beyond the misfiring of introspection."
