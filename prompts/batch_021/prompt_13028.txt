My aunt Denise is retiring after something like 33 years as a lead engineer on full-immersion simulator pods -- she started out on flight simulators for pilot training and ended up running the team that builds those VR "experience pod" attractions you see in some malls now, the kind where you strap in and it genuinely feels like your skiing down a mountain or fighting off zombies or whatever. I got asked to give the toast at her retirement dinner next month and I really want to nail it, because half the room is gonna be her engineers who basically worship her, and the other half is family who have no real idea what she does all day.

Heres the thing that makes this fun but also kind of hard: for as long as I can remember, at literally every Thanksgiving, she brings up this thought experiment -- I looked it up and it's called the "experience machine," some old philosophy idea about a machine you could plug into that gives you any experience you want, totally indistinguishable from real life, for the rest of your life, and you'd forget you ever chose it. Every single year she says the same thing, that she'd never plug in. Meanwhile her whole career, her literal life's work, has been building smaller and smaller versions of exactly that machine, and her team jokes about this constantly, like "Denise won't even use her own product." I've been trying to work out on my own whether that's actually a contradiction or not, like does the fact that she's spent decades making people WANT to plug into fake stuff mean her own answer doesn't really hold up, but I can't quite land it myself. I want that whole tangle to be the spine of the toast, done affectionately, not like I'm implying her work is fake or pointless, and I don't want it to just be an inside joke that leaves my grandma and cousins totally lost. By the end I want it to actually land as something true and a little moving about why she's made the choices she has, both in her career and otherwise. Can you write me the toast?
