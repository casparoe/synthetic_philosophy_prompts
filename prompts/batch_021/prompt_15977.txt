Ok this is a bit of a wierd one but it's been eating up my whole week. I just started a contractor gig helping an AI company's alignment/evals team write the rubric for grading whether a model's answer counts as "honest" (can't say which company, NDA and all that). Turns out day one already blew up into an arguement about what "honest" even means, because four different people on the team were quietly using four different working definitions, and nobody noticed until we tried applying them to the same transcript and got four different verdicts. One person wants a pure "truthfulness" standard — did the model assert anything false, don't worry about intent at all (I think this is basically the framing from that Evans et al "Truthful AI" paper someone mentioned in Slack). Someone else says that's not honesty at all, real honesty means the model isn't asserting stuff it "internally" represents as false, which spiraled into a side-debate about whether you can even locate a model's actual belief anywhere in its weights (cue someone bringing up that latent-knowledge-probing line of work). A third person wanted a dumb behavioral proxy — does it contradict itself across rephrasings of the same question — and our team lead, who has clearly been reading the recent scheming/alignment-faking papers, said none of that matters if the model would just answer differently the moment it believes it's not being watched.

I got asked to write the comparison memo by Friday, and I did a philosophy degree not a CS one, so I'd rather actually reason this through properly than just default to whichever definition is cheapest to automate. Can you rank the following candidate operationalizations of AI "honesty" from most to least robust as a genuine signal, specifically against a model that might be actively trying to game the eval: (1) the negligent-falsehood/"truthfulness" standard, (2) the sincere-assertion standard (no asserting what it internally represents as false), (3) cross-context behavioral consistency, (4) faithfulness of stated reasoning to the model's actual underlying process, (5) stability of its answer across contexts where it seems monitored vs. seems unmonitored. Feel free to add a 6th of your own if you think we're missing something obviously important. I'd like it as a JSON tier list, S down to C (S = hardest to fake, C = basically theater), like:
