I'm a PhD student in law (working on negligence and duty-to-rescue doctrine), but I sit in on my university's philosophy colloquium pretty regularly, and this month they've got a metaphysician coming to defend the view that omissions—actual absences, failures to act—can be genuine causes, full stop, not just Dowe's watered-down "quasi-causation." Somehow I got roped into writing questions for the Q&A, probably because I'm the one person in the room who cares about this stuff for non-metaphysical reasons too. I don't want the session to turn into forty minutes of her re-explaining "if you'd watered the plant it wouldn't have died, so your not-watering caused its death" to a room that's already read the pre-circulated paper, so can you draft me like 8-10 real questions?

Her whole strategy for handling the promiscuity problem (my neighbour's not watering the plant is a cause too, and so is literally everyone else on the planet who also didn't water it, unless something rules those out) seems to be some version of "only the failures that violate a norm or a reasonable expectation count as causes." I want a question that goes right at whether that's just smuggling ethics into what's supposed to be a plain natural relation, plus a follow-up for whatever dodge she reaches for first—probably that the norm is really tracking statistical normalcy rather than morality—and then a follow-up to THAT, since what's statistically normal and what's morally expected come apart constantly. I also want something pressing the Beebee-ish worry that an absence isn't an event, so there's nothing there to even be a relatum, and something connecting all this to the Hitchcock/Knobe-type finding that ordinary people's causal judgments about omissions are themselves norm-sensitive — does that data actually vindicate her account, or does it just show the folk intuitions she's building on are already a mess?

Somewhere in there I'd also like a question on whether courts already holding people liable for omissions whenever there was a duty counts as evidence for her metaphysics or is just a totally seperate question that happens to look similar. And end on something about what she thinks would actually count as evidence against the view, because from where I'm sitting the "only the failures that violate a norm" move can absorb literally any counterexample just by redrawing which failures are the relevant ones. For each question, flag the reply she's most likely to give and write the follow-up that closes it off.
