I'm a software engineer on my company's Trust & Safety team, and I've been pulled onto the content-moderation policy committee because I maintain the tooling our moderators use to review flagged posts. We're deciding on a proposed change to that workflow. Right now moderators see everything together: the post, the report reason, the account's strike history, and prior reviewers' notes. The proposal is a "blind first pass" -- moderators judge the post alone, record a verdict and a confidence level, then see the account history and may revise their call. The reasoning, borrowed from blind grading, is that seeing the history first colors what a moderator perceives as hostile or threatening in the post itself, so stripping it out should yield a cleaner read.

A second faction on the committee rejects the premise that there's a clean, context-free read to protect. Their point: the very act of perceiving a comment as hostile rather than sarcastic, or as a slur rather than a reclaimed term, already depends on expectations the moderator brings to it -- that dependence is what lets them resolve ambiguous cases correctly at all, not a contaminant sitting on top of some neutral percept. On their view, withholding account history just swaps one set of priors for another while discarding real diagnostic signal (a slur's valence depends heavily on who's using it), without touching whatever is actually shaping the snap read.

Both sides can point to real cases from our moderation logs. Please draft the two-page memo I'll circulate before next week's vote: a concrete recommendation -- adopt the pilot, adopt some modified version, or shelve it -- what we'd measure and over what period to judge it, and a fair accounting of the strongest point on the losing side.
