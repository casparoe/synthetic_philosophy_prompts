For the past several years I've kept a private log of something I only ever call "the drift" in my own head — a specific inner shift, not quite anxiety and not quite fatigue, that comes over me roughly once a week. Since I could never find an ordinary word that fit it exactly, I started putting a small check mark next to the date in my planner whenever I noticed it, originally out of curiosity about whether it tracked my sleep or diet. Over the years the check mark has become a kind of private shorthand: I don't consciously re-derive what I'm looking for each time, I just seem to recognize the feeling when it arrives. Until recently, I had never described this habit to anyone.

A couple of weeks ago my partner noticed the marks and asked what they meant, and trying to explain the system out loud left me strangely unsettled. I realized that I have no actual way of checking whether I've applied my own private mark consistently over the years; in the moment, all I ever have is the impression that "yes, this is another one of those." I went back and tried cross-referencing the marked days against sleep and stress data I happened to have from a fitness tracker, and while there's a loose overlap, it isn't tight enough to let me just redefine the mark as "a bad night's sleep" instead of whatever it is I think I've been tracking. When I raised this with my partner, they said I obviously know my own feelings better than anyone else could, so there was nothing to worry about — but that answer felt beside the point, since my worry isn't about whether I'm being honest with myself. It's about whether there's any real difference between correctly applying my own private mark and merely feeling, on each occasion, that I've applied it correctly.

I genuinely can't tell whether I'm overthinking an inconsequential habit or have stumbled onto a real problem with the whole idea of tracking something this way. If there is no way, even in principle, to distinguish "using the mark correctly" from "it merely seeming to me that I'm using it correctly," has the log ever tracked anything at all? And is there anything I could change going forward that would actually resolve this, or is a purely private record of this kind doomed from the outset?
