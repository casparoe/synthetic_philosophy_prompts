So I'm outlining a scene for my new novel: a ferry capsizes near a small island and the only rescue boat can reach just one of two life rafts before they drift out of range -- one raft has a lone fisherman clinging to the side, the other has five tourists. I read an early draft of the scene to my writers' group and two people there, Sana and Marcus, got into a whole thing about it afterward that I still haven't been able to shake. (1) Sana said it isn't even a real dilemma -- you save the five, because five deaths is a bigger loss than one, obviously. (2) Marcus said bigger for who? Nobody actually experiences "five deaths" as one big loss the way you'd experience five injuries happening to you; the fisherman's death is total and complete for him, and it doesn't get any less total just because five other people are, separately, also losing everything. So there's no single big loss on the raft side to outweigh the one on the other side -- just five small ones that only add up to something on paper. (3) Sana said that proves too much, because by that logic you'd have to flip a coin between saving one person and saving a hundred, and no one actually believes that, so the numbers have to matter somewhere. (4) Marcus said fine, maybe a coin flip is really only for genuine one-on-one ties -- he admitted the hundred-person case feels different -- but then said that must be because past some point the scale of a disaster starts mattering in its own right, not just each person's individual claim. (5) Sana pounced on that, saying he'd just spent five minutes insisting numbers dont count and was now smuggling them back in through the side door. (6) Marcus didn't have a great comeback, but wouldn't concede the whole thing either -- he just kept insisting that even if he couldn't say exactly where, there's a difference between simple addition and whatever's actually going on morally. They both wanted me to declare a winner, which I dodged, mostly because I want to steal whichever lines are best for the characters later. Could you go through the exchange move by move, say who's got the better of each point and why, and flag anything you think they're both missing? If you've got a gut take on who comes out ahead overall, throw that in too.
