My partner and I hold different ethical priorities: I consider factory farming one of the great moral failures of our era and have been vegetarian for a decade, yet I fly to several conferences a year without much guilt; she considers flying one of the most damaging things a person can do to the climate and hasn't set foot on a plane in years, yet eats meat most days without it troubling her. I'm a PhD student in economics, and this domestic situation keeps nagging at me in a way my usual toolkit doesn't quite resolve. Somewhat jokingly, we've discussed a deal: I give up flying, she gives up meat, even though neither of us is personally bothered enough by our own habit to drop it unilaterally. The arrangement has the shape of gains from trade: each of us gives up the thing we care about less, so the other gets more of the thing they care about more, except the currency is moral conviction rather than money. A friend of ours dismissed the whole idea as essentially carbon-offsetting for one's conscience with a person standing in for a project, and said we'd simply be outsourcing our scruples to each other instead of living by our own stated principles. Is our friend right that something has gone wrong here, or would this arrangement genuinely make the world better by both of our lights at once?
