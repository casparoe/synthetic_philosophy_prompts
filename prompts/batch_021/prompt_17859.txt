I'm a retired analytical chemist, and since retiring i've been slowly working through some philosophy of math for fun. Ran into Skolem's paradox recently and it's really bugging me. As I understand it, the Lowenheim-Skolem theorem guarantees that ZFC set theory has a countable model, if it has any infinite model at all. And yet ZFC also proves Cantor's theorem, that the set of all subsets of the natural numbers is uncountable. So that countable model ends up containing an object that the model itself insists is uncountable. To an old scientist that looks like a flat out contradiction, the kind of result that would make you scrap a theory on the spot, kinda like a machine that violates conservation of energy. But every book I've found just calls it a 'paradox', shrugs, and moves on, like its a curiosity rather than a five alarm fire for the foundations of math. So did Cantor's argument miss something, is ZFC secretly inconsistent, or am I misunderstanding what's actually being claimed here?
