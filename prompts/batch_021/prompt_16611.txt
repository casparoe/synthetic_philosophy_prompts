So I'm a PhD student working on classical Indian epistemology (mostly Nyaya and Jaina material), and this week's reading group did Graham Priest's paper trying to read syadvada as a paraconsistent logic, plus the pushback on that (Ganeri, if im remembering the name right) saying that's basically anachronistic and misses what the saptabhangi is actually for. We spent like an hour arguing about whether "syat asti nasti ca" is a genuine contradiction being tolerated or just a relativized claim that was never contradictory to begin with, and I don't think anyone left more sure of anything, myself included.

Anyway, this has nothing to do with my actual dissertation but I can't stop chewing on it: every single popularization of anekantavada reaches for the blind men and the elephant, which as far as I can tell isn't even from a canonical Jain source, and worse, it flattens the whole doctrine into "everyone's a little bit right," which totally misses the actual bite of it -- the nayavada apparatus, the idea that dogmatic one-sidedness (ekanta) is basically a form of himsa, and especially the weird status the whole apparatus has once you bring in kevala jnana. A kevalin doesn't need "syat" qualifiers, since they just directly perceive all the infinite modes of a thing at once -- so conditional predication is explicitly a concession made for the rest of us non-omniscient chadmastha types, and even a Tirthankara's own teaching supposedly has to get broken down and translated by the ganadharas into the sprawling, occasionally-conflicting textual tradition we actually inherited.

Could you write me a short story (a few pages, doesn't need to be long) that actually lives inside that gap, rather than just describing it -- between a complete, non-perspectival grasp of something and the necessarily partial, syat-qualified language that has to carry it to people who aren't omniscient? No elephants, please. It doesn't have to be set in ancient Magadha either -- modern, or even sci-fi, is fine if that helps you dodge the "ancient parable" register. I just want the ideas to be earned by the plot, not stapled onto it.
