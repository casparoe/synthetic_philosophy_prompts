So I'm working on a piece about whether AI can grade technical homework as reliably as a human TA, and a friend who teaches intro game theory sent over one exam question plus a students answer as a test case - she asked me to get a second opinion before she posts grades, so I said I'd throw it at you first, no pressure!

Here's the question:

"Consider the two-player game below (entries are Player 1's payoff, Player 2's payoff). Player 1 picks a row (U, M, or D), Player 2 picks a column (L or R), simultaneously.

        L         R
  U   3, 3      0, 2
  M   0, 2      3, 3
  D   1, 1      1, 1

(a) Carry out iterated elimination of dominated strategies, allowing for dominance by mixed strategies as well as pure ones. Show your work at each round.
(b) Would the final set of surviving strategies have come out any different if you'd eliminated strategies in a different order? Explain why or why not, in general."

And here's the student's answer, word for word:

"(a) No pure strategy dominates D for Player 1 - U beats D at L but looses at R, and M beats D at R but looses at L. But mix U and M together and D falls: playing U and M each with probability 1/2 gives an expected payoff of 1.5 against either L or R, which beats D's guaranteed payoff of 1 either way. So D is strictly dominated by the mixed strategy (½U, ½M) and gets eliminated. More generally, any mixture of U and M dominates D, since D never does better than 1 and a blend of U and M always averages out to something higher. Once D is gone, look at the reduced 2x2 game: does L dominate R for Player 2, or vice versa? At U, L gives 3 > R's 2; at M, L gives 2 < R's 3. Neither column dominates, so nothing more can be eliminated. Final surviving strategies: {U, M} for Player 1, {L, R} for Player 2.

(b) No, the order never matters. That's the whole point of iterated dominance - whether you eliminate one strategy at a time or several at once, and no matter which dominated strategy goes first, you always converge on the same surviving set, and this holds whether the dominance involved is strict or weak."
