So my friend Mara and I got into a whole thing last night about panpsychism (she's a lot more sold on it then I am) and it ended in this weird stalemate about the combination problem that's been bugging me since. Her big move was that physical stuff combines into bigger physical stuff all the time — quarks into protons, atoms into molecules, cells into organs — so why is it supposed to be some special mystery that tiny bits of proto-experience combine into my one unified experience of, I don't know, drinking a beer. I said it feels different somehow but couldn't actually say why, and she said that's just me being spooked by unfamiliarity, not an actual argument. We went in circles for like an hour and neither of us budged.

Afterwards I looked into it a bit and it turns out there isn't just one "combination problem" — people split it into different versions. There's a "subject" version, about how a bunch of separate micro-subjects could somehow become, or give rise to, one single macro-subject, without either those micro-subjects sticking around alongside the new one in some weirdly crowded way, or just getting erased with no story about where they went. And there's a separate "quality" (or structural) version thats less about subjects merging and more about whether experiential qualities can even be built up out of parts-and-arrangement the way physical stuff obviously can. Apparently Goff's fix for some of this is a "phenomenal bonding" relation, which sounds to me like it might just be relabeling the mystery instead of solving it, but I honestly can't tell if that's a cheap shot or a fair complaint.

Can you actually walk me through this properly, in a way that would've helped me win that argument? Specifically I want: what's the real disanalogy panpsychists have in mind between ordinary physical composition and experiential "composition" (not just "it feels weird"), what the subject-vs-quality split adds on top of that, and whether phenomenal bonding is a genuine answer or just a name for the gap. I don't need the full literature review, just enough that I actually understand the shape of the problem instead of vibing about it the way we were.
