I'm giving the toast at my colleague's retirement dinner next month. She's spent nearly forty years teaching trolley problems and the doctrine of double effect, and about a decade ago she did to her own seminar what Thompson did to the whole field in "Turning the Trolley" -- stood up one day and announced she no longer thought the bystander may flip the switch, after twenty-odd years of teaching the opposite. Her former grad students (a decent chunk of the guest list) know exactly why that's not just "she changed her mind" but actually knocks out the one intuition the whole bystander/footbridge literature was built on top of. Her husband and her sister just remember her being unbearable about it at Christmas for a year. Give me a five-minute toast, built around one good joke about the reversal, that works for both halves of the room -- it needs to land as affection, not a lecture on double effect, and I don't want it dumbed down so far that the double-effect people start rolling their eyes.
