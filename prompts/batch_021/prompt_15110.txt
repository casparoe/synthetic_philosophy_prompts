In pieces examining whether large language models truly understand language, writers often invoke John Searle's Chinese Room and Ned Block's China Brain (sometimes called the Chinese Nation) almost interchangeably, as though the two thought experiments were just different costumes for the same objection to "mere mechanism." Before repeating that move in a piece I'm drafting, I'd like to understand exactly where it breaks down. Please compare the two thought experiments closely: what each is actually designed to establish — intentionality and meaning in Searle's case, phenomenal consciousness and qualia in Block's — and what is doing the philosophical work in each setup, given that one relies on a single rule-following clerk and the other on an entire population standing in for individual neurons. Do the standard replies to the Chinese Room, especially the Systems Reply, transfer cleanly onto the China Brain case, or does something get lost or gained in translation? And if a critic ran both arguments together against the same opponent, would they reinforce each other, or would defending one quietly undercut the other?
