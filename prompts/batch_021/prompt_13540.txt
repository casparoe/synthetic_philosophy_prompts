So I fell down a Wikipedia rabbit hole a few weeks ago that started with the Curry-Howard correspondence (don't ask) and somehow ended on Gottlob Frege's page — the guy who basically invented modern predicate logic and the sense/reference distinction. Turns out that near the end of his life he kept a private diary that only got found and published decades after he died, and its apparently full of genuinely ugly stuff — anti-democratic rants against the Weimar republic, antisemitic remarks about stripping Jews of political rights, that kind of thing. It kind of messed with my picture of the guy, since in every logic textbook he's just "the father of modern logic," this careful, almost machine-like thinker. I brought it up with my partner who's doing a philosphy PhD, and she said this is actually something philosophers genuinely go back and forth on — whether it should change how we read his actual philosophical work (the Begriffsschrift, his weird platonist "third realm" for objective senses/thoughts, his loathing of psychologism about logic) or whether that's a totally separate matter from the politics. She mentioned that Michael Dummett — like THE guy most responsible for reviving Frege's reputation in English-language philosophy — wrote something about being genuinely shaken when he found out, though I could be misremembering that part.

Anyway I can't stop chewing on it, and I'd rather see the actual disagreement played out properly than get a flat "obviously it's irrelevant" or "obviously it taints everything." Could you write me a dialogue between two philosophers who both take the question seriously — one arguing the diary has basically no bearing on the soundness or value of Frege's logic and philosophy of language, and the other arguing it's not that easy to wall off — where they really engage with each other's strongest moves instead of either one being a pushover? I'd like at least a couple of rounds where each side has to respond to a real objection rather than just restating their opening position, and it's totally fine if they don't reach a neat resolution by the end.
