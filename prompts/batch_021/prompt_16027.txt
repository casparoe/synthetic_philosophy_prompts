I'm a philosophy of science PhD student, and I've grown frustrated with popular treatments of underdetermination that quietly collapse into its weaker, transient form: the idea that we simply lack enough data yet, and some future experiment could settle things. I'd like a short story that instead sits with the stronger, Duhem–Quine-style thesis: that two rival theories of the world could be empirically equivalent at every possible observation, so that no amount of further evidence, even in principle, could favor one over the other. Feel free to draw on real cases along these lines, such as Bohmian mechanics versus orthodox quantum mechanics, or Lorentz's ether theory versus Einstein's special relativity, where the observable predictions coincide exactly but the pictures of reality underneath differ sharply. Beyond the epistemic setup, I'd like the story to dramatize the practical bind this creates for its characters: whether to adopt one theory as a working convention, treat both as equally legitimate, or refuse to commit to either. Characters, setting, and plot are entirely up to you.
