So I've been noodling on a short story about Thomas Hobbes and his weirdly stubborn belief that he'd squared the circle. Apparently he didn't even get into geometry until his forties — found a copy of Euclid lying open in some gentleman's library, saw the Pythagorean theorem, said 'that's impossible,' and worked backwards through the proofs until he was hooked for life. Which is what makes it kind of tragic that this is the same guy who spent basically the rest of his life getting publicly demolished by John Wallis over his circle-squaring 'proofs,' and never once backed down even as the pamphlets got nastier and he got quietly frozen out of the Royal Society. Can you write me a short story, a few pages, that gets inside Hobbes's head during that whole fight? I don't want a biography lecture, i want a scene (or two) that makes his stubbornness feel like it comes from the same place as Leviathan — this need for geometric-grade certainty about everything, politcs included.
