So this happened at a family bbq a couple weekends ago and its been rattling around in my head since. My cousin Danny did four years in the army (never deployed, mostly training and one peacekeeping stint) and somehow after a few beers he ended up in a full blown argument with his girlfriend Rae about whether an ordinary soldier fighting on the "wrong side" of a war — like, a war everyone later agrees was a straight up unjustified invasion — is basically a murderer, or whether that's a crazy standard to hold a regular grunt to. I wasn't really part of it, just sat there with my burger watching it go back and forth, and I honestly still couldn't tell you who had the better of it.

It went roughly like this. (1) Danny opened with: a soldier who's drafted, or even signs up on his own, and just does what he's trained to do — follows the rules of engagement, doesn't target civilians, all that — hasn't done anything wrong, even if it turns out his government started the war for rotten reasons. Soldiers on both sides are in the same boat, he said, they're equally entitled to defend themselves and shoot back once they're in it, and it's the politicians who started the thing who deserve the blame, not the guy in the trench. (2) Rae said that's backwards: if your side's war is unjust, you don't actually have a right to kill the other guy, because he's not doing anything wrong to you — he's defending his country from an actual invasion — while you're the one attacking, so him getting killed by you would be more like a mugger who gets to claim self defense because his victim fought back. (3) Danny said okay, but then you're judging some 19 year old private on whether he correctly worked out the geopolitics of a war he's being fed propaganda about, possibly at gunpoint if he's a conscript in the wrong kind of country — and that's an insane thing to expect of him. Nobody thinks a soldier should have to independently verify his government's foreign policy before he's allowed to pull the trigger. (4) Rae said that just means he might not be to blame for it — like he's got a decent excuse — but that's different from saying what he did was actually fine. A getaway driver who genuinely had no clue his buddy was robbing the bank might not be guilty of anything, she said, but that doesn't mean robbing the bank was okay, it just means the driver gets a pass. Danny said that distinction sounds like a word game to him, and that's about where it fell apart into "well what about the Nuremberg trials" and somebody changing the subject to dessert.
