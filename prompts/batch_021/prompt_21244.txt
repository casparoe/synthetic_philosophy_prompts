Been working through the old MIRI tiling agents paper (Yudkowsky and Herreshoff, I think?) for my advisor's reading group on reflective AI systems, and I don't get why the Löbian obstacle is treated as a problem instead of a fix. Löb's theorem shows that any consistent system strong enough for arithmetic can prove the schema 'if I can prove P, then P is true' -- which is exactly the self-trust guarantee you'd want if you're getting an agent to certify a successor version of itself before deferring to it. So why does everyone in the alignment lit act like this is a wall you crash into rather than the theorem that actually lets tiling go through? feel like im missing something obvious here.
