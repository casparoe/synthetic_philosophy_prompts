I'm a novelist and long-time member of the ethics and standards committee at a mid-sized writers' association. A member with a documented bipolar diagnosis, during a manic episode, sent threatening messages to another member and published an essay lifting several paragraphs nearly verbatim from her unpublished manuscript, which he had read in an association workshop. He has since stabilized, remains in treatment, and says that although he knew what he was typing, his judgment simply "wasn't his own." Our bylaws authorize sanctions from a warning to expulsion for conduct done "knowingly and willfully" but say nothing about a case like this. The committee has split. Some worry that leniency teaches every harmed member a diagnosis is a trump card; others think full answerability ignores that he wasn't, in the relevant sense, the one acting. Draft the memo I'll circulate before next week's vote on his membership. It should recommend one concrete course of action with a timeline, and take seriously the strongest reason a thoughtful member would have for opposing it, not just mention that reason in passing.
