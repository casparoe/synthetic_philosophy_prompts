I'm a few stories into a linked collection where each piece is built around one specific metaphysical argument that I try to smuggle into an actual plot instead of just lecturing the reader -- I'm going for something like a Ted Chiang or Greg Egan vibe. The one I keep chickening out of is about laws of nature, specifically that fight between people who think a law is basically just the best, most economical summary of everything that happens to be true across the entire history of the universe -- so nothing is actually "forcing" anything, there's no real necessity, and something like conservation of energy is just the tidiest possible headline for an enormous pile of otherwise unconnected facts -- versus people who think that gets it backwards, that the necessity is baked into what the things themselves are, so an electron literally could not fail to repel another electron and still count as an electron. (I think the first camp gets called "Humean" about laws, if I'm remembering my undergrad notes right, don't quote me on it.) I did that philosophy minor a lifetime ago, and I brought this up at dinner with my partner, who's a physicist, and we ended up arguing about it long enough that I decided I should finally write the story instead of just cornering people with it at parties.

Here's the setup I want to build it around, though push back if it doesn't hold together: a small, unglamorous physics lab keeps getting a result that reproduces perfectly -- inside one sealed fifteen-centimeter chamber, under very exact conditions, they measure more energy coming out than went in. Every single time. Nowhere else does it happen, it's not remotely useful for anything (way too small to run so much as a light bulb), but its completely real and repeatable and nobody can find the leak. I want two characters at the lab -- old collaborators, exes, an advisor and a former student, whatever you think earns it -- who have opposite gut reactions to the result, and crucially I want their disagreement to actually track the philosophical split above rather than collapsing into a generic "is this a hoax or a miracle" story.

Could you write the actual short story (a few thousand words, doesn't need to be a doorstop)? I'd rather it not resolve into a reveal that it's a simulation glitch, a hoax, or divine intervention -- I want the ending to leave the underlying metaphysical disagreement genuinely open, since I don't think one weird experiment should get to settle something that deep anyway.
