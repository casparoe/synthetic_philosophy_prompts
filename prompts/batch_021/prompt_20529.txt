So I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole reading decision theory stuff online lately (started with some AI safety blogs, ended up buried in lesswrong posts), and the thing that really messes with my head is "counterfactual mugging." Quick recap in case it helps: Omega, the perfect predictor who never lies, flips a fair coin. If it lands tails, Omega gives you $10,000 -- but only if it had already predicted you'd hand over $100 in the case it came up heads instead. If it lands heads, Omega just walks up and asks you for the $100, no further strings attached. A regular causal decision theorist (CDT), once they're actually staring at a heads coin, has zero causal reason to pay -- the tails world already isn't real, and paying now can't make it real. But updateless decision theory (UDT) says pay anyway, because the kind of agent who pays does better on average across both branches, even though this particular agent, this one time, is just being asked to hand over cash for literally nothing back.

Can you write me a dialogue between the UDT person and the CDT person, set at the exact moment Omega is stood there with its hand out after the heads flip? I want the CDT person to really push on "why should a commitment from a scenario that never even happened bind me right now, nothing is forcing me" -- and I want the UDT person to have an actual answer to that instead of just restating "expected value across both branches" again. Then have the CDT person hit back with something like "ok then what stops anyone who just claims to be Omega from shaking me down like this forever." Make them genuinely respond to each others points instead of just alternating speeches.
