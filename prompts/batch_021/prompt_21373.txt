Okay so I've been down a mechanistic interpretability rabbit hole for a while now (Anthropic's superposition and sparse autoencoder papers, the Golden Gate Claude demo, Neel Nanda's posts on the alignment forum) and it turned into whole argument with a friend of mine about whether any of this actually licenses *realism* about representations in neural nets, or whether we're just doing a fancier version of saying a thermostat "believes" the room is cold. My friend is fully Chris-Olah-brained about it — circuits are like a periodic table, we're literally doing biology on the network, features are just as real as neurons in a brain — and I want you to push back hard on that, but in a way that actually engages with the strongest evidence rather than just going "lol it's just matmuls."

Here's the specific hook I want you to use. There's this move from philosphy of cognitive science — I believe it's William Ramsey's "job description challenge," from Representation Reconsidered — which says that reliably co-varying with, or even causally mediating behavior in response to, some feature of the world doesn't automatically make something a representation of it. A thermostat's bimetallic strip is causally sensitive to temperature and drives the furnace, but on Ramsey's view that's a mere "receptor," not a representation, because nothing downstream actually *uses* it as a stand-in the way a cognitive map gets consulted. To earn the title "representation" in a non-deflationary sense, something has to be exploited for its structural correspondence to whatever it's supposedly about — ideally even "offline." The obvious hard case for my friend's position is stuff like the Othello-GPT result (plus Neel Nanda's linear-probe follow-up), where the network seems to carry around something that's actually queried like a little board-state model, not just a feature detector that lights up when it sees a pattern.

So: please argue that even granting the best current cases like that, we're still not entitled to full-blown realism about representations in today's neural networks — either because SAE-derived features, and even Othello-GPT-style "world models," don't actually clear Ramsey's bar once you look closely at how (or whether) they're used downstream, or because clearing that bar wouldn't get my friend the kind of thick metaphysical realism he actually wants anyway. Please don't give me the lazy version of this argument ("it's just linear algebra, so it can't really represent anything") — take the job description challenge seriously as the standard, and either make an actual case for why current networks still fall short of it, or explain why meeting it wouldn't settle the realism question the way interpretability people seem to assume it does.
