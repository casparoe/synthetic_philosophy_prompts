I am a software engineer who runs the A/B tests for our checkout flow. In our latest test, the treatment showed a 1.5% lift in overall conversion, so we shipped it. A data scientist later discovered that it had actually underperformed control within every individual traffic source (paid, organic, referral); the aggregate lift appeared only because the treatment ramped up gradually over the test window just as paid traffic increased, leaving the treatment group with a disproportionate share of paid users, who convert better regardless of checkout flow. She called this an instance of Simpson's paradox and argued that we should always trust segment-level numbers over topline ones, but a colleague countered that this is not automatically true either, since it depends on the causal relationship between the segmenting variable, the treatment, and the outcome. I have no formal background in statistics or philosophy. Please explain the most important implications of Simpson's paradox for how my team should design and interpret A/B tests, including how we should decide whether to trust an aggregated or a segmented result.
