I'm drafting a piece on the philosophical fallout of Skolem's paradox and want to be sure I have the upshot right before it goes to print. As I understand it, the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem guarantees that Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory, if it has a model at all, has a countable one, even though that same theory proves that sets like the real numbers are uncountable. It seems to follow that Cantor's proof of uncountability is less a discovery about mathematical reality than an artifact of whichever model happens to be in play, so that, seen from the right vantage point, the continuum turns out to be countable after all. If uncountability can be relativized away this easily, I'm not sure what objective content is left in the claim that some infinities are bigger than others. Have I got the moral of the paradox right?
