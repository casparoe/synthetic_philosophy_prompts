Ayer is best known for using the verification principle from Language, Truth and Logic to dismiss talk of God and an afterlife as strictly meaningless. His actual position on personal survival is more careful than that reputation suggests: he treats the claim that one's own stream of experience simply continues after bodily death as empirically meaningful, since it describes something the survivor could in principle verify, and reserves the charge of meaninglessness for a soul or spiritual substance whose persistence could never be experienced by anyone at all. I'm trying to work out how this framework, together with Ayer's own well-documented, equivocal reaction to his 1988 cardiac arrest and the essay he wrote about it afterward, "What I Saw When I Was Dead," would shape his verdict on the hospital research into near-death experience that has emerged since his death — studies associated with the physician Sam Parnia, for instance, which conceal target images near the ceiling of resuscitation rooms specifically to test whether patients who report leaving their bodies can later identify them. If such a study ever produced one rigorously documented positive case, would that satisfy Ayer's own bar for meaningfulness on this question, or would he say it settles only a narrow empirical matter that is quite separate from the metaphysical one everyone actually cares about?
