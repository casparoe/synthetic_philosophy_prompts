I'm a software engineer, but for the past three years I've also been one of five parents who sit on the governing board of a small cooperative "micro-school" that our five founding families started when regular school stopped working during the pandemic and simply never disbanded. We rent two rooms from a community center, jointly employ two part-time teachers, and currently have about twenty kids between six and eleven attending four days a week. When we founded the pod we wrote a one-page "educational philosophy" that nobody has looked at since, which says, among other things, that discipline should "help children build their own judgment and character, rather than simply securing compliance." That sentence is suddenly at the center of a real dispute.

One of our two teachers wants to roll out a points-and-badges behavior app, in the style of ClassDojo, across all the age groups: kids earn points on a shared screen for things like finishing work on time or helping a classmate, lose points for the opposite, and cash points in each week for small prizes she'd buy out of the supply budget. She argues that it has replaced what was effectively discipline-by-whoever's-patience-ran-out-that-day with something consistent and transparent, and two of my fellow board members are enthusiastic, partly because their own kids have responded well to it. Two others are opposed, arguing that a running scoreboard redeemable for treats is precisely the "compliance" mechanism our philosophy statement was meant to rule out, and that it teaches kids to perform for points rather than to want to be kind or careful for its own sake; one of them, a longtime homeschooler, keeps invoking Locke's writing on education to back this up. That leaves me the deciding vote, which is less comfortable than it sounds, since I hadn't opened a philosophy book since a college gen-ed requirement and have sat through enough "gamification" rollouts at work to distrust dashboards and leaderboards on principle without wanting to just be voting my priors. So I actually went and read some of "Some Thoughts Concerning Education," and there's a lot more in there bearing on this than the "blank slate" line I already half-knew (which, I now gather, isn't even from that book): Locke's specific arguments about rewards, punishment, esteem and disgrace, and how habits actually get formed seem to cut in more than one direction on our situation, if I'm reading him right.

The board meets next Tuesday to vote, and since I'm the swing vote I've been asked to circulate a short memo beforehand laying out a recommendation and the reasoning behind it. Could you draft it? It should come down clearly on adopting the app, rejecting it, or adopting some modified version (a trial period, a changed reward structure, whatever you think defensible), and it should do real interpretive work connecting that recommendation to Locke's actual arguments rather than just invoking his name the way our board member does. It should also take the teacher's practical argument seriously rather than waving it away, and it should leave the board with something concrete to check on after a semester so we aren't having the identical argument again in the spring.
