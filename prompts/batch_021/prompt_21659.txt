Our school newspaper's faculty advisor spent this whole year having the editorial staff read Kierkegaard's essay "The Present Age" right alongside our actual reporting, insisting that his account of how an idle, gossiping press dissolves individuals into the abstraction he calls "the public" -- leveling away whatever made them distinct -- says more about a school that runs on group chats and anonymous confession accounts than anything in our journalism textbook. I'm the outgoing editor-in-chief, and at our spring banquet I have to give the toast handing the paper off to my successor, in front of a room that's half staff and our advisor, who will notice immediately if I get Kierkegaard wrong or just drop his name for flavor, and half parents, siblings, and administrators who have never read a word of him. Please write that toast, about three minutes long, built around the irony that Kierkegaard himself was later mocked mercilessly by an actual satirical newspaper: it can't collapse into an uncomplicated tribute to journalism, but it still has to send the room off warm, proud of the paper, and laughing rather than lectured at.
