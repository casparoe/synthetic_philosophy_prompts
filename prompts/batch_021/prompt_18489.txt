I've been turning Aumann's agreement theorem over in my head ever since I first came across a summary of it, and as far as I can tell, it shows that two honest, rational people cannot go on disagreeing about the same question once each of them knows what the other believes -- because simply learning the other person's honest opinion should be enough for both of them to converge on the same view. If that's right, it seems to have an unsettling consequence: whenever two intelligent, sincere people argue something out at length -- over politics, religion, or anything else -- and still end up disagreeing, at least one of them must be reasoning badly, since it shouldn't be possible for two genuinely rational people to face the same disagreement and both remain rational at the end of it. Have I understood the theorem correctly, and if so, does it tell us anything about which side of a persistent disagreement is more likely to be the irrational one?
