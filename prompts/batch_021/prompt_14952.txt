I'm a philosophy professor who sits on my hospital's mass-casualty triage committee as its resident ethicist, and I've been asked to draft the rationale for our scarce-resource protocol before it goes to the medical board next month. The clause in dispute: a single vial of antitoxin can be given whole to one patient who needs the full dose, or split into five partial doses that would save five other patients who each need only a fifth as much. Most members want a flat rule favoring the larger group, since five deaths are worse than one. A minority, invoking Taurek's argument that separate people's losses cannot be summed the way burdens on a single person can, wants the protocol to instead specify a lottery giving the one patient a genuine chance rather than automatically favoring the five. Please draft the memo: recommend one policy with a concrete decision procedure clinicians can follow under time pressure, and make sure whichever side loses -- simply saving the greater number, or Taurek's objection to letting numbers decide -- gets a serious hearing rather than a dismissal.
