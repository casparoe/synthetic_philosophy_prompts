So a pet peeve of mine: every pop-sci treatment of Zahavi's handicap principle teaches it as "costly = honest, the peacock's tail is a wasteful proof of quality and that's why it works." But that's not actually what Grafen's ESS model requires - the thing doing the real work is that the marginal cost of upping your signal has to fall off faster for high-quality signalers then low-quality ones (basically Spence's single-crossing condition, wearing feathers). Make the case that the handicap principle, read literally as a claim about waste or absolute cost, is kind of a red herring - what actually sustains honest signaling is this differential-cost/sorting structure, and the equilibrium cost paid by the honest signaler could in principle be quite low. Use your own stripped-down signaling example (please, not peacocks) to make the sorting condition do the work instead of "cost."
