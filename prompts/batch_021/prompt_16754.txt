I'm teaching an upper-level phil of science seminar this term and we just spent two sessions on Popper's verisimilitude, and I still don't think I explained the punchline as well as I wanted to, so let me try it out on you instead. Popper needed a notion of "closer to the truth" that could apply even to theories he already conceded were false -- the whole point being that we're rational to prefer, say, Einstein over Newton even though neither is exactly true. His original qualitative definition ranked theories by comparing their true logical consequences (truth-content) against their false ones (falsity-content). Then Tichý and Miller each published papers in '74, more or less independantly, showing that this definition is basically useless for real science: on Popper's own criterion it turns out no false theory can ever count as more verisimilar then any other false theory. Since Popper thinks literally every scientific theory we've ever had is false, that result guts the whole application of the concept, not just some edge cases.

What I want from you is an actual essay (not just a survey) on whether verisimilitude can be rescued after Tichý-Miller. Get into the later "similarity" or "distance-from-the-truth" measures that people like Niiniluoto and Oddie proposed to replace Popper's subset approach, and also engage with the worry that these quantitative measures just relocate the problem, since your ranking of which theory is closer to the truth ends up depending on which language or set of properties you carve the world up with in the first place. Weigh the case for thinking some version of truthlikeness is rescuable and doing real philosophical work against the case for thinking Popperians should quietly drop the notion and reach for something else instead (probabilistic confirmation, a Kuhnian story about theory change, whatever). Don't give me a "reasonable people disagree" ending -- I want an actual verdict, defended, even if it's a hedged one like "it works for toy propositional languages but not for anything resembling real scientific theories."
