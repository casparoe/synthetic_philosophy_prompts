Random tangent from my diss reading that I can't shake: Gregory Kavka (the toxin puzzle guy) was also a serious Hobbes scholar -- wrote that whole book on Hobbesian moral and political theory -- and the more I sit with it, the more the puzzle looks like a Hobbesian covenant problem dressed up in decision-theory clothes. So, genuine question: what do you think Hobbes himself would've said about it?

(Quick refresher in case its not fresh: an eccentric billionaire pays you a million dollars if, by midnight, you simply intend to drink a nasty-but-harmless toxin tomorrow -- you don't actually have to go through with it, the money's yours before the drinking deadline even arrives.)

Would Hobbes say the intention is basically a covenant with your future self, and therefore void from the jump for the same reason he thinks covenants of mutual trust collapse in the state of nature absent a common power? Or does he have some other move available -- reputation effects, the Foole passage, whatever -- that lets the intention still count as rational even with zero external enforcement and the "other party" being just future-you? I genuinely don't see how his usual fix for covenant-keeping (fear of the sovereign) is even supposed to apply when there's no one around to punish but yourself.
