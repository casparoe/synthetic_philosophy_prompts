I help moderate the Discord server for my school's philosophy and effective-altruism club, which has outgrown its original membership and now includes a number of outside high schoolers who found the server through a rationalist blog that linked to us. Our written charter says only that moderators "may remove content that is unduly distressing to members," without specifying what that means in practice, and this week that vagueness turned into a real dispute. A member posted her own write-up of Roko's basilisk: the argument that a future benevolent superintelligence, reasoning in something like Yudkowsky's timeless decision theory or Wei Dai's updateless decision theory, could benefit from committing in advance to harm anyone who understood the argument and failed to help bring the AI into being sooner, since the mere predictability of that policy — not any causal signal actually sent backward in time — is supposed to be what moves people today. The name is a nod to the mythical serpent whose gaze was said to kill anyone who looked at it, since on this view merely comprehending the argument is what exposes you to the threat.

Two younger members told me privately that they had genuinely lost sleep over it, which a few people compared to what reportedly happened when the argument first circulated on LessWrong back in 2010, before Eliezer Yudkowsky deleted the thread and restricted discussion of the topic for years afterward. Other members are now accusing the mod team of wanting to censor an idea simply because it makes people uncomfortable, pointing out that Yudkowsky's own attempt at suppression is usually credited as the reason the idea became so notorious in the first place, rather than the other way around.

Our mod team meets tomorrow and has narrowed the options to three: delete the post and ban the topic going forward, leave it exactly as it stands, or leave it up while pinning a rebuttal and moving any future discussion of it into an opt-in channel with a content warning. I have been asked to write the recommendation memo the team will actually vote on, and I do not want it to just lay out both sides and shrug. Could you draft that memo for me? Please commit to one of the three options rather than splitting the difference, ground the recommendation in the actual structure of the acausal-blackmail argument rather than just its internet reputation, engage seriously with Nick Bostrom's "information hazard" framing as a possible justification for restricting content independent of whether it is true, and explain concretely why the free-inquiry objection is, or is not, strong enough to outweigh it.
