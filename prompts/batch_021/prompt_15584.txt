Write a dialogue between two AI alignment researchers arguing about the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents. One of them is convinced the only honest fix stays inside proof theory -- stuff like parametric polymorphism / tiling agents, where an agent is only ever allowed to trust a strictly weaker copy of itself, never an equal, so Löb's theorem never gets a foothold. The other thinks that just defers the problem down an infinite regress of weaker and weaker systems, and that real self-trust has to go probabilistic -- logical induction, reflective oracles, that whole direction. Make them actually engage with each other's strongest point instead of just restating their own side -- e.g. does the probabilistic route actually dodge Löb's theorem, or does it just relocate the same tension somewhere the theorem doesn't technically apply but something just as bad is still lurking?
