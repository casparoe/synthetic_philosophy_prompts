Ok, this has been rattling around in my head for a while. Molina came up with "middle knowledge" (scientia media) specifically to explain how God can know with complete certainty what a free creature would do in any hypothetical circumstance, without that knowledge being what causes the creature to do it -- the truth of the counterfactual is suppose to depend on the creature's free choice, not the other way around. That's structurally almost identical to the Omega setup in Newcomb's problem: an agent that reliably knows what you're about to freely choose before you choose it.

So here's my question: if you sat Molina down and explained Newcomb's problem to him, what do you think he'd say? Would his own framework commit him to saying Omega's prediction is compatible with your choice still being free, the same way he thinks God's foreknowledge is? And if so, does his theory actually push him toward one-boxing or two-boxing, or does it just dissolve the freedom worry without settling the decision theory question? Bonus points if you can tie your answer into how Molinists handle the grounding objection against counterfactuals of freedom.
