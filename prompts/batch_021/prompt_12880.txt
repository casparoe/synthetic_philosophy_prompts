Defenders of Kelly betting sometimes advance a claim stronger than that maximizing expected log wealth is a sensible strategy for repeated, compounding gambles: they hold that it is the uniquely correct rule for any rational agent facing such gambles. The "ergodicity economics" program associated with Ole Peters presses this claim by arguing that ordinary expected-utility theory covertly evaluates a gamble by its "ensemble average" across hypothetical parallel outcomes, when what actually matters to an agent living through a single history is her own time-average growth rate; since the two diverge precisely for multiplicative gambles, log-optimal ("geometric") betting is presented as following from the structure of the gamble itself, rather than from any contestable attitude toward risk. Critics, extending a line of objection associated with Paul Samuelson's attacks on Kelly betting, deny that this move dispenses with a utility function at all: privileging the almost-sure long-run growth rate over, say, expected terminal wealth at a fixed horizon, or protection against a ruinous drawdown along the way, is itself a substantive evaluative commitment, not a value-free discovery.

Write an essay that engages with the strongest form of each side of this dispute, and that reaches and defends a definite verdict on whether geometric rationality is rationally mandatory for compounding gambles or is instead merely one legitimate risk attitude among others.
