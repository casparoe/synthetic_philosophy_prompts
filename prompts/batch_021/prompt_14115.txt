I've spent a good chunk of my free time over the last year or two reading through Isaac Newton's theological writings, purely as a hobbyist with no background in theology or history — I got pulled in once I learned how much more he wrote about biblical prophecy than about physics. Two things about his actual views matter here: he was a convinced anti-Trinitarian who believed the doctrine had been fraudulently imposed on the church by Athanasius and the Council of Nicaea, and in his commentaries on Daniel and Revelation he was a strict historicist, reading the prophetic symbols as being fulfilled gradually across real church history rather than as a drama still mostly ahead of us — all while explicitly criticizing confident date-setters for bringing prophecy into disrepute. Given those two commitments, what do you think Newton would have made of the pretribulational, futurist "rapture" theology that only took shape after his death, starting with John Nelson Darby in the 1830s and later spreading through the Scofield Reference Bible into mainstream American evangelicalism — a system that treats almost all of Revelation as still future, and that is overwhelmingly a Trinitarian phenomenon? Would he have recognized it as a natural extension of his own project, or as everything he spent his life warning against, on both fronts?
