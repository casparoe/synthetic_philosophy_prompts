Write a dialogue between a proponent of counterpart theory and a proponent of transworld identity (the view that one and the same individual can be numerically identical across distinct possible worlds). The two meet to work through Chisholm's paradox: two actual individuals are linked by a long chain of possible worlds, each only minutely different from the last in the relevant individual's properties, so that any two neighboring worlds in the chain seem, intuitively, to contain the very same individual, while the two ends of the chain plainly do not. Each philosopher should explain how their theory handles the chain, then press the other on whether their proposed solution genuinely dissolves the paradox or merely relocates it, responding to the specific objection just raised rather than restating their own position.
