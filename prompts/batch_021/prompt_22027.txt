I'm a PhD student in philosophy. My dissertation is on a fairly unrelated corner of philosophy of mind, but this term I've been sitting in on a graduate seminar on formal epistemology and rational choice - which is probably why a small, faintly ridiculous situation in my shared office has been bothering me more than it should. Three of us in my cohort split an office with three desks: one by the window, which gets lovely light but sits directly under a noisy air vent; one next to the printer, which is central and convenient but constantly interrupted by people making copies; and one in the back corner, which is quiet and out of the way but dim and a little isolating. Comparing any two of these desks head-to-head, I find I have a firm preference every time: I'd rather have the window desk than the printer desk, since the light matters more to me than the foot traffic; I'd rather have the printer desk than the corner desk, since I'd rather be interrupted occasionally than feel cut off; but when I compare the corner desk directly against the window desk, I actually prefer the corner desk, because in that particular match-up the vent noise is what I mind most. Each of these three judgments feels completely sincere and carefully considered when taken on its own, yet laid side by side they form a loop, with no single desk coming out on top overall.

This stopped being a merely amusing curiosity once I noticed a pattern in how we informally reshuffle desks every few weeks. Whenever the chance arises to move to a desk I prefer over my current one, I take it, and, to make the swap appealing to whoever currently holds that desk, I generally throw in something small: covering a week of coffee runs, say, or taking an extra turn watering the office plants. Each of these individual decisions has felt obviously sound at the time it was made. Yet I've now gone all the way around the loop and find myself back at the very desk I started at in September - no better off in terms of desk quality, but out two weeks of coffee runs and a plant-watering shift. When I mentioned this to a friend outside the department, she said I was overanalyzing a trivial roommate-style squabble, but it strikes me as more interesting than that: it seems to show how someone could make an impeccably reasonable choice at every single step and still end up on a guaranteed losing streak overall, with nobody lying, hiding information, or acting in bad faith at any point.

I brought a version of this up in my seminar in passing, but we didn't have time to work through it properly, and none of the assigned readings so far have addressed a case quite like it. So I would genuinely like your take: is there something rationally defective about how I'm forming or acting on these desk preferences, or is a loop like this a perfectly legitimate feature of preferences that respond to context and comparison rather than sitting fixed in some master ranking? And, practically speaking, now that I've noticed the pattern, what follows for how I should behave at the next reshuffle? Must I simply refuse any future swap, even one that looks like a straightforward improvement in the moment, or is there a more sensible way to think about my predicament?
