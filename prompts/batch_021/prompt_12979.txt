So this came up after my philosophy of physics seminar last week -- two of my grad students got into it over coffee afterward and I honestly couldn't adjudicate on the spot. Let's just take Everettian/many-worlds QM as a given for the sake of argument here -- nobody in the dialogue should be relitigating collapse vs. no-collapse, that's not the interesting question. The actual dispute is about quantum suicide, and whether "quantum immortality" is a rationally defensible attitude once you've accepted branching: if you keep triggering a device whose outcome is coupled to a genuine quantum event with, say, even odds of killing you instantly, is it reasonable to eventually stop being scared, on the grounds that every future continuer of yours who's still around to ask "did that work?" will always answer yes? One student's line was that this is just a selection effect and tells you nothing about what you should've expected beforehand; the other basically bit the bullet and said that once branching is real, first-person anticipation isn't governed by Born-rule weights the way we normally assume, so theres a genuine sense in which you shouldn't be afraid.

Could you write me the dialogue that should've happened between them -- two committed Everettians standing right next to the machine, one of them (call her A) actually reaching for the trigger, totally calm about it, the other (call him B) trying to talk her out of it? I want real back-and-forth, not just the "someone always survives" one-liner lobbed at each other -- B should press A on why branch weight shouldn't do the same work ordinary objective chance does in decision theory even under many-worlds (why isn't a vanishingly-weighted surviving branch still something to dread, same as a low-probability survival would be in a single-world setup?), maybe raise the Sleeping-Beauty-style worry about self-locating belief, and A needs an actual answer rather than a restatement of the punchline. Bonus points if someone brings in a Parfit-ish fission framing -- if branching is basically fission, "what matters" in survival might not track which continuer gets to call itself you at all, which could cut against both the fear and the comfort. Keep it to maybe 800-1000 words, real friction the whole way through, and don't let either of them just cave at the end.
