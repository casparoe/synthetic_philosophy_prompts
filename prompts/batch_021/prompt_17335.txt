So I've been stuck for like two months on a big decision - I've got two job offers, one's a big pay bump in a city where I don't know a soul, the other is way less money but I get to stay near my family and keep doing the specific kind of work I actually love. Every time I try to make a pros and cons list it just falls apart, because the stuff on each side doesn't even feel like its measuring the same thing, like how do you weigh "more money and a fresh start" against "being around for my parents while they're still healthy," there's no shared unit for that kind of comparison. I went down a rabbit hole reading about this on some philosophy forums at like 1am and apparently theres an actual term for it, "incommensurable values" - basically meaning some goods just can't be put on the same scale as each other.

But here's where it's kind of bumming me out: if the two options are genuinely incommensurable, doesn't that mean there's just no fact of the matter about which one is actually better? And if there's no fact about which is better, isn't picking one basically just as arbitrary as flipping a coin, even though it obviously doesn't feel that way when you're the one living through it? That seems to clash with how everybody treats big decisions like this as if they're meaningful and worth losing sleep over, like why would anyone agonize for weeks over something if, structurally, the choice is no more rational than a coin toss? A friend of mine who took one philosophy class in college said something about "incomparable" being a different thing from "incommensurable," and also mentioned values being "on a par" instead of equal, but she couldn't really explain why that would change anything about the arbitrariness problem, so now I'm honestly more confused than before I asked her. Am I missing something basic about how this is supposed to work, or is choosing between incommensurable things really just dressed up randomness once you strip away the agonizing?
