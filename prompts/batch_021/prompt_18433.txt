I'm a retired physicist who's fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole in retirement, and something about Bayesian epistemology is bugging me. There's this thing called the "problem of old evidence" (I think it traces back to a guy named Glymour) which says that if you already know E for certain, so P(E) = 1, then Bayes theorem implies learning that some theory entails E can never actually raise your credence in it. But the textbook example everyone loves is Mercury's perihelion precession being one of the great confirmations of general relativity - and that precession was already known and measured decades before Einstein ever wrote GR down. So which is it: were physicsts just wrong all along to treat that old data as strong evidence for GR, or is the whole Bayesian account of confirmation simply broken? Feels like I have to bite one of those bullets and neither one sits right with me.
