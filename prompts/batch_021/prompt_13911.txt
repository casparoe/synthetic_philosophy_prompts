The question was: Explain what Kuhn means by "incommensurability" between successive paradigms, using the shift from phlogiston theory to Lavoisier's oxygen chemistry as your example. Does incommensurability imply that a scientist's move from one paradigm to another is not something evidence or reasons can settle?

Here's what Priya wrote:

Kuhn thinks mature sciences work through periods of "normal science": stretches where the community shares a paradigm -- a shared exemplary problem-solution plus the theory, instruments, and assumptions that go with it -- and mostly just fills in details, treating anomalies as puzzles rather than as evidence against the paradigm. When anomalies keep piling up and resist being solved this way, the field slides into crisis, and eventually a revolution swaps the old paradigm for a new, incompatible one.

Kuhn's favourite example is the switch from phlogiston chemistry to Lavoisier's oxygen theory. On the phlogiston view, flammable bodies contain a substance, phlogiston, that is released on burning, and ordinary air only supports combustion because it can soak up the phlogiston being released. Priestley, still a committed phlogistonist, isolated a new gas by heating mercury calx and found a candle burned unusually brightly in it; he called it "dephlogisticated air" because it seemed to have so little phlogiston already in it that it could absorb a lot more. Lavoisier ran comparable experiments but concluded that combustion is really combination with a distinct element, which he named oxygen, and used this to scrap phlogiston altogether. Since the two chemists were really just using two different names for one and the same gas, this is a good illustration of what Kuhn means by incommensurability: the paradigms share no common vocabulary, so nothing forces a scientist to move from one to the other on logical or evidential grounds. That's why Kuhn compares crossing over to a Gestalt switch or a conversion experience rather than a step in an argument: in the end, on his view, paradigm change just isn't something evidence can settle.
