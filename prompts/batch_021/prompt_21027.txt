So I was out with a friend last night — he's doing a postdoc in formal epistemology — and we ended up spending like two hours arguing about van Fraassen's Reflection Principle, of all things. The rough statement (he had to remind me, i only vaguely remembered it from undergrad) is that your current credence in some proposition, conditional on the supposition that your credence in it will be some specific value x at a later time, should just equal x — basically you're rationally required to defer to your own future opinion. He was defending this hard because van Fraassen apparently proved it from a Dutch book argument: if you don't conform to Reflection, a bookie can line up a sequence of bets now and later that guarantees you a loss no matter what actually happens, which sounded to me like a genuine knock-down argument that it's a real requirement of rationality and not just a nice heuristic.

I pushed back with the obvious case though: what if I know for a fact that tomorrow I'm getting hypnotized, or joining a cult, or just getting completely blackout drunk, so that my future credence in something is going to shift for reasons that have nothing to do with getting good evidence? Reflection seems to say I should already start shifting my current opinion toward whatever I predict I'll believe after the hypnosis/cult/tequila, which feels backwards — shouldn't I trust current, sober, non-brainwashed me *more* in that situation, not less? He said the Dutch book argument doesnt care at all about *why* your credence will change, only that you can predict what it'll be, and that this is the genuinely unsettling part of the result rather than a bug in the proof — though he did mention van Fraassen wrote a whole later paper wrestling with basically this exact problem, something with Ulysses and the Sirens in the title, but neither of us could remember what the actual proposed fix was, or whether it even works.

Can you help settle this? First I want the gut-level version of why the Dutch book argument for Reflection actually works — like, why does a mismatch between your current credence and your predicted future credence necessarily hand a bookie guaranteed profit, in a way that's importantly different from ordinary synchronic incoherence (like just having probabilities that don't add to 1)? And then I want your take on the real issue we couldn't resolve: is there a principled way to restrict Reflection so it doesn't force you to defer to a future self you already have specific reason to distrust, without that restriction basically collapsing into "defer to your future credence only when your future credence is trustworthy" — which seems to gut the principle of any independent force? Or is this basically a fatal problem for treating Reflection as a genuine rational requirement rather than just a fact about what happens when belief updating is done honestly?
