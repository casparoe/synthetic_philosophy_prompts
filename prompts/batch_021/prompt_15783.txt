Reviewer 2 absolutely tore into us, said we're basically redoing the whole ENCODE "80% of the genome is functional" fiasco from 2012, and that unless we can show these regions are under purifying selection (i.e. conserved across species, actually mattering for fitness — they even threw in the classic "80% vs less than 10% conserved" stat), we have no business calling anything "functional," we're just describing correlations and calling them biology.

I'm not a philosopher though and honestly it's making my head spin a little, so I'd really appreciate it if you could properly compare the two views for me — not the one-paragraph-each textbook version, but really dig into where they actually pull apart for a case like mine: an element that does something biochemically detectable and even seems to causally affect a phenotype when perturbed, where we simply don't know (yet, or maybe ever) whether that effect was "selected for." Is my reviewer's insistence on evidence of selection actually the philosophically correct standard for "function" talk, or is that itself a substantive and contestable choice between two views that are each internally coherent?
