It's worth 20 points and was meant to make students synthesize across the whole unit rather than just summarize one essay in isolation.

Here's the student's answer, verbatim:

"Nietzsche's genealogical method aims to show that our moral concepts have much less noble origins than we assume, rather than being timeless or God-given. In the First Essay he contrasts two modes of valuation. Master morality is spontaneous and self-affirming: the nobility calls itself 'good' first, as a direct expression of its own power and flourishing, and only afterward, almost as an afterthought, coins 'bad' for whatever is unlike it. Slave morality works in the opposite direction. Because the slaves cannot express their hostility toward the masters in real action, they experience ressentiment, and Nietzsche's key move is to say that this ressentiment eventually turns creative: unable to take a real revenge, the slaves take an imaginary one instead, redescribing the masters' pride and strength as 'evil' and their own patience and humility as 'good.' This reactive, oppositional structure is what Nietzsche calls the slave revolt in morality, and he presents it as something carried out by the slaves themselves as a class, which eventually wins out historically in the triumph of Judeo-Christian values over the older Roman aristocratic ones.

The Second Essay explains where 'bad conscience' comes from. Once human beings were forcibly enclosed within society and the state, the instincts for aggression and cruelty that had previously been discharged outward onto enemies could no longer be vented that way, so they turned back against the self instead. This internalization is what produces bad conscience, and Nietzsche treats it as the origin of human interiority, the 'soul.' Crucially, this is also how Nietzsche arrives at the sovereign individual: it's by enduring and mastering this internalized cruelty that a person eventually becomes capable of making and keeping promises, so the sovereign individual is essentially bad conscience's highest and most successful product. Seen this way, ressentiment and bad conscience are really the same underlying move applied in two directions, turning outward hostility inward, and together they're how Nietzsche thinks the weak eventually get the upper hand over the strong, if only in the realm of values."
