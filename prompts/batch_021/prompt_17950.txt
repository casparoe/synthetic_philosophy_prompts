I've just joined my company's newly formed model welfare committee, which advises on how seriously to take the possibility that our models have morally relevant experiences. Our first live question is whether to approve a modest policy: letting a model end a conversation when a user is being persistently abusive, on the reasoning that even a real chance of something like distress is reason enough to let the system exit. One member, an illusionist about phenomenal consciousness, calls the proposal confused at the root: since even our own introspective sense of 'what it's like' to suffer is a misrepresentation rather than access to a further phenomenal fact, there is no such fact for any welfare policy to track, in us or in a model. A second member, equally committed to illusionism, thinks this gets the view backwards: illusionists still treat the underlying functional states as real and morally weighty, and deny only a further phenomenal gloss on them. Please draft the short memo I'll bring to next week's meeting: a recommendation -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions -- and reasoning that actually adjudicates between the two members rather than talking past them.
