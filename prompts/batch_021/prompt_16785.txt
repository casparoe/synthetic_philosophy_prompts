My high school's Great Books club spent the last few weeks on the Enlightenment, and after our session on Rousseau's Letter to d'Alembert on the Theatre — written against d'Alembert and Voltaire's push to open a public theater in Geneva — two members, Theo and Naomi, kept arguing about it in our group chat afterward. Neither would budge, and I don't know the material well enough to referee it myself.

(1) Theo said Rousseau is simply a hypocrite: he wrote and staged a hit opera, Le Devin du village, for the king himself, and later filled Julie with page after page of engineered sentiment, so his claim that theater corrupts by manufacturing counterfeit feeling can't be sincere.

(2) Naomi said that misses the actual target: Rousseau isn't condemning staged emotion as such, but one specific institution — a permanent public theater dropped into a small, still-egalitarian republic — because it would import an idle audience, a luxury economy of tickets and fashion, and a paid caste of professional pretenders, displacing the cercles (Geneva's citizens' clubs) that currently hold its public life together.

(3) Theo replied that Rousseau also makes a plainer argument that mentions none of that: watching staged suffering lets spectators discharge their sense of duty in a few tears at no real cost, so by Rousseau's own logic Julie should corrupt its readers exactly the same way.

(4) Naomi pointed to Rousseau's own extended reading of Molière's Misanthrope, where he argues the play trains its audience to laugh at Alceste's rigid sincerity and side with Philinte's flattery — a specific claim about what one play does to one audience, not a blanket rule against represented feeling — and said flattening it into that blanket rule is exactly what makes the hypocrisy charge look stronger than it is.

(5) Theo granted that, but pushed back that Rousseau still owes a principled reason why Geneva counts as innocent enough to be protected while Paris gets written off as too corrupt to bother saving, and that the only visible basis for that line is Rousseau's own prior loyalty to Geneva and distaste for Paris.

Could you go through moves (1) through (5) and adjudicate them one at a time?
