I spent almost forty years as a fisheries biologist, so my whole career was basically one long tragedy-of-the-commons problem. Now that I'm retired I've finally had time to read alot of philosophy, and last week I got to Kant's universalizability test and had a bit of an 'aha' moment: it's really just Hardin's tragedy of the commons dressed up in 18th century language. Both ask 'what happens if everybody does this,' and both say an act can be wrong even though its completely harmless when only one person does it. So doesn't that mean Kant's famous 'ignore the consequences' ethics is secretly just consequentialist reasoning about aggregate harm -- the same kind of thing any fisheries regulator does?
