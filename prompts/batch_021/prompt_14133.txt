The prompt was: "Using Frankfurt's Jones/Black case, reconstruct his argument against the Principle of Alternative Possibilities (PAP). Then explain the 'dilemma defense' raised against Frankfurt-style cases, and explain how 'blockage' cases such as Pereboom's Tax Evasion case are meant to meet it. Finally, does Frankfurt succeed in showing that moral responsibility never requires any alternative possibilities whatsoever, or only that it doesn't require the sort of alternative possibilities libertarians have in mind? Defend your answer in a paragraph."

The student's answer, in full:

"Frankfurt's Principle of Alternative Possibilities (PAP) states that a person is morally responsible for what she has done only if she could have done otherwise. Incompatibilists rely on PAP together with the observation that determinism rules out alternative possibilities to conclude that determinism rules out moral responsibility. Frankfurt tries to block this argument by showing that PAP itself is false.

In his example, Black wants Jones to perform a certain action. Black has a device that lets him monitor Jones and, if Jones is about to choose otherwise, force him to perform the action anyway; but if Jones goes ahead and chooses the action on his own, Black never intervenes. In the actual sequence of events, Jones decides on his own, for his own reasons, to do the action, so Black never has to step in. Frankfurt says Jones is morally responsible for what he did, because he did it entirely on his own initiative, and yet he could not have done otherwise, because if he had shown any sign of choosing differently, Black would have made him do it anyway. So we have a case of responsibility without alternative possibilities, and PAP is false.

Critics have replied that Frankfurt's case is caught in a dilemma. John Martin Fischer pointed out that in order for Black to know in advance whether Jones is going to choose the action on his own, there has to be some reliable prior sign of what Jones will do. But if the world is deterministic, then of course there can be such a sign, since Jones's whole future is fixed by his past — but then the case simply assumes determinism is compatible with responsibility, which is exactly what is in question against a libertarian opponent. On the other hand, if the world is indeterministic, then no prior sign can be fully reliable, since Jones's choice is not fixed by anything prior to it; so there is always some chance that Jones goes the other way without Black's device picking it up in time, which means Jones does, after all, have a genuine alternative possibility. Either way, the case does not do the work Frankfurt needs it to do.

Blockage cases such as Pereboom's Tax Evasion case try to escape this dilemma by not relying on a predictive sign at all. Instead, a mechanism is set to trigger on a specific neural event that occurs partway through Jones's deliberation, before his choice is settled one way or the other, and if that event occurs the mechanism forces the outcome Black wants. Because the trigger does not have to predict anything, it works whether or not determinism is true, and it removes the alternative possibility even in an indeterministic setting, since Jones's deliberative process is preempted before it can issue in a different choice.

Overall, I think Frankfurt succeeds in showing that free will is compatible with determinism: since Jones is responsible even though he could not have done otherwise, an agent can be free and responsible even in a deterministic universe. The blockage cases show that this holds up even once we take indeterminism seriously, so PAP is simply false, not just false for some restricted notion of alternative possibilities."
