ok this is something that's been bugging me for months now. i keep running into the word "supererogation" in stuff about ethics - acts that go above and beyond duty, praiseworthy but not actually required (donating a kidney to a total stranger, jumping on a grenade for people you dont even know, that kind of thing). the more i sit with it though, the more i think this idea just doesn't actually fit inside utilitarianism. if the "right" act is just defined as whatever act produces the most good, then that above-and-beyond act IS the right act in that situation, which makes it required, not optional. so wheres the room left over for "better than whats required, but still not required"? ive seen people try to patch this with "satisficing" utilitarianism, where you only have to clear some good-enough bar instead of maxing out the good, but honestly that feels like its quietly giving up on being a maximizing theory just to dodge the problem, not actually solving it.

so does actual maximizing utilitarianism have real room for supererogation or not, and are the usual fixes (satisficing or whatever else is out there) any good? write me an essay that actually works through this properly, weighs the strongest arguments on both sides, and then commits to a real answer at the end - not a "well it depends, good points on both sides" cop out.
