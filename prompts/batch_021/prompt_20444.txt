So last week after our philosophy of physics reading group, a few of us ended up at the pub arguing about quantum suicide and quantum immortality – you know, the Tegmark thought experiment where you sit in front of a device wired to a quantum random trigger, and if many-worlds is right you should apparently expect to survive every single run of it, since theres no branch of the wavefunction in which you're around afterward to notice you didn't survive. One of my colleagues cracked a joke about it that got a chuckle but was really just a generic "ha, physicists are nuts" gag – it didn't engage at all with why the argument is supposed to be unsettling (or, depending who you ask, weirdly comforting).

I'd like something better, partly for my own amusement and partly because I want to open next week's seminar on personal identity with it. Could you write me a joke about quantum immortality/quantum suicide that would actually land with someone who acutally understands the argument – i.e. the punchline should turn on the real conceptual weirdness (the gap between the first-person "guarantee" of survival and the fact that from everyone else's point of view you're dead in almost every branch; or the funny business of using anthropic, self-locating reasoning to do that work in the first place; or, if you want extra credit, the further wrinkle David Lewis raises in a paper of his on this – that even the branches where you "survive" are mostly ones where you end up horribly maimed rather than unscathed, since there are just so many more ways to survive-but-badly than to survive-cleanly). What I don't want is a generic "quantum mechanics is spooky, lol" joke. One or two good ones is plenty, you don't need to give me ten.
