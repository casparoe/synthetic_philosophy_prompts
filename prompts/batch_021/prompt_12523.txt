Ok so with all the tariff/trade-deficit stuff back in the news I went down a rabbit hole rereading Hume's essays on money and trade ("Of the Balance of Trade," "Of Money"), and I'm tired of the shallow version of this debate -- "mercantilists thought hoarding gold = wealth, Hume refuted them with the specie-flow mechanism, free trade good, done." Write me a dialogue between Hume and a genuinely sharp mercantilist contemporary where the mercantilist presses on the actual weak spot in Hume's own theory: he grants theres an interval, before prices catch up, where an inflow of money stimulates domestic industry, so why not just keep engineering that inflow forever with tariffs instead of trusting his long-run "automatic" equilibrium? Have them also fight over what the Spain situation (all that New World silver, still comparatively poor) really proves, since I think both sides can spin it their way. I want genuine back-and-forth -- both characters equally sharp, actually responding to each other's last point -- not Hume just steamrolling a strawman.
