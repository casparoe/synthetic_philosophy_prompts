I've spent years reading rationalist blogs and forums, and a handful of us from an old reading group have turned that habit into a small podcast on philosophy of religion, mostly as an excuse to keep having the arguments we used to have in the comments. Our guest next month is a philosopher who has built her career defending a Schellenberg-style divine hiddenness argument: the claim that a perfectly loving God would ensure that no one capable of relationship with him ever ends up in nonresistant nonbelief; that such nonbelief plainly exists; and that, consequently, no perfectly loving God exists. She has a ready answer for most of the standard objections, and her favorite move under pressure is to insist that hiddenness is "a different animal from the problem of evil" and so is not hostage to the usual theodicist's business of weighing goods against evils.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions that push past having her simply restate the argument? I would like at least one question on why the argument needs the narrower notion of nonresistant nonbelief rather than the more intuitive reasonable nonbelief, and what that substitution actually does for her dialectically; one on the free-will or soul-making style reply on which epistemic distance from God is precisely what makes a freely chosen relationship possible, and why she thinks it misfires; one on the rival theistic strategy that treats hiddenness as morally and volitionally selective — God withholding himself from those not yet ready to respond rightly, rather than withholding evidence indiscriminately; one on how anyone, including the nonbeliever, could ever confirm that a given case of nonbelief is truly nonresistant rather than resistant at some level the person lacks conscious access to; and one on whether her later sympathy for a broader "ultimism" quietly narrows the argument's conclusion or genuinely preserves it. For each of these, please add a short follow-up question keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and close the list with a question asking what she would accept as actual evidence against the argument — something that would make her abandon it rather than patch it further.
