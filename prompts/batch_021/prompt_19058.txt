My aunt Ellen just retired after 38 years authenticating paintings for a big auction house, and I got voted into giving the toast at her retirement dinner. She's got no formal philosophy training but is weirdly obsessed with Nelson Goodman's autographic/allographic distinction -- her go-to example is that a forged Vermeer is a lesser, different artwork even if nobody alive can tell it from the real one, while a photocopy of a poem loses nothing at all, since paintings and poems are just different kinds of art -- and she'll definately notice if I get it wrong. Half the room is conservators and auction-house people who'll catch every reference, the other half is family who thinks she "checks if paintings are fake," full stop. She's also banned the word "aura" from the speech -- some old grudge with a Walter-Benjamin-quoting coworker. Write me a warm, funny five-minute toast that gets her actual position right, works for both halves of the room, never says "aura," and doesn't accidentally turn into a philosophy lecture.
