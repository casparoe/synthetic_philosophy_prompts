Hintikka's classical possible-worlds semantics models knowing that p as p being true at every world epistemically accessible to the agent. Because the underlying logic is a normal modal logic, closed under the K (distribution) axiom and the necessitation rule, any agent modeled this way turns out to be logically omniscient: she knows every valid formula and every logical consequence of anything she knows, no matter how remote or hard to derive — on this semantics, an idealized knower already "knows" whether Goldbach's conjecture is true. This is the problem of logical omniscience, widely regarded as the chief obstacle to treating possible-worlds epistemic logic as a serious model of real, resource-bounded reasoners.
