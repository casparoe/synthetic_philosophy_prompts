I've been working through some of the MIRI-era technical agenda on self-modifying agents for a reading group on AI alignment theory, and I keep running into the so-called "Löbian obstacle" to self-trust. The basic difficulty, as I understand it, is this: if a formal agent's proof system could derive the general reflection schema "if I can prove P, then P is true" for an arbitrary sentence P, then by Löb's theorem it could already derive P outright — an unacceptably strong closure property for any consistent system with even modest arithmetical strength. This is presented as a serious obstacle to designing agents that build trusted successors, or that reason about the reliability of their own future reasoning ("Vingean reflection"), since the natural design pattern of "defer to whatever my (successor) proof system proves, without re-deriving it from scratch" turns out to be formally unavailable in its naive form. The literature's proposed workarounds include things like parametric polymorphism (routing trust through a schema quantified over proof strength, in the spirit of Turing–Feferman reflection towers, rather than having one fixed system vouch for an identical copy of itself) and probabilistic alternatives to proof-based reflection, such as reflective oracles or logical induction, which try to recover reflective self-trust without relying on classical provability at all.

What I'd like is an argument for the position that the Löbian obstacle, real as it is within the idealized setting of first-order proof-based agents, is of comparatively minor importance for the AI systems we are actually likely to build, and that the amount of theoretical weight this corner of alignment theory has placed on it is somewhat misplaced. Please make the strongest version of this skeptical case you can, engaging with the actual logical mechanics of Löb's theorem and with at least one of the proposed workarounds above, rather than resting the argument on an informal dismissal of formal methods in general; I'm especially interested in how far a technically literate version of this skepticism can be pushed before it has to concede the substance of the obstacle back to those who take it seriously.
