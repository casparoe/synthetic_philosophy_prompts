So I had this totally pointless argument with my brother in law over the holidays about the moon landing (he's convinced its fake, no amount of evidence moves him) and afterward I went down a rabbit hole trying to figure out why some beliefs just don't budge no matter what you show someone. That's how I landed on "hinge epistemology" and Wittgenstein's On Certainty, which is apparently where the idea comes from. Part of it clicked right away, like this bit:

"the questions that we raise and our doubts depend on the fact that some propositions are exempt from doubt, are as it were like hinges on which those turn" (341), and a couple sections later, "If I want the door to turn, the hinges must stay put" (343).

Great, clean picture: some propositions are just fixed, beyond question, and everything else turns on them. But then I noticed that earlier in the book he's got this whole river metaphor going that seems to say something pretty different:

"It might be imagined that some propositions, of the form of empirical propositions, were hardened and functioned as channels for such empirical propositions as were not hardened but fluid; and that this relation altered with time, in that fluid propositions hardened, and hard ones became fluid" (96), and then, "The mythology may change back into a state of flux, the river-bed of thoughts may shift. But I distinguish between the movement of the waters on the river-bed and the shift of the bed itself; though there is not a sharp division of the one from the other" (97).

That sounds like nothing is a hinge in any permanent sense, its just whatever a given community happens to not be questioning at the moment, and even the "bed" itself can move over time. Which, if that's right, seems like it could describe my brother in law's whole worldview just as tidily as it describes mine.

So two things I actually want help with: is the fixed-hinge picture in 341-343 really consistent with the shifting river-bed picture in 96-97, or is that a genuine tension in Wittgenstein's view? And if hinges can drift the way 96-97 suggests, does On Certainty leave any principled basis for saying my hinges are epistemically better off than his, or does the river-bed stuff quietly concede that it's hinges all the way down with no ranking between people's frameworks? I've read a few online summaries of hinge epistemology and they all just breeze past this, so I'd rather get an actual close reading of how (or whether) these two bits of the text fit together.
