I co-produce a philosophy-of-mathematics podcast aimed mostly at graduate students, and next month's guest is a logician who defends intuitionism -- not on Brouwer's original grounds of mathematics as a mental construction unfolding in intuited time, but by way of Dummett's later meaning-theoretic route, on which a workable theory of meaning must ground truth in what can be recognized as a proof, so that classical bivalence for currently undecided statements can't simply be taken as the default. While preparing, I reread Dummett's essay making that case along with some of the secondary literature, and noticed that her standard move under pressure is to say the objection just reasserts a realist, verification-transcendent notion of truth rather than arguing for it -- effectively accusing the objector of begging the question right back. I don't want an interview that lets her either restate the elevator-pitch version of the view or lean on that parry to do all the work.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions, each keyed to a specific commitment of the position rather than inviting a summary of it, with a short follow-up anticipating the reply she's likely to give. I want at least one question on the intuitive cost of denying that a statement like Goldbach's conjecture is already determinately true or false, and on whether "weak counterexamples" genuinely dissolve that discomfort or just relabel it; one asking her to explain why the meaning-theoretic argument is supposed to license intuitionistic logic specifically, given that classical mathematicians seem to use and understand their own connectives perfectly fluently, rather than licensing some other non-classical revision entirely, or none at all; one on whether the Gödel-Gentzen double-negation embedding of classical arithmetic into Heyting arithmetic shows that she and her classical opponents actually agree on the mathematics and disagree only about a philosophical gloss layered on top; and one that forces her to take a side on splits internal to constructivism itself, such as whether she accepts Markov's Principle or Brouwer's fan theorem, and whether a meaning-theoretic argument is even fine-grained enough to decide such questions rather than merely motivating constructivism in some unspecified form. For at least one of these, make sure the "you're just begging the question against anti-realism" parry genuinely isn't available to her.
