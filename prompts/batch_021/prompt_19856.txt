Write a dialogue between a substantivalist about spacetime and a relationist, set against the backdrop of the hole argument rather than Newton's bucket. In response to the argument, have the substantivalist retreat from naive ("manifold") substantivalism to "sophisticated substantivalism": they accept that models related by the diffeomorphisms at issue in the hole argument represent one and the same physical possibility, and give up the claim that spacetime points have primitive identity (haecceities) independent of the metrical structure imposed on them, while still insisting that spacetime itself is a real, mind-independent entity. The relationist should press on whether this concession leaves substantivalism with any content that relationism doesn't already have, so that the two end up trying to work out whether their remaining disagreement is substantive or merely verbal. Each side should engage the other's specific moves in detail -- especially the substantivalist's attempt to articulate just what, if anything, still distinguishes their view from relationism once this concession is made -- rather than talking past one another, and neither side should be easy to refute.
