I'm a software engineer, and until recently I mainly knew John von Neumann for the computer architecture that bears his name, so I was surprised to stumble on his 1955 Fortune essay "Can We Survive Technology?" It reads less like computer science than like geopolitical forecasting. Two things stood out, if I'm remembering the piece correctly: he flagged the carbon dioxide released by industrial activity as something that could measurably warm the planet, and he argued that large-scale weather and climate control, once achievable, could be even more destabilizing than nuclear weapons — partly because an engineered drought or storm might be indistinguishable from a natural one, making the culprit almost impossible to identify or deter. More broadly, he was skeptical that any tidy, permanent fix to problems like these — world government, comprehensive treaties — was realistic, and expected that survival would depend on continual, ad hoc improvisation rather than one grand solution.

Given that specific outlook, what do you think von Neumann would have made of today's proposals for solar geoengineering, especially stratospheric aerosol injection, as a response to the warming he anticipated? Would his own reasoning about weather control as an unattributable, destabilizing weapon have pushed him to oppose unilateral deployment, or would his impatience with moralizing and his general technological pragmatism have led him to back it anyway, at least as a research program?
