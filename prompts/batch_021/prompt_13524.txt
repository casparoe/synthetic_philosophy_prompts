And here is the student's answer, reproduced in full:

> The Repugnant Conclusion is Parfit's result that, for any population of ten billion people all living extremely high-quality lives, there is some much larger population whose members all have lives barely worth living that would nevertheless be better, simply in virtue of its size, on a total utilitarian axiology that values a population at the sum of the welfare of its members. Parfit motivates the conclusion through the Mere Addition Paradox. Begin with population A, a comparatively small group enjoying very high welfare. Add to it a further group of people whose lives are well worth living but below A's level, without lowering the original group's welfare, to get A+; since no one has been harmed and some lives worth living have been added, A+ is not worse than A. Now compare A+ to a population B of the same size, with the same total welfare, but distributed perfectly equally; since equality is valuable when the sum is held fixed, B is better than A+, and hence, by transitivity, better than A. B is larger and more equal than A but has a lower average welfare, and repeating this same two-step process of addition and equalisation, starting each time from the previous outcome, eventually produces a vast population Z of lives barely worth living that is better than A — the Repugnant Conclusion.
>
> Critical-level utilitarianism responds by fixing a positive constant, the critical level, and then ranking populations by their average welfare once that constant has been subtracted from every life, rather than by the sum of raw welfare. Because a population of lives clustered just above zero has a net average that is low, or even negative, once the critical level is taken into account, mere addition of such lives can no longer drive up the ranking of a population indefinitely, and the pull toward Z that powers the Mere Addition Paradox is blocked. Since this move only reweights how lives count rather than abandoning aggregative reasoning altogether, critical-level utilitarianism secures this result without incurring any of the further costs that make average utilitarianism and strict person-affecting views so unattractive to most theorists in this debate.
