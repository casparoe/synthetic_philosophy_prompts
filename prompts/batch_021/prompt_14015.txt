So here's something that's been bugging me since a slightly humbling coffee-hour conversation with a colleague last week. I've defended a pretty standard compatibilist line on free will in print for something like fifteen years now, and I genuinely think the arguments for it are good, not just "well, it's my turf, I have to defend it." A very sharp first-year in my seminar recently sent me a draft paper laying out what she thinks is a decisive counterexample to my exact view. My gut reaction, before I'd even opened the PDF, was basically: I already know compatibilism is true, so whatever's in this paper has to be flawed somewhere -- some equivocation, a hidden question-begging premise, whatever -- I just have to go find it. Which is a little alarming once you notice it, because thats structurally the exact same move I'd roll my eyes at if some flat-earther said "I already know the earth is flat, so any photo of it from space has to be faked, I just need to figure out how they pulled it off."

That's the bit I can't shake: if I really do know my view is true, it seems to actually follow that I'm entitled to be confident, in advance, that any future evidence or argument against it must be misleading or mistaken in some way -- which apparently licenses me not to take the objection seriously on its own terms before I've even engaged with it. My partner, who isn't a philosopher, said I'm overthinking a simple case of "keep an open mind," but that feels like it dodges the actual puzzle rather than solving it -- open-mindedness is exactly what I thought I already had, argument by argument, for fifteen years, and yet here I am ready to prejudge this one sight unseen. Is there an actual mistake somewhere in that piece of reasoning, or is this just a real bind that comes attached to having justified, well-earned confidence in something? I'd love an actual diagnosis rather than a pep talk about staying humble.
