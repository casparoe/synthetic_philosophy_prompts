My studio art teacher is retiring after thirty-one years, and for as long as anyone can remember, she has had the same answer whenever a student asks, mid-critique, "but is it actually good?": "Go stand in front of it for twenty minutes and then tell me." She has never once simply told a student that a painting was good or bad, because she thinks a verdict you haven't earned through your own looking isn't really a verdict you have at all -- a position, I later found out, that philosophers actually have a name for: pessimism about aesthetic testimony. I've been asked to say a few words at her retirement dinner this Friday. Former students and fellow art teachers, who will instantly recognize what thirty-one years of that answer amounted to, will be sitting beside family, school board members, and administrators who just want a warm, simple story about a wonderful teacher with impeccable taste. Write the toast so it works for both halves of the room without ever actually asking anyone to just take her word, or mine, for what's good.
