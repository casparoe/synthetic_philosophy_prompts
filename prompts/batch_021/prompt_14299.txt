For my seminar on fairness in machine learning, I've been reading about the COMPAS controversy. ProPublica's 2016 investigation found that, among defendants who did not go on to reoffend, Black defendants were classified as high-risk by COMPAS at nearly twice the rate of white defendants, while Northpointe, the company behind the tool, maintained that COMPAS was equally well-calibrated across racial groups. My professor mentioned that Chouldechova, and separately Kleinberg, Mullainathan, and Raghavan, later proved that calibration and equalized error rates across groups cannot both hold once the base rate of reoffending differs between the groups, as it does here. Given that result, doesn't it follow that Northpointe's statisticians were simply right and that ProPublica's finding must reflect a methodological artifact rather than genuine evidence of bias? I'm struggling to see why this is still presented as an open, contested question in the fairness literature rather than something the mathematics has already resolved.
