So she bolts on a separate risk function that reweights the decumulative probabilities themselves, seperately from how good or bad the outcomes are, which is supposed to let her treat Allais-paradox-style choices as rationally permissible instead of just a mistake.

Here's what I keep getting stuck on: isn't this just adding a second free parameter to fit the exact same preference data that a sufficiently weird concave utility function could already fit inside plain old expected utility theory? Why couldn't a committed expected-utility diehard just say "fine, your utility function has this specific shape" and call it a day, instead of positing a whole extra risk-weighting mechanism on top of the outcome utilities? Could you compare risk-weighted expected utility to standard expected utility (with a concave utility function doing all the risk-aversion work) specifically on this point - is the risk function doing real, independent work, or is it basically redundant relabeling? And as a bonus, I'd be curious whether giving up the independence axiom the way REU does opens it up to money-pump-style dynamic consistency problems worse than what plain expected utility has to deal with. Bottom line, which of the two do you think is the more defensible theory of rational choice under risk - I want an actual verdict, not a "well, it depends."
