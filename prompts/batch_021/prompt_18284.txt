I spent thirty-five years as a radio astronomer before retiring, and in retirement I have been filling my evenings with philosophy, partly for its own sake and partly, I have come to realize, because it might help me understand something that has been troubling me closer to home. Over the past several years, an old friend from graduate school -- a physicist as capable as anyone I trained alongside -- has become convinced that the Apollo landings were staged, and that all the evidence against that view, from the photographs and the trajectories that observatories like the one I worked at tracked independently, to the retroreflectors still used today for lunar laser ranging, was itself fabricated by a conspiracy spanning several governments and two generations of scientists.

What unsettles me is not that he believes this but the shape of our arguments about it. He is not careless, and he is not uninformed: for every piece of evidence I raise, he has a specific, technically fluent reason to distrust the instrument, the institution, or the witness that produced it, and the reason is always of the same kind -- that particular source cannot be trusted either. I have come to realize that I cannot, even in principle, hand him a piece of evidence whose reliability is not itself exactly the sort of thing he is prepared to doubt in the same way. And yet he is not a skeptic about everything: he trusts his own eyes and memory, ordinary testimony from people he knows, most of the rest of physics and chemistry, even the computer he uses to build his case against me. The doubt is total only in this one province, and it seems to live entirely off a great deal of surrounding trust that he never thinks to question.

I would like your help thinking about the structure of this, rather than more evidence about the moon, of which I have no shortage. Is my friend simply reasoning badly, and if he is, can you say exactly where the error lies, given that he applies ordinary standards of evidence everywhere else? Or is a disagreement like this one pitched at a level that evidence and argument cannot actually reach? If so, I am no longer sure what grounds my own confidence that I am the one in the right, and I would rather have your honest assessment of which of these it is than a balanced tour of the possibilities.
