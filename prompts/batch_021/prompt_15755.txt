So in my decision theory seminar (it's technically cross-listed with the CS AI-safety course) we just spent a week on this thing called the "commitment races problem," and I've been trying to explain it to my roommate all week with mixed success lol. From what I got out of lecture: if your a sophisticated enough bargaining agent, theres an actual strategic payoff to locking yourself into an irreversible commitment as early as possible, before you've reasoned much about the situation or even worked out your own values, because once that commitment is common knowledge, any rational counterpart basically has to treat it as fixed and plan around it instead of around you. The catch is everyone bargaining has that same incentive, so you get a race to commit fast and hard, sometimes to pretty extreme threats, before anyone involved is actually in a good epistemic position to know what they should be locking in. Our professor's low-tech analogues were stuff like Dr. Strangelove's doomsday machine and "we don't negotiate with hostage-takers" policies, but said the real version of the worry is about future AI systems (or civilizations, whatever) bargaining with each other, where taking more time to think before you commit is itself a move your opponent can exploit.
