I've ended up on the five-person Compliance Committee for the condo association where I live -- not something I ever expected to do, but nobody else volunteered, so I said yes, figuring I could at least be fair about it. We have a case I want to get right before we bring a recommendation to the full board. Two years ago the association tightened its short-term-rental rule after a fairly contentious vote, and one of our owners, Marcus, has apparently been renting his unit out on and off ever since, in plain violation of it. Another owner, Renata, filed the complaint against him, with dated screenshots and everything, and on the facts there isn't much dispute. The complication is that Renata ran short-term rentals out of her own unit for almost two years before that vote, and by more than one account she was among the loudest voices at the meeting pushing for the ban while she was still doing it herself; she only stopped once the new rule passed. Marcus doesn't deny what he did. Instead, his response is that Renata has no business bringing this complaint at all, and that the committee should throw it out rather than rule on it. Our bylaws say the committee "may decline to act on a complaint brought by an owner who is, at the time of filing, in violation of these same restrictions," and Renata isn't currently in violation, so on the strict wording Marcus loses that argument. But two of my fellow committee members think the wording misses the point, and that letting her pursue this, given what she did and how she helped get the rule passed in the first place, would make the committee look ridiculous. A third has proposed a middle path: set Renata's complaint aside specifically, but have the committee open its own inquiry into Marcus's rentals using the same evidence, so the violation doesn't just disappear along with her standing to raise it. I keep turning this over myself, and I'm honestly not sure the bylaw is even asking the right question, since it only checks whether someone is breaking the rule right now, not whether they helped write the rule while breaking it, which feels like it should count for something.

Could you write the memo I'll circulate to the full board before we take this up? I need an actual recommendation on both questions -- whether Renata's complaint should proceed as filed, and what happens about Marcus's rentals either way -- with a realistic timeline for acting before our annual meeting in six weeks. I'd also like it to take the throw-it-out argument seriously on its own terms rather than just acknowledging it and moving past it, since that's a position two of my colleagues genuinely hold and they'll want to see it actually engaged with, not dismissed.
