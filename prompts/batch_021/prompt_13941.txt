As a reminder of the setup: a fair coin is tossed repeatedly until it first lands heads, on toss n, which happens with probability 1/2^n; you are then paid (-1)^(n+1) · 2^n / n. Weighting each payoff by its probability turns the n-th term into (-1)^(n+1)/n, so summing over all n in the natural temporal order gives the alternating harmonic series, 1 − 1/2 + 1/3 − 1/4 + ⋯, which converges to ln 2. But this series is only conditionally, not absolutely, convergent, so by the Riemann rearrangement theorem the very same countable collection of possible outcomes can be reordered to sum to any real number whatsoever, or to diverge to +infinity or -infinity. Unlike the classical St. Petersburg game, whose expected value is at least well-defined (if infinite), the Pasadena game's expected value seems not to be well-defined at all.
