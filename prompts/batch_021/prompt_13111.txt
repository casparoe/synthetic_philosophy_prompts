As a novelist long fascinated by George Eliot's gift for making moral seriousness feel like a substitute for faith, I keep returning to an odd biographical detail: the only book she published in her lifetime under her own name, Marian Evans, was not a novel but her 1854 translation of Feuerbach's *The Essence of Christianity*. I'd like you to compare Feuerbach's central claim -- that the attributes of God are really humanity's own attributes, alienated and misread as external and supernatural -- with the secular sense of the sacred that Eliot goes on to dramatize in her fiction: the reverence for "unhistoric acts" and hidden lives that closes *Middlemarch*, or the community-based redemption that replaces Silas Marner's lost chapel faith. Does her fiction simply supply the imaginative flesh for Feuerbach's argument, or does it quietly reintroduce something -- an order of value, a species of grace -- that his own logic of projection would have to unmask as illusion? Please argue for one answer rather than simply cataloguing points of agreement and difference.
