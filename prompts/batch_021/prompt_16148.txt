Over the past year I went through a rough patch — a long relationship ending around the same time a job I badly wanted fell through — and I coped, in part, by falling down a rabbit hole of physics podcasts and popular books on the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics, including Sean Carroll's *Something Deeply Hidden*. There was something genuinely consoling about the thought that whenever the universal wavefunction branches, a version of me just as real as I am is living out the timeline where the relationship survived or the job came through. When I mentioned this to a friend finishing an MA in philosophy, she told me I had essentially rediscovered David Lewis's modal realism on my own, and that professional metaphysicians had already established, quite independently of physics, that every possible way things could have gone is just as concrete and real as the way things actually turned out. That felt like a remarkable convergence, as though two entirely separate fields had arrived at the same picture of reality from opposite directions.

Trying to read parts of Lewis's *On the Plurality of Worlds* for myself has left me less sure of this, though, since he seems preoccupied with worlds that share nothing at all with ours — different laws of nature, histories with no common origin — rather than branches splitting off from a single quantum state at some definite moment. So I would genuinely like to know: is Everettian many-worlds essentially the physics version of Lewisian modal realism, such that the two views support or vindicate one another, or have I been sliding between two unrelated senses of "many worlds"? And separately, if Lewis's other worlds really are just as real as this one, does that give me any legitimate basis for feeling consoled about how things went here, or am I helping myself to a comfort that his own view doesn't actually license?
