ok so I've been down a rabbit hole rereading von Neumann's 1955 Fortune essay "Can We Survive Technology?" (someone linked it in a comment thread about AI pause proposals) and its bugging me. his core move is basically: technology keeps shrinking the effective size of the globe and raising the ratio of destructive power to effort required, so some catastrophic misuse becomes close to inevitable given enough time, and there's no way to un-invent the relevant knowledge -- iirc he even says something like "for progress there is no cure." but his conclusion isn't "therefore ban/pause the dangerous tech." he's pretty explicit that prohibition, treaties, or anything like a permanent fix won't work, and argues instead we're stuck with patience, tolerance, and a long series of ad hoc "opportunistic" decisions -- muddling through crisis by crisis forever instead of solving the underlying problem once.

what I can't figure out is what this actually implies for the current AI pause/slowdown debate in rationalist-adjacent circles. is von Neumann's argument a real objection to pause-style proposals, since on his view even a successful pause just delays an eventually-inevitable outcome? or is "buy time so we can muddle through better" exactly the kind of opportunistic move his own framework would recommend? can you spell out the most important implications of his actual argument (not just "smart guy worried about tech") for whether someone who buys his overall picture should support or oppose AI pause efforts today?
