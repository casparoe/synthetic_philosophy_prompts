I am drafting a novel in which two minor characters—an aging Kierkegaard scholar and a philosopher formed by the later Wittgenstein—share a train compartment and fall into an argument about the nature of religious conviction. I want their exchange to be philosophically exact rather than a caricature of "faith versus reason." Compare Kierkegaard's account of faith in Fear and Trembling and Concluding Unscientific Postscript with Wittgenstein's remarks on religious belief in his 1938 Lectures on Religious Belief. Both refuse to treat religious conviction as a hypothesis to be weighed against evidence, yet they arrive at that refusal from quite different directions. Where precisely would these two men's pictures of faith align, where would they simply talk past one another, and is there a point at which one position quietly collapses into, or is exposed by, the other?
