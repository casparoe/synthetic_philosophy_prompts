I just turned in the final manuscript for my first book (nothing fancy, a pop-science type thing) after almost three years, and now I'm stuck in this weird proofreading purgatory where my editor wants one more full read-through before it goes to the printer. Here's the thing thats been messing with my head: I've checked every single one of the ~600 footnoted factual claims in the book individually, and I genuinely believe each one is true. But I also believe, just as strongly, that with that many claims there's almost certainly at least one small error hiding in there somewhere that I haven't caught. My friend who did a philosophy PhD heard me describe this feeling and just said "oh, that's the preface paradox," like it was a known thing with a name (apparently some guy called Makinson wrote it up back in the 60s). She then dragged me down a rabbit hole with her and it turns out there's a whole cluster of these puzzles, including an older, more famous one called the lottery paradox, that's basically got the same shape underneath once you squint at it - you're perfectly justified believing of any one ticket that it won't win, and yet just as sure that some ticket will.

I've now read like three encyclopedia-style explanations of both and they all do the same thing: state the puzzle cleanly, then walk through a menu of "possible responses" (reject conjunction closure, go subjectivist about belief, bite the bullet, etc), and honestly it's stopped scratching the itch. What I actually want at this point is fiction - a short story that makes a reader feel that specific vertigo from the inside rather than have it explained to them: the sensation of being fully rational and fully confident in each individual piece of a large set of beliefs, while being equally rational and confident that the set as a whole contains a mistake, with no honest way to point at which piece is the guilty one without that piece stopping being the kind of thing you were confident about. It doesn't have to involve a book or an author at all - you could set it in a courtroom, on a factory quality-control line, at a card table, wherever feels right - I just don't want something that reads like a philosophy lecture with a plot stapled onto it. Give it an actual character and some real texture, not just a walking thought experiment.
