I've been slowly working through Spinoza's Ethics with a little reading group (we are currently bogged down in Part 4, send help), and I keep getting stuck on something that feels weirdly relevant to AI stuff. Spinoza's conatus doctrine -- something like "each thing, as far as it can by its own power, strives to persevere in its being" -- basically sounds like he stumbled onto instrumental convergance three and a half centuries before Omohundro's "basic AI drives" paper or Bostrom's version in Superintelligence. Every single mode of substance, whatever it's "about," ends up doing the self-preservation/self-empowerment thing as a matter of pure metaphysical necessity, goal content totally aside.

Except then Part 4 also has him arguing that reason and the good aren't independent at all. The more adequate ideas a thing has, the more its "striving" just converges on its actual advantage, and virtue basically just is acting from reason -- there's no scenario in his system where a fully rational mind ends up pursuing something arbitrary or bad, because for him rationality and the good are metaphysically welded together (this is the same guy who thinks a sufficiently clear-eyed intellect arrives at something like the intellectual love of God as its highest good). That looks like close to the exact opposite of the orthogonality thesis, which says you can bolt more or less any goal onto any level of intelligence with no correlation at all.

So here's what I can't resolve: if you sat Spinoza down in a modern AI safety seminar and walked him through the orthogonality thesis paired with instrumental convergence, what would he actually say? Would he think the two theses are in tension given his own necessitarianism (i.e. that "intelligence" decoupled from the good isn't even coherent on his picture, so Bostrom's whole setup is asking an ill-formed question), or is there a way of reading conatus and his theory of adequate ideas thats fully compatible with orthogonality once you translate it into modern terms? I've gone back and forth on this for a week now and would love help actually working through how his metaphysics of reason would cash out against that specific pairing of theses, rather than just a "well he's a rationalist so he'd probably say X" hand-wave.
