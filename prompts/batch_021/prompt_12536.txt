I have been reading through Gottlob Frege's writings on my own, particularly his campaign against psychologism in logic and his claim, in "The Thought," that thoughts and logical laws belong to an objective "third realm" entirely independent of what any individual or community happens to believe. I later learned that in 1924, during the last year of his life, Frege kept a private diary, not published until decades after his death, expressing strongly nationalist, antisemitic, and anti-parliamentary views, including the wish that Jews be denied full political rights and that Germany be ruled by a single strong leader rather than through party democracy. Most discussions of this I have come across treat it as a purely biographical embarrassment, on the grounds that the soundness of a logical argument cannot depend on the character of the person who advances it. I would like you to resist that quick separation and argue for the less comfortable position: that Frege's insistence on a single, mind-independent order of truth that no individual or group may dissent from in logic, and his insistence on a single, hierarchical order that no individual or group may dissent from in politics, are two expressions of one and the same anti-pluralist temperament, so that the diary does bear on how we should understand his anti-psychologism, even if it leaves the validity of his actual arguments untouched. Please engage seriously with the strongest objection to this: that his foundational logical work was essentially complete decades before 1924, and that plenty of other committed logical objectivists, before and since, have held unremarkable or even left-wing politics.
