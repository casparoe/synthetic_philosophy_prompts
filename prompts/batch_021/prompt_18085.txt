Me and my housemate got into it last night over Kripke's book on Wittgenstein and rule-following - the plus/quus thing - and it went on way longer than either of us planned (we were only trying to unwind after work lol). She's convinced that Kripke's "skeptical solution," the community-agreement move, actually rescues meaning from the paradox: sure, no fact about my past behaviour pins down whether I mean plus or quus by "+", but what makes it true that I'm following the rule is just that my answers keep matching what everyone else in my linguistic community would say, and if I ever diverged they'd correct me. I kept trying to say that this doesn't actually get you out of the hole, it just digs the hole one level down, but I couldn't get the argument fully straight after a few beers and she wasn't budging either.

Heres the thing thats been bugging me since: doesn't the exact same sceptical argument apply to the community that Kripke applies to me? The community's whole collective history of using "+" is also just some large finite set of past responses, so by his own logic there should be a bent quus-like function fitting all of their past answers too, and nothing in their actual behaviour (as opposed to some further interpretation of it) picks out one function as the one they've "agreed" on. If "agreement" is just as indeterminate as individual meaning was supposed to be, then saying my usage is correct because it agrees with the community isn't grounding anything - it's assuming the very sort of determinate meaning-fact the paradox was meant to rule out in the first place. There's also that bit where Kripke says a guy totally alone on a desert island forever can't really be said to follow a rule right or wrong, which always struck me as basically him admitting the solution needs an actual community standing behind you, not just some fact about you.

So: can you build the strongest version of the case that Kripke's communitarian skeptical solution fails on its own terms - that it doesn't dissolve the rule-following paradox so much as relocate the exact same indeterminacy from the individual up to the community, meaning it isn't really an answer to the sceptic at all? I'd like it to actually engage with why Kripke thought the community case is different (the stuff about "checking" and correction over time, not just matching answers) rather than just asserting the parallel and leaving it there, since that's the move my housemate kept retreating to whenever I pushed.
