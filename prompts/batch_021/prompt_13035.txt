I cover science and ideas for a living, which apparently means I'm contractually obligated to overthink every family argument until everybody's sick of me. Latest one: my brother had our grandad's old gold pocket watch sitting in a drawer for decades, case all dinged up and the movement long dead, so last month he had a jeweler melt the case down and recast it into a locket for his daughter's graduation. Our mom is genuinely upset about it, she says he basically destroyed a family heirloom and its gone for good. He thinks thats ridiculous, since (his words) "the gold didn't go anywhere," pretty much every ounce of it is sitting right there in the locket, just reshaped, minus a tiny pinch of dust the jeweler admitted got lost somewhere in the process. I put it to our family group chat hoping for backup and got about six different opinions, which did not help.

Here's what's actually been bugging me though: mom and my brother aren't disagreeing about any fact, they both know exactly where every speck of that gold ended up, they just can't agree on whether that means the watch survived or not. So who's actually right, if either of them is? And if it turns out there's genuinely no settled answer even once all the physical facts are in, I'd love to understand why that's even possible, because I keep running into some version of this exact disagreement in other contexts (restored instruments, rebuilt houses, you name it) and I've never had a satisfying account of what's really going on underneath it.
