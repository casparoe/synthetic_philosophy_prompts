Weird rabbit hole from work today - I was looking into Ramsey numbers for a graph-coloring problem and remembered that Frank Ramsey guy is also kind of one of the fathers of Bayesian decision theory (the expected-utility, subjective-probability stuff that underlies a ton of RL). Then I found out he also wrote this short, pretty obscure philosophy piece near the end of his very short life (he died at 26) called something like "Epilogue," where his whole argument for life still mattering despite the universe being huge is basically: stars cant think or love, so their size doesn't matter, meaning is human-scale not cosmic-scale.

Given that hes basically the guy who gave us the math for "weigh outcomes by probability times how much you value them," which is pretty much exactly the reasoning longtermist/EA folks use to justify caring a huge amount about astronomically large numbers of future people (or future digital minds etc) spread over millions of years - what do you think Ramsey would've actually made of longtermism? Does the "meaning is human-scale, stars are like threepenny bits" side of him make him an instinctive critic of that whole project, or would the decision-theory side of him actually have been sympathetic to it? Curious for your real best-guess reasoning here, not just a definition of what longtermism is.
