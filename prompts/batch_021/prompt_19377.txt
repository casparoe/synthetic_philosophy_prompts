I'm a PhD student in history and philosophy of science. During our reading group on the Vienna Circle, two cohort-mates fell into a disagreement about Carnap that none of us could settle.

Lina argued that Carnap's Principle of Tolerance -- the view that adopting a logical framework is a pragmatic choice relative to a purpose, so that "in logic there are no morals" -- sits badly with his and Neurath's enthusiasm for centralized economic planning and the Unity of Science program's drive toward one physicalist vocabulary for all the sciences. (Neurath, after all, had briefly headed an office planning the socialization of Bavaria's economy in 1919.) A genuine pluralist about frameworks, she said, ought to be suspicious of any project that imposes a single "rationalized" scheme on social and economic life, rather than championing it.

Owen replied that this conflates two different questions: tolerance is about which apparatus to adopt for a given cognitive purpose, while planning is about how to coordinate action once your ends are already fixed. Treating Euclidean geometry as merely one option among others doesn't commit you to opposing city grids built on it.

Lina pushed back: the whole point of Neurath's drive toward a single unified scientific vocabulary was to hand planners a common language purged of ideological noise, precisely so that coordinated planning could happen. So "tolerance" and "unity" spring from the same project, which suggests the tolerance was never neutral to begin with -- just instrumental to a further, substantive social end.

Owen answered that this proves too much: toleration is almost always instrumentally justified by some further aim (religious toleration by appeal to civil peace, for instance) without thereby becoming fake toleration. Carnap's tolerance was always explicitly relative to a purpose, so wanting one vocabulary for the purpose of planning is an application of the principle, not a violation of it.

Could you go through this exchange move by move, saying who has the better of it at each step, and flag anything both of them seem to be missing about how Carnap's technical philosophy of language actually relates to his and Neurath's politics?
