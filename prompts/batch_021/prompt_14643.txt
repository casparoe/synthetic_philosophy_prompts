A couple of friends of mine got into it after we watched some pop-sci video on quantum entanglement together, and they never really resolved it. (1) Dana said the loophole-free Bell tests prove nature is nonlocal, full stop -- measuring one particle really does instantly affect its far-away entangled partner, thats just settled now. (2) Marco said that's an overreach: the theorem and the experiments only rule out local hidden-variable theories, and you can keep locality if you drop realism instead -- on a Copenhagen-ish view theres no definite hidden state sitting there to be nonlocally disturbed, so nothing local actually gets violated. (3) Dana shot back that measuring one side still instantly fixes the joint probabilities on the other side no matter what you say about hidden states -- isn't that just spookiness relocated, not removed? (4) Marco said that's exactly what the no-signalling theorem rules out: you can't use the correlation to send an actual message, so relativity's safe, and calling the correlation itself "nonlocal causation" just begs the question. (5) Dana said no-signalling only bars sending messages, not real physical influence, and brought up Bohmian mechanics as a counterexample: it's explicitly nonlocal and yet demonstrably obeys no-signalling, so "can't signal" clearly doesn't mean "nothing nonlocal is going on." (6) Marco's comeback was that Bohm is just one interpretation among several (many-worlds, superdeterminism, QBism) that reproduce the same statistics with no real nonlocal influence, so pointing to it doesn't prove nonlocality is forced on everyone -- all the theorem actually forces is a choice between giving up locality, realism, single outcomes, or free choice of settings, and which horn you grab is an interpretive call, not a discovery.

Can you go through this move by move and say who's got the better of it at each step, not just who wins overall? Also flag anything both of them seem to be quietly eliding about what Bell's theorem actually licenses you to conclude.
