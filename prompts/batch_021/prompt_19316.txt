Ok so this has been rattling around in my head for a couple weeks. A few of us from my local ACX-adjacent meetup have been working through the Anthropic interpretability stuff -- toy models of superposition, the dictionary-learning/SAE papers, the whole Golden Gate Claude demo where they cranked up one feature and the model wouldn't shut up about the bridge. And we keep landing on the same disagreement: when Chris Olah's team says they "found a feature for the Golden Gate Bridge," is that a discovery about something that was already determinately there in the network, just waiting to be located -- the same kind of fact as "this neuron in my visual cortex fires on faces" -- or is it more like us projecting a category onto a big pile of floating point numbers, because humans are pattern-matching machines who can't help but see faces in clouds?

It struck me that this is basically the exact fight Wittgenstein spent the second half of his career having, just about human minds instead of transformers -- the private language argument, the rule-following paradox, "meaning is use," his whole campaign against the idea that an inner state gets to count as "about" something just by sitting there, independant of any wider practice of checking, correcting, and agreeing. And it's not like he's some rando with zero connection to computing either -- he and Turing were literally in the same room, arguing with each other, at Wittgenstein's 1939 lectures on the foundations of mathematics at Cambridge. So he was right there for the birth of the theory of computation, just a few decades too early for anyone to hand him a residual stream.

So, genuine question: if you resurrected Wittgenstein and sat him down with a mechanistic interpretability paper, do you think he'd side with the realists here -- yes, the network determinately represents the bridge, that's a fact about its internals we're discovering -- or would he think the whole "realism about features" framing is confused in more or less the way he thought talk of private inner representations in people was confused? And if it's the latter, what would he say the interpretability researchers are actually doing when they "find" a feature, if it isn't literally uncovering a representation that was sitting there all along?
