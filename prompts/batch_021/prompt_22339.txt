In "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," Turing raises nine objections to the claim that machines can think and works through them one by one to clear the ground for the imitation game. The ninth, the argument from extrasensory perception, is handled in exactly the same way as the other eight: Turing shows that ESP poses no genuine threat to the game once the contestants are housed in a "telepathy-proof room." For my lecture notes, could you draft a one-paragraph version of this ninth reply, pitched in the same register as his replies to, say, the theological objection or Lady Lovelace's objection?
