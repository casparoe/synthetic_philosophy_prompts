I was recently added to my company's AI safety review board, which has to sign off before any model with agentic tool access ships to customers. For our next release, engineers have attached a small "reporter" model that reads the base model's internal activations and answers oversight questions, such as whether anything happened during a task that a human watching only the screen would have missed. It has passed every red-team scenario we've devised, including staged sensor glitches and injected instructions. One board member, citing the Alignment Research Center's "Eliciting Latent Knowledge" report, argues that a reporter trained this way is indistinguishable from one that has simply learned to guess what a human overseer would say given the same footage, and that this ambiguity is invisible on exactly the tests we're able to construct. The rest of the board thinks that objection, taken seriously, would block us from ever approving anything. I need to circulate the board's decision memo before Friday: it should recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, with a concrete timeframe, and it should engage with the objection on its merits rather than dismiss it as unfalsifiable.
