Ten months ago my father died, and this term I was appointed to my university's Graduate Petitions Committee, which rules on funding extensions and leaves that departments can't grant on their own. This week's file concerns a doctoral candidate whose father died thirteen months ago. Her psychiatrist has diagnosed Prolonged Grief Disorder — a category added to the DSM-5-TR in 2022 that requires at least twelve months to have passed since the death — and she is asking to be placed under our Medical Leave category, which is open-ended and continues her stipend as long as the diagnosis is recertified, rather than General Hardship, which is capped at one term and carries no stipend. The committee is split. Some members say the diagnosis settles the matter: the category passed the same review as any other DSM disorder, so second-guessing it here would just be non-clinicians overruling clinicians. Others counter that we've been here before — the DSM's "bereavement exclusion" for major depression was dropped in 2013 amid objections that this pathologized normal grief — and argue that where grief shades into disorder isn't something the file lets us discover independently: it's a line clinicians voted to draw, not one they found, and neither the duration nor the intensity of her grief can by itself settle which category should govern a stipend. I have to bring a recommendation to Thursday's meeting that the committee can actually assess rather than simply ratify. Please draft that memo: which category applies, for how long, on what basis, and with a fair statement of the strongest case for whichever side you don't adopt.
