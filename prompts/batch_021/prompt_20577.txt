My great-aunt Rosa died last week at eighty-nine, after a forty-year career as a set theorist. I'm a PhD student in logic myself, which is apparently why I got handed her eulogy: Rosa was a completely sincere believer in Gödel's own private argument for an afterlife -- not the ontological proof people trot out when they want to sound clever, but the weirder stuff he actually wrote to his mother and told Hao Wang, that a world built this rationally wouldn't bother growing a mind over a whole lifetime just to snuff it out for good. She'd bring this up completely straight-faced at Thanksgiving, unprompted. She left written instructions saying the eulogy has to use the real argument and can't soften into "she's in a better place" or "watching over us," which she considered exactly the kind of sloppy thinking the argument is supposed to rule out. Problem is, three or four of her old department colleagues are coming and will clock it immediately if I flatten the argument or get it wrong, but the other forty people are cousins who've never heard the word 'monad' and just want to feel okay by the time its over. Can you write the eulogy -- something that actually does real work with Gödel's argument for the people who'll catch it, while still landing as comfort for everyone else?
