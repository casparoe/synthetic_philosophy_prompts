I'm the only novelist in the family, which is apparently why I've been asked to write my father-in-law's eulogy. He converted to Catholicism at fifty-odd purely on Pascal's wager logic, and he never pretended otherwise -- dinner guests got the full expected-value pitch, then he'd admit that since you cant just decide to believe, he'd gone and done the other half of Pascal's argument too: mass every week, holy water, acting the part until it stuck, and he loved that Pascal's own word for what this does to you is "abêtira" -- it stupefies you, dulls you down to a beast. Half the church on Saturday is his old maths department, who'll clock that joke immediately; the other half is the parish, who knew him only as a sincere convert and won't want his faith called a bet. He left a note asking that the eulogy not smooth this into a road-to-Damascus story, because there wasn't one. Can you write it -- true to how he actually got there, funny for the mathematicians, and still real faith and not a con for everyone else?
