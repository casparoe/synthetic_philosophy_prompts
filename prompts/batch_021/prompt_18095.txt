Here's the question as it was posed on the exam: "In his unfinished Chapters on Socialism and in relevant passages of the Autobiography, explain (a) how Mill distinguishes communism from other varieties of socialism, (b) why he expresses a preference for Fourierism among the schemes he surveys, and (c) what Mill takes to be the main obstacle to adopting socialism in the near term — is this obstacle, on his view, a matter of justice or of practicality? And here's the student's answer, verbatim:

"Mill's Chapters on Socialism, unfinished at his death and brought out afterward by his widow Harriet Taylor, marks a real hardening of the sympathy for socialism found in the Principles of Political Economy. In the essays, Mill defines communism as the strand of socialist thought that abolishes private property altogether and distributes the produce of labor equally among all members regardless of the amount or kind of work performed, while he treats 'socialism' as the broader genus: any scheme that vests ownership of capital in the community or in associations of workers rather than in private individuals, whether or not the resulting shares are equal.

Of the schemes on offer, Mill singles out Fourierism as the most carefully worked out, because, unlike Owenite communism, it doesn't require members to be rewarded equally — each associate draws a share of the produce in proportion to the labor, capital, and talent they contribute, so ordinary incentives to effort and saving aren't removed at a stroke. Mill thought this made Fourierism more likely to succeed than a communism that, in his view, could only work among an already highly moralized population.

Mill's real hesitation about socialism, in the end, is a moral one: he thought that redistributing property accumulated over generations of lawful transactions would itself be a species of injustice to the people currently holding it, not so different from confiscation — and it's this worry about vested rights, which he voices at length in the Autobiography (where he says his and Harriet's ultimate ideal 'would class us decidedly under the general designation of Socialists'), that leads him to conclude that socialism can only be pursued gradually, through voluntary experiments like producer cooperatives, rather than by law."
