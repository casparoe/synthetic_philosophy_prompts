Two coworkers of mine — we're all engineers on the same team — got into a real argument in our Slack channel about Bas van Fraassen's Reflection Principle: the claim that your current confidence in a proposition should already match whatever confidence you're certain you'll have in it later, since failing to do so leaves you exploitable by a "diachronic" Dutch book — a pair of bets, one placed now and one later, that jointly guarantee a loss regardless of outcome. What set them off was this case: suppose you know for certain that tomorrow you'll take a pill that, by stipulation, makes anyone who takes it completely convinced for an hour that they're a brilliant public speaker, regardless of their actual ability. Right now, before the pill, you're just an average speaker who is fully aware of all this.

1. Priya said Reflection straightforwardly implies that your current confidence that you're a brilliant speaker should already be 1, since you're already certain that's where it will land in an hour, and anything less leaves you exploitable by the Dutch book argument.
2. Marcus said that's absurd: the pill gives you no evidence whatsoever about your actual speaking ability, so a principle telling you to become confident you're brilliant merely because you're about to be drugged into thinking so isn't a theory of rationality, it's a theory of surrendering to a predictable delusion.
3. Priya replied that this is precisely the sort of case van Fraassen's own qualification of Reflection is meant to rule out: the principle only governs future credences you expect to arise from your own rational, evidence-sensitive updating, not ones produced by drugs or manipulation, so the pill case simply falls outside its scope and was never a real counterexample.
4. Marcus said that fix rescues the letter of Reflection only by gutting its content: once the principle binds only when you already trust the process that will produce your future credence, all the real work is being done by that prior trust, not by anything to do with the credence being yours or being future — the identical patch would rescue "defer to a stranger's opinion" or "defer to a coin flip" just as well.
