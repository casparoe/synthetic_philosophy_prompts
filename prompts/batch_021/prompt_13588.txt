So I've been kind of obsessed with John von Neumann ever since I read a biography of him a couple months ago, and I found out that near the end of his life (he died in 1957) he wrote this essay called "Can We Survive Technology?" that came out in Fortune magazine in 1955. From what I remember, his big point was that technology's destructive reach had basically grown to cover the whole globe (nukes being the obvious example, but he also brought up stuff like humans messing with the climate on purpose or by accident), and that there's no one-time clean "solution" to this, just something like patience, flexibility and intelligence applied over and over again as new crises pop up. I also read that his friend Ulam wrote after von Neumann died that the two of them used to talk about technology accelerating toward some kind of "essential singularity" beyond which human life as we'd known it couldn't really continue, which is kind of wild to me since that's basically the exact word people throw around now with AI.

Obviously von Neumann never saw modern computing, let alone anything like an LLM, so this stuff came way after his time. But he seems like maybe the one guy back then who was simultaneously a top expert in computing AND seriously worried about tech outrunning humanity's ability to handle it, so I feel like his take would actually mean something here, more than randomly asking "what would Einstein think of AI" or whatever. My brother and I got into an argument about this actually, he thinks anyone that smart about computers would obviously see current AI-risk worries as overblown hype, and I'm not so sure. So based on what von Neumann actually argued in that essay and in his general outlook, where do you think he'd land on the modern AI existential-risk debate? Would he be more sympathetic to the "this could be a genuine species-level threat" camp, or the "we always freak out about new tech and muddle through fine" camp? I'm mainly interested in what specific parts of his own reasoning would point one way vs the other, not just a vibes-based guess.
