I have no formal training in philosophy; I became interested in the Victorian philosopher Henry Sidgwick after learning that he spent the better part of two decades personally investigating mediums and telepathy claims as a founder and first president of the Society for Psychical Research. What strikes me is that this was a thinker known even among his contemporaries for extreme caution and rigor, and that his own circle personally exposed several of the mediums they had once found promising as frauds -- yet none of this made him abandon the inquiry the way a less serious person might have after the first such disappointment. It seems to me that this kind of persistence, from someone with that reputation, is itself a sign that whatever he was looking at was more genuinely puzzling than the sort of debunked psychic claims we dismiss without a second thought today, and that the case has simply faded from memory rather than ever really being settled one way or the other. Is that a fair way to read his long involvement in the subject?
