Ok so this has been bugging me for like a week now. I was down a wiki rabbit hole (as one does at midnight instead of sleeping) and ran into this thing called the "dogmatism paradox" - pretty sure it traces back to Kripke, though the actual write-up I read was by some guy named Gilbert Harman. The argument is stupidly simple and I can't find the bug in it: if I know that P is true, then I also know that any future evidence against P has to be misleading (since P is true, and "evidence against a truth" is basically the definition of misleading evidence). But if I know THAT in advance, it seems like right now, today, I'm entitled to pre-commit to ignoring any evidence against P that shows up later, no matter how convincing it looks in the moment it actually shows up. Which is exactly the move every stubborn crank makes ("I already know I'm right, so whatever your showing me must be fake") except here the argument seems to actually be valid, not just some rationalization people use after the fact.

I brought this up with two coworkers and we just went around in circles. One of them said "well obviously knowledge doesn't work that way," which, sure, but that's not an explanation, that's just re-asserting that the conclusion is wrong. Digging a bit more, I found that people try to block the argument with a "defeasibility" requirement on knowledge (something like: you don't really know P if there's some true fact out there that would undermine your justification if you learned it), and other people apparently just deny that knowledge is "closed" in the way the second step of the argument needs. Neither explanation actually clicked for me - they both felt more like rules that happen to block the conclusion than like real explanations of where the reasoning breaks down.

Could you write me something like a short essay on this? Lay out the best 2-3 candidate solutions people have actually proposed, be honest about what each one costs you rather than just asserting it "works," and then actually commit to a verdict on which one (if any) succeeds - or tell me straight if you think none of them fully do and we're stuck accepting some limited form of dogmatism. Please don't end on a "reasonable people disagree" note, I'd like an actual answer I can walk away with.
