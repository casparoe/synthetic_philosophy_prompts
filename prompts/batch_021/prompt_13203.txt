There's 5 of us, and for each applicant we check three separate criteria -- strong methods, feasible timeline, and clear contribution -- with the rule that you only fund someone if they clear all three. Out of curiosity I tallied one applicant's file two different ways: (1) majority vote on each of the three criteria seperately, then fund only if all three passed by majority, vs (2) ask each committee member what THEIR overall fund/no-fund call is, given how they personally weigh the three criteria, then majority-vote on that final call. Same five people, same underlying opinions on the file, but the two methods gave opposite verdicts! A couple people on the committee are each convinced their way of tallying is obviously the "correct" one. I mostly do quant methods, not formal/social choice theory, so this isn't really my wheelhouse -- is one of these procedures actually more defensible than the other, or does the committee just not have one coherent collective opinion on this applicant?
