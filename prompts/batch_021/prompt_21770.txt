I have spent a fair amount of my spare time over the last couple of years reading about the Doomsday Argument, even though I have no formal background in philosophy, mathematics, or probability theory. I have never taken a class on any of this; I have just followed my own curiosity through old internet forum threads and a handful of essays and papers by Nick Bostrom until I felt I had a reasonably solid grip on the basic reasoning: if I picture myself as having been drawn at random from the set of everyone who will ever be born, the fact that I happen to find myself so early in that sequence, among at most the first hundred-odd billion humans, counts as mild evidence against there being some astronomically larger number of people still to come, and therefore as evidence for a nearer rather than a more distant "doomsday."

Where I keep getting stuck is the standard rebuttal built around what is called the Self-Indication Assumption, or SIA. As I understand it, SIA says that, other things being equal, you should favor hypotheses on which more observers exist in the first place, because you were more likely to exist at all under those hypotheses, and this move is supposed to roughly cancel out the pessimistic shift produced by the Doomsday reasoning. But then I read that this very same move is what gets you into trouble in Bostrom's "Presumptuous Philosopher" scenario, where a philosopher confidently sides with whichever physical theory implies vastly more observers exist, purely from an armchair, before the physicists have even run their experiment. I cannot quite see, on a gut level, why the reasoning that is supposed to rescue me from doomsday is the very same reasoning that produces such an obviously unreasonable level of confidence in the Presumptuous Philosopher case.

I have also seen people mention, without really spelling it out, that all of this connects somehow to the "halfer" versus "thirder" split in the Sleeping Beauty problem, though I do not follow how. Could you walk me through, in fairly plain language, why the SIA-style "more observers means more likely" move is supposed to have this double-edged character, cancelling one seemingly absurd conclusion only by generating another one just as strange? And is there some principled middle position that avoids both the doom-soon conclusion of the plain Doomsday Argument and the runaway overconfidence of the Presumptuous Philosopher, or have you come to think that you are simply forced to pick your poison between something like SSA and something like SIA?
