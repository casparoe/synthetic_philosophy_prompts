As a software engineer who works on governance tooling for DAOs, I recently came across the "boundary problem" in democratic theory—the puzzle of who should count as part of the demos for a given collective decision. It strikes me that this has mainly been a measurement limitation rather than a deep conceptual one: democracies have historically relied on crude proxies like citizenship or residency simply because there was no reliable way to identify everyone actually affected by a given policy. Now that we have verifiable digital identity, geolocation, and on-chain reputation, couldn't a well-designed DAO dissolve the boundary problem entirely by automatically enrolling anyone demonstrably affected by a proposal and weighting their vote by the size of that effect? Once the measurement problem is solved this way, what's actually left of the boundary problem?
