My mom's retiring after 35 years of teaching high school, and I (phd student in history and philosophy of science, so this is extremely on-brand for our family) got asked to give the toast at her retirement dinner. The complication is that she wrote her master's thesis on Schlick's essay on the meaning of life back in the 80s and has spent the decades since actually trying to live by it — his whole argument that meaning doesn't come from purposive activity aimed at some future payoff (which is basically what "work" is) but from stuff that's worth doing for its own sake, the way kids play, and that most people lose that capacity for good as they get older and get swallowed up by purposiveness. She's told me straight up that if anyone stands up at this dinner and praises her "hard work," her "dedication," or says her "legacy will live on" through her students, she will be low-key furious, because thats exactly the framing Schlick thought was the tragedy of adult life. The room's mostly former students and fellow teachers who've never heard of Moritz Schlick, plus three or four people from her old grad program who will absolutely clock it if i get the position wrong or just dodge it. Can you write the toast, like four minutes tops? It has to genuinely honor 35 years of teaching without leaning on the work/dedication/legacy language she'd hate, it needs to actually argue the philosophical position instead of just gesturing at it, and it still has to land for people with zero interest in Vienna Circle philosophy.
