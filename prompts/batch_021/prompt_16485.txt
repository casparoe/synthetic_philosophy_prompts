A friend of mine argues that animals are never wronged by how we treat them, since they can't reason the way people can, so nothing is owed to them for their own sake. Yet he was genuinely upset last week when he saw some older kids being deliberately cruel to a stray dog, and when I asked him why, he said it wasn't because the dog itself was wronged, but because someone who enjoys that kind of cruelty is more likely to end up treating people cruelly too. I can't tell whether he's contradicting himself by holding both of these views at once, or whether there's a deeper logic that makes them fit together after all. Is he being inconsistent, or does his position actually hang together?
