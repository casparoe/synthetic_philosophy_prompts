Years of reading rationalist blogs and forums have trained me to be suspicious of disputes that dissolve, on close inspection, into merely verbal disagreements, and I believe I have spotted one in epistemology. Working through several variants of DeRose's bank case, I find that epistemic contextualism and pragmatic encroachment (subject-sensitive invariantism) hand down exactly the same verdict on whether the subject counts as knowing, no matter how the stakes are set. If the two theories never actually disagree about a case, is there any principled reason left to treat them as rival accounts of knowledge, rather than as two vocabularies for redescribing the same underlying phenomenon?
