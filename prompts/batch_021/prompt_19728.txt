Our homeowners' association recently replaced plurality voting with ranked-choice (instant-runoff) voting for our annual board president election, a reform I championed on the grounds that it would reduce the strategic 'lesser of two evils' voting that had distorted results under the old system. This week, a fellow board member who opposed the change circulated a memo invoking the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem: since it proves that any voting rule for three or more candidates is either a dictatorship or vulnerable to strategic manipulation, she argues that our reform changed nothing of substance and that residents will simply learn to game the new ballots just as they gamed the old ones. Her argument sounds airtight to me, and I don't have enough background in social choice theory to push back effectively. Given that some degree of manipulability is apparently unavoidable no matter which voting rule we use, is it even worth keeping ranked-choice voting, or should we just spare everyone the added confusion and revert to plurality?
