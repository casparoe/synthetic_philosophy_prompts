My cousin and I jointly inherited our grandparents' lake cabin and, as equal co-owners, have always split the dozen or so usable summer weekends evenly, with the understanding that if we ever cannot agree on a schedule, we will sell the cabin and split the proceeds instead — an outcome neither of us wants. Last year he moved to a town twenty minutes from the lake, while I remain a full day's drive away, so he could realistically use eight or nine weekends a summer if I let him, whereas two or three is the most I could ever make good use of, no matter how many fall to me. He now argues our split should shift in his favor, since an extra weekend is worth far more to him than it costs me to give one up, and although I suspect something is wrong with this — not least that the same logic seems to justify giving him almost everything — I cannot articulate a better alternative. This has bothered me ever since, especially because it came up right as I started a game theory course this term, though I still cannot see how, if at all, the material applies here. Is he onto something real, or is there a more principled way to divide our time at the cabin, and if so, what would it actually recommend?
