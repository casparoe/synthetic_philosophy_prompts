In the course of reporting on AI-safety research, I keep running into references to the "Löbian obstacle" as a fundamental barrier to building agents that can trust their own future reasoning, or the reasoning of successor systems they design. Most of the write-ups I've found either gesture vaguely at Gödel's incompleteness theorems or plunge straight into the formalism of Löb's theorem and provability logic, without really bridging the two. Could you walk me through, in intuitive terms, why this obstacle specifically hinges on Löb's theorem rather than incompleteness in general, and why an agent's seemingly modest wish to trust that "whatever I (or my successor) can prove must be true" turns out to be so treacherous? I'd like to understand why this counts as a genuine, structural problem for self-trust and self-modification, rather than a mere technical curiosity that clever engineering could route around.
