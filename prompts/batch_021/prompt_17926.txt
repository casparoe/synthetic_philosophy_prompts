Ok, this is going to sound like an oddly specific question, but it's been rattling around in my head all week and I can't shake it. I sit on a small committee that awards a scholarship our philosophy department funds for first-generation majors, and at our last meeting two colleagues got into a genuine disagreement over how to choose between our final two candidates. One of them argued we should favor the student who genuinely would not enroll without the money, over the student who's more of a safe bet and would probably scrape tuition together some other way (loans, a job, family help) regardless of what we decide -- his reasoning being that funding the second student doesn't actually change anything about the world, so its basically wasted money. The other colleague said that's a nice principle in theory, but pointed out that we never get to see the version of events where we say no to a given student, so theres no real way of knowing what either of them would have done -- and then went further, wondering whether there's even a fact of the matter to know, given that enrolling is supposed to be a free choice on the student's part, not something that just falls out of their financial situation in some predictable way.

It turned into a bit of a standoff and, because apparently having a philosophy PhD makes you the designated tiebreaker, I got asked to rule on it. I've been avoiding doing so all week because I honestly don't know who's right. Something about the shape of the disagreement nags at me too, like I've run into this exact structure before somewhere completely unrelated to scholarship committees, but I can't place it. Is there a real fact about what a specific person would have freely chosen in a circumstance that, by hypothesis, never actually comes to pass -- and if there is, what could possibly make it true, rather than just a story we tell ourselves to feel better about our funding decisions? I'd genuinely like to know if there's a defensible answer here, and whether any philosophers have actually worked this out properly, because right now it just feels like we're arguing over a ghost.
