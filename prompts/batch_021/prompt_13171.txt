My office-mate of almost twenty years died last month after a rough year with cancer, and I've been asked to say a few words at her memorial next week. She was the last true-believing Leibnizian I know -- not the Pangloss strawman Voltaire stuck us with, but someone who could actually explain why compossibility (not all possible goods can coexist, so God's stuck with the best combination on offer, not an à la carte world) means this really is, in the strict technical sense, the best of all possible worlds, evils included. She used to get genuinely annoyed when people flattened that into 'everything happens for a reason.' Half the room will be colleagues who'll clock it immediately if I fudge the metaphysics or lean on the strawman; the other half is her family, who just watched her suffer for a year and have zero patience left for cosmic silver linings. Can you write the eulogy? Its got to actually honor what she thought -- including the bit she always insisted on, that no particular evil comes with its own neat justification, only that the whole is the best available balance -- without turning into a metaphysics seminar, and without making either half of that room wince.
