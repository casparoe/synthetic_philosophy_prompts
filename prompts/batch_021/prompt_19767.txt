I went down a rabbit hole and found out Michael Dummett — who my brother (he's doing a philosophy degree) says is basically THE big name in philosophy of language and logic, anti-realism, Frege, all that dense technical stuff — actually wrote a whole little book in 2001 called On Immigration and Refugees, which feels like such a random pivot. Turns out he'd spent decades as an anti-racist campaigner in Britain fighting things like the old Immigration Acts, and his actual position in the book (from what I can piece together) isn't "open borders" but also isn't "control immigration for the nation's sake" — its more like: states aren't incoherent in principle for wanting to restrict entry, but pretty much none of the real restrictions rich Western countries impose are actually for the neutral reasons they claim, and refugees fleeing persecution specifically are owed something close to an unconditional duty of admission, way stronger than the claim of someone migrating just for economic reasons.

I feel like a decent essay has to actually sit with the tension there instead of pretending his own view makes it obvious, so I want your best reconstruction of how he'd reason it through, not just a guess at the conclusion.
