My cousin's getting married in a few weeks and I somehow ended up on toast duty (long story, I'm closer to her than her actual siblings). Here's the complication: she and her fiancé met in the same modal metaphysics PhD program, and for basically their entire relationship they've had this running bit where one of them says something sappy like "there's no possible world where I don't end up with you," and the other one very deliberately ruins the moment. She's a died-in-the-wool Lewisian — counterpart theory, no object exists in more than one world, "you" in some other world is really just a counterpart who resembles you along whatever dimensions happen to be contextually relevant, so strictly speaking there's no metaphysical "you" out there for her to be reunited with. Her fiancé is Team Kripke — transworld identity, you don't need a similarity metric to know it's really you in another world, you just stipulate it, which is basically the whole point behind Kripke's objection to Lewis (something about how the actual Hubert Humphrey couldn't care less whether some Humphrey-resembling guy wins an election in a different world, because that guy isn't him). I've absorbed enough of this from years of holiday dinners to follow the argument, but I'm not a philosopher myself, so I don't fully trust myself to get the details right without help.

What I want is a toast, maybe three or four minutes, that's actually structured around this disagreement instead of just name-dropping it once as a joke — and that ends up being a genuinely sincere, romantic thing to say about these two specific people rather than either (a) a straight, uncomplicated "you'll be together in every possible world" line, which is exactly the sentence they'd each independently object to for their own technical reasons, or (b) a dry seminar on the metaphysics of modality that leaves the room cold. It also has to actually work as a toast for my aunt, the flower girl's parents, and everyone else who has never heard the word "counterpart" used this way in their life. Can you write it?
