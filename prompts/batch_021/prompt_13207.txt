My grandfather passed away back in March, and this month my family somehow ended up in a whole argument at Sunday dinner about his wedding ring. My mom wants to take it to a jeweler, melt it down, and have it recast into a pendant for me since I never wear rings but would actually wear a necklace. My uncle is really against it and keeps saying things like "once you melt it down its not dad's ring anymore, you'd basically be destroying the one thing we have left of him," and got pretty worked up about it. My mom thinks that's silly — her point is that it's literally the same gold, the same atoms, nothing gets thrown away or added in the process, so how can anything be "destroyed" if all the material is still sitting right there afterward, just in a different shape?

Honestly I keep flip-flopping on who's right. My mom's logic about the atoms seems hard to poke holes in, but my uncle's reaction doesn't feel like pure sentimentality either — it feels like he's tracking something real that he just can't articulate, which, same, because I can't either. I mentioned this whole thing to a friend from college who took one philosophy class years ago, and she said something about how "the ring and the metal it's made of might not actually be the same thing," which honestly just confused me more instead of clearing anything up. Is there an actual principled way to work out whether the pendant would really be "grandpa's ring" continuing on in a new form, or a brand new object that just happens to share its material with the ring, or does this question just not have a real answer beyond what it means to each of us emotionally? I'd love something more substantive than "it's whatever you make of it," because I genuinely think my mom and my uncle are each onto something true and I want to understand what that is before we decide anything.
