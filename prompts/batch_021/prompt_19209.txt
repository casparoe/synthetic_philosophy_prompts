I'm a retired physiologist who's spent part of retirement falling down the philosophy of science rabbit hole, especially the stats side. I still sit on my old society's Research Integrity Committee -- I guess they keep the emeritus types around so somebody remembers doing this with a slide rule -- and we've got an awkward case for Tuesday. A mid-career PI checked her data every few weeks as animals came through and stopped the moment the effect crossed p<.05, landing at n=54: classic optional stopping, the kind of thing that's supposed to be a big driver of the replication mess. There was no preregistration (this was 2011, before that was standard in our field). Her defense is that peeking is only a sin if your a frequentist about what a p-value means -- the data don't care how she decided to stop collecting them. One committee member, who's been reading Mayo, wants a misconduct finding, since to her this is exactly the error-rate violation behind our field's replication problems. Please draft the memo recommending where this lands -- dismiss it, call it a 'questionable practice' short of misconduct (with whatever corrective step that implies), or refer it up as misconduct -- and actually engage the stopping-rule defense rather than just asserting that error rates are what matter.
