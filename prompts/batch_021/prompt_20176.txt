I teach a graduate seminar on social choice theory and collective decision-making, and this term I would like to pair the technical readings on judgment aggregation with a piece of short fiction. Please write a short story, roughly 1,000 to 1,500 words, that dramatizes the discursive dilemma (sometimes called the doctrinal paradox in the legal literature): a three-member hospital ethics committee must rule on whether to authorize withdrawal of a patient's life support, under a standing policy that permits withdrawal only if two independent conditions both hold (for instance, that the advance directive clearly applies to the patient's present state, and that physicians agree there is no reasonable prospect of meaningful recovery). Construct the members' individual judgments on those two conditions so that the committee's decision would come out one way under a premise-based procedure (vote on each condition separately, then apply the policy) and the opposite way under a conclusion-based procedure (vote directly on the verdict). The logical structure should be legible through plot and dialogue rather than through a character pausing to lecture on judgment-aggregation theory, and the story should leave open, rather than resolve, the question of which procedure the committee ought to have trusted.
