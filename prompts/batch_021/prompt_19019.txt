I went down a bit of a rabbit hole recently on Gödel's letters to his mother, where he apparently laid out an actual argument for why there has to be some kind of afterlife -- roughly: the world isn't meaningless, it's "rationally organized" (I think this connects to what people call his "rationalistic optimism," basically a Leibniz-style principle-of-sufficient-reason view of the universe), and it would be absurd for a mind to spend decades carefully developing a whole personality and body of knowledge only to be totally annihilated right when it's finally mature -- so there has to be some further stage where that development isn't just wasted. He says something similar later on in that religious questionnaire he filled out for a sociology grad student named Grandjean, where he says his belief in an afterlife rests on rational grounds, not on theology or church doctrine. Every time this comes up online it's just "wow, even Gödel believed in Heaven," and nobody actually leans on the argument to see if it holds up.

Write me a dialogue between two philosophers who both take rationalism (in that Leibnizian, principle-of-sufficient-reason sense) completely seriously -- so neither one gets to dismiss the argument as unscientific from the outside -- but who end up disagreeing about whether Gödel's specific inference actually goes through. One should push it about as far as it can honestly be pushed; the other should grant the rationalist premises but argue they smuggle in way more than they license -- like, does "the universe is intelligible" really get you all the way to individual persons surviving death, as opposed to some impersonal process just continuing on, and is the "wasted potential" bit doing real argumentative work or just importing the conclusion he already wants. I want them actually responding to each other's specific moves turn by turn, not each just restating their own view past the other, and its fine -- good, even -- if it doesn't end in a neat resolution, since i don't think this argument obviously has one.
