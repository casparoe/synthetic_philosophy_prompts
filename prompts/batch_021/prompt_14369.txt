I write novels, which means my working life is spent deciding why invented people do what they do, and what that does or doesn't make of them. Lately I can't manage the same clarity about myself. My mother is dying slowly, and for the past two months I have flown home every other week to oversee her hospice care and sit with her at night, while my brother, who lives fifteen minutes away, visits rarely and seems entirely untroubled by it. If I'm honest, none of this comes from love — I haven't been close to my mother in thirty years — but from an inability to bear being remembered by our family, and eventually by my own children, as the one who wasn't there. My brother has taken to calling me "the good one"; everyone agrees, and the care my mother is getting is genuinely excellent, as attentive as it would be if I adored her. But something about the praise sits wrong with me, in a way I can't quite pin down. Since she is getting exactly the care she would get if my motives were nobler, is there really anything to the feeling that what I'm doing counts for less than it looks like from the outside — or is that just a more sophisticated way of feeling guilty?
