Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I've taken up philosophy, and last year I joined the review panel of a small foundation that funds research at the intersection of physics and philosophy. One proposal argues that the modern search for a "final theory" in physics quietly assumes that nature's hierarchy of grounding is well-founded and bottoms out in something fundamental, when the history of physics — molecules, atoms, nucleons, quarks — seems just as consistent with an endless descent that never bottoms out ("grounding infinitism") as with matter that is "gunky" all the way down; the applicants want funding to work out what would count as evidence for either picture, and whether a groundless physics could still explain and predict as well as one that terminates. Two panelists call this unfalsifiable metaphysics dressed in physics vocabulary and want it rejected outright; two others consider it precisely the boundary-crossing work the fund exists for, and mine is the deciding vote. Please draft the panel's short decision memo — fund, reject, or fund with revisions — due before Friday's meeting, with reasons the skeptical panelists could actually engage with rather than dismiss.
