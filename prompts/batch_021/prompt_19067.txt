So I'm midway through a PhD in philosophy of biology, and I've been having this arguement with my advisor for weeks now about whether there's anything actually left of the units-of-selection debate once you take the math seriously. The basic thing bugging me: if you write out Hamilton's rule (rb > c) via the Price equation and partition fitness into within-group and between-group covariance terms, you can build a "kin selection" model and a "group selection" (multi-level selection) model of the exact same social-evolution scenario, and they spit out identical predictions for how allele frequencies change. This kind of formal equivalence between kin-selectionist and group-selectionist bookkeeping is pretty well established at this point in the theoretical biology literature. My advisor's take is that this settles the whole debate in favor of a deflationary view: which "level" you say selection acts at is just a matter of which partition/regression is more convenient for the problem at hand, full stop, there's no further metaphysical or causal fact about whether selection "really" happens to genes, individuals, or groups. I keep wanting to push back using something like Okasha's MLS1 vs MLS2 distinction, or Sober and Wilson's stuff about group-level fitness being a genuine causal category rather than just relabeled individual fitness, but every time I try to spell it out loud it feels like I'm just insisting there's a further fact instead of actually showing one.

Can you make my advisor's case for her, as rigorously as possible? I.e., argue that the formal equivalence between kin-selection/Hamilton's-rule formulations and multi-level-selection formulations of social evolution shows that the units-of-selection question is ultimately notational/perspectival, with no substantive further fact (causal, ontological, or otherwise) about which level selection "really" acts on. Please engage directly with the natural Okasha-style objection — that MLS2, where group fitness is counted in offspring groups rather than average offspring per individual, picks out a genuinely distinct causal structure that mere reparameterization of one model into another can't capture — rather than just flagging the objection and moving past it.
