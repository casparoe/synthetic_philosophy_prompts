Over dinner last week, a friend and I fell into an unexpectedly long argument that began from a far more mundane question: whether I should propose to my partner. I had spent weeks weighing our compatibility, our finances, and whether we were both ready, when my friend interrupted to say that the entire exercise was, in a sense, beside the point. She was careful to specify that she was not invoking brain chemistry, upbringing, or any hidden causal chain steering my choice; her claim had nothing to do with physical determinism, she insisted. It was this: right now, before I have decided anything, it is already either true that I propose and we are happy together years later, or true that I propose and it falls apart, or true that I never propose at all, and so on for every way the story could unfold. Exactly one of these is already the fact about my future, whether or not anyone, even a perfect predictor, could know which one.

She granted that my deliberating might well turn out to be part of that true story - perhaps the true version reads, "he proposed because he thought it through carefully and concluded he should." But she insisted that which conclusion I end up reaching was itself already settled, long before I sat down to weigh anything, so that I am less the open-ended author of that conclusion than a character finding out which lines he was always going to deliver. On her view, treating the decision as genuinely "up to me," capable of tipping one way or the other depending on how carefully I reason, is a comforting illusion, rather like imagining you could change what is written inside a sealed envelope by staring at it harder before you open it.

We argued for well over an hour and resolved nothing; I keep returning to it days later. It does not sit right with me to conclude that careful deliberation achieves nothing simply because there is already a definite truth about how things will go, but I cannot cleanly locate the mistake in her reasoning, if indeed there is one. Does the step from "it is already true or false how this will turn out" to "my deliberating cannot be doing any real work" actually hold up under scrutiny? And if it does not hold up, is the trouble in what she is quietly taking "already true" to mean, or does it lie somewhere else in the chain entirely? I would like an answer precise enough to bring back to her, rather than a general reassurance that free will simply "feels real" from the inside.
