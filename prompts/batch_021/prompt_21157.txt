My uncle Ray is retiring after 38 years on the floor at the auto plant. Everybody there calls him "the professor," even though he never finished high school -- he taught himself Marx out of secondhand paperbacks decades ago and never stopped. He's insufferable about exactly one thing: say "exploitation" when you mean "alienation," or the other way round, and he'll stop you mid sentence. To him exploitation is the cold hard math of it (they pay you for part of what your labor's worth and pocket the rest), and alienation is the seperate, sadder thing about not recognizing yourself in what you make, or in who the job's turned you into after 30 years on the line. I'm giving his toast at the retirement dinner next month -- half the room is old union guys who'll clock it immediately if I mix the two up, the other half is his grandkids who just want a nice speech about grandpa. Write me something around five minutes long: funny, warm, keeps the two ideas straight the way he'd insist on, and never turns into an actual lecture on Capital.
