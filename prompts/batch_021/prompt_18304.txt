"Humeans think that reality is, at bottom, just a huge arrangement of separate, local facts -- what Lewis calls the 'Humean mosaic' -- and that the laws of nature aren't some extra ingredient pushing this arrangement around, they're just the most efficient description of it. On Lewis's Best System Analysis, out of all the true deductive systems you could write about the whole history of the world, the laws are whichever axioms belong to the system that best balances being simple with being informative. So laws are read off of the pattern of events, not the other way around; this idea actually goes back to Bertrand Russell's early work on causation and was only later formalized by Lewis, which is why its sometimes called the Russell-Lewis account.

Armstrong disagrees with this picture. For him, a law like "all F's are G's" holds because there is a relation of necessitation, N(F,G), between the universals F-ness and G-ness themselves, and not just between the particular F's and G's that happen to exist. Because this necessitation is a real connection between universals rather than just a summary of instances, it holds with metaphysical necessity, meaning the very same regularities would have to hold in absolutely any possible world where those universals are instantiated at all -- which is exactly the kind of necessary connection between distinct things that Hume said we could never actually find.

The classic problem on Lewis's side is that mere patterns don't seem able to explain their own instances: if it's a law that all F's are G's just because that regularity happens to show up in the best system, then saying "this F is G because it's a law" is basically circular, since the lawhood itself is just constituted by the pattern being there. The classic problem on Armstrong's side is the "inference problem": even granting that N(F,G) is a real relation between universals, it's not obvious why that should guarantee that every particular F actually is a G -- you need some further story about how a connection "up there" among universals manages to constrain what happens "down here" among particulars."
