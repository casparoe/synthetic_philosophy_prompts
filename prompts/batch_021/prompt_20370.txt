So I spent about 35 years running a visual neuroscience lab before I retired, and these days I fill the time reading philosophy, which is humbling in a way running a lab never quite was. Lately I've been stuck on Bradley's regress and the "unity of the proposition" business, and I noticed what feels like a real parallel to a problem from my own field. I've written my thinking up below as a rough little essay (I'm mostly working from secondary sources so I could easily have some detail wrong) — could you give me comments, both on whether I've gotten the philosophy right and on whether the analogy is actually doing any work or is just a nice-sounding coincidence?

Here's what I've got:

In vision science there's the "binding problem": area V4 gives you colour, area MT gives you motion, some other patch of cortex gives you shape, and none of these areas are wired to just hand their output to a central assembler. Yet what you experience is one red ball rolling left, not three orphaned facts — "red," "round," "moving" — lying around unconnected. The old fix people reached for was a further signal that ties the features together, but that just relocates the question: now what ties the tying-signal to the features it ties? The fix a lot of us ended up liking instead (Singer and Gray's old work, and Crick and Koch ran with it for consciousness) was synchrony: neurons coding red and neurons coding round and neurons coding moving-left just fire in step with each other, around 40Hz, and the unity isn't produced by some further item that then itself needs binding to the rest — it just is the synchronized firing, full stop. No extra relatum, so no extra step in the regress.

Reading about Bradley, I can't shake the feeling this is the identical shape wearing a toga. "Socrates is wise" isn't just Socrates and wisdom sitting in a heap; something has to make it one fact rather than two loose items — thats the unity-of-the-proposition problem Russell and everyone after him inherited. If that something is a relation (instantiation, say), Bradley's regress says you now need a further relation tying instantiation to Socrates and to wisdom, forever. And a move I keep running into — denying instantiation is a further relation at all, some kind of primitive "non-relational tie," or Frege's line that concepts are inherently "unsaturated" so nothing further is needed to attach them to objects — smells exactly like my synchrony story: you stop the regress by insisting the unifier just isn't the kind of thing that itself needs unifying.

What I want pushback on: is this actually the same philosophical move, or am I just seeing my own field's furniture everywhere I look? And if it is the same move, does that help Bradley's regress along any (because hey, it worked well enough for vision science) or does it just mean neuroscience never really solved binding either, and "synchrony" is doing the same hand-wave as "unsaturated" — a label for the mystery rather than a solution to it. Don't go easy on me, i'd rather be told its a bad analogy now than repeat it at a dinner party later.
