So a couple nights ago a friend of mine and I ended up way down a rabbit hole about acausal threats and blackmail -- you know, the whole Roko's-basilisk-adjacent idea that a sufficiently capable agent (a future AI, a simulator, whatever) could get leverage over you right now just by both of you being able to model each other's reasoning, with no actual causal channel between you. I'd read a blog post a while back about a proposed defense against this called "surrogate goals": you deliberately commit to treating some decoupled, kind of arbitrary goal as if it mattered just as much as what you actually care about, so a rational threatener -- who only wants leverage, not harm for its own sake -- ends up indifferent between targeting the real thing and targeting the decoy. If a threat ever does get carried out, it lands on the fake goal instead of the real one.

My friend thought this was clever but ultimately confused, and we argued about it for like an hour without getting anywhere. Her point was basically: for the decoy to actually pull threats away from the real goal, you have to genuinely commit to defending it exactly as hard as you'd defend the real thing -- so by your own revealed behavior it just becomes a second real terminal value, and you havent reduced your vulnerability at all, you've doubled it. Plus, anything smart enough to threaten you acausally in the first place is presumably smart enough to just target "whatever this agent protects most fiercely, structurally speaking," and for the surrogate to actually be less costly to lose than the real goal (otherwise what's the point), it can't be quite as fiercely protected -- which means that kind of threat just resolves back onto the real goal anyway. I kept trying to say that credibility and actual stakes can come apart, that you can act on a commitment without the outcome mattering equally on reflection, but I never landed a response that actually satisfied her, or me if im honest.

Could you write a dialogue between two characters who each hold one side of this (name them or just label them, whatever's easier), where they really engage with each other's strongest points instead of talking past each other? Give the "surrogate goals can work" side a genuine shot, not a strawman, and same for the skeptic -- and if there's an actual crux hiding in here, I'd love for it to surface, even if the dialogue doesn't fully resolve it.
