Okay so this argument happened at the pub after our philosophy of psychiatry reading group last week and I still havent gotten it out of my head. We'd just gone through Hacking on looping effects, and somebody brought up the recent surge in adult autism diagnoses as a test case. Two of my postdocs, Mia and Dan, ended up going a few rounds over it.

(1) Mia said looping basically debunks the idea that we're "discovering" more autistic people who were there all along: once autism became a public, self-applicable label -- helped along by autistic people's own memoirs and online communities -- people learned to recognize and perform the traits in themselves, clinicians learned to look for subtler presentations, and the criteria loosened to match. So the surge is substantially the classification creating its own targets, not uncovering a pre-existing population.

(2) Dan said that's exactly the phenomenon Hacking calls a "moving target," but Mia's helping herself to a much stronger conclusion than looping actually licenses: an interactive kind can still be tracking something real even while it moves. The category shifting and expanding doesn't show there's no fact about who'd meet sober diagnostic criteria; it shows the criteria, and peoples relationship to them, are unstable in a way that "has three copies of chromosome 21" isn't.

(3) Mia replied that once the target is partly constituted by the classification -- people learning to see and describe themselves through it, clinicians primed by that self-description -- there's no classification-independent fact left for the surge to be a surge *in*. It's looping all the way down.

(4) Dan said that proves too much: the whole point of Hacking's distinction between interactive and indifferent kinds is that a kind can loop *and* still be interacting with something -- a temperament, a cognitive profile -- that isn't just made of the classifying. Otherwise "looping effect" collapses into "social construct all the way down," which is a much stronger thesis than Hacking actually wanted.
