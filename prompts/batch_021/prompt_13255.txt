I'm on my university's IACUC (the committee that has to sign off on animal research protocols) and theres a fight brewing over a proposed study that repeatedly zaps octopuses with noxious stimuli to test analgesics. One member wants to slot it into a lower pain category, and her argument leans on higher-order theories of consciousness: since an octopus's nervous system is so decentralized (most of its neurons sit out in the arms, not the central brain), she says there's no good reason to think it has whatever higher-order machinery those theories say is needed for a nociceptive signal to become an actually-felt pain rather than just a reflex. Another member thinks that's way too much weight to hang on a contested theory when animal welfare is on the line. Can you draft the memo I'll bring to next week's meeting -- approve, approve with added safeguards, or reject -- that actually engages with the higher-order argument instead of just waving it off as too speculative to matter?
