Write a short story built around updateless decision theory, the decision-theory idea that comes up a lot in AI alignment and rationalist circles. The basic idea: what's rational for you to do can depend on the policy you'd have committed to before you knew which way a situation was going to break, not on what actually benefits you now that you already know how it broke. The standard toy example is counterfactual mugging: a predictor wants payment from you in the one branch where paying cant possibly help you anymore, precisely because being the kind of agent who pays is what made the deal good on average across both branches. Don't just retell that one though, invent your own characters and stakes. The hard part is making the "wait, why follow through now, it can't actually help me anymore" pull land emotionally through the plot itself, rather than having a character explain the logic out loud. Doesn't have to involve AI or an explicit predictor either, if you find a better way into it.
