Now retired after a career in experimental physics, I have finally had time to read philosophy seriously, and I confess I always assumed the free-will debate simply was the debate over determinism. Galen Strawson's Basic Argument unsettled that assumption: he holds that true moral responsibility requires being causa sui in certain crucial mental respects — responsible for the mental character behind one's choices, which would require already being responsible for whatever produced that character, and so on in a regress that can never be completed — and he insists this regress bites whether determinism, indeterminism, or some mixture of the two obtains. Write an essay assessing whether Strawson has actually earned that neutrality claim. Address in particular whether libertarian appeals to indeterministic choice, such as Robert Kane's "self-forming actions" made at moments of unsettled motivation, manage to halt the regress, or whether they merely substitute luck for necessity without restoring any responsibility-conferring control. Reach and defend a definite verdict: does the Basic Argument succeed, and does its claimed independence from the determinism/indeterminism dispute survive scrutiny?
