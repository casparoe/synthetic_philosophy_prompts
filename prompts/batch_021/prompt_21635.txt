So this happened at a group house dinner and I haven't been able to stop thinking about it. My friend Gabe and his housemate Noor jointly held a Manifold Markets position that resolved YES for $8,000 combined. Before it resolved, Noor could've cashed out her share alone for a guaranteed $1,500; Gabe's chunk was bigger but way less liquid, so his solo walkaway was only $500. Now they actually have to agree on a split.

(1) Gabe's opening move: split the surplus over each person's walkaway 50/50, so $3,500 for him and $4,500 for her. He said this isn't even a value judgment, it's literally the Nash bargaining solution, the unique split satisfying a short list of pretty minimal-looking axioms.

(2) Noor wasn't having it: equal dollars over the walkaway isn't equal value over the walkaway. Gabe sold a startup last year, so an extra $500 barely registers for him, wheras it's a big deal to her. She thinks the fair benchmark is for each of them to get the same fraction of the best they could conceivably get -- Kalai-Smorodinsky style -- since that actually tracks what's at stake for each person instead of just raw dollars.

(3) Gabe: that "best you could conceivably get" number is a hypothetical neither of us would ever jointly reach, so why should some scenario that was never on the table move what I owe you?

(4) Noor: sure, but I've read that your rule has a mirror-image problem -- there are cases where someone's own outside options improve and the "split-the-surplus-evenly" answer moves against them anyway. That's apparently the actual reason Kalai and Smorodinsky proposed their solution in the first place, mine is built so that can't happen.

(5) Gabe: doesn't this whole thing just wash out to "use whichever formula happens to favor you," dressed up in game theory?

I've seen this exact Nash-vs-KS thing referenced on rationalist blogs before but only ever in the abstract, so I have no idea who's actually landing real points here versus talking past each other. Can you go through it move by move and say who's more right at each numbered point -- not just who wins overall -- and flag anything you think both of them are missing?
