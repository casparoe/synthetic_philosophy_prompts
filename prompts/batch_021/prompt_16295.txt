I cover evolutionary biology, and next week I'm profiling a theoretical biologist who has built her career rehabilitating group selection as a genuine causal force behind traits like eusociality and large-scale human cooperation, working in the tradition of multilevel selection theory. In public debate she reliably closes down the standard 'it's just bookkeeping' objection -- the charge that any multilevel-selection model can be losslessly rewritten as an inclusive-fitness (kin-selection) model once you run the numbers through the Price equation -- with the line 'that's a translation, not a refutation.' Please draft eight to ten interview questions that make that line progressively harder to lean on: I want at least one question pressing her on what would distinguish a genuinely selected group from an arbitrary statistical aggregate of individuals, at least one asking what empirical finding could in principle favor a multilevel-selection description over an inclusive-fitness one rather than a mere relabeling of the same variance, and a final question asking whether she would concede that the units-of-selection debate is, at bottom, not an empirical dispute at all but a disagreement over which partition of the same process is most illuminating. For each question, add a short follow-up keyed to the dodge she is most likely to reach for.
