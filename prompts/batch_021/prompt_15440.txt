Hey — I'm the ethics professor who got roped into my state parole board's risk-tool advisory panel (mostly so they can say a philosopher signed off). We ran two competing actuarial tools on the same redacted case file and got 63% vs 21% chance of reoffending in three years for the same guy — not a bias thing, both use defensible, race-blind variables, they just build his comparison group differently (one matches him nationally on offense type and criminal-history score, the other locally on a tighter set including age and neighborhood). Both vendors insist their number is simply the correct probability for this individual. Half the panel wants to mandate whichever tool matches on more variables, since "more specific" sounds more accurate. The other half says theres no principled place to stop narrowing the match — match him on his birthday too and every "probability" collapses to 0 or 1 for a sample of one — so maybe handing commisioners one clean percentage was never coherent in the first place. I owe the panel a short memo by Thursday: give one concrete recommendation (adopt tool A, adopt tool B, drop both for clinical judgment, require a range instead of a point estimate, whatever), real reasons for it, and an honest crack at the best argument for whichever option you don't pick.
