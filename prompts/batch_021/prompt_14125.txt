Six weeks ago, after my mother's breast cancer diagnosis, I had a multi-gene hereditary-cancer panel done, mainly to see whether I carry any of the same variants. The results came back positive for one, and the report itself described it, in a line I found oddly comforting at first, as part of the "code" that tells cells when and how to regulate their own growth. When I went over the results with my actual genetic counselor, she gave a much more hedged picture: the variant is associated with some increase in lifetime risk, she said, but penetrance is incomplete, meaning a substantial fraction of carriers never develop the disease, and diet, hormones, other genes, and what she candidly called "chance" all contribute in ways nobody can currently disentangle. The gap between "this is the code for cancer" and "this shifts a probability that depends on a hundred other things" has been bothering me ever since.

My husband, who works in telecommunications, tried to reassure me by comparing my situation to a compressed file that has been corrupted in transit: the information is still "in there," he said, it just isn't always read out faithfully. I couldn't quite say why, but that comparison felt like it was smuggling in the very thing I'm now unsure about — that there is a determinate message sitting in my cells waiting to be decoded correctly or incorrectly, rather than something whose eventual meaning only gets fixed jointly, after the fact, by the DNA together with everything around it. Reading further, I was surprised to find that this isn't just my own confusion: biologists and philosophers of science have argued for decades over whether it is a substantive discovery that genes carry "information" or "instructions," or whether that vocabulary is an analogy borrowed from telecommunications and cryptography that molecular biology never quite outgrew. I would like to actually understand what is at stake in that disagreement rather than be soothed in a way that talks past it. Could you lay out the strongest version of each side, and then tell me whether taking either side should change how much weight I put on what my own results are supposedly telling me about my future?
