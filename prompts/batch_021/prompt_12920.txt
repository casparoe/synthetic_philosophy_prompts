Random personal thing that's been rattling around in my head and I can't quite pin down. My friend from undergrad got diagnosed with ADHD last year at 28 - before that she just thought of herself as flaky and bad with deadlines. Since then she's gotten really into adhd tiktok and a couple of subreddits, and she does seem different now: she narrates most of her day through "my adhd brain," and stuff she used to just quietly push through (losing track of time, blurting things out, ect.) is way more visible and frequent than before. Part of me thinks she's just finally unmasking, which is great, but another part wonders if the label and the online community are partly producing some of this rather than just revealing it. I'm in a clinical psych phd program so I know the usual "is adhd overdiagnosed" arguments, but this feels like a different question - less about whether the diagnosis is accurate and more about whether the diagnosis and the person are now shaping each other over time. Does it even make sense to ask whether her adhd was "real" before she got the label, or am I overthinking a fairly normal thing?
