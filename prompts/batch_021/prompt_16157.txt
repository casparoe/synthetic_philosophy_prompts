I sit on the internal governance committee for Meridian Analytics' employee forecasting platform, a Hanson-style subsidized prediction market that leadership built with the standard no-trade-theorem worry already in mind: if everyone shares a common prior and reasons like a good Bayesian, a colleague's willingness to take the other side of your bet should, per Aumann, already tell you your apparent information edge is illusory, so without a subsidized market maker absorbing the other side there might be no volume at all. Lately the platform's most actively traded contract asks whether the Atlas project will ship on schedule, and its largest and most consistently profitable trader is Priya, a senior engineer who also sits on the review board that grants schedule extensions -- she is, in effect, trading on private information her own board generates. Several other employees have quietly stopped trading Atlas contracts, reporting in exit surveys that a large Priya position now reads as an announcement that the question is already settled, and contract volume has fallen by more than half since the pattern became visible. Our written trading policy requires only that positions "reflect good-faith, individually held beliefs" and says nothing about board members trading on matters within their own purview. Please draft the committee's decision memo recommending a specific, adoptable policy on insiders trading on markets that touch their own institutional authority -- with a concrete rule and a timeline for next week's meeting -- and give a candid account of what the platform's core purpose would lose if Priya's trading were restricted.
