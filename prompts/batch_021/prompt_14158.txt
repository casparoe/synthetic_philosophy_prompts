So I'm a retired analytical chemist -- 35 years running HPLC and mass spec before I hung up my pipettes for good -- and since retiring I've fallen into a bit of a philosophy rabbit hole, started by a used copy of Sklar's Space, Time, and Spacetime that I picked up at a library sale. Now I'm part of a little monthly meetup of other retired science types who read this stuff together, and at the last session two of the regulars, Warren (taught high school physics for thirty-some years) and Nadia (retired systems engineer), got into a genuinely heated back-and-forth over whether Newton's bucket actually proves absolute space is real. I wasnt equipped to referee it myself, since I've only been at this a few months, so I wrote down the exchange as best I could reconstruct it afterward, and I'm hoping you can do what I couldn't.

Roughly how it went:

1. Warren said the bucket settles it: right after you let the twisted cord go, the bucket is spinning hard relative to the water but the surface stays flat; once the water catches up and rotates right along with the bucket -- zero relative motion between them -- the surface goes concave. So the concavity does the opposite of what "relative motion explains it" would predict, meaning it has to track rotation relative to space itself, not relative motion to any nearby object.

2. Nadia said that only shows Newton skipped the control that actually mattered -- rotating the water relative to everything else in the universe, not just the bucket. Mach's whole point, she said, was that nobody's actually removed all the other matter and re-run the experiment, so for all the bucket shows us, the water could be responding to its rotation relative to the fixed stars: still a relation, just a cosmic one instead of a local one, so relationism survives.

3. Warren answered that you obviously can't run Mach's version for real, so as stated it's untestable -- but there's a cleaner way to see it's not just semantics: picture spinning the bucket in a universe containing only the bucket and the water. Newton's picture says the water still climbs the sides. Strict Machian relationism has to say nothing happens, since theres nothing left to rotate relative to. Different predictions, so it's a genuine disagreement, not a verbal one.

4. Nadia said fine, but physics went looking for Machian effects and actually found some: frame-dragging is real, and Gravity Probe B was built partly to detect it, and did. Rotating masses measurably drag nearby inertial frames around with them -- matter influencing "absolute" inertial structure exactly the way Mach said it should, and Newton's picture says it can't. So the evidence has moved toward Mach, not away from him.

5. Warren conceded frame-dragging is real and Machian in spirit, but said it's a small correction sitting on top of a spacetime that already has inertial structure built in with no matter required at all -- flat empty spacetime is a perfectly good solution of general relativity's own equations. And Gödel found a solution where the whole universe's matter rotates relative to local gyroscopes with nothing outside it to be rotating relative to, which is just as bad for strict Machianism. So general relativity ends up backing a "sophisticated" substantivalism -- spacetime itself as a dynamical thing playing Newton's old role -- more than it saves relationism.

6. Nadia's last word was that "sophisticated" substantivalism only dodges a nasty indeterminism problem (if spacetime points are individuals in their own right, general relativity's equations stop pinning down a unique future) by denying spacetime points have any identity apart from the web of relations they sit in -- which just smuggles relationism back in at the level of points. So Warren's victory lap is premature.

By around step 5 I'll admit I was just scribbling to keep up, so I'd love an outside read on this. Could you go through it move by move -- maybe score each of the six points from -2 (pretty clearly wrong) to +2 (pretty clearly right) in a short json block, then explain each score in a couple sentences -- and then tell me if there's some bigger point about this whole substantivalism/relationism business that both Warren and Nadia are just skating past?
