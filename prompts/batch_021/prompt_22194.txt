I recently submitted my doctoral dissertation, and while writing the preface I caught myself wanting to add the hedge that authors seem to include as a matter of course: I am confident in every individual claim the dissertation makes, but given its length and the inevitability of human error, I would be surprised if it did not contain at least one mistake somewhere. A friend in the philosophy department told me, with some amusement, that I had just rediscovered the preface paradox, and pointed me to its close cousin, the lottery paradox. I've since spent a few evenings reading around the topic, and what strikes me is that the two puzzles are very often introduced together, as though they were simply two illustrations of one underlying problem -- justified belief in each of a set of propositions alongside justified belief that not all of them are true -- yet a good deal of the literature is devoted precisely to arguing that they should not be treated alike.

I'd like an essay that takes this question head-on rather than using the two paradoxes as interchangeable set-up for a single moral. Present the strongest case for a unified diagnosis of both puzzles -- presumably some restriction on the closure (or agglomeration) of justified belief under conjunction -- and then present the strongest case for pulling them apart, engaging with considerations such as the contrast between the merely statistical grounds for any single lottery proposition and the direct, case-by-case, non-statistical evidence behind each sentence of a dissertation, and with the suggestion that the individual lottery beliefs might undercut one another in a way the individual claims of a preface do not. Address, too, what a defender of closure has to give up instead -- most obviously the Lockean thesis tying rational outright belief to sufficiently high credence -- and whether that cost is really more bearable. Don't leave the essay hanging between these options: conclude by saying whether the lottery and preface paradoxes ultimately call for the same solution or for different ones, and defend whichever specific principle you think should be sacrificed to get there.
