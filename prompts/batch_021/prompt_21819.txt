I spent thirty-five years as a condensed-matter physicist before I retired, and somehow in the last year or two I've gotten completely hooked on philosophy of mind, which baffles my old colleagues to no end. It started when a former postdoc of mine sent me a paper on Integrated Information Theory and joked that it read like "thermodynamics for consciousness," and honestly the vocabulary did feel like home to me — integration, differentiation, this whole Phi quantity you're supposed to maximize over partitions of a system. Once I pulled on that thread I found Scott Aaronson's old blog post where he builds some toy circuit of simple logic gates, wired up like an expander graph, that ends up with enormous Phi despite doing nothing anyone would call thinking, and then the whole business a couple of years back where over a hundred consciousness researchers signed an open letter basicaly calling IIT pseudoscience.

So here's my actual situation. I'm part of a small monthly philosophy discussion group — mostly other retirees, plus two grad students who keep us honest — and next month we've got a philosopher of mind joining us by video call who has spent her career defending IIT. I got voluntold to write the questions, on account of being "the science one" now. I've heard her interviewed before, and she has a favorite move: whenever someone brings up a case that sounds absurd, she says something like "you're only calling that absurd because you're assuming consciousness has to look like ours, and that's exactly the assumption the theory exists to question" — and that just ends the conversation every time. I dont want to hand her that exit again.

Could you draft me eight to ten real interview questions, with at least one question — and a sharp follow-up keyed to whatever dodge she'll probably reach for — on each of: the overgeneration problem those high-Phi-but-trivial systems raise; the exclusion postulate and how strange it gets once you apply it to split-brain patients, or to real cases like those craniopagus twins who share a thalamic bridge; the fact that Phi is essentially uncomputable for anything the size of an actual brain, and whether that, plus the fact that the one big empirical showdown between IIT and its main rival theory came back ambiguous, makes the "pseudoscience" charge fair; and whether she's willing to just own the panpsychism-adjacent stuff, like the claim floating around that even a photodiode has some tiny flicker of experience, or whether she tries to wriggle out of it. I don't want questions that just let her restate the axioms — I want ones where restating the axioms is visibly not an answer.
