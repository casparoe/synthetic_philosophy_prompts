I'm a machine learning engineer at a small startup that trains reinforcement-learning agents inside game-like simulated environments -- agents that learn to forage, navigate mazes, or compete against each other over tens of millions of episodes, all of it run on the same vocabulary of "reward" and "punishment" signals that moral philosophers use, a coincidence I'd never really thought about until recently. A colleague dropped a link in our team channel to an old essay on suffering-focused ethics and negative utilitarianism -- the family of views holding that reducing suffering matters enormously more than producing happiness, perhaps to the exclusion of everything else -- and I spent the following weekend falling down a genuine rabbit hole. The detail that's stuck with me most is a reductio that suffering-focused philosophers have had to take seriously since at least the 1950s: if only suffering counts morally, then a being able to painlessly and permanently erase all sentient life wouldn't just be permitted to do it -- it would be obligated to, since that's the only guaranteed route to zero future suffering, and nothing less can match it. I keep thinking about that argument sitting next to the very literal button on my screen that ends a training run early.

I don't have any formal philosophy background, so rather than an essay I'd like a short story out of this. Could you write one centered on an ML engineer -- reinvent the character and setting as much as you like -- who becomes genuinely persuaded by the asymmetry between suffering and happiness that motivates suffering-focused ethics, and starts seeing her own job differently as a result? I want that asymmetry to actually drive the plot rather than get name-checked in a paragraph of exposition, and I want the "painless destruction is the safest option" argument to show up as a real, concrete dilemma tied to some small decision she has to make at work, rather than something she neatly reasons her way past. I'd rather the story end with that tension still unresolved than have it deliver a tidy moral in the last line.
