Last night a friend and I had a long, unresolved argument about a decision I'm facing, and I'd like an outside opinion. For three years I've run a small pottery studio that only recently started breaking even; it's still precarious work, but I love the creative direction and the community I've built there. I've now been offered a tenure-track position teaching ceramics at a respected art school in another state — better pay, security, and prestige, but it would mean walking away from all of that. My friend's view is that if I can't say which option would make for the strictly better life, I'm being irrational to agonize over it and should just estimate which one yields more expected happiness and take it. When I pushed back, he said that if neither option beats the other, they must simply be equal, so I might as well flip a coin. Neither response feels right to me, though I couldn't explain why in the moment. Is my friend correct that it has to be one or the other, or is there some third possibility we're overlooking?
