I'm the philosophy faculty member on my college's committee that reviews requests for exceptions to the residential alcohol policy, which otherwise forbids any event at which a faculty member supplies or supervises alcohol for students. A seminar just finishing Plato's *Laws* has asked for an exception for one evening: a wine-drinking symposium, run by their preceptor as the sober president of the evening, explicitly modeled on the *Laws'* argument that a regulated drinking party is a safer analogue to fear-based tests of courage and a genuine training ground in self-command rather than indulgence. Half the committee wants to refuse the request as classical veneer on an ordinary party and dispose of it on liability grounds alone; the other half notes that the policy was written to stop faculty from exploiting drunk students, not to bar a faculty member from acting as designated sober authority, and is counting on me to judge whether Plato's argument actually supports what the students are proposing. Please draft the memo I'll bring to next week's meeting: recommend a specific decision on this year's request, specify whatever conditions would make that decision defensible, say whether it should set precedent for future terms, and give the strongest version of whichever side you reject a fair hearing.
