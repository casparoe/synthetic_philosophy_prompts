Been down another antinatalism rabbit hole lately (theres a whole cluster of old LW/EA forum posts on population ethics that keep sucking me back in), and there's one move in Benatar's asymmetry argument I can't shake. Quick recap for context: he says the presence of pain is bad, the presence of pleasure is good, the absence of pain is good even when there's nobody around to enjoy that good, but the absence of pleasure is only bad if there's someone who actually exists to be deprived of it. That last part always struck me as him wanting it both ways -- impersonal 'good' for the pain side, strictly person-relative 'bad' for the pleasure side, conveniently whichever one keeps the antinatalist conclusion afloat.

Could you write me a dialogue between two genuinely sharp people about this -- a committed Benatarian antinatalist and someone who likes person-affecting views in population ethics generally but thinks this particular asymmetry is inconsistent for exactly that reason -- working through whether the inconsistency charge actually sticks, or whether theres a principled way to keep the asymmetry that isn't just special pleading. Neither of them should be a pushover, i want real back and forth where they actually respond to each other's specific moves instead of just restating their opening positions.
