I've been reading P. F. Strawson's essay "Freedom and Resentment" on my own, and what strikes me most is his move to ground moral responsibility not in some metaphysical proof of libertarian free will, but in the natural human proneness to reactive attitudes such as resentment, gratitude, and indignation -- attitudes he thinks we could not, and should not, simply reason our way out of, even if determinism turned out to be true. Strawson published this in 1962, well after Nietzsche's death, so Nietzsche never had occasion to respond to it directly. Yet Nietzsche devoted much of the first essay of the Genealogy of Morality to treating ressentiment as a corrosive, fundamentally reactive posture born of impotence, something to be diagnosed and overcome rather than enshrined as the basis of ethical life. Given that background, what do you think Nietzsche's verdict on Strawson's argument would have been: would he have welcomed Strawson's naturalism as an ally against metaphysical theories of the will, or would he have regarded the whole framework -- resting as it does on treating resentment as fitting and legitimate -- as exactly the sort of moral psychology he was trying to unmask?
