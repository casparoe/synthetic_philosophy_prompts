Aristotle distinguishes "natural" ways of acquiring wealth, oriented toward meeting genuine household needs, from "unnatural" wealth-getting (chrematistike) pursued as an end in itself. He singles out lending money at interest as the most unnatural and most justly hated form of all, since money is meant to function as a medium of exchange rather than a good that breeds more of itself. Drawing on this framework from the Politics, and on his account of exchange and value in the Nicomachean Ethics, rank the following practices from the one he would judge most natural to the one he would judge most unnatural.

1. A currency trader who profits from short-term fluctuations in exchange rates without holding any currency long-term.
2. A family that grows its own grain and trades the surplus for wool with a neighboring household.
3. A shareholder who earns dividends from a manufacturing company's profits.
4. A high-frequency trading firm that profits from momentary price discrepancies between markets.
5. A grain merchant who buys wheat cheaply at harvest and stores it to sell at a higher price during a later shortage.
6. A bank that earns most of its income by lending out its depositors' money at interest.
7. A carpenter who sells the tables he builds in the market to support his family.
8. A landlord who collects rent on an apartment he owns but does not live in.

Please give your answer as a JSON array listing the numbers 1 through 8, ordered from the practice Aristotle would judge most natural to the one he would judge most unnatural.
