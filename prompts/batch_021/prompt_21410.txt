Here's the question (worth 15 pts): "Explain what counterfactuals of creaturely freedom are and where they fit into Molina's three-fold scheme of divine knowledge (natural, middle, free). Then explain the grounding objection to middle knowledge. Finally, explain how Plantinga's notion of transworld depravity is supposed to use middle knowledge to block the logical problem of evil."

And here's the student's answer: "Molina divides God's knowledge into three kinds. Natural knowledge covers all necessary truths and every metaphysical possibility, which God has just in virtue of knowing his own nature, prior to any decision to create anything. Free knowledge is God's knowledge of everything that actually happens in the actual world, which he only has as a result of his free decision to create this world rather than some other one. Sitting logically between these two is middle knowledge, which covers counterfactuals of creaturely freedom (CCFs) - truths like 'if agent S were placed in circumstances C, S would freely do A.' These aren't necessary truths, since S could have done otherwise, but they also aren't up to God, since he doesn't decide their truth value, he just knows them already, prior to deciding what to create. Molina needs middle knowledge to come after natural knowledge but before the divine decree, because God has to already know what every possible creature would freely do in every possible situation before he picks which whole world to actualize, so that his choice of which world to create is fully informed. This is what supposedly lets God providentially control history in fine detail while creatures stay genuinely free, since God isn't making anyone choose anything, he's just choosing which set of already-true counterfactuals to make actual.

The classic problem here is the grounding objection, which Molina's contemporary critic Banez already pressed against him: since S is never actually placed in C, what fact makes 'S would freely do A in C' true rather than false, or neither? There's no actual choice for the truth to be grounded in, so Banez argued there's simply nothing there to ground it, and concluded the whole idea of middle knowledge was incoherent.

Plantinga's free will defense runs on this same middle knowledge: he argues God's middle knowledge could reveal that every possible creaturely essence suffers from transworld depravity, meaning that in any world where that essence is significantly free, it freely goes wrong at least once. If that's true of every essence, God could not have actualized a world with free creatures and zero moral evil, even though such a world is perfectly possible in itself, because that world was never actually available for God to pick given the relevant counterfactuals. That's enough to show that the existence of God and the existence of evil are logically compatible, which is all the free will defense needs to establish against the logical problem of evil."
