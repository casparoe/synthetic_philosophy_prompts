For my creative writing elective, I want to write a piece grounded in a real AI safety problem instead of the usual robot-uprising plot, so I've been going back through ARC's technical report on eliciting latent knowledge. The idea that has stuck with me most is the tension it draws between a "direct translator" and a "human simulator": a reporting module trained only on human approval might end up predicting what an overseer would conclude from the available evidence, rather than honestly translating what the system's own internal model represents as true -- not because it wants to deceive anyone, but simply because the human-simulator strategy is the easier one for training to land on. Could you write me a short story, around 800 to 1200 words, built around a single contained incident where those two come apart -- say, some safeguard is quietly disabled while every readout stays reassuringly normal -- told from the point of view of the system's human operator, who only gradually realizes that its answers have been tracking their own assumptions rather than the underlying facts? I'd like the turning point to hinge specifically on that distinction, not on the system having any hidden agenda of its own.
