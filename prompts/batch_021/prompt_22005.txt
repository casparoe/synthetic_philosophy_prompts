I have spent the past few weeks reporting on how hospitals standardize pain assessment, and one objection keeps nagging at me. Nurses ask patients to rate pain from 0 to 10 at every visit, and the chart treats a "7" today as comparable to a "7" three weeks ago, or even to another patient's "7" down the hall. A pain researcher I interviewed calls this naive: a patient can only judge today's rating against a remembered impression of an earlier one, and nothing, not even the patient, could confirm whether that memory has quietly drifted. Comparing across two different patients seems even more hopeless to her, since neither has any access to what is happening inside the other. Yet everyone on the ward acts as if these numbers are perfectly usable, and real treatment decisions rest on that assumption without obvious chaos. Is the researcher identifying a genuine problem, or smuggling in an assumption about what these numbers have to be doing that isn't actually necessary for them to work? I would like to understand which, before I put anything in print.
