I'm putting together a collection of short fiction where each story is built around one idea from decision theory, and for the next one I want to tackle superrationality -- Hofstadter's old idea that two sufficiently identical reasoners facing a one-shot prisoners dilemma, with no communication and no history together, should each cooperate anyway, because the symmetry of the situation means whatever's rational for one of them is automatically rational for the other. I don't want the story to fall back on 'they learned to trust each other' -- no repeated rounds, no messages passed, no prior relationship for the character to lean on, so the logic itself has to be doing all the work. Could be two strangers, two copies of the same person, two AI instances spun off the same weights, whatever vehicle earns the idea best. And don't let it resolve too cleanly -- plenty of decision theorists think the whole move is just a rationalization, since your choice can't actually cause a stranger's choice to change, so I'd like the ending to leave open whether the character reasoned their way to something true or just talked themselves into it. Something like 1000-1500 words, and no lecturing the reader about game theory in the middle of it -- it should all live in the scene.
