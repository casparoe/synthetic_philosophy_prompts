My parents are hitting their 40th anniversary next month and, since I'm the one who teaches this stuff for a living, I've been roped into giving the toast. I want to build it around the statue-and-the-clay puzzle from material constitution -- not the ship of theseus please, my uncle already ruined that one two anniversaries ago. The angle I like: the marriage is constituted by the two of them without being identical to either of them, or even to the pair of them lumped together -- its got its own persistence conditions, it survives changes that would count as huge for either person alone, but it can also just end (divorce) while both of them live on totally unchanged, which is the same kind of modal mismatch the original puzzle uses to argue that constitution isn't identity. One of my colleagues works on persistence and personal identity and is coming to this thing, so she'll absolutely clock it if I fumble the metaphysics, but my parents' bowling league friends just want to laugh and tear up a little, not sit through a lecture. Can you draft the toast? About three minutes out loud, and it actually has to be philosophically careful, not just jargon for atmosphere.
