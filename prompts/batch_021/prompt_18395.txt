Here is the question:

Explain Hempel's paradox of the ravens. Your answer should (a) identify the two principles whose combination produces the paradox, (b) show how the paradoxical conclusion follows from them, (c) explain how Hempel himself proposed to resolve the paradox, and (d) note one respect in which philosophers have found that resolution unsatisfying.

Here is my classmate's answer:

"The paradox arises from combining two principles that each seem obviously correct. Nicod's Criterion says that a universal claim like 'all ravens are black' is confirmed by any instance that is both a raven and black, while instances that are not ravens at all are simply irrelevant to it. The Equivalence Condition says that anything which confirms a statement must also confirm every statement logically equivalent to it. Since 'all ravens are black' is logically equivalent to its contrapositive, 'all non-black things are non-ravens,' and a white shoe is a non-black, non-raven object that satisfies Nicod's Criterion for the contrapositive, the Equivalence Condition forces us to say the white shoe also confirms 'all ravens are black.' That conclusion looks absurd, since checking your closet shouldn't tell you anything about birds.

Hempel's own resolution is to argue that the two formulations aren't actually on equal footing when it comes to confirmation: what makes the shoe feel irrelevant is that we're smuggling in background knowledge about how enormously outnumbered ravens are by non-black things, so once that background knowledge is factored in, confirmation should really be judged relative to whichever formulation is actually under investigation, rather than assumed to transfer automatically between logically equivalent statements. This is the same insight behind Hempel's later 'grue' cases, where swapping in gerrymandered color predicates changes which instances count as confirming. Even granting this move, critics have pointed out that it is still strange that almost all of the available confirming instances for 'all ravens are black' turn out to be non-ravens rather than ravens."
