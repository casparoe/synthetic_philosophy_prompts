There are about 200 students in my grade, and we are currently disagreeing over the departure time for our end-of-year trip. The venue only offers our grade a single scheduled entry slot, so we cannot split into groups that arrive separately. Leaving at 5 a.m. would give everyone two extra hours at the destination, but every student would have to wake up unpleasantly early — a real, though fairly minor, annoyance for any one person. Leaving at 7 a.m. would sacrifice those two hours for the entire grade, but it would spare one student with a documented sleep disorder from a severe, multi-day migraine that an early start reliably triggers for them. Several student council members argue that two hundred people's minor grogginess clearly adds up to more total unpleasantness than one person's migraine, while others insist that this is the wrong way to compare the two kinds of harm. Who is right, and why?
