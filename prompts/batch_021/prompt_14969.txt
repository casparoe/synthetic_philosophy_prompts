So I'm about a third of the way into a novel set in a mill town that gets torn apart by a strike, and I keep getting stuck on the same problem every time I sit down to write the chapter where it actually kicks off. Half the time I want to tell it through my main character Nell -- her specific grudges, the fight she had with her foreman, the exact moment she decides shes had enough -- like the strike is really just the sum of a hundred people making choices the way she did. Other times it feels dishonest not to zoom out and talk about the mill's ownership structure, the wage cuts that had been coming for years, and the union organizers whod already been laying groundwork in three other towns -- like the town got swept up in something bigger than any one person's decision. I've drafted the scene both ways and neither one feels like cheating, but they also don't feel like two descriptions of the same event, more like two different claims about what actually caused it. Is one of these actually the truer way to explain why the strike happened, or is there a way of thinking about this where they're not even competing explanations?
