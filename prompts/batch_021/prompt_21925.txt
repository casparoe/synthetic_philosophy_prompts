I cover physics and philosophy of science for a science-and-ideas magazine, and next month I'm recording a long-form interview built around the substantivalism/relationism debate about spacetime -- essentially an update of Newton's bucket for the era of general relativity. My guest, for the purposes of this piece an invented but representative defender of "sophisticated substantivalism," holds that spacetime points are genuine entities, but denies that swapping which point plays which role in the metric and matter fields -- the maneuver behind Earman and Norton's hole argument -- describes a distinct physical possibility; giving up that "haecceitism" about points is supposed to save substantivalism from the indeterminism the hole argument otherwise threatens. I've been rereading the Leibniz-Clarke correspondence and some of the hole-argument literature to prepare, and I keep running into the charge that this "sophistication" just is relationism wearing a substantivalist's badge -- that's the pressure point I want the questions to circle.

Could you draft nine or ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the position for a general audience? I want at least one question that forces her to say what observable or explanatory work "the points are real" is still doing once haecceitism about them is gone; one that uses Gödel's rotating-universe solution -- where the entire matter content of the universe rotates and there is, by construction, nothing external for it to rotate relative to -- to ask what "absolute rotation" could even mean on her view; and one asking her to weigh in on whether frame-dragging (the Lense-Thirring effect) vindicates Mach's original hunch about the bucket or only complicates it. For each question, add a short, pointed follow-up anticipating the deflection she's most likely to reach for, so the exchange doesn't just settle back into the textbook version of her view.
