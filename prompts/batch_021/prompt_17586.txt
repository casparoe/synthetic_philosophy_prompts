I am taking a graduate seminar on the metaphysics of relations, and we have just spent two weeks on the argument in F. H. Bradley's Appearance and Reality that later commentators dubbed 'Bradley's regress.' For my response paper, I want to confront Bradley with a solution proposed by a philosopher born after his death: D. M. Armstrong's claim, within his states-of-affairs ontology, that the regress is blocked because the tie binding a particular to a universal is a fundamental, non-relational form of togetherness that itself needs no further relation to connect it to its terms — a move later carried over into the philosophy of language by theorists who identify true propositions with such states of affairs. Reconstructing his reply from his actual commitments — the doctrine that relations are internal to their terms, his claim that relational thought is self-contradictory at the level of appearance, and his appeal to the Absolute as a unity beyond all relations — what do you think Bradley would say? Would he grant that Armstrong's 'non-relational tie' escapes the regress, or would he diagnose it as simply asserting, under a new name, the very unity his argument shows cannot be built out of separate terms plus a relation? And is Bradley's own resolution, on which the terms and relations of finite judgment are ultimately absorbed into a single all-inclusive experience, any less exposed to that same charge?
