After my graduate seminar on the semantics of conditionals, two of my students, Nora and Dev, kept arguing in the corridor about what David Lewis's triviality results actually establish, and have since asked me to settle it -- something I would rather not do myself. Here is their exchange, as best I can reconstruct it:

(1) Nora said the triviality result shows, full stop, that no indicative conditional can express an ordinary proposition: any semantics that assigns "if A, C" a proposition while honoring Adams's Thesis -- that its probability equals P(C|A) -- collapses into absurdity, so truth-conditional theories of indicatives are simply untenable.

(2) Dev objected that this overreaches. Lewis's proof needs the further premise that the Equation hold not just for one's current credence function, but for every credence function one might move to by conditionalizing on new evidence. Drop that robustness requirement -- as a Stalnaker-style semantics does, by letting the proposition expressed by "if A, C" shift with the speaker's epistemic state -- and one keeps both truth conditions and the Equation for one's actual state, without triviality.

(3) Nora replied that this "fix" concedes nearly everything: if the content of "if A, C" depends on whose credence function is doing the asserting, two people uttering the same sentence in the same circumstances may not be asserting the same thing, making ordinary agreement and disagreement about conditionals unintelligible. Calling that "truth conditions" is a technical victory that cedes to Adams everything of substance.

(4) Dev answered that content varying with an aspect of the speaker is not some embarrassment invented to save conditionals -- plenty of expressions are context-sensitive without thereby lacking truth conditions -- so this is a genuine semantic proposal, not an evasion.

(5) Nora's last word was that even this retreat may not be safe: she had read that later, weaker versions of the triviality argument dispense with full robustness altogether, meaning the trouble can resurface even for a single, fixed credence function -- so Dev's fix may not be reaching the actual source of the problem.

Neither has budged. Could you adjudicate their exchange move by move?
