I serve on my university's Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee. Federal rules on research-animal welfare cover only vertebrates, so a lab's proposal to test a candidate analgesic in crayfish technically needs no review -- but our charter lets us impose equivalent conditions on unregulated species when we judge it "scientifically and ethically warranted," without saying how. The trial design needs an unmedicated arm: crayfish shocked at intensities that would require anesthesia in a comparable vertebrate protocol, as the baseline against which the drug is judged. The investigator points to rapid withdrawal and documented trade-off behavior in crayfish -- forgoing food or shelter to escape a shock-associated site -- as evidence of real pain, not reflex. Some committee members argue, on a higher-order view of consciousness, that absent evidence that crayfish can represent their own nociceptive states as their own, this is unconscious processing, not felt pain, and vertebrate-style limits are misplaced. Others argue, on a first-order view, that the integrated, motivationally weighted trade-off is itself sufficient for a state to be felt, representation or not. Draft the memo I'll bring to committee -- approve as submitted, approve with conditions, or reject and require redesign -- with a concrete review timeline, and give the position you reject a real hearing, not a token one.
