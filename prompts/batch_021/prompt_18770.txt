Ok so I've been rereading Benatar's asymmetry argument and there's this move he makes that I feel like never gets its due outside of academic philosophy papers: he keeps a hard line between the ethics of starting a life and the ethics of stopping one. The asymmetry is supposed to show coming into existence is always a harm, basically never in the created person's interest, but he's adamant that this doesn't mean existing people have any reason to hurry up and die. Can you write me a dialogue between a committed Benatarian defending that start/stop split and a sharp critic who thinks it's a patch job to dodge an ugly implication of his own pessimism about how good lives actually are? I want the critic to really lean on the question of why a merely possible person's non-existence is no loss to them, while an existing person's (on Benatar's own accounting, probably net-negative) life getting cut short IS a loss. Both people should be equally sharp, actually responding to each others points instead of monologuing past each other - think a real philpapers-style back and forth, not the "but overpopulation!" stuff you get on reddit. Maybe set it as two people hashing this out at the bar after an EA conference or smth, whatever framing feels natural to you.
