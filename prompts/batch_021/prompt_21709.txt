"(a) The pamphlet was drawn up under the auspices of the Verein Ernst Mach and presented to Moritz Schlick in October 1929 as a token of thanks for declining a call to Bonn and remaining in Vienna. It carries no named author on its title page, though its preface bears three signatures. Carnap was the principal drafter, working from notes and suggestions supplied by Hahn and Neurath, and it is these three, especially Neurath and Carnap, who are usually identified as the Circle's 'left wing' and credited with the manifesto's programmatic, almost activist tone, in contrast to more moderate members such as Schlick himself.

(b) In his Intellectual Autobiography, Carnap writes that almost everyone in the Circle, himself included, considered themselves socialists, and that he thought the great problems of the age could not be left to spontaneous, unplanned forces but required deliberate rational planning: in economic life this meant some form of socialism, and internationally a gradual move toward world government. This was not just an abstract sympathy. (c) It would be a mistake to read the dedication as Schlick's endorsement, since a dedication is a personal courtesy rather than a co-signature, and since we know that at least two other Circle members, Gödel and Menger, privately objected to the manifesto's collectivist, quasi-programmatic language.
