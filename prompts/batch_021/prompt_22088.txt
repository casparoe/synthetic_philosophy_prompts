I'm working on a novel with a sculptor as a side character, and while I was picking my actual sculptor friend's brain for details, two people who were there, her studio-mate Nora and her partner Jules, got into an arguement that's been stuck in my head since. My friend had just been roughing out a bust in wet clay, still nowhere near fired, and joked that she could always squash the whole thing back into a ball if she ended up hating it. That's what set the other two off.

Nora: if you squashed it, the bust would be gone, but the clay would still be sitting right there as a ball. Since one of them can survive that and the other can't, they can't be the same object -- identical things can't differ in what they're able to survive.

Jules: that's a cheap way to multiply objects. Right now there's exactly one lump of stuff on that table. Saying there are actually two things there, the bust and the clay, perfectly overlapping and made of the exact same atoms, is a way bigger weirdness than the one it's supposed to fix.

Nora: the weirdness is real, but so is the argument. It's true right now, not just someday, that the clay could survive being squashed and the bust couldn't -- that's an actual difference between them as they sit there this second, not some fact about the future. You don't get to reject a conclusion just because it's uncomfortable.

Jules: except "could survive squashing" isn't about how anything is shaped or arranged right now, it's a claim about what would happen if someone did something nobody's actually doing. If hypotheticals like that count as real, present differences, then nothing ever stays itself through any change, since you can always dream up some counterfactual where "it" supposedly fails to do what it's doing right now. That can't be right, so the argument's proving way too much.

They made me referee and I had nothing useful to say. Can you go through this round by round and say who's actually got the better of it at each move -- not just an overall winner -- and flag anything both of them seem to be missing?
