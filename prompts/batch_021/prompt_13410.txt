So I went down a bit of a rabbit hole on the effective altruism forum last week and stumbled on this idea called "evidential cooperation in large worlds" (I think it used to be called multiverse-wide superrationality or something like that?). The rough idea seems to be that if the universe or multiverse is really big, there are tons of agents out there who reason kind of like I do but who I'll never causally interact with, and that by acting cooperatively toward the whole "portfolio" of values represented by all those similar reasoners, instead of just my own values, im somehow providing evidence that those other agents are doing the same thing for me. I tried explaining this to my roommate over dinner and completely butchered it, and honestly I'm still not sure I get why this isn't just wishful thinking dressed up in decision theory jargon.

Assuming the basic argument actually goes through (I know it leans on evidential decision theory plus a bunch of other contested premises, so let's just grant all that for now), what are the most important implications of this idea for how an ordinary person trying to be a good effective altruist should actually behave day to day? Like, does it mean I should be donating to different causes than I currently do, or giving real weight to moral views I don't even hold myself, or is the whole thing basically a fun seminar-room curiosity that cashes out to approximately nothing once you're back in real life? I'd love a breakdown of the two or three implications that actually matter in practice, rather than the ones that just sound cool but don't really change anything.
