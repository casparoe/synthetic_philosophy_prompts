I'm a senior taking an independent study in philosophy, and before I turn my reading on Richard Rorty into full prose for my teacher, I would like feedback on whether the central objection my paper is built around is fair to him, and whether it survives the reply of his that I already have in mind. These are still notes rather than a finished essay.

Background I'm relying on: Rorty denies that beliefs are meant to "mirror" a mind-independent reality (Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature); vocabularies are tools for coping, not devices for capturing the world's intrinsic nature, and, following Davidson's attack on the scheme/content dualism, the world causes us to hold beliefs without ever selecting which vocabulary is the correct one. In Contingency, Irony, and Solidarity he calls himself a "liberal ironist": someone who holds their "final vocabulary" — the words they would use to justify their beliefs and life, for which there is no non-circular defense — as contingent, while still hoping for an ever-widening circle of solidarity and treating cruelty as the worst thing human beings do. In "Solidarity or Objectivity?" he explicitly recommends grounding this hope in solidarity rather than in objectivity.

My objection: if no vocabulary can be shown to track reality better than any other, Rorty seems to have no principled basis for saying that a wide, inclusive circle of solidarity is better than a narrow or cruel one — only that he, and people who already think like him, happen to prefer it. I know his considered answer, in "Human Rights, Rationality, and Sentimentality," is that the circle expands through sentimental education, i.e., stories that make strangers feel like "one of us," rather than through argument. But this looks like it just relocates the problem, since sentimental education only moves people already disposed to be moved by such stories, so the strategy seems to presuppose the very liberal sensibility it is meant to produce.

Is this a fair statement of the dialectic, or am I misreading the objection, Rorty's reply, or both? I would especially value pushback on where a committed Rorty defender would attack the argument first.
