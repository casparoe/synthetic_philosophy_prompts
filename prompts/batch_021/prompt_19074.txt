Two people from my rationalist meetup have been arguing, across several messages in our group chat, about whether a startup one of them works at should adopt a small futarchy-style mechanism for a major strategic decision. The proposal: run two conditional prediction markets, one estimating company value "if we pivot to enterprise sales" and one "if we stay direct-to-consumer," and have the board pre-commit to following whichever conditional price ends up higher.

Talia's opening objection was that this would be theater rather than genuine information aggregation: since everyone already expects the founders to stick with direct-to-consumer regardless, few employees will bother doing real research or trading seriously on the enterprise branch, so its price will mostly reflect apathy rather than belief -- and the board would then cite that low, uninformed price as evidence against pivoting.

Owen replied with the standard defense: if the enterprise price really were uninformatively low, that's a standing arbitrage opportunity, and anyone who does the legwork and believes it's undervalued can profit by buying in, which is exactly the self-correcting mechanism that's supposed to make these markets worth trusting in the first place.

Talia said that story assumes independent, well-capitalized traders show up regardless of what the founders already want -- but in a company with a few dozen employees, whoever bothers to "correct" the price is likely just an enterprise-sales partisan restating an opinion in dollar terms, not a neutral estimator, so the self-correction story doesn't obviously survive the trip down to this scale.

Owen answered that this is a complaint about running futarchy badly, in a setting far smaller than anything Robin Hanson ever proposed it for, not a problem with the underlying logic: a market that thin failing to police itself shows the tool is being misapplied, not that it's broken.

I couldn't tell who was actually ahead at any given point. Could you go through the exchange move by move, say who has the better of it at each step, and flag anything about conditional prediction markets that you think both of them are overlooking?
