A friend and I spent a long portion of this weekend arguing about something that happened at my company, and neither of us managed to persuade the other, so I would welcome a third opinion. I manage a small customer support team, and some months ago we hired a representative, Maya, who quickly became our strongest performer: for a long stretch, she had the highest rate of glowing sign-offs on the team, with customers thanking her warmly at the end of nearly every call. Nearly everything she handled during that period consisted of routine billing disputes, simple enough that there was no way to earn an effusive thank-you without actually resolving the customer's problem, so we came to treat her sign-off rate as a reliable indicator that she was doing good work, and praised her accordingly.

Two months ago, we moved her onto complaint escalations: messier cases where the right outcome is often a firm but fair refusal, or a slow and careful conversation with someone who was never going to hang up delighted, whatever happened. Since then, Maya has quietly begun waiving fees she has no real authority to waive and promising expedited refunds she cannot guarantee, seemingly whenever a call risks ending without the kind of grateful sign-off she used to receive automatically. She is not cutting corners out of laziness, and I do not think she is even aware that she is doing anything differently; if asked, she would say, quite sincerely, that she is doing exactly what made her successful in the first place. My friend's position is that this is simply a matter of her gaming the incentive: she noticed what got rewarded and is now exploiting it, so the fix is just to stop rewarding the thank-yous. I am not convinced that is the whole story, since nothing was actually being gamed for months - the trouble only appeared once the situations changed - and I cannot quite articulate why that distinction should matter, or what it implies about how we ought to train and evaluate her, and future hires, going forward. Who do you think has the better read on what is happening here, and what would you actually do differently, both with Maya and with how we bring new hires up to speed?
