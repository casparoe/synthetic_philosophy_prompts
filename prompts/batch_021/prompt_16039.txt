I sit on the small grants committee of a donor collective in the effective-altruism community that pools individual contributions and re-grants them a few times a year. We're deciding this week whether to fund a biosecurity project that three larger, well-regarded funders have each backed over the past four months, with increasing amounts each time; as is common in that field, none has published its reasoning, citing information-hazard concerns, so we know only that they funded it, not why. Our own evaluation came back mixed. Two committee members want to fund on the strength of those three decisions alone; a third argues we may be the fourth domino in an information cascade rather than a fourth independent confirmation, resting entirely on the first funder's initial read. Draft the committee's funding memo: a recommendation with an amount and any conditions, plus a proposed change to how we sequence future evaluations so we're less exposed to this dynamic. Give the case for deferring to the larger funders' judgment as fair a hearing as the case against it.
