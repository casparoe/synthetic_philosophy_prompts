Random one from my quals reading — been going through Leibniz's whole best-of-all-possible-worlds argument (the compossibility stuff, God surveying all the possible worlds and picking the one w/ max perfection, etc). Got me wondering what Leibniz would actually say about modern ideas where "other possible worlds" turn out to be real in some sense — like Everett's many-worlds interpretation in QM (physically real branching universes) or Lewis's modal realism (every possible world is just as concrete and real as ours, only causally cut off from it). Would he see those as basically vindicating what he meant by non-actualized possible worlds all along, or as a totally different and kind of confused notion? And does "this is the best of all possible worlds" even stay coherent once being "possible" stops meaning merely possible? Genuinly curious what his actual position would be given how his metaphysics works, not the Voltaire-satire version everyone already knows.
