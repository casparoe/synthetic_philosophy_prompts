So my company just wrapped up its quarterly promo calibration cycle and something happened thats been living in my head rent free ever since. Quick context for anyone who's never sat through one of these: to get bumped to senior, our rubric says you need to "clearly meet the bar" on all three of Impact, Technical Depth, and Leadership. I facilitate these sessions -- I compile the scores and run the meeting, but I don't actually get a vote -- so I watched this whole thing play out for a candidate I'll call Jordan. Everyone agreed Jordan cleared Leadership easily, so it really came down to the other two axes, and that's where things got messy.

The three voting reviewers were Priya, Marcus, and Deb. Priya thought Jordan clearly had both Impact and Technical Depth, so she voted promote. Marcus thought Jordan had the Impact but not the Technical Depth, so he voted no. Deb thought Jordan had the Technical Depth but not the Impact, so she also voted no. Straight vote on "promote, yes or no": 2-1 against. But when someone tallied it a different way instead -- majority verdict on each axis separately (2 of 3 said Impact was there, 2 of 3 said Technical Depth was there), then applied our own "must clear both" rule to those two majorities -- it comes out promote. Both are completely standard ways of using the same rubric on the same three ballots! HR went with the second method and Jordan got the promotion, which is a little wild given two of the three actual humans in the room voted no. (Worth noting: Deb still defends how it was tallied even though it's not the outcome she personally voted for, so this isn't just her rationalizing her own preference.)

Marcus and Deb ended up hashing this out over Slack afterward and I've been re-reading the thread for days trying to figure out who's actually right. Roughly, paraphrased:

1. Marcus: "The by-axis method is bogus. It stitches your yes on Technical Depth together with my yes on Impact into a conclusion that only Priya, one person out of three, actually believes start to finish. You can't call that majority-backed when the one person who holds the complete view is outnumbered two to one."

2. Deb: "It's not manufactured though -- a real majority (me and Priya) thinks Technical Depth is there, and a seperate real majority (Marcus and Priya) thinks Impact is there. If you believe majorities mean anything at all, you have to believe both of those, and the rubric just tells you what follows once you do."

3. Marcus: "Then believe the majority on the question we were actually asked, which is 'promote or not' -- that majority is me and Deb, both no. It doesn't matter that we have different reasons for it. Two out of three people in that room do not think Jordan should go up this cycle."

4. Deb: "By that standard the rubric is decoration. Swap 'must clear both Impact and Technical Depth' for any other rule you like and it'll never once change the outcome, because you'll always just fall back on the raw promote/don't-promote tally regardless of what the rule actually says. So why bother scoring the individual axes at all instead of just asking everyone 'thumbs up or down' from the start?"

Neither of them budged.
