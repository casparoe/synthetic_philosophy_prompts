In discussions of quantum foundations that circulate on rationalist blogs and forums, I keep running into the claim that Bell's theorem settled the matter against hidden variables: quantum mechanics, the story goes, is irreducibly probabilistic, and Einstein's hope of recovering a deterministic, realist picture was simply a dead end. What I've never been able to square with this is why pilot-wave (Bohmian) mechanics is still taken seriously by physicists and philosophers of physics as a fully deterministic, hidden-variable rival to orthodox quantum theory, more than half a century after Bell's result. Is its persistence mostly a matter of a few holdouts unwilling to accept settled physics, or is there a real technical sense in which Bohmian mechanics slips past what Bell's theorem actually rules out?
