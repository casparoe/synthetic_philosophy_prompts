Below is a question from the take-home midterm and one student's answer to it, reproduced verbatim.

Question (20 points). State the axioms underlying Harsanyi's Social Aggregation Theorem (1955) and explain why they force the social welfare function into the form of an affine combination of the individual utility functions. Then, in a few sentences, give the intuition for why this specifically additive form emerges, rather than some more general function of the individual utilities.

Student's answer.
Harsanyi's setup takes a fixed population of individuals 1, …, n, each of whom has preferences over lotteries on a common outcome space satisfying the usual von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms (completeness, transitivity, continuity, and independence), so each individual's preferences are represented by a Bernoulli utility function U_i, unique up to a positive affine transformation. Society's preferences over the same set of lotteries are assumed to satisfy the same axioms, giving a social utility function U_0. The key normative axiom is Pareto Indifference: whenever every individual is indifferent between two lotteries p and q, society is indifferent between them as well. Harsanyi shows that this axiom, together with expected-utility representability on both sides, is enough to force U_0 into the form of an affine combination of the individual utilities, U_0 = a_0 + a_1U_1 + a_2U_2 + ... + a_nU_n.

Intuitively, the reason the social utility function has to be additive across individuals, rather than some more general aggregate, is that the construction really asks us to evaluate lotteries the way an impartial spectator behind a veil of ignorance would: someone with an equal 1/n chance of turning out to be any given member of society. Applying the independence axiom to that equiprobability lottery over identities immediately yields a rule that is linear in the individual utilities, with each person entering with the same weight. This is why the theorem is usually read as a decision-theoretic reconstruction of utilitarianism: the social planner is simply an expected-utility maximizer who does not yet know who they are.
