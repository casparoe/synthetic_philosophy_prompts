I have been rereading John von Neumann's 1955 Fortune magazine essay "Can We Survive Technology?" for a seminar on early Cold War thinking about technological risk, and I would like a second opinion on one of its arguments. At one point von Neumann considers, and rejects as a mere "pseudosolution," the proposal that the dangers of runaway technological progress could be managed by banning particular dangerous lines of research while leaving the rest of science and technology untouched. His case against this rests on a few distinct claims: that technology and the underlying sciences are too "intertwined" for anything short of a total halt to count as genuine inhibition; that useful and harmful applications lie so close together that one can never "separate the lions from the lambs"; and that any ban narrow enough to be worth having would in any case require worldwide enforcement. It is this last step I find hardest to parse. He puts it like this: "...the only authority that could do this effectively would have to be of such scope and perfection as to signal the resolution of international problems rather than the discovery of a means to resolve them."

Could you help me with three things? First, restate his grounds for rejecting selective prohibition as explicitly and charitably as you can, and tell me whether you think they are genuinely independent of one another, or whether one of them is really doing most of the work. Second, unpack the quoted sentence: is the difficulty he has in mind a broadly conceptual one — the proposed remedy is circular, since the authority capable of enforcing it would already have to embody the very international settlement it is supposed to help bring about — or merely an empirical claim that such an authority would be extraordinarily hard, though not impossible in principle, to build? Third, tell me whether this argument, if sound, would equally undercut present-day proposals for narrow, targeted bans or pauses on specific "dangerous" lines of research — in AI or synthetic biology, say — or whether some disanalogy between his mid-century case and ours blocks the transfer.
