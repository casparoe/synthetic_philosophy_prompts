Okay, fair warning, this is going to be a pretty inside-baseball question. A while back someone at my local ACX meetup brought up the "Luring Lottery" -- the actual postcard contest Hofstadter ran through Scientific American back in 1983, where your share of the prize got divided by the sum of every number submitted, so a bunch of readers wrote towers of exponents on there postcards just to mess with it, and the payout rounded down to basically nothing. That sent me back to reread the original superrationality columns properly (had to dig up a scan of the real Metamagical Themas chapters, not just the wiki summary), where the actual argument is that two sufficiently similar reasoners facing a symmetric dilemma should both cooperate with zero communication, because each one can deduce that the other -- being relevantly identical -- will land on whatever conclusion they themselves land on.

Then a friend sent me Caspar Oesterheld's old "multiverse-wide cooperation" paper, which as far as I can tell is basically the founding document behind what people on LW and the EA forum mean now when they casually toss around the phrase "acausal trade" -- agents scattered across causally disconnected parts of a big or infinite multiverse (sometimes people stretch it to cover Everett branches, or even a future AI and present-day humans) coordinating their behavior with zero communication, purely through mutual modelling. Oesterheld is pretty upfront that superrationality by itself isn't action-guiding at all, and only becomes relevant once you bolt on the multiverse assumption -- at which point he's got agents with genuinely different values striking something like a deal, each promoting the others' values a bit in proportion to how correlated they are, and he even names the resulting aggregate thing a "compromise utility function," like comparative advantage stretched across the whole multiverse.

What I can't work out is how much actual daylight there is between the two ideas, versus how much is just repackaging. Does "acausal trade" require something superrationality's original twin-prisoner's-dilemma case never needed -- real differences in value and situation between the parties, i.e. actual gains from trade -- or is the multiverse move basically just dressing up an inconsequential 1983 party-game observation as a decision-relevant fact about reality? Could you compare the two properly for me: what does acausal trade add to superrationality beyond "apply it to weirder and more numerous cases," and which of the two actually holds up better as a piece of decision theory once you strip away the cosmological speculation? I keep flip-flopping on this and would rather get an actual argument out of you than more vibes.
