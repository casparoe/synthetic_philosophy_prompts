Ok, kind of a weird one, but it's been eating my brain for about a week so bear with me. A few of us who met through the local rationalist meetup run a tiny forecasting collective on the side -- we give a handful of people small stipends to produce probabilistic forecasts on stuff like elections, drug approvals, GDP nowcasts, that kind of thing, and then publish an aggregate that a couple of newsletters actually cite. Twice a year the five of us on what we grandly call the 'grants panel' decide who keeps getting funded, and this cycle it's turned into a genuine fight -- to the point that I got stuck writing the memo we circulate before Tuesday's call, and I'm blanking on how to make it more than a "well, on the one hand..." shrug.

Here's the dispute. One of our forecasters (call him Dmitri) runs some spreadsheet thing he's tuned for three years and just flatly refuses to explain, and honestly I don't think he could explain it even if he tried -- he says a number feels off and nudges it. His Brier score over that period is 0.09, way better than anyone else we fund. Our other flagship is a fully transparent structural model built by two people with econ PhDs, every input and mechanism documented and checkable by anyone on the panel, and their Brier score is a respectable-but-clearly-worse 0.15. Three of the five panelists want to gut the transparent team's funding and just ride Dmitri, on the grounds that he's the one who's actually right more often.

I keep bringing up that bit in the Meno where Socrates says a guy who's merely gotten the road to Larissa right so far isn't in the same position as a guy who actually knows the road, because the first guy's correct opinion isn't tied down by anything and could come apart on the very next question -- plus something I half-remember about Edward Craig's idea that the entire point of having a concept like 'knowledge' is to flag good informants, and the swamping problem thing (Zagzebski? the expresso machine example?) about how once the output is already true, whatever process produced it shouldn't add further value on top of that. The other two panelists think I'm smuggling in a bias toward legible reasoning for its own sake, and that calibration is calibration. Can you draft the actual memo, the one I'll send Monday night? I need it to land on a real recommendation -- keep the funding split as is, reweight the aggregate, make Dmitri's renewal conditional on at least trying to externalize his method, whatever the case actually supports -- not a both-sides shrug. And before you get to that recommendation, I want you to build the strongest version of 'just fund whoever has the better track record, understanding be damned,' because I don't think anyone on our panel, me included, has actually stated it as well as it could be stated.
