My grandmother is eighty-four and spent almost forty years working as a hospital nurse before she retired, so she is, by temperament and training, an unusually evidence-driven person: she wants to see the test results before she trusts a diagnosis, she's quick to call out a relative who's reasoning from anecdote, and she revises her views on medical and practical matters readily whenever better information turns up. Since my grandfather died three years ago, though, she has said, calmly and consistently, that she knows she will see him again. When I gently laid out the obvious objections — that there's no evidence for an afterlife, that this is exactly the kind of belief people form because they need it to be true, that plenty of couples who loved each other just as much never expected any reunion — she agreed with every point, without a trace of defensiveness, and then said it made no difference to what she knows. When I asked what, in principle, would change her mind, she said nothing would, and didn't seem at all bothered by admitting this, even though she'd be alarmed to catch herself reasoning that way about anything else.

This has turned into an ongoing disagreement between my sister and me. My sister thinks it's a straightforward case of denial, made worse rather than better by how calm Grandma is about it, and that we should keep gently challenging her on it. I'm not convinced that's the right diagnosis: denial usually comes with some flinching away from the subject, and Grandma does the opposite, meeting every objection head-on without disputing any of it. It's almost as if "know" is doing a different job in her mouth here than it does when she's talking about a scan result, even though she'd object if you suggested she meant something weaker or looser by it. Before deciding whether it's even worth raising this with her again, I'd like to understand what's actually going on: is my sister right that this is ordinary wishful thinking that a good enough argument should in principle be able to dislodge, or is Grandma doing something else with the word "believe" here — and if so, does that mean continued argument is simply beside the point, or just that I've been going about it the wrong way?
