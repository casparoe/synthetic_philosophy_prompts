I'm a philosophy PhD student working on metaethics. Two friends of mine, neither of whom has any philosophical background, recently had an argument that -- without either realizing it -- amounts to a real-life version of the wrong kind of reasons problem for fitting-attitude accounts of value. I'd like your take on how the exchange actually went.

Setup: Jake's nonprofit offered a $500 bonus to anyone who wrote a heartfelt tribute letter for the retiring founder -- a man everyone privately considered a mediocre, uninspiring boss. Jake wrote one, took the bonus, and now insists he genuinely admires the founder.

1. Jake: "The bonus gave me a real reason to find things to admire about him, and I did. A reason's a reason -- once I have sufficient reason to feel something, what more is needed for the feeling to be correct?"
2. Meera: "That conflates two things: having reason to get yourself to feel admiration, and admiration actually being the fitting response to him. The bonus explains the first, not the second."
3. Jake: "Then name the difference instead of just asserting it. Take what I actually wrote: that he personally answered customer emails for years. That's a fact about him, not about me -- so if your distinction is 'facts about him' versus 'facts about me,' this one's on your side of the line. But the bonus is still doing all the work, since nobody unpaid would find that fact remotely admiration-worthy."
4. Meera: "So a fact being about him isn't sufficient on its own. Here's the real test: would it give a total stranger with nothing to gain any reason to admire him? Genuine excellence at the job clears that bar; answering emails because a memo said to doesn't. That's a real distinction, and it has nothing to do with incentives."

Neither budged. Could you go through the exchange move by move, saying who has the better of it at each step -- including whether Meera's move 4 actually closes the gap Jake opens in move 3 -- rather than just naming an overall winner? I'd also welcome your sense of what, if anything, both of them are missing.
