So this happened after our free will reading group last month -- we'd just gone through Strawson's "Freedom and Resentment" the week before, and two of the members, Leah and Marcus, ended up litigating a case involving a mutual friend of theirs, Ben, who'd been genuinely nasty to a bunch of people (blowing off commitments, snapping at his partner, one pretty ugly scene at a party) during a stretch last year that turned out to be an undiagnosed manic episode. He's medicated and stable now and by all accounts pretty embarassed about it. Leah's opening position: since he wasn't himself, holding onto resentment toward him for that period is basically a confusion, on par with being angry at someone for something they did sleepwalking -- you don't blame people for things that just happen to them rather than things they do. Marcus pushed back hard: if you exempt Ben wholesale like that, he said, you've stopped relating to him as someone you have a relationship with and started managing him the way you'd manage a weather system, and Strawson's whole point about the "objective attitude" is that adopting it toward someone, even out of kindness, has a real cost -- it's a way of ceasing to see them as a participant. Leah shot back: fine, but is Marcus saying she should resent Ben exactly as much as if he'd done all this with a clear head, which seems nuts given how obviously compromised his agency was. Marcus said no, definately not -- diminished capacity should mitigate, the way "he was under great strain" or "he didn't realize how it would look" mitigate all the time without turning into full exemption -- the real question is just whether Ben's case belongs in that "great strain" bucket or the "doesn't really count as an agent here, like a young child" bucket, and you can't settle that just by reaching for the diagnostic label. Leah's comeback: that just relocates the question without answering it -- why isn't "acutely manic and off medication" exactly the kind of case where, like with a small child, the ordinary business of reasons and reasoning has broken down, so that Marcus is just stipulating the line wherever it happens to suit him?
