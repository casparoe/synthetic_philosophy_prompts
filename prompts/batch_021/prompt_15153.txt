I help moderate a mid-sized rationalist-adjacent Discord that grew out of a bunch of ACX meetup people migrating somewhere with actual search after the SSC subreddit got too chaotic. We inherited, mostly by unspoken tradition rather than any policy we ever actually wrote, LessWrong's old soft taboo on discussing Roko's basilisk in detail -- the "don't think too hard about the acausal blackmail thing, Eliezer says it's an infohazard" norm from back in 2010, imported wholesale and never revisited. That changed last week when a member who's clearly done real reading in decision theory posted a long, careful writeup arguing the taboo is embarrassing at this point. His core claim: the basilisk was never a coherent threat against an agent running anything like updateless or functional decision theory, because such an agent's policy is fixed prior to being simulated or threatened, so precommitting to ignore acausal blackmail costs it nothing -- and a rational blackmailer gains nothing by torturing an agent that was never going to pay out anyway, so a future AI smart enough to actually pull this off is also smart enough to know the punishment changes nothing at that point and is pure waste. He wants it stickied as the canonical answer to the "wait, what's Roko's basilisk?" question we get from every third newcomer.

Mod chat immediately split three ways. One camp -- closer to my own instinct, honestly -- thinks he's basically right, and that quietly keeping up the old taboo just makes us look like we're guarding a cult secret. A second camp says that's missing the point: the original ban was never really about whether the argument holds up, it was that actual people reported genuinely bad nights and intrusive thoughts over this back in the day, and a long, rigorous, stickied "debunking" thread still plants the same vivid image in front of exactly the anxious newcomers who'll skim past the reassuring decision-theory part and remember the torture part. A third mod, who mostly stayed out of the object-level fight, kept pointing out that if we're a community that supposedly prizes reasoning transparency, we can't keep invoking "infohazard" as an unfalsifiable trump card without ever saying what would count as evidence against it -- and that's worth fixing regardless of what we decide about this particular post.

We meet Thursday, and since I'm the one who started the thread that caused all this, I got voluteered to write the memo. Could you draft it for me? I need an actual recommendation -- post it as-is, reject it outright, or some defensible middle option (heavily edited, comments locked, restricted to a subforum, whatever you think holds up) -- with a rough timeline for implementing it, and it has to genuinely engage the "vulnerable reader" objection instead of just gesturing at it on the way to endorsing my own camp. It'd also help if you weighed in on whether the updateless-agent argument for why the basilisk fails actually holds up, since I've caught myself repeating it in mod chat without being fully sure I could defend it if someone leaned on it.
