I'm TAing an intro philosophy of physics course this term, and we just spent two weeks on the hole argument, and I still don't feel like I can hold both sides of it in my head well enough to explain it properly to my students on Thursday. I've got the basic setup down: GR's field equations are generally covariant, so given a solution (M, g) you can pick a "hole" H where theres no matter, build a diffeomorphism that's the identity everywhere outside H but smoothly non-trivial inside it, and drag g along by that map to get a new solution g'. Outside the hole g and g' agree completely; inside it they can disagree totally, even though both satisfy the same equations given the exact same data. Earman and Norton ran with this back in the 80s (I think the paper's literally subtitled "The Hole Story") to argue that manifold substantivalism — the view that spacetime points have identities independent of whatever fields happen to sit on them — commits you to a totally unphysical kind of indeterminism, since nothing in the theory decides between g, g', and infinitely many diffeomorphic cousins as "what really happens" inside the hole. What I can't do justice to is the substantivalist comeback: the sophisticated-substantivalist move of keeping the points real but dropping haecceitism about them, or Maudlin's line that points have their metrical properties essentially, so g and g' were never two rival descriptions of the same points to begin with.

Could you write me a dialogue between a relationist and a substantivalist working through this from scratch, like they're standing at a blackboard building the argument together before turning on each other over what it actually shows? I want genuine back-and-forth — each of them responding to the specific thing the other just said, not reciting a position — so the relationist should press the indeterminism worry as far as it goes instead of accepting a hand-wave, and the substantivalist needs one real sophisticated reply (pick whichever move, sophisticated substantivalism, metrical essentialism, or something else, you think actually holds up) rather than just folding halfway through. Neither one should be a pushover for the other; by the end I want it to genuinely be unclear which side a sharp reader should walk away agreeing with.
