quick one — I'm procrastinating on my prospectus by trying to work out a new angle on the wrong kind of reasons problem, and I want a gut check before I bring it to my advisor.

background in case it's useful: buck-passing accounts say X is good iff X has properties that give reasons to favor X (desire it, admire it, promote it, whatever). The classic problem case: an evil demon threatens to torture you unless you admire a saucer of mud. Now you've got a reason to admire the mud, but that obviously doesnt make the mud admirable. So buck-passers need a principled way to seperate "right kind" reasons (ones that actually bear on whether admiring the mud is fitting) from "wrong kind" ones (like the demon's threat) - and it can't just be "right kind = the ones that make it fitting," since that's circular.

most of the fixes I've read (the content-relatedness stuff from Danielsson and Olson iirc, Way's transmission account, the object-given/state-given split) try to locate the line in the reason's relation to the object. my idea: locate it in the agent instead. wrong-kind reasons are the ones that survive full transparency - you can go on admiring the mud while fully, consciously aware that fear of torture is the ONLY thing doing the work, no dissonance involved. right-kind reasons, by contrast, get shaky or dissolve once you're fully aware they don't track the object's actual properties. so: right vs wrong tracks stability under transparency, not some purely object-relational feature.

two things I want your honest take on:
1. am I just smuggling "fittingness" back in through "shaky under transparency" - does this actually dodge the circularity worry or not?
2. can you think of a case where a reason is obviously wrong-kind but transparency-resistant, or obviously right-kind but unstable under transparency?

not fishing for a full lit review, just want to know if the core move has legs before I sink a whole chapter into it.
