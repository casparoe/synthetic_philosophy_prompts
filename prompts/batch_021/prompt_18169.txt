I've been reading about imprecise probabilities for my school's philosophy club. On this view, instead of assigning a single number to how confident you are in some claim, you assign a whole interval — say, [0.3, 0.7] — whenever the evidence doesn't settle on one sharp value. But I'm not convinced this adds anything to ordinary subjective probability: isn't an interval like that just a roundabout way of saying your credence is 0.5, since that's the natural best guess inside the range, and won't any sensible decision rule just average over the interval and collapse it back into a single number anyway? If a rational agent's imprecise credence always cashes out as one precise number whenever it actually matters for choice, what philosophical work is the imprecision itself doing?
