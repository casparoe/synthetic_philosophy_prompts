So after 38 years running an analytical chemistry lab, I retired and immediately fell down a philosophy rabbit hole (my wife says it's worse than when I got obsessed with birdwatching). Lately it's causation that's got its hooks in me. I was reading around the SEP entry on causation and got pulled into Phil Dowe's and Wesley Salmon's "conserved quantity" theory of causation — the idea that a genuine cause has to be hooked up to its effect by an actual physical process, something that transmits energy or momentum or some other conserved quantity from the one to the other. I love this picture, its basically how I thought about cause and effect my whole career at the bench. But it seems to flatly rule out omissions ever being causes: if nothing happens, there's no process, nothing gets transmitted, so nothing gets caused. And that clashes badly with how we actually talk and act — we say a lifeguard's not noticing a swimmer caused the drowning, or a mechanic's failure to check the brakes caused the crash, and we treat that as more than a loose way of speaking; people get sued and go to prison over exactly this kind of thing.

I brought this up at a little discussion group I go to now (mostly other retired scientists, we used to just argue about real science instead) and it turned into a proper argument that nobody won. What I'd like from you is the strongest possible case that omissions are never genuine, literal causes — at most they're difference-makers we single out for practical or moral reasons — and that a physical-process theory is right to exclude them from the causal relation proper. Two things I really want you to actually deal with, not dodge: first, the obvious "many omissions" comeback — if my not watering your fern while you're away counts as causing its death, why doesn't the King of England's not watering it count too, or the sky's not raining fern food, since neither of those involves a process either; and second, I gather Dowe himself patched this by inventing a separate "quasi-causal" relation just for omissions and preventions, which smells to me like admitting defeat and taping a spare category on the side. I'd rather you either bite the bullet without that patch, or actually convince me the patch is principled and not just an ad hoc save.
