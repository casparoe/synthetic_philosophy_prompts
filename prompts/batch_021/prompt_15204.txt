I've spent the last few weeks of my graduate seminar on formal epistemology working through Bas van Fraassen's 1984 paper on the Reflection Principle -- the requirement that one's current credence in a proposition, conditional on the supposition that one's credence in it will be r at some specified later time, must itself equal r, on pain of a diachronic Dutch book. Purely by coincidence, our department's colloquium series is hosting a speaker next month whose entire research program is built around defending an unrestricted reading of the Principle: she refuses the standard hedge that would confine Reflection to cases in which the agent has no antecedent reason to doubt her own future rationality, and she has a habit of dissolving the classic worry cases -- you know you're about to be drugged, hypnotized, or otherwise cognitively degraded, and it seems absurd that your credence now should already track whatever credence you'll have then -- by insisting that they equivocate on what it means to "know" one's future credence in the relevant sense.

I'm chairing the Q&A after her talk and would like eight to ten questions that don't just give her room to restate that dissolution. I need at least one question that presses on whether the diachronic Dutch book argument for Reflection actually establishes irrationality or merely exploitability, and is therefore vulnerable to the kind of "depragmatizing" worry that's been raised against Dutch book arguments generally; one that puts the drugged-or-hypnotized case to her in its sharpest form and anticipates the equivocation move before she can make it; one that asks her to place her view relative to the Sleeping Beauty literature, since memory loss seems like a structurally different threat to Reflection than drugging is, and I want to know whether she thinks that's right; and one that asks her to spell out the analogy between Reflection and Lewis's Principal Principle -- treating one's future self as an epistemic expert -- and say whether that analogy actually explains anything or just relabels the puzzle. For each of these, please add a short follow-up tailored to the answer she's most likely to give, and close the set with a question about what she would count as a genuine counterexample to the Principle, as opposed to merely an unwelcome intuition.
