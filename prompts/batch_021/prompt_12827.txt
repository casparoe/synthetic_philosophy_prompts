Since Newtonian mechanics can be derived from Einstein's special relativity simply by taking the low-velocity limit (v ≪ c) — with relativistic mass, momentum, and energy all reducing smoothly to their Newtonian counterparts — the two theories evidently agree on the concepts of mass, space, and time throughout the range in which classical mechanics is ordinarily applied, differing only once velocities become extreme. Given this mathematical continuity, why does Kuhn treat the shift from Newtonian to Einsteinian dynamics as a paradigm case of incommensurability, rather than as the sort of cumulative refinement in which a later theory simply extends and corrects the quantitative range of an earlier one?
