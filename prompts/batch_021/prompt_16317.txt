So this is a weird one. I sit on the small conduct/grievance panel for a research nonprofit in the EA/rationalist orbit -- maybe fifteen people total, way too small for an actual HR department, so a rotating group of us handles internal complaints ourselves. We've got a case that's basically pure he-said-she-said: a research assistant told us, in confidence, that one of our more senior and well-liked fellows cornered her during a break at last spring's off-site retreat (one of those multi-day, slightly-too-earnest ones) and pressured her in a way that left her shaken. No texts, no other witnesses, nothing but her account, and she only came forward months later. When we put a sanitized version to him, he denied it flatly and pointed out, accurately, that the two of them had clashed hard over a grant decision the week before, so she'd have a motive to make something up.

Its turned into a weirdly philosophical fight. Priya (did a philosophy MA before switching to ops) keeps invoking the reductionism/anti-reductionism debate about testimony: Hume's idea that testimony only ever transmits as much warrant as you already have, independently, for thinking this kind of speaker telling this kind of story is reliable, versus the Reid-and-Coady line that testimony is a basic source of justification in its own right, on a par with perception, so we're entitled to just believe people absent some specific defeater. About half the panel has landed on something like the reductionist position -- "one sincere but uncorroborated account isn't yet the kind of evidence you can sanction someone on" -- and half thinks that exact standard, applied to precisely the situations where corroboration is structurally impossible, guarantees we never act on real cases, and is the kind of credibility discount Miranda Fricker was writing about, not a neutral evidentiary rule.

I want it to actually recommend something concrete we do within the next two weeks, not "gather more information" as a way of not deciding, and I want it to steelman the loosing side rather than strawmanning it -- including giving a real answer to whether Priya's reductionist/anti-reductionist framing is even the right lens for what's actually at stake here. Write it like an actual memo, not an essay.
