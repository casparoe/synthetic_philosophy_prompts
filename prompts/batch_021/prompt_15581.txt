Next week's guest on my podcast on the history of analytic philosophy is a scholar who works on Frege and defends a provocative thesis about the diary he kept in 1924, the year before his death: that its antisemitism, its contempt for parliamentary compromise, and its fear of "foreign" corruption of German life are not biographical noise but continuous with his logic. On her reading, the same refusal to relativize truth to any thinker's psychology that grounds his doctrine of thoughts (Gedanken) inhabiting an objective "third realm" — his own term, coined some five years before Moeller van den Bruck put a very different "third realm" into nationalist circulation — is what made him receptive to a politics that blamed pluralism itself for Germany's decline. Draft nine interview questions that do not let her simply restate this thesis. Include at least one question that forces her to specify the actual inferential link between anti-psychologism in logic and antisemitic conspiracy thinking in politics, rather than mere coincidence within one biography; one that asks why Husserl, who reached a comparably robust anti-psychologism, ended up a target of that politics rather than a herald of it; and one that puts to her Michael Dummett's own uneasy acknowledgment of the diary once it came to light, asking whether his handling of it now looks honest or evasive. Close with a question asking what evidence would actually count against her continuity thesis rather than simply illustrate it further, and for each question append a brief follow-up keyed to the deflection she is most likely to reach for.
