I sit on the small discretionary research committee for my university's psych department (it's leftover alumni money, something like $15k a year, meant for pilot studies too speculative for NIH/NSF to touch). We've got a proposal from one of the PhD students that's splitting the committee right down the middle and I need to draft the recommendation memo before Friday's meeting. Short version: she wants to test something like the "upsuck" hypothesis for female orgasm -- that variation in whether/when it happens relative to a partner's ejaculation evolved to bias sperm retention toward higher-quality mates -- by correlating self-reported orgasm timing with partner ratings and, for a subset of willing couples, at-home measures of ejaculate retention. One committee member (did her MA thesis partly on Gould) is against funding it as written: her point is that the proposal never engages with the live alternative that orgasm capacity is just a developmental byproduct of the shared embryonic origin of clitoris and penis, the same style of story Gould and Lewontin tell about spandrels and male nipples, and that nothing in the design could come out looking bad for the adaptive story, so its a just-so story wearing a p-value. Another member thinks that's an unreasonable bar: no hypothesis about what got selected for back in the Pleistocene can be tested the way you'd test something in a lab tomorrow, and if we demand evo-psych proposals rule out "mere byproduct" while nobody asks that of a proposal on, say, the evolution of birdsong, we're just quietly defunding the topics that make people uncomfortable.

Could you write the actual memo I'd circulate -- recommending fund, reject, or fund-with-required-changes, with reasons specific enough that the student could actually act on them if we go with the third option. I dont want boilerplate like "more research is needed"; I want someone to actually engage with whether this kind of study could ever be designed so it's not just begging the question in favor of adaptation, and I want the second committee member's complaint taken seriously rather than quietly waved off just because the first one has the classic paper on her side.
