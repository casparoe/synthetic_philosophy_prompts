Me and my friend Theo got into a whole thing last night (like two hours, way too many beers) about this "commitment races" idea from AI safety stuff he'd been reading on some rationalist blog. The basic gist, if I'm getting it right, is that if two agents can each make binding, irreversible commitments, and each can roughly predict what the other is likely to commit to, then there's a huge incentive to lock in your move before you've actually thought it through - because whoever commits first in a "logical time" sense (meaning however much reasoning they've done, not the actual clock) basically gets to set the terms the other side has to react to, kind of like a country finishing its doomsday machine before the enemy even finishes doing the math on whether building one is wise. Theo's point, which I'm still not sure I buy, is that this flips the usual assumption that more intelligence or more deliberation is always better - a careful, still-thinking agent can straight up lose to a hastier one that locks in an aggressive stance first, and if that ever happened between two superintelligent AIs it could get genuinely catastrophic, not just theoretically interesting.

Anyway, instead of arguing about it more, I wanna see the idea actually dramatized, kind of the way Asimov used fiction to work through robot ethics stuff. Could you write me a short story (a page or two, doesnt need to be long) where this exact dynamic drives the plot? Something like two AI advisors, one loyal to each side of a brewing conflict (border dispute on some half-settled colony world, resource grab, whatever works for you), each having to decide in real time whether to keep reasoning toward a smarter strategy or just commit RIGHT NOW to lock in an advantage before the other one does. I want the actual tension to hinge on that tradeoff - careful-but-slow vs hasty-but-first - and it'd be great if the ending didn't make it obvious which approach was "right," since that's basically the thing Theo and I never resolved either.
