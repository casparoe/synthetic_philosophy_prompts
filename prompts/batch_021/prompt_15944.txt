Ok so for our department's small AI-and-ethics podcast (it's me and two other grad students, maybe 40 regular listeners, very glamorous) I'm interviewing a guest next week who's built a chunk of her career defending coherent extrapolated volition as the right target for an aligned superintelligence -- the Yudkowsky-style idea that instead of coding in humanity's actual present-day preferences, you have the system extrapolate what we'd want "if we knew more, thought faster, were more the people we wished we were, had grown up farther together" (or however exactly the original phrasing goes), with the extrapolation supposed to converge across humanity rather than diverge. When I described the interview to my advisor, the immediate reaction was "that's just Railton and Brandt's idealized-preference theory of the good wearing a trenchcoat," and now I can't unsee it -- CEV seems to inherit a bunch of unresolved problems from that older full-information-account literature (Sobel's worry about idealization losing its authority over the actual person, Rosati's stuff about alienation) that basically nobody in the alignment world engages with directly, they just reinvent the wheel with new vocabulary.

I want the interview to actually make her sit with that lineage instead of letting her re-explain what CEV is for the tenth time (I've read the original writeup, and honestly so has most of our audience). Can you draft me 8-10 questions? I need at least one that presses the authority question head on -- why my extrapolated self's preferences get to bind actual-me just because that self is smarter/more coherent/more informed, given the standard worry that idealizing a person can talk them out of caring about whatever made the exercise worth doing in the first place. I need one on the "hidden complexity of wishes" issue -- that tiny changes to how you spec out the extrapolation (how much further "growing up," what info gets fed in and in what order) can swing the output wildly, so "coherent" might be doing more work in the name than the actual procedure earns. And I want at least one on aggregation: whose volition, and what happens when idealized-me and idealized-you just don't converge, since nothing about the extrapolation step actually guarantees the eventual agreement across people that the original pitch seems to assume. Shes got a habit in panel discussions of answering objections like these with some version of "that's exactly what the extrapolation process is there to handle" -- so for each question, give me a sharp follow-up ready for that dodge, and end the list with one question that asks her point blank what would have to be true for her to admit CEV-style idealization is just the wrong approach.
