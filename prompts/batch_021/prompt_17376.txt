A colleague of mine in the philosophy department -- an epistemologist who has spent her career defending Williamson-style margin-for-error, anti-luminosity solutions to the surprise-exam paradox against Roy Sorensen's rival 'blindspot' analysis of the same puzzle -- is retiring next month. In a joke only our department would find funny, the organizing committee has told her only that her retirement party will happen on some unspecified day within the next two weeks. I have been asked to give the toast, and I'd like a five-minute speech built around one good joke that turns on the paradox's own logic -- one that rewards the epistemologists in the room, who will see exactly what has been done to her, without losing her family and the administrative staff, who will not. Please write it so the joke carries the philosophical weight itself, rather than a potted explanation of the paradox, and so the whole thing lands as affection rather than as a lecture.
