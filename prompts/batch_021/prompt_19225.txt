I've got a metaphysician coming on my little philosophy-of-science podcast next month who has built her whole career defending the Humean line on laws of nature -- the Lewis-style "best system" picture, where a law just is whatever comes out as an axiom of the true deductive systematization of all the particular facts that best trades off simplicity against strength (plus "fit," once objective chance gets thrown into the mix). Last time I had a Humean on the show I let her basically just restate Lewis for half an hour and got mocked for it in the department group chat, so this time around I want questions with actual teeth. Could you draft me eight to ten interview questions, each with a follow-up keyed to whatever answer she's most likely to give, that push on the real soft spots in the view instead of inviting a recap?

At minimum I want one question on the worry that if laws are just a redescription of the mosaic of particular fact, it's unclear how they can explain or "govern" those very facts rather than merely summarizing them after the fact; one on the "undermining futures" headache for her account of objective chance and how that's supposed to sit with the Principal Principle; one that presses on whether her appeal to "natural" properties -- needed to fix which systematization even counts as simplest -- is quietly smuggling in some non-Humean ingredient (the quidditism worry, basically); and one built around the objection that quantum entanglement shows the world isn't seperable into a mosaic of local matters of particular fact to begin with, which, if right, undercuts Humean Supervenience before you even get to the best-system story about laws. Throw in a couple more if you spot a good angle I've missed, but please don't let her wriggle out of any of these with an answer that's really just a paraphrase of the postscript to "A Subjectivist's Guide to Objective Chance."
