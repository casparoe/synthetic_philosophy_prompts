Question (12 points): Samuelson developed revealed preference theory partly in order to eliminate the need for a psychological or introspective notion of utility in demand theory. (a) Explain what it means to say that, on this approach, "utility" is defined by an agent's choices rather than something those choices merely give us evidence of. (b) Name and explain one axiom that a person's choices must satisfy for them to be representable by a consistent preference ordering at all, and briefly explain why economists thought this consistency requirement gave the theory empirical content rather than making it circular.

Classmate's answer:
"Before Samuelson, economists like Jevons and Marshall treated utility as a real psychological quantity - something like a measure of pleasure that existed in a person's head independently of what they actually did. Samuelson's innovation was to reverse this: instead of using utility to explain choice, he used choice to define utility. On this view, saying a consumer prefers bundle A to bundle B just means that, when both were available, she chose A; there is nothing left over for utility to 'be' beyond the ranking implicit in what she actually picks. This is why the resulting utility function is called ordinal rather than cardinal - it only has to preserve the order of her choices, and any transformation that preserves that order works equally well, which is exactly the lesson von Neumann and Morgenstern drew when they proved that rational choice can only ever pin down a ranking, never a magnitude.

The axiom doing this work is the Weak Axiom of Revealed Preference: if she chooses A over B when both are affordable, she should never choose B over A in some other situation where A is affordable too. It's tempting to think this is exactly what rescues the theory from a charge that is sometimes made against it - that because 'preference' is just defined as 'whatever is chosen,' revealed preference theory is circular and no pattern of choices could ever count as evidence against it. But the Weak Axiom doesn't really escape this: since it is stated purely in terms of what gets chosen, any consistent chooser satisfies it automatically just by never contradicting her own past choices, so in the end no possible sequence of choices could reveal a person's preferences to be irrational, and the charge of circularity stands."
