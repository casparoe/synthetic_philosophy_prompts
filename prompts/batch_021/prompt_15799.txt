I've been working on a magazine piece about how physicists talk about time, and an interview I did last week has been rattling around in my head ever since (not helped by the fact I'm finishing this pitch at 1am). The physicist I spoke to said there isn't really an objective "now" shared across the whole universe — because of relativity, two observers moving differently relative to each other can disagree about which distant events count as happening "at the same time," so there's no universal present moment, physically speaking. From there she went further and said that ALL future events already exist, in exactly the same sense that past events do, just further along a four-dimensional structure we haven't reached yet — and that the feeling we have of the present being special, with the future still open and undecided, is basically a byproduct of how brains process information, not a fact about the universe itself.

That conversation crashed into something my friend Deepa told me the week before. She'd just sat her bar exam and was a wreck waiting on results, until she landed on a coping trick: "the grade's already sitting in a database somewhere, its already true whether I passed or not, I just dont have the information yet, so there's nothing left to be anxious about." She said it actually calmed her down.

Now I can't stop turning over whether my physicist source and Deepa are basically making the same move at wildly different scales, or whether Deepa's trick only works because one specific exam has, in fact, already been graded by a specific person — which seems like a different kind of claim than "the future in general already exists out there." And if it is different, does the bigger idea, that the flow of time and the specialness of "now" are illusions, actually follow the way she made it sound, or is that a leap past what the physics itself gives you? I'd like a real answer rather than just a sense of who sounded more confident, because I genuinely can't tell anymore if I've stumbled onto solid physics-backed metaphysics or just a coping mechanism wearing a lab coat.
