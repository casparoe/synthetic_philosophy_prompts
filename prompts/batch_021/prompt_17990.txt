Write a dialogue between two metaphysicians debating whether disputes about mereological composition are substantive or merely verbal. One character defends Eli Hirsch's thesis of "quantifier variance": the view that there is no single privileged meaning of "there exists," so that disagreements over whether certain fusions—such as an object made of the front half of a trout and the back half of a turkey—exist are merely verbal, turning on which of several equally legitimate languages the disputants happen to be speaking. The other defends Ted Sider's "ontological realism," on which some candidate meaning of the existential quantifier objectively carves reality at its joints, making the dispute over composition substantive rather than merely verbal. Have the two work through the trout-turkey case in real detail, each engaging the other's strongest arguments rather than talking past them, and close the dialogue with the pair confronting whether their own disagreement might itself be merely verbal.
