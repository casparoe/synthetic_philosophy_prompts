I've been turning over a puzzle about prayer that I can't quite resolve on my own. On the Molinist account of providence, prior to any decision about what to create, God already has middle knowledge: He knows exactly what every possible free creature would do in every possible circumstance. His decree to create then simply selects one feasible world, out of all those consistent with those counterfactuals, to be made actual. If that's right, it seems that whatever happens when I pray, including whether the prayer "works," is already settled by which counterfactuals of freedom happen to be true, quite apart from anything God does in response to my praying. Please describe the most important implications of middle knowledge, understood this way, for whether petitionary prayer can really be doing any explanatory work within providence, rather than being a purely subjective exercise that changes nothing about how events actually unfold.
