Introductory treatments of Simpson's paradox teach a simple rule of thumb: when an association between two variables reverses upon stratifying by a third, you should trust the within-group result, since the pooled association is presumed to be confounded. Explain why this rule of thumb does not hold in general. Using the distinction between a confounding variable and a mediating variable in a causal graph, construct a concrete scenario in which stratifying is actually the mistake and the pooled, unstratified data give the causally correct answer. Then state, in intuitive terms rather than in the formal language of the back-door criterion, the graphical feature that determines which of the two situations you are actually in.
