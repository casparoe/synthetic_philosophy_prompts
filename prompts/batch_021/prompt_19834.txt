For my African philosophy seminar we've been reading about ubuntu -- not just the "umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu" (a person is a person through other persons) slogan everyone quotes, but Menkiti's much weirder version where personhood is actually graded and achieved over a lifetime through your relationships and duties to a community, so like a baby has less personhood than a fully incorporated elder, and its apparently possible to lose personhood status through serious ethical failure. This is so different from how basically every AI/robot story handles the "is it a person" question, which always treats personhood like a switch that flips on once something is conscious/smart enough on the inside. Could you write me a short story that takes Menkiti's version seriously instead -- where a character's personhood is gradual and relational, depending on being recognized and incorporated by others rather than on some internal property they either have or lack? Doesn't need to involve AI at all if theres a better way in, I just don't want another "robot discovers its feelings" story.
