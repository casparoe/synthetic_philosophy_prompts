I mentioned all this to my niece, who's finishing a philosophy PhD, and she just laughed and admitted the field basically recycles the same five examples forever.

So here's what I'm hoping you can do for me: come up with a genuinely fresh example, ideally drawn from biology rather than furniture and artifacts, that really brings out what's conceptually at stake in the special composition question. Things like a siphonophore (a Portuguese man o' war is actually a colony of specialized zooids, not one single animal, which always struck me as a metaphysical can of worms), a slime mold in its aggregation phase, a lichen, or an ant colony all seem like promising raw material, since in each case it's genuinely unclear whether your looking at one organism or a bunch of organisms fused into a "super-individual." Feel free to go with something else entirely if you've got a better idea. Whatever example you land on, please walk through how at least three of the standard answers to the SCQ would actually rule on it, and pin down exactly where and why they part ways -- that's the part I can never find spelled out clearly anywhere.
