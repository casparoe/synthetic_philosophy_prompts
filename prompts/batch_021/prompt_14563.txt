In my philosophy elective, we've been studying A. J. Ayer's logical positivism, including his claim that statements about surviving one's own death are not merely false but strictly meaningless, since no possible observation could confirm or disconfirm them. While researching him further, I learned that late in his life Ayer suffered a brief cardiac arrest, was resuscitated, and afterward wrote an essay called "What I Saw When I Was Dead" describing a painful red light and a sense that space and time had gone wrong and that he was somehow responsible for fixing them. Apparently the experience was intense enough that it shook his lifelong atheism and left him convinced some form of afterlife must be real after all. Doesn't this mean Ayer effectively disproved his own verification principle, since the philosopher most committed to it ended up being persuaded by direct personal evidence that survival of death is both meaningful and true?
