I am working on a piece about the ethics of behavioral "nudges" and would like to see a genuine disagreement worked through in detail rather than just asserted. Please write a dialogue between two philosophers who study the ethics of influence and who both accept that some ways of influencing people's choices are manipulative while others count as legitimate persuasion, but who disagree sharply about what actually marks the difference. One is an agency theorist, who holds that influence is manipulative exactly when it works by bypassing or subverting a person's capacity to reflect and choose for good reasons, whatever the influencer's intentions. The other is a transparency theorist, who holds that influence is manipulative exactly when it would lose its effect if the target were told in advance, precisely and honestly, how and why it works. Have the two apply their competing tests to automatic enrollment in a workplace retirement plan with an easy opt-out, bearing in mind that such defaults are known to keep shaping people's behavior even after the mechanism has been fully explained to them. Each should press the other with whichever case is hardest for their own view, rather than simply restating their opening position.
