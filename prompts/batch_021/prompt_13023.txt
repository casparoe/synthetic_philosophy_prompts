A close friend of mine, a researcher on wild-animal suffering and s-risk at a small EA-aligned institute, died suddenly of a stroke last month at forty-one. She held a strict, suffering-focused, negative-utilitarian view -- that averting severe suffering has moral priority over producing happiness -- and for years she patiently corrected journalists, myself included, whenever we slid that into "so life generally isn't worth living" or "so a painless end to any suffering being is automatically good." I'm giving her eulogy next week, to a room split between colleagues who will hear any sloppy paraphrase of her ethics as a small betrayal, and relatives who've never heard the phrase "suffering-focused" and just want to hear that her life was full and good. Please write it: it needs to stay true to the narrow, actual shape of her view and resist both of those misreadings -- especially the harder, personal version the second one now takes, that her own death is somehow a relief -- while still comforting people who won't parse a single technical distinction.
