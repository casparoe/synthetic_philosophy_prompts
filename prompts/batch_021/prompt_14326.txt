A few of us now run a small monthly meetup where we bring in a speaker for a live Q&A, and next month's guest is a philosopher who's written a whole book defending a pretty strong epistocratic proposal: you have to pass a civics/policy competence test to get a full vote, and if you don't pass you keep some small reduced vote rather than nothing at all. I've watched a couple of her older talks and she's clearly got smooth, rehearsed answers ready for the obvious objections, so I don't want to just lob her the same softballs everyone else does.

Could you draft me like 8-10 interview questions that go beyond her just re-explaining the pitch? Specifically I want: one question that presses on how every real-world competence-test scheme (literacy tests, property requirements, etc) has historically ended up screening out poor people and racial minorities even when that wasn't the stated goal, one on the jump from "some people know more about policy" to "so they should get to rule the rest of us" (I think there's a name for this objection, something like epistocratic hubris?), and one on how a test is even supposed to seperate "knows the relevant facts" from "has good values" when in practice people's actual political opinions seem to tangle the two together.
