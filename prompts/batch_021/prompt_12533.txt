I've spent the past few weeks reporting a longer feature on experimental tests of Bell's theorem, partly prompted by the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics awarded to Alain Aspect, John Clauser, and Anton Zeilinger for their work confirming violations of Bell's inequality. I'm comfortable with the headline version of the result: no theory built on local hidden variables can reproduce the correlations quantum mechanics predicts, and every experiment keeps coming out on quantum mechanics' side. What's tripping me up is a more technical wrinkle I ran into once I moved past the popular accounts and into the foundations-of-physics literature, and I'd like to think it through with you before I try writing about it for a general audience.

As I understand it (correct me if I'm garbling the terminology), the single "locality" condition used to derive the Bell/CHSH inequality actually factors into two logically independent conditions: what I've seen called "parameter independence," the requirement that the probability of your outcome not depend on the setting your distant partner chose, and "outcome independence," the requirement that it not depend on the actual outcome your partner got, once both settings and any shared hidden state are fixed. Violating the conjunction of the two is what the experiments force on us, but from what I can tell, quantum mechanics only makes us give up the second condition -- the first survives intact, and that survival is apparently the real substance behind the no-signalling theorem and the claim that none of this lets anyone send a message faster than light.

Could you walk me through why it's that particular condition, rather than the other one, that gives way? Is there something in the structure of quantum probability that makes this the only coherent way to assign the "blame," or could one just as easily imagine a theory that preserves outcome independence at the expense of parameter independence instead? And once the violation has been pinned down that precisely, what should I make of it philosophically: does confining it to outcome independence actually rescue locality and relativistic causal structure in some substantive sense, or does it just relabel a genuinely strange feature of the world carefully enough to respect the letter of "no superluminal influence" while dodging its spirit? I'd rather get this right than fall back on the "spooky action at a distance" shorthand that most coverage -- including some of my own earlier drafts -- has leaned on.
