At work we do these blameless postmortems after outages where the whole goal is nailing down "the root cause," and I've noticed it almost always gets pinned on something that *didn't* happen -- on-call didn't get paged, the reviewer didnt catch the bug, the migration script didn't have a rollback, whatever. I went down a rabbit hole reading about how philosophers deal with "absences" as causes, and apparently theres a real puzzle here: if you use the obvious counterfactual test (the outage wouldn't have happened if X had occurred), you get a stupid number of unrelated non-events qualifying too -- like technically if my cat could read code she'd have caught the bug, so did her not-reading-code also cause the incident? Can you walk through the most important implications of this omission-causation puzzle for how we assign "root causes" in incident reviews -- basically, is there some principled way to say the engineer's omission is THE cause and the cat's isn't, or is "root cause" always secretly just blame/convention dressed up as objective causal language?
