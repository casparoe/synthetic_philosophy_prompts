Harsanyi's aggregation theorem shows that if both individuals and society satisfy the von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms, and the social ordering respects the Pareto principle, then the social welfare function must be an affine combination of individual utility functions — in effect, a proof of utilitarianism from minimal premises. Rawls's original position asks rational parties behind a veil of ignorance to choose principles of justice under uncertainty about which position in society they will occupy, which looks like exactly the choice situation those premises are meant to describe, and Harsanyi himself pressed this parallel, arguing that reasoning correctly from behind the veil yields utilitarianism rather than the maximin criterion. Given this, why do political philosophers still treat maximin as a serious rival to utilitarianism rather than as a position Rawls simply derived incorrectly?
