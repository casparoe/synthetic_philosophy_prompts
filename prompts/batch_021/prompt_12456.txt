So this has been nagging at me for about a week, and after years of reading rationalist blogs I want an actual gut check, because normally I'm the first one to go "shut up and multiply" when someone's ethical intuition is fighting the math, and this time the two are pointing in different directions and I can't tell if that means my gut is wrong or the math is missing something.

A friend of mine works at a mid-size startup, and her team kept getting hit with production outages because someone (maybe a couple people) kept skipping code review before pushing changes live. Nobody would own up to it, and actually investigating every single incident to nail down who was responsible would've taken the manager weeks, with a decent chance of never figuring it out anyway. So the VP announced in a team-wide meeting that everyone's quarterly bonus was getting cut in half "until this stops," and was pretty explicit that the point was to get the two-thirds of the team who'd never skipped a review in there life to lean on whoever was doing it, either to make them stop or get them turned in. It worked - within a month the outages basically disappeared, and the VP is now something of a legend internally for "fixing" the team.

When my friend and I actually ran the numbers on this - cost of half the bonuses vs. the cost of the outages, plus the ongoing deterrence value - it's honestly not close, the VP's move comes out way ahead. Normally that's exactly the kind of case where I'd just accept the math and move on, the same way I'm totally fine with "we laid off the bottom 10% to protect runway" or "we discontinued a product people relied on because it wasn't worth maintaining anymore," even though blameless people get hurt in both of those too. But this one sits different to me and the feeling won't go away no matter how many times I look at the spreadsheet - and it's not just that innocent people get hurt, because that's just as true of the layoffs. Is there something actually wrong with what the VP did that survives the cost-benefit math, or am I just being inconsistent about when I let the numbers overrule my gut? And if there is something wrong with it, I'd love help actually naming it, because "it feels icky" is not going to beat my friend's spreadsheet on its own.
