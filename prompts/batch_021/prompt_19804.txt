Question: Explain the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem as an objection to buck-passing accounts of value, using the demon case. Then explain how the object-given/state-given reasons distinction is meant to solve the problem, and say whether the fix actually succeeds.

Answer: Buck-passing views, developed by Scanlon, hold that being good is not itself a reason-giving property. Instead, an object is good just in case it has other (natural) properties that give us reasons to have pro-attitudes toward it -- to desire it, admire it, promote it, and so on. 'Good' just reports that such reasons exist; it doesn't add anything further that needs explaining.

The wrong-kind-of-reasons (WKR) problem, illustrated by Rabinowicz and Rønnow-Rasmussen's demon case, is that this analysis looks too permissive. Suppose a demon announces he'll torture a village unless you admire him. You now have an excellent reason to admire the demon, but the demon hasn't become admirable -- he's exactly as vicious as before. This is basically a version of the Euthyphro dilemma for fitting-attitude theories: either the demon is admirable because we have reason to admire him (which gets the order of explanation backwards and lets a threat manufacture value), or we have reason to admire him because he's independently admirable (which is false, so the reason must be tracking something other than admirableness). Either way, buck-passing needs some principled way to throw out reasons like the demon's threat as the wrong kind, while keeping the reasons that really do constitute value.

The standard fix restricts buck-passing to object-given reasons: reasons grounded in the object's own features, as against state-given reasons, which are grounded in the value of being in the attitude-state itself (here, the value of not being tortured). Since the demon's threat only generates a state-given reason to admire, restricting buck-passing to object-given reasons means the demon case is no longer a counterexample. This settles the WKR problem: wrong-kind reasons turn out to be state-given and right-kind reasons turn out to be object-given, so sorting reasons into those two boxes just is sorting them into right and wrong kinds.
