Since the start of this term I've been serving as the teaching assistant for a graduate seminar in formal epistemology, and this week's unit covers the problem of logical omniscience in epistemic logic: the standard Hintikka-style possible-worlds treatment of knowledge and belief has the awkward consequence that any agent who knows anything at all automatically knows every valid formula of the underlying logic, together with every logical consequence of what she knows, however remote or difficult to derive. Grading the problem sets turned this from an abstract curiosity into something I now have to defend in office hours, since several students have concluded that the framework is simply too unrealistic to take seriously as a model of actual believers.

One student pushed the discussion somewhere I wasn't prepared for. She argued that the whole difficulty is an artifact of modeling doxastic states as all-or-nothing, and that it dissolves once you move to a Bayesian setting: if rational belief just is a credence function rather than a binary knows/doesn't-know relation, an agent is never required to be certain of any non-obvious logical or mathematical claim, and can perfectly well assign, say, credence 0.4 to some conjecture whose proof she hasn't worked out, updating later if a proof turns up. On her view, probabilism succeeds exactly where possible-worlds epistemic logic fails, since it was designed from the outset to represent partial, graded ignorance rather than perfect deductive competence. I found this more persuasive than I expected, partly because my own dissertation treats idealized Bayesian conditionalization as the norm of rational belief revision, and I would rather not discover that I have been quietly relying on the very idealization I was assigned to critique. Is she right that trading knowledge for graded credence lets Bayesians off the hook for logical omniscience, or does something equivalent to the original problem resurface once you spell out what coherence actually demands of a probability function?
