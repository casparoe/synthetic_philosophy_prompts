During a career spent mostly on numerical relativity — gravitational-wave source modeling, in the main — I treated the general covariance of Einstein's field equations as a computational nuisance to be gauge-fixed away, not as an object of metaphysical interest. Having spent a recent weekend working through the original paper and several of these replies with a small reading group, I find I can follow the technical moves in each proposed escape from what Earman and Norton call the "indeterminism dilemma," yet I have no settled view about which of them, if any, actually earns its keep rather than simply relabelling the problem.

I would like your ranked judgment of the following substantivalist-preserving responses to the hole argument, ordered from most to least successful at blocking the indeterminism conclusion without simply abandoning the claim that spacetime points are more than bookkeeping devices for the metric field: (a) unrepentant manifold substantivalism, which accepts the indeterminism as a brute cost rather than a reductio; (b) anti-haecceitist "sophisticated" substantivalism, in the family running from Butterfield and Brighouse through Hoefer, which denies that spacetime points carry trans-world identity; (c) Maudlin's metric essentialism, on which each point has its metrical properties essentially; (d) Melia's strategy of rejecting both Earman-Norton's and Butterfield's conceptions of determinism in favor of a different one meant to block the argument on independently plausible grounds; and (e) Dasgupta's more skeptical diagnosis in "The Bare Necessities," which presses his general "no individuals" metaphysics against spacetime points to argue that even the anti-haecceitist move remains too obscure to count as a genuine rescue.
