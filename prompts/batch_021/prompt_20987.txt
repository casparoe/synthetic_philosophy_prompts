I'm a physician on my national shooting federation's therapeutic-use-exemption (TUE) panel -- sports really do have these, I didn't know until I got roped into it. Current case: one of our best young pistol shooters has documented panic disorder and a mild hand tremor, diagnosed years before she ever picked up a gun, and her doctor wants to start her on propranolol. Problem is propranolol's banned in this sport precisely because it slows the heart and steadies the hand, which is more or less the entire skill being tested. The rule we're suppose to apply says a TUE is fine as long as the drug doesn't give more benefit than simply returning the athlete to normal healthy functioning -- but here "normal healthy functioning" and "the thing the sport measures" are the same variable, so the rule can't actually seperate therapy from enhancement in her case. Meanwhile, I happen to know half the string section of every major orchestra pops the same pill before auditions and nobody blinks. Some panel members want to grant it quietly and worry about precedent later; others think it's a backdoor to legalized doping for anyone with a sympathetic diagnosis. I have to circulate our written ruling before her qualifier in 8 weeks -- can you draft it (grant, deny, or grant-with-conditions), with reasoning stated plainly enough that a rejected athlete's lawyer could actually engage with it, and an honest account of the strongest argument against whichever way you land?
