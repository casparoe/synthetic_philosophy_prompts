The question was: "Reconstruct Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against ultimate moral responsibility in your own words (3-5 steps), then explain whether the argument's force depends on the truth of determinism." Its worth 10 points total, 4 for the reconstruction and 6 for the determinism part, since thats what we spent two lectures on.

Here's the answer, copied exactly:

"Strawson's Basic Argument says that we act the way we do because of the way we are -- our character, values, and desires. To be truly, ultimately responsible for an action, we'd have to be truly responsible for being the way we are. But we can't be responsible for that: we didn't choose our genes or upbringing, and any earlier choice that shaped our character was itself made by a self we didn't choose either, so tracing responsibility back just generates an infinite regress. Strawson (Freedom and Resentment, 1962) puts this by saying nothing can be causa sui, the ultimate 'cause of itself,' so no one can be the self-made author of their own character. Since the regress works by each stage of the self being fixed by an earlier stage, this is really a worry about determinism specifically -- if some choices were genuinely undetermined rather than fixed by prior states, they could break the chain and count as a fresh, self-authored starting point, which is presumably why the argument is usually filed under 'hard determinism' rather than under libertarian theories of free will."
