There's an impossibility-type result in inverse reinforcement learning (I think it's Armstrong & Mindermann's "Occam's razor is insufficient to infer the preferences of irrational agents") showing you can't recover a unique reward function from an agent's behavior even with a simplicity prior over reward functions -- because for pretty much any (reward, rationality-model) pair that fits the behavior, theres an equally simple alternative pair that fits just as well but says the agent wants something totally different. Like, an agent that's rational and wants R behaves exactly like an agent that's anti-rational (always picks the worst available action) and wants -R. Can you argue that this is basically a modern, formal version of Quine's indeterminacy of translation, just applied to desires instead of meanings, so the real lesson is there's no fact of the matter about what someone actually wants beyond whatever rationality model you've assumed?
