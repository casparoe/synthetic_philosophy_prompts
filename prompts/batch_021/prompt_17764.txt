For a seminar on conventions and social norms, I've been revisiting David Lewis's Convention (1969), which treats social norms not as agreements or edicts but as self-perpetuating solutions to recurring coordination problems: a regularity counts as a convention just when everyone conforms to it, everyone expects everyone else to, and some different regularity would have served everyone equally well had precedent settled on it instead. What first tips a population toward one solution rather than another is often mere salience—some feature that makes an option "stick out"—after which precedent and common knowledge alone keep it in place. I'd like a short story, roughly 800–1200 words, that puts real pressure on this picture rather than simply illustrating it: have two populations—human communities, alien species, or AI agents, whichever suits the story—each with its own long-entrenched but incompatible convention for the same recurring problem, thrown into sustained contact, and show what actually happens to salience, precedent, and expectation as their solutions collide. Keep the theory implicit in the plot rather than naming Lewis or using terms like "coordination equilibrium"; afterward, I should be able to point to specific scenes and say which feature of his account each one tests.
