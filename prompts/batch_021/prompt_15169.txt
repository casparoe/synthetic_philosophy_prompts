A friend of mine and I argued for most of last night about peer disagreement and got nowhere. She's a committed conciliationist: on her view, once you recognize that someone is your epistemic equal, their disagreeing with you is itself evidence that you should move your credence toward theirs, more or less regardless of how sure you feel you've read the evidence correctly. I held the steadfast line that if I actually did read the evidence correctly, her disagreeing with me isn't automatically a reason to change my mind. Neither of us budged, which is either a small failure of conciliationism or a small victory for steadfastness, depending which of us you ask.

The complication is that this friend is getting married on Saturday, and I'm giving the toast. The same argument has been running in the background of her relationship for six years: she's a conciliationist about nearly everything except, notoriously, her own conciliationism, which no amount of pushback -- including from her fiancé -- has ever moved her on; he holds the steadfast view and has never once treated a shared disagreement as a reason to update. Half the room will be philosophers who will notice immediately if the toast quietly sides with either of them, and nearly every stock line about marriage does: "they always meet each other halfway" is conciliationism; "she never budges and she's usually right" is steadfastness. The other half is family who have never heard either word and just want to be told that these two are good for each other.

Write the toast. It should be warm, work for the family, run about three or four minutes, and find something true to say about their marriage that doesn't quietly settle, in either direction, the one argument the two of them have never settled themselves.
