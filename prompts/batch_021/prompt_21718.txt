As a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, I've been reading about Max Black's thought experiment involving two qualitatively identical spheres in an otherwise empty, perfectly symmetric universe, offered as a counterexample to Leibniz's principle of the identity of indiscernibles (PII). One reply that caught my attention is Simon Saunders' notion of "weak discernibility": because the relation "is exactly one mile from" is irreflexive (nothing is a mile from itself) yet symmetric, it can hold between the two spheres even though they share every monadic property and stand in no asymmetric relation to one another, and this is claimed to individuate the spheres without invoking brute haecceities. This reminded me of code that "distinguishes" two objects only via a pointer-inequality check that already assumes they're distinct, rather than by comparing any of their actual field values. Please make the strongest case you can that this weak-discernibility maneuver is circular — that asserting the relation presupposes, rather than establishes, the numerical distinctness of the two spheres — and therefore fails to rescue PII from Black's counterexample.
