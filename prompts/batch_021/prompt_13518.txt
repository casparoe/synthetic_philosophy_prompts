I have no formal training in philosophy or logic, but I have spent a good deal of time on my own working through the old Chinese "White Horse Dialogue" attributed to Gongsun Long, in which he defends the claim that a white horse is not a horse. The argument I keep coming back to is the one about color: he points out that the word "horse" excludes no color, so that a yellow horse or a black horse each count as answering to it, whereas the word "white horse" excludes yellow and black, so that only a white horse answers to it; since what answers to one differs from what answers to the other, he concludes that white horse is not horse. This has reminded me of something I once read about how logicians separate the "is" of identity, as in "the morning star is the evening star," from the "is" of predication, as in "Socrates is a man." I would like you to compare Gongsun Long's color-exclusion argument with this identity/predication distinction. Does drawing that distinction expose his argument as simply sliding between two senses of "is not," or does it instead give his paradoxical-sounding conclusion a genuine, non-fallacious reading? Please defend whichever verdict you reach.
