Been reporting a piece on how new parents' senses change, and one thing keeps nagging at me. A few different moms I interviewed independantly told me that within days of giving birth, before they'd even had much time to bond with the baby, they could already pick their own newborn's cry out of a room full of other crying babies in the hospital nursery, instantly, without even trying to listen for it, it just jumped out at them. When I ran this by a psychologist she waved it away as "oh that's just attention, nothing deep going on there," which feels like a dodge to me, though I can't quite say why. Is there an actual difference between your hearing itself changing, so that cry becomes basically a different category of sound to you, versus your attention just getting really efficient at grabbing onto it while the raw hearing stays exactly the same? And if there's a genuine difference there, should it make me any less trusting of what my own ears tell me in moments when I'm anxious or on edge about something? Curious who's got the better of the argument here, the psychologist or my nagging doubt about her answer.
