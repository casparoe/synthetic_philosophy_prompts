In my formal epistemology seminar this week, we covered Aumann's agreement theorem: if two Bayesian agents share a common prior and have common knowledge of each other's posterior probabilities for some event, their posteriors must be identical. It seems to follow that whenever two rational people disagree about something, all they need to do is state their current credences to one another once, since at that point each party knows the other's posterior and the theorem's conclusion should apply. My study partner and I tested this on a disagreement over the answer to a problem set question, told each other our credences, and still ended up disagreeing, even though neither of us doubts the other's honesty or competence. Where exactly does this reasoning go wrong?
