I've spent years reading rationalist blogs and forums, and these days I co-host a small podcast on philosophy of language for much the same audience. In those circles, terms like "implicature," "the cooperative principle," and "flouting a maxim" get invoked constantly in arguments about honesty and misleading-without-lying, almost always at the level of a two-paragraph gloss rather than real engagement with Grice's actual apparatus. Next week's guest is a philosopher who has spent her career defending something close to Grice's original picture: the Cooperative Principle plus all four maxims (Quantity, Quality, Relation, Manner), more or less intact, against both the neo-Gricean move to compress everything into two or three default heuristics and the relevance-theoretic move to discard the maxims altogether in favor of a single principle of relevance. I'd like eight to ten interview questions that don't let her simply restate the picture from "Logic and Conversation," but instead push on the places where the view has taken the most fire -- with a follow-up for each question keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.

At minimum, I want one question on the reflexive-intention analysis of speaker meaning from "Meaning," together with the Strawson/Schiffer worry that ruling out covert manipulation forces an infinite regress of "recognizing a recognition," and a follow-up pressing on whether patching this with mutual knowledge actually dissolves the regress or just relocates it inside an infinite conjunction of iterated knowledge claims; one on whether keeping all four maxims as distinct primitives is defensible once Horn's and Levinson's neo-Gricean heuristics predict most of the same data with far fewer principles, with a follow-up asking her to name a single case where the four-maxim story gets a prediction right that a leaner story gets wrong; one on the relevance-theoretic charge that Manner was never really a maxim of content to begin with, with a follow-up asking her to supply a canonical Manner-generated implicature and then pressing on whether it can be redescribed as an ordinary Quantity or Relation effect; and one on the experimental finding that computing a scalar implicature like "some, so not all" takes measurably longer than accepting the literal reading, with a follow-up on what work "calculability" is still doing as a criterion for implicature if it was never meant to track real-time psychological processing in the first place.

Close with a question about what kind of finding -- linguistic, developmental, or experimental -- would actually count as evidence against the whole intention-based program, rather than just another puzzle for it to absorb. For the remaining one or two slots, feel free to draw on other standard pressure points, such as whether conventional implicature is a principled category or a dumping ground, or whether the line between "what is said" and "what is implicated" survives once free enrichment and relevance-theoretic "explicature" are on the table.
