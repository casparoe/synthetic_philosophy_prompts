I've read Eliezer's quantum physics sequence on LessWrong probably three times over the years, and the bit that always stuck with me is the arguement that Bell's theorem doesn't actually prove nonlocality -- it only rules out local theories where a measurement has one definite outcome, and since many-worlds drops the single-outcome assumption instead of locality, it just walks straight through the theorem untouched. Very satisfying, cake and eat it too. But I've since run into physicists, Tim Maudlin especially, who think this is kind of a bait-and-switch: that MWI isn't "local" in the sense that actually matters once you ask what the theory has to posit to get the correlations right, something about the wavefunction not being a thing in ordinary space, or the branches needing to line up across space in a way that smells suspiciously frame-dependent. Can you actually walk through where the real disagreement is? Like, are Eliezer and Maudlin just using "local" to mean two different things, in which case fine, or is there some actual physical commitment MWI has to make beyond "no collapse" that quietly smuggles back in something Bell-theorem-adjacent, just not the signalling kind. And please don't give me the "well it depends what you mean by local" non-answer -- I want to know what's actually being disputed and who's got the stronger case.
