On my own time I have been working through Carnap's Logical Syntax of Language alongside the 1929 Vienna Circle manifesto, The Scientific Conception of the World, and one thing about its politics has never sat right with me. Carnap's principle of tolerance holds that choosing a logical framework, a form of language, is purely a matter of practical convenience, to be judged by its fruitfulness rather than its truth, since no such choice can be shown objectively correct. Yet the same circle, Neurath most explicitly, and by his own later account Carnap too, hoped that this scientific philosophy would help usher in a rationally planned society organized around one method rather than left to custom, precisely because planning was supposed to be the objectively better way to arrange economic life. Please argue that these two commitments cannot both be sustained: that anyone who takes tolerance seriously has thereby surrendered any principled ground for preferring planning over its alternatives. In making the case, address directly the obvious reply that tolerance was only ever meant to govern the choice between formal languages, not between substantive political or economic arrangements.
