For my history of economic thought seminar, I have spent the past couple of weeks on Hume's economic essays, and I think there is a tension between two of them that we have mostly talked past in class discussion. "Of Money" concedes that a growing stock of specie is not neutral in the short run: because new money reaches merchants and manufacturers before it reaches everyone else, it genuinely quickens industry and employment during the interval before wages and prices catch up. Yet "Of the Balance of Trade" is Hume's classic refutation of mercantilism, and it rests on the claim that no nation can hold on to an artificially inflated money supply, since the price-specie-flow mechanism will always drain the surplus back out. Please argue that a mercantilist could take Hume's own short-run mechanism from "Of Money" and use it to defend precisely the policy that "Of the Balance of Trade" is meant to demolish, rather than trying to show how Hume's position can be rescued from the tension.
