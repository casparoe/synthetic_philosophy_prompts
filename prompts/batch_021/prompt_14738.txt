That's just always been Nick, and normally I don't think about it much.

I kept pushing the classic point — if suffering is what actually matters morally, and having a kid just creates a new person who can suffer with no equivalent tragedy on the other side for the people who never get brought into existence and therefore never miss out on happiness, how do you square that with wanting to make more people? He had some responses, something about not being a strict lexical NU and tranquilism and having cooperative reasons not to be a jerk about your own philosophy, but honestly niether of us really won and we just gave up around 1am. It's been bugging me since.

Could you write me a five minute best man speech that's actually honest to what Nick thinks — so no generic "wishing you two a lifetime of uninterrupted happiness" type lines, since that's exactly the kind of sentence that would make him wince — but that still works as a genuinely warm, funny toast and doesn't turn into a philosophy seminar? If you can fit in one joke that the philosophy crowd will get without leaving everyone else confused, even better, but landing as affectionate rather than as a lecture matters more than the joke does.
