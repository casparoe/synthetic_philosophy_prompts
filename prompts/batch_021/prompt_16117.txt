I'm a few years into a philosophy PhD focusing on formal epistemology, and I can't shake this knot in epistemic game theory: Aumann's result that common knowledge of rationality forces the backward induction outcome in games like the centipede game. But then there's the "paradox of backward induction" side of the literature (Pettit and Sugden-style arguments, plus Reny's results that common knowledge of rationality can be outright impossible at certain nodes in some games), which suggests the whole epistemic story doesn't survive an off-path node actually being reached. Can you write me an essay that really grapples with both sides here -- what Aumann's proof actually establishes vs what the paradox literature shows it can't -- and lands on an actual verdict? Don't hedge into a "reasonable people disagree" ending; commit to something specific, even a qualified verdict is fine, e.g. "the theorem holds up formally but the paradox shows the rationality concept it needs isn't the one we actually care about" would count, if thats where the arguments actually point.
