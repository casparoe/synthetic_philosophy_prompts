I'm a philosophy PhD student working on the philosophy of emotion, and I serve as the graduate student member of my university's IRB. A psychology protocol before us puts participants through a fully immersive VR simulation of a home invasion, complete with a threatening intruder, to study how acute fear affects moral decision-making; participants are told beforehand, explicitly, that none of it is real. Most of the board wants to approve it as minimal risk, reasoning that since participants know it's fiction, the psychological burden can't exceed that of watching a scary movie. Two members object that this gets the paradox of fiction backwards: our capacity for real, physiologically consequential fear toward things we know to be unreal is exactly what the paradox describes, so "they know it's fiction" isn't doing the protective work the majority assumes; they want the study reclassified as more than minimal risk, with added safeguards. Draft the short memo I need to circulate before next week's vote: it should recommend a concrete classification — minimal risk, more than minimal risk with specific safeguards, or a request for revisions — and give the losing side's argument real weight, not a token nod.
