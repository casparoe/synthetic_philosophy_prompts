At work, two colleagues of mine, Elena and Raj, got into a running lunchtime debate about a third colleague, Marcus, and I still can't tell who's right.

Marcus buys the $2 drip coffee over the $5 latte every day of the week except Friday, when the office café also offers a discounted 'latte plus pastry' combo, which he takes instead. (1) Elena said this shows Marcus's preferences, as revealed by his own choices, are simply inconsistent: Monday through Thursday he reveals that he prefers drip coffee to a latte, so by what's sometimes called the Weak Axiom of Revealed Preference, he shouldn't turn around and choose the latte over drip coffee — yet on Friday he does exactly that. (2) Raj replied that this isn't an inconsistency at all, because the Friday alternative isn't 'latte,' it's 'latte-plus-pastry,' a different option from what's on the menu the rest of the week, and revealed preference only constrains you when the actual alternatives on offer are the same. (3) Elena answered that this move proves too much: if you can always redescribe the alternatives whenever a choice looks inconsistent, the theory can never be falsified by anyone's behavior, so it stops saying anything real about what Marcus prefers and becomes true no matter what he does. (4) Raj said there's a principled limit on this: redescription is only legitimate when it's independently motivated — say, by an actual change on the menu — rather than invented after the fact purely to save the theory, and here it's the menu that changed, not just his story about it. (5) Elena said 'independently motivated' is doing all the work there and isn't itself something you can read off Marcus's choices, so Raj has quietly smuggled back in exactly the kind of appeal to Marcus's private reasons that talk of 'revealed preference' was supposed to let us avoid needing.

I don't have any background in philosophy or economics, so I genuinely can't tell whether one of them is missing something well known here. Could you go through the exchange move by move and say who has the better of it at each step, rather than just picking an overall winner? I'd also like to know whether, after all that, there's anything left to the claim that Marcus 'really prefers' drip coffee to a latte, beyond a compact summary of what he happens to choose.
