So I got into a whole argument with a friend at a rationalist meetup last weekend about block universes and whether the future is "already there." He was running the classic move: if eternalism is true (past, present, and future all equally real, no metaphysically privileged "now," the four-dimensional block just IS, full stop) then your choices are in some sense already sitting "in" the block the same way 1962 already is, so agonizing over a decision is basically like agonizing over what you already did yesterday - a waste of subjective effort, since its all just there regardless of how the deliberating feels from the inside. I pushed back that this is smuggling in determinism through the back door, and that eternalism and determinism are actually orthogonal axes - you could have a block universe that bakes in genuinely irreducible quantum randomness, so the future-facing part of the block isn't fixed by the past part even though it's just as real as the past part. Neither of us could fully cash out why the "your choices are already fixed" intuition feels so forceful once you accept eternalism, even in a universe with real, objective chance.

I've read the standard SEP-tier stuff on McTaggart and the A-series/B-series distinction, and a while back I went through some of Eliezer's old "Timeless Physics" posts on Overcoming Bias/LW, but I still don't have a crisp answer for my friend and it's been bugging me since. Could you lay out what you think are the most important implications of presentism vs. eternalism specifically (as opposed to the separate determinism/indeterminism axis) for whether "the future is open" in whatever sense actually matters for free will and for it being rational to deliberate at all? Mainly I want the most charitable, steelmanned version of my friend's worry, if there is one, once you strip out the determinism confusion - or whether eternalism on its own is actually pretty toothless here and the "it's already written" feeling is really just imported wholesale from determinism.
