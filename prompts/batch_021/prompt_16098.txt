According to the simple conditional analysis of dispositions, an object counts as fragile just in case it would break if struck, as soluble just in case it would dissolve if immersed, and so on for any disposition and its characteristic manifestation. Two classic complications for this analysis are finks and masks. A finkish disposition is one that is gained or lost at the very moment the triggering stimulus occurs, so that the object's behavior at that moment no longer reflects whatever disposition it had beforehand. A masked disposition, by contrast, remains completely unchanged and still receives its triggering stimulus, yet fails to manifest because something else intervenes to block the effect.

Write a short story, built around ordinary human characters and relationships rather than the standard props of glass, wiring, or poison, in which some underlying trait of one character — courage, loyalty, honesty, or whatever you find most promising — is finked at one point in the plot and masked at another, without the story ever naming or explaining either term. Let a second character spend the story trying to find out, through some kind of trial or test, what the first character is "really" like, and end it in a way that leaves genuinely open whether any such test could have told them the truth.
