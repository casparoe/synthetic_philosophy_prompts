A live debate on rationalist blogs concerns how much weight to give one's own inside-view judgment versus deferring to well-calibrated aggregates such as prediction markets — precisely the question Eliezer Yudkowsky raises in his writing against what he calls "modest epistemology." I think David Lewis's Principal Principle is directly relevant here, but I can't quite work out how. Lewis ties rational credence to objective chance only via evidence that is "admissible," and he offers a working crystal ball as his paradigm case of inadmissible evidence, yet he is notoriously vague about how to draw that line for anything less exotic. Suppose a well-calibrated Metaculus question sits at 70 percent, and I have some private consideration — inside information from my job, a hunch based on a pattern I've noticed, or simply a different reference class I trust more — that isn't priced into that number. Could you set out the most important implications of the Principal Principle, and of the unresolved admissibility problem specifically, for how much weight someone in my position should give the market price versus their own private evidence when settling on a final credence?
