I cover consciousness research, and one wrinkle in Integrated Information Theory (IIT) strikes me as better suited to fiction than to another explainer. Because IIT locates experience in a system's intrinsic causal structure rather than in what it does, the theory predicts that a purely feedforward circuit engineered to reproduce exactly the input-output behavior of a recurrent system that IIT credits with high Φ will itself have a Φ of zero. On IIT's own terms, that means there is nothing it is like to be the feedforward circuit, however sophisticated its performance looks from the outside. This is the crux of a line of criticism sometimes called the "unfolding argument": unfold any conscious recurrent system's feedback loops into an equivalent feedforward network, and the theory is committed to calling the unfolded twin a philosophical zombie, even though no experiment on its behavior could ever tell the two apart. Write a short story that takes this specific bind seriously, in the spirit of Greg Egan's Permutation City on the nature of consciousness, rather than defaulting to a generic "machine wakes up" narrative. Ground the story in IIT's actual commitments — Φ and the feedforward/recurrent distinction — and let the philosophical unease sit unresolved rather than settling, one way or the other, whether the machine is conscious.
