I've been asked to do a recorded Q&A for our department's public philosophy series, and the guest is a philosopher who's spent basically her whole career defending Confucian role ethics -- the Ames/Rosemont line, where the self isn't an individual who happens to occupy roles (son, teacher, elder sister, ruler...) but just is the sum of those roles, properly enacted. I've sat through enough talks on this to know she can happily fill the whole hour restating the relational-self picture and the critique of Western individualism, so I need help with questions that actually corner the view instead of inviting a restatement of it.

Three things I specifically want covered, each with a follow-up keyed to whatever shes likely to say in response. First, the "no self prior to its roles" claim: if there's no independent self standing behind the roles, I want to press on what exactly is doing the criticizing when someone rejects or reforms an unjust role (I'm guessing she'll reach for reciprocity/shu here, so have a follow-up ready for that move). Second, a question built around the Mencius story of Shun and his father Gusou -- Mencius's answer is that Shun lets his minister Gao Yao arrest Gusou according to law, but then abandons the throne himself and secretly carries his father off to live out his days on the coast -- because that's the go-to case people use to say role ethics just dresses nepotism up as virtue, and I want to know if there's a principled way for the view to rank conflicting roles or whether it really does come down to "whichever relation is closest wins." Third, something on Rosemont's rejection of rights-talk, but aimed specifically at someone stuck inside a bad role -- say a wife under the traditional husband-wife relation -- since part of the point of rights-language is to give that person a lever that doesn't depend on the relationship reforming itself from the inside first.

Could you draft me 8-10 questions total along these lines? Use the three above as anchors (with their follow-ups), and fill out the rest with whatever else you think a sharp interviewer should ask -- e.g. whether role ethics is even a distinct ethical theory or just virtue ethics in different clothes -- and end on a question about what she'd accept as evidence against her own view, rather than just another hard case for it to absorb.
