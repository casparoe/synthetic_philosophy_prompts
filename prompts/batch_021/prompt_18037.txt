I've been a longtime lurker on LessWrong and the surrounding rationalist blogosphere, and one topic that keeps pulling me back in is the relationship between subjective credence and objective chance. It started, as these things often do, with an old thread arguing about the Sleeping Beauty problem, which sent me down a rabbit hole into David Lewis's Principal Principle: the idea that rational credence should defer to known objective chance, conditional only on evidence that is "admissible" (roughly, historical facts and facts about the chance-laws themselves, as opposed to anything that outruns what the chance process itself tells you).

What I actually want help with is a more specific wrinkle that I don't think gets discussed much outside specialist philosophy-of-probability circles: the "undermining" problem. If chances supervene on the entire Humean mosaic, including events in the future relative to the chance event itself, then propositions about the far future can bear on what the chances were at any earlier time -- which threatens to make paradigmatically admissible historical evidence behave as though it weren't admissible after all, undercutting the clean deference the Principal Principle was supposed to secure. Lewis's own patch, worked out in "Humean Supervenience Debugged" and developed in parallel by Ned Hall and Michael Thau, has agents defer not to flat-out chance-propositions but to conditional chances given a complete theory of the world's chance-laws (often called the New Principle). I've never been sure whether this counts as a principled repair or a face-saving retreat, and I'd like to see the dispute actually argued out rather than summarized.

Could you write me a dialogue between a committed Humean about chance, who thinks the New Principle rescues everything worth wanting from the original principle, and a primitivist about chance, who holds that objective chance is a basic, non-supervenient feature of the dynamical laws rather than a pattern in the mosaic, and who thinks the whole undermining episode shows that Humeanism was never entitled to the Principal Principle in the first place? I'd like both of them steelmanned as genuinely sharp, technically fluent philosophers rather than one being a foil for the other: the Humean should press the primitivist on what could possibly justify deferring credence to a brute, unanalyzed propensity if not the very functionalist story that Humeanism tells about chance, while the primitivist should press the Humean on whether the New Principle quietly gives up the idea that an agent could know what to believe about tomorrow's coin toss without first having views about the entire future history of the universe. Please write it as a real back-and-forth, in which each of them responds to what the other has just said rather than restating a prepared position, and let it end not with a resolution but with each side naming the one consideration that would actually change their mind.
