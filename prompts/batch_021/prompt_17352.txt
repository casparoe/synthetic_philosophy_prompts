I'm a backend engineer by day, but I also help run the "collections committee" for a small volunteer-run vintage computing museum a few of us started a couple years back (I got roped onto the committee mostly because I was the only one who knew how to touch our janky Wordpress site, so, authority creep). For a few years now we've had a board on display labeled as one of maybe 200 or so hand-built Apple-1 units from the original garage assembly run Woz and his crew did back in '76. Last month a collector who's authenticated a bunch of these for auction houses came through, and based on the chip date codes and the solder joints, he thinks ours almost certainly wasn't touched by anyone on that original crew -- his best guess is it got put together a few years later, maybe early 80s, by somebody skilled, using real leftover period-correct chips and the original schematic. So its not a modern fake, and it's not one of the reproduction kits people openly sell as reproductions now either -- more like a "shadow" build out of genuinely old parts, done well enough that nothing about the board itself, materially or functionally, gives it away.

Our collections policy just says "originals must be clearly distinguished from replicas," which obviously nobody wrote with this in-between case in mind. The committee's split three ways: relabel it as a later assembly and knock its insured value down, pull it off the floor entirely till we know more for sure, or leave it as is since "probably not original" isn't the same as "definately not." One of our members, who's big into vintage guitars, keeps bringing up the "partscaster" thing from that hobby -- a guitar built later out of genuine old parts sells for way less than one that actually left the factory assembled, even though you can't tell the difference by playing it -- and his point is that since the object itself hasn't changed at all, only what we know about it, none of that should matter here either.

Could you write the short memo I'd actually forward to the rest of the committee before we vote next month? Pick one of the three options and argue for it. Don't just assert that provenance obviously matters and move on -- I want it to actually engage with the "the board hasn't changed, only our information about it has, so why should the label or the value" argument, and explain where you think it holds up or falls apart, before landing on a recommendation.
