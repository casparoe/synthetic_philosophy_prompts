I host a podcast where I get physicists and philosophers of physics to defend the more contrarian corners of their field, and next month's guest is a physicist who has become one of the more prominent defenders of superdeterminism: the position that Bell's theorem can be reconciled with a fully local universe if we give up "measurement independence" — the assumption that the hidden variables fixing a particle's behavior are statistically independent of whatever setting an experimenter will later choose. The view has picked up real momentum over the last decade, partly through the advocacy of a few well-known physicists, and I'd rather give it a serious, well-informed hearing than either a hit piece or a puff piece. I already understand the basic move — a common cause reaching back into the overlapping past of the particle source and the experimenters could correlate the two without any signal crossing between the wings of the experiment — and I know the reflexive objection, that this just relocates Einstein's "spooky action" into an equally spooky conspiracy among initial conditions. What I don't want is forty minutes of my guest simply restating that exchange.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that move past that standoff, each with a short follow-up keyed to the answer I'd expect her to give. I'd like at least one question that presses the fine-tuning problem specifically: granting a genuine common cause that predates the measurement choices, why should it produce exactly the correlations quantum mechanics predicts rather than any of the countless other patterns compatible with mere determinism — and why the answer "it's all just determinism" doesn't actually explain that particular fact. I'd also like one question on whether denying measurement independence can be quarantined to Bell experiments at all, given that the same move, taken seriously, would let any theory explain away any inconvenient result by positing a hidden correlation between the world and the experimenter's choices. One question should use the "cosmic Bell tests" — experiments that fixed the detector settings using light from quasars billions of years old, so that any conspiratorial correlation would have to trace back to before that light was emitted — to ask how much further the common cause can be pushed before superdeterminism stops being a live empirical hypothesis and becomes an unfalsifiable metaphysical stance. And I'd like to end on a question asking her to state, as concretely as she can, what observation would ever count as evidence against her view, or whether she thinks a fully worked-out deterministic substrate, along the lines of 't Hooft's cellular-automaton models, is required before that question can even be answered.
