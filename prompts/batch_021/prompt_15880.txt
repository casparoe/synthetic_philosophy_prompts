Between LessWrong and Astral Codex Ten, Goodhart's Law has become background knowledge for me: once a measure becomes an optimization target, agents -- or optimizing processes more generally -- start gaming the metric, and it decouples from the goal it was meant to track. Ian Hacking's concept of the looping effect, developed across *Rewriting the Soul* and *The Social Construction of What?*, is sometimes treated as this idea's humanities-side counterpart: sort people into a human kind -- Hacking's own paradigm case is multiple personality disorder -- and their awareness of the classification changes their self-understanding and conduct, so the kind itself drifts and the classification has to be revised, looping back into how people understand themselves again.

I'd like a comparison that goes beyond noting the family resemblance. Does looping require something Goodhart's Law does not, or the reverse -- for instance, does only one of the two presuppose what Hacking calls an "interactive kind," a classified party that is a self-interpreting agent aware of being classified? Construct one case that clearly instantiates looping without being an instance of Goodhart's Law, and one that clearly instantiates Goodhart's Law without being a case of looping, and use both to pin down exactly where the concepts part ways.
