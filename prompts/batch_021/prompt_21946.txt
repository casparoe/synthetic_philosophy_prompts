I'm a retired chemist, and back in grad school (more decades ago than I'd like to admit) everybody was reading Kuhn's Structure of Scientific Revolutions, so the idea that scientists working in different paradigms are almost living in different worlds -- phlogiston chemists and oxygen chemists supposedly talking past each other because their key terms don't really line up -- got drilled into me early, it was basically folklore in my field. Now that I've got time on my hands I've been reading philosophy of language, and I ran into Davidson's stuff on radical interpretation and his argument against the very idea of a "conceptual scheme." As I understand it, his point is that if we can even recognize Priestley's phlogiston talk as a theory at all -- i.e. interpret him as saying and believing things -- then charity guarantees its translatable into our terms well enough that "incommensurable paradigms" isn't really a coherent category, its just ordinary theory change plus some false beliefs mixed in. Which is a pretty deflationary thing to say about a book that excited a whole generation of scientists like me.

So here's what I'm after: could you write me a dialogue between a committed Davidsonian and a Kuhnian philosopher of science really going at each other on this, using the phlogiston/oxygen case and maybe also the Newtonian vs. relativistic "mass" case as the concrete example they keep coming back to? I want both sides to be genuinely sharp -- the Kuhnian shouldn't just keep asserting incommensurability, they need to directly answer the charity/radical-interpretation argument, and the Davidsonian shouldn't get to win just by definitional fiat, they need to say something about whatever it is in paradigm change that makes Kuhn's fans so sure ordinary translation isn't doing the job. I brought this up with my old book club a few weeks back and the discussion went nowhere fast, so I'm hoping you can actually make some progress on it.
