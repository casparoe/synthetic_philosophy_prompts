My mother recently became unable to live alone, and my sister and I have been trying to work out a fair way to divide her care between us without hiring the outside help we cannot really afford. My job is demanding and inflexible, so every hour I spend caring for her costs me a missed shift or a scramble to cover my other responsibilities. My sister works fewer hours, so taking on those hours is logistically easier for her, but she finds the caregiving itself far more emotionally draining than I do; I feel little strain from the caregiving as such, only from the schedule it displaces. Whenever we propose splitting the hours evenly, or in proportion to our free time, the other points out that raw hours do not capture what the arrangement actually costs each of us, and we have no way of checking whose account of their own strain is accurate, let alone whether the two are even comparable. Neither of us doubts the other's honesty; the problem is that each of us is the only witness to our own experience. Is there a legitimate way to weigh a logistical burden against an emotional one across two different people, or are we simply stuck with two first-person reports that cannot be reconciled?
