I read about Kuhn's paradigm shift idea a while back (never studied it properly, it just stuck with me) -- the thing about how science doesn't just add new facts onto the old picture, sometimes it swaps out the whole framework and old data ends up meaning something different. It actually came up at a barbecue last weekend when my friend Sonia and her husband Greg got into it about how much nutrition guidelines have flip-flopped over the decades (fat's bad, fat's fine, eggs bad, eggs fine...).

(1) Greg said the constant reversals prove nutrition science isn't real science -- real science accumulates, so if it were legit it'd be refining earlier findings instead of flatly contradicting them every ten years.

(2) Sonia said thats not fair: real hard sciences do this too -- physics didn't just add onto Newton, relativity replaced huge chunks of the picture -- and nobody says physics isn't real science because of that, so reversals alone don't prove nutrition is fake.

(3) Greg said there's still a difference: Einstein's equations turn back into Newton's at everyday speeds, so the old theory comes out true again in the range where it always worked. New nutrition advice doesn't do that -- it doesn't explain why the old advice looked right for decades, it just says the old studies were garbage. That's not a paradigm shift, that's just being wrong the first time.

(4) Sonia said the comparison's unfair too, because nutrition guidelines were never a mature paradigm the way physics was -- it's shakier observational science that was always vulnerable to confounding and bad methodology, so of course it won't "reduce" to the new picture the way one real paradigm reduces to another. But she admitted that if that's true, maybe calling it a "paradigm shift" in the Kuhnian sense was the wrong word from the start too, and neither of them could say what to call it instead.
