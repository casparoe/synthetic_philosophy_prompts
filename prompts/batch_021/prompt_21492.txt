I'm a retired physicist (condensed matter, mostly semiconductors) who finally has time to read all the philosophy I ignored for forty years, and I've been chewing on Ian Hacking's idea of "looping effects" from The Social Construction of What? and some of his older essays on multiple personality and autism. As I understand it, Hacking's point is that a kind like an electron or a quark just sits there being whatever it is, totally indifferent to how physicists classify it -- our theories change, the electron doesn't. But a psychiatric category is different: classify someone as having multiple personality disorder, or as autistic, and the classification changes how the person understands themselves and behaves, which changes what clinicians see, which changes the diagnostic criteria, and round it goes. Its a nice idea and I can see why he needed a whole new word for it.

Here's where I got stuck and started an argument with my book club (mostly other retired lab-rat types, we're insufferable). If a diagnostic category and the people in it are busy reshaping each other in this loop, then surely the category isn't tracking anything that was there independently, the way tuberculosis was doing its thing in people's lungs long before Koch found the bacterium. It sounds to me like these looping categories are basically self-fulfilling prophecies -- we write the diagnosis into the manual, people start living into it, and then we point to their behavior as confirmation the diagnosis was right all along. Doesn't that mean multiple personality disorder, autism, and probably half of what's in the DSM aren't real illnesses at all, just labels doing a lot of the causal work themselves, and that psychiatry should be a lot more embarrassed about this than it seems to be? My friend Ruth, a retired GP, thought I was being unfair to her old profession but couldn't really explain where the reasoning goes wrong. Can you sort out whether I've actually understood Hacking's argument, or whether I've smuggled in a conclusion he wouldn't sign off on?
