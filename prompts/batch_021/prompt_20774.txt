My great-aunt Ruth taught set theory for almost forty years and was completely, unembarrassedly obsessed with Godel -- kept a postcard of him in her wallet next to her license. Her favorite dinner-table fact was that Godel actually worked out a rational case for an afterlife, nothing to do with God or judgment or harps, something about the world being fundamentally "rational" and it being absurd for a mind to spend eighty years getting wiser only to be switched off right when its at its best. She died last month and told me straight out, more than once, that whoever gives her eulogy (me, since im the novelist of the family) isn't allowed to make her sound like she believed in clouds and harps, and also isn't allowed to make her sound like she thought it was just lights-out. Funeral's Saturday: half the room is her very Catholic sisters who want something warm, the other half is her old math department, who will clock it instantly if the shape of the argument is off. Can you write it? Five minutes tops, and it actually has to work as a eulogy, not a logic lecture.
