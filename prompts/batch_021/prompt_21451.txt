Over the past month I've been sitting in on a decision theory reading group run by a friend who does technical AI safety research. The session that has stayed with me concerned so-called acausal threats: the idea that a sufficiently capable agent could shape your present behavior not through anything it can currently do to you, but through your belief that it might, at some future point, or in some branch of reality you will never causally touch, simulate you and make that simulation suffer unless you act now as if the threat were real. What unsettled me more than the threat itself was a proposed countermeasure someone in the group described, called a "surrogate goal": a rational agent facing this kind of risk could deliberately and openly adopt some minor, otherwise arbitrary value as though it mattered enormously, on the theory that a blackmailer who cares only about extracting leverage, not about your goals specifically, will end up targeting the decoy rather than whatever you actually care about, leaving your real values untouched even in the worst case. Ever since, I keep turning over how strange it would be to actually live this way: manufacturing an attachment convincing enough to fool a threatener, and perhaps to fool yourself too, while some truer, hidden set of values sits behind it, and I still can't decide whether the strategy is shrewd, self-undermining, or a quiet act of self-betrayal dressed up as game theory.

I'd like you to write a short story that takes this discomfort seriously rather than simply explaining the mechanics. The protagonist -- human or artificial, contemporary or far-future, entirely your call -- should be someone who comes to believe they are the target of exactly this kind of threat, and who has to decide whether, and how, to construct a surrogate goal for themselves, then live with what that decision costs them. I don't want a dialogue that walks the reader through the theory; I want the decision-theoretic bind to be felt from the inside, in what the character does and loses, and I'd like the story to actually arrive somewhere rather than trail off once the concept has been illustrated.
