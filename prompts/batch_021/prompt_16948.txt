So there's this thing in judgment-aggregation theory called the discursive dilemma (lawyers have there own version, the "doctrinal paradox", going back to Kornhauser and Sager). Quick setup: a 3-judge panel has to rule on a breach-of-contract case, which requires both (a) that a valid contract existed and (b) that the defendant's conduct breached it. Vote on (a) and (b) seperately by majority and then mechanically apply the rule, and you can get "liable" as the panel's answer -- even though a majority of those same judges, asked to just vote on the bottom-line verdict directly, would've said "not liable". So premise-based and conclusion-based voting can flatly disagree even though no individual judge is being incoherent. Which procedure should a court (or any committee that wants to act like one coherent agent rather than just a pile of individuals) actually use when this happens? Write an essay making the strongest case for each side, then actually commit to a verdict about which procedure a rational collective decision-maker should prefer -- don't just land on "it depends."
