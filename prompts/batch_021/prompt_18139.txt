My Grandpa is convinced none of his grandkids ever actually follow through on stuff we promise him, so lately he's started "testing" people before he'll hand over any money. Last week he told me he'd Venmo me $2,000 tonight, no strings attached, if I could convince him over the phone, right then, that I genuinly intended to spend this whole Sunday clearing out his garage — something I have zero desire to do and that, once the money's already sitting in my account, would get me literally nothing except a wasted day. The catch is that he's weirdly unbeatable at telling when someone's bluffing (he's called out every single one of us at some point, in exactly the moment we were lying), so half-assing the call clearly wasn't going to work. He also told me straight up that once he sends the money, that's the end of it: he won't ask for it back and won't hold it against me if I skip Sunday completely, he just cares that I meant it in that moment on the phone.

Here's what's been bugging me since, and it kind of connects to something from my practical rationality seminar this semester even though I can't place exactly how. I obviously wanted the $2,000, so I wanted to be able to form that intention and pass his test. But the second I pictured myself already having the cash sitting in my account the night before, I knew for a fact I'd have zero reason left to actually give up my Sunday hauling boxes around a garage. My roommate says this proves I couldn't have really formed a genuine intention in the first place — that "meaning it" while already knowing you'll have no reason to follow through later is basically incoherent, so I must've just been fooling myself, or fooling him. That feels too quick to me, but I can't put my finger on why. Did I actually manage to sincerely intend to do it there on the phone, or is my roommate onto something and the whole setup was self-defeating before I even dialed?
