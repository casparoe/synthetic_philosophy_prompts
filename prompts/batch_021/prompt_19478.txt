My cousin's a public defender and just got handed a case where the whole defense is duress -- her client says she only drove the getaway car because her boyfriend threatened her kid, so she basically had no other option. Its gotten me pretty deep into reading about free will and moral responsibility this month, and she dragged me along to a law-and-philosophy meetup she goes to, where two regulars, Priya and Dev (both a few years into philosophy PhDs), got into an argument about Frankfurt cases that I couldn't referee myself.

(1) Dev made the standard move: Black is rigged to make Jones do a certain thing, but only steps in if Jones looks like he's about to do otherwise; Jones ends up doing it entirely on his own, Black never has to lift a finger, and yet Jones couldn't have done otherwise. So responsibility doesn't require alternate possibilities, full stop.

(2) Priya said not so fast: for Black to know when to step in, there has to be some sign, prior to Jones's decision, that reliably indicates what he's going to decide. But if Jones's choice is genuinely open in the way the libertarian pictures it, nothing can reliably signal it that far ahead of time -- so either the setup is quietly assuming determinism, which just begs the question against the person who thinks determinism is the problem in the first place, or Black can't actually be rigged that way and Jones keeps a real alternative after all.

(3) Dev came back with Mele and Robb's fix: make Black's device a completely separate backup process, running deterministically alongside Jones's own indeterministic deliberation, timed to produce the exact same decision at the exact same instant, only if Jones's own process hasn't gotten there first. No prior sign needed at all, so Priya's dilemma never even gets off the ground.

(4) Priya wasn't buying it: she said that only works if a deterministic process and an indeterministic one can produce the literal same token decision, and that's not something you get to just stipulate your way into -- its exactly the thing that's in dispute, so Dev's "fix" is smuggling in the answer instead of earning it.
