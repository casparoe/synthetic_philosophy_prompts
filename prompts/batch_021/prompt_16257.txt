Ok here's a random one, not obviously a philosophy thing at all but its been nagging at me. I sit on a small committee that decides how to spend our town's flood relief fund, and this year we've got exactly enough money for one of two options, no mixing them: (a) fully repair the single worst-hit house, which is genuinely unlivable without it, or (b) split the same money five ways across five other flooded houses, each needing about a fifth as much to become livable again, but right now just as unlivable as the first one. Most of the committee thinks its obviously (b), since that helps five families instead of one, simple as that. Something's bugging me about how fast everyone jumped to that though, like the one family's situation just stopped mattering in itself the second there was a bigger number sitting on the other side of the ledger. Am I just being precious about this, or is there actually something wrong with settling it purely by headcount?
