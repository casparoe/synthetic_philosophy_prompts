Three months ago I was finally assessed and diagnosed with adult ADHD, after years of putting it off. What has struck me since isn't really the diagnosis itself but what happened afterwards: within a few weeks I was reading ADHD forums most evenings, had picked up vocabulary like "time blindness" and "rejection sensitive dysphoria" that I had never previously used to describe myself, and started noticing (or perhaps producing) behaviours I don't remember flagging before the assessment. Over dinner, a friend finishing a philosophy PhD told me this sounds like a textbook case of what Ian Hacking called the "looping effect of human kinds": his claim that classifying a person, unlike classifying a quark or a rock, changes the person classified, who may then change the classification in turn, in a feedback loop with no analogue among what he calls "indifferent kinds."

Since that conversation I have been working through "Making Up People," "The Social Construction of What?," and the material on Multiple Personality Disorder in "Rewriting the Soul," partly to make sense of my own case and partly out of genuine curiosity about how general the phenomenon really is. MPD/DID is obviously Hacking's central illustration, but it is much less clear to me how, say, schizophrenia, PMDD, or obesity compare on this dimension: whether looping is basically an on/off property of "constructed" categories in general, or a matter of degree tracking something more specific, such as whether there is a community of the classified able to read about, contest, and perform the classification, as opposed to a label applied from outside with little uptake by those it describes.

So here is what I would like help with. Please rank the following twelve categories by how strongly you would expect each to exhibit a Hacking-style looping effect specifically, not "how socially constructed" or "how controversial" each one is in general, but the degree of feedback between the classification and the self-understanding and behaviour of the classified. (I know Hacking does not discuss all of these explicitly, so treat this partly as an extrapolation exercise from his criteria.) The categories are: Dissociative Identity Disorder (formerly Multiple Personality Disorder), Autism Spectrum Disorder, Adult ADHD, PTSD, Major Depressive Disorder, Schizophrenia, Anorexia Nervosa, Borderline Personality Disorder, Gender Dysphoria, Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder, Homosexuality (as classified in DSM-I/II prior to its removal in 1973), and Obesity. Please give your answer as a JSON object with two keys: "tiers", mapping each of S/A/B/C/D (S = strongest expected looping, D = weakest, closest to an indifferent kind) to a list of category names; and "notes", mapping each category name to one sentence justifying its tier by reference to Hacking's own criteria rather than to general plausibility or stigma.
