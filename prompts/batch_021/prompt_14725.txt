This semester I'm taking my department's graduate seminar on metaethics, and outside of class I help produce a small student-run philosophy podcast. Next month's episode is with a philosopher who has built her career defending quasi-realism: the view that moral sentences fundamentally express attitudes rather than describe mind-independent facts, while insisting we can still earn back ordinary talk of moral truth, knowledge, and even moral facts in some suitably deflationary sense. We invited her partly because my seminar just spent three weeks working through the Frege-Geach problem, and I'd rather the episode actually test her position than let her repeat the "attitudes, not beliefs" pitch she presumably gives to undergraduates.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions for the recording? I need at least one question that puts the original embedding problem to her the way Geach posed it against ascriptivism: given a conditional like "if lying is wrong, then getting your little brother to lie is wrong," ask her what the antecedent expresses on her view, since it plainly isn't an act of disapproving of lying. I also want a question that moves past whatever "attitude to attitudes" answer she opens with by raising Unwin's negation problem directly -- get her to say what distinguishes the attitude expressed by "lying is not wrong" from the mere absence of any attitude toward lying -- with a tight follow-up in case she reaches for Gibbard-style hyperplans or normative "worlds" to do the work, pressing her on why that apparatus isn't just borrowed truth-conditional semantics in thin disguise. Third, include a question asking her to extend whatever solution she gives beyond conditionals to disjunctions or quantified moral claims (something like "everything the treasurer did was permissible"), since fixes tailored to modus ponens don't obviously generalize. For each of these three, add the follow-up you think her most likely answer will invite. Finish the list with a single question about creeping minimalism: given how much realist-sounding vocabulary she is willing to reclaim, ask her to state plainly what would actually be lost -- for her, or for the realist she opposes -- if quasi-realism simply turned out to be realism wearing different clothes.
