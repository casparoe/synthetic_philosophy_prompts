I'm a third-year PhD student in metaphysics, and for the past several weeks I've been drafting a term paper on conditional analyses of dispositions. Before I commit to its central move in writing, I want a second opinion. As I've reconstructed the debate, C. B. Martin's finkish cases (the standard illustration involves a live wire hooked up to a hidden device that cuts the current the instant a conductor touches it, so that "if touched, it would conduct" comes out false even though the wire is live right up until contact) and the later masking or antidote cases associated with Johnston and Bird (a fragile glass packed in protective foam, or a lethal dose of poison rendered harmless by a simultaneously administered antidote) are usually presented as two distinct families of counterexample to the simple conditional analysis. My argument is that this separation is mostly an artifact of exposition rather than a deep difference: in both kinds of case, some further factor intervenes so that the triggering stimulus occurs without the expected manifestation, and once David Lewis's reformed analysis patches the theory by tying a disposition to the persistence of its intrinsic causal basis across the test, that single fix ought to cover both phenomena at once, since masking just looks like a milder version of finking in which the basis survives a little longer but the interference is the same structural kind of thing.

When I ran this by my advisor, she said this was "exactly backwards," and that Bird's whole point in introducing antidotes was that they survive Lewis's fink-fix in a way that finks themselves do not — but our conversation got cut short before she could spell out why. I'd rather understand this properly than just take her word for it: is there a principled reason why tying dispositions to the persistence of a causal basis, which seems to neutralize the finkish cases, leaves the masking cases untouched? And if the two really are importantly different in this respect, what does that difference suggest about whether any conditional analysis, however reformed, can be rescued at all?
