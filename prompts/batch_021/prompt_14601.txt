He's presented as this almost inhumanly rigorous, dispassionate mind, the patron saint of "just follow the argument wherever it leads." So I was kind of thrown when I fell down a wikipedia rabbit hole and found out that in 1924, the year before he died, he kept a private diary that's just full-on right-wing, antisemitic, anti-democratic ranting -- stuff about Jews and the Weimar republic and so on. Apparently it sat around unpublished for decades and only came out way later, and from what I can tell it genuinely rattled a lot of the people who'd built careers studying him, since it clashed so hard with the "pure reason" image.

Now I can't stop turning it over in my head and I don't have any philosopher friends to bother about it, so I'm hoping you can help instead. Can you write me an essay on whether, and how, that diary should actually bear on how we evaluate Frege's philosophical work -- not just "was he a bad guy" (obviously yes) but specifically whether it should change our assessment of the logic and philosophy themselves. Like, on one hand, a formal system doesn't care about the psychology of the person who wrote it down, so maybe this is just an ad hominem trap. But on the other hand, Frege was famously obsessed with purging logic of anything "psychological" or subjective, insisting thoughts live in some pristine mind-independent "third realm" -- and its at least a live question whether that whole purity project is more continuous with the political purity stuff than we'd like to admit, versus totally orthogonal to it. I don't want a wishy-washy "it's complicated, both sides have a point" answer -- actually pick a side and defend it.
