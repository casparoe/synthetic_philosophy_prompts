I'm a PhD student in health services research evaluating a hospital-level program in which 'rapid response teams' intervene early when a patient's condition appears to be deteriorating, with the aim of reducing 30-day readmissions. Pooled across all patients, hospitals that adopted the program show a noticeably lower readmission rate than those that didn't. But when I stratify patients by whether they were escalated to the ICU within 48 hours of admission, the apparent benefit disappears in both strata and even reverses slightly in one. This looks like exactly the reversal pattern behind Simpson's paradox, and the lesson I've always taken from that paradox is that when pooled and stratified estimates disagree this way, the stratified estimate is the one to trust, since stratifying is what corrects for the confounding distorting the pooled comparison. Given that, is it fair to conclude the program doesn't actually work and that the pooled result is just a statistical artifact?
