I've been re-reading Bostrom's work on observation selection effects after a long comment thread under a Less Wrong post on anthropic reasoning left me more confused than when I started, and I want to sanity-check something about how the self-sampling assumption (SSA) and self-indication assumption (SIA) are usually framed. As I understand it, SIA basically just falls out of doing ordinary Bayesian conditionalization honestly once you treat your own existence as evidence: if one theory implies there are vastly more observers than another, then, all else equal, finding yourself existing at all is correspondingly more likely under the theory with more observers, in exactly the way that drawing a particular colored ball is more likely from an urn where that color dominates. SSA, by contrast, seems to need an extra, somewhat unmotivated restriction — you have to stipulate a reference class of "actual" observers to sample from, which isn't something Bayes' theorem hands you for free; it looks more like a patch bolted on specifically to dodge the conclusion SIA would otherwise force.

If that's right, I don't understand why the choice between SSA and SIA is still treated as a genuinely open, roughly even debate in the literature, rather than SIA simply winning by default as the "no extra assumptions" option, with SSA surviving mainly because people find the Doomsday Argument distasteful and want an escape hatch from it. In particular, I'm not sure what's actually supposed to be wrong with the presumptuous philosopher case: if population size really does carry that much evidential weight, then a philosopher who tells the physicists not to bother running their experiment, since the population-weighting has already settled which cosmology is correct, seems to just be following the Bayesian math wherever it honestly leads, however rude or overconfident that looks from the outside. So: am I missing some way in which SIA smuggles in its own substantive assumption that's really on par with SSA's reference-class stipulation, or is the presumptuous philosopher case actually a more serious problem for SIA than I'm giving it credit for?
