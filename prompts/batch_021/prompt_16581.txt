So we watched Ruth Chang's TED talk in my school's philosophy club this week (the "on a par" one about hard choices — half the club was honestly just there for the free pizza but whatever), and it somehow kicked off a whole argument between two of my friends that I can't stop thinking about. Backstory: my friend Mia spent basically all of April trying to decide between a really competitive genetics research fellowship and a songwriting intensive for the summer, which felt like a huge decision partly because of how it'll look on college apps. She made like four different pro/con lists that all came out dead even and finally just flipped a coin over it (music won, if anyone's curious). When she told us how she'd decided at lunch, my friends Jae and Sam got into it about whether that was actually the reasonable way to handle it, and neither of them backed down.

Here's roughly how the argument went. (1) Jae said the coin flip was basically Mia's only rational option becuase the two things are incommensurable — you can't put "getting good at genetics" and "getting good at songwriting" on the same scale, so there's no way to actually compare them, meaning there was no point agonizing over it like she did. (2) Sam said not having a shared scale doesn't mean you can't compare two things at all — we do it constantly with stuff that has no common unit, like judging which of two friends is more loyal — and that Mia's options were comparable, just not in the normal better/worse/equal way, more like they were "on a par," roughly in the same league without being tied. (3) Jae shot back that this was just a fancier way of saying the same thing: if neither option beats the other and they aren't tied either, there's still no actual reason to prefer one, so the pick is arbitrary either way, and slapping a new label on it doesn't change that. (4) Sam said no, because Mia could've just committed hard to one path, like deciding "I'm becoming a musician," and that decision itself would've handed her a reason she didn't have before, which is totally different from a coin flip, since a coin doesn't track anything about who she is and a commitment does.
