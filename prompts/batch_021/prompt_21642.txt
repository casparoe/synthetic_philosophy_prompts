The common prior assumption in Bayesian game theory (sometimes called the Harsanyi doctrine) holds that two fully rational agents reasoning about the same state of the world should, in principle, share the same prior probabilities, with any later difference in belief traceable only to the private information each has since received. Aumann's agreement theorem then shows that such agents cannot agree to disagree once their opinions have been made known to each other. Given this, if two equally rational, equally careful thinkers each lay out their full reasoning to one another and still end up holding different conclusions, doesn't that prove that one of them has access to some relevant evidence the other lacks?
