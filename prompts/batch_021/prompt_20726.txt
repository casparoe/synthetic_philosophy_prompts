So in my philosophy of religion seminar we just wrapped up the unit on ontological arguments and ended on Gödel's modal version, and honestly its messing with my head way more than Anselm's ever did. Like, the backstory alone is wild - from what I remember he'd apparently had some version of this worked out for decades but wouldn't let anyone actually see the final proof while he was alive, only showed it to like two people (I think Dana Scott was one of them, and the version that eventually got published in his collected works after he died is basically built off of Scott's notes), and reportedly didn't want it circulated because he was worried people would think he was some kind of crank for "proving God exists" with a couple pages of modal logic, when what he actually seemed to care about was just whether the argument went through given his axioms about "positive" properties, not whether it was religiously convincing to anyone. Which feels almost darkly ironic given how paranoid he got about totally unrelated stuff near the end of his life (the whole being-poisoned thing). My friend Jake thinks the modal collapse objection people raised later basically breaks the whole proof, but I'm not 100% convinced that's fatal, so I've kind of got that argument rattling around in my head too.
