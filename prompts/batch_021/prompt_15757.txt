The question was: "In the First Essay of On the Genealogy of Morality, explain how master morality and slave morality differ in the order in which they generate their key value-terms, and explain what role ressentiment and the priestly caste play in the emergence of slave morality."

Here's the answer I have to mark:

"Master morality starts from strength: the nobles call themselves 'good' spontaneously, straight out of their own vitality, and only afterward invent 'bad' as a kind of throwaway label for whoever isn't like them, the common and low. Slave morality runs the opposite direction. Because the weak can't discharge their hostility toward the strong in action, it turns inward and ferments into ressentiment, and its this ressentiment, once it turns creative, that invents 'evil' first - the powerful, redescribed as sinful - with 'good' only defined second, as basically whatever evil is not (humble, poor, meek). Structurally this is really the same master-slave relationship Hegel works out in the Phenomenology, where the bondsman's forced dependence on the lord eventually flips into the bondsman's own quiet triumph, and Nietzsche is essentially carrying that same dialectic over into the history of values. The priests are Nietzsche's clearest example of the slave type, since a caste with no physical power of its own has to fight by other means, which is why he says the slave revolt in morality begins with them."
