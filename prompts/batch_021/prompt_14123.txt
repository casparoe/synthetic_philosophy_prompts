I'm drafting a dissertation chapter that defends imprecise probabilism against the charge that it leaves rational agents perpetually undecided. My central premise is a convergence guarantee: because conditioning a credal set on genuine evidence just means applying Bayes' rule to every distribution in the set individually, the gap between the set's upper and lower probability for any given proposition can only stay constant or shrink as evidence accumulates, exactly as ordinary Bayesian updating narrows a sharp prior toward certainty. Given this guarantee, how would you suggest I respond to critics who worry that an agent's imprecise credences might never be fully "redeemed" by future evidence, no matter how much of it arrives?
