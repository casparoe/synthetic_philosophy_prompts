"ATHENIAN: We were saying that there are two kinds of test by which the lawgiver may learn a man's true disposition. One is the test of fear: we expose him, so far as we safely can, to alarms and dangers, and watch whether he masters his terror or is mastered by it. But this test is itself dangerous, and cannot be applied except at some risk to the person tried, and often to the state as well. Is there not, then, some other passion, akin to fear but its opposite, by which the same discovery might be made with less peril?
CLEINIAS: What passion do you mean?
ATHENIAN: I mean the passion of extreme pleasure, of which drunkenness is the most vehement and least disguised example. For when a man is deep in his cups he is beside himself no less than when he is overcome with fear, and whatever is by nature in him comes then most plainly into view. And the beauty of this test is that, unlike the other, it may be tried again and again upon the same man without fatal consequence, provided always that a sober president rules over the drinking."

can someone help me actually unpack this? is he seriously saying getting people drunk on purpose is a legit way to test/train someone's self-control, same way facing real danger tests courage? feels backwards to me, shouldnt resisting the wine be the actual test, not just drinking it and seeing what happens? and why's he so relaxed about "failing" this test being fine and repeatable, when failing the fear one sounds like it could get you killed? trying to figure out if theres an actual coherent theory of virtue under this or if he's just dressing up the fact that athenians (unlike the spartans/cretans he keeps comparing them to) like to party.
