so we're doing augustine's de mendacio in my philosophy of religion seminar this week (the "lying is always wrong, even to save a life" text) and two people in my discussion group got into an arguement about it after class that never really got resolved.

the case on the table was the standard one: someone's hiding a friend from a guy who's shown up at the door meaning to kill him, and the guy asks where the friend is. Leah said augustine actually gives you an out here: his own definition of a lie is asserting something you yourself believe to be false with the intent to deceive, so if you just go quiet, or say something true but misleading like "i haven't seen him since this morning," you haven't lied by augustine's own standard, you've just declined to be fully forthcoming, which he allows.

Marcus said that's a dodge, and brought up augustine's stuff on "jocose" lies (jokes) - augustine says those aren't real lies specifically because no one actually intends to deceive anyone, which shows what he really cares about is the intent to deceive, not just the narrow technicality of whether your words are false, so a true statement designed to mislead should count as exactly the sin he's condemning, even if it slips past the letter of his own definition.

Leah said if intent alone were enough to make something a "lie" regardless of truth value, augustine wouldn't have bothered defining lying so carefully around asserting what's false in the first place - the reason he treats silence and misleading-but-true statements differently isn't sloppiness, it's that for him the false assertion itself is what triggers the absolute prohibition, even if deceiving people through true statements might be shady for some other reason.

Marcus came back with the eight kinds of lies augustine ranks in de mendacio, where lying to save someone's life is literally the least sinful of the eight - if augustine himself is willing to grade how bad a lie is by its consequences, that shows even he wasn't fully comfortable with pure absolutism, so its not exactly a stretch to think the silence/misleading-truth workaround is exactly the kind of practical escape valve he'd want, whatever his official definition says.

neither of them budged and then we had to go actually do the reading for next week. can you go through this move by move and say who's got the better of each point, and flag anything about augustine's actual view that you think both of them are missing?
