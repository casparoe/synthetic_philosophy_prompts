Here's the student's answer, close to word for word:

"The symbol-grounding problem is Harnad's worry that in a pure symbol system, a symbol only connects to other symbols by shape, never to what it actually stands for. He illustrates it with trying to learn Chinese from a Chinese-only dictionary: you can look up any character, but you only ever land on more characters, so you never get out of the circle to what the words mean -- he calls this the "dictionary-go-round." His fix is a hybrid system where the lowest-level symbols get tied directly to two kinds of nonsymbolic representation: iconic representations, basically analog sensory copies of whatever's out there, and categorical representations, which sort those copies into categories by picking out the features that reliably separate one category from another. Once enough elementary symbols are grounded that way, bigger symbolic strings and propositions built out of them inherit the grounding from below.

This is really just Harnad taking Searle's own Robot Reply and cashing it out properly -- Searle had floated the idea that sticking the symbol system in a robot body with sensors might save it, and Harnad's grounding story is the detailed version of exactly that, so it answers Searle on his own terms: once symbols are hooked up to the world through a body that can perceive and sort things, the system isn't just pushing meaningless syntax around anymore, it actually understands and is conscious of what its doing, not just simulating it."
