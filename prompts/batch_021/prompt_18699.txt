For my seminar on Borges and the metaphysics of time, we've just spent two weeks working through "A New Refutation of Time" alongside the Berkeley and Hume material it borrows from. For the creative portion of my final project, I need a short story (roughly 800–1,200 words) that actually earns its connection to that essay, rather than leaning on the usual Borges shorthand of labyrinths, mirrors, and forking paths. It should dramatize, through character and event rather than exposition, the essay's central move: first denying a mind-independent world along Berkeleyan lines, then denying any self that persists through the perceptions making up that world, along Humean lines. And it should end where Borges's essay does — with the argument intellectually intact but powerless, so that the protagonist, having dismantled time and self in thought, is still left inside an irreversible, unrepeatable moment, unable to shake the feeling that something real is being lost. Could you write this story for me?
