I'm a political theory PhD student on my grad employee union's steering commitee this year, and we've got a fight brewing that's basically the Wilt Chamberlain argument wearing a hoodie.

Our union's dues-funded "solidarity fund" gives every member an equal travel grant and childcare stipend, on the premise that we're all in the same boat. Lately, CS/stats students have started landing $25-35k summer industry internships (fully allowed, stipend untouched), while classics and art history students have zero outside earning options. One faction wants a mandatory 10% levy on outside earnings over $5k, redistributed equally, since letting this slide quietly turns "we're all equal" into a fiction. The other faction says these are voluntary contracts with employers who value specific skills, and taxing them just punishes people for being employable.

I dont want a memo that just reruns the Wilt Chamberlain thought experiment - half the commitee's already read Nozick and it hasn't moved anyone. Can you draft the actual decision memo for the vote: a real recommendation, reasoning both sides would have to engage with, and whichever way you land, don't let the other side's strongest argument off easy.
