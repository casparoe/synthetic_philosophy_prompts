So I was at a barbecue last weekend and ended up quietly taking notes for a scene (novelist habit, sorry) because two friends got into the best argument I've heard in ages. Backstory: our friend Neel is a hardcore vegetarian for animal-welfare reasons but flies constantly for work and couldnt care less about carbon. His partner Ash eats meat with zero guilt but is fanatical about climate stuff. At dinner they announced a deal: Neel pays to offset all his flights (which he thinks is pointless, but Ash cares about), and Ash goes vegetarian at home for the year (which Ash thinks is pointless, but Neel cares about). Both seemed thrilled with themselves.

Then Priya and Duncan, also at the table, started going at it over whether this "deal" is actually admirable or kind of gross.

1. Priya said it's basically comparative advantage applied to ethics: each of them cares way more about the other's issue than about their own inconvenience, so trading gets more total moral good done, by both of their own lights, than either one just sitting there being self-righteous about their pet cause alone.
2. Duncan said that's a nice way of describing paying someone to be moral. If animal welfare actually matters, Ash should go vegetarian whether or not he gets a side payment for it -- needing a bribe to do the right thing is the problem, not a clever solution to it.
3. Priya shot back that we literally run cap-and-trade pollution schemes on this exact logic, and environmentalists love those, so Duncan can't just declare the structure corrupt.
4. Duncan said cap-and-trade works between parties who already agree pollution is bad and are just haggling over who reduces it more cheaply; here, Neel still privately thinks Ash's meat-eating is wrong and Ash still thinks Neel's flying is fine, so the trade doesn't touch their actual disagreement, it just buys silence about it -- like paying someone off instead of persuading them.
5. Priya said fine, but the animals and the atmosphere don't care about anyone's sincerity -- less suffering and less carbon happened, full stop -- so what exactly is Duncan's objection tracking, if not just squeamishness about the word "deal"?

Nobody at the table could settle it and its been bugging me since. Could you go through this move by move and say who's got the better of it at each numbered point -- not just an overall winner -- and flag anything you think both of them are missing?
