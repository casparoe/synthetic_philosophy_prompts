My partner Riley and I are stuck on a disagreement from our AP U.S. History group project that I haven't been able to resolve on my own, even though it feels like there should be a clean answer. Under our teacher's grading policy, a project's grade drops a full letter if it contains either a major factual error or incomplete citations, and the two problems don't stack: having both drops the grade by exactly the same amount as having just one. When our project came back, it turned out that my section was missing several citations and, completely independently, Riley's section had a factual error the teacher caught — neither of us had known about the other's mistake beforehand. Sure enough, our grade dropped the one full letter the policy calls for.

Riley keeps apologizing, arguing that the factual error was the "real" reason we lost the letter grade and that my missing citations didn't actually cost us anything, since the grade was dropping regardless. That sounded right until I noticed the same logic runs the other way: by that standard, my citation gaps were just as harmless, since the grade would have dropped because of Riley's error no matter what I did. Put together, that seems to mean neither of us actually caused the deduction, which can't be right, since the deduction obviously happened for some reason. One friend I asked said that whichever mistake the teacher would have technically noticed first while reading through the paper is the "real" cause, but that feels arbitrary, since neither of us knew about the other's mistake going in, and it's really just an accident of which section happens to come first in the document. Another friend says we're each half responsible, which feels less like an answer than a way of avoiding the question.

The whole thing reminds me of bigger disputes I've come across, like arguments about two factories that each separately dump enough pollution into a river to kill all the fish in it, but I've never seen anyone actually explain the principle involved. So did either Riley or I really cause our grade to drop, and if the honest answer is "no" for both of us, where exactly does that reasoning go wrong?
