So I had this whole argument with my friend Priya on Friday and I've been stewing about it since. We were at her place after her philosophy-of-logic reading group (I just tag along for the wine, im not actually in the program) and somehow ended up forty minutes deep into deflationism about truth. I've always liked the deflationist line that "true" doesn't name some deep metaphysical property, it's basically a logical convenience for disquoting and generalizing, so you can say "everything Jim said is true" instead of listing out a million individual sentences. But then Priya brought up this thing called the conservativeness argument and I honestly didn't have a response. Her point, as best I can reconstruct it now that I've sobered up: if truth is really that thin, then bolting a proper compositional truth theory onto a base theory like Peano arithmetic shouldn't let you prove any new arithmetic sentences you couldn't already prove without it — the truth theory should be "conservative" over the base theory. Except it isn't. Add the usual Tarski-style compositional axioms for truth to PA, and crucially let induction range over sentences that contain the truth predicate itself, and you can suddenly prove things like Con(PA), the consistency of PA — which by Gödel's second theorem PA can never prove about itself. So the truth predicate is clearly doing real deductive work and not just sitting there as shorthand, which sure looks like it kills deflationism dead.

I want to go back to her with something better than "huh, ok, fair enough." Can you build me the strongest possible version of the deflationist comeback here — i.e. actually argue that the conservativeness argument doesn't succeed against deflationism? I'm especially interested in whether conservativeness was ever a fair thing to demand of deflationism in the first place, and whether there's a real difference between a truth theory giving you new proof-theoretic muscle about your OWN theory (consistency statements, reflection principles) versus giving you new substantive facts about numbers or the world, because those feel like they should be different to me but I dont have the vocabulary to say why yet. Feel free to pick whichever flavor of deflationism (disquotationalism, minimalism, whatever) makes the case strongest, I just don't want a wishy-washy "both sides have a point" — I want the actual best shot at saving deflationism from this.
