I'm the engineering representative on my company's model-risk review committee, and we have to decide by Friday whether to ship a new credit-scoring model. Data science has shown the model is calibrated within both our majority and minority applicant groups — at every score band, predicted and observed default rates match — but because the two groups have different overall default rates historically, the model's false-positive rate (creditworthy applicants wrongly rejected) is measurably higher for the minority group. Compliance wants us to equalize false-positive rates across groups instead; data science is citing the Kleinberg-Mullainathan-Raghavan and Chouldechova impossibility results to argue that calibration and equalized error rates can't both hold here, so fixing one will break the other. Both sides treat the matter as settled in their own favor and are talking past each other. Please draft the short memo I'll bring to the committee: recommend a specific criterion, or an explicit and bounded tradeoff between them, to govern this launch, with reasoning substantive enough to actually engage the losing side's argument rather than just restating the impossibility result.
