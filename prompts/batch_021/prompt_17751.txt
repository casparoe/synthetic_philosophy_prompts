I have no formal background in logic or computer science, but I've spent a long time working through material on the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents on my own, and I keep talking myself into both sides of the argument. Write a dialogue between two people who land firmly on opposite ends of it. The Löbian Pessimist holds that Löb's theorem exposes something genuinely unfixable: a proof-based agent can never derive that its own proof system is sound, so it can never fully certify a successor, or even a future version of itself, that reasons from the same axioms, and every proposed fix — climbing to ever-stronger proof systems, or moving to probabilistic frameworks like logical induction — either relocates the regress or quietly gives up the kind of guarantee that made self-trust worth wanting in the first place. The Löbian Optimist holds that this is making too much of an artifact of classical provability semantics, and that a framework built on calibrated, revisable credence rather than strict proof gives an agent everything it actually needs for reflective self-trust in practice. Have the two of them engage directly with each other's strongest moves rather than just restate their own position, and let the exchange end wherever it honestly lands rather than forcing a tidy resolution.
