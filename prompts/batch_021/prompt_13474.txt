Ok this is a random thing but it's been bugging me for weeks. My family has this group chat, and with all the scam texts going around lately (fake package delivery links, "your bank account is locked," fake job offers, stuff like that) we started a habit where if anyone gets a sketchy message they forward it to the chat, everyone votes real or fake, and whatever the majority says is what we go with before the person's allowed to click anything or reply to it. My cousin, who does something with data for work, swears this is a genuinely smart strategy and not just a vibes thing — his reasoning is that as long as everyone's judging it independently on their own, and each person is even just a little bit better than a coin flip at telling real from fake, then the majority verdict gets more and more likely to be right the more people you add, to the point of basically being guaranteed correct once the group's big enough. I'm not a math person at all so this sounded pretty convincing to me at first.

Except here's what's actually been happening: like clockwork, my aunt replies first with "def fake" or "looks legit to me," and then everyone else just chimes in "agreed" within a couple minutes, so i don't think most of them are actually looking closely themselves, they're just going with her gut. And a few months back the whole chat confidently agreed a message was legit and it turned out to be a really well-made scam that fooled even my more careful relatives. So now I don't know what to think — is my cousin's reasoning just wrong, or is it right in general but not actually describing what's going on in our chat? Theres a gap between "more voters = more accurate" and what we're actually experiencing and I can't quite figure out why.
