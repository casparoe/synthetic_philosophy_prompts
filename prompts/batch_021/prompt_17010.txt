I maintain a scheduler that negotiates resource splits between tenants on shared infrastructure, and while researching fairer allocation rules I came across the Kalai–Smorodinsky (KS) bargaining solution as an alternative to the Nash bargaining solution. My understanding is that Kalai and Smorodinsky introduced KS specifically to fix a defect in Nash's axioms by adding a monotonicity requirement, while still preserving every axiom that makes the Nash solution attractive: Pareto efficiency, symmetry, scale invariance, and independence of irrelevant alternatives. If KS really does satisfy all of Nash's original axioms plus monotonicity on top, it sounds like a strict upgrade with no real tradeoff involved. So why do allocation systems, and the literature more generally, still default to plain Nash bargaining instead of just standardizing on KS everywhere?
