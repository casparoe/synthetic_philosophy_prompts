I was recently appointed to the clinical advisory board of the residential meditation center where I practice, and our first case has just come before us. A student on our current three-month retreat, deep in noting practice, underwent a sudden and stable shift five weeks in: the ordinary felt sense of 'being someone behind experience' switched off and, three weeks after the retreat ended, still hasn't returned. She isn't distressed -- she describes it as finally seeing that the self was never there, exactly what anatta claims, and her teacher calls it a genuine fruition. But she is also noticeably blunted: less reactive, less relationally engaged, indifferent to things she used to care about. Half the board wants to call this textbook depersonalization-derealization and require psychiatric evaluation before she teaches again; the other half warns that this simply assumes the clinical frame is right and her account naive, when the tradition has its own vocabulary for exactly this kind of experience. She is scheduled to co-lead a retreat for new students in three weeks. Draft the board's decision memo: what happens to that assignment, what we require of her before then, and criteria -- grounded in what the tradition itself says realization should do to a person, not just clinical checklists -- we could actually use next time this happens.
