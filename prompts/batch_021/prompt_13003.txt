Ok so this might sound like a dumb thing to be stuck on but it's been bugging me all week. A few years ago my therapist suggested I start tracking my moods, but none of the normal words ever felt specific enough, so I started making up my own on a private note on my phone that literally no one else has ever read. Like "thrimming" is this exact tired-but-wired feeling I get most days around 4pm that isn't the same as just being sleepy, and "coldwelling" is this lonely-but-cozy feeling I only seem to get on rainy days when im home alone. I jot down whichever word fits every night, and honestly over the past few years it's felt like real self knowledge, like noticing "thrimming" shows up way more on days I skip lunch.

Last week my roommate saw the note and told me the whole system doesn't actually mean anything. Her point was that since I'm the only person who's ever used these words, and the only person who ever checks whether im using them "right," there's literally no difference between me being consistent with them for years versus me just vaguely feeling something each night, picking whatever made up word feels closest, and then convincing myself afterward that it matches the past. She said if I quietly started using "thrimming" for a totally different feeling starting tomorrow, nothing and nobody, not even me, could ever catch it, because there's nothing outside my own head for the word to actually be checked against. That got to me more than i expected, because id always thought of that note as a genuinely careful record of my inner life, not just random guessing dressed up as precision. Is she actually right that a private system like this can't really be "correct" or "incorrect," or is this just a clever-sounding argument that falls apart once you actually think it through?
