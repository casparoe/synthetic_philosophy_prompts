The question was: "Explain Ian Hacking's distinction between 'indifferent kinds' and 'interactive kinds,' and use it to analyze the looping effects observable in the historical extension of the ADHD diagnosis from a childhood-onset category to one now routinely applied to adults. In your answer, address whether this looping process is compatible with ADHD nonetheless picking out a genuine natural kind with a stable biological basis."

Here is the student's answer, verbatim:

"Hacking distinguishes 'indifferent kinds,' such as the natural kinds studied by physics and chemistry, from 'interactive kinds,' which are the kinds used to classify people. An indifferent kind like the quark does not care that it has been classified: quarks behave the same way whether or not anyone names or studies them. Interactive kinds are different because the people who are classified can become aware of the classification, and this awareness changes how they behave, which can in turn force revision of the classification itself. Hacking calls this feedback process the 'looping effect of human kinds,' and it is essentially the same mechanism as Merton's self-fulfilling prophecy: once people are told they belong to a category, they start acting in ways that confirm it.

ADHD is a clear case of looping. It was introduced as a disorder of childhood, but as awareness of the condition spread, adults increasingly recognized inattentive or hyperactive patterns in themselves and sought diagnosis, and clinicians and researchers responded by developing adult-specific criteria and diagnostic instruments. The diagnosed adults' self-understanding and help-seeking behavior thus altered the very category that classified them, and the category expanded to include them, which is exactly the loop Hacking describes. Hacking's own paradigm case was multiple personality disorder, but the same structure appears wherever a diagnostic category is applied to self-aware subjects who can read what is written about them. Because the criteria for ADHD have never been fixed and have been revised repeatedly in response to how patients present and describe themselves, ADHD cannot be a natural kind in the way that, say, Huntington's disease is: on Hacking's view, any classification that loops in this way is thereby shown to have no real biological basis, and to be, at bottom, a social construction dressed up in medical language."
