Last week a friend and I ended up arguing for hours after a philosophy of science reading group. My friend, who works on philosophy of physics, is convinced that ontic structural realism (OSR), in the style developed by Ladyman and French, is the only stable place to land once you take the history of theory-change seriously — her example, like everyone's, was the move from Fresnel's elastic-solid-ether account of light to Maxwell's electromagnetic theory, where the mathematical structure survived even though the underlying ontology was completely overhauled. Her argument was that Worrall's original epistemic structural realism (ESR) is caught in a dilemma: either the "natures" lying behind the structure are knowable in principle, in which case ESR just collapses into ordinary scientific realism, or they are permanently unknowable, in which case talk of hidden natures is idle, and we might as well go all the way to OSR and say there is nothing to the entities but the structure itself. I pushed back that OSR pays too high a price for this stability, since "structure without individuals" looks close to unintelligible — relations seem to need relata to relate — and that as soon as OSR tries to say what makes its structure more than a bare mathematical pattern (which it has to do to avoid Newman's old objection to Russell, that any domain of the right size trivially instantiates whatever structure you specify), it ends up reaching for modal or causal notions that make it hard to see how it is really different from a relation-heavy version of ESR after all.

We gave up around midnight without resolving anything, so I would like a more careful outside opinion. Could you compare Worrall's epistemic structural realism with Ladyman and French's ontic structural realism, focusing specifically on how each is supposed to handle the Newman objection, and on whether the collapse dilemma I sketched above is really fatal for ESR? I would also value your honest take on whether OSR can make good on a coherent notion of relations without relata, or whether that idea is ultimately just as unstable as the epistemic position it is meant to improve on. If possible, please skip over the standard motivations for structural realism in general — the pessimistic meta-induction, the Fresnel–Maxwell case, and so on — since my friend and I already agree on those; what actually divided us is which of the two developed versions can survive sustained scrutiny.
