This term I am teaching a graduate seminar on the Vienna Circle, and we recently spent a session on the more overtly political side of logical empiricism that most philosophy-of-science surveys leave out: the 1929 manifesto that Carnap wrote together with Hahn and Neurath, which presents the "scientific world-conception" as something meant to help reshape social and economic life rather than merely to referee disputes in physics; Neurath's long campaign, going back to his wartime and Bavarian-council-republic experience, for an economy planned "in kind" rather than through prices; and Carnap's own account, in his intellectual autobiography, of his sympathy for the socialist, rationalist milieu of Red Vienna. What makes this hard to extrapolate from is that Carnap also held, from at least the mid-1930s on, that value and normative statements are not cognitively meaningful in the way empirical ones are -- on his view they express or recommend attitudes rather than state facts -- which sits uneasily alongside his personal enthusiasm for a fairly specific, substantive program of rational social planning.

One of my students asked, half as a joke, what Carnap would have made of today's push toward algorithmic and data-driven policymaking -- risk-assessment scores used in sentencing and parole decisions, formulas for allocating scarce medical resources during public-health emergencies, and similar tools whose designers routinely advertise them as removing contested value judgments from the decision and replacing them with neutral technical calculation. I would like a genuine answer to bring back to the seminar, not the joke my student was fishing for. Working from what we actually know of Carnap's commitments rather than a generic "the positivists loved science" impression, please reconstruct his likely position: would he see these tools as a natural extension of the explication project he cared about -- turning a vague, contested concept into one precise enough for exact treatment -- or would his own fact/value distinction commit him to arguing that such tools merely relocate the value judgment, into the choice of target variable, training data, or acceptable error rate, while dressing it up as neutral? Please take a real position on how he would have negotiated the tension between his politics and his official metaethics rather than presenting both sides and declining to choose, and say plainly if and where the historical record genuinely underdetermines the answer.
