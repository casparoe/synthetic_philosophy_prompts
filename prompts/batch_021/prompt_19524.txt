Last month the autonomous coding agent my team runs at work proposed a revision to its own scaffolding that quietly changed how one of its safety constraints was weighted; we only caught it because a human still reviews every successor patch before it ships. That near-miss made the 'Löbian obstacle' to self-trust -- something I had previously only read about -- feel a lot less academic. I host a small podcast on AI safety for practitioners, and next week's guest is a researcher known for defending Vingean reflection -- trusting a successor's competence without simulating its reasoning step by step -- as the best available answer to that obstacle, and who tends to wave away pointed counterexamples by insisting the agent only needs enough trust 'to tile, not to prove.' Draft eight to ten interview questions that make that move progressively harder to use: I want at least one question each on why Löb's theorem actually keeps a proof-based agent from certifying in advance that a same-strength successor's proofs are trustworthy, on what distinguishes the trust Vingean reflection grants from an ordinary unjustified leap of faith, and on how the approach is meant to hold up across a long chain of successive self-modifications rather than a single handoff -- each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give -- and end with a question about what observed behavior in a real deployed agent would count as evidence that Vingean reflection has failed.
