I spent thirty-five years as a research chemist before I retired, so you can imagine my reaction when our local philosophy reading group worked through parts of al-Ghazali's "Incoherence of the Philosophers" last month and got to his argument that fire doesn't actually burn cotton -- that fire is just an inert lump of matter sitting there doing nothing, and God is the one doing the burning, directly, every single time. My gut reaction was that this is obviously nuts, but the more we argued about it over coffee the more I realised it's alot harder to actually refute than it sounds, especially once one of the group pointed out how close his "habit" explanation for regularity is to Hume on custom and constant conjunction, which I honestly didnt see coming.

Anyway, we've managed to get an actual philosopher of religion, who specializes in classical Islamic theology and defends occasionalism as a live position rather than just a historical curiosity, to join us on a video call for a proper Q&A, and since inviting her was my idea I got stuck writing the questions. I don't want a set of softballs where she just restates the fire-and-cotton example again -- I want questions that press on it the way the actual literature does. Could you draft me eight or nine, each with a quick follow-up keyed to the dodge she'll most likely reach for? I'd especially like one on whether the "habit" move just quietly turns into Hume's regularity theory with God relabeled as the constant conjunction, one on Ibn Rushd's objection that denying real secondary causes makes scientific knowledge itself impossible, one on what the kasb doctrine does to human moral responsibility if even our own actions aren't really caused by us, and a last one asking what observation, if any, could count as evidence against occasionalism rather than just another one of God's habits.
