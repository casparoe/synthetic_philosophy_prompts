My school just started using an AI proctoring tool for remote tests. It watches students through their webcams and afterward marks each recording 'clear' or 'flagged.' The company built it by having staff hand-review thousands of recordings and label the obvious cases, like a phone visible in frame, and it now cites accuracy above 99 percent against that reviewed set as reassurance. What bothers me is that the tool also picks up things like typing rhythm and tab-switching that a human reviewer barely looks at, yet every recording it has ever been corrected on is one where a person could already tell what happened just from watching. For a method clever enough to fool a human reviewer too, I don't see what in training would have taught it to flag that case rather than default to 'clear,' even if some part of its processing is quietly registering that something is off. So the 99 percent figure feels like it's answering a different question than the one I actually care about. Am I onto something real here, or am I missing why that number should still reassure me?
