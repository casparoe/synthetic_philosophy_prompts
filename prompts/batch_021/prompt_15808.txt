Six months ago I was prescribed a low dose of propranolol to take before presentations, after a panic episode in the middle of a product review cost me a promotion I'd been up for. It has worked far better than I expected: the racing heart, the shaking hands, the mind going blank mid-sentence, all of it is simply absent now. I gave the keynote at our company's annual offsite last month, and colleagues who had watched me struggle through smaller meetings for years told me afterward how composed I seemed; one said, only half joking, that I'd "finally grown a spine." I didn't correct her, but the remark has stayed with me in an odd way, because I don't actually feel any braver walking in. The dread beforehand is exactly what it always was, right up until I take the pill and step under the lights.

That mismatch is what I can't get past. I have no way of knowing whether the pill stops the fear from ever being generated in the moment, as though something intercepts it right at the source every single time I go on stage, or whether the fear is produced exactly as before and the pill only keeps it from ever reaching my hands, my voice, my face, so that it's fully present somewhere I simply can't access anymore. My therapist has raised a version of the same question from a different angle: she thinks it matters for whether I'll ever be able to walk into a room like that without the prescription at all. On a hunch, I skipped the dose before a low-stakes internal meeting a few weeks ago and had a worse spiral than I'd had in years, which felt like proof that the old fear had been there the whole time, dammed up rather than gone. But I'm told a skipped dose after months of consistent use can produce its own reaction, so maybe that proved nothing at all. From the inside, and as far as I can tell from the outside too, the two stories about what's happening to me are indistinguishable, and I don't know what further evidence could ever separate them.

I now have to decide whether to keep relying on the prescription for something with considerably higher stakes: I've been asked to present to our board next quarter, where I would genuinely like to be brave rather than merely symptom-free, if there is a difference between the two. So I would appreciate your honest opinion rather than reassurance: does it even make sense to ask which of these two stories about myself is true, and, if it does, how should someone in my position go about trying to answer it?
