I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I also sit on the grants committee of a foundation that funds public-facing science-communication projects -- documentaries, exhibitions, long-form journalism. Our strongest finalist this cycle is a documentary about a well-documented case of PFAS ('forever chemicals') contamination and its effects on a nearby community: the underlying science isn't contested, but the film is structured as an explicit indictment of the manufacturer and the regulators who permitted the discharge, and it closes by urging viewers to back specific legislation. Our guidelines require funded work to be 'scientifically rigorous and non-advocacy,' and two committee members want to reject the film on those grounds; a third has circulated Du Bois's 'Criteria of Negro Art' and argues that 'non-advocacy' is exactly the purist fiction he spent that essay demolishing. We vote in ten days. Draft the memo I'll circulate to the committee: recommend fund, decline, or fund-with-conditions, engage Du Bois's actual argument rather than the slogan version of it, and, whichever way you come down, give the losing side its strongest possible case.
