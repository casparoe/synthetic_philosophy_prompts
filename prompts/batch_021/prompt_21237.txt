I spent something like thirty-five years running a low-temperature physics lab before I retired, so entropy and the second law were basically my bread and butter -- I could probably derive the Boltzmann distribution before I could tie my shoes properly. Retirement left me with a lot of free time, and somewhat to my own surprise I've fallen into philosophy, of all things -- these days I go to a "philosophy of physics" discussion group that meets every other week at the library. At the last meeting two of the regulars, Meredith and Owen, got into a real argument about the arrow of time that's been bugging me ever since, and I liked both of their points enough that I honestly can't tell who comes out ahead. I want to lay it out move by move and get your take.

(1) Meredith opened by saying that the reason we have records of the past -- memories, photos, fossils -- but never of the future is just entropy: it was low near the Big Bang and climbs toward what we call the future, and that gradient is basically the whole explanation of why time feels like it has a direction at all. (2) Owen pushed back hard, saying this is close to circular: the underlying dynamical laws are time-symmetric, which is exactly what got Loschmidt and Zermelo so worked up at Boltzmann -- entropy climbs away from any non-equilibrium state in both temporal directions, so you only get a one-way arrow once you've already picked one end and stipulated, by fiat, that it's the low-entropy one. That stipulation (people call it the "past hypothesis") isn't derived from the physics, he said, it's just asserted, so smuggling it in and then acting like entropy alone explains the arrow is circular.

(3) Meredith granted that the laws are time-symmetric but denied this makes her circular: plenty of good physics pairs a time-symmetric law with a seperate boundary condition -- planetary orbits need Newton's law plus initial positions and velocities, and nobody calls that cheating -- so a law-plus-boundary-condition package is a legitimate explanation, full stop. (4) Owen answered that even granting her the boundary condition for free, a universe-wide entropy gradient still doesn't by itself explain why creatures like us end up carrying records of the low-entropy end only and never the other one; that needs a further story about why reliable record-formation is tied to local, entropy-producing processes, and that extra piece is at least as substantive as the past hypothesis -- so "entropy explains the arrow of memory" is quietly relying on two unexplained ingredients, not one. They left it there, both looking pretty pleased with themselves, so I guess its down to you: could you go through the four moves and say, for each one, whether that person is right, wrong, or only partly right, and why -- and then tell me what you think both of them are still missing? Please end with one line, exactly like this: Overall: Meredith, Overall: Owen, or Overall: neither clearly wins.
