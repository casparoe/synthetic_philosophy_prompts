I train machine learning models for a living, and it has never troubled me that I typically run a large hyperparameter or random-seed sweep and then simply keep whichever run happens to work well, even though the vast majority of the runs are duds. I have recently been reading about the fine-tuning argument for the existence of God, and it strikes me that the standard multiverse reply to it — that a life-permitting universe like ours is unsurprising if there are enough other universes with different physical constants — has essentially the same structure as what I do with my sweeps. Critics of that reply, however, argue that it illegitimately helps itself to the one thing my sweeps have direct evidence for, namely that the other trials actually took place. What are the most important implications of this debate — and particularly of its treatment of observer selection effects — for whether my sweep intuition is actually justified?
