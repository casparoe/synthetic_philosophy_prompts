I'm a 3rd-year CS PhD student working on fairness in ML, and I've been prepping for quals by going deep on the classic impossibility results -- the Kleinberg/Mullainathan/Raghavan risk score paper and Chouldechova's recidivism paper. The math itself is pretty clean once you sit with it: if two groups have different base rates for the outcome you're predicting, there's no score/classifier that can simultaneously be calibrated within each group (same predicted risk = same actual risk regardless of group) AND balanced across the positive and negative classes (equal average score/error rate for people who do vs don't actually reoffend, across groups) -- except in degenerate cases like a perfect predictor. Working through the proof made something click for me about the whole COMPAS controversy: ProPublica's "unequal false positive/negative rates" finding and Northpointe's "we're calibrated" defense weren't actually contradicting each other, they were both just... true, simultaneously, because the theorem says that's exactly what you'd expect once base rates differ by race.

Which lead me to a conclusion I got kind of excited about and then pitched at my advisor's group meeting: that this basically proves "algorithmic fairness" isn't a coherent target at all -- there's no fact of the matter about which definition is "the" fair one, the ProPublica/Northpointe fight was a pseudo-debate between two camps using incompatible metrics without realizing it, and the only intellectually honest response left is to drop fairness constraints entirely and just optimize for accuracy, since that's atleast a well-defined and value-neutral thing to maximize. My advisor kind of grimaced and said "that's not quite the right lesson to draw," but the meeting ended before she could get into why. I've been chewing on it since and can't tell if shes just being diplomatic or if there's an actual gap in my reasoning here -- is there some way to rescue "fairness" as a meaningful concept given the impossibility result, or did I basically nail it and she just didn't want to shut down a 3rd-year in front of the whole lab?
