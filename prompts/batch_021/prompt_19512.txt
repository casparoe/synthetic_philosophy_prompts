A friend and I spent much of last night arguing about whether Kolmogorov complexity genuinely rescues Occam's razor from charges of arbitrariness, without either of us convincing the other. My friend held that the invariance theorem settles the matter: since any two universal Turing machines can emulate one another, the complexities they assign to a given string differ by at most a fixed additive constant, so the choice of description language stops mattering once hypotheses are complex enough, and Solomonoff's convergence theorem washes out whatever dependence remains as evidence accumulates. I argued that this claims more than it delivers: the constant can be made arbitrarily large simply by wiring some favored hypothesis into the reference machine as a primitive, nothing bounds how much evidence convergence requires for any specific pair of machines, and since real inference is always finite, an asymptotic guarantee cannot show that the razor is machine-independent in practice. Please make the strongest possible case for my side: that the invariance theorem is too weak to make Kolmogorov complexity machine-independent for any actual, finite body of evidence, and that Solomonoff induction therefore fails to deliver the objective version of Occam's razor it is often advertised as providing.
