After a seminar on rescue and aggregation, two of my graduate students, Marcus and Ines, kept arguing over a case modeled on Taurek's famous example: a clinic's single dose of antitoxin can save either Nora, who needs the whole dose, or five other patients, each of whom needs only a fifth of it. (1) Marcus said the answer is obvious: five deaths is a worse outcome than one, and refusing to weigh that is just refusing to take numbers seriously. (2) Ines objected that Nora's death is a complete loss for Nora, and each of the five patients' deaths is a complete loss for that patient alone -- there is no one who experiences the loss of five as five times worse than the loss of one -- so absent a further reason, the fair response is to flip a coin and give Nora a real chance. (3) Marcus replied that even granting this, if you pair one of the five, Devi, against Nora, their claims are a standoff, but the remaining four have claims that nothing on Nora's side answers to, so nothing stands in the way of saving them -- which is to say, saving all five. (4) Ines answered that this only works if unmatched claims automatically win, which simply assumes the numbers-based tie-break in dispute rather than arguing for it. (5) Marcus came back with a contractualist point: any choice has to be justifiable to everyone it affects; you can justify saving the extra four, since no rival claim competes with theirs, but there is nothing comparable you could say to Nora to justify favoring her over an equally strong stranger, so the side with no claim left unanswered should win. (6) Ines's last word was that 'no claim left unanswered' is just 'the bigger group' wearing a disguise, and that if the claims are genuinely separate and equal, the principled response is a lottery weighted toward the larger side, not a flat rule that it always prevails.

Neither would concede the point to the other. Could you go through moves (1) through (6) and say who has the better of it at each step, rather than simply naming an overall winner -- and note what, if anything, you think their exchange overlooks?
