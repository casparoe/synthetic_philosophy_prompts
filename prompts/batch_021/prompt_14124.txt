Since finishing graduate school this past year, I've kept up a habit from my program with Marcus, my closest friend from our cohort: each earnings season, we fund a small joint brokerage account and take turns proposing single-stock trades to one another before either of us opens a position. What has started to unsettle me is not that we sometimes disagree, but that we almost always do, and always with full confidence on both sides, even though we're reading the same analyst notes, the same filings, and the same morning headlines before placing the trade. When he wants to go long on a name, I almost always want to go short, and neither of us has been right often enough over the last several quarters for the pattern to be explained by one of us simply being the better trader.

I mentioned this over the holidays to my aunt, who spent her career as an actuary, expecting her to dismiss it as ordinary market noise. Instead, she grew unusually serious and said that if Marcus and I are both genuinely rational, each knows the other is too, and we're truly working from the same public information, then the mere fact that I want to take the other side of his trade ought to be new information for both of us — enough, in principle, to make the disagreement dissolve before any money ever changes hands. She added that something in that description of our situation must be quietly false for the trades to keep happening the way they do, but she was called away to help with dinner before I could get her to explain, and I haven't had the chance to follow up since.

I would like help thinking through whether my aunt's intuition is actually right, and if so, what it implies about what is really going on between Marcus and me. Is one of us failing to be rational in some specific, identifiable way? Is "the same information" doing less work in this story than it appears to? Or is there some other feature of our situation that would allow two people who trust each other's intelligence and honesty to keep landing on opposite sides of the same bet indefinitely, without either of them being foolish? I'm less interested in trading advice as such than in understanding what this pattern actually reveals about what it takes for reasonable people to end up in genuine, lasting disagreement.
