Two postdocs in my metaphysics reading group reached an impasse after our session on the special composition question, and I would value a neutral assessment of their exchange. Mara's opening claim was that whether there is, strictly speaking, a table in the room—over and above some particles arranged tablewise—is a merely verbal question: once the particle facts are fixed, nothing further remains to dispute, so "the table exists" and "there are only particles arranged tablewise" are just two equally adequate descriptions of the same situation. Devesh objected that this cannot be right, since several things we care about seem to turn on the answer—how many objects are in the room, whether the table or merely the particles explains why the cup did not fall, whether the same table survives a leg being replaced—and none of these strike him as merely verbal questions. Mara replied that each of these can be restated in particle-and-arrangement terms without any residual disagreement, which is exactly what marks the dispute as verbal. Devesh countered that this proves too much, since by the same reasoning the dispute between the mathematical platonist and the nominalist over whether numbers exist would also dissolve into a merely verbal one—which seems wrong. Mara answered that the cases are disanalogous: paraphrasing away the table is uncontroversial once the particle facts are granted, whereas paraphrasing away commitment to numbers is itself hotly contested. Devesh's parting shot was that this concedes his real point: whether a paraphrase is genuinely available and cost-free is itself a substantive question, so Mara cannot use paraphrasability to certify a dispute as merely verbal without already having settled a substantive matter in her favor.

Would you go through the exchange move by move, saying who has the better of it at each step rather than just who wins overall, flag anything important that both of them are missing, and close with your own verdict on whether the original dispute over the table is, in the end, verbal or substantive?
