Last weekend a friend and I spent several hours arguing about Bernard Williams's internalism about practical reasons, after he pointed out that the view seems to let genuinely awful people off the hook: if a man has no desire, however indirect, that correct reasoning could connect to treating his family decently, then strictly speaking he has no reason to change, and calling him selfish or cruel is just venting rather than identifying any rational failure in him. I tried to defend the internalist line by leaning on how much work the "sound deliberative route" is supposed to do, but I don't think I fully convinced him, or myself.

As it happens, I record a small philosophy podcast out of my apartment, and next week's guest is a philosopher who has built her reputation defending a broadly Williams-style view: that a reason statement about an agent is true only if it connects, by a sound deliberative route, to some element already present in that agent's subjective motivational set. I'd like your help drafting eight or nine interview questions that go beyond having her restate the position, since I can already predict most of that restatement from the secondary literature.

Please build in at least one question that forces her to say exactly what sound deliberation is permitted to add to, or discover in, an agent's motivational set without quietly importing reasons from outside it altogether -- using Williams's Owen Wingrave case as the test. Include at least one question built around the later case, from his piece on the obscurity of blame, of the husband who is simply nasty to his wife and openly says that he does not care -- pressing on whether internalism can make sense of blaming him at all once we have granted that he is not irrational. And include at least one question that puts Korsgaard's objection to her directly: that Williams's argument only works by treating an agent's being unmoved by a consideration as proof that the consideration was never a reason for him, rather than as evidence of his own irrationality, and so simply assumes the very thing at issue against the externalist. Attach a short follow-up to each of these, keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and close the list with one question asking what kind of case, if any, she would accept as genuine evidence against internalism.
