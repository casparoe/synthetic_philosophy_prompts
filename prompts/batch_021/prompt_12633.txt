So I write novels for a living, and last month I sent a death scene from my current WIP to my two regular beta readers. One of them (R) admitted he got a little choked up reading it. The other (J) laughed and said that's kind of silly, since nothing bad actually happened -- the guy he's mourning doesn't exist and never did. That kicked off a whole side-argument between them over text, and I got copied on the thread and can't stop thinking about it.

R said obviously he doesn't believe the character's real, but he was picturing the scene so vividly that his body reacted the way it would to something sad anyway -- like how just imagining falling off a ladder can make your stomach drop even while you're sitting safe on the couch. J said fine, but then it's not actually grief, just a knockoff that resembles grief, because a fitting emotion needs a real object and there isn't one here. R shot back that nobody says this about being scared on a rollercoaster or in a haunted house -- everyone agrees that's real fear even though you know your not in danger, so why should fiction get held to a stricter standard. J said that's disanalogous: a rollercoaster is actually moving fast under you and a haunted-house actor is actually lunging at your face, so there's a real perceptual trigger -- a paragraph of prose has none of that, it's all happening in your head.

They've both now asked me to settle it, which feels like a trap. Can you go through the exchange and say who's got the better of it at each step, not just who wins overall -- and point out anything you think both of them are missing?
