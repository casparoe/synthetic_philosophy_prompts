I did two tours as an infantry officer some years back, and until recently the philosophical side of that experience was something I'd mostly boxed away. That changed this spring when my younger brother enlisted and started talking about deployment; I found I could no longer just tell him what I used to tell myself back then — that a soldier's personal moral standing doesn't depend on which side turns out to have the just cause, provided he keeps to the rules of engagement. I'm now finishing a philosophy degree I'd put on hold, and for my department's ethics podcast I've arranged to interview a philosopher who defends exactly that claim — the moral equality of combatants — in its more sophisticated, epistemic form: not Walzer's old war-convention story, but the argument that because soldiers on the ground can't reliably know whose cause is just, equal permission to kill is the only standard that could actually govern their conduct.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions for this guest? I don't want the conversation to simply replay the position as I already understand it, so please make sure at least one question presses the epistemic argument on its own terms (if uncertainty about the justice of a war is what licenses equal permission to kill, why doesn't comparable uncertainty excuse plenty of conduct we don't in fact treat as permissible), one puts McMahan's distinction between merely being excused and actually possessing the moral right to kill directly to the guest and asks whether the epistemic argument answers that distinction or just relabels it, and one addresses the difference between a conscript and a volunteer who deploys already suspecting the cause is unjust — which is closer to my brother's situation than I'd like. For each of these, add a short follow-up question keyed to whatever response the guest is likeliest to give, and close with one question about what the view implies a soldier should feel about their own conduct afterward, since that's the part no version of this argument I've read has actually settled for me.
