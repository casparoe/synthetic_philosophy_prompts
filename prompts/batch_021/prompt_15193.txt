I've spent years reading rationalist blogs and forums, where a recurring preoccupation is the gap between having a reason to be in some mental state and having a reason for that state to be correct -- the whole cluster of worries around Pascal's mugging, wireheading, and the general principle that you cannot reason yourself into believing something merely because believing it would be convenient. Recently I ran into what looks like the exact mirror-image debate on the value side of things, one I had somehow never encountered despite all that reading: the "wrong kind of reasons" (WKR) problem for buck-passing, fitting-attitude accounts of value -- the kind of view Scanlon holds when he says that for something to be good just is for it to have properties that give us reasons to favor it. The canonical worry, going back to Rabinowicz and Rønnow-Rasmussen's demon paper, is that a demon who will torture you unless you come to value a perfectly worthless saucer of mud thereby gives you a strong reason to want to value the mud, without coming anywhere close to making the mud valuable -- and the ensuing literature has produced one increasingly baroque attempt after another to specify, without circularity, what separates "reasons of the right kind" from this sort of contamination.

I run a small podcast that normally covers decision theory and formal epistemology for a rationalist-adjacent audience, and next month I'm interviewing a metaethicist who has spent a good part of her career trying to rescue the buck-passing view from exactly this problem. She has defended her own version of a relevance restriction on which reasons are allowed to count toward value, she leans heavily on the state-given/object-given distinction to try to explain away the demon cases, and in her most recent paper she conceded that buck-passing might be worth keeping even if the WKR problem turns out to be strictly unsolvable.

I would like help drafting eight to ten interview questions that go beyond inviting her to re-explain the view for newcomers. I want at least one question on the demon/mud case itself and what her considered diagnosis of it actually is, one on her relevance restriction and the standard charge that any such restriction ends up either circular or merely stipulative, one on whether a Kavka's-toxin-style scope distinction -- a reason to want to value the mud, rather than a reason to value it -- can survive a demon who is explicitly monitoring the attitude itself and not just its downstream effects, one on whether "buck-passing survives even an unsolved WKR problem" is a real position or a graceful way of admitting defeat, and one asking whether she expects any solution she favors to carry over to the structurally identical debate about pragmatic versus evidential reasons for belief. For each of these five, please add a natural follow-up question keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, aimed at making it harder for her to retreat into a restatement of the original view.
