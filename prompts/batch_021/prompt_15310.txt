For a piece on the Vienna Circle I've been reading Moritz Schlick's weirdly personal essay "On the Meaning of Life," where he argues real meaning only turns up in play -- activity you do purely for its own sake -- and that most adult life is meaningless because its just one means-to-an-end chain after another (work for money, money for security, keep asking "what for" and you never hit bottom). He died in 1936, long before anything like today's "monetize your hobby" economy -- Etsy shops, Twitch streams, Patreon, side-hustling your art or your gaming. Given his actual argument, what do you think he'd say about that trend? Would turning a hobby into an income stream necessarily kill the "play" quality that made it meaningful in his sense, or could it still count as play if you're doing it purely for love of the thing? Curious what really follows from his position, not just a generic passion-vs-money take.
