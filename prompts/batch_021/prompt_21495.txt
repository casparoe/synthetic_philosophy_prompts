Please describe the most important implications of the philosophical debate over causation by omission for a dispute currently underway on the volunteer board of the small tenants' co-op that runs my apartment building. A fire-code violation went unaddressed for years — several successive board members each had occasion to notice and flag it, and none did — until it eventually caused an electrical fire that did serious property damage (no one was hurt). We are now trying to apportion responsibility among the board members who served during that period, and I would like the analysis to draw on the substantive philosophical arguments about whether, and in what sense, an omission can be a genuine cause, rather than resting on intuition alone.
