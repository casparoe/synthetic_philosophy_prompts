This semester, in my seminar on Quine, we've been reading one of his few sustained attempts to address ethics directly, in which he extends his naturalized, holistic epistemology to moral values. He denies any principled fact/value seam, on essentially the same grounds on which he denies the analytic/synthetic distinction, and treats our most basic evaluative responses as playing a role rather like observation sentences, with more abstract ethical principles functioning more like theoretical posits, the whole outlook standing or falling together under something like the Duhem-Quine thesis rather than being checked piece by piece. I keep running into a personal version of the problem this raises: when a specific moral intuition of mine pulls against a general principle I otherwise accept, I cannot tell whether Quine's picture gives me any real guidance about which one should give way, or whether it merely redescribes the conflict in holist vocabulary. Please lay out the most important implications of Quine's view of moral values for how I should handle this kind of conflict between intuition and principle, and say plainly whether his picture offers anything beyond the generic coherentist point that something in the web has to yield somewhere.
