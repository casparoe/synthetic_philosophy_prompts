My sister is finishing a philosophy PhD on evolutionary debunking arguments in metaethics — roughly, the worry that our moral convictions can be fully explained by the reproductive advantage they gave our ancestors, an explanation that would hold equally well whether or not those convictions are true, so we have no good reason to think they actually track moral reality. I have no academic background myself, but after years of hearing her talk about this, I've noticed the same move seems to work on romantic love: an evolutionary story about pair-bonding explains perfectly well why two people feel made for each other, without anything needing to be written in the stars. She's getting married in a few weeks, roughly half the guests will be philosophers from her department who will recognize the argument immediately, and I've been asked to give the toast. Please write me a short toast — a few minutes long — that takes this tension seriously enough to actually shape what it can and cannot say, rather than naming the argument as an inside joke, while still landing as a warm, sincere tribute for the relatives who have never heard the word 'metaethics.'
