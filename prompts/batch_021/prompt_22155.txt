I'm drafting a scene in my novel where two brothers who haven't really spoken since one of them got out of prison end up cornered at their mum's kitchen table after the wake, and the conversation turns into a genuine arguement about whether blame makes any sense at all. The older brother (the one who went away) has basically talked himself into a hard line, close to Galen Strawson's "Basic Argument" against moral responsibility -- I want him to actually run the regress properly (you do what you do because of the way you are, you didn't make yourself the way you are, so you can't be truly responsible for what you do or for the way you are, and so on down), and ideally use something like his "heaven and hell" example to make it land rather than just saying "nobody really chooses anything." The younger brother should push back, but I don't want him reaching for libertarian free will or "well actually determinism is false" -- I want him arguing more like P.F. Strawson in "Freedom and Resentment," i.e. that our whole practice of resentment, gratitude, blame etc. doesnt actually rest on that kind of ultimate self-authorship, and that stepping back into the "objective" attitude toward someone (treating them as a thing to be managed or treated rather than someone to be angry at) is its own move that has to be earned, not a default you fall into once you've merely noticed that determinism might be true.

Could you write that dialogue for me? Maybe a page, page and a half, no stage directions needed, just the talking. The main thing I care about is that neither of them is a pushover for the other -- I want real back and forth where each one is actually responding to the specific thing the other just said, not two speeches that happen to be in the same room, and I don't want it resolved with one of them clearly "winning." If it's not too cute, I'd also love it if one of them noticed, mid-argument, that in real life Galen Strawson is literally P.F. Strawson's son, arguing against a form of responsibility his own dad had basically already tried to talk everyone out of needing, decades earlier -- feels like exactly the kind of detail this particular family would clock, given how they argue.
