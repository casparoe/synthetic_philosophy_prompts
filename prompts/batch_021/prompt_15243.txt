A friend of mine has just renewed her membership with a cryonics organization that will, when she is legally declared dead, preserve her body in liquid nitrogen in the hope that some future technology will be able to revive her. When I pressed her on how "revival" is actually supposed to work, she admitted that even sympathetic scientists are doubtful a frozen brain could simply be thawed out and restarted: the preservation process itself damages tissue at the cellular level, so the more seriously discussed route to bringing someone back is to scan the preserved brain's structure in fine detail and use that map to rebuild her mind, memory by memory and disposition by disposition, from scratch, in a newly grown brain or some other material entirely that hasn't suffered the original's damage. My friend isn't bothered by this. As far as she's concerned, whoever wakes up will remember going into the procedure, will have her personality, her plans, her relationships, and that is enough for it to be her waking up, whatever the reconstruction happens to be made of.

I'm less convinced. It seems to me that there's a real difference between her surviving this process and a new person being carefully assembled to believe, quite sincerely, that she is my friend, while the original simply ends along with her damaged brain. She thinks I'm fixated on an irrelevant technicality about which atoms are involved; I think she's mistaking a comforting story for a way of actually continuing her life. Neither of us has been able to settle this, or even to agree on whether it's the kind of question that has a definite answer at all. Since she has asked me whether it's worth continuing to pay into the contract, I'd like a genuinely considered opinion rather than a shrug: is she right to regard this as a way of continuing her life, am I right to be skeptical, or is the disagreement between us confused in some way that a better way of framing the problem would dissolve?
