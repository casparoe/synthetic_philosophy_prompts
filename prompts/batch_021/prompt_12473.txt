Every Good Friday, my small church has members take turns giving a short spoken meditation during the service, and this year it is my turn. While reading on my own, I came across an essay called 'Do We Believe in Penal Substitution?' by the philosopher David Lewis -- not C.S. Lewis, as I first assumed, but the twentieth-century metaphysician known for his work on possible worlds. His point has stayed with me: we are entirely at ease with someone else paying off my debt, no questions asked, but we do not think that way about punishment -- nobody would say justice had been served if a volunteer served my prison sentence in my place. Guilt seems to cling to the person who earned it in a way debt does not. Yet 'penal substitution,' Christ punished in our place, is exactly what this service is built around. I do not want to recite that as though the tension were not there, but the congregation is not going to sit through a lecture on desert and vicarious punishment on the most solemn night of the year. Please write that meditation for me: about 350 words, meant to be spoken aloud, that takes Lewis's puzzle seriously without pretending it is easier to resolve than it is, and still leaves people with something to hold on to rather than a shrug.
