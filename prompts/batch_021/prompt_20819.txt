I'm preparing a session on non-standard decision theory for a graduate seminar and would like a second opinion on how the standard objections to "acausal trade" compare in force. As you'll know, acausal trade is the proposal — developed mostly outside academic philosophy, in the rationalist blogosphere and in papers associated with the Machine Intelligence Research Institute — that two agents with no causal means of interacting (because they are separated by cosmological distance, occupy different branches of a modal or physical multiverse, or stand in a simulator/simulated relationship) can nonetheless benefit from coordinating, provided each can reliably model the other's likely dispositions, typically by adopting an updateless or functional decision theory rather than a causal or evidential one.

Please rank the following six standing objections to the practical viability of acausal trade (as opposed to its mere conceptual coherence), from most to least damaging:

(a) the verification problem: absent any causal channel, neither party can confirm, even after the fact, that the other complied;
(b) the partner-selection problem: the space of possible counterparts is so vast and symmetric that there is no principled way to fix on who, exactly, one is trading with;
(c) bounded simulation: no realistic agent can model another's decision process with the fidelity that mutual predictability requires, only crude heuristics and priors;
(d) the commitment-race worry: incentives to commit unconditionally and early, so as to gain bargaining leverage, can leave every party worse off than more cautious commitment would;
(e) the counterfactual-dependence objection: making one's choice depend on a causally disconnected outcome invokes a notion of "dependence" that critics regard as metaphysically obscure, if not simply confused;
(f) preference opacity: one is rarely in a position to know enough about a causally isolated agent's actual values to identify mutually beneficial terms in the first place.

Please answer as a JSON object with four tiers, ordered from most to least damaging, using the objection letters rather than their text, formatted as follows:

```json
{
  "fatal": [...],
  "serious": [...],
  "moderate": [...],
  "negligible": [...]
}
```

You may place more than one letter in a tier or leave a tier empty, but each letter should appear exactly once across the four tiers.
