I have followed Yudkowsky's quantum mechanics sequence since its original run on Overcoming Bias, back before Less Wrong existed as its own site. I still remember its treatment of Bell's theorem: because Everettian branching explains entangled correlations through decoherence within a single global wavefunction rather than any faster-than-light influence, the sequence concludes that locality survives once collapse is abandoned. I would like the case for a related but more general thesis, one that does not depend on accepting Many-Worlds at all. Jarrett's decomposition splits the Bell factorizability condition into two logically independent conditions, standardly labeled parameter independence (a measurement's statistics cannot depend on the distant setting) and outcome independence (a measurement's statistics cannot depend on the distant outcome, given the complete state and both settings). Please argue, in the spirit of the "peaceful coexistence" position associated with Jarrett and Shimony, that quantum correlations violate only outcome independence, that parameter independence survives intact and interpretation-neutrally as guaranteed by the no-signalling theorem, and that the popular claim that Bell's theorem "proves nonlocality" therefore equivocates between two conditions, only one of which relativity actually rules out.
