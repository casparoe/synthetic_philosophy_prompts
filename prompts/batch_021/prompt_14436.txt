I'm a science journalist working on a long-form piece about how certain puzzles from decision theory have migrated out of seminar rooms and into AI safety research. Next week I'm interviewing a researcher at a small institute that studies long-term risks from advanced AI; she's an invented stand-in for a real, specific position rather than an actual named person, and she has spent several years developing the idea of 'surrogate goals' as a defense against acausal threats. For background, since this corner of the literature is fairly obscure: some researchers in this space worry that a sufficiently capable predictor could extract concessions from a target purely by credibly threatening to harm something the target values, with no causal channel between threatener and target ever needing to exist -- the threat works because each side can model the other well enough to anticipate how it would respond. The proposed countermeasure has the target commit in advance to caring about some deliberately chosen decoy objective exactly as much as it cares about its real one, so that a threatener has no special reason to aim at what the target actually values rather than at the decoy.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions that push past a simple restatement of the idea -- left to her own devices, she'll happily just walk me through the concept again? I want at least one question that presses on whether the commitment to treat the surrogate goal as real is actually credible, given that at the moment a threat comes due the target seems to have every incentive to quietly abandon the pretense; one on whether the strategy, if widely adopted, could backfire by making threats cheaper to issue in the first place, so that the total volume of attempted blackmail rises even as any single attempt grows less costly to its target; and one on the regress worry -- if the policy of defending the surrogate goal is itself something the target's credibility depends on, doesn't that policy need defending too, which seems to call for a further surrogate, and so on outward. For each of these three, please add a natural follow-up question keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, so the exchange doesn't stall after her first response.
