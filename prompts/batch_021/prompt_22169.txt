I've been half-reading Ecce Homo for a seminar and got kind of derailed by the part where Nietzsche goes on this long, weirdly specific tangent about diet -- not just vibes about strength, but actual concrete opinions. Nutrition, climate, place, and how you spend your downtime matter way more, he says, than basically any grand philosophical idea ever has; German cooking (and beer especially) dulls the spirit; English food is basically a step back toward eating like an animal; and he's oddly precise about stuff like how strong your tea needs to be. All of this is tangled up with his idea of "great health" -- I think it's in the "Why I Am So Clever" chapter -- which isn't just not being sick, but the strength that comes from actually getting sick, badly, over and over, and clawing your way back each time, which is basically the story of his own body for his whole adult life.

So I was arguing with a friend about what he'd actually say about Ozempic and the whole GLP-1 weight-loss drug wave. My friends take is that he'd obviously hate it -- some "the weak now get a shortcut to cheat at life" take. But it seems more complicated to me: this is a guy who spent decades chasing the right climate and food because he thought these "small" physiological causes wreck more lives (and more geniuses) than bad philosophy ever did, and who probably would've killed for a fix that actually worked on his own body. At the same time he's got all this stuff about narcotics, comfort, and outsourcing your instincts instead of developing your own taste and discipline, which cuts the other way. Can you actually work through this using what he really wrote, instead of just grabbing whichever one-liner supports "drugs bad" or "drugs fine," and tell me where you think he'd actually land and why?
