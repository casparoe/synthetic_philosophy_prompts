Write a dialogue between an Edgington-style 'no truth value' theorist and a Stalnaker-style selection-function semanticist about how to handle Lewis's triviality result for indicative conditionals. One wants to save the equation (P(if A, C) = P(C|A)) by ditching the idea that conditionals express propositions at all; the other wants to keep truth conditions and ditch the equation instead. Push them to actually press on each others weak spots rather than just asserting their own view -- e.g. how's 'no truth value' supposed to handle a conditional embedded under negation or inside an 'or', and isn't restricting the equation just a way to dodge the proof rather than a principled fix?
