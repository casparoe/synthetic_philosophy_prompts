I'm drafting a collection of short stories, each built around a specific philosophical thought experiment rather than a diffuse theme, and for the next one I want to test whether a Frankfurt-style counterexample to the principle of alternative possibilities can bear real dramatic weight. Please write a short story, 800 to 1,200 words, about a character who commits some morally weighty act while a hidden mechanism has been monitoring her throughout, poised to force that very act the moment she shows any sign of choosing otherwise, but which never has to act, because she goes ahead and chooses it herself, for her own reasons. Avoid the stock political-assassination version of this scenario, and steer equally clear of any "controller unmasked, hero fights back" plot; the philosophical point only survives if the mechanism stays entirely inert throughout, whether or not the reader is let in on it before the act itself. Please skip the tidy lecture on free will as well -- let the irony of the situation do the work, and leave it open, rather than resolved, whether she deserves praise or blame for something she was never actually able to avoid doing.
