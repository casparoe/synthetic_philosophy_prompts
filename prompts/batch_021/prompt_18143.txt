After forty years running a neurophysiology laboratory, I retired three years ago and have spent much of my time since working through the philosophy of mind literature; I now co-host a small podcast on the science and philosophy of consciousness. Next month's guest is a philosopher who defends a higher-order-thought theory of consciousness built on Rosenthal's transitivity principle, and I do not want the conversation to stop at a restatement of her view's basic architecture, which our listeners already know well. Please draft eight to ten interview questions: I want at least one on whether targetless or misrepresenting higher-order thoughts trivialize phenomenal consciousness by manufacturing it out of a mere mistaken thought about a state that is not actually there; at least one on why the higher-order state need not itself be conscious, and whether that stopping point is principled or just an ad hoc way of avoiding an infinite regress; at least one that confronts her with the fact that richly conscious patients can present with extensive bilateral prefrontal damage, asking what pattern of lesion or imaging evidence she would accept as disconfirming a frontal locus for the relevant higher-order states; and at least one on the view's apparent commitment to denying phenomenal consciousness to most non-human animals and preverbal infants, pressing on whether the conceptual threshold it sets is independently motivated or merely calibrated to fit prior intuitions about which creatures already seem to matter -- and for each, a brief follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give.
