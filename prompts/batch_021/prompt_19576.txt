I'm a philosophy professor and the outside ethicist on my hospital's clinical ethics committee. An overloaded telemetry nurse noticed signs of early sepsis but did not escalate care per protocol; the patient deteriorated overnight and died, and a subsequent review concludes that timely escalation would very probably have saved her. Our peer-review committee has deadlocked over how to classify the case under hospital policy, which routes events to a disciplinary process when a staff member's action caused the harm, and to blame-free systems review when the harm was merely not prevented; it has asked us for an opinion. One faction argues that, given so clear a counterfactual, failing to escalate simply is causing the death; the other argues that only the sepsis caused the death, since nothing was set in motion by the nurse's inaction, and her omission counts only as a failure to prevent a death that had another cause. Please draft the short opinion I will bring to Thursday's meeting: recommend which track applies here, state a general principle the committee can use for future omission cases, and engage honestly with the strongest argument for the side you reject.
