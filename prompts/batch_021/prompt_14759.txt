I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and next month I'm interviewing an epistemologist who defends conciliationism about peer disagreement: the view that a well-informed colleague's disagreement should pull your confidence toward theirs, rather than leaving you free to simply hold your ground. The piece is about why equally credentialed experts can study the same data and end up confidently, permanently opposed, so I want questions that go beyond a restatement of the view. Please draft eight interview questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, including at least one on how genuine "peerhood" is supposed to be established when the track records that would settle it are themselves usually contested; one on the self-undermining worry that, since conciliationism is itself controversial among epistemologists, a consistent conciliationist should presumably have already lowered her confidence in it; and one that uses Aumann's agreement theorem -- that rational agents sharing a common prior can't knowingly agree to disagree -- to ask why so much real expert disagreement persists if her view is correct.
