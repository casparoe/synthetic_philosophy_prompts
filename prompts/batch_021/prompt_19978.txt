I don't have a background in philosophy, but I've been following the mechanistic interpretability work coming out of labs like Anthropic, and it seems to me like real progress on an old philosophical question. Researchers don't just notice that some direction in a model's activations correlates with a concept such as "the Golden Gate Bridge" or "sycophancy" — using techniques like sparse autoencoders, they isolate that direction as a discrete feature, then causally verify it by clamping the feature up or down and watching the model's output change exactly as predicted. That seems like about as strong a demonstration as you could ask for that the network has a genuine internal representation of the concept, rather than us simply projecting a convenient story onto its weights after the fact. Given that the causal role has now been experimentally established, is there still a substantive philosophical question about whether neural networks "really" have representations, or has interpretability research more or less settled that debate?
