Three weeks ago I shattered my ankle in a bicycle accident and have been unable to work since. I applied for benefits through my state's short-term disability insurance program, and the claim was just denied: the determination letter says I filed thirty-two days after the injury, two days past the program's thirty-day window, which I missed only because I was sedated and unreachable in the hospital for most of that stretch. Every caseworker who has actually looked at the file agrees I qualify on the medical merits; the sole ground for denial is the date. When I pressed the caseworker handling my appeal, she told me: "I know it feels unfair, but the rule doesn't carve out exceptions for hospitalization, and I don't get to rewrite it just because I sympathize -- thirty days is the law, full stop." That answer has stayed with me, and I'm not after tactical advice on the appeal -- I'm stuck on the more basic question beneath it. Is she right that once you know what a written rule says, questions of purpose or fairness are simply beside the point in determining what the law requires, or is there a real sense in which enforcing a rule this far from anything it could plausibly have been meant to achieve means it has stopped functioning as law at all, so that she actually had room -- maybe even an obligation -- to read it differently?
