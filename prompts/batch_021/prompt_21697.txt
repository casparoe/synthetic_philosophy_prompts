Over dinner last week, a friend and I found ourselves in a long-running argument about my cousin's recent diagnosis of complex PTSD. I maintained that Ian Hacking's account of looping effects in the human sciences effectively vindicates Thomas Szasz's old claim that mental illness is a kind of myth: if a diagnostic category shifts as the people classified under it absorb the label, revise their self-understanding and behavior accordingly, and thereby prompt clinicians to redraw the diagnostic criteria in turn, then surely the category cannot be tracking any independent, objective condition—it is simply a moving target that clinician and patient jointly construct. My friend was convinced I was flattening some important distinction in Hacking's actual position, but neither of us could say exactly what it was. Am I right that looping effects undercut the objectivity of a diagnosis like this one, or is there a difference between Hacking's view and Szasz's that I'm collapsing?
