I'm putting together the natural theology unit for my philosophy of religion seminar, and something that never really comes up in the standard textbook treatment has been bugging me: kalam people and Leibnizian/contingency people are not actually running the same argument, or even really on the same team, even though intro anthologies lump them together under "the cosmological argument." Craig is pretty upfront that he doesn't want to lean on a full-strength Principle of Sufficient Reason the way Pruss (or Leibniz, or Samuel Clarke) does, because he thinks that premise is exactly what gets you into trouble -- modal collapse, or the old Schopenhauer complaint about riding PSR like a taxicab to get the universe explained and then hopping out right when it would also demand an explanation of God. So he'd rather run on the much narrower "whatever begins to exist has a cause," plus the arguments against actual infinites. But as I understand it, the PSR crowd has a decent comeback: that restricted causal principle looks arbitrary -- why should only things that begin to exist need an explanation, and not everything contingent? -- and they think full PSR can survive the modal collapse worry anyway if the explanation on offer is a free choice rather than a necessitating cause.

Could you write me a dialogue between two serious, equally sharp theistic philosophers -- one running the cosmological argument via kalam, the other via the Leibnizian contingency version -- arguing about which strategy is actually more defensible, not about whether God exists (they both already think he does, they just dont trust each others route to that conclusion)? Call them whatever you like, doesn't matter. I want real back-and-forth: modal collapse and the libertarian-free-will response to it, the taxicab worry, whether kalam's restricted causal principle is non-arbitrary, and the Hilbert's Hotel stuff about actual infinites if it fits naturally in there. Neither one should fold easily or misread what the other is saying -- I want them landing real punches and the other one having a genuine comeback, the way it'd go between two people who've each spent a decade on this and just disagree about where the weak joint in the other's armor is. Something like a page's worth of exchanges is probably about right.
