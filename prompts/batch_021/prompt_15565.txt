I've noticed something about the way I perceive people that I can't quite shake, and I've been turning it over on my own for a while now, so I'd like to see two sharp thinkers really fight it out. When I go into a conversation already braced for someone to be cold or dismissive with me - even in cases where, looking back, I don't think I had any solid reason to expect that - I don't just later judge that they seemed a bit off. In the moment, their face and tone genuinely look and sound hostile to me, in a way a friend standing next to me, who didn't share my worry, would say wasn't really there at all. Something similar happens with ambiguous situations at night: a coat slung over a chair can visibly look like a crouching animal until the light changes, not because I reason my way there, but because that is just how it looks to me. The more I think about it, the more it seems like my beliefs and fears aren't only judging my perception after the fact - they seem to be reaching inside it and changing what I actually experience seeing and hearing in the first place.

Here is what I can't settle for myself. If a prior belief or fear, even a fairly groundless one, can reach into perception itself and shape how something looks or sounds, does that make the resulting experience worse evidence than an "untouched" experience would have been? Or does none of this matter, since from the inside a "contaminated" experience and a clean one can feel completely identical, and I have no way of telling, in the moment, which kind I am having? A friend of mine insists it must be the second option, since whether an experience justifies a belief has to be something you can tell from where you are standing, not some fact about hidden causes you have no access to. I am not convinced, because that seems to prove too much: it would mean that a conclusion you reached by secretly smuggling in the answer you wanted as a hidden premise is just as justified as one you reached honestly, provided both feel equally compelling from inside your own head.

I would like you to write a dialogue between two people who take opposite sides on this question, ideally picking up more or less where my friend and I left off. One should argue that when a perceptual experience is shaped in this way by a prior belief or emotion, this can rob it of some or all of its power to justify whatever belief you go on to form from it. The other should argue that an experience justifies believing the world is as it presents it purely because of how it presents the world, regardless of any hidden history behind it. Please have them genuinely respond to each other's strongest moves rather than just restate their opening positions - in particular, I would like to see how each of them handles the "ordinary" cases where background knowledge obviously shapes what we perceive without anyone finding it suspicious, such as recognizing a friend's face at a distance or hearing words instead of noise in a language you know well, since it is not obvious to me that these are different in kind from the more troubling cases above.
