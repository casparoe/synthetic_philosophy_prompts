My grandfather turned eighty last month, and at the dinner my family threw for him, he ended up being far more candid than usual about how he actually feels about it. He said that since he retired from his firm three years ago, he doesn't feel like anything he does really matters day to day; that his hip and his eyesight have declined enough that he had to give up the woodworking and hiking that used to fill his weekends; that his cardiologist has him on such a restricted diet that the meals and occasional glass of wine he genuinely used to enjoy don't do much for him anymore; and that, underneath all of it, turning eighty mostly just makes him feel like he's running out of road. My aunts jumped in immediately with the usual "eighty is the new sixty" and "you have so much to be grateful for," and I could tell from his face that none of it actually landed; it just moved the conversation along.

I've been turning his four complaints over in my head ever since. This happened right as I was finishing a personal-essay assignment for English, and I almost wrote about the dinner, but I scrapped it because I couldn't figure out what I actually thought beyond feeling like my aunts' answer was too easy. My hunch is that these four things aren't really one complaint wearing four different masks, and that they might call for genuinely different kinds of responses rather than one big pep talk. Could you help me work through them one at a time? For each of the four, I would like to know whether there is a real argument to be made against it, or whether it is simply true and the more honest response is to change how much it should matter to him rather than to deny it.
