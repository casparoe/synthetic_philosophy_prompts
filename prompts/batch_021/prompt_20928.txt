Popper tried to explain how science can progress even though our best theories keep turning out to be false, by defining 'verisimilitude': roughly, theory A counts as closer to the truth than theory B if A entails all of B's true consequences (maybe more) while not entailing any false consequences that B doesn't already have. Turns out that once you actually try to apply this to two false theories -- which is the only case that matters, since every real theory is at least a little false -- the definition runs into a nasty technical problem. Can you construct a small toy example (a couple of made up claims about some simple scenario, doesnt need to be real science) where this way of comparing verisimilitude gives an obviously wrong verdict -- like rating the clearly worse theory as at least as close to the truth, or just refusing to rank them at all even though intuitively one clearly wins?
