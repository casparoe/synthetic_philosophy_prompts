I'm a PhD student in philosophy of physics, and for a seminar on the ontology of spacetime I've spent the last couple of weeks working through Earman and Norton's original presentation of the hole argument, along with the main responses it provoked. I can follow the formal machinery well enough: general relativity is diffeomorphism invariant, so if (M, g_ab) solves the field equations and d is a diffeomorphism of M that acts as the identity everywhere outside some vacuum "hole" region H but smoothly departs from the identity inside it, then (M, d*g_ab) is a distinct solution that agrees with the original everywhere outside H, disagrees inside it, and is compatible with exactly the same matter distribution throughout. What I can't yet produce on my own is the gut-level sense of why this is supposed to be a genuine metaphysical problem rather than a curiosity about our descriptive apparatus.

Two things are tripping me up here, and my cohort disagrees with me about both. First, my instinct says that of course you can push the fields around by a diffeomorphism and get "the same" spacetime back out, in the way that relabeling coordinates never changes anything physical — so I don't see why the argument isn't defused the instant someone points out that this is "merely" a diffeomorphism, exactly as a passive coordinate transformation would be. Second, one person in my seminar insists the whole worry dissolves once you notice that general relativity is "just" a gauge theory, no different in kind from electromagnetism, and nobody thinks the gauge freedom of the vector potential casts doubt on the reality of the electromagnetic field — so why should diffeomorphism freedom cast any special doubt on the reality of spacetime points, rather than being run-of-the-mill gauge redundancy that a substantivalist can shrug off exactly as an electromagnetic realist does?

Could you talk me through this in a way that actually earns its keep against both worries: why the "active" reading of the diffeomorphism is doing work that a mere relabeling wouldn't do, and why the resulting problem lands specifically on the manifold substantivalist while leaving the relationalist untouched? And if you have room for it, I would love your take on whether "sophisticated substantivalism" — accepting Leibniz equivalence while denying that spacetime points have any primitive, model-independent identity — is a genuine way of keeping substantivalism about spacetime while conceding everything the argument demands, or whether it is really just relationalism in substantivalist's clothing. I would rather come away with the intuition that would satisfy a stubborn substantivalist across a seminar table than the gloss I could already get from the SEP article.
