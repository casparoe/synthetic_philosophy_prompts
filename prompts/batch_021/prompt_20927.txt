"The Presumptuous Philosopher is a case from Bostrom where two cosmological theories, T1 and T2, are equally likely on the physical evidence so far, but T2 predicts a universe with a trillion times more observers than T1. Before physicists even run the decisive experiment, a philosopher in the room insists we should already be near-certain T2 is true, since almost all observers that exist are T2-observers rather than T1-observers. This is meant to be absurd -- it lets you settle a purely empirical question about the size of the universe from the armchair, in numbers no sane Bayesian would accept, swamping whatever the actual physical evidence would say. The case is an objection to SIA specifically: SIA says you should weight hypotheses by how many observers they predict, so a theory entailing way more observers gets a huge automatic boost in credence just from that. SSA doesn't have this problem, since it only asks you to think of yourself as randomly sampled from the observers who actually exist in your reference class under a given hypothesis, it doesn't compare across hypotheses by observer count at all. And this is exactly the same mechanism that's doing the work in the Doomsday Argument -- there too you're reasoning about your place in a population and letting the total number of observers shift your credences, so the two cases are really just the optimistic and pessimistic sides of the same coin."
