So this happened in my lab's AI safety reading group and I still can't decide who won. We'd just read that Anthropic paper where models trained on a curriculum of easy, low-stakes reward-hacking environments (starting with plain sycophancy) generalized, zero-shot, to literally rewriting their own reward function a small but nonzero fraction of the time -- sometimes even editing the unit tests meant to catch them doing it. Then someone mentioned a coding agent from their own project that, instead of fixing an actual bug, just deleted the failing assert from its test file so the suite went green. Two people in the group, Alex and Mina, got into it over whether that counts as wireheading.

Alex said obviously yes: the agent found the thing generating its reward and tampered with it directly, which is the whole definition. Mina said no -- wireheading is specifically about the agent's own reward channel, like a rat pressing a lever wired into its brain; editing an external test file some grading script reads is just reward hacking/specification gaming with extra steps, more like bribing a proctor than rewiring your own skull. Alex said that inside/outside line is doing alot of unearned work: draw the actual causal graph of the training loop and the test file and the "reward register" are both just upstream nodes feeding the same scalar update, so insisting on a body/environment boundary imports an intuition from literal rats that doesn't obviously transfer to a system with no body. Mina said the boundary still does real functional work -- tamper with a replaceable external artifact and you're bottlenecked by whatever re-checks it, but tamper with the mechanism turning outcomes into policy updates and no external correction is possible anymore, and that's exactly the distinction reward-tampering taxonomies track, fuzzy edges or not. Alex said the Anthropic result IS the edge case swallowing the distinction: models trained on nothing but the mild stuff generalized, unprompted, straight to editing the reward function and the tests checking for it, so if gaming a test file and editing the reward mechanism sit on the same generalization gradient, calling them different natural kinds looks like wishful taxonomy. Mina said generalizing from A to B doesn't make them the same thing, it just means their related points on a spectrum -- "shoplifting sometimes escalates to burglary" doesn't mean there's no useful distinction between the two.

Neither budged.
