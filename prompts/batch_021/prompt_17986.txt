I've been down a rabbit hole of decision theory blogs about "acausal" threats lately -- the idea that some future AI or alien civilization could threaten to simulate and hurt you (or copies of you) to make you comply now, even though you never actually interact with them causally. One defense that keeps coming up is "surrogate goals": you deliberately make yourself care about some cheap made-up thing, so when you get threatened you can "give in" by sacrificing the fake thing instead of what you actually value, and supposedly the threatener gets nothing real out of it so never bothers threatening you in the first place. Honestly I can't tell if that's genuinely clever or just a shell game that falls apart once the threatener is smart enough to see through it -- like, if you've fully rewired yourself to act in every way as if you value the fake goal, in what sense is it still fake and not just a new real vulnerability? And wouldn't a sufficiently smart threatener just target the actual thing you care about anyway, or punish you extra for trying to dodge them?
