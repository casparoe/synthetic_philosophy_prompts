I know a couple, neither of them a philosopher, who have had a private ritual since they began dating: on long drives, one calls out two numbers and the other instantly gives back their "score" under a rule the two of them worked out together years ago, purely by trial and error. They have never once put the rule into words, and until last weekend, every pair of numbers they had tried was smaller than a hundred.

Last weekend Elena called out two four-digit numbers, and Marcus's answer wasn't the one she expected. She said that's just not the rule -- anyone who'd watched them play for years could tell him what the rule actually is. He said every round they've actually played, hundreds of them by now, fits his answer exactly as well as it fits hers, so nothing in their whole shared history makes her answer correct and his a mistake. She said that's ridiculous, since she knows what she meant by the rule this whole time, even for numbers they'd never tried before. He said whatever she "meant" has to be some finite thing -- a formula, an image, a feeling -- and he can describe a rule that matches that same finite thing exactly, yet disagrees with her once the numbers reach four digits; besides, if she wants to say it's really about what she's disposed to answer, dispositions are finite and can misfire, so she still hasn't explained why her disposition should count as correct and his as an error, rather than just a different rule. She said fine: ask her sister, who has watched them play for a decade and will back her up.

Go through this exchange move by move, saying who has the better of the argument at each turn, and note anything significant that both Elena and Marcus are overlooking.
