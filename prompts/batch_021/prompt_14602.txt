I'm a retired chemist, and since hanging up my lab coat I've gotten pretty into philosophy in a self-taught kind of way. Lately I've been rereading Franklins autobiography, especially the bit where he tracks 13 virtues on a little chart, one virtue as the weekly focus, a black mark for every slip -- total scientist move, treating his own character like a lab notebook. Funny thing is he admits he never really licked Order (something about a speckled axe and being "incorrigible" even in old age), and Pride kept creeping back too, to the point he jokes that even conquering it would just leave him proud of his own humility. Anyway, this got me thinking about all that situationist psychology from the 20th century -- Darley and Batson's seminary students who stepped past a guy in distress because they were running late, regardless of whether they'd just been assigned to talk on the Good Samaritan parable, plus John Doris and that whole argument that stable "character" mostly doesn't survive contact with real situations. What do you reckon Franklin, ever the experimentalist, would've made of that whole reserach program? Does his own decades of relapsing into the same faults hand the situationists a data point, or is there something about his method -- the charts, the weekly rotation, the whole approach -- that actually gives him a decent comeback?
