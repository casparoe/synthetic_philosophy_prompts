My mom's turning 80 next month, and since she taught Latin for thirty five years she's already vetoed the standard "age is just a number" toast. What she actually loves, and has made basically the whole family read at some point, is Cicero's De Senectute -- especially how Cato doesn't dodge the four classic complaints against old age (you can't be active anymore, the body's weaker, the pleasures are gone, death is close) but takes each one on directly and answers it. I got asked to give the toast, probably because I actually teach philosophy for a living, even though Cicero specifically isn't really my period. About half the room is her old students and collegues who'll clock exactly what I'm doing if I lean on Cicero, the other half is just family who wants cake and has no idea who Cato even is. Can you write me something around 4 minutes long that actually uses Cicero's four part structure -- including his answer to the death one, without it getting morbid -- to say something specific and true about her, and not just a classics lecture dressed up as a toast?
