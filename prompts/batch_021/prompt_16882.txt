Ok, random question thats been bugging me for a book I'm working on. My main character, Elena, has been blind since birth, and about two thirds through the novel she gets an experimental treatment that gives her sight for the first time, in her thirties. In the scene I want to write, someone sets a wooden cube and a wooden ball on the table in front of her (both objects shes handled by touch a thousand times) and I want to know if she'd be able to tell which is which just by looking, no touching allowed. My gut says obviously yes, a shape's a shape whether you see it or feel it, but a friend from my writing group insists this is actually a genuinely open question people have argued about for centuries and that the real answer is weirder than it sounds. Who's actually right, and however it shakes out, how should I write that moment so it feels true to life instead of just convenient for the plot?
