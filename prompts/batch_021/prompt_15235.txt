I'm writing a feature on the biohacking and longevity-optimization scene — the Bryan Johnson-style regimens built on continuous glucose monitors, timed fasting windows, gut-microbiome panels, and supplement stacks calibrated to blood-test data — and I want a section that uses Nietzsche as an unexpected foil, since most popular invocations of him stop at "will to power" as a slogan. He was actually one of philosophy's great dietary obsessives: chronically ill for most of his adult life, he argued in Ecce Homo's "Why I Am So Clever" that nutrition, climate, and recreation mattered more than any question of God, soul, or virtue, savaged German and English cooking as stupefying to the intellect, and in Twilight of the Idols used Luigi Cornaro's famously austere diet as his go-to example of confusing a rare metabolic idiosyncrasy with a universal law of health. Working from that material rather than his more quotable metaphysics, how do you think he would judge today's self-tracked optimization culture: a genuine, disciplined technology of self-overcoming in his own idiom, or decadence and priestly asceticism wearing a lab coat? Give me an actual verdict rather than a list of pros and cons, and tell me which of his own arguments a committed biohacker could most plausibly turn back against you.
