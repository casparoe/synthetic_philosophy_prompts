My mother died a little over a year ago, and I still think about her every day, cry more often than I would like to admit, and have not managed to move or give away most of her belongings. My therapist recently suggested, gently, that under the updated clinical guidelines therapists now use, this might qualify as a distinct disorder rather than ordinary grief, mainly because it has lasted so long and has begun to affect my sleep and work. Part of me finds that almost comforting, since it would explain why I have not simply moved on; but another part resists it, because my family's mourning customs run considerably longer than whatever counts as typical here, and it unsettles me that a fairly specific cutoff could be the difference between a normal, if painful, way of continuing to love someone and a mental illness. Is there really a medically discoverable line between ordinary grief and a disordered version of it, or does any such line always rest on somebody's judgment about how long, and in what way, it is acceptable to keep grieving? I would like to think this through clearly before my next appointment.
