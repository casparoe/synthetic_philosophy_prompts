I'm the only engineer on my company's dual-use technology review board, which vets licensing requests for our target-verification system: software that flags whether a proposed strike target meets the criteria for a lawful military objective, mainly to cut down on civilian misidentification. We've received a request from a foreign air force whose current bombing campaign most outside legal scholars call a war of aggression, though the government insists it's self-defense and the UN Security Council is deadlocked on the question. A group of engineers wants the board to reject the deal outright, arguing that precise targeting doesn't make it acceptable to help fight an unjust war. Legal and business development counter that our policy only withholds tools from buyers who violate the laws of war in the field, and that whether the war itself is justified isn't something a vendor is equipped to adjudicate. Please draft the memo I'll circulate before next week's vote: a recommendation -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions -- with a concrete timeframe, and reasoning the objecting engineers could actually respond to, rather than just restating that how a war is fought and whether it should be fought are traditionally treated as separate questions.
