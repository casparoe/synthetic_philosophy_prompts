Random thought from grading medieval philosophy papers today: Avicenna's flying man argument obviously predates computers by about a millennium, but I keep wondering what he'd make of mind-uploading or teleporter thought experiments (the Parfit/sci-fi kind - scan your brain, destroy the original, reconstruct an atom-for-atom copy somewhere else). Given both (a) the flying man's suggestion that we're immediately aware of our own selfhood quite apart from any awareness of our body, and (b) his less famous but IMO more interesting view that individual souls only become particular, individual souls in the first place because of their original attachment to a specific body - what do you think Avicenna would actually say about whether the person who steps out of the teleporter is "you," or just a very convincing new soul that thinks its you? Don't just tell me "he's a substance dualist so obviously he'd say no" - walk me through how the individuation story specifically bears on the case, and flag it if you think his own principles might actually cut against the intuitive answer somewhere.
