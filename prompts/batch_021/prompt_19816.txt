Ok, this has been bugging me for a while and I don't think I've seen anyone tackle it head-on. Whenever anthropic reasoning comes up in Bostrom's work or on LW, people invoke "the reference class problem" for the Self-Sampling Assumption -- in the Doomsday Argument, are you a random sample from "all humans who will ever live," "all observers," "all observers subjectively indistinguishable from you," etc, and the doom probability you get shifts a lot depending on which one you pick. I always figured this was some bespoke headache Bostrom cooked up just for anthropics, until I learned "reference class problem" is actually a much older term from frequentist probability (Reichenbach and that whole tradition) -- the problem that any single case belongs to indefinitely many reference classes (a 50-year-old, a 50-year-old smoker, a 50-year-old smoker with your exact medical history...), each giving a different frequency, with no principled place to stop before the class shrinks down to just you.

So: is Bostrom's version basically Reichenbach's old problem of the single case wearing a new outfit, or a geniunely different puzzle that just happens to have borrowed the name? Compare the two properly -- what's actually structurally shared, what isn't -- and defend a verdict. "Bit of both" is a cop-out unless you can say exactly which parts carry over and which don't.
