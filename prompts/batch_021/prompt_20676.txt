My grandfather served two tours as an infantryman in Vietnam and died last month at seventy-seven without ever accepting that the men he had killed there made him a murderer, even though he had long since concluded that the war itself was unjust. His own reasoning, which I only recognized as a real position in just war theory after I started my PhD, was that soldiers who fight within the laws of war answer only for their own conduct, not for the justice of the cause -- what philosophers call the moral equality of combatants -- so that combatants on both sides are equals regardless of who is actually right. He left instructions that his eulogy call him neither a hero, since he never believed the cause deserved that word, nor a man with anything to apologize for. I am giving that eulogy. Half the room will be his old unit and hometown friends, who want him honored and have no patience for philosophy; the other half will be my own philosophy department, several of whom work on the ethics of war and reject the very position he held. Please write it: it needs to comfort everyone present without saying anything he would have considered false, and without either lionizing the war or quietly implying that he was complicit in a crime.
