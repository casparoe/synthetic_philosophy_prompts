So I've been reading Plato's Laws for a small reading group (we usually just stick to the Republic and the Symposium, so this is new territory for us), and I got kind of obsessed with how much space the Athenian Stranger spends, across Books 1 and 2, defending regulated communal drinking as an actual tool of moral education -- wine as a controlled way of stripping off people's inhibitions so a sober supervisor can see, and correct, their real character, and later this whole idea that Dionysus's gift of wine is precisely what lets older men loosen up enough to still be reshaped through choral song and dance. Its such a weird contrast with the Republic, where Socrates won't even let the guardians hear stories about gods and heroes losing control -- getting drunk, laughing uncontrollably, that sort of thing -- and throws out the "soft" musical modes associated with drinking parties altogether.

I also help run a tiny philosophy podcast on the side, and we've got a classicist booked for next month who just published a book claiming this isn't some throwaway bit of local color -- that Plato's pro-symposium argument in the Laws is a serious, load-bearing piece of his moral psychology, continuous with everything else he says about virtue and self-mastery. Can you draft me 8-10 interview questions (with a short note on the reply she'll most likely give, and a sharper follow-up to that reply) that really lean on the weak joints of her reading -- things like the seeming circularity of using an inhibition-lowering substance to train inhibition, the fact that the whole scheme depends on an already-virtuous sober supervisor just showing up from nowhere, and whether this is really Plato's considered view or just a dialectical move aimed at his rather teetotal Spartan and Cretan interlocutors. I'd like it to build to one last question that makes her confront the double standard head-on: Plato is so worried about the corrupting power of mere fictional depictions of vice that he wants to censor the poets, so why's he fine legislating actual, literal intoxication as long as a sober minder is watching?
