I sit on the incident review board at work -- we're a mid-size e-commerce company. We had a nasty one during a flash sale: checkout was down for about 40 minutes, and it turned out two unrelated things were happening at once -- the platform team had pushed a config change to the db connection pool that would've exhausted it under that day's traffic all by itself, and separately, a retry bug in a feature team's client library was hammering that same pool hard enough to exhaust it on its own too. We replayed that day's traffic in staging with each change reverted one at a time and confirmed either alone would've taken checkout down. Our postmortem template's root-cause test is basically 'would this still have happened if X hadn't occured,' and honestly the answer's yes for both -- so neither team clears the bar for root cause under our own policy, which matters because whoever's tagged owns the fix items and eats the error-budget hit for the quarter. Now both teams are pointing at this to say they're in the clear since 'it would've happened regardless of us,' and the board's split down the middle: half think that's a fair reading of our own rules, half think it's obviously letting two teams who both actually contributed off the hook on a technicality. A couple of us floated asking whether each factor was actually doing something at the time instead of whether it was strictly necessary, but couldn't turn that into a rule anyone trusted. Draft the memo the board should send recommending how we handle root-cause attribution in cases like this going forward -- I want something we could actually apply next time, not 'use your best judgement,' and it needs to take the 'it wasn't actually necessary so it's unfair to blame us' argument seriously instead of just brushing past it.
