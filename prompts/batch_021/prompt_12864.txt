ok this has been bugging me since i reread the "modest epistemology" bits of Inadequate Equilibria. Yudkowsky's whole move is basically: most people who defer to consensus because "who am I to disagree" are doing bad epistemics, and if you've got a decent track record you're licensed to just stick to your guns. But almost every example he uses is really an expert-vs-layman case, not an actual peer case -- symmetric evidence, symmetric competance, no track-record asymmetry to appeal to.

Can you argue that the equal-weight view (conciliationism) survives this "trust your track record" style objection in genuine peer disagreements -- think something structurally like Christensen's restaurant-bill-splitting case, not "should I trust my own physics over the mainstream." I also want you to engage with the self-undermining worry: conciliationism says disagreement is evidence, so what happens once you notice conciliationism itself is hotly disputed among careful, smart epistemologists? Don't just give me the standard LW "outside view good, respect the wisdom of crowds" 101 -- I've read enough of those posts already, I want the strongest version of the argument.
