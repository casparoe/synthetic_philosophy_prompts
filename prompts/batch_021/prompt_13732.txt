I've been down an imprecise-probabilities rabbit hole lately (partly bc of all the Knightian uncertainty / infra-Bayesianism chat floating around LW), and I want a dialogue that actually engages with the hard parts instead of just vibing about "well, some things you just can't put a number on." Write a dialogue, set at the bar after a rationalist meetup, between a strict "sharp" Bayesian who thinks every credence has to cash out as a single precise number, and an imprecise-probability theorist who represents beliefs as a set of distributions (a credal set) instead. Have the sharp Bayesian press Elga's sequential-bets arguement on them -- the one where an agent with a mushy credence can, under a seemingly reasonable decision rule, turn down two bets offered one after another that, taken together, would've guaranteed her a better outcome than what rejecting both leaves her with -- basically a diachronic dutch book. The imprecise theorist shouldn't just fold -- give them a real counter, like disputing the decision rule Elga assumes, or biting the bullet in some principled way. Then have the sharp Bayesian bring up dilation too: conditioning on new evidence can make a credal set MORE spread out no matter what you end up learning, which seems to make imprecise updating look kind of unprincipled. I want genuine back and forth where each person responds to the other's specific move rather than just restating their opening position, and neither side should be an obvious strawman.
