"In the classic experiments, an image is flashed to only one visual field at a time, so it only reaches one hemisphere. Because only the left hemisphere can usually produce speech, a split-brain patient can verbally report an object flashed to the right visual field but will deny seeing anything flashed to the left visual field — even though their left hand, controlled by the right hemisphere, can correctly pick the object out by touch. This shows that once the callosum is cut, the two hemispheres have seperate, independent streams of consciousness, which is obviously a problem for the idea that a person has just one unified stream of experience at a time.
