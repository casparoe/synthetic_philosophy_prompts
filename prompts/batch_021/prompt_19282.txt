So I'm an econ PhD student on the game theory side, and this went down in a reading group I'm part of with a few philosophy grad students, right after we covered Aumann's argument that common knowledge of rationality (CKR) forces backward induction in games like the centipede game. Two people in the group got into a real argument about it afterward that never got resolved, and I still can't decide who actually had the better of it at each step.

(1) He said the result is airtight: if everyone's rational, everyone knows everyone's rational, everyone knows that they know it, and so on forever, then every player should take/defect at their very first move in the centipede game, full stop -- so when actual people cooperate for a few rounds in experiments, that just shows real humans lack CKR, not that anything's off with the argument itself.

(2) She said thats too quick, because the whole backward-induction chain works by reasoning about what a rational player would do at later nodes that, by the theory's own lights, should never be reached if everyone was rational from the start -- so once you're actually sitting at round 5 because your opponent didn't take earlier, why should you still be entitled to assume CKR holds for the rest of the game? The argument for taking at move 1 quietly needs that assumption to go through.

(3) He replied that she's misreading the logic -- those later nodes don't need to ever be reached for the reasoning to work, it's just a chain of counterfactuals, like steps in a reductio, and that chain alone is what pins down the unique rational move at node 1.

(4) She said fine, it's counterfactual, but each hypothetical step still needs CKR to hold at that hypothetical node too, and there's no independant reason to think it would, precisely because you only get to that node if someone already deviated from what CKR predicted -- so either CKR is just stipulated to survive anything (which feels like begging the question) or the chain doesn't actually pin down a single answer the way he thinks it does.

Neither of them budged after that. Can you go through moves (1) to (4) and say who actually had the better of it at each step -- not just an overall winner -- and flag anything you think both of them are still missing?
