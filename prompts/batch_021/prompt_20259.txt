Having spent my career in experimental physics before retiring, I have never been able to read about the Manhattan Project without also wanting to understand its moral reckoning, and that curiosity eventually led me into philosophy and, more recently, into hosting a small podcast on ethics and the history of ideas. Next week's guest is a moral philosopher who defends the charge that Elizabeth Anscombe pressed in her 1956 pamphlet "Mr Truman's Degree" -- written to oppose Oxford's decision to award Truman an honorary degree -- namely, that the President was a murderer for ordering the bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond inviting him to simply restate that charge. I want at least one question on how the doctrine of double effect is supposed to establish that the killing of civilians was intended rather than merely foreseen in this case, given that a critic could just as well redescribe the target as "Japan's capacity to make war"; at least one on why the prohibition she invokes is meant to admit no exceptions at all, rather than being a very heavy consideration that a sufficiently lopsided body count could still outweigh -- exactly the style of reasoning she later coined the term "consequentialism" to describe and condemn; and at least one that tests the argument against Michael Walzer's "supreme emergency" exception, noting that Walzer himself argues the emergency conditions had already lapsed by August 1945. For each of these, add a brief follow-up keyed to the reply the guest is most likely to give.
