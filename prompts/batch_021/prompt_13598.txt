Okay so this has sort of taken over my brain for the past couple weeks. I work on a small team building negotiation agents -- basically pairs of LLM instances that hash out contract terms with each other (sometimes two copies of the same model, sometimes ours vs. a partner company's) before a human signs off on anything. My manager's latest pitch is to let the two agents read each others full system prompt before they negotiate, the idea being that mutual transparency should nudge them toward the cooperative split instead of the cagey, defect-by-default one. A guy on my team offhandedly called this "basically program equilibrium," which sent me down a rabbit hole into Tennenholtz's original setup of agents submitting programs that can inspect each other, and then into the fancier Lob's-theorem version where something like FairBot (and its more careful cousin PrudentBot) cooperates with you iff it can actually prove you'll cooperate back, so two FairBots end up guaranteed to cooperate with each other. But I also remembered, from a philosophy of mind elective ages ago, Hofstadter's old idea of "superrationality" -- that two sufficiently rational agents in a symmetric dilemma should each reason their way to cooperating just from the symmetry of the situation, no source-code-reading required.

Now I can't tell if these are basically the same insight wearing two outfits, or if program equilibrium is actually doing something superrationality only ever gestured at (or the other way round). Could you properly compare the two for me? I want to know what each one actually requires of the agents involved -- mutual transparency, common knowledge of rationality, something else -- what kind of real guarantee against defection each one gives you, and a case where they'd come apart: an agent that would count as superrational but wouldn't be in program equilibrium with its counterpart, or vice versa (my same-model-vs-cross-company split above might already be hinting at one). And then give me an actual verdict, not a "well, it depends": does the program-equilibrium/Lobian-cooperation stuff basically formalize and vindicate what Hofstadter was gesturing at, or does something philosophically important get lost, or added, once you cash it out that way?
