A friend and I have kept an informal arrangement running for several weeks now, built around a small shared pot of money that started with a friendly bet: at the start of each week, whichever one of us holds the option can either cash out the pot immediately, keeping it all and ending the arrangement, or add a fixed amount to it and hand the option to the other person for the following week. If we ever reach the tenth week without either of us cashing out, the pot is simply split evenly between us; but if I pass and my friend then cashes out the moment the option becomes theirs, I get nothing at all. We are now in week eight, and the option is currently mine. Working through the sequence from week ten back to today, cashing out looks like the better move at every single week, including this one, since my friend, reasoning the same way, would otherwise cash out the instant they had the chance. Yet neither of us has done this so far, and doing it now, on the strength of that reasoning alone, feels less like a smart decision than a betrayal of the trust that has gotten us this far. Is the reasoning actually correct, and if so, why does acting on it feel so wrong?
