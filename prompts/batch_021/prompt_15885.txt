So I'm in a jurisprudence seminar and last week we spent a session on Bentham, and the prof mentioned almost in passing that he wrote this whole unpublished manuscript, apparently starting in the 1780s, arguing for decriminalizing sex between men on strictly utilitarian grounds -- his term for it was "paederasty" but back then that word basically just meant consensual sex between men, not the modern narrower meaning. His central move was that the laws against it weren't tracking any actual harm, just "antipathy" -- basically disgust dressed up as if it were a harm argument -- and since the felicific calculus only cares about pleasure and pain to actual sentient beings, disgust alone can't justify punishing something that makes people happy and hurts no one. He was apparently so nervous about the backlash that he never published it in his lifetime, and it only really got out into the world something like two centuries later. Kinda wild for a guy mostly known for the Panopticon and "greatest happiness" one liners.
