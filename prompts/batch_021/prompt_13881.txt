This semester I'm taking a seminar on the metaphysics of facts and propositions, and this week's session on Bradley's regress left me wanting to think through something we didn't have time to settle in class. We'd read Bradley's chapter on relation and quality in Appearance and Reality alongside excerpts from Frege's "On Concept and Object" and Armstrong's A World of States of Affairs, and our professor kept lining the two responses up against each other: Frege blocks the regress by making concepts inherently "unsaturated," so that no further relation is needed to glue a concept to an object, while Armstrong blocks it by holding that a state of affairs is unified through "non-mereological composition" -- a brute fact about the whole that isn't built up out of its constituents plus some connecting relation. Set side by side like that, the two moves start to look interchangeable, as though each side has simply relocated the same primitive unity and given it a different name, and I'd like to see whether that impression survives actual argument.

Could you write me a dialogue between a committed Fregean and a committed Armstrongian, each trying to convince the other that only their own solution really stops the regress? I'd like the Armstrongian to open by pressing the concept horse problem -- that the moment you try to say what unsaturatedness amounts to, you've named the concept and thereby turned it into an object, so the doctrine seems to undercut itself as soon as it's stated -- and I want the Fregean to have a real answer to this rather than concede the point outright. I'd then like the Fregean to go on the offensive by asking how a state of affairs, which by Armstrong's own account exists only when the corresponding proposition is true, can explain the unity of a proposition that is false or merely being entertained, given that there's no state of affairs there to do any unifying at all. Please have each side engage directly with the other's last move rather than simply restate their own position, keep both equally sharp, and end with that last question still open rather than have either side concede.
