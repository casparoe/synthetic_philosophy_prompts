So this argument happened at a friend's place last weekend and its kind of been stuck in my head since. Quick context: my friend Jen has one of those health-insurance wellness apps that knocks a bit off her premium if she logs stuff like exercise, sleep, and a short "three things you're grateful for today" entry -- and lately she's been forcing herself to write grateful entries about her landlord, who she actually finds pretty exasperating and who hasn't done anything notable for her, purely because the discount adds up over a year. She mentioned this pretty casually at dinner and two other friends of ours, Marco and Sofia, ended up going back and forth on it for like twenty minutes while the rest of us just watched.

(1) Marco said there's nothing wrong with what Jen's doing: if she keeps it up and genuinely ends up feeling grateful toward the landlord, her gratitude is just as real as anyone else's, because a feeling doesn't care what caused it. (2) Sofia said that's the problem -- the whole point of gratitude is to track whether someone actually did something for you, and a premium discount has nothing to do with the landlord, so whatever Jen cultivates isn't really gratitude toward him, no matter how sincere it ends up feeling. (3) Marco pushed back that this seems too strict, since we nudge people into virtues for impure reasons all the time -- we bribe toddlers into sharing, we bribe employees into showing up on time -- and nobody says the resulting virtue is fake once it takes hold. (4) Sofia said sharing and punctuality are just behaviors, so bribing your way into them is fine, but gratitude is a feeling about whether someone deserves credit, and no amount of successful self-persuasion changes whether the landlord actually deserves it. (5) Marco wasn't having it, and said basically nobody's gratitude toward anybody is ever fully "pure" -- there's always some self-interested reason mixed in somewhere, even if it's just wanting to feel like a decent person -- so if Sofia's standard were right it would disqualify almost all gratitude everywhere, not just Jen's, which suggests the standard itself is broken.

Nobody at the table actually settled it, and honestly I keep flip-flopping on who's making the better point. Could you go through the exchange move by move and say who's got the better of it at each step -- not just who wins overall -- and flag anything important you think both of them are missing?
