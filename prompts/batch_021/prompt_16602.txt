I'm a philosophy PhD student working on Africana aesthetics for my qualifying exams, and I keep coming back to a pairing that I think gets flattened whenever it shows up in survey treatments: W. E. B. Du Bois's 1926 address "Criteria of Negro Art" (delivered at the NAACP's Chicago conference and published that October in The Crisis) against Alain Locke's short 1928 rejoinder "Art or Propaganda?," which appeared in the single issue of Wallace Thurman's little magazine Harlem before it folded.

The classroom-shorthand version has Du Bois as the propagandist who wants Black art harnessed to racial uplift, and Locke as the art-for-art's-sake formalist who wants artists left alone to create. I don't think that survives contact with the actual texts. Du Bois isn't indifferent to beauty as such — his argument keeps tying Beauty back to Truth and Goodness rather than treating art as merely useful — and Locke isn't apolitical either; his entire career, from editing The New Negro onward, is organized around a racial-political project of its own. So the more I sit with these two pieces, the less sure I am what the disagreement actually consists in once the slogans are stripped away.

Could you reconstruct both men's positions as precisely as you can and then compare them? I'm especially interested in: (1) whether Du Bois and Locke are even using "propaganda" to pick out the same thing, or whether some of the apparent disagreement is really terminological; (2) what Du Bois's actual argument is for the stronger claim that art by and about Black Americans is unavoidably propaganda regardless of the individual artist's intentions, as opposed to the weaker claim that it merely ought to be used that way; and (3) whether Locke's alternative is best read as a rival theory of what art is for, or as a strategic disagreement about means given ends the two men substantially share. Finally, I'd like your own considered verdict rather than a diplomatic non-answer: which position offers the more defensible account of the relationship between aesthetic value and political function, and why?
