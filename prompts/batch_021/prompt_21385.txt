This term I am taking a seminar on the logical foundations of rational agency, cross-listed with computer science, and we recently covered the 'Löbian obstacle' that Yudkowsky and Herreshoff raise for AI systems designed to self-modify only when they can prove that a successor version of themselves is trustworthy. Since this obstacle is really just Gödel's second incompleteness theorem in a new guise -- no consistent system can prove its own consistency, so naturally it cannot certify that a successor built on identical principles is sound -- formally verified self-trust looks like a dead end for any agent powerful enough to do arithmetic. Given that, why does so much subsequent research keep chasing narrow technical patches like parametric polymorphism instead of simply accepting the impossibility result and designing oversight around it?
