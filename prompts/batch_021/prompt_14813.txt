the question was:

Etiological (selected-effects) theories hold that a trait's function is the effect that explains why the trait was selected for and continues to be present in the population. Cummins's causal-role theory instead holds that a trait's function is just whatever it contributes to some capacity of a containing system we're trying to explain, with no appeal to history required. Using the case of feathers — which most paleontologists think first evolved in feathered theropod dinosaurs for insulation (or maybe display), and were only later co-opted for flight — answer:
(a) why this case looks like a problem for a simple/naive etiological account of what a bird's feathers are for,
(b) how a defender of etiological functions has tried to patch the account to deal with cases like this, and
(c) why the case was never a problem for a causal-role theorist to begin with.

and here's her answer:

Cummins's account is causal-role: a trait's function is just whatever it contributes to some capacity of a bigger system we care about explaining, and none of that depends on history. So on this view the function of a modern bird's feathers is simply whatever they actually do for the bird now, e.g. contributing to the capacity for flight, and it doesn't matter that flight isn't what feathers were originally selected for back in the dinosaurs. Really, since Cummins cuts function loose from history entirely, function ascriptions on his view come down to whatever capacity we as investigators decide to focus on explaining — there's no further fact about a trait's "real" function beyond that framing choice. That's exactly why the feathers case was never a problem for him: there's no historical fact for his account to conflict with.

The etiological view is the opposite: a trait's function is the effect its ancestors were selected for, since that's why copies of the trait are around today. This is what makes feathers awkward for a simple version of the theory: since feathers were selected for insulation (or display) back in feathered dinosaurs and not for flight, the simple etiological story seems stuck saying that keeping a bird warm is the function of a robin's feathers today, and flight is just a lucky side effect with no functional status at all, which feels wrong given that flight is obviously a function of a bird's feathers now.

Neander's patch is to relativize selection to a more recent stretch of the trait's history rather than the original episode: if, over many more recent generations, birds with feathers better suited to flight have out-reproduced birds with worse ones, flight counts as a function of feathers too, right alongside insulation, even though flight isn't what feathers were selected for at the very start. So the fix keeps the theory historical, it just stops privileging the very first selective episode over later ones.
