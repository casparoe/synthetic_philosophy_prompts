Quick question for a piece I'm noodling on about how we come to trust our own instruments. I keep bumping into this 'bootstrapping' or 'easy knowledge' problem in epistemology -- the gas gauge case, where someone checks their gauge against itself a bunch of times and ends up concluding the gauge is reliable. Feels to me like thats just confirmation bias wearing a lab coat, and the obvious fix is to require an independant check before anyone's allowed to trust an instrument. So why do epistemologists treat this like a genuinely open problem instead of a solved case of bad statistical practice?
