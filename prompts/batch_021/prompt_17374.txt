I'm drafting a scene for a novel in which a magazine journalist interviews a philosopher known for her work on transformative experience -- the claim that major life decisions, from having a child to leaving a religious order, cannot be settled by ordinary cost-benefit reasoning, because their true value can only be 'revealed' by living through them, and no amount of testimony from people who already have can convey that value in advance. I want the exchange to read as informed pushback rather than a lecture, so draft eight interview questions that press the substance of her view instead of inviting her to restate it. Include at least one question on why the testimony of everyone who's already made the same choice shouldn't just settle the matter statistically; one testing whether her framework actually needs the distinction between experiences that merely teach you something new and experiences that remake your preferences; and one asking her to name a real decision her own theory implies no one could ever make rationally. For each question, note the deflection she is likely to reach for and a follow-up that closes it off.
