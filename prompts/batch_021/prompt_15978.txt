The Price equation allows total selection on a trait in a group-structured population to be partitioned into a between-group component and a within-group component. Multi-level selection theorists such as Elliott Sober and David Sloan Wilson treat a nonzero between-group term as evidence that group selection is a genuine causal process operating alongside individual selection, whereas critics such as Kerr and Godfrey-Smith have argued that the very same partition can be redescribed without remainder as ordinary individual-level selection, in which fitness merely depends on group composition. Since both camps accept the same mathematics, what exactly is left for them to disagree about? I would like an intuitive, gut-level explanation of why a mathematically uncontroversial partition of fitness variance does not, on its own, settle the further question of how many levels are really doing the causal work, and whether that further question could be adjudicated empirically at all or only terminologically.
