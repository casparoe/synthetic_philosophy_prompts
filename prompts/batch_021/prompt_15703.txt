I'm a software engineer on the internal review board that has to sign off before we ship any autonomous agent that negotiates on a customer's behalf. We're close to shipping a procurement agent that renegotiates a customer's cloud-vendor contracts every week. Its ranking of contract terms shifts with how an offer is framed (per-unit discount vs. flat surcharge vs. bundled rate), so across a run of weekly offers it can end up preferring contract A to B, B to C, and then C back to A. One vendor's sales team seems to have caught on, and has been steering the agent through a slow rotation of "improved" reframed offers that each look like a win in isolation but have quietly raised the customer's total spend for three cycles running. Three fixes are on the table: (1) hard-code a fixed ranking of contract terms up front that the agent can never revise, however an offer is framed; (2) leave the agent's week-to-week judgment as is, but bolt on an after-the-fact audit that flags repeated spend increases; (3) have the agent forecast the likely multi-week trajectory of any new offer before accepting it, and refuse offers whose predicted trajectory it would later regret. The team is split, partly because option (1) feels safest but also seems to throw away real signal about the customer's actual priorities that the reframed offers reveal. Please draft the memo I'll bring to next Tuesday's board meeting: a concrete recommendation, a rollout timeline, and a genuine, specific response to the strongest objection against whichever option you choose.
