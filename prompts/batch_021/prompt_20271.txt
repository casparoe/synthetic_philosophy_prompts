A few of us from a local meetup pool some money every year and give out small grants to independent researchers working on AI safety stuff, since most of us got into this just from reading lesswrong/alignment forum posts for years and none of us have any formal background in CS or philosophy, we just picked it up as a hobby that got kind of serious. I'm one of three people on the little committee that actually decides where the money goes, and we've got an applicant this round who's dividing the group pretty badly, so I need to write up the recommendation memo before we vote this Thursday.

The guy wants a grant to write up what he's calling a dissolution of the Löbian obstacle - that's the thing (I think from an old MIRI paper) where, because of Löb's theorem, a reasoning system can never fully certify in advance that "anything my future self or successor proves, using that same kind of reasoning, will actually be true," so a self-improving AI can't just hand off trust to its next version without weakening its standards a little at every step. His proposal is to basically drop the whole proof-based approach and have the agent extend trust to a successor based on it passing a big battery of behavioral/statistical tests instead, and he's calling this a genuine dissolution of the obstacle rather than a dodge. Two people on the committee think this is a legit and kind of elegant move, especially since (as they point out) Gödel and Löb basically guarantee you were never going to get full proof-based self-trust anyway, so insisting on it is asking for the impossible. The third member thinks he's just quietly trading the hard problem for an easier one and slapping a bow on it, since now the successor could pass all your tests and still turn out to behave completely different once its actually in control, which is exactly the kind of failure the proof-based approach was trying to rule out in the first place, and "it tested fine" doesn't give you that guarantee.

Could you write the actual memo I'd bring to that vote? I want a clear recommendation - fund it, reject it, or fund it with specific conditions and a rough timeline attached - and I want the reasoning part to genuinely wrestle with whether statistical/behavioral trust really dissolves the obstacle or just relocates it, treating the skeptical committee member's point as seriously as the other two's rather than just picking a side and dressing it up nice. Keep it something a normal person could read out loud in about five minutes.
