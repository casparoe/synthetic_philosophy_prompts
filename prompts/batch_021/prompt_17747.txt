So this came up after our proseminar on Frege and I cant stop thinking about it. Background in case you don't know it: in the last year of his life (1924) Frege kept a private diary that only came to light decades after he died. It's a hard-right nationalist rant -- he thought Weimar parliamentary democracy was basically mob rule, he was still bitter about the war and Versailles, and he wanted Jews stripped of political rights (he wasnt shy about socialists either). Which is a weird thing to learn about the guy whose whole philosophical brand was ruthlessly purging logic of anything psychological, subjective, or merely empirical.

Two people in my cohort got into it about what to make of this. Mara said it's straightforwardly irrelevant to Frege's philosophy: Begriffsschrift and the definition of number don't become less valid because their author turned out to be a bigot, and if we start grading arguments by the character of whoever wrote them we'd have to throw out half the canon (Heidegger was a card-carrying Nazi and we still teach Being and Time). Dan agreed on the narrow point -- the proofs aren't touched -- but said that's too quick: Frege's anti-psychologism was never just a technical result, it was part of a bigger picture about protecting objective, "pure" thought from contamination by anything subjective or empirical, and the diary shows that same purity-obsessed cast of mind curdling into "the nation must be purged of its contaminating elements." Same root, even if neither view entails the other.

Mara said that's a just-so story: plenty of rigorous, purity-obsessed logicians and mathematicians from that era were left-wing, Jewish, or totally apolitical, so "rigor mindset causes ethnonationalism" can't be right -- Dan's just doing armchair psychobiography and dressing it up as philosophy. Dan said fine, it's not a causal law and he's not claiming the logic entails the politics, just that in Frege's particular case the vehemence of the anti-psychologism and the vehemence of the antisemitism are stylistically of a piece -- both are about contamination and purity -- and that's worth knowing even if it never shows up in a soundness proof. Mara said okay, but that's now such a weak claim ("huh, neat rhetorical parallel") that it can't support what he seemed to want at the start, which was for this to actually change how we read or teach Frege.
