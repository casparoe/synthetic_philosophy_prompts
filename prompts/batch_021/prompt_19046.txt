For my graduate seminar on philosophy of science, I want a dialogue that goes beyond the usual Popper-versus-Kuhn contrast most students already know. Write one between a strict falsificationist in the Popperian mold and a philosopher defending Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes, arguing over where exactly to draw the line between legitimate science and the kind of ad hoc theory-saving that Popper diagnosed in astrology and psychoanalysis. Ground the argument in the classic pair of episodes used to illustrate Lakatos's distinction between progressive and degenerating problem shifts: the astronomer Urbain Le Verrier used essentially the same Newtonian reasoning to posit an unseen planet beyond Uranus, which led to the discovery of Neptune, and later an unseen planet he called Vulcan to explain the anomalous precession of Mercury's perihelion -- a search that came up empty, with the puzzle eventually dissolved by general relativity instead. Have the falsificationist open by pressing the worry that calling either move scientific already surrenders naive falsificationism's clean verdict against rescuing a theory by fiat, have the Lakatosian answer by drawing on the actual features of the two episodes to explain what distinguishes a progressive auxiliary hypothesis from a degenerating one, and then let the falsificationist play their strongest card: whether that distinction can guide a scientist's decision at the time it has to be made, or is only ever available in hindsight. Don't let either side concede outright -- I want the Lakatosian to answer the hindsight worry rather than simply absorb it.
