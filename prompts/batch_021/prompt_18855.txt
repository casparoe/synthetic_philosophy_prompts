Working on a scene in my novel—there's a subplot about a color-vision researcher named Mary (yes, I know, I couldn't resist) and I want two side characters, both philosophy professors, to get into it at a party over what happens when she finally leaves her black-and-white room. Both of them are physicalists, no closet dualists, but they disagree with each other: one's defending what I think is called the "ability hypothesis," that Mary only picks up new abilities (recognizing red, imagining it, sorting swatches by feel) and never gains any new propositional knowledge. The other thinks Mary does learn something new and real, just that it's an old physical fact she already knew, now grasped through a new phenomenal concept rather than a new fact about the world. Can you write their argument as an actual back and forth, each one pressing on the weak spot in the other's view, rather then two monologues stitched together? Keep them evenly matched, no strawmanning either side, 500-600 words is about right.
