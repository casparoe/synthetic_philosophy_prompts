At a family dinner last month, my cousin Callum spent much of the meal criticizing his brother for canceling plans at the last minute yet again, calling him flaky and inconsiderate. My cousin Renata, sitting across the table, cut in to say Callum had no standing to say that, since he had canceled on her twice that year with just as little warning. The two of them argued for a while, and it has stayed with me since, because neither one clearly won.

Renata's opening move was that Callum's own record disqualified him from making the accusation. Callum replied that standing was beside the point: his brother is flaky, full stop, and that doesn't stop being true no matter how often Callum cancels on people. Renata granted that the description might be accurate but said that was never what she was disputing: blaming someone is different from merely describing them, since it means holding them to a standard you are implicitly claiming to uphold yourself, which Callum can't do credibly from outside that standard. Callum answered that this proves too much -- everyone falls short of some standard sometimes, so on Renata's view only a flawless person could ever criticize anyone. Renata's comeback was that occasional lapses were never her point: what actually costs you standing is treating your own violations as always excusable while treating the identical violation in someone else as damning, which is exactly what she thought Callum does every time he shrugs off his own cancellations. Callum's last word was that his reasons for canceling are, in his own estimation, usually better than his brother's, so that even granting Renata's whole framework, he thought the comparison still favored him.

Could you work through their exchange move by move and say who has the stronger argument at each point, rather than just declaring an overall winner? I'm especially interested in whether Renata's distinction between blaming and describing really does the work she wants it to, whether Callum's "only saints could criticize" objection is answered by her later refinement or just deferred, and whether Callum's closing move is a legitimate reply or just another instance of the very self-exemption Renata is describing. If you think the underlying premise that hypocrisy can strip someone of the standing to blame is itself confused or overstated, say so and explain why. Close with a brief verdict on whether Renata's original charge against Callum holds up.
