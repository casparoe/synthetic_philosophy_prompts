I'm a philosophy PhD student working on formal decision theory, and my reading group has been debating what, if anything, is genuinely novel in Lara Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility theory. My own view is that the risk function r she layers on top of the utility function does no independent work: the standard Arrow-Pratt story already lets us represent any stable pattern of risk-averse choice simply by choosing a sufficiently concave utility function over outcomes, so dressing this up with the rank-dependent machinery borrowed from Quiggin just relabels curvature rather than offering a genuine alternative to expected utility theory. Am I missing something, or does risk-weighted expected utility ultimately collapse into ordinary expected utility with a concave utility function?
