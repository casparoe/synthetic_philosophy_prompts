A close friend of mine is finishing a dissertation on the semantics of conditionals, and last weekend we spent most of a dinner arguing about how to respond to Lewis's 1976 triviality result for the Adams thesis -- the claim that your credence in an indicative conditional "if A, then B" should just equal your conditional credence in B given A. Lewis's proof works by comparing one credence function to that same function updated on B, and to it updated on not-B, and it needs the sentence "if A, then B" to express one fixed proposition across all three. My friend defends Stalnaker's way out: deny exactly that fixed-proposition assumption, and let the content of an indicative conditional shift with the interpreter's own credal state, so that the proof's key substitution move is illegitimate even though the Adams thesis still holds at each stage taken on its own. I objected that this concedes too much -- if the very proposition a conditional expresses can change just because my credences changed, rather than because anything in the world did, then two people can no longer be said to agree or disagree about one and the same conditional in any ordinary sense, and Stalnaker has quietly given up most of what made truth-conditional semantics attractive to begin with. She came back with the standard reply that I was just restating Edgington's non-cognitivism with extra steps, and that I owed her an account of how a conditional with no truth value could behave the way conditionals obviously do under negation, disjunction, and as the antecedents of further conditionals. Neither of us budged before the restaurant closed.

I'd like you to write the argument I couldn't quite pull together that night. Make the case that, in light of Lewis's triviality result, we should follow the Adams-Edgington line and deny that indicative conditionals have truth conditions at all, rather than rescuing truth conditions via Stalnaker's context-shifting maneuver -- or, for that matter, via Jackson's strategy of keeping the material conditional as the truth condition and relegating the Adams thesis to a story about robustness-governed assertability. Reconstruct enough of Lewis's proof to make clear exactly what each rescue buys and what it costs, take seriously the embedding objection my friend raised against non-cognitivism, and explain why the price Stalnaker and Jackson pay strikes you as worse than the price of giving up truth conditions. Don't retreat into "both sides have a point" -- I had enough of that over dinner. I want an actual verdict, defended against the strongest version of the reply it's going to get.
