Been down a rabbit hole this week reading Adam Smith's actual arguments about colonies in the Wealth of Nations, and its way more scathing than the "invisible hand" greatest-hits version everyone knows. His big point about the East India Company running Bengal was basically that merchant and sovereign are incompatible characters -- give a trading company the power to tax and govern a territory and it'll wreck the place, because monopoly-profit incentives and decent government pull in opposite directions. He even argued Britain would come out ahead just cutting the American colonies loose (or fully incorporating them with representation in Parliament) rather than keep bankrolling an empire that mainly served a narrow merchant class at everyone else's expense. So here's my question: given that specific argument, what do you think Smith would make of the charter-cities / private-governance-zone stuff going on now, like Prospera in Honduras, where a private company gets to write the laws and run the courts over a chunk of territory in exchange for investment? Same East India Company problem in new packaging, or does the voluntary, no-army-no-conquest setup actually get it past his objections? Try to engage with the actual mechanics of his argument rather than just vibes-based "Smith = free markets good."
