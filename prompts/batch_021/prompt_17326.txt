I've recently gotten interested in decision theory through some writing on AI safety, and there's one part of updateless decision theory (UDT) that doesn't sit right with me. My understanding is that a UDT agent works out, once and for all from the standpoint of its prior, the single course of action that maximizes expected utility, and then simply carries that out no matter what it later observes. This is supposedly why UDT agents pay up in the classic Counterfactual Mugging scenario -- where Omega asks for $100 after a coin lands tails, on the promise that it would have handed over $10,000 had the coin landed heads and you'd have committed in advance to paying -- even once they know for certain the coin came up tails and stand to gain nothing from handing over the money. But surely an agent whose behavior can respond to what it actually learns should never do worse, and will sometimes do better, than one that's locked into a plan regardless of the evidence. So why do decision theorists treat UDT as a genuine advance over causal or evidential decision theory, rather than just a clever-looking way of throwing away information?
