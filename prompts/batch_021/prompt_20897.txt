So this might be a weird thing to be loosing sleep over, but I started a new job a few months back as a fact-checker at a mid-size online magazine, and it's stirred up this nagging logic problem I can't shake (I did a bit of formal logic back in undergrad and apparently it never fully left me). For our long features I go claim by claim and either verify it against a primary source or kick it back to the writer, so by the time we hit publish I genuinely believe each individual factual claim in the piece is true - I personally checked it. But these features run long, sometimes 300+ discrete claims, and looking back through the archive, the magazine has issued at least one correction on almost every single issue for years now, no matter how careful the fact-check was. So for any given issue I end up believing both that every claim in it is true (I checked each one myself!) and that the issue almost certainly contains at least one false claim somewhere, even though I couldn't tell you which one if you asked. My editor thinks this just means I'm being muddled - he says either I trust the fact-check, in which case I should think the whole issue is clean, or I don't, in which case I shouldn't be signing off on the individual claims either.

The odd part is a version of the same feeling shows up even when I have zero specific reason to doubt anything, like with those "97 wild facts about the ocean" style listicles we run sometimes. There I don't suspect any particular fact more than the others - they all look equally solid - but purely from having fact-checked a hundred of these lists before, I'd bet real money that two or three of the 97 are subtly off, with no candidates in mind at all. I tried explaining all this to my roommate over dinner and just got a blank stare back, so now I'm bringing it to you instead: is my editor right that I'm being logically incoherent, or is there something legitimate going on here that his argument is missing?
