The question as given:

Consider the two-player game below (Player 1 picks the row, Player 2 picks the column; payoffs are listed as (Player 1, Player 2)):

              L           C           R
   U       (3, 2)      (3, 0)      (0, 1)
   M       (1, 0)      (1, 5)      (1, 0)
   D       (0, 2)      (0, 0)      (3, 3)

Use iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies to reduce this game as far as possible. You're allowed to use a mixed strategy as the dominating strategy: a strategy can be eliminated if some probability mix over the other available strategies does strictly better than it against every strategy the opponent could play, even when no single pure strategy manages that. At each round, state which strategy is eliminated, why, and (where relevant) which mix you used. Give the fully reduced game at the end and say whether the original game is dominance solvable.

The student's answer:

Round 1: Neither U nor D dominates M on its own — U loses to M when Player 2 plays R (0 < 1), and D loses to M when Player 2 plays L or C (0 < 1 both times). So M can't be eliminated by a pure strategy. But the 50/50 mix of U and D gives 1.5 against L, 1.5 against C, and 1.5 against R, all of which beat M's guaranteed 1. So this mix weakly dominates M, and row M can be deleted.

Round 2: With M gone, compare Player 2's columns using only rows U and D. L gives (2,2), C gives (0,0), R gives (1,3). C is beaten by L in both of the remaining rows, so C is strictly dominated and gets deleted.

Round 3: That leaves a 2x2 game: U/D for Player 1 against L/R for Player 2, with (U,L)=(3,2), (U,R)=(0,1), (D,L)=(0,2), (D,R)=(3,3). For Player 1, U beats D against L and D beats U against R, so neither dominates. For Player 2: at D, R gives 3 versus L's 2; at U, L gives 2 versus R's 1 — but since the 3 is Player 2's best payoff anywhere in the reduced game, R strictly dominates L, so L can be eliminated too.

That leaves the single cell (D, R), payoffs (3,3). So the game is dominance solvable, and (D, R) is the outcome iterated elimination predicts.
