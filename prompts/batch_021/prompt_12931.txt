I serve on the grants committee of a foundation that funds narrative-nonfiction books on science and history. The strongest application this cycle proposes a book arguing that a single mechanism — population growth that outpaces available resources, multiplying the elite aspirants competing for a shrinking number of top positions until the state can no longer buy off or suppress the losers — explains the collapse of the Late Bronze Age palace economies, the western Roman Empire, and the Classic Maya alike, and that the same mechanism flags which states are brittle today. Two committee members trained as historians don't dispute the facts cited, but argue that compressing three unrelated collapses into one reusable formula erases the contingent decisions, personalities, and local particulars that make each case an explanation rather than a curve fit. The applicant counters that without some generalizable claim, calling anything an "explanation" of collapse is just storytelling dressed up as analysis. Draft the committee's short decision memo — fund, decline, or fund with conditions — for our Friday meeting, with reasoning the applicant could actually respond to, rather than one that simply sides with the historians by default.
