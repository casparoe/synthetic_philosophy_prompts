Ok so this happened to me about two weeks ago and I still can't stop thinking about it. I'm co-captain of my school's Science Olympiad team, and back in September I promised everyone I'd be at regionals since I'm basically the only person who knows how to run our Machine Design event. Then completely seperately, my grandma's 85th birthday party (which my mom has been planning for months) got moved to that exact same weekend because the venue double booked us, so what used to be zero overlap suddenly became a full blown conflict. There was no way to physically be in two places, so I ended up going to the party since my mom made it really clear how much it meant to her, and I gave my team like five days notice so they could find a backup. They actually did fine without me and nobody's mad, everyone keeps telling me it's not my fault since the scheduling collision was totally out of my control. But I still feel like I owe my team something, like I broke a real promise to them, even though there was genuinely no way I could've kept both.

I brought this up with my older brother, who's on the debate team and loves being annoyingly logical about everything, and he said that's irrational — if it was actually impossible to do both things, then logically I can't have done anything wrong, so the guilty feeling is just something I need to reason my way out of. That kind of makes sense, but it also feels like he's saying my promise to the team just stopped counting for anything the second it became inconvenient, which seems off too. So who's actually right here — is the guilt pointless since I truly had no choice, or is there something real I still owe my team even though I couldn't have done anything differently? Basically, can two different "shoulds" both be genuinely true and pulling on you at once, or does one of them always have to cancel out?
