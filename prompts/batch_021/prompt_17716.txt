My younger sister has bipolar I disorder. Four years ago, while she was stable, she signed what her psychiatrist called a Ulysses contract: a directive naming me as the one person authorized to have her hospitalized and medicated against her express wishes if she became manic again, because she knew a manic version of herself would experience the whole intervention as persecution and beg everyone involved to stop. Fourteen months ago, that happened, and I invoked it. She called me a traitor in front of people we both love, and it was the better part of a year before she spoke to me again. She's fully well now, and has asked me to give the toast at her wedding this weekend. I want to say something true, without turning the reception into group therapy, and without implying that the sister who screamed at me was somehow less real, or less her, than the one who wrote that contract. Could you write the toast? It has to work for guests who know nothing of this history, but it shouldn't quietly drop the real question sitting underneath it all: why keeping a promise to someone can sometimes mean overriding the very person standing in front of you, begging you not to.
