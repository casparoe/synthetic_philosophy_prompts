My younger brother is getting married in three weeks and I somehow got roped into giving the best man speech, which would normally be no big deal except he's a philosophy postdoc whose entire research area is future contingents -- his dissertation was basically 200 pages on Aristotle's sea battle passage in the De Interpretatione, and he's spent years arguing in print that "there will be a sea battle tomorrow" isn't true or false yet. I already learned the hard way that this isn't just an academic quirk: at his engagement party I said something like "you guys were meant for each other" as a toast and got a fifteen minute, half-affectionate lecture about how that sentence smuggles in exactly the fatalism (he brought up some ancient guy named Diodorus and his "master argument") that he's built his whole career arguing against. To make it worse, his fiancée works on the same stuff but takes the other side -- she thinks the future is already just as fixed and real as the past, some "block time" thing -- so whether "our future together is already true" has apparantly come up as an actual argument between them at dinner, lovingly, because that's what happens when two philosophers fall for each other.

His whole department is coming to the wedding, including people who will absolutely catch a reference to supervaluationism or the Ockhamist view without blinking, but so is our enormous extended family who has never heard of any of this and just wants a nice toast about two people in love. I don't really understand half of what the two of them argue about, I've just absorbed the vocabulary from being trapped at enough family dinners to be dangerous. Could you write me a five minute best man speech that's genuinely warm and funny, doesn't accidentally assert anything either of them would flag as metaphysically false (no fate, no destiny, nothing "meant to happen"), and gets at least one real laugh out of the philosophers -- ideally by turning the fact that they can't even agree on whether today was already true yesterday into the joke -- without loosing my aunts completely?
