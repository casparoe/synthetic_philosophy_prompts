I've spent more years than I care to admit reading rationalist blogs and forums, and a few years ago that reading turned out to be unexpectedly practical when I joined the protocols subcommittee of a volunteer backcountry search-and-rescue unit, where we maintain the rules incident commanders use to build probability-of-location maps (a homemade, much cruder cousin of the Bayesian search-theory tools that outfits like the Coast Guard use for planning searches at sea). Last month's callout has split the subcommittee down the middle in a way I'd like to settle properly rather than re-litigate every time it comes up, especially since the map is literally what tells commanders which zone to send scarce search teams to at first light. The search area was divided by a ridge into an East zone and a West zone, and given the subject's last known position and the elapsed time, we had good reason going in to treat the two zones as equally likely. A few hours into the search, a hunter radioed in a tip everyone found credible: if the missing hiker had gone into the West zone, she was far more likely to be headed for the lake basin than for the old lookout trail, and he had no opinion at all about East versus West. Half the subcommittee wants to leave our probability of West exactly where it was, on the grounds that a tip about the inside of West can't be evidence about East versus West; the other half reran the map using the maximum-entropy update our software defaults to, feeding in only the new conditional constraint, and got a P(West) that had shifted noticeably away from one half, which they insist is simply what the math requires whether or not it feels right. I recognized this as a field-grade version of a puzzle I first ran into years ago in a rationalist-blog discussion of Bayesian conditionalization, under a name I'm fairly sure was the Judy Benjamin problem, and learning that it's a genuinely disputed case rather than a solved exercise has not, unfortunately, told me what our unit should actually do the next time a tip like this comes over the radio, which at the rate we get them will probably be before the season is out.

As the subcommittee member who volunteered to turn this into policy, I need to bring a short written recommendation to our meeting before the spring training weekend, when new field volunteers are briefed on protocol. Please draft that memo. It should commit our unit to one specific, usable procedure for folding a conditional-form tip like this into the probability map going forward, stated clearly enough that a commander under field pressure could apply it without re-deriving the epistemology on the spot; it should give the reasoning in enough depth that a skeptical, numerate coordinator would actually be persuaded rather than just told to comply; and it should engage honestly with whichever camp's procedure you end up not recommending, since both are being argued for in good faith by subcommittee members whose judgment I otherwise trust.
