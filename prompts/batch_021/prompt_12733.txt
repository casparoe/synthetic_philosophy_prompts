For my decision theory seminar we've been running a multi-week betting simulation that counts for part of our grade: every team starts with a pretend $10,000 balance, and each week we choose what fraction of our current balance to wager on a bet the professor rigged to have good odds — it wins 60% of the time, a win doubles whatever you put in, and a loss means you lose whatever you put in. My partner on the project, Jordan, decided in week one that the "rational" move every single week is to shove the whole balance in, since each dollar wagered has an expected payout of $1.20 (0.6 chance of getting $2 back, 0.4 chance of getting $0 back), so betting more should always mean higher expected earnings, full stop. I went along with it for a bit, but Jordan's balance has already hit zero twice now (you get a small stipend each week so you're not fully out of the game, but it puts you way behind), while my approach of only wagering part of the balance each week has been climbing pretty steadily the whole time.

Jordan says the zeros are just variance and that going all-in every round has to be the correct strategy in the long run since it's literally the choice that maximizes expected earnings each week, and that my hesitation to bet everything is just me being irrationally risk averse and not "trusting the math." I even brought it up with my roommate, who's a stats major, and she just shrugged and said Jordan's probably right about the math, which honestly made me doubt myself even more. But something about it still doesn't sit right with me — it seems like if you bet your entire balance every single week for enough weeks in a row, you're basically guaranteed to eventually hit a loosing week and go to zero, and "the expected value is higher" doesn't actually seem to respond to that problem. Is one of us actually correct here in some rigorous way, or is this really just a difference in risk tolerance with no right answer? And is there an actual principled way to figure out what fraction of the balance to wager each round, instead of just picking a number that feels reasonably safe?
