This term I'm taking a philosophy of mathematics seminar that runs alongside a mathematical logic course, and the two collided in an interesting way when we got to Skolem's paradox: the fact that, if ZFC is consistent, the (downward) Löwenheim–Skolem theorem guarantees it has a countable model, even though ZFC proves, via Cantor's theorem, that the real numbers are uncountable. Our professor walked us through the standard resolution — that "countable" and "uncountable" are relativized to a model, since the bijection that would witness the model's own reals as countable simply isn't among the sets the model contains, even though such a bijection exists at the level of the metatheory — and more or less treated this as closing the case. Afterward, though, a friend of mine in the course, who is more sympathetic to Skolem's own unease about the result and keeps bringing up Putnam's model-theoretic argument against metaphysical realism (which explicitly trades on this same phenomenon), argued that the technical resolution doesn't settle the further philosophical question: whether there is any fact of the matter about which model our set-theoretic language is really about, as opposed to some countable elementary submodel that agrees with it on every first-order sentence we can write down.

We argued about this for a good hour without either of us budging, and I'd like to see the exchange conducted properly by someone who can argue both sides at full strength. Could you write a dialogue between the two of us — call her Priya, who thinks the internal/external distinction fully dissolves the paradox and leaves realism about the cumulative hierarchy untouched, and call him Daniel, who thinks the relativity phenomenon is a symptom of a genuine indeterminacy in what our set-theoretic quantifiers refer to? I don't want Daniel's Putnam-style worry waved away with a quick "V is a proper class, so Löwenheim–Skolem doesn't even apply to it" — if Priya reaches for that move, she should have to defend it against the obvious follow-up about how we could ever know our theory is about V rather than about some smaller model satisfying all the same sentences. Please have them respond to each other's actual arguments as the exchange develops, rather than each restating their opening position in slightly different words each time.
