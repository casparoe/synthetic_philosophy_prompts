I'm a backend engineer, not a philosopher, but I got pulled into AI safety over the last couple of years through work, and I now co-host a small interview podcast on alignment topics for an audience that's mostly other engineers. A few of us on my team have a running lunchtime argument about whether scalable oversight proposals are more than security theater for a regime we don't yet have models capable of triggering, and AI safety via debate -- where two copies of a model argue opposite sides of a question in front of a judge too weak to verify the answer directly -- keeps coming up, since unlike most alternatives it comes with actual theoretical backing rather than just "have a bigger model check a smaller one." Next episode's guest is a researcher who has spent years defending debate as the right long-term protocol, and who tends to retreat to its theoretical motivation -- the analogy to interactive proof systems, in which a bounded verifier gets walked up to a PSPACE-hard truth by an unbounded prover -- whenever a practical objection comes up, as though the analogy settles the practical question of whether debate tracks truth once debaters are far more capable than the judge.

I'd like eight to ten questions that don't let her rest on that analogy.
