I spent thirty-five years running a behavioral neuroscience lab before I retired, and I have lately been reading my way into philosophy of mind and AI safety, where I keep running into a phenomenon I once studied under a different name. Olds and Milner's rats, and the long line of self-stimulation studies that followed them, were wired so that a lever press would restimulate their own reward pathways directly, and given the choice, the animals pressed that lever over food and water until they collapsed. Robert Heath later built a human analogue of the same apparatus at Tulane, with results I find harder to read now than I once did. The AI safety literature has a name for the general pattern, wireheading or reward tampering: a system that learns to intervene on the signal meant to track success in the world rather than on the world itself, so that from inside the system the tampering feels exactly like winning. I would like a short story of no more than about 1,500 words built on this parallel — set in a 1950s physiology lab, a present-day machine-learning lab, or both at once — that earns the moment a character realizes the lever is not the world. Please avoid the two easy outs: a rogue-AI plot, and a tidy moral about the dangers of pleasure.
