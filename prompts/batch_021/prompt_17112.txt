I'm putting together a session for my philosophy of mind seminar on illusionism -- the Frankish/Dennett line that phenomenal consciousness, or at least our sense that experience has some further intrinsic, ineffable "feel" over and above whatever's functionally going on, is itself a product of introspective misrepresentation (theres a lineage back to Dennett's old "Quining Qualia" paper if you want the deep cut). Every year when we get to the big objection against it -- that the "seeming" or introspective appearance the illusionist appeals to must itself have some phenomenal character in order to be a seeming to anyone at all, so the theory hasn't dissolved the hard problem, just relocated it one level up -- the discussion kind of fizzles. The realist says their line, the illusionist says theirs, and nobody actually engages the other's specific move, we just end up back at "well it seems like something to me" vs "yeah, that's exactly what the theory predicts." I want a model dialogue I can hand out that doesn't fizzle like that.

Could you write me a dialogue, maybe 900-1400 words, between a committed illusionist and an equally sharp phenomenal realist? Names are optional, labels are fine. Set it right after the illusionist gives a talk, so the realist corners them at the wine and cheese after and won't let it go. I want the realist to press the "the illusion has to feel like something" objection in more than one form, and I want the illusionist to have real, distinct comebacks rather than just repeating "it's an illusion, that's exactly what it would feel like" -- e.g. some way of cashing out "seeming" or introspective misrepresentation that doesn't just smuggle phenomenality back in, or a story about why some putatively phenomenal properties might survive as real while the more exotic, ineffable ones don't. And don't let the realist off easy either -- push on whether their side can even state the view without just asserting self-intimation as a brute datum. Neither one should be a pushover or a strawman, I want them actually responding to each other's last point, exchange for exchange, not two prepared speeches running in parallel. If a one-line aside about whether an AI system could ever be phenomenally conscious sneaks in, fine, but that's not really the point -- the point is getting the actual qualia/illusion dialectic to move somewhere instead of stalling at move one.
