In my graduate seminar on the philosophy of mathematics, we recently spent a session on a question that unsettled several students more than anything else this term: given Gödel's second incompleteness theorem -- no consistent theory strong enough to formalize arithmetic can prove its own consistency -- what actually entitles working mathematicians to their evident confidence that the standard axioms, whether Peano Arithmetic or the more ambitious ZFC, are in fact consistent? One student pushed back that this worry is not merely abstract: Frege's own logical system, which he took to be self-evidently true, turned out to harbor Russell's paradox; Kunen later proved outright that a Reinhardt cardinal (a hypothesized cardinal witnessing a nontrivial elementary embedding of the set-theoretic universe into itself) is inconsistent with the axiom of choice, even though nothing about the idea looks obviously incoherent on its face; and as recently as 2011, Edward Nelson circulated a serious, sustained argument that Peano Arithmetic itself is inconsistent, a claim he retracted only once a specific error in it was pinned down some weeks later. Another student countered that relative consistency proofs -- e.g., deriving Con(ZFC) from a large cardinal hypothesis further up the consistency-strength hierarchy -- together with a century or more of trouble-free practical use, surely count for a great deal, even if they fall short of an absolute guarantee.

I would like your help sharpening, and ideally settling, this dispute. Write an essay on how seriously we should take the live possibility that PA and/or ZFC are inconsistent. In doing so, address what work, if any, relative consistency proofs and the consistency-strength hierarchy can really do here given Gödel's theorem; whether the historical precedents above genuinely support a non-negligible credence in inconsistency, or are instead disanalogous to the ZFC case in some important respect; and whether an inconsistency, were one lurking, would be at all likely to surface in ordinary mathematical practice rather than remaining hidden in some remote corner of the theory. Don't retreat into indefinite hedging: reach and defend a specific verdict on how worried mathematicians and philosophers of mathematics are actually entitled to be, and say explicitly whether your answer differs between PA and ZFC.
