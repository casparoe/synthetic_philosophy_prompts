I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of a decade, and a disagreement that keeps resurfacing in my own family has left me genuinely unsure who has the stronger argument, so I would like an outside, analytical read on it.

My mother, who is 78, was diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment two years ago, though she remains legally competent and entirely capable of ordinary conversation. She has stated clearly and consistently, on many separate occasions, that she wants to go on living alone and driving. My sister believes we are obligated to respect what our mother is actually telling us: overriding a competent adult's stated wishes because we think we know better is exactly the kind of paternalism we would object to in any other context. My brother counters that what should guide us is not what she is saying right now, but what she would want if she could fully take in her own situation: absorb the accident statistics for drivers with her diagnosis, vividly picture causing someone else's injury, and reason it through without the emotional pull of losing her independence. He is confident that this clearer-headed, fully informed version of her would ask us to hand over the keys, even though the actual woman in front of us is resisting.

What unsettles me is a suspicion that this move is rigged in a convenient way: whenever we try to imagine what Mom would want "if she really saw things clearly," the imagined version of her always seems to end up agreeing with whatever my brother already believed was correct. Is there a principled way to distinguish between genuinely inferring someone's more considered, informed preferences and simply dressing up your own judgment as theirs? Or is my sister right that any appeal to a hypothetical, clearer-headed version of a person, set against the actual person in front of you, is illegitimate more or less by construction? I would rather hear your actual analysis of the underlying issue than another generic bit of advice about caring for aging parents.
