Okay so this has been rattling around in my head for like two weeks now. Me and a friend got into a whole argument over dinner about whether anyone actually deserves blame or credit for anything they do, and afterwards I went down a rabbit hole reading about this philosopher Galen Strawson and something he calls the "Basic Argument." From what I can piece together: you act the way you do because of the kind of person you are, and to really deserve praise or blame for it (not just some watered-down everyday sense, but really deserve it) you'd have to be responsible for being that kind of person. But you're not, the argument goes, because either you never did anything to shape your own character (obviously not on you then) or you did shape it, through some earlier choice — except that choice itself came out of an even earlier version of you that you also never picked, and so on backwards until you hit birth and genes and upbringing, none of which was up to you. So on this view nobody ever really authors themselves, and true, ultimate responsibility is impossible for anyone, period.

When I brought this up with my friend (who reads way more of this stuff then me) she said the regress doesn't actually get you all the way to "nobody's ever responsible," because being responsible for something doesn't require that you personally created your own character out of nothing — it just requires that the action flows from wants and values that are genuinely yours right now, ones you'd stand by if you thought it through, as opposed to stuff that's forced on you or totally alien to you. Where those values originally came from is beside the point, she said. That sounded pretty reasonable in the moment, but I've been wondering since if that's actually answering the regress or just quietly changing the subject so it doesn't have to.

So could you write me a dialogue about this — an actual back and forth, not each side just stating their view once and stopping — between someone defending Strawson's regress argument and someone defending my friend's "it just has to be genuinely yours right now" answer? I want both of them to be sharp and to really respond to the other's specific points instead of talking past each other. And at some point I'd like the Strawson side to deal with the obvious follow-up: "fine, then say some part of what shapes you is random/undetermined instead of fixed by earlier causes — doesn't that get your opponent off the hook from the regress?" I have this nagging memory that Strawson has an answer for why bringing in randomness doesn't actually save the other side, but I've never seen it actually spelled out, and I'd like to watch that get argued rather than just asserted.
