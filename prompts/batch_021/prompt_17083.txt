So I'm prepping next week's seminar on Aquinas and tyrannicide, and there's a case that's been bugging me that Aquinas obviously never had in mind. Picture a leader who wins a genuinely fair election, then spends his time in office quietly gutting the courts and jailing critics through "legal" channels, rewriting the constitution so he never has to leave, all without ever technically seizing power by force. Given that he wasnt a usurper to begin with, what do you think Aquinas would actually say about a guy like that? And if ordinary citizens became convinced there was no lawful route left to remove him, would Aquinas bless them acting on their own, or would he still insist on waiting for "public authority" to act, even once that authority is itself the thing that's been captured? He says slightly different things about this across the commentary on the Sentences and De Regimine Principum, so dig into that if it helps. Curious how far his caution actually stretches once the normal channels are gone.
