My sister and I have inherited our late parents' small guesthouse, along with its outstanding debts, and we are negotiating how to divide both ongoing control of the business and the eventual proceeds of its sale. Our fallback positions differ substantially: I have a secure job elsewhere that I could return to with little loss, while she has run the guesthouse for the past eight years and has far fewer alternatives. While reading about bargaining theory on my own, I came across the Kalai-Smorodinsky solution, which seems to differ from the more familiar approach of simply splitting a surplus down the middle. Please describe its most important implications for a negotiation like ours, particularly the role our respective "ideal," or best-case, outcomes would play in it, and whether it would lead to a noticeably different arrangement than an even split.
