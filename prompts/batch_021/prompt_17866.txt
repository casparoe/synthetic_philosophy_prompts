I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on a dissertation chapter about the metaphysics of propositions, and I've been stuck for a few weeks now on whether the newer "act-based" theories of propositions genuinely solve Bradley's regress or merely relocate it. My advisor thinks the worry is basically a wash, but I'm not convinced. The standard setup: if the proposition expressed by "Socrates is wise" is a structured complex built from an object and a property, we owe an explanation of why that complex counts as a single, truth-evaluable unity rather than an inert list of its constituents. Positing a further relation (instantiation, exemplification) to do the tying threatens a vicious infinite regress, since that relation itself seems to need relating to its terms, and so on ad infinitum. Frege's unsaturated concepts and Armstrong's non-mereological composition of states of affairs are the classic ways of stopping the regress by brute ontological fiat at the level of the complex itself. Soames and Hanks instead try to dissolve the problem by relocating unity into cognition: on their view, a proposition just is a type of cognitive act, an act of predicating a property of an object, so there is no antecedent aggregate of separate items that subsequently needs gluing together -- performing the act of predication already is producing the unity. What I can't settle for myself is whether this really escapes Bradley's regress or whether the regress simply reappears one level up, as a question about why predicating wisdom of Socrates counts as a single act rather than two separate sub-acts (entertaining Socrates, entertaining wisdom) that would themselves stand in need of combination.

Could you write me a dialogue that stages this disagreement properly? I'd like one character to defend the traditional picture of propositions as structured, language- and mind-independent complexes (drawing on whichever regress-blocking resources it likes, whether Fregean unsaturation, brute non-mereological composition, or something else in that vein), and the other to defend the act-based view in the Soames/Hanks mold. Please have the act-theorist give a genuine, non-question-begging answer to the "relocated regress" worry rather than simply insisting that acts are obviously unified in a way objects and properties are not, and have the traditionalist pressed, in turn, on whether unsaturation or brute composition is really any less of a stipulation than what the act-theorist is being accused of. I want real engagement -- each character responding to the specific last thing the other said -- rather than two parallel monologues, and I'd rather the dialogue end on an honest impasse or a genuinely sharpened disagreement than on a tidy resolution that isn't earned.
