Apparently Newton spent years secretly writing anti-trinitarian theology on the side - he sent this particular essay to John Locke in 1690 as a letter, and it didn't get published till decades after he died, since this stuff would've wrecked his career if it got out. The argument is that the "three that bear record in heaven" bit in 1 John 5:7 got inserted into the Bible centuries after the fact and isn't original. At one point he heads off an obvious objection - maybe it's not that Trinitarians added the phrase in, maybe Arians deleted it out instead - like this:

"Will you now say that the testimony of the three in heaven was rased out of their books by the prevailing Arians? Yes truly those Arians were crafty Knaves that could conspire so cunningly & slyly all the world over at once (as at the word of Mithridates) in the latter end of the reign of the Emperor Constantius to get all men's books into their hands & correct them without being perceived: Ay & Conjurers too, to do it without leaving any blot or chasm in the books, whereby the knavery might be suspected & discovered; & to wipe even the memory of it out of all men's brains, so that neither Athanasius nor any body else could afterwards remember that they had ever seen it in their books before, & out of their own too so that when they turned to the consubstantial faith, as they generally did in the West soon after the death of Constantius, they could remember no more of it then any body else."

Two things I can't work out. First, what's the "word of Mithridates" crack actually referencing - its clearly meant as a jab but I don't get it. Second, and this is the bit I actually care about: is this a legit argument or is he just loading the dice rhetorically? Is there some real asymmetry between "a phrase got added somewhere and then slowly spread through careless copying over a couple centuries" and "a phrase got deleted everywhere at once in a coordinated hush up," such that the first is just inherently more plausible as history - or is Newton making the deletion theory sound cartoonishly organized on purpose so he doesn't have to engage with a more modest version of it?
