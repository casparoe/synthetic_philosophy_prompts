Years of reading rationalist blogs and forums have left me with the tidy, prepackaged version of Frege's puzzle about identity: two names, one referent, yet a real difference in cognitive significance between "a = a" and "a = b" that calls for explanation — illustrated, invariably, with Hesperus and Phosphorus. Sitting down with "On Sense and Reference" itself for the first time, I was struck that Frege doesn't open with names at all. The paper begins something like this:

"Equality gives rise to challenging questions which are not altogether easy to answer. Is it a relation? A relation between objects, or between names or signs of objects? [...] Now if we were to regard equality as a relation between that which the names 'a' and 'b' designate, it would seem that a = b could not differ from a = a, provided a = b is true. [...] A difference can arise only if the difference between the signs corresponds to a difference in the mode of presentation of that which is designated. Let a, b, c be the lines connecting the vertices of a triangle with the midpoints of the opposite sides. The point of intersection of a and b is then the same as the point of intersection of b and c. So we have different designations for the same point, and these names — 'point of intersection of a and b' and 'point of intersection of b and c' — likewise indicate a mode of presentation; and hence the statement contains actual knowledge."

Only after this does he coin "Sinn" and "Bedeutung" and move on to proper names. What I can't quite reconstruct is the dialectical role the geometry is playing that a name-based example couldn't play just as well at this point. Is it that a definite description like "point of intersection of a and b" wears its mode of presentation openly, letting Frege establish the a = a / a = b distinction before he has to confront the much harder question of what a bare proper name like "Hesperus" could possibly present? Or is the triangle doing no special argumentative work, and just serving as a gentler warm-up before the real cases? I want the actual logical function of this passage within the essay's structure, not the standard executive summary of where the argument ends up.
