I don't have any formal background in linguistics or philosophy, but a conversation with some friends a while back left me stuck on a puzzle about slurs that I haven't been able to work out on my own. What we noticed is that a slur seems to hold onto its offensiveness no matter how you dress up the sentence around it. If someone says "John is not a [slur]" (I'll use brackets as a stand-in for an actual slur, the way I've seen done in careful writing on the subject), the sentence still feels like it has done something ugly, even though the speaker is technically denying, not asserting, that John belongs to that category. The same thing happens if you put the word into a conditional, like "If John were a [slur], I would have nothing to do with him," or into a question, like "Is John a [slur]?" None of these framings seem to launder the word the way they would an ordinary insult: if I say "John is not a jerk" or "Is John a jerk?", there is no insult left over, and "jerk" behaves the way you would expect an ordinary predicate to behave once it is negated or questioned. Slurs do not seem to play by those rules, and I cannot tell whether that is just a fact about social taboo or whether it is telling us something deeper about what kind of meaning these words actually carry.

Could you walk me through, in some depth, how philosophers of language who work on this make sense of it? I would like to understand the reasoning behind treating this "leaking through negation" behavior as evidence for locating the offensive content somewhere other than the ordinary, truth-conditional meaning of the sentence, as opposed to the competing view that the ugliness really is part of what the sentence literally says, so that the negated sentence should, strictly speaking, come out true and inoffensive, with any residual discomfort we feel being mere squeamishness about the word itself rather than anything semantically principled. I am also curious whether this same resistance to being neutralized by negation is what allows a slur to be used without offense by members of the group it targets while remaining fully loaded in the mouth of an outsider, or whether reclamation is really a separate phenomenon that just happens to look similar on the surface.
