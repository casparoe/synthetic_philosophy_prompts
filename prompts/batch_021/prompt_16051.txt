I recently joined the founding steering committee of a new civic reform movement, formed after a corruption scandal at city hall, that plans to run candidates in next year's municipal elections. We're due to bring draft bylaws to our founding congress next month, and the sticking point is party discipline. One faction has just circulated Simone Weil's 1943 essay "On the Abolition of All Political Parties," arguing that any political party is, by its very structure, a machine for manufacturing collective passion, a device for pressuring every member's mind into conformity, and an organization whose own indefinite growth becomes its real and, eventually, its only good, regardless of ideology; they want no binding platform beyond a short statement of principles, no whip, and elected members free to vote their own judgment on every question. The other faction argues that without a binding platform and some expectation of unified votes, we can make no credible promises to voters, hold no one accountable, and will collapse into exactly the incoherence we were founded to oppose. Could you draft the short memo I'll circulate to the steering committee before that vote? It should recommend a specific bylaw structure and take Weil's worry seriously on its merits, rather than treating it as naive.
