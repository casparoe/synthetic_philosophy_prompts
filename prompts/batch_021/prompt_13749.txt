A mutual friend, Marcus, snapped at a junior colleague during a team meeting last month, calling her incompetent in front of everyone. He explained afterward that he'd had his first panic attack in over a decade, triggered by unrelated news about his father's declining health. When this came up at dinner, two friends of mine, Ana and Colin, argued about how the rest of us should feel about it now. Ana said the panic attack explains the outburst but doesn't erase it: Marcus chose those words and knew exactly who he was talking to, so people are entitled to stay angry with him. Colin said that's the wrong test -- resentment tracks whether someone was in control of what they did, not whether they were conscious of it, and by that measure this is closer to something that happened through Marcus than something he did, the way we don't resent someone for what they blurt out while sleep-talking. Ana replied that sleep-talkers have no idea what they're saying, whereas Marcus clearly did, and that if "I was overwhelmed in the moment" is enough to remove control, almost nothing said in anger would ever count as anyone's fault. Colin said that's a fair worry, but it only shows his distinction needs a defensible line, not that "he knew the words" can be that line, since plenty of genuine diminished-responsibility cases involve people who knew exactly what they were saying. Neither budged, and I've gone back and forth on it myself ever since without landing anywhere. Could you go through their exchange move by move, say who has the better of it at each point, and note anything important that both of them might be missing?
