My dad died last week and I'm giving his eulogy Saturday. About twelve years ago he donated a kidney to a total stranger - just signed up as a living donor and let the transplant center match him with whoever was next in line - and for the rest of his life he wouldn't let anyone call him a hero for it. His exact words, more than once, were "either everyone who didn't do this is a monster, or I'm not a hero, pick one," which is a hard thing to build a eulogy around, and also not exactly comforting to my uncle, who got asked to get tested as a match for a cousin years back and said no, and nobody's really let that go. Dad also always insisted the whole thing "cost him nothing," which wasnt true and everybody at the table knew it. He taught philosophy at the community college for thirty years and used the word "supererogatory" completely straight-faced at the dinner table, so half of Saturday's crowd will actually know what he meant by that, and the rest is just family and neighbors. I need the eulogy to be honest about what he did and how strangely he insisted on talking about it, without turning into a lecture and without my uncle feeling singled out. Doesn't need to be long, five minutes tops - can you help me write it?
