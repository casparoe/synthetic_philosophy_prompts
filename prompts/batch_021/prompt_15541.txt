So I spent my career as a physicist, and now that I'm retired I've been falling down a philosophy of mind rabbit hole, which is how I stumbled on Fodor's language of thought stuff. The bit that stuck with me is his argument against pure connectionism back in the 80s with Pylyshyn: neural nets can't really explain the systematicity and productivity of thought (why being able to think "John loves Mary" means you can also think "Mary loves John") unless theyre secretly implementing a classical, combinatorial symbol system underneath -- otherwise any systematic-looking behavior is just a happy accident of training.

Fodor died in 2017, right before the whole LLM boom really took off. Say you could sit him down in front of GPT-4 or Claude, fluently cranking out grammatical, systematic-seeming sentences from nothing but a giant trained network with no hand-coded symbols in sight -- what do you reckon he'd actually say? Would he treat that as vindicating the systematicity argument, refuting it, or does he have some other move available that I'm not seeing?
