I'm currently taking a seminar on vagueness, and we've spent the last few weeks contrasting Timothy Williamson's epistemicism with the supervaluationist semantics developed by Kit Fine and Rosanna Keefe. One issue I can't get straight is how the two views handle higher-order vagueness: since "borderline" itself seems to be a vague predicate, supervaluationists need some hierarchy of "definitely" operators to locate the borderline cases of borderline cases, and I can't tell whether that hierarchy actually stops the sorites series from reappearing one level up or just relocates it. Williamson treats exactly this difficulty as evidence that sharp semantic boundaries are unavoidable, though his margin-for-error account of why we can never know where those boundaries lie seems to commit him to a strange kind of skepticism about our everyday competence with vague words. Could you write a short dialogue between an epistemicist and a supervaluationist, equally sharp and equally well-versed in the other's position, who work through this specific disagreement together -- engaging each other's actual commitments rather than caricaturing them -- ideally by walking through a concrete sorites series (say, a run of color patches shading gradually from red to orange) rather than resting on slogans?
