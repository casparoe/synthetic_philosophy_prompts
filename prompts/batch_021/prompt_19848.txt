Kurt Gödel is well known for formalizing the ontological argument for God's existence in modal logic, a formalization rigorous enough that logicians have since checked it step by step with automated proof assistants and confirmed it to be logically valid. Gödel used this same argument to conclude that there must be an afterlife, a conviction he defended in letters to his mother. Given that a proof this careful, coming from a thinker as rigorous as Gödel, has now been machine-verified, why isn't this generally regarded by philosophers as having settled the question of whether we survive death, rather than remaining just one more speculative opinion among many?
