Over the past couple of months I have been working through G. E. Moore's Principia Ethica on my own; I do not have any formal background in philosophy, but a passing reference to the term "naturalistic fallacy" in something I read led me to track down the original book to see where the phrase came from. Most of the commentary I have since found online is about that opening argument, but a later part of the book has actually stuck with me more, and I keep bringing it up with friends, somewhat to their annoyance. There, Moore asks the reader to compare an exceedingly beautiful world with one that is nothing but a heap of filth, stipulates that no one will ever exist to perceive either, and asks whether it would still be reasonable to prefer that the beautiful world exist. He then goes on to argue, using what he calls the principle of "organic unities," that a whole experience, such as appreciating something beautiful together with a person one loves, can be worth considerably more than the sum of the values of its separate parts, and that this particular combination of beauty and personal affection is, in his view, close to the best that life has to offer.

I would like to ask you for a joke that actually makes use of this material, rather than a generic joke about philosophers with Moore's name attached to it. What I have in mind is a joke whose punchline only really lands for someone who understands what Moore is specifically claiming here -- about beauty retaining value even when no one is there to see it, or about two good things combined being worth more than either is on its own -- rather than a setup that would work equally well for any philosopher. I will leave it to you whether to build the joke around one of these two ideas or to combine them, so long as the humor depends on the actual content of the view rather than being a generic setup with philosophical dressing.
