I'm part of a small giving circle: a handful of us pool money every year and vote on which of several disaster-relief or public-health proposals to fund, without knowing in advance which will pan out. One member with a finance background wants to formalize this: each of us states our preferences over the options as if they were lotteries, he fits a von Neumann-Morgenstern utility curve to each person's answers, and combines those curves into one ranking with weights he says Harsanyi's aggregation theorem forces on us, since each of us reasons consistently under uncertainty and none of us wants to overrule a preference we all share. I've been reading about the theorem on my own and have real doubts: it doesn't seem to pin down those weights without smuggling in an assumption about a common scale the axioms never gave us, and it isn't obvious that how boldly someone bets on an uncertain outcome tracks how much they actually care about the result. Please draft the memo I'll bring to our next meeting recommending whether we adopt this scheme for the coming funding cycle, revise it, or drop it, and engage fairly with the finance-minded member's reasoning rather than dismiss the theorem outright.
