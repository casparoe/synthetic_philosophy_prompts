I think I've got the 101 version of the epistemicism vs. supervaluationism debate about vagueness down -- Williamson-style epistemicists say there's an actual sharp cutoff for "heap" or "bald," we're just constitutionally incapable of ever knowing where it is, while supervaluationists say a borderline claim is only true if its true on literally every admissible way of sharpening the term, and otherwise it's a truth-value gap.

But the thing that's been bugging me is higher-order vagueness. Isn't "which precisifications count as admissible" itself a vague matter? And if you try to supervaluate over that, doesn't it either regress forever or bottom out in some brute fact about the one true set of admissible precisifications that we have no access to -- which just sounds like epistemicism wearing a trenchcoat? Is that collapse objection actually correct, or is there a principled reason supervaluationism dodges it? Want the gears-level version of this dialectic, not the wikipedia-summary one.
