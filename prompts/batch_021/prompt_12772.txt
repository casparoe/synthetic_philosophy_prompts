So here's a thing about the "emotions are judgments" view (the Solomon/Nussbaum style cognitivist line) that I can't get past: recalcitrant emotions. Like, you can be genuinely terrified of flying while still fully believing/judging that flying is statistically very safe. If fear just IS the judgment "this is dangerous," that looks like a flat contradiction, but it happens to people constantly and doesn't feel like garden variety irrationality. I know perception theorists (Prinz and that crowd) like to reach for the anaology to visual illusions -- you keep perceiving the Muller-Lyer lines as unequal even once you know/judge they're equal -- and say emotions persist the same way. Does that comparison actually hold up, or is there a real disanalogy between how an illusion sticks around and how something like a phobia sticks around that perception theorists are just gesturing past?
