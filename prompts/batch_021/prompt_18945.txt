There's this running argument at my family's dinner table that I've been shamelessly mining for my novel: my sister Rosa just got back from Lourdes utterly convinced she'd witnessed an honest-to-god miracle (some woman in the healing baths supposedly cured of stage-four cancer), and my brother Tom, a hospice doctor who's seen every flavor of "unexplained remission" there is, has been dismantling her with pure Hume ever since. Problem is, every time I actually try to draft the two of them as characters it comes out lopsided -- Tom ends up sounding like a smug Reddit atheist, or Rosa ends up sounding like she's never read a book, and neither is true of the real people they're based on. So I went and actually read Hume's essay "Of Miracles" properly instead of just working off the "extraordinary claims" cliffs-notes version everyone quotes, and got kind of obsessed with the footnote where he brings up the Jansenist miracles at the tomb of the Abbé Paris -- where he more or less admits the testimony there was about as solid as testimony for any ordinary historical fact ever gets (good witnesses, contemporaries, hostile opponents who couldn't catch them in a lie) and still tells us not to beleive it, purely because it's a miracle. That seems like it should be a much bigger problem for his whole argument than the two sentences most summaries give it credit for.

Could you write me the dinner-table scene between Rosa and Tom (steal the names or swap them, doesn't matter to me), something like 600-900 words? I want Hume's actual two-part case represented -- the a priori argument about weighing testimony against the entire weight of uniform experience, not just the "extraordinary evidence" shorthand, plus the separate empirical stuff about credulous witnesses and rival religions undercutting each other's claims -- and I specifically want Rosa to bring up the Abbé Paris footnote and press Tom on whether Hume's actually being consistent there, or just defining "miracle" in a way that guarantees no possible evidence could ever outweigh it. Tom needs to give a real answer, not a dodge -- maybe he bites the bullet, maybe he's got a principled way of telling the two cases apart, I honestly don't know which and don't want you fudging it just to let one of them win the round.
