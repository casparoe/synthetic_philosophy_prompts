I spent most of my working life in observational cosmology, and it was only after I retired that I began reading philosophy with any seriousness, largely because I kept running into anthropic reasoning in multiverse debates and was never quite convinced I understood what was doing the argumentative work. Tracing those arguments back through the Boltzmann brain literature eventually led me to the Sleeping Beauty problem, which I now gather the specialists treat as a stripped-down laboratory version of the self-locating puzzles that show up in cosmology in a far messier form. A handful of retired colleagues and I record an informal podcast in which we interview philosophers and physicists about questions at the boundary of the two fields, and next month's guest is a philosopher whose research program is built around defending the self-indication assumption (SIA): she treats thirding about Sleeping Beauty as just the local instance of that general principle, uses it to argue that the self-sampling logic behind the Doomsday Argument collapses once self-indication is properly factored in, and is unusually willing to accept the Presumptuous Philosopher case as a cost worth paying rather than a refutation. Her stock response to the latter is that our discomfort with it is just discomfort with Bayesian conditionalization wearing a cosmological costume, and that one does not get to keep the theorem for small numbers while disowning it for large ones.

Please draft eight interview questions that do not simply hand her an opportunity to restate that line. I would like at least one question that presses on the Presumptuous Philosopher case directly and asks her to specify what, if anything, would make a conclusion too presumptuous to accept; one that asks her to reconcile her optimism about the Doomsday Argument with the fact that cosmologists run what looks like the same typicality reasoning in the opposite direction when they worry that Boltzmann brains vastly outnumber ordinary observers in certain multiverse models; and one that probes whether her SIA-based case for thirding is really doing the same work as Elga's original indifference argument for thirding, or whether the two could be pulled apart in some variant of the puzzle. Please attach a short follow-up to each question, keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and make the last question one about what observation or thought experiment would actually get her to give up self-indication reasoning.
