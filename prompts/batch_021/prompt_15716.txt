Last spring I was at a writing residency, revising my second novel, when one of the other fellows -- Julian, who was finishing a philosophy PhD on the side -- got into an argument with his old graduate-school friend Renata, a visiting decision theorist. It started almost as a joke and ran for the rest of the week.

Julian was about to leave for a ten-day silent retreat and said, half-seriously, that he already expected to come back convinced he should quit the PhD and write full-time. By Van Fraassen's Reflection Principle, he said, since he could already predict his post-retreat credence, rationality required him to start shifting toward it now, rather than waiting ten days to catch up with himself.

(1) Renata said this was a misuse of the principle: Reflection is meant to apply when your future self has learned something, not when it has merely been altered by ten days of silence and meditation that have nothing to do with whether quitting is wise.
(2) Julian said Renata couldn't actually draw that line in advance: any change of mind is just some causal process in the brain, and she had no principled way of saying beforehand which processes count as learning and which count as mere alteration.
(3) Renata said the line isn't drawn in the brain but in the world: a process counts as learning to the extent that its output is sensitive to the truth of the proposition, and a silent retreat isn't sensitive to whether quitting a PhD is a good idea.
(4) Julian said this only pushed the problem back a step, since Renata's confidence that the retreat is "insensitive to the truth" already assumes quitting would be a mistake -- someone who expected the retreat to bring clarity would describe the very same process as truth-sensitive, and nothing settles in advance which of them is right about that.

Neither gave ground, and I've been turning the exchange over since. Could you go through their four moves and say who has the better of each one, not just who comes out ahead overall? I'd also like to know whether you think the principle can be repaired to handle cases like this, or whether Julian's last move shows it collapses into something like "defer to your future self only when you already agree with them."
