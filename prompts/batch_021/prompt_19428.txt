ok so I got into a whole two-hour argument with a friend last night about Molyneux's problem -- whether someone born blind who suddenly gains sight would be able to tell a cube from a sphere just by looking, before ever touching either one -- and it turns out this isn't just an armchair thought experiment, its literally on my plate right now. I sit on my university's research ethics board, and monday we're voting on an amendment to a vision-restoration study (the kind that does free cataract surgery for people who've been blind since infancy in low-resource settings, and uses the rare opportunity to test stuff like this) asking to run cross-modal shape-matching tasks within a day of the bandages coming off, before ordinary visual experience lets patients learn the very visual-tactile correspondence the study is trying to measure in the first place. Half the board thinks testing someone that soon -- while they're still overwhelmed from seeing for the first time ever, and not exactly free to say no to the same team that just gave them their sight -- is exploitative no matter how short the task is; the other half says any delay ruins the only real shot at answering the question, and the task itself is harmless. can you draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with an actual timeframe -- that takes the exploitation worry seriously instead of just asserting the tasks are low risk?
