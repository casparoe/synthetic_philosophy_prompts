I'm a retired particle physicist, and since I stopped working I've been slowly making my way through a stack of old philosophy papers a friend keeps pressing on me over coffee every other week. The one that's really gotten under my skin lately is Max Black's dialogue from the early 50s, where he's arguing against Leibniz's principle that two distinct things can never share literally all the same properties (the "identity of indiscernibles," I gather it's called). His move is to imagine a universe containing absolutely nothing except two solid spheres — same material, same size, same temperature, the same everything basically (iron, a mile or so across, though don't quote me on the exact numbers) — sitting some fixed distance apart, forever, with nothing else existing anywhere to tell them apart or give either one its own separate history. Two voices in the dialogue argue back and forth over whether that setup even makes sense, and whether there'd really be two spheres there or just one thing being unhelpfully described twice. It reminded me a lot of the arguments I used to have about identical particles and exchange symmetry back in my actual working life, just blown up from a lab into a whole toy cosmos.

Anyway, I'd love to see someone turn that into an actual short story instead of two philosophers arguing in the abstract — something with a real narrator or characters in it, maybe someone who lives on (or discovers) that perfectly symmetric little universe, kind of a Borges or Ted Chiang angle if that makes sense to you. You don't have to keep the spheres literally, some other stand in works fine too (duplicated minds, mirror rooms, twin planets, whatever), as long as it actually sits with the hard part of the question rather then just using "two identical things" as a cute twist at the end. Doesn't need to be long, I'm mostly curious what a story looks like that takes seriously the idea that two things could be utterly indiscernible and still be two, rather than one.
