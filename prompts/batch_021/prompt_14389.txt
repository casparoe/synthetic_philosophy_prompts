It turned into this whole beef between two guys in the club, Nikhil and Owen, that never actually got resolved and has been bugging me since. The setup from the handout: say you rank a window seat over an aisle seat, an aisle over a middle seat, but ALSO a middle seat over a window seat (so its a cycle, not a normal ranking). The argument is that someone could then milk you for money forever: you're in the middle seat, they offer to swap you to the aisle for a penny (you like aisle better so you pay up), then they offer to swap aisle for window for a penny (you pay again), then they offer to swap window back to middle for a penny (you still prefer middle to window, so you pay AGAIN) -- and now you're back exactly where you started, just minus three cents, and they can run this loop on you forever.

Nikhil's takeaway was that this proves having cyclic preferences is just irrational, period, because it makes you exploitable like that. Owen said that's too fast: all the pump actually shows is that a NAIVE person who doesn't see the pattern coming gets exploited -- if you just notice what's happening and refuse one of the later trades, you keep your cyclic preferences AND lose nothing, so the preferences were never the problem, only being caught off guard was. Nikhil shot back: "ok but which trade do you refuse? Say you refuse the last one, so you keep the window instead of trading back to middle. Owen's comeback was that refusing the trade doesn't mean he stopped preferring middle to window at all -- it's like Ulysses and the sirens: Ulysses still finds the song desirable in the moment, he just cares more about not getting shipwrecked, and nobody says that makes him irrational, so "sticking to a plan even when one step looks tempting" can't automatically count as incoherent either.
