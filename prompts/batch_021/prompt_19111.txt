So I got picked for jury duty for the first time last week — civil case, nothing dramatic, just a dispute between a guy and his former contractor over unfinished work — and it's been messing with my head since. There was basically no physical evidence either way, no photos, no paper trail for the one conversation the whole case hinged on. It just came down to whether you believed the guy's account that this conversation happened at all. During deliberation, one of the other jurors kept saying stuff like "his word alone isn't evidence of anything, we need something independent backing it up before it counts for anything," and a couple people nodded along like that was obviously true. But that felt off to me. Like, I believe my coworkers' accounts of meetings I missed all the time, I believed the mechanic about what was actually wrong with my car, I believe my sister when she tells me why she was late — none of that comes with corroborating evidence, I just believe it by default unless somethings seems fishy. Are we being irrational every time we do that?

Now I can't stop turning it over: is there some actual principled line between reasonably taking someone's word for something and just being credulous, or is the guy who kept pushing back right that a claim is basically worth nothing, evidence-wise, until something independent backs it up? We went back and forth on this for like 40 minutes during deliberation and never landed anywhere, and it's still bugging me days later.
