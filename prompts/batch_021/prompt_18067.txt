Several of my graduate students working on AI safety have been urging me to engage with the "embedded agency" literature that grew out of the MIRI research agenda, most notably Scott Garrabrant and Abram Demski's 2018 expository sequence published on the AI Alignment Forum. That work contrasts "embedded" agents — reasoners who are smaller than, and causally entangled with, the very environments they must model and act within — with the idealized "Cartesian" or "dualistic" agents presupposed by classical decision theory and Bayesian epistemology. I am putting together a graduate seminar on rational agency and self-knowledge for next term, and while I can follow the technical exposition — the realizability and grain-of-truth problems for embedded world-models, robust delegation and tiling agents, subsystem alignment, and so on — I have not yet worked out what, if anything, this framework should change in how philosophers think about agency once it is taken outside its original context in AI design.

I would therefore like your help thinking this through. Please set out what you take to be the most important implications of the embedded agency framework for at least three debates that will feature prominently in the seminar: first, whether an agent could in principle possess a complete and accurate model of itself, given that any such model must itself be part of the very system it purports to model; second, the rationality of binding precommitments and Ulysses contracts as devices for managing one's own future conduct, given that the "future self" being bound is not a distinct agent but a continuation of the very system doing the binding; and third, the plausibility of sub-personal or "multi-agent" pictures of the mind that are sometimes invoked to explain akratic action. I am less interested in a summary of the AI safety literature on its own terms than in a philosophically substantive verdict on what, if anything, changes once embeddedness is taken seriously as a constraint on any real reasoner — human or artificial — rather than abstracted away as a mere implementation detail.
