I spent something like thirty-five years doing radio astronomy, and a good chunk of that was fighting with maximum-entropy deconvolution algorithms to clean up dirty interferometer images. Since retiring I've been working through formal epistemology with a little reading group of other retired scientists (mostly ex-physicists and one very patient statistician), and a few weeks ago we ran into Bas van Fraassen's old "Judy Benjamin" puzzle about updating on uncertain evidence, and it's been bugging me because it uses the exact same machinery I used to trust for entirely different reasons.

Quick recap in case its useful: Judy Benjamin is dropped into terrain divided into four equally likely quadrants -- Red Territory/First Company, Red Territory/Second Company, Blue Territory/First Company, Blue Territory/Second Company, each with prior probability 1/4 -- and she gets a garbled radio message saying that, conditional on being in Red Territory, she's three times as likely to be in the area held by Second Company as the one held by First Company. That's not the tidy "here are your new probabilities for a partition" evidence that Jeffrey's rule wants, so the fix people reached for (originally proposed by P.M. Williams, I believe, then turned against itself by van Fraassen) is to update by minimizing cross-entropy relative to the prior subject to that one constraint, as the natural generalization of Jeffrey conditionalization to this kind of case. Here's the sting: if you actually grind through the Lagrange multipliers (I did it longhand on a rainy afternoon, so don't just trust me), the probability that Judy Benjamin is in Red Territory at all *drops* below one half, purely as a consequence of a constraint that, on its face, is only about how the odds split up inside Red Territory -- even though the split between Blue's two areas stays perfectly symmetric the whole time. Van Fraassen treats this as a real embarrassment for the whole minimum-cross-entropy approach, since it looks like the rule is smuggling in information about Red-versus-Blue that the evidence never actually supplied.

I've become half convinced that this isn't actually a mark against minimizing cross-entropy at all, and that the "obviously correct" alternative -- freeze P(Red Territory) at one half and redistribute probability 1:3 only within Red -- is the one smuggling in an unearned assumption, namely that P(Red Territory) is somehow evidentially walled off from a constraint about relative frequencies inside it. So here's what I'd like from you: argue, as rigorously as you can, for the position that the cross-entropy-minimizing update really is the rationally correct response to the Judy Benjamin evidence, and that the intuition driving the "paradox" -- that P(Red Territory) has to stay put -- is what we should give up, not the update rule. Please take the objections seriously (van Fraassen's own worry, and the general "irrelevant conditioning" complaint against minimum-cross-entropy updating more broadly) rather than just declaring that the math is the math and moving on.
