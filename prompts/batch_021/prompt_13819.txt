Over the past year I've fallen into the habit of reading AI safety and decision theory blogs in my spare time. I have no formal background in philosophy or economics, so I've mostly had to reconstruct the arguments myself from scattered posts rather than from any structured source, and one idea I keep coming back to without ever feeling I fully understand it is the notion of a "surrogate goal" as a defense against acausal threats. As far as I can piece together, the basic move goes like this: an agent might worry that some other agent it has no causal contact with — a distant superintelligence, say, or a simulation of itself run by one — will try to extort it by threatening something it genuinely values. Its defense is to commit, in a way the threatening agent is somehow able to predict or verify, to treat some separate and much less important goal as though it were the thing it cared about most, so that a rational extortionist, recognizing this, targets the decoy goal instead of the real one, since threatening the decoy is what actually produces concessions.

Three things about this keep bothering me, though, and I'd like your help sorting through them. First, if the whole scenario depends on the threatening agent being able to see how the target actually reasons or is disposed to act — which seems to be the only way an acausal threat could get off the ground in the first place — why wouldn't that same insight let it see through the surrogate goal and threaten the real one anyway? Second, even granting that the redirection works, I don't understand why the agent would actually go through with "paying out" once its surrogate goal is threatened, since that looks like exactly the credibility problem that made it exploitable to begin with, just relocated onto a new target rather than solved. And third, I can't tell how much of this is genuinely specific to acausal threats, where there is no shared history or external enforcement mechanism to fall back on, as opposed to being a general fix for extortion of any kind that merely happens to be illustrated with acausal examples. Could you walk through the logic of surrogate goals in enough detail to settle these three worries, ideally with a concrete toy scenario showing exactly where and why the strategy is supposed to help?
