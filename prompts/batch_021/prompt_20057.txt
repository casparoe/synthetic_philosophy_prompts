I've been on this self-taught philosophy kick for a while now (no formal background, just books and wikipedia rabbit holes at night), and a couple weeks ago I got kind of obsessed with the Victorian philosopher Henry Sidgwick. He wrote this big dense book called The Methods of Ethics that's basically about whether looking after your own happiness or everyone's happiness is the more "rational" thing to do, and it famously just ends without fully resolving that tension, he openly admits near the end that the two don't quite line up. I actually respected that, it felt more honest then a lot of philosophy that pretends to wrap everything up neatly.

Then I found out the same guy spent like the last 20-30 years of his life co-running the Society for Psychical Research, testing mediums, going to seances, chasing down evidence for telepathy and ghosts and life after death, pretty much right up until he died. Every summary of his life I've read just drops this in as a fun bit of Victorian colour, "oh and he was also into ghost hunting, how quirky," totally seperate from the actual ethics stuff, like it's just what bored upper-class Cambridge academics did for kicks back then. So I guess my question is: given that the psychical research was basically a personal side hobby that had nothing to do with the real philosophical arguments in his book, why do serious write ups of his philosophy even bother bringing it up instead of just treating it as random biographical trivia? And does it maybe say something bad about his rigor that a guy this careful and skeptical in his actual philosophy would waste decades chasing what turned out to be a dead end?
