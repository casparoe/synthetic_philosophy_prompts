I'm a philosophy PhD student preparing for comprehensive exams in formal epistemology, and I've run into a gap in my understanding of the Dutch Book argument for probabilism that the standard surveys don't quite close. The Dutch Book theorem itself is just mathematics: credences that violate the probability axioms let a bookie construct a set of bets, each one fair by the agent's own lights, that jointly guarantee a loss. But turning that into a verdict that incoherence is irrational seems to require something further, since as stated the theorem only establishes a hypothetical vulnerability to an omniscient, adversarial bookie. My understanding is that Christensen's proposal to "depragmatize" the argument is meant to close exactly this gap, by relocating the defect from the threat of a guaranteed loss to an inconsistency already built into how the incoherent agent evaluates the bets themselves. Could you explain how that relocation is actually supposed to work, why it's thought to survive the standard complaint that the bookie's omniscience is doing all the real work, and whether the same strategy carries over cleanly to the diachronic Dutch Book for conditionalization -- or whether updating over time introduces problems depragmatizing doesn't touch?
