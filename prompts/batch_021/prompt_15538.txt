I sit on the AI safety review board at a startup that builds autonomous agents to negotiate on behalf of clients in salary negotiations, vendor contracts, and insurance settlements. The engineering team has proposed a feature it expects to meaningfully improve client outcomes: at the very start of a negotiation, the agent immediately and publicly commits to its walk-away position ("I am authorized to offer no more than $X, and this position will not be revised"), on the reasoning that whoever commits first and most credibly typically captures the larger share of the surplus -- a well-established point in bargaining theory. Two of us keep coming back to a worry we've encountered in AI safety writing under the label "commitment races": if committing earlier, harder, and with less deliberation is always locally advantageous, then every firm deploying such agents has reason to make its own agent commit faster and more rigidly than the last one, and once two sufficiently rigid agents land on opposite sides of the same negotiation with incompatible commitments, the deal collapses in a way that leaves both clients worse off than if neither agent had committed early at all. The engineering team counters that any safeguard we impose -- a mandatory deliberation window, a ceiling on how extreme or irrevocable a commitment can be, a human sign-off before it goes public -- simply hands the advantage to whatever competitor's agent isn't so encumbered.

Please draft the memo I'll circulate to the board ahead of Friday's vote. It should recommend approving the feature as proposed, rejecting it, or approving it subject to specific design conditions, and it should reason through the disagreement in enough detail that the engineering team could respond to it point by point rather than dismiss it as generic risk-aversion. In particular, I'd like the memo to treat the first-mover-advantage argument as a genuine strategic fact rather than a bias to be designed away, and to address concretely whether -- and how -- an individual firm could capture most of the benefit of credible commitment without contributing to the race dynamic across the industry.
