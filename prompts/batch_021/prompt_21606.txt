I spent thirty-odd years as an experimental physicist before retiring, and lately I've fallen down a philosophy rabbit hole -- right now I'm stuck on the grandfather paradox. Thing is, there are two genuinely serious ways people dissolve it that never seem to get put in the same room. One side (goes back to that old David Lewis paper, and seperately to the physicists' "self-consistency" idea out of the wormhole-time-machine literature) says sure, climb into the machine and take your shot at grandad -- you'll just fail every single time, not by magic, but because reality only has room for the one timeline that actually happened. The other side (basically Deutsch's quantum take on closed timelike curves) says no, you really can kill him, it's just that pulling the trigger is what shunts you into a different branch of the multiverse, so the "you" whose existence was ever at stake was never in that branch to begin with.

Could you write me a dialogue between someone holding each view, set right at the mouth of the machine while their friend is about to step through, gun in hand, determined to settle which theory's right by actually trying to shoot her grandfather as a young man? I want real back-and-forth, each going after the other's actual weak spot -- the "suspicious coincidence"/free-will problem for the first view, the ad-hoc-extra-universe (and doesn't-this-let-you-build-absurdly-powerful-computers) problem for the second -- not just each restating their own side. Keep them evenly matched, neither one a pushover.
