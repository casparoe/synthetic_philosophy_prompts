the question was: "Explain the core disagreement between Humeans and anti-Humeans about laws of nature, using Lewis's Best System Analysis and Armstrong's necessitation view as your central cases. Then explain van Fraassen's 'inference problem' for Armstrong's view, and say whether an analogous problem threatens the Best System Analysis."

the student's answer:

"Humeans think laws are nothing over and above patterns in how things actually are; anti-Humeans think laws are some further thing that makes those patterns hold, so that laws govern the world instead of just summarizing it. Lewis's Best System Analysis is the classic Humean view: take every true deductive system whose axioms are stated in terms of the perfectly natural properties instantiated across the whole history of the world (the 'Humean mosaic'), and the laws are the theorems of whichever system best balances simplicity against strength/informativeness.

Armstrong's anti-Humean alternative says laws are relations of natural necessitation, N(F,G), holding between universals rather than particulars. Once N(F,G) obtains it holds as a matter of metaphysical necessity, which is exactly what's supposed to give laws the power to support counterfactuals in a way mere regularities never could -- an F that lacked G in some other possible situation would be metaphysically impossible once the necessitation relation is in place.

Van Fraassen's inference problem says Armstrong hasn't actually explained anything: even granting N(F,G) as a further fact distinct from 'all Fs are Gs,' nothing has been said about why this extra fact should have any bearing on which particular things fall under F and G -- so positing N(F,G) doesn't help us infer which particulars will conform to the regularity. But this is just as much a problem for the Best System Analysis, since the very same gap between an abstract system and the particular facts shows up there too: knowing the axioms of the best system doesn't, by itself, tell you what happens at any particular time and place either."
