Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism, developed in his discussion of causality in *The Incoherence of the Philosophers*, holds that there is no necessary connection between cause and effect: when fire touches cotton and the cotton burns, the burning is created directly by God rather than by any power inherent in the fire, and the regularity we observe is only God's reliable habit ('āda), not a necessity in the nature of things. Suppose he were confronted with Bell's theorem, which rules out local hidden-variable explanations of quantum entanglement, and with the contemporary debate between Humean "best-system" theories of laws of nature and dispositionalist or "powers" theories that ground laws in things' own causal natures. Working from the actual structure of his argument rather than just its conclusion, would his occasionalism align with the Humeans, the dispositionalists, or neither -- and would he take quantum mechanics to vindicate his position, or merely to recreate, in a new guise, the very problem of necessitarian causation he was trying to escape?
