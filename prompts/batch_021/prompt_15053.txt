A few nights ago, my partner and I stayed up late arguing about whether to refinance our mortgage now or wait another year in case rates drop further. I laid out the whole case for refinancing step by step, walking through the break-even math, the risk of rates moving the wrong way, and a handful of other factors, and by the end I was completely convinced I was right. I'd also had a couple of glasses of wine over the course of the evening, though I didn't feel the least bit impaired at the time. The next morning, my partner brought this up half-jokingly, saying the decision had basically been made by the wine.

That comment has been bothering me more than it probably should. When I go back over the reasoning now, stone sober, I honestly can't find anything wrong with it: every step still checks out as far as I can tell. But I also know, in the vague way everyone seems to know this, that alcohol makes people overconfident and worse at noticing mistakes in their own thinking, even when they don't feel drunk at all. So I seem to be stuck holding two thoughts at once that don't sit well together: "I've gone through this argument carefully and it holds up," and "I have a genuinely good reason to think my ability to check it was compromised at the time I checked it." Sticking with my original conclusion at full confidence feels a little dishonest, but abandoning an argument I can't actually find anything wrong with feels just as strange.

I brought this up with a couple of friends and got completely opposite reactions: one said the wine is irrelevant once the reasoning has been re-examined sober, the other said the whole problem is that I can't fully trust my own re-examination either, since I'd be using the same brain that was supposedly compromised before. I'd like to actually work out which response makes more sense, rather than just going with whichever one happens to favor refinancing. Is there a sound way to decide how much my knowledge about the wine should lower my confidence in a conclusion that, on every inspection I'm currently capable of, still looks completely correct?
