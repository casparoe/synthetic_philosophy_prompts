So a colleague in the psych department told me about something one of her PhD students did, and it's been bugging me since. At n=40 the student's key comparison was sitting at p=.07, so instead of stopping there she just kept recruiting, checking in every so often, untill she hit p=.03 at n=76 and called it a day. When I raised an eyebrow, my colleague shrugged it off, saying the data is what it is regardless of when you decide to stop, so the evidence is exactly the same either way - and added that a Bayesian analysis of that exact final dataset wouldn't care one bit about the stopping rule. I teach philosophy, not stats, so I couldn't push back properly, but it still feels off, and it seems like exactly the sort of thing people blame for the replication crisis. Was the student's approach actually problematic, or is my discomfort just a frequentist intuition that doesn't survive scrutiny? And if a Bayesian genuinely wouldn't blink at this, what does that say about the disagreement between the two camps?
