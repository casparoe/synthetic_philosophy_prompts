I'm the outgoing chair of a youth grants committee that our local community foundation runs each year: about ten of us high schoolers get trained over the fall, spend the winter reviewing applications from small nonprofits, and then decide together each spring how to divide roughly $10,000 among them. For the past two cycles we've scored applicants mainly on projected impact -- for each one we estimate how many people a program will reach, how much it will help them, and at what cost per person, basically borrowing the logic that sites like GiveWell use to rank global health charities, and we've funded whichever combination of grants scores highest on that measure. This year one of our newer members, who's been reading about effective altruism on her own, keeps raising an objection she calls "cluelessness": she argues that any grant we make will ripple outward through the community for years afterward -- who gets hired or laid off, which families stay in the district versus move away, which kids end up in whose classroom -- in ways we have no way of tracing, and that we have no actual grounds for assuming those unforeseeable effects are small or will cancel out rather than swamping whatever short-term benefit we can actually measure. If she's right, she says, our whole scoring system is an illusion of rigor rather than an improvement on guessing. Most of the committee's response has just been that this can't be a good reason to give up on comparing applicants, since we obviously have to decide somehow -- and when I raised the disagreement with friends outside the committee, they split along the same lines without really engaging with each other's arguments.

We meet this Thursday to finalize how we'll score this year's eleven applications, and as chair I'm supposed to send everyone a short memo beforehand recommending how to proceed. I'd like it to take my committee member's objection seriously on its own terms, rather than just brushing past it the way we have so far, but it still needs to leave the committee with something concrete and usable by Thursday, since the grant decisions themselves can't wait on us resolving the philosophical question first. Could you draft that memo for me?
