So Frege kept a private diary for about a month in 1924 that only came out decades after he died, and its got some genuinely ugly stuff in it -- antisemitic remarks (e.g. that Jews shouldn't have full political rights), contempt for parliamentary democracy, a preference for authoritarian rule. Kind of jarring given the guy basically invented modern formal logic. The instinctive reaction is "who cares, judge the Begriffsschrift and 'On Sense and Reference' on their own terms, this is just biography." Argue against that instinct -- make the strongest case you can that there's a real, substantive link between specific parts of his logic/philosophy of language (the demand that concepts have sharp boundaries, his anti-psychologism, the "third realm" of mind-independent thoughts from "Der Gedanke") and the political temperament in the diary. Don't just say "he was rigid in both areas" -- tie it to the actual doctrines.
