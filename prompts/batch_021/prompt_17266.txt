I've spent a good deal of my free time teaching myself about Kolmogorov complexity, mainly because I wanted a rigorous answer to the old complaint that "simplicity," as it figures in Occam's razor, is just a subjective, language-relative notion. What convinced me the complaint has finally been answered is the invariance theorem: it guarantees that any two universal Turing machines can disagree about the complexity of a given string by no more than a fixed constant that doesn't depend on the string itself. So no matter how you choose to encode your hypotheses, the length of the shortest program that generates one of them is, up to that constant, an objective fact about it rather than an artifact of the language you happened to pick. Doesn't Solomonoff's use of this measure to formalize induction therefore give Occam's razor a genuinely machine-independent foundation? And if so, why do philosophers of science still treat the question of which of two theories is "simpler" as hostage to an arbitrary choice of description language?
