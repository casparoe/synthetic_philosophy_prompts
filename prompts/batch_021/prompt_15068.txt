I spent my working life as an evolutionary biologist, and one of the first things drilled into us was to say 'teleonomy' instead of 'teleology', precisely so you never accidentally imply evolution is aiming at some goal. Now that I'm retired and reading philosophy of mind for fun, I keep bumping into 'teleosemantics' -- Millikan's idea that a mental state gets its content from the 'proper function' it was selected for -- and honestly it smells like teleology sneaking back in through the side door to do the heavy lifting on meaning. Is the historical, selection-based framing actually enough to keep this naturalistic, or is it just a verbal trick that relabels the same old purposiveness everybody agreed to ditch after Darwin? Genuinely curious what im missing here.
