I've been chewing on Harry Frankfurt's argument against the idea that you need to be able to do otherwise to be morally responsible for something - the classic setup where Jones does some action on his own, while Black (some scientist who's rigged up Jones's brain) stands by ready to force his hand but never actually has to, since Jones goes ahead and does the thing anyway. The standard takeaway is that Jones is responsible even though he literally couldn't have chosen differently, so the whole "principle of alternative possibilities" idea is supposedly false.

Here's my hangup though: for Black to know he doesn't need to step in, there has to be some sign beforehand that tips him off to what Jones is gonna do. And it seems like either that sign guarantees Jones's choice, which just feels like determinism sneaking in the back door and quietly assuming the very thing that's in question, or it doesnt guarantee it, in which case Jones could'a given a different sign and gone another way - so there was some alternative possibility hiding in there after all. Either way it seems like the thought experiment doesn't actually prove what everyone says it proves.

Can you argue the other side here and really try to convince me that Frankfurt's argument survives this problem anyway? Not the wishy-washy "well he still had no choice in the moment" answer - I want the strongest real case for why my objection doesn't hold up.
