I'm on the search committee for my philosophy department's tenure-track opening in philosophy of mind. Our strongest finalist works on integrated information theory: her job talk defended IIT's causal-structure account of consciousness against the 'unfolding argument' and argued that deriving the theory from phenomenological axioms is a virtue, not the liability critics take it to be. Several senior colleagues want to rank her below our second finalist -- a philosopher of neuroscience uncommitted to any single theory of consciousness. Their reasoning: IIT was publicly branded pseudoscience in a widely covered 2023 open letter signed by well over a hundred consciousness researchers, timed to get ahead of adversarial-collaboration results some read as partly vindicating the theory, and a decades-long hire built on it is a bad bet against the field's own verdict. Others on the committee think deferring to a letter-writing campaign is exactly what a philosophy department shouldn't do, and that the demarcation question is one we're better placed than most to adjudicate on the merits. I need to circulate a memo before Monday's full-department vote recommending how to rank the two finalists: it should reach a specific recommendation, and it should treat the pseudoscience charge seriously enough to say what would actually have to be true of her work for it to count as a good reason to rank her lower, rather than waving the charge away or letting it settle the matter by itself.
