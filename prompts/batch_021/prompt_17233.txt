I spent almost 40 years as an atmospheric chemist before retiring, and now that I've got time on my hands I've been slowly working through some of the philosophy I never had room for during my career (my wife jokes that I've traded ozone models for Kant and Hegel, and honestly that's about right). Lately I've been slogging through Hegel's Philosophy of Right, and I got hooked on the sections in the 320s-330s where he talks about the state's relation to other states and, in particular, war -- partly because he tosses in this meteorological analogy at one point, something about winds keeping the sea from stagnating the way a becalmed pond would, which amused me professionally more than it probably should have.

Anyway, I went down a rabbit hole reading about how later people reacted to this material. From what I can tell, Russell and especially Popper (in Open Society) read Hegel's remarks on war as him basically glorifying conflict and treating individuals as disposable for the sake of the state's "ethical health" -- practically a proto-fascist reading. But I also came across some pushback (I want to say it was Avineri, or maybe a discussion of Avineri's work, my memory's fuzzy) suggesting that's a caricature, and that the actual logical structure of the argument is closer to a realist point about there being no sovreign authority above states to enforce anything like Kant's perpetual peace -- so war, in Hegel's system, functions more like the Hobbesian state of nature persisting between states than like some romantic endorsement of bloodshed.

I don't have the training to referee this myself, so here's what I'd like from you: please construct the strongest possible argument that this "realist/deflationary" reading is basically correct and that the Russell/Popper "militarist" reading is a misreading of the text -- not just a wishy-washy "it depends how you read it," but an actual argument that engages with the parts of the Philosophy of Right that Russell and Popper would presumably lean on, and shows why they don't get you all the way to the militarist conclusion they draw. I'd definately like you to also flag, honestly, whether there's some genuinely strong objection to the deflationary reading that you can't fully answer, rather than just papering over it.
