I've lived in the small town I grew up in my whole life, and for the past year I've been one of the volunteer members of our town's zoning board, which is not something I ever expected to say about myself. Lately we've been consumed by a fight over a proposal to let a logistics company build a large distribution warehouse just inside our town line. Only people registered to vote in our town get any say in that decision, which nobody had ever thought twice about before, but this case has made me genuinely uneasy: the trucks in and out of that warehouse will mostly run along a county road that passes through the unincorporated area just across our border, so it's the dozen or so households out there, not any of us, who will live with the noise, the exhaust, and the wrecked pavement, and they have no vote in our town at all. Meanwhile plenty of people who did get to vote live on the opposite side of town and will barely know the warehouse exists.

When I suggested at a meeting that we should at least give some weight to those outside households before voting, another board member pushed back in a way I haven't been able to answer. He said that once "who will be affected" becomes the test for who gets a say, there's no natural place to stop, since a little extra noise and traffic radiates out for miles in every direction; you'd either have to draw a new line somewhere else, which would just raise the same question about who gets to vote on where that line goes, or admit that almost anyone, anywhere, has some tiny claim to a say in almost anything. His conclusion was that we might as well keep using the existing town boundary, precisely because it's already settled on paper, even though he agreed it wasn't drawn with this kind of decision in mind and is arbitrary in that sense. It still seems wrong to me that the people who'll actually live with the consequences are shut out while people who won't are the ones deciding, but I no longer feel sure my instinct is more than a feeling. Is there an actual principled way to work out who should get a say in a decision like this one, or was he basically right that any line we draw is arbitrary in the same way the town border is?
