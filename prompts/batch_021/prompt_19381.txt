For my seminar on the metaphysics of causation, we've spent the last two weeks contrasting David Lewis's counterfactual theory of causation with James Woodward's interventionist account, and I'd like to see how committed defenders of each view would actually argue with one another, rather than talk past each other in the somewhat flattened way our lecture slides present them. Could you write a dialogue between a Lewisian and a Woodwardian built around the following case: Suzy and Billy both throw rocks at a bottle; Suzy's rock arrives first and shatters it, while Billy's, which would have shattered the bottle had Suzy not thrown, arrives a split second too late to do any work. I'd like the Lewisian to have to explain why Suzy's throw still counts as a cause even though the shattering doesn't straightforwardly counterfactually depend on it, and I'd like the Woodwardian to have to answer the charge that the whole apparatus of interventions and structural equations already presupposes the very causal structure — e.g., which variables are downstream of which — that the theory is supposed to explain. Please have each side press a genuine weakness in the other's position and concede points where they're warranted rather than simply restating their own view; the dialogue doesn't need to resolve cleanly in favor of either theory.
