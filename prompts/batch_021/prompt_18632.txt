Got into it with my friend Jordan last night about the 'driver score' his insurance app gives him — he's had his license under two years and pays way more than a buddy whos been driving for 15. The insurer's defense: drivers who land on the same score, say an 80, file a claim that year at basically the same rate no matter their experience. But Jordan knows somebody on the data team there who ran the numbers, and among drivers with zero claims all year, way more of the new ones still got stuck with a low 'risky' score than the zero-claim veterans — meaning two equally safe drivers can get treated totally differently just depending on which group they're in. I said that's just what happens when one group files more claims overall, its not the app's fault. He says 'it averages out correctly' is a cop-out if safe people still get screwed by it. We argued for like an hour and neither of us budged — who's actually got the stronger case, and could a score even be built where both things we care about hold at once, or is that just not possible?
