So I'm doing my M.Div, and this term I'm in a philosophy of religion seminar that just spent two weeks on Molinism and the grounding objection, which has turned out to be way more relevant to my life than I expected, because I'm also the (non-voting, but I take notes and draft stuff) student member on my presbytery's candidates and credentials committee. One of the guys coming up for licensure this cycle wrote, in his doctrinal exam, that he holds a Molinist account of providence: God's middle knowledge of what every possible free creature would do in every possible circumstance is logically prior to his decree, so God provides for everything and stays fully sovereign by choosing which world to create out of the "feasible" worlds those counterfactuals leave him, rather than unilaterally determining what anybody does. He's filed this as a declared exception to the part of our confession's chapter on God's eternal decree that says God "freely and unchangeably" ordains whatsoever comes to pass, and he's arguing pretty forcefully that his view secures just as much real providence and soverignty, only without determinism, so it isn't out of accord with the system of doctrine.

The committee is basically split three ways, and we meet again Tuesday to settle our recommendation before it goes to presbytery. One camp thinks this is just libertarian free will smuggled in under fancy Jesuit vocabulary and should sink his candidacy outright. Another camp points out that middle knowledge is a live position even some Reformed-leaning philosophers take seriously, and that the confession never actually settles the metaphysics of how God knows his own decree, so this should get treated like any other permissible exception. A third camp doesn't even care about the sovereignty question -- they think the real problem is the grounding objection, i.e. that there's nothing to make counterfactuals about a person's free choices true "before" that person (or the circumstances they'd be in) exists, so the whole framework is incoherent regardless of whether it protects sovereignty. Could you draft the short written recommendation I'll bring Tuesday -- accept the exception, reject it, or accept with some qualification -- with reasoning our chairman and the candidate himself could actually engage with, instead of just quoting the confession at him?
