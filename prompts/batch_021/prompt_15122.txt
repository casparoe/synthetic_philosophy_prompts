For years, my book club has argued about whether listening to an audiobook counts as "reading" a book, and the discussion never really goes anywhere: each side just restates its own definition of "reading" and nobody changes their mind. While looking into why arguments like this happen, I came across an idea from the philosophical literature on metaontology, associated especially with David Chalmers, that a great many disagreements which feel deep and factual are actually "merely verbal." The thought is that the two sides are silently attaching different meanings to the key term, and once those meanings are made explicit and separated out, there is no remaining disagreement about the facts — only a further question of which meaning the word ought to carry going forward. Please describe the most important implications of this way of thinking about verbal disputes for my book club's audiobook argument. Specifically, I would like to understand how I might actually go about testing whether our disagreement is merely verbal in this sense, and, if it turns out that it is, whether that means there is simply no truth to the matter of whether audiobooks are "really" reading, or whether something substantive can still hang on which definition of "reading" we end up adopting.
