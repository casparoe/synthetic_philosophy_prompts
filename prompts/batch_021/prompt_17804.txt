I somehow ended up on the small grants panel for a shoestring foundation that funds speculative physics-and-philosophy work too weird for the NSF, basically a mini version of those foundational-questions-in-physics grant programs. This round's headache is a postdoc's proposal asking for a year of funding to argue we should prefer multiverse "measures" (the rule for assigning probabilities across regions of an eternally inflating spacetime) that avoid Boltzmann brain domination -- the fact that a de Sitter universe running forever eventually cooks up, via random thermal fluctuations, way more freak brains with memories just like ours than there are genuine evolved observers, so on most measures a randomly sampled member of your reference class (in the Bostrom sense) should expect to be one of those. Her framing: this is a legitimate theoretical constraint, the same kind of move cosmologists already make with things like the flatness problem. Two panelists think its circular instead -- you can't tally what fraction of observers are Boltzmann brains without already picking a measure to do the tallying, so "no measures with too many Boltzmann brains" just smuggles in whatever prior you wanted and calls it a physics result. I've gotta circulate the panel's decision -- fund, reject, or fund with conditions -- by Friday. Can you draft that memo? Whatever you recommend, it needs to actually be a recommendation, not a "more research is needed" dodge, and it has to seriously engage whichever objection ends up on the losing side -- the circularity worry if you fund her, or the flatness-problem analogy if you don't -- instead of waving it off in a clause.
