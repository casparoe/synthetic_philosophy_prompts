so I got into it with my friend Dan last night about the lobian obstacle stuff (the self-trust thing for ai agents that comes out of Lob's theorem) and I dont think either of us actually won, so I wrote up my side as notes for a blogpost and want a sanity check before I post it anywhere. can you read through and tell me if my argument actually holds together or if im just fooling myself?

quick context in case it helps: the "obstacle" is that a proof-based agent can't in general prove a blanket rule like "whenever my successor proves some action is safe, it actually is safe" — because that schema has basically the exact shape Lob's theorem forbids (if you could prove P just from the provability of P, you'd already be able to prove P outright, no free lunch). so a fully self-identical successor can never be trusted "for free" the way you'd want it to be.

Dan's position was that this is basically a solved problem, two ways. (1) you don't need an identical successor — you build a ladder of successors each running on a slightly stronger theory that can vouch for the one directly below it, and since the ladder can be as long as you like, the loss is negligible for any real purpose. (2) or you drop proof entirely and have the agent reason in calibrated probabilities about its successor's actions instead of demanding proofs, since Lob's theorem is specifically about weird properties of the provability predicate and theres no obvious reason a probabilistic reasoner has to inherit that limitation.

my position, which I defended for like an hour straight, is that both of those are relocations, not actual solutions. the ladder never gives you an agent that trusts an exact copy of itself, full stop — you've just made the failure "small" by shoving it up a level each time, and there's still no top rung where the property actually holds. and swapping proof for probability doesnt dissolve the issue either, it just redefines what "trust" means, from certain-and-verified to well-calibrated, which is a real concession not a free win, and honestly I'd bet there's some analagous limitation lurking for probabilistic self-reference too even if it's not literally Lob's theorem doing the work. so my actual claim is: no sufficiently expressive, consistent, self-referential reasoner gets unconditional trust in an unmodified copy of itself, and every proposed fix works by quietly smuggling in an asymmetry (a stronger outside vantage point, or a downgrade from certainty to calibration) instead of actually closing the loop.

that's the whole post basically. specific things I want your take on:
1. is my "relocation not solution" framing actually fair to Dan's probabilistic option, or am I strawmanning the side I understand less well
2. is there a tighter way to explain why the ladder-of-theories fix doesnt count as solving it rather than just hiding it
3. am I overreaching with the last line about literally ANY self-referential reasoner — should I walk that back to just proof-based ones specifically
