For an independent research paper in my school's philosophy elective, I've been reading about the conditional analysis of dispositions: the idea that to say a glass is fragile is just to say that it would break if struck. Two classic counterexamples are meant to refute this analysis: finkish dispositions, where the very stimulus that would test the disposition causes it to be gained or lost right before it can manifest, and masked (or "antidoted") dispositions, where the disposition remains fully intact but something else intervenes to block its manifestation. Could you compare these two problems for me? Specifically, I want to know whether a fix designed to handle finks, such as David Lewis's Reformed Conditional Analysis, is also capable of handling masking, or whether masking poses a deeper and distinct challenge for anyone trying to save a conditional-style analysis of dispositions.
