David Lewis's triviality result (1976) shows that no non-trivial probability function can satisfy the Equation — the thesis that the probability of an indicative conditional "if A, then B" equals the conditional probability of B given A — for arbitrary A and B. Working independently, Allan Gibbard argued, via his well-known "Sly Pete" poker case, that any indicative conditional satisfying Import-Export together with a handful of otherwise plausible logical principles must collapse into the material conditional. Compare these two results: what exactly does each purport to establish, and what different conceptions of the indicative conditional does each one target? Could a theorist coherently accept the moral of one argument while resisting the other, or do the two ultimately stand or fall together? Defend your view on which result poses the more serious obstacle to any theory on which indicative conditionals express robust, non-material propositions.
