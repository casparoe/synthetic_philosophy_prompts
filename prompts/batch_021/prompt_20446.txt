In a reading group on formal epistemology, we have been working through Garrabrant, Benson-Tilsen, Critch, Soares, and Taylor's paper on logical induction, and a disagreement arose that I would like an outside opinion on. As I understand the setup, a logical inductor is generated by a market in which a countable collection of efficiently computable trading strategies bet play money on the truth values of arithmetical sentences over a sequence of trading days; a sequence of market prices counts as a logical inductor just in case no single efficiently computable trader can exploit it for unbounded profit as trading continues without limit. One of the framework's headline results is that whenever a sentence is eventually proved or refuted by the underlying deductive process, the inductor's credence in it converges to 1 or 0 respectively — essentially because leaving the price wrong once a proof exists would hand some trader free money.

One member of the group took this further, arguing that logical induction effectively erases the distinction between "open" and "settled" mathematical questions. Her reasoning was that the exploitability argument behind convergence does not care what a sentence is about, only whether money can be made off a mispriced bet, so running the trading process long enough should push the price of any well-formed arithmetical sentence — including a presently open conjecture like the Riemann hypothesis, or even a statement independent of our axioms, like the continuum hypothesis relative to ZFC — arbitrarily close to 0 or 1. On her reading, logical induction amounts to a general, if computationally expensive, method for resolving mathematical questions, and "we simply have not traded long enough yet" is really the only thing standing between us and an answer to any of them. I found the argument fairly persuasive, but before repeating it in a paper I am writing, I would like to check: does the convergence guarantee actually reach that far, and is "more trading days" really the right way to think about what separates us from settling an open conjecture?
