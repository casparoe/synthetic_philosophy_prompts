So this is going to sound like an odd ask, but bear with me. An old editor of mine talked me into judging the nonfiction category for a mid-size foundation's annual science-writing award this year -- small enough you won't have heard of it, so let's just call it "the Prize." There are five of us on the panel, and the charter is oddly precise about what counts as prize-worthy: a finalist wins only if it clears at least two of three named bars -- "rigor" (the science actually holds up), "originality" (it's not a rehash of stuff that's already out there), and "craft" (it's genuinely well written, not just important). We're deadlocked on the last finalist, a deep dive into a gut-microbiome study that turned out to be quietly p-hacked, and honestly the deadlock itself is way more interesting to me than the piece itself.

Three of the five of us gave the piece an overall thumbs-up, but for three different reasons -- I thought it nailed rigor and craft but was thin on originality, another judge thought it was original and rigorous but badly written, and a third thought it was original and beautifully written but scientifically shaky. The other two judges gave it a flat thumbs-down on all three bars. So the straight headcount is 3-2 in favor. But our coordinator, just out of curiousity, also tallied things bar by bar instead of judge by judge -- and on every single one of the three bars, "fails" beat "passes" 3-2. So depending on whether you add up judges or add up bars, the piece either wins clearly or fails every single thing it needed to clear to win at all. Half the panel wants to just certify the 3-2 headcount and move on; the other half says that's absurd when a majority of us rejected the piece on every named criterion, and wants the bar-by-bar result to stand instead. Both halves think the other is being dense about it, and we announce winners in nine days.

I cover this kind of stuff for a living and I'm annoyed at myself for not seeing this coming. Could you draft the short memo I'd circulate to the other four judges and the foundation's oversight board? It should recommend an actual course of action for this year's finalist, plus whatever tallying method you think the panel should commit to for future cycles so we're not refighting this every year, with a concrete point by which the board should adopt it. Don't give me a split-the-baby memo -- pick a side. But whatever you land on, deal honestly with the strongest thing to be said for the method you're rejecting, because I'll have to defend this to whichever half of the jury looses.
