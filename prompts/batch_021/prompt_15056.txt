Here's the passage:

"Moreover, it must be confessed that perception, and that which depends upon it, are inexplicable by mechanical causes, that is to say, by figures and motions. And, supposing that there were a machine whose structure produced thought, sensation, and perception, we could conceive of it as increased in size while retaining the same proportions, so that we might enter it as one enters a mill. Now, on going into it, we should find only pieces working upon one another, but never anything by which to explain a perception. This must be sought for, therefore, in the simple substance, and not in the composite or in the machine."
