I'm the token philosopher on my national swimming federation's Therapeutic Use Exemption committee (everyone else on it is a doctor or an endocrinologist), and we've got an application that's split the room right down the middle. One of our athletes has confirmed adult growth hormone deficiency from a pituitary tumour removed four years ago — that part isn't in dispute, she's got the imaging and two failed stimulation tests, so this isn't the usual "is this even a real diagnosis" fight people expect. The actual argument is about where her endocrinologist wants to titrate her IGF-1: he's recommending the upper end of the age-adjusted normal range because that's where her bone density and lean mass numbers look best, but that also happens to be the range our sports-science guy says gives the most competitive benefit. There's no test that can tell us whether a given blood draw reflects "restored to upper-normal" versus "quietly nudged past what she'd make on her own," and honestly the whole "normal range" she's being restored to was built from sedentary reference populations, not elite athletes, so it's not even obvious that's the right baseline to be restoring her TO in the first place.

Half the committee wants to just approve whatever her own doctor prescribes, since she has a genuine, undisputed disease and it feels wrong to make her prove a negative to compete; the other half wants to cap the exemption at the low-to-mid part of the range on the grounds that anything higher is enhancement wearing a doctor's note, even though nobody can actually say why mid-range is the principled cutoff rather than an arbitrary one. I need to draft the committee's actual decision memo — grant, deny, or grant-with-conditions, including a specific IGF-1 target and monitoring plan if we go that route — and I want it to seriously grapple with why this case is hard (the fact that "restore her to normal" stops being a clean idea once normal is a range rather than a point, and the reference population isn't obviously the relevant one for her) instead of just falling back on "defer to her own doctor" or "any performance benefit disqualifies it," since both sides on our committee already know those lines and neither finds them satisfying anymore.
