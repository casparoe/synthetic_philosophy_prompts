Ok this is going to sound a little unhinged but I've been down a math rabbit hole all week for a piece im writing and I need a reality check. Zorn's Lemma (the maximal-element-in-a-poset thing, same logical strength as the Axiom of Choice) is apparently the exact tool behind the Banach-Tarski paradox — you can cut a solid ball into a handful of pieces, something like five, and just rotate and slide them back together into two balls that are each identical to the original. No stretching, no melting anything down and adding more material. If thats a genuine theorem and not just a trick of notation, why isn't anyone in physics or materials science treating it as an actual blueprint for duplicating matter? Even a clunky first version of that machine would let you double your gold reserves or your fuel rods for free, which feels like exactly the sort of thing that should be setting off alarms about conservation of mass-energy. Is this purely an engineering problem nobody's cracked yet, or does Zorn's Lemma itself guarantee this can never make the leap off the chalkboard?
