I chair the steering committee for an open-source library of formalized mathematics, built on a proof assistant and used in several university verification courses as well as by one industry team that extracts certified code from our proofs for use in safety-critical systems. The library's core is intuitionistic, so a proof of an existence statement always yields a computable witness. A faction of contributors wants to add the law of excluded middle and the full axiom of choice as global axioms, so we can port large parts of classical analysis and set theory without re-proving everything constructively; they point out that local, explicitly-flagged classical axioms confined to individual files are already accepted as harmless. Several long-standing contributors resist this on principle, holding that a proof of existence that supplies no construction hasn't actually shown anything exists. Draft the decision memo I'll bring to our release-planning meeting: it should come down on one side -- global adoption, local-only axioms, or a separate classical fork of the library -- and it should engage the losing position seriously rather than caricature it.
