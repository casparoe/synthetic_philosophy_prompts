I host a podcast on philosophy of physics for a semi-technical audience, and next week's guest is a philosopher of physics who defends the Everettian claim that Bell's theorem never actually forced anyone to give up locality: what the theorem rules out is a local hidden-variable theory with a single, determinate outcome, and once outcomes are allowed to branch, the argument has no purchase. I've spent enough time on rationalist blogs and forums absorbing this line to know it cold, and I don't want the episode to just have her restate it. Draft eight to ten interview questions that push past the standard framing. I want at least one question each on: (1) why the popular gloss that 'Bell's theorem proves nonlocality' elides the gap between ruling out local hidden variables and ruling out locality itself, with a follow-up on why even physicists routinely blur that distinction; (2) the Jarrett decomposition of Bell locality into parameter independence and outcome independence, with a follow-up on whether calling a theory 'local' once it violates outcome independence is still physics or just relabeling; and (3) Wallace's own concession that Everettian mechanics has local dynamics but nonseparable, and hence nonlocal, states, with a follow-up on whether that concession already gives away most of what worried Einstein, and how it squares with the rival Deutsch-Hayden claim to recover full separability too. Also ask her to compare her strategy with Bohmian nonlocality and superdeterminism as rival responses to Bell, whether a frame-independent branching structure secretly requires a preferred foliation, and close with a question about what could actually count as evidence against her locality claim, given that every no-collapse rival reproduces identical statistics.
