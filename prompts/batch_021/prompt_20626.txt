"Intuitionistic logic rejects A ∨ ¬A as a theorem schema because on the BHK reading, to assert a disjunction you need a proof of one of the disjuncts together with knowledge of which one you've got. For an open problem like the twin prime conjecture we currently have neither a proof nor a refutation, so we're not entitled to assert P ∨ ¬P. This is why intuitionistic logic is standardly classified as a three-valued logic: alongside true and false there's a third status, undetermined, and the failure of excluded middle just reflects that a proposition can sit in that third bucket until somebody proves it or refutes it. Negation is defined constructively as well: ¬A is A → ⊥, a construction turning any proof of A into a proof of absurdity, which is a stronger notion than the classical primitive truth-functional operator. Reductio still works in one direction: if assuming A gets you a contradiction you're licensed to conclude ¬A, since that's just unpacking the definition of negation. What fails is going the other way, from a contradiction under ¬A to concluding A, since that move needs double-negation elimination, which intuitionistic logic drops. Brouwer worked all this out in the 1920s, formalizing his intuitionistic critique of Hilbert's formalism into the axiomatic logical calculus that now bears his name, building his alternative foundaton with the same kind of rigorous axiomatic method Hilbert himself championed."
