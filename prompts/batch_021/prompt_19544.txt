I've started using Lean at work to formalize a few proofs, and reading up on the history behind it has left me stuck on something I can't quite resolve. Hilbert wanted a finitary proof that arithmetic, and eventually all of mathematics, is free of contradiction, but Gödel's second incompleteness theorem showed that no sufficiently powerful consistent system -- including Peano Arithmetic and ZFC -- can prove its own consistency. As far as I can tell, this means there is no way to rule out the possibility that these systems are secretly inconsistent, and thanks to the principle of explosion, if ZFC really is inconsistent, then every well-formed statement and its negation are technically theorems, which would make any proof that Lean, or a human mathematician, has ever checked, worthless. Since the matter can apparently never be settled from inside the system itself, is trusting ZFC (or Peano Arithmetic, or any proof assistant built on top of them) ultimately just an act of faith? And if so, why doesn't this seem to bother working mathematicians nearly as much as it bothers me?
