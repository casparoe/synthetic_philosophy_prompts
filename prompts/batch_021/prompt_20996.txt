Two people at my job have been arguing for like a week about something that happened on our team and I can't tell who's right. Two different people each pasted a bad formula into a shared budget spreadsheet without checking it -- same careless mistake, same deadline pressure, neither one looked back over their own numbers. IT caught one of the errors before the report went out. Nobody caught the other for three weeks, and by then the department had over-committed something like $40k and a contractor's contract got cut short because of it.

At lunch, Renee said the second guy is obviously more to blame, since real money got lost and someone actually lost work over it. Marcus said that's crazy -- they did the literal same thing, the only difference is which spreadsheet IT happened to double-check, so the blame has to be identical. Renee said that's too clean: we never actually treat identical intentions as equally blameworthy anywhere, attempted stuff gets punished less then completed stuff even in law, and if Marcus's view were really true he'd have to say a drunk driver who kills someone isn't any more culpable than one who makes it home fine -- which nobody actually believes once it's their kid crossing the street. Marcus said the legal comparison doesn't help her, because most people who actually think it through agree that asymmetry is just there for practical reasons like deterrence and evidence, not because it tracks anything morally real, and plenty of people do bite the bullet on the drunk driving case once they're pushed on it, even if it feels harsh in the moment.

Can you go through this move by move and say who's got the better argument at each point? And is there any way to actually settle this, or are they just each grabbing onto one horn of a real dilemma?
