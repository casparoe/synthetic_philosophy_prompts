For a seminar on formal epistemology, I've been working through Bostrom's writings on anthropic reasoning, and a few of us in the reading group keep getting stuck on the same impasse: the self-sampling assumption and the self-indication assumption each seem to buy their way out of one embarrassing commitment only by taking on another. SSA avoids the wildly overconfident verdicts that SIA licenses in cases like the Presumptuous Philosopher, but only at the cost of something like the doomsday argument, plus the standing headache of how to fix a reference class; SIA escapes the doomsday argument cleanly enough, but then has to explain why pure self-locating reasoning should ever be allowed to settle empirical bets from the armchair. Nobody in the group agrees on which cost is worse, so rather than another summary of both positions side by side, I'd like to see the trade-off actually fought out.

Could you write a dialogue between a philosopher who accepts SIA and one who accepts SSA, set at the moment of Bostrom's Presumptuous Philosopher scenario itself: physicists in the year 2100 are about to run a costly, decisive experiment to decide between two cosmological theories that are empirically on a par except that one implies a trillion trillion observers exist in the cosmos and the other a trillion times that number, and the SIA-proponent has just risen to announce that the experiment is a waste of money. Have the SSA-proponent challenge this from the floor, and then have the SIA-proponent, once pressed, turn the objection around by pointing out that the SSA-proponent's own view commits them to an equally confident, evidence-free-seeming verdict about how soon humanity is likely to go extinct. Each side should engage the specific objection just raised rather than simply restate its own view: I'd like the SSA-proponent to say something substantive about whether narrowing the reference class actually escapes the doomsday-style commitment or just relocates it, and the SIA-proponent to address whether there is a principled reason to trust self-locating evidence in one of these cases and not the other. It's fine to leave the dispute unresolved, but neither side should simply concede, or be left holding an objection the other never has to answer.
