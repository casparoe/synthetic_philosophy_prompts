Two people in my philosophy program's Principia Ethica reading group have been arguing for weeks about Moore's claim that personal affection and the enjoyment of beauty are the two best things there are. Sana's take is that when you get them together -- appreciating something beautiful WITH someone you love -- they form a further "organic unity" worth more than the two added up seperately, and that this explains something real: its why people go out of their way to share art or a sunset with someone they love instead of just enjoying it solo. Jon says "organic unity" isn't an explanation, it's a shrug in fancy dress -- you could say any pairing you happen to like exceeds the sum of its parts (good food plus good weather, whatever) and nothing rules it out, since there's no independent way to weigh the parts against the whole except by already knowing the answer you want. Sana says that's unfair, because Moore doesn't invent the move just for this case -- he uses the exact same principle earlier in the book, like when he explains why taking pleasure in something vicious isn't simply worth pleasure-value plus vice-value, so it's a general, independently motivated idea and not an ad hoc patch for beauty-and-friendship specifically. Jon says having other uses doesn't give it content -- an empty label stays empty no matter how many cases you slap it on -- and what's actually missing either way is an account of why THIS combination bumps value up rather than down, since plenty of combinations obviously make things worse together. I sat through the whole thing and still couldn't tell you who actually won. Can you go through it move by move, say who's got the better of each exchange, and flag anything about Moore's actual view that both of them seem to be missing?
