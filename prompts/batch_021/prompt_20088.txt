quick one from the decision theory weeds -- I'm a phil PhD student doing dissertation work on FDT-adjacent stuff, and I keep bumping into the 'surrogate goals' idea for defusing acausal threats/blackmail: you commit to treat some cheap, low-stakes outcome as just as catastrophic as the actual thing you care about, so a rational extortionist targets that instead of the real thing. I get why that's supposed to help game-theoretically, but I can't shake the feeling it just relocates the vulnerability instead of removing it -- if you've genuinely commited to respond to threats on the surrogate exactly like you would to the real threat, then a threat on the surrogate just is a real threat against something you now disvalue enormously, so what's actually been gained? Can you spell out the trick that's supposed to let the commitment be credible to the threatener without it turning into an actual new terminal value your on the hook for? Every writeup I've found just gestures at this instead of explaining the mechanism.
