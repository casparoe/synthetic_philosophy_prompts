In the standing-to-blame literature, the usual move is that hypocrisy only kills your standing to blame someone if you're guilty of basically the same fault (or a "relevantly similar" one) -- because if literally any moral flaw counted, nobody would ever have standing to blame anybody for anything, since everyones flawed in some way. I think that restriction is kind of an ad hoc patch though. Can you argue that whatever actually explains why hypocrisy undermines standing (like implicitly exempting yourself from a standard you enforce on others) doesn't really care whether it's the same fault or not, so the view should honestly generalize into something close to global skepticism about standing to blame in general?
