Me and my friend Priya got into it pretty hard last night about our friend Theo's whole "Franklin thing." For something like five years now he's kept an actual notebook ruled out exactly like the chart in Franklin's autobiography — a page per virtue, a box for each day of the week, and he gives himself a little black mark whenever he slips (temperance, order, sincerity, the full thirteen, though he swapped the order of a couple to suit himself). Priya thinks it's kind of a sad, uptight thing to build your whole personality around; I was arguing there's something actually admirable in it, especially after I went back and reread the real chapter instead of just going off the meme version of "founding father invents to-do list." We never really settled it, and now im the one giving the best man speech at Theo's wedding on Saturday, so I want to build the speech around this exact argument instead of picking a side.

Here's the constraint that's got me stuck: I can't get up there and say Theo actually achieved moral perfection, because he plainly hasn't (his roomates from college will literally be sitting right there), and because Franklin himself, by his own account, never fully got there either — Order beat him completely, and there's that bit near the end of the whole project where he brings up the guy with the axe and the grindstone, basically wondering out loud whether a "speckled" axe, i.e. a character with a few permanent faults left in it, isn't the saner thing to settle for than grinding forever for a perfectly bright one. But I also don't want the speech to read as mocking Theo's project, because I genuinely don't think it's mockable, I think it's kind of moving. And half the room — his mom, his aunts, a couple of his work friends — have never heard any of this and definately won't get an inside joke about a virtue spreadsheet. Can you actually write the speech, something that'd run about five minutes read out loud? Use Franklin's own ambivalence about the project to say something true and toast-worthy about Theo — not just the "added Humility to the list, then joked about being proud of his own humility" line, since that's the one bit of this everybody already knows — without either overclaiming he's finished the project or being snide about the whole enterprise.
