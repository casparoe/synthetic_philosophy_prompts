Okay, this one's got a slightly ridiculous backstory but bear with me. My dad's a mostly-retired philosophy professor whose entire worldview, for as long as I can remember, has basically been P.F. Strawson's "Freedom and Resentment" -- his go-to line is that asking whether determinism is "really" true is the wrong question, and that as long as we've still got ordinary human reactive attitudes like resentment and gratitude and guilt, we don't need libertarian free will to justify holding people responsible, we just need to look honestly at how humans actually relate to eachother. We had another blowup about this at Thanksgiving over my brother -- he's screwed up again and Dad went on his usual "that's just who he's chosen to be" routine, point blank refusing to grant him any kind of pass -- and afterwards I went down a rabbit hole and found Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility. Then I discovered, a little deliriously, that Galen is literally P.F. Strawson's son. Probably means nothing philosophically but I can't stop thinking about it.

What I don't want is the generic "if determinism's true nobody's responsible" line thrown at my dad, because he's got a canned reply to that already: the reactive-attitudes picture was never trying to prove "ultimate" desert in the first place, so showing ultimate desert is impossible just changes the subject, it doesn't actually touch his account. I want something more surgical. Strawson senior's essay has this move where certain pleas ("he didn't mean it," "he was pushed") only excuse the particular act, while other things -- the person being a small child, being seriously deranged, having been warped by genuinely rotten formative circumstances -- get the WHOLE person exempted from the participant stance and shifted into what he calls the objective attitude. Can you argue that if the Basic Argument's regress is sound -- i.e. if literally nobody can be responsible for the tangle of nature-plus-formation that produced them, not because determinism is true but because that chain bottoms out somewhere nobody chose either way -- then the very condition Strawson senior reserves for exempting cases turns out to hold of every action anyone has ever performed, which should collapse his excuse/exemption line rather than leave it standing next to the Basic Argument as some separate, untouched subject? I especially want the argument to anticipate his best fallback, which is that he was only ever talking about proximate stuff like sanity and self-control, never ultimate self-origination -- and to show why that retreat quietly gives up exactly what he needs it to hold onto.
