For a little over two years I have been co-authoring a paper with a colleague from my PhD program -- I'll call him N -- on a fairly technical question about how professional forecasters revise their confidence intervals after being surprised by an outcome. The division of labor between us shifted more than either of us tracked carefully as the project went on, and we kept putting off the question of author order on the assumption that we would settle it once the paper's final shape was clear. Ten days ago, with the submission deadline closing in, N emailed our two other collaborators and our shared advisor making a detailed case for why he should be listed first, and phrased it as though this had already been agreed between us ("as we'd talked about, I'll take first authorship given how the project developed"), when in fact we had never discussed it in those terms. My first impulse was to reply to the same group within the hour with an equally firm case for why I should be first, before his version of events hardened into the one everyone simply remembered as true.

When I described this to my partner that evening, her advice was simply to send back something just as forceful, and fast. That felt incomplete to me, though I couldn't immediately say why. I have spent enough years reading LessWrong, Slate Star Codex/ACX, and similar sites to have a nagging sense that there is a real, worked-out answer to what is actually happening here -- something about why the person who stakes out a hard, public position first can gain an advantage simply by having moved first, and about why that fact tends to make both sides rush to stake out positions before they have thought them through, in a way that can leave everyone worse off than if neither had rushed -- but I cannot reconstruct the argument well enough to know whether it really applies to my situation, or what it implies I should do instead of simply "conceding" or "matching him." Is there a good way of thinking about what is actually going on here, and what would you advise me to do before we submit?
