Quick one for a piece im noodling on. Kant's classic objection to the ontological argument is that existence isn't a real predicate -- you can't fatten up a concept by tacking "and it exists" onto it, hence his hundred-thalers bit. Godel's modal version seems to sidestep that though: its not existence but *necessary* existence doing the work, smuggled in as one of his "positive properties" and derived through S5 modal logic rather than packed into a definition upfront. Say Kant somehow got handed Godel's proof -- do you think his objection still bites, or does the move to modal logic let Godel slip past it clean? And is there a Kantian-style comeback that would still land on Godel, even if the hundred-thalers point doesn't quite reach him?
