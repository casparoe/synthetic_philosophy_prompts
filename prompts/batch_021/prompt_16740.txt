So this happened after my Philosophy of Law discussion section (we're in the unit on constitutional precommitment right now, the Elster "Ulysses and the Sirens" stuff) and it's been bugging me since. Two people in my section, Owen and Nadia, got into it about whether things like supermajority amendment rules and judicial review can actually be justified the way our professor frames it: as a kind of Ulysses-tying-himself-to-the-mast move, where "the people" bind their future selves against their own panic or bad judgement.

Owen said yes, obviously: a constitution just is a precommitment device, so something like free speech cant get suspended the next time everyone's scared. Nadia said the analogy doesn't actually hold up, because Ulysses is the same guy who gets tied up and who's later straining against the ropes, whereas with a constitution its mostly dead people binding people who never agreed to anything - that's a "dead hand" problem, not self-control. Owen replied that every law binds people who didn't vote for it (nobody thinks a ten-year-old statute is illegitimate just because todays officials weren't around to pass it), so mere non-identity between the rule-makers and the people bound by it can't be the actual problem. Nadia said there's still a real difference: an ordinary statute can be repealed by whatever majority makes law today, but the whole point of entrenchment is that today's majority specifically can't undo it, so its not just old rules binding new people (that's true of all law) but old rules binding new people more strongly than new people can bind themselves. Owen's last move was that some of that asymmetry is actually unavoidable and enabling rather than purely restrictive: stuff like voting procedures and how amendments happen has to be insulated from whoever currently holds power, otherwise a temporary majority could just rewrite the rules that produce future majorities (gerrymandering, messing with who gets to vote, etc), so you can't object to entrenchment as such, only to which specific things get entrenched.
