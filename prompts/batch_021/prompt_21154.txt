Two kids at our table, Dana and Wes, got into a whole thing about what this actually proves, and neither one backed down, so I want someone smarter then me to sort it out.

Dana said this basically kills the whole Gettier industry: if the intuition that "he doesn't know" isn't stable across cultures, there's no fact being tracked, it's just philosophers dressing up one culture's folk concept as a universal truth about knowledge. Dana shot back: mistaken by what standard, and isn't it a little convenient that the standard everyone reaches for just happens to be whatever the mostly-Western-trained philosophers already believed — that's not a tiebreaker, that's begging the question. Wes said you could run that same "it's just bias" move against literally any expert consensus, including whatever science Dana wants to trust, so it can't be enough on its own to toss out trained philosophical judgment. Dana's last point was that Gettier's own original case was also just an intuition pump, so if intuitions are this culturally wobbly, the wobbliness goes all the way back to the start of the literature, not just to later refinements of it.
