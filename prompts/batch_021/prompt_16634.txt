I've been reading rationalist blogs and hanging around the associated forums for well over a decade, and a few years ago several of us who met through that scene started a small private forecasting group in the spirit of Tetlock's work on calibration, where the norm is that you attach an actual percentage to a belief rather than hiding behind words like "probably" or "unlikely." This discipline has served us well on questions with decent reference classes, but it broke down for me recently when we hit a question that felt qualitatively different from our usual sports scores and election calls: whether a specific emerging AI capability will trigger a serious regulatory crackdown somewhere in the world within the next five years, a question with essentially no relevant track record to anchor on. When my turn came, the most I could honestly offer was "somewhere between 5% and 35%, and I don't think I have grounds to be more precise than that." One of the sharper Bayesians in the group wouldn't let it rest: he argued that if my beliefs are genuinely coherent, some exact number is already implicit in them whether I can introspect it or not, and that offering a range instead is just hedging dressed up as rigor. To press the point, he offered to bet with me at prices corresponding to each edge of my range, reasoning that if 5% and 35% were really my honest boundaries, I ought to be willing to trade at either price. I found I didn't want to take either bet, and he treated my reluctance as proof that some single hidden number, not the range itself, was my actual belief.

That exchange has stayed with me, because his argument feels like it should be decisive, yet something about it still nags at me. Is he right that intellectual honesty about a genuinely thin-evidence question like this one has to bottom out in a single number, so that my discomfort with betting at either edge simply unmasks the exact number I was too timid to state? Or can a belief state be irreducibly spread across a range in a way that would make declining bets at both edges the rationally appropriate response, rather than a tell?
