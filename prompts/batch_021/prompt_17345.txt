I somehow ended up on our children's hospital's clinical ethics committee, mostly because I teach a grad seminar on rational choice and decision theory and someone decided that made me qualified. We're revising the informed-consent protocol for pediatric cochlear implantation and it's gotten heated. Right now parents just sign the standard surgical consent after one meeting with the audiology team; a faction on the committee, backed by some vocal folks from the local Deaf community, wants to require parents to also meet with Deaf adults (some implanted late, some never implanted) before they're allowed to consent, on the grounds that hearing parents have no real way to picture what they'd be choosing for their kid either way. The surgeons are furious, partly because they think it's concern trolling, but mostly because the critical window for spoken language acquisition means any mandated delay is itself doing harm to the very kid the policy claims to protect.

It's obviously a proxy-decision version of L.A. Paul's transformative experience problem -- neither the surgeons nor the Deaf mentors can hand the infant their own first-personal acquaintance with the "vampire"-style unknown on the other side, and testimony from either camp is exactly the third-personal substitute Paul argues can't do the rational work that's needed here. It's also a nastier case then the ones she usually discusses, since it's not even the deciding parties' own epistemic access that's at stake, it's someone else's, someone who doesn't yet have the standing to weigh in. Could you draft the actual memo I'd bring to next week's meeting? I need a real recommendation for what the consent protocol should require and on what timeline, not a summary of both sides, and I want whatever you recommend to actually reckon with the surgeons' harm-from-delay point rather than wave it away -- and if you think Paul's own proposed fix (valuing the revelation itself rather than the specific unknowable outcome) has anything to offer a proxy decision-maker who isn't the one who'll live the outcome, I'd like to see that reasoning too.
