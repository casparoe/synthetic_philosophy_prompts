I'm a PhD student in philosophy and the graduate student representative on my university's academic grievance committee. A student has filed a grievance arguing that his professor's syllabus line, "at least one unannounced quiz will be given before the midterm," could not validly authorize the quiz he actually received: a rational class, he says, can rule out the last possible day (since by then they would see it coming), then rule out the day before that by the same logic, and so on back to day one — so no day could ever count as "unannounced." He is, in effect, running the surprise exam paradox against his own grade. A committee member who works in game theory backs him, calling the clause incoherent as written; two longtime instructors, whose quizzes still reliably catch students off guard, call this a clever irrelevance. Please draft the short memo the committee will vote on next week: resolve the grievance, say whether "unannounced quiz" clauses may still appear in syllabi going forward (with model language if you would allow some version), and reason it through in a way that neither dodges the paradox nor lets it settle the matter by fiat.
