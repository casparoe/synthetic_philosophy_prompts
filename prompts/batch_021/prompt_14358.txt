I'm a physics PhD student working on the foundations of statistical mechanics, and over the last several sessions of a philosophy-of-physics reading group I belong to, we've been working through David Lewis's Principal Principle, starting with the original 1980 paper and moving into the responses it provoked. The core idea -- that your credence in a proposition, given its objective chance and any otherwise admissible evidence, should simply equal that chance -- struck me as close to self-evident on first encounter, and it matches how I already think informally about the Boltzmannian probabilities in my own work. What has genuinely stumped me is the 'undermining futures' problem that Ned Hall and Michael Thau independently raised against Lewis's original formulation in the mid-1990s, together with the 'New Principle' that Lewis proposed in response in "Humean Supervenience Debugged."

Here is my current, and probably somewhat shaky, grasp of the trouble. If chance is Humean -- if the chance distribution at a time is itself made true by the entire pattern of events across history, future included -- then for almost any chance distribution at t there seems to be some possible continuation of history, itself assigned positive chance by that very distribution, which would, if actual, make the best-system chance at t something other than what it is. Running that back through the Principal Principle looks less like a tension than a flat contradiction: conditional on 'the chance at t is ch,' your credence in that undermining continuation has to be zero, simply because the two propositions cannot both be true, yet the Principle simultaneously insists that this same conditional credence match ch's own positive verdict on that continuation.

Lewis's response, as far as I can execute the mechanics, is to stop conditionalizing credence on the bare chance at t and instead conditionalize on the actual history up to t together with a complete 'theory of chance,' matching credence to whatever chance that whole package would assign. I can follow this move step by step without yet feeling why it earns its keep -- why loading a full chance-hypothesis into the conditioning proposition defuses the contradiction rather than simply relocating it one level up. Could you talk me through the intuition here, treating the problem and the fix as two halves of a single story rather than as separate technical points? And if you are willing to venture a verdict of your own: does the New Principle amount to a genuine dissolution of the difficulty, or does it merely displace the original admissibility worry onto the theory of chance and present that displacement as a solution?
