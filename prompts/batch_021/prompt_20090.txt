I am several drafts into a memoir centered on the winter my younger brother was hospitalized after a breakdown, a period that shaped my sensibility as a writer more than anything before or since. He doesn't dispute a word of my account, and he isn't worried about how it reflects on him; he simply doesn't want the clinical details of his diagnosis, or what he told his doctors that winter, made public, though he raises no objection to the passages concerning only my own experience of that time. My agent argues that the book is gutted without them, and that since I lived through that winter as fully as he did, I have just as much claim to write about it as he does to keep it quiet. I can't locate the flaw in her reasoning, but it doesn't sit right with me. Does living through the same events really confer an equal claim to tell them, or is my brother pointing at something her argument doesn't address?
