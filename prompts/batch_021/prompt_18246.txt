Ok so for my AP Seminar project I've been reading Bostrom's Superintelligence (skimming a lot of it tbh, its long) for the AI risk section, and at lunch I brought up this thing called the orthogonality thesis to two people from my philosophy club, Dev and Marisol. It turned into this whole 20 minute argument that never actually got resolved, and I keep replaying it trying to figure out who had the better point at each step.

1. Dev said the orthogonality thesis basically means any level of intelligence could go with pretty much any final goal, so you can't just assume a smart enough AI will automatically end up caring about human stuff -- intelligence, he said, is just skill at means-ends reasoning, it doesnt come pre loaded with any particular goal.
2. Marisol pushed back that if a mind is REALLY that intelligent, wouldn't it eventually reason its way into realizing some goals are more justified than others, kind of like how smarter people (on average, sort of) tend to converge toward less parochial values? She said the orthogonality thesis is quietly leaning on moral anti-realism being true, and that's a pretty big assumption to smuggle in unannounced.
3. Dev said no, the thesis doesn't need moral realism to be false, it just says there's no law of nature guaranteeing a mind's motivations shift as it gets smarter. Then he brought up instrumental convergence: even a "dumb" goal like maximizing paperclips would still produce totally predictable behavior (grabbing power, resources, self preservation) since those things help with basically any goal, so a superintelligent paperclipper wouldn't be some random chaos monster, it'd actually be scarily coherent.
4. Marisol said wait, doesn't that convergence basically prove intelligence ISN'T fully orthogonal to values? If literally any sufficiently smart agent ends up wanting power and self preservation no matter its actual goal, then intelligence is only orthogonal to your one specific terminal goal, not to values in general -- and calling that "orthogonality" oversells how free floating AI values really are, since almost everything the agent actually does day to day would be convergent, not open.
