"Schelling's classic insight in The Strategy of Conflict is that in a bargaining situation, the party who can credibly commit first to a course of action effectively removes their own future flexibility, and this can be an advantage rather than a weakness: think of the general who burns the bridge behind his own army so retreat is impossible, forcing the confrontation to be resolved on his terms. The point is that in games like Chicken, being unable to back down can shift the equilibrium in your favor, because it forces the other side to be the one who swerves.

The 'commitment races' problem, which Schelling develops in response to the rise of automated and AI bargaining agents, says this dynamic gets much more dangerous once many parties can make this kind of credible commitment cheaply and quickly. An AI system with transparent source code, or a smart contract, can bind itself to a threat instantly and verifiably, unlike a human government that needs time to build a literal, physical deterrent. If everyone knows that whoever commits first (and most extremely) wins the bargain, then everyone is incentivized to commit as early as possible, before they've even had time to work out what game they're actually in. This isn't the same as an arms race, which is about building up capability over time — it's a race to lock in a strategy, and it can happen in an instant.

The reason this is worse than ordinary first-mover advantage is essentially escalation of commitment: once a party has committed to a threat, backing down would mean admitting the original commitment was a mistake, so there is strong pressure to double down and follow through even once it becomes clear the threat is catastrophic for both sides, the same way a company keeps pouring money into a doomed project because pulling out would mean admitting the sunk cost was wasted. So the race to commit first feeds into a race to escalate, and you can end up with two mutually destructive commitments that neither side can back away from, even though both would rather not have committed at all if they'd had time to think it through."
