As a novelist, I have never quite understood why my French publisher insists that my droit moral cannot be signed away even after every economic right in a book has been sold, whereas my American contracts treat copyright as ordinary property, divisible and licensable however the parties see fit. I would like you to compare the two classical philosophical foundations for owning a piece of expression: Locke's labor-desert theory of property and Hegel's theory of property as an extension of personality. Which of the two better accounts for the perpetual, inalienable rights of attribution and integrity that survive in the French tradition even after a work's economic rights have been sold or have lapsed into the public domain, and does Locke's proviso that an appropriation must leave "enough and as good" for others do any real work once what is being appropriated is something as non-rivalrous as a story?
