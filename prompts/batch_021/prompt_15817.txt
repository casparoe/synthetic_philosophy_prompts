My mother is elderly but still in good health, and she recently decided to sign her house over to me now rather than leave it to me later through her will. She says she is doing this because she can tell, from how attentive I already am with her, that I genuinely intend to move in and personally care for her if she ever becomes unable to live alone, and that this kind of promise means more to her than anything a legal document could guarantee. I told her, quite sincerely, that she could count on me, and the transfer of the house is already going through. Afterward, though, it occurred to me that if that day ever comes, giving up years of my life to care for her full-time will be exhausting, and by then the house will already be mine regardless of what I choose to do, so there will be nothing further for me to gain by actually following through. Since I can already foresee my future self being tempted to back out once nothing more is at stake, I've started to wonder whether what I feel right now can honestly be called a genuine intention, or whether I'm fooling both of us by calling it one. Was I being dishonest with her when I made that promise, even though I meant every word of it at the time?
