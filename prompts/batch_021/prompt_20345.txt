For my seminar on distributive justice, we've just finished Nozick's entitlement theory and the Wilt Chamberlain argument from Anarchy, State, and Utopia, and our response paper asks us to extend his framework to a case he never anticipated: generative AI. Large language models are trained by scraping enormous amounts of text, images, and code that people posted online without payment or consent, and the resulting products are then sold to millions of subscribers, making a handful of companies extraordinarily wealthy. Nozick defends unequal fortunes, like Wilt Chamberlain's gate receipts, when they arise through long chains of voluntary transfers, but the scraping stage looks less like a voluntary transfer than an appropriation of material other people already have some claim to. Working strictly from Nozick's own categories of justice in acquisition and justice in transfer, including his Lockean proviso, rather than generic pro- or anti-capitalist intuitions, how do you think he would judge whether the scraping counts as a legitimate original acquisition or an illegitimate use of already-owned property, and, either way, whether the fortunes built on top of it are as justified as Chamberlain's was?
