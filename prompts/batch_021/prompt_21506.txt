Both things ended up scheduled for the same evening on opposite sides of town, neither one can be moved, and neither promise was made knowing about the other, so it's not like Jordan was being careless. Caleb's opening claim was that it can't be a real dilemma because "ought implies can" (his words, from our teacher) -- if Jordan really ought to keep both promises, then combining those means he ought to keep both of them at once, but he literally can't be in two places at the same time, so both "oughts" can't actually be true together. Caleb's last move was that maybe the guilt is really about Jordan being careless enough to end up in that position at all, not about the dilemma itself, but Deja pointed out the case already says neither of them could've predicted the scheduling collision, so there's no carelessness left for the guilt to be about.
