Been down a rabbit hole on Henry Sidgwick's Society for Psychical Research years and it messes with the tidy "he's just the utilitarian guy" picture. He was the SPR's first president, spent decades with Myers and Gurney cross-examining mediums and telepathy subjects for the Phantasms of the Living project, and was rigorous enough that his group personally caught some of their own early star subjects cheating. It wasnt idle curiosity either -- in the Methods of Ethics he basically admits egoism and utilitarianism don't fully reconcile on reason alone (the "dualism of practical reason"), and by his own diary entries part of what drove all the ghost-hunting was hoping for actual evidence of a soul or moral order that could close that gap.

Here's what I can't stop chewing on (very ACX-brain of me, I know): say Sidgwick somehow gets to watch the whole Daryl Bem "Feeling the Future" saga play out -- a mainstream psychologist getting seemingly solid p<.05 evidence for precognition into a top journal in 2011, then the Bayesian reanalyses tearing into it, the failed replications, and the preregistration/replication-crisis reforms that grew out of that mess. Does he come away more hopeful or less, both about psi itself and about ever getting evidence that actually speaks to the dualism of practical reason? And does his own 1880s methodology hold up well against modern meta-science standards, or does it look embarrassingly credulous by comparison?
