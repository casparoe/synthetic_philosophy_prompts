Please give an intuitive, gut-level explanation of why Lewis's triviality results for indicative conditionals hold. I understand that the trouble starts with requiring the probability of "if A, then B" to match the conditional probability P(B | A) not just for one's actual credence function, but for every credence function in the class one might reach by conditionalizing on new evidence — and that the formal proof shows this collapses the space of propositions rather than merely constraining it. What I'm missing is why that closure requirement is so devastating at a conceptual level, and why the result specifically threatens views on which conditionals express ordinary propositions, while comparatively sparing more deflationary treatments of the conditional.
