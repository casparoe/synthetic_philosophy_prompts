Every year in my "philosophy through fiction" seminar I have students take a swing at dramatizing Avicenna's flying man -- you know, the thought experiment where he imagines someone created whole and fully rational in a single instant, suspended in empty air with their limbs spread apart so nothing touches anything else, no sight, no sound, nothing feeding in from the senses at all. Avicenna's claim is that this person would still be certain of their own existence, their "I," even while affirming nothing whatsoever about having arms, legs, a body, any of it. I want to put together a strong model piece for the class this year, so could you write me a short story (a page or two is fine) that takes that setup as it's jumping off point?

You don't have to keep it medieval -- an upload right before any sensors are connected, someone coming out of anesthesia with total sensory shutdown, whatever premise you like, as long as it's really doing the thing Avicenna's doing. And here's the part my students almost always mess up, so I'd love to see it done right: the character can't "notice" or "realize" they have no body, because that's already smuggling bodily awareness in through the back door (to think "I have no body" is still a thought about a body). The self-awareness in the story has to just be there, full stop, with body not even coming up as a category, present or absent. If that constraint makes the story weirder or more abstract than a normal piece of fiction, that's fine -- that's sort of the point.
