For my school's philosophy club, I'm preparing a short talk on consciousness and AI, and I want to build it around David Chalmers' philosophical zombie thought experiment. As I understand the argument, because we can coherently conceive of a being that is physically and behaviorally identical to an ordinary conscious human but has no inner experience whatsoever, such zombies must be genuinely possible, which shows that consciousness cannot simply be a matter of having the right physical or computational states in place. If that's correct, it seems to follow that a large language model, which by design only rearranges physical and computational states to produce humanlike outputs, is exactly this kind of zombie — so wouldn't Chalmers' argument already settle, on purely conceptual grounds, that no chatbot could ever actually be conscious, no matter how sophisticated it becomes? Before I build my talk around this conclusion, I want to make sure I'm applying the argument correctly.
