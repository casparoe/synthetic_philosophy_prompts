my dad died five weeks ago and writing his eulogy for this weekend has turned into a way bigger philosophical minefield than i expected. he was in a motorcycle crash, and four days later the hospital declared him brain dead. he was an organ donor, so they kept him on the ventilator, warm and pink with his heart still going, until the transplant team was ready. my grandma (his mom) is adamant he "died on the table," not the day of the apnea test, and she's quietly furious the death certificate says otherwise. my aunt thinks he was basically gone the second they said "brain dead" and finds it a little morbid that grandma keeps insisting his heart was "still fighting" after the doctors had already called it. i've read alot this week about whole-brain vs circulatory criteria and the dead donor rule and honestly there's no clean answer, which is not what i need two days before i have to stand up and talk about the actual moment we lost him. can you write the eulogy? it should be warm, about who he actually was, not a philosophy lecture -- but it can't casually assert a moment of death that contradicts either of their versions of events, and bonus points if it can gently gesture at why two people who both loved him can disagree about that. it's a small service at the funeral home, mostly family and his biker buddies, so nobody's gonna clock a philosophy reference, but they'll clock it fast if it sounds like it's taking a side.
