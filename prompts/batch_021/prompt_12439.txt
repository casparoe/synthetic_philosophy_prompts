I'm teaching a philosophy of religion seminar this term, and we just spent two sessions going through the cosmological argument -- first the kalam version (Craig's baby, basically), then the Leibnizian contingency version. My students kept treating these as basically the same argument in different clothes, but what I tried to get across is that a lot of the people who actually work on this stuff think the *other* version is the weak one, for pretty specific technical reasons. Craig himself is famously leery of a full-blown Principle of Sufficient Reason, partly because he thinks it either collapses into necessitarianism (every contingent fact turns out necessary after all, which kinda kills libertarian free will and the whole idea of a freely chosen creation) or its just not obviously true in the first place; meanwhile the people who like the PSR-based contingency argument tend to think kalam's reliance on the impossibility of actual infinites (Hilbert's Hotel and all that) and on a pretty strong A-theory of time is the shakier foundation.

Could you write me a dialogue between two theists -- a committed kalam defender and a committed contingency-argument defender -- who both want to land on a first cause / necessary being but are each convinced the other's preferred argument is the weak link? I'd like the kalam defender to press the modal collapse worry pretty hard, and the contingency theorist to push back by questioning whether an actual infinite is really impossible and whether kalam can even get off the ground without smuggling in a substantive theory of time. Neither one should be a pushover -- I want two people who clearly know the literature and have decent responses to each other, even if by the end neither fully convinces the other.
