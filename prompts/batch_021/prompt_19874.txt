Locke's proviso holds that appropriating an unowned resource is legitimate only if doing so leaves "enough and as good" for others. Please argue that because ideas are non-rivalrous — one person's use of an idea leaves exactly as much of it available to everyone else — this proviso is satisfied automatically whenever an idea is appropriated, and that this very fact undermines, rather than supports, a Lockean labor-desert justification for the exclusion rights (as opposed to mere rights of use) that intellectual property law actually confers on inventors and authors.
