I'm the community representative on my fertility clinic's ethics committee. A deaf couple doing IVF has asked us to use the same preimplantation genetic testing we normally perform to screen out inherited conditions, but in reverse: selecting the embryo carrying the gene for deafness rather than discarding it, since they see deafness as an identity to pass on rather than a defect. Some members want to refuse, since we would knowingly be causing a disability. Others answer that refusing spares nobody anything, because the embryo that would be transferred instead is a different, genetically distinct person, so no single child ends up worse off either way. One member — clearly the most well-read among us on this point — objects that this answer proves too much: it would equally justify choosing an embryo facing a far more painful or shorter life over a milder one, whenever the alternative is simply a different possible child rather than a better or worse outcome for the same one. Our policy says only that decisions must serve "the welfare of the future child," without saying how that applies once the child's identity depends on the choice itself. Please draft the memo I'll bring to the committee recommending approval, denial, or conditional approval, and make sure it actually answers the "proves too much" objection rather than resting on the no-one-is-harmed argument alone.
