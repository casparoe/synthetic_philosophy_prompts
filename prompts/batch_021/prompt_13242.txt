At a political philosophy reading group, two members fell into a running dispute over Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain example from Anarchy, State, and Utopia. (1) Mara opened by arguing that the example settles the issue: if D1 is a distribution that any patterned principle of justice would count as just, and a season's worth of fans then freely and knowingly hand Wilt Chamberlain twenty-five cents each to watch him play, the resulting D2 is just as well, since it arose from D1 through a chain of individually just transfers -- even though D2 wrecks whatever pattern made D1 just in the first place. No patterned principle, she concluded, Rawls's difference principle included, can be sustained without the state continually overriding people's chosen transfers, which shows that justice must track a distribution's history rather than its shape. (2) Owen replied that this proves less than she thinks: no serious patterned theorist holds that the pattern must be preserved instant to instant. Rawls's principle governs the basic structure over time; fans may hand Wilt every quarter they like, and the pattern is simply restored at the next round of taxation, with nothing in the story requiring that the transfers themselves be forbidden. (3) Mara answered that "restore it later" is the same move on a delay, not a different move: the state still has to reach into Wilt's earnings and redirect pieces of them against his wishes, and shifting from continuous to periodic doesn't locate any principled amount of overriding people's chosen transfers that a patterned view is entitled to rule out. (4) Owen came back that the identical charge sinks Mara's own side: the only reason Wilt keeps any of those quarters is that courts and police stand ready to stop other people from simply taking them, which restricts those people's liberty exactly as surely as a tax restricts Wilt's. Deciding in advance that enforcing Wilt's holdings doesn't count as interference while taxing them does isn't an argument for entitlement over patterned justice, he said; it's a decision about whose freedom counts, dressed up as one. Neither side budged.
