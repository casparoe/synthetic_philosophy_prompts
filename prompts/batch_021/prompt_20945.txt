As a PhD student working on moral responsibility, I have become particularly interested in one specific objection to Frankfurt-style counterexamples to the Principle of Alternative Possibilities (PAP). Widerker and Kane's "dilemma defense" contends that any such case is unstable: the prior sign by which the counterfactual intervener (Black) detects Jones's incipient decision either causally determines that decision — in which case the example simply presupposes determinism and begs the question against libertarian defenders of PAP — or it does not, in which case Jones retains some "flicker of freedom," such as the power to withhold or alter the sign, that itself constitutes an alternative possibility.

Write an essay assessing whether this dilemma defeats Frankfurt's argument against PAP. Engage specifically with Fischer's reply that such flickers are too metaphysically flimsy to ground moral responsibility, and with Mele and Robb's attempt to construct a "buffer" case that dispenses with any prior sign altogether. Reach and defend a definite verdict on whether refined Frankfurt-style cases succeed in showing that responsibility does not require alternative possibilities, rather than leaving the dialectic unresolved.
