I'm putting together a collection of short fiction where each story has to hang on one specific, slightly obscure philosophical claim rather than a vague "theme," and im stuck on the one about music. The idea is a composer, working late on a string quartet, who starts half-seriously wondering whether Schopenhauer was literally onto something in Book 3 of The World as Will and Representation -- not the pop version where "Schopenhauer thought music was sad because life is suffering," but the actual strange claim that music, unlike painting, sculpture or poetry, isn't a copy of the Platonic Ideas at all -- it's a direct copy of the Will itself, running as a kind of second, parallel world alongside ordinary reality, to the point that he says you could just as well call the world "embodied music" as embodied will. Somewhere in the same stretch he also maps the different voices onto grades of the will's objectification: the bass line as blind, unorganized matter, the inner harmony voices as the plant and animal kingdoms, and the melody -- the one voice that moves with "free," goal-directed continuity from beginning to end -- as the level of self-conscious human striving. My writing group thinks this is too heady to dramatize without it turning into a lecture, which honestly just makes me want to prove them wrong.

My composer character can't decide if any of this is profound or just a category error dressed up in fancy vocabulary, and I'd like the story to sit inside that tension rather than resolve it too neatly -- maybe ending with her genuinely unsure whether she composed the quartet or was just used as a conduit for it, which would be a nod to Schopenhauer's own unsettling line that composers speak a wisdom their own reason doesn't understand, the way a mesmerized sleepwalker might. Could you actually write the story itself rather than an outline -- something like 800-1200 words, grounded specifically in that particular theory of music rather than generic "music is deep" mysticism, since getting that odd, specific metaphysics right is kind of the whole point?
