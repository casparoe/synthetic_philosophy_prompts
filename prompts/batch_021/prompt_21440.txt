A close friend on my presbytery's Candidates and Credentials Committee and I spent an evening arguing about a case now before us, and neither of us budged. A candidate for ordination affirms the Westminster Standards' teaching on providence and salvation, but explains it through Molinism: God governs the world by His middle knowledge of what any possible free creature would freely do in any circumstance, logically prior to His decree to create, rather than by an unconditioned decree that simply determines whatsoever comes to pass. Our book of church order lets a candidate register a stated exception as long as it doesn't touch anything essential to the system of doctrine, but gives no guidance on which side of that line this case falls. Please draft the short recommendation I need to circulate before our next stated meeting: it should settle on one of three options -- approve without exception, approve with a recorded exception, or refer the matter to presbytery -- with a timeline for acting on it, and it should take seriously the strongest form of whichever position it doesn't adopt.
