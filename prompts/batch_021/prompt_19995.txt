ok so I've been chewing on this "surrogate goals" idea as a defense against acausal blackmail/threats -- the trick where an agent precommits to treat threats against some fake, cheap goal exactly as seriously as threats against its real goal, hoping a threatener targets the fake one instead, so that if the threat is ever carried out it doesn't actually cost the agent anything real. I like it in the abstract but can't tell if its an actual solution or just a shell game that falls apart once the threatener is smart enough to know what you're doing and route around it. Could you write me a dialogue between two people at a rationalist meetup, both the updateless-decision-theory type who already accept that acausal threats are a real strategic concern (that part isn't up for debate between them), who disagree about whether adopting a surrogate goal actually helps -- one thinks it's basically a free lunch (costs nothing, might deflect real harm, so just do it), the other thinks it can't survive contact with a threatener who models your commitment, and that the whole strategy is tangled up with the "commitment races" problem (both sides racing to lock in favorable commitments before the other does, in "logical time"). I want real engagement, not both of them just restating priors -- have the skeptic push on why a threatener would ever bother targeting the surrogate instead of calling the bluff and going after the real goal, and make the advocate actually answer that instead of dodging. Neither should be a pushover, and they should be responding to each other's specific points as they go. Something like 15-20 exchanges is probably enough.
