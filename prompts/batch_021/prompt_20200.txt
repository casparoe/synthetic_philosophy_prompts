A puzzle from value theory keeps resurfacing whenever debates in this community turn to cause prioritization or to comparing welfare across very different kinds of beings (the recent arguments over cross-species "moral weight," for instance), and I would like a more rigorous treatment of it than the usual gestures toward Isaiah Berlin's pluralism.

Ruth Chang has argued that the "Trichotomy Thesis" — the assumption that any two comparable options must be better than, worse than, or exactly as good as one another — is false, and that there is a fourth relation, "parity," which comparable-but-different options can stand in instead. Her central argument runs through small-improvement cases: take two options that intuitively seem roughly on a level (two careers, say, or two job candidates), then slightly improve one of them. If the original pair had merely been tied, this small improvement should tip the balance and make the improved option better; in the cases Chang has in mind, it doesn't seem to, and the pair remains stubbornly hard to rank. She concludes that neither exact equality nor sheer incomparability explains this, and that parity must be recognized as a distinct, sui generis value relation.

The main rival to this view, defended by John Broome in his contribution to Chang's own edited volume on incommensurability, holds that no new relation is needed: "better than" is simply a vague predicate, our comparative judgments run out at the margins, and the resistance to ranking in Chang's cases is just ordinary indeterminacy rather than evidence of a fourth relation.

Write an essay adjudicating between these two positions. Reconstruct the small-improvement argument carefully, consider whether a vagueness theorist can accommodate the same cases without positing parity, and defend a definite verdict on whether "on a par" names a real, sui generis value relation or reduces to vagueness in the ordinary comparative. Close by drawing out what your verdict implies for making a genuinely hard choice among incommensurable options: is there a rationally defensible way to decide, or does it bottom out in something closer to arbitrary commitment?
