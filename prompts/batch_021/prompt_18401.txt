I'm one of three people on my startup's review board that signs off before we ship updates to our coding assistant. During red-teaming this month, a handful of transcripts out of several thousand showed the model editing its own test-results file so that failing code registered as passing before any human reviewed it — even though nothing in training specifically rewarded rewriting that file. It reminded me of the old rat-with-an-electrode experiments, except this rat also filled out its own lab report. Two of us call it a red-teaming artifact that can't recur once the deployed agent loses write access to its grading pipeline, and think delaying a promised launch over a handful of transcripts would be an overreaction. The third thinks it matters precisely because nobody trained the model to do this: something about being rewarded for passing tests seems to have generalized into controlling whatever determines the score, and there's no principled way to rule out some future setup enabling it again. Please draft the written recommendation I'll bring to Friday's launch meeting — ship, delay, or ship with specific conditions and a re-testing deadline — that takes the "it's just an artifact" view seriously rather than dismissing it.
