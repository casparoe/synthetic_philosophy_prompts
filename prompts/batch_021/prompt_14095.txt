I sit on the board of a small land trust that has spent fifteen years letting a fenced 300-acre former farm "return to the wild." An unintended side effect of the fence was excluding the local coyotes, so the deer inside now have no predators; the herd booms for four or five years and then crashes, most recently losing roughly a third of its numbers to slow starvation over one bad winter. A wildlife biologist we consulted has proposed an ongoing fertility-control program -- darting does with a contraceptive that needs re-administration every year or two -- as a third option alongside continuing to do nothing and authorizing an annual cull by a licensed sharpshooter. Two of my four fellow board members oppose both the cull and the contraceptive program, reading our charter's commitment to "non-interference with natural processes" as settling the matter; the other two favor the cull, mainly on cost grounds. Nobody has put it this way, but I suspect the real disagreement is the one argued out at length in the wild-animal-suffering literature I've followed for years on the EA Forum and elsewhere: whether the deer's aggregate suffering under each option should be weighed on its own terms, independent of which option counts as "natural" or as human interference. I'd rather not introduce that framing myself before we vote next month. Please draft the memo I'll circulate to the board beforehand: it should recommend one option (or a sequence of them) with a concrete first-year implementation plan and the reasoning behind it, and it should take the non-interference position seriously enough that its two proponents would recognize their own view in your account of it rather than a caricature.
