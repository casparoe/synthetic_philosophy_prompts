After inheriting a cottage on a small private lake from my aunt, I was asked within weeks to join the lake association's bylaws committee — presumably because, as a brand-new seasonal owner, I am not yet allied with either side of a dispute that has now derailed two consecutive annual meetings.

The association has 150 member properties: 50 full-time residences and 100 seasonal ones, like mine. All owners pay identical annual dues and share joint liability for the lake's dam, whose latest inspection could trigger a special assessment of roughly $10,000 per property. Only full-time residents, however, may vote at the annual meeting. Seasonal owners have petitioned for one vote per property, on a "no taxation without representation" theory: they are bound by, and financially liable for, decisions in which they have no say. Full-time residents point to a neighboring association where a seasonal majority voted down the winter road-plowing budget that only full-timers actually need, and argue that seasonal owners — who could already outvote them two-to-one — do not live with the year-round consequences of decisions about plowing, road wear, emergency access, or rental and noise rules, so simply reversing who is disenfranchised is not a fix.

The committee reports to the full board in three weeks, and I have been asked to draft its recommendation on the voting question. Could you write the memo? It should propose a specific voting arrangement rather than call for further discussion, and it should take the strongest argument on whichever side it rejects as seriously as the one it adopts.
