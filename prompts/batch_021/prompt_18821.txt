I've been reading Enlightenment philosophy on my own, without any formal training, and I recently learned that Rousseau's opera Le Devin du village was a genuine hit at the French court, yet less than a decade later he wrote his Letter to d'Alembert on the Theatre, arguing at length that his native Geneva should never build a public theater. This looks to me like a fairly blatant case of hypocrisy: a man who owed much of his early fame to the stage turning around and trying to keep that same art form away from his fellow citizens. Please help me understand how such a contradiction is best explained, and what Rousseau thought was actually wrong with theater.
