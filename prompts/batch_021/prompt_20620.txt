So I've been chewing on Putnam's twin earth thing and think I found a hole in it. His whole point is that "water" refers to H2O because that's literally what water is, even though nobody back then could tell H2O apart from the XYZ stuff just by looking at it or drinking it. But he sets the whole story in 1750, which is before chemists had even figured out water was made of hydrogen and oxygen (that didn't happen til the 1780s). So doesn't that mean that in 1750, "water" didn't actually refer to anything specific yet, since the very fact that's suppose to fix the reference (being H2O) hadn't been discovered by anybody, on earth or twin earth? Feels like meaning would've had to just sit around waiting for the chemists to catch up before "water" could refer to H2O specifically instead of just "whatever clear drinkable stuff is around here." Am I missing something, or does the thought experiment kind of undercut itself by being set before the relevant science even existed?
