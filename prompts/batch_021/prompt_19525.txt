I sit on my paper's sourcing and standards committee, and we're stuck on a case that's split the room ahead of next week's meeting. A reporter has one very well-placed source -- a scientist inside a pharma company -- alleging it buried bad trial data, but there's zero independent corroboration, just the reporter's long history with someone whose been right before. House rule is two independent sources minimum for any fraud claim, full stop. Half the committee wants to hold that line: one person's word, however solid their track record, isn't knowledge until something independent backs it up. The other half says that's the wrong lesson -- insist on outside corroboration every time and you'd kill every real whistleblower story, and anyway "corroboration" usually just means a second bit of testimony, so the regress doesn't really go away just because we demand it. Can you draft the memo I'd bring to that meeting -- an actual recommendation on when, if ever, a single uncorroborated source is enough to run, and whichever way you land, take the losing side's strongest argument seriously instead of just waving it off.
