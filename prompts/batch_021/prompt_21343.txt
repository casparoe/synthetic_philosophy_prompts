I sit on my country's military conscientious objector review panel, and we've got a case that's splitting us down the middle. A soldier on his second tour wants a CO discharge -- he's upfront that hes not a pacifist and would fight off an actual invasion, no problem, but he's become convinced this particular conflict is a war of aggression with no just cause, even though nothing he's personally been ordered to do breaks the laws of war. Current policy only grants CO status to objectors to war 'in any form,' on the idea that ordinary soldiers don't get a say on whether a given war is justified, and are in the clear as long as they follow the rules of engagement. His unit redeploys in three weeks. Half the panel wants to hold the line, half thinks this is exactly the kind of case CO protection should cover -- can you draft the memo recommending grant, deny, or a middle option, and touching on whether the policy itself needs rethinking, that actually engages with why the rule exists instead of just waving it away?
