So in my metaontology seminar this week we went over Eli Hirsch's "quantifier variance" idea, applied to the whole mereological composition debate — you know, the classic case of whether some particles "arranged tablewise" actually compose a further object (a table) or whether, strictly speaking, only the particles exist. From what I got out of lecture, Hirsch's view is that the universalist and the nihilist aren't actually disagreeing about any facts at all, they're just using "exists"/"there is" with different meanings, so once you disambiguate the quantifier the whole dispute dissolves and both sides agree on literally everything (where the particles are, how their arranged, etc). He calls this a verbal dispute.

Me and my study group were talking about this after class and honestly it's kind of blowing our minds, but I think we might be taking it further than we should? My thought was: ok, if thats true, then there's just no fact of the matter about how many objects are in any given room, and by extension a huge chunk of metaphysics (not just mereology but personal identity, material constitution, all of it) is basically just people fighting over definitions, so its kind of a waste of time as a research program. But then I noticed we're also assigned Ted Sider's stuff on "ontological realism" which is arguing against Hirsch, and I don't get why he'd even bother writing a whole book about it if Hirsch is basically right that it's all just semantics. Is there something I'm missing about what "verbal dispute" actually means here, or is this genuinely as deflationary for metaphysics as it seems?
