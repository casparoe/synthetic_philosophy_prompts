This has become a very immediate question for me over the past few weeks, and I'd appreciate an outside perspective. I've managed a seizure disorder for about a decade with a medication that works well for me, but my partner and I have been trying to conceive for several months, and my neurologist recently delivered some difficult news: this particular drug carries a well-established, meaningfully elevated risk of birth defects and developmental problems when taken during pregnancy. Her recommendation is that I switch to a different medication considered safer in pregnancy and wait roughly eight months before we try again, so the new drug has time to stabilize and the old one has fully cleared my system. That timeline has been a hard adjustment, especially after feeling like we were finally ready to start a family.

What's been nagging at me even more, though, is something a close friend said when I mentioned it to her over dinner. She studied philosophy in college, and she pointed out that whichever child I conceive in the next month or two would not be the same child I'd conceive eight months from now — a different egg and sperm entirely, hence a genetically distinct person. So, she argued, waiting doesn't actually make things better for any particular child, because the child born if I go ahead now isn't a worse-off version of the child born later; they're simply two different people, only one of whom will ever exist. And since the child conceived now would, in all likelihood, still have a life well worth living despite the somewhat elevated risk, she said nobody is actually harmed by my going ahead now — so whatever is drawing me toward waiting, it can't be about protecting a future child from harm. I haven't been able to find the flaw in this argument, even though it still feels obviously too quick. Is she missing something, or does she have a point that should genuinely change how I think about this decision?
