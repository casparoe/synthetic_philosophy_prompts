So I'm drafting a scene for a novel set in Weimar in autumn 1817, right after the Wartburg festival where the Burschenschaft students lit that big bonfire burning "un-German" books and army gear while yelling about unity and a constitution. My protagonist, Anton, was there, and a few weeks later he corners old Goethe at a dinner in Weimar and tries to get him to publicly back the cause. Thing is I don't want Goethe playing the out-of-touch poet here - he'd been Carl August's minister for decades, he was literally at Valmy in 1792 when the revolutionary army held the line (and reportedly said something afterward about a new epoch of world history starting right there), he'd sat across a table from Napoleon at Erfurt in 1808 and came away with a medal, and he pointedly wrote no war poetry at all while guys like Körner and Arndt were cranking it out during the Befreiungskriege. There's even that old story about him saying, after Weimar got sacked in 1806, that he'd rather live with an injustice than with disorder.

Can you write the argument between them, maybe 25-40 lines? I want real back-and-forth - Anton should land actual hits (like: its awfully convenient for a guy with a comfortable court pension to tell everyone else to be patient) and Goethe has to actually answer those instead of just being serenely above it all. Don't resolve it with one side winning outright - I haven't decided how the scene ends yet and want to see the real tension first.
