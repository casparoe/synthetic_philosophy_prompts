Leibniz held that monads are "windowless": no causal influence passes between one created substance and another, yet their internal states correspond perfectly across the whole universe, a harmony he attributed entirely to God's design at creation rather than to any interaction among the substances themselves. On the strength of the principle of sufficient reason, he further held that even eternal and necessary truths, including those of logic and mathematics, cannot simply obtain unsupported but must be grounded in an actual understanding: God's.

Contemporary decision theorists describe "acausal trade," in which agents between whom no causal channel is even possible -- because they occupy disjoint branches of a multiverse, are separated by unbridgeable stretches of time, or exist only as each other's simulations -- coordinate for mutual benefit purely by each inferring what the other's decision procedure will predictably output and conditioning its own choice on that inference. The correlation itself is typically explained by appeal to shared logical or algorithmic structure between the two agents' reasoning, rather than to anything causal or providential.

Drawing on Leibniz's actual views on monads, pre-established harmony, and the divine grounding of necessary truths, how would he assess this proposal? Would he regard "acausal trade" as pre-established harmony rediscovered under the alias of logic, so that its hidden sufficient reason is still God, or does his own rationalism already provide the resources for such coordination among finite creatures with no coordinating mind behind it?
