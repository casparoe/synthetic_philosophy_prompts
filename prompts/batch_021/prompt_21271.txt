Q3 (15 pts): In What Is Art?, Tolstoy rejects definitions of art based on beauty and proposes his own "infection" theory instead. (a) Explain the theory and the criteria Tolstoy uses to judge how good a given work of art is. (b) Using at least one specific example from the text, show how applying Tolstoy's criteria could lead him to reject a work that's widely considered a masterpiece.

Student's answer:
"Tolstoy's whole point is that the tradition of defining art through beauty and the pleasure it gives us is a dead end, because 'beauty' never gets pinned down to anything more than 'what pleases the people who currently get to decide these things,' so it can't ground a real theory of art. Instead he says art happens when someone who has actually felt something manages to pass that feeling on to someone else through words, sound, color, movement etc., so the audience 'catches' it too -- his famous 'infection' idea. How infectious a piece is comes down to three things: how individual the feeling is, how clearly it's expressed, and how sincere the artist was, and since sincerity is really the load-bearing one of the three, Tolstoy's bottom-line test for whether something counts as good art just is whether the artist actually, truly felt what they're putting across, rather than faking it for effect. That's basically his whole case against Shakespeare: he thought the characters all rant in the same overblown, unnatural way no matter who they're supposed to be, which for Tolstoy is a dead giveaway that nothing real is being felt or transmitted, so King Lear fails as art even though everyone's told its a masterpiece. He was willing to bite this bullet across the board -- even War and Peace gets a pass from him only because its patriotic, all-of-Russia-together theme is exactly the kind of unifying feeling his theory says great art should spread, so it's one of the few 'big' novels he doesn't lump in with the bad stuff."
