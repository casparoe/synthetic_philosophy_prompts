My younger brother Dan is getting married in three weeks and somehow I got stuck giving the toast, which is stressing me out because of who's going to be in that room. Half the guests are his coworkers from the software company, plus a bunch of his fiancée Priya's old classmates from when she did a masters in logic before switching to UX work, and the other half is just family who've sat through the two of them bickering about "the Gödel thing" every Thanksgiving for something like eight years without ever really knowing what it's about. They actually met arguing in a philosophy of mind elective back in undergrad, over whether Gödel's theorem means a machine could never fully match a human mind - the whole Lucas and Penrose argument, if that means anything to you. Dan thinks it's a cute magic trick, there's no sentence he can't eventually get some machine to handle one way or another. Priya's position is that for any machine you hand her, there's a sentence that machine can't prove but that she can step outside of it and just see is true, which she takes to mean she can't just be that machine, or any other one like it (she will also tell you Penrose lost her once he got to the quantum microtubule stuff, she only holds the plain Lucas version). Dan's standard comeback, which I've heard enough times to do it myself now, is something like "how do you even know you're not just as buggy and inconsistent as any program, in which case your seeing-its-true trick doesn't prove anything" - and as far as I can tell Priya has never fully put that one away, it just became a running bit between them.

I don't have any background in this stuff, I've only picked up the shape of it from a decade of dinners, so I genuinely couldn't tell you who's winning. What I need is a toast, maybe five minutes long, built around one real joke that actually uses the argument properly - accurate enough that the logic and CS people in the room don't cringe - but that still works for my mom and my aunts, who could not define an algorithm if their lives depended on it. Ideally the joke shouldn't just land as a "gotcha" for whichever side, it should turn into something true about the two of them getting married, something about the kind of thing you can be completely sure of about a person even though you could never write it down as a proof. Could you write me that toast?
