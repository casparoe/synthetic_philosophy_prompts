Write a dialogue between Gongsun Long, the School of Names logician defending his 白马非马 ("a white horse is not a horse") argument, and a contemporary philosopher of language who thinks the whole puzzle just dissolves once you seperate the "is" of identity from the "is" of predication. Don't let them just trade the one-liner back and forth: Gongsun Long has to actually run his real arguments (the move where "horse" names a shape and "white" names a color, plus the asymmetry between seeking a horse vs. seeking a white horse), and the modern philosopher has to show that her tidy dissolution isn't secretly just Gongsun Long's own point wearing new vocabulary. Have them try to work out whether they actually disagree about anything, or whether it's the same insight showing up in two different idioms.
