I've been down a rabbit hole on functionalism in philosophy of mind lately. The basic idea (as I understand it, which is probably a bit off) is that what makes something a "pain" or a "belief" is just the causal role it plays, kind of like an interface in code, not what it's implemented in underneath. That's supposedly why philosophers say a mental state could be "multiply realized" — the same interface running on totally different backends (neurons, silicon, whatever) as long as the causal wiring matches up. Theres this old thought experiment, Ned Block's I think, where the entire population of a country each act as a single neuron, passing signals by radio according to the exact wiring diagram of a real brain, and for an hour the whole system ends up functionally identical to a person's brain mid-episode of pain. His point is that our gut says that system obviously isn't feeling anything, but functionalism doesn't have an easy way of explaining why not.
