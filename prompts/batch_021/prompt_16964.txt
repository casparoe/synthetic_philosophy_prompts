A few of us from my grade rewatched Avengers: Endgame over break, and two of my friends, Maya and Diego, ended up in an argument that's stuck with me. Maya said the movie is right that changing the past has to create a new branching timeline, since a single timeline can't survive the grandfather paradox: if you could go back and prevent your own birth, you'd get a flat contradiction, so any story that keeps one timeline instead -- like the Time-Turner in Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban -- is quietly incoherent, no matter how cleverly the loop is closed. Diego disagreed: the paradox only shows that some attempts, like killing your grandfather before your parent is conceived, can never succeed -- not that traveling into the past is impossible; in Prisoner of Azkaban nothing is actually changed, the loop just closes, which is perfectly consistent. Maya replied that this makes time travelers unfree once they're back there: something must always conveniently stop them, which is really just fate patching the timeline -- branching time travel wearing a single-timeline disguise. Diego answered that no hidden hand is needed: it's no stranger than the ordinary fact that you can't do things that, in fact, never happen; a jammed gun or a sudden loss of nerve are mundane events, and calling that 'fate' just because the story stays consistent doesn't require a puppeteer. Neither budged, and I couldn't decide either. Could you please go through their exchange move by move, say who has the stronger position at each step, and point out anything important that both of them seem to be missing?
