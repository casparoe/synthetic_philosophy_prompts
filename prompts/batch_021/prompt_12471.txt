My decision theory seminar spent basically all of last week on "acausal" stuff, i.e. threats that don't route through any normal causal channel — the idea being that a good enough predictor (a future AI, a simulator, whatever) could have leverage over you right now just because it can model how you'd respond, and you can model it back. The professor kept insisting this a real problem in bargaining theory and not just the old Roko's basilisk meme, and the proposed defense we spent the most time on was "surrogate goals": you get yourself (or an AI you're designing) to genuinely care about some decoy value, so that if a threat does land it lands on the decoy instead of anything you actually value, which is supposed to make extorting you pointless to begin with. That got me wondering how it actually stacks up against the other defenses that came up in the readings, since none of them seemed obviously best to me and I felt like my prof kind of dodged the question when I pushed on it in office hours.
