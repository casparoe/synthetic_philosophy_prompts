My grandmother wrote an advance directive fifteen years ago stating that she never wanted to be kept alive by artificial means "if there is no reasonable prospect of meaningful recovery." She is now several years into dementia, and on her good days she seems calm, even content; if you ask her in the moment what she wants, she says clearly that she wants to keep living. My family has spent the last few weeks arguing about whose preferences should govern her care: the sharp, reflective woman who drafted that document, or the person she has become. A friend of mine who works in AI alignment pointed out that this is structurally the same problem that shows up in proposals like Coherent Extrapolated Volition, where a system is meant to act not on a person's present, possibly confused or "unextrapolated" wishes, but on what they would want, in Yudkowsky's own phrase, "if we knew more, thought faster, were more the people we wished we were, had grown up farther together." That comparison has stayed with me all week, because it reframes my family's situation from a one-off tragedy into an instance of a much older and harder question: which version of a person is entitled to speak for the others.

With that in mind, I'd like you to write a short story of roughly 1,000-1,500 words that uses this same tension as its engine, but transposes it into a future where the "extrapolation" is performed not informally by grieving relatives but by an actual system built along CEV-like lines: something that can model, with unsettling confidence, what a person would want if they were wiser, calmer, and more coherent than they currently are, and that is asked, or asks itself, to act on that model even when it conflicts with what the person is saying right now. Please don't let the story resolve into an easy verdict where the system is obviously right or obviously monstrous; I would rather it stay honest about how strong the case for extrapolation can genuinely be, while still letting the reader feel what is lost when someone's present, embodied voice is overruled by a hypothetical, better-informed version of themselves. Feel free to invent whatever concrete characters and setting bring that out most vividly.
