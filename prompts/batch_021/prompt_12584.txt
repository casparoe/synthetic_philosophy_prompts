I got interested in the Kelly criterion while building a bet-sizing module for a personal trading project, and I've since run into a disagreement between two camps that I haven't been able to adjudicate on my own. One camp — associated with Ed Thorp and, more recently, Ole Peters' "ergodicity economics" — holds that maximizing expected log wealth is the objectively rational strategy for anyone facing repeated multiplicative bets, since the quantity that actually matters to a single persisting agent is the time-average growth rate of one trajectory, not the ensemble average across hypothetical parallel copies of that agent. Samuelson's classic rebuttal calls this reasoning a fallacy: he argues that a strategy winning "almost surely" as the number of bets goes to infinity tells you nothing about which strategy maximizes expected utility for a bettor with a finite horizon and a utility function that isn't already logarithmic. Please make the strongest case you can that Samuelson is wrong here, and that the ergodicity framing genuinely rescues growth-rate maximization as objectively correct rather than as log utility smuggled in under a new name.
