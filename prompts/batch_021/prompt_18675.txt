I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, but I've been reading about Ruth Millikan's version of teleosemantics as a naturalistic account of mental content, since it seemed relevant to ongoing debates in AI about whether trained models represent anything at all. As I understand the theory, a state's content is fixed by the function it was selected to perform: the standard illustration is that a frog's neurons fire at small, dark, moving objects, and represent flies specifically because catching flies is what that firing pattern was selected for. This seems to map directly onto how we train neural networks -- gradient descent selects the weights that make a network fire reliably in the presence of cats in training images, so by the same reasoning, the network's internal units should end up representing "cat" in exactly the sense Millikan's theory grants the frog's neurons. Given that, why do people in AI interpretability and philosophy of mind still treat it as an open question whether trained models have genuine representational content, when teleosemantics already seems to settle it?
