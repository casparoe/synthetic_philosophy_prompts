I'm the token philosopher of science on my hospital's data-safety monitoring board (DSMB), and two colleagues are in a real standoff two days before we report out on a smallish antiemetic trial. Pooled across all 400 patients, Drug A has the better complete-response rate (no vomiting, no rescue meds) -- 58% vs 47% for Drug B. But split by nausea severity, B wins in both subgroups (mild: 75% vs 70%; severe: 35% vs 30%). Our biostatistician's first move was 'obviously trust the stratified numbers, severity's a confounder, end of story.' Then our clinical lead pointed out severity was actually scored after the first dose, not before -- so it might partly reflect each drugs own early effect, making it more like a mediator (or even a collider) than a confounder, in which case the pooled number is the one reflecting the true total effect. Neither's budging, and since apparently thinking through stuff like this is my job, I've been handed Friday's memo to write. Can you draft it? I need something that actually lands on a recommendation -- which drug the evidence favors, and what the board should do about the trial in the meantime -- not a tour of both positions. Whoever's wrong here isn't being dumb, so be fair to them too.
