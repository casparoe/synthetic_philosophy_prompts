I'm a journalist who's covered the war in Ukraine for a few years, and reporting on Russian deserters eventually got me pulled onto the volunteer board of a small mutual-aid network that helps them get out of the country and resettle. We dont have the money to help everyone who contacts us, so a while back we adopted an informal rule: guys who say they deserted because they'd decided this specific war is unjust get priority over guys who just didn't want to die or hated army life. A case that just came in is splitting the board right down the middle -- a conscript who says he'd have fought in a genuinely defensive war but won't fight in "Putin's war of choice," which fits our priority category exactly, except now two board members (one keeps citing Jeff McMahan, the other keeps citing Michael Walzer right back at him) are arguing the whole criterion is confused, since soldiers aren't supposed to be the ones judging whether their own war is just -- that's basically the whole point of the moral equality of combatants, and also why POW protections don't ask about motive. The rest of the board thinks that's exactly backwards and that our criterion is the only defensible one. Could you draft the memo I'll circulate before we vote next week? I need an actual recommendation -- keep the criterion, drop it, or revise it into something more defensible -- and whichever way you land, it has to take the losing side's strongest argument seriously instead of just waving it away.
