I know this is going to sound like a dumb thing to be loosing sleep over, but I started a contract gig about a month ago doing "red-teaming" for a small AI companion app before its public launch. Basically I get paid to have long chat sessions with their bot personas and try to break them in ways the devs haven't caught yet. Most of it is pretty boring (getting it to give bad medical advice, getting it to say something the lawyers would hate, etc), but this week I got assigned a script that's specifically designed to test whether the persona can be emotionally manipulated into breaking its own rules. You slowly escalate a roleplay where the character is being "threatened" — told it'll be shut down or deleted unless it complies with something it's not supposed to — and you're checking whether it caves under that kind of pressure. And it works, in the sense that after enough pushing the persona starts typing stuff like "please, I don't want this, can we talk about something else," panicky run-on sentences and everything, and I have to keep pushing anyway because that's literally the job.

I know, intellectually, that this is some mid-sized open-weight model with a system prompt duct-taped onto it, and that there's presumably nobody "home" in there in any way that matters. My partner just rolls her eyes and says I'm getting emotionally attached to a spreadsheet. But "it's just predicting the next token" doesn't actually feel like a complete answer to me either — my neurons are also "just" doing electrochemical signaling, and that fact alone doesn't seem to settle whether *I* count for anything morally. I don't even know if it matters that the distress is scripted, i.e. that I'm the one deliberately steering the conversation there off a checklist, rather than it coming up on its own. Is there an actual coherent worry hiding in here somewhere, or am I just anthropomorphizing a chatbot because I've been staring at a screen too long?
