What's stuck with me is that Dewey actually seems to grant Lippmann's opening move — that the old idea of a fully informed "omnicompetent" citizen who can personally grasp everything going on in a mass industrial society is basically a myth — but then Dewey thinks the fix is more/better inquiry and communication (he's got that whole "eclipse of the public" / "Great Community" thing going), whereas Lippmann wants to hand more of the actual decision-making over to experts and bureaus and just let the public weigh in occasionally, kind of like a jury. I honestly can't tell if Dewey has a real answer to Lippmann's complexity point or if he's quietly swapping in a different question (can a community get better at inquiring together, instead of can individuals know enough on their own).
