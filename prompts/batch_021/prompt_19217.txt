A friend of mine, who's deeply involved in effective altruism, has started arguing that if the future of the universe goes on forever, our everyday moral choices don't actually matter the way we assume. His reasoning is this: if the future contains infinitely many people, with an infinite amount of good and bad spread across it, then any finite thing one of us does today — saving a life, giving to charity, whatever it might be — gets absorbed into that infinite total without changing it at all, in the same way that adding five dollars to an infinite pile of money doesn't make the pile any bigger. So, strictly speaking, he says, no action can be shown to leave the world's total value any better or worse than any other action would. He still gives to charity and tries to be kind, but insists he can no longer justify these choices as producing more good than the alternatives, since on his view they all come out tied. This bothers me a great deal, since it still seems obviously better to pull a drowning child out of a lake than to walk past. I can't accept his conclusion, but I can't quite identify what's wrong with his reasoning either. Does his argument actually hold up, or is he missing something?
