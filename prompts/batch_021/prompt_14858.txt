So I've been on the crypto/DeFi beat for a while now, including reporting on the Terra/Luna implosion back when it happened, and I keep coming back to all these "algorithmic stablecoin" and "yield farming" setups where a smart contract just auto-compounds returns on whatever coin you've staked, no bank or lender anywhere in sight. Somewhere in the middle of re-reporting this stuff I went back and actually read the bit in Aristotle's Politics on money and exchange, not just the pull-quote version everyone recycles about "usury bad," and it kinda broke my brain, because so much of what's happening in crypto seems almost custom-built to test his specific arguments rather than just generically offend them.

So here's what I want from you: reconstruct as carefully as you can, using Aristotle's actual reasoning in the Politics and the Nicomachean Ethics rather than the cliché version, what he'd say about three specific things. First, would something like Bitcoin or an algorithmic stablecoin even count as "nomisma" (money) under his own definition, given that he ties money's authority to nomos -- convention agreed on by a political community -- and there's no polis backing any of this? Second, using his oikonomike/chrematistike distinction and his etymological point about tokos (interest as money literally "giving birth" to more money, the offspring resembling the parent), is fully automated, code-generated DeFi yield a MORE unnatural case of tokos than a human moneylender charging interest, or actually less unnatural, since no individual person is doing the exploiting? And third -- this is the one that got me -- a bunch of hard-money and gold-standard types like to cite Aristotles account of the origin of currency as ancient support for "real," commodity-backed money over fiat. But he's also pretty explicit elsewhere that moneys value rests on agreement/convention, not on any natural property of the metal itself. Doesn't that actually cut the other way, and make him more sympathetic to purely conventional currencies (even a badly-run algorithmic one) than his hard-money fans assume? Feel free to flag where you're extrapolating beyond the text versus where he's fairly explicit -- I'd genuinely rather have the uncertainty marked than get a cleaner answer that's overconfident.
