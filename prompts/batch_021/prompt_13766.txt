Ok this might sound like a weird thing to be stuck on, but I've had chronic lower back pain for about six years now, and my pain clinic has me keep a daily log where every evening I rate the pain from 0 to 10, mostly so my doctor can track patterns over time and adjust meds accordingly. I've been doing it so long it's basically automatic, but a few weeks ago something started bugging me and I can't shake it: how do I actually know I'm using the scale consistently over time? Like, is a 6 I wrote down back in March the same amount of pain as the 6 I wrote last night? There's no ruler for this and no outside check involved, its literally just whatever number feels right to me in the moment I write it down.

I mentioned this to a couple of friends and one of them said I was definately overthinking a simple form, that of course its consistent because I just remember what past pain levels felt like and calibrate against that memory each time I write a new number down. That answer doesn't really satisfy me though, because couldn't my memory of what a 6 felt like also just be quietly drifting over time, with nothing outside my own head available to check it against either? It feels like there's no way, even in principle, for me to step outside my own experience and confirm the number is tracking the same thing month to month. Am I overthinking this, or is there actually something off about my friend's answer? And if the worry does hold up, does it mean logs like mine (and mood trackers, and pretty much any self-rating scale) can't really be trusted to track anything real over time, or is there some way around that?
