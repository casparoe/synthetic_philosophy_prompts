I spend most of my day job thinking about distributed consensus (raft, paxos, that whole world), and I recently went down a rabbit hole on Arrow's impossibility theorem for the first time. Honestly it seems like basically the same problem wearing a different hat: you've got a bunch of independent nodes/voters, each with there own inputs, and you're trying to combine them into one fair, consistent group outcome — and it turns out that's just mathematically impossible without breaking some perfectly reasonable rule somewhere. So is Arrow's theorem basically just the OG version of the FLP impossibility result, except economists found it first and used ballots instead of message logs? Kind of blows my mind that econ people and distributed systems people apparently hit the exact same wall decades apart and don't even cite each other. Am I missing something, or did we basically just reinvent the same theorem twice under different names?
