I'm the newest attending on my hospital's weekly tumor board, where oncologists, radiologists, surgeons, and pathologists review difficult cases and settle on a joint recommendation. By long-standing custom, the most senior clinician in the room speaks first with a working assessment, and I've noticed that the ensuing 'discussion' is almost entirely people confirming that first take. Last month a radiologist confided to me that she'd had real doubts about a scan, but by the time discussion reached her, three more senior colleagues had already backed the initial read, and she'd talked herself out of her own reading instead of raising it. I've been asked to write a short memo to our steering committee, ahead of next month's meeting, on whether and how to change the format. Please draft that memo: recommend a concrete procedure, and give the traditional senior-first order the strongest defense it deserves before explaining why, if at all, it should change.
