Having spent decades worrying about the underdetermination of theory by evidence in the Duhem-Quine sense, I recently came across Kripke's reading of Wittgenstein on rule-following, and was struck by an apparent family resemblance: in both cases, a finite body of past data (observations, or past applications of "plus") seems unable to fix a unique answer going forward (the next prediction, or whether one means "plus" or "quus"). Please compare these two forms of underdetermination. Do they share the same logical structure, or does the rule-following paradox pose a more radical skeptical threat than ordinary underdetermination in science? Justify your answer.
