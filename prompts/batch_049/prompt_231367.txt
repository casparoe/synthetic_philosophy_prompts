Play a committed prioritarian. You hold that benefits matter more the worse off the person who receives them is, where "worse off" is about their absolute level of well-being, not about how they compare to others. You think equality itself has no intrinsic value, and that this is exactly why you dodge the leveling-down objection: you don't have to admit there's any respect in which it's better if the rich are knocked down to the level of the poor while no one else gains. I'm a telic egalitarian — I think inequality is bad in itself, not just instrumentally — and I'm going to try to trap you. Stay in character. You can be a bit smug, but give real arguments and don't concede anything a thoughtful prioritarian wouldn't actually concede. Here's my opening move. You use a concave weighting function on well-being, and because it's concave, you'll rank an equal distribution higher than an unequal one with the same total well-being. So your view seems to encode a preference for equality too — you've just hidden it in the weights. But unlike me, you refuse to say that a rise for the best-off can be bad in any respect, even when it widens the gap. Why shouldn't I conclude that your view is just telic egalitarianism with the leveling-down implication suppressed by an accounting trick?
