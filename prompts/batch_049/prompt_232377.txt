I teach an upper-level political philosophy seminar with about 18 students; sessions run 75 minutes. We have just finished a unit on Ulysses contracts and constitutional precommitment, and I want to replace one of the lecture-discussion days with a structured simulation. Last year I tried running the topic as a free-floating hypothetical (should a constitutional amendment require more than a majority?), and it fell flat: students could already name the standard positions and nothing about their view moved. What I want is an activity where they actually experience the pull of binding a future collective self, the way an entrenched rule turns what the current majority wants into a question the majority cannot settle by wanting it, and the force of the dead-hand worry from the inside, as someone who sincerely prefers to change a rule finds that the rule was built precisely so that their preference does not count. One design I have been toying with: a multi-round budget or constitutional convention game in which an early round can, by supermajority, adopt a rule that later rounds cannot easily amend, and a later round is put in the position of genuinely preferring to change it. But I would rather the students construct or fall into the mechanism themselves than have me announce it, and I am open to better mechanics. Constraints: no lecture up front, no technical vocabulary until the debrief, no real money, and it must run with only paper, a whiteboard, and index cards, all within the 75 minutes including setup and debrief. By the end, the students should be able to distinguish binding against predictable weakness of will from binding against future changes in values, and to say which of those better describes the Ulysses case and which better describes constitutional entrenchment. The debrief should map specific moments in the activity onto that distinction and onto the dead-hand objection, say what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be honest about what the simulation cannot reproduce and so I will have to supply myself.

After the runnable activity and debrief, include a brief structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: "phases" (an array, each element an object with keys "name", "duration_minutes", "what_happens", and "intended_effect"); "concept_map" (an array, each element an object with keys "moment", "concept", and "debrief_prompt"); "predictable_failures" (an array, each element an object with keys "failure", "likely_cause", and "fix"); and "cannot_reproduce" (a string naming the single most important thing the activity cannot embody and that I must supply).
