I host a small philosophy podcast, and next week's guest has just published a book defending the claim that no one is ever truly morally responsible for anything they do. His centerpiece is the Basic Argument: in outline, you act as you do because of the way you are; to be truly responsible for what you do, you would have to be truly responsible for the way you are; and you cannot be, because the way you are traces back to your genetic inheritance and early experience, neither of which you chose. I recently found myself in a late-night argument with friends about whether a mutual acquaintance really deserved the blame he received, and I was struck by how quickly the conversation cycled through the same three or four moves without landing anywhere. I want this episode to move past restating the argument and actually stress-test it.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions for him. I want at least one substantive question on each of the following, with a follow-up keyed to the reply he is most likely to give: (a) the slide from being responsible to being ultimately responsible, whether the argument only works by demanding that agents be self-created, and whether ordinary practices of blame ever really demanded that much — he tends to answer that compatibilists merely recover a pale, surface-level substitute; (b) the argument's claimed independence from determinism, and whether adding undetermined events to the causal story would help at all; (c) the status of blame, praise, gratitude, and resentment once the conclusion is accepted — he grants that the belief is unliveable in practice, so press him on what those attitudes amount to after he is convinced, and on when, if ever, he thinks they should be softened or abandoned; and (d) how much work the qualifier "truly" is doing, and whether the dispute collapses into something merely verbal if the qualifier is dropped. Sequence the questions so they build on one another, and end with a question that forces him to say what about the world, or about human agency, would have to be different for the argument to fail.
