I'm trying to pin down the doctrine of double effect and I think my notes might be garbled. As I have it, DDE basically is the doing/allowing distinction — harms you cause are harder to justify than harms you merely allow — so in the standard bystander case, pulling the lever is an action, and I'd have expected DDE to forbid it. But most people I've read say DDE allows it because the one death is just a foreseen side effect. Then they flip and treat the loop case like the footbridge, where you push a heavy person in front of the trolley to stop it. But in the loop case it's the same sort of act as the lever case: you redirect the trolley onto a side track with one person on it, except the side track loops back onto the main line before the five, so if the one person weren't there the trolley would circle round and kill the five anyway — the one's body stopping the trolley is what actually saves them. Since both are just lever-pulls, I can't see how the doing/allowing distinction separates them. And in the transplant case, if the surgeon doesn't harvest the organs from the one healthy person, he's only allowing the five patients to die, which on my notes should be the most permissable option of all — yet DDE is usually wheeled out to forbid the transplant. What exactly am I missing? I'm not after the history; give me the actual rule and walk me through how it applies to the lever, the loop, and the transplant cases.
