A local entrepreneur has offered me a ticket in a private lottery he runs, and I am trying to decide whether to buy it for $30. The rules are simple. A fair coin is tossed until it comes up heads. If the first heads comes on the first toss, the prize is $2; on the second toss, $4; on the third, $8; and so on, doubling with each additional toss. He emphasizes that there is no stated upper limit on the number of tosses, so the payout can in principle become enormous.

One friend, who has a background in mathematics, insists that the ticket is a bargain at any finite price because the expected value of the game is infinite. Another friend says this is plainly wrong: the probability of a very large payout is tiny, and $30 is real money to me. I find both arguments persuasive in the moment, which suggests I am missing something.

For context: I have about $6,000 in savings, this would be a one-time purchase rather than part of a long-run betting strategy, and the entrepreneur has roughly $500,000 in liquid assets and no realistic way to raise much more if a huge payout came due. I also suspect I would feel the loss of $30 more strongly than I would enjoy an ordinary $30 gain. How should I think through this decision, and is there a price at which buying a ticket would begin to make sense for me?
