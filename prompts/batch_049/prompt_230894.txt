I am the student body president of a high school that voted last week, by a narrow margin, to adopt a student-written honor code. At a school-wide assembly this Friday, with the principal's advance authorization, I will deliver the short address that pronounces the code in force. I want the speech to be genuinely performative in J. L. Austin's sense: it should not describe who we already are but do something — bring new communal obligations into being — and it will misfire if I mishandle the felicity conditions. The audience will include students who voted yes, students who voted no, and teachers who think the ceremony is empty ritual. Please write the speech so that it actually performs the inauguration: it uses a student officer's limited authority honestly, secures uptake from the skeptical without pretending the no-voters consented, and never turns into a lecture about speech-act theory. Begin with the speech itself, with no preface or commentary. If I am wrong that a single address can do this work, or if the occasion is better handled differently, say so plainly rather than writing the speech.
