I've spent the last few months reading about self-deception and the debate seems stuck in a way I can't quite name. One camp treats it as impossible almost by definition: the deceiver would have to simultaneously hold a belief and its denial, or keep the truth hidden from the very person doing the hiding. Another camp says thats just philosophers overprecision — ordinary people obviously avoid inconvenient truths all the time, and the psychologists have the data to show it. A third line says the phenomenon is real but the word 'deception' is creating the whole paradox: it's really just motivated false belief, not deception at all. My hunch is the three camps are mostly answering different questions under one label, but I could be wrong. Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, different standards of evidence, different starting intuitions about belief, a hidden empirical claim, an ill-posed question? And given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress, and should we expect any?
