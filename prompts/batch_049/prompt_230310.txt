so ive been going down a rabbit hole about whether sentience is a matter of degree, like a bee is more sentient than a fruit fly, which is more than, i dunno, a roomba, and eventually you get down to a thermostat and most people want to say zero there. im a backend engineer, did some embedded hobby stuff, so control loops and pid controllers are kinda my native language. i never took a philosophy class, and my eyes glaze over at the actual papers, but heres my best reconstruction of the arguements. the thing i keep noticing is that a lot of the positions sound like theyre really arguments about software architecture with the vocabulary stripped out.

the way i see it there are at least three positions in this debate (im paraphrasing, not citing anybody):

1. the gradient position: what matters is valence, roughly the good-for/bad-for signal. a thermostat has a setpoint and an error signal and it steers back toward the setpoint. a bee has prediction error, a bit of memory of past states, and can steer toward and away from lots of learned values rather than one fixed setpoint. for the gradient person, the thermostat has a microscopic sliver of the same kind of thing the bee's bad-feeling is, becuase the error signal is doing real steering. moral status just scales with how rich the steering is.

2. the owner position: valence only counts when there is a "someone" who owns the error. a thermostat's error signal isnt bad for anybody; its just a difference between two voltages. a bee might have a minimal subject, or a central-ish value function that is picked out from inside the system rather than read off it by an observer. below the owner threshold you get zero status no matter how fancy the behavior is; above it you can still have degrees.

3. the projection position: says "wants" talk about thermostats is like reading a log line as an intent that was never in the code. the setpoint is something the designer or operator mounts into the system, so the system has no internal norm of its own. the thermostat is zero not becuase its simple but becuase the norm is external. (this position then has to say something awkward about a system that learns and rewrites its own value function, wich complicates it nicely.)
