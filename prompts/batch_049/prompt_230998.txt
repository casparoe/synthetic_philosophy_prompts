Ok here's one. I'm in a reading group with two people who have been going back and forth for months about the doctrine of double effect, specifically about the switch/footbridge cases and close variants. One of them (call her A) is a defender of DDE: she thinks the intention/foresight distinction is doing real moral work, and that what makes the surgeon case impermissible and the strategic bombing case (on her view, maybe) permissible is whether the death is intended as a means or merely foreseen as a side effect. The other (call him B) thinks DDE is mostly post hoc pattern-matching: he claims that tweaking low-level details—like whether the fat man is pushed, dropped through a trapdoor, or shoved into the path of the trolley—changes people's verdicts in ways the intention/foresight distinction can't track. They've agreed to do an adversarial collaboration.

Write the document they should have produced. It must have: a numbered list of claims both can sign, which should be substantial and not just "they disagree about X" (cover what they both accept about the standard cases, about what the distinction can and cannot explain, and about which features of cases are doing the work). Then a disagreements table: each remaining disagreement stated in neutral wording, plus a column or row for how each side would phrase its own view. For each disagreement give at least one crux—a subquestion or pair of cases such that settling it would actually move at least one of them. For each crux, spell out a concrete case or argument both would accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what A and B each predict about it (e.g., what pattern of judgments they expect, where they think the other side's description collapses or becomes arbitrary). Be really exact about where they part ways: is the dispute about descriptive moral psychology, about normative relevance, or about whether "intending as a means" can be defined non-arbitrarily in the first place? Use the switch, footbridge, and loop cases, and at least two non-trolley cases (hysterectomy vs. craniotomy, strategic vs. terror bombing, or something similar), but only in ways that test the distinction, not cases that just restate it. Also seperate out the points where B thinks A's view is a verbal move from the points where it makes a real difference.
