US patent law draws a strange line: if a company isolates the BRCA1 gene from a cell, it's treated as a product of nature and can't be patented; but if the company synthesizes a cDNA version of it — roughly the same protein-coding sequence, just with the introns spliced out — that counts as a human invention and is patentable. What does that line presuppose about what a gene, or DNA itself, is? In particular, does it presuppose that DNA is literally a code or information that can be separated from the molecule it's written in, and is that coherent? Could someone who thinks DNA is just chemistry rather than information draw the same line for a different reason, or would the line have to move?
