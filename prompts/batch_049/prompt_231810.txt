My uncle's a criminal-defense attorney and over thanksgiving he told us about two guys he represented at different points, both caught for basically the same fraud — same amounts, same planning, same damage. One got a substantially lighter sentence mostly because he apologized to the victims and the judge said she found the remorse credible. The other didn't apologize, sat there looking furious, and got hit with almost the max. What got me isn't the outcome exactly, it's the logic underneath it. We keep saying the system punishes acts, not character — that its not the state's job to punish who you are. But remorse is about who the person is now, after the act: an emotional state, an attitude toward what he did, maybe a sign of character. So why should changing who someone is after the crime make the act itself less punishable?

And it gets weirder. Courts put real effort into sorting sincere remorse from performance — did the defendant blame the victim, did he make specific amends, has he shown anything beyond words. If the point were only forward-looking, about whether this person will reoffend, sincerity per se wouldn't matter much. You can be genuinely sorry and still dangerous, or a smooth faker who's actually safe, so the focus on sincerity suggests some other theory of blame and guilt is doing the work quietly. I've been reading Strawson on resentment and blame and I think I'm circling the answer but can't make it precise. So I'd like you to reconstruct the implicit theory this sentencing practice presupposes. Does it actually require something like the reactive-attitudes view, or is it compatible with several different theories? Which parts of the practice are philosophically underdetermined? And if we replaced that implicit picture with a purely deterrent or incapacitation-based view, what exactly would have to change — would we stop treating sincere remorse as a reason to reduce the sentence, stop treating lack of remorse as a reason to increase it, or could they somehow keep all of it for other reasons?
