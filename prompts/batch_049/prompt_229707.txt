I recently spent a long evening arguing with a friend about epiphenomenalism, and I would like the dispute set out more carefully than either of us managed in conversation. Please write an essay on the paradox of phenomenal judgement and what, if anything, it shows about epiphenomenalism.

The essay should first explain what epiphenomenalism claims: roughly, that conscious experiences are produced by physical processes in the brain but have no causal effects of their own, so that all behaviour, including every report one makes about one's experiences, is caused by physical processes alone. It should then develop the paradox in its strongest form: if experiences never cause anything, then the very judgements in which we affirm and describe our experiences are not caused by those experiences. That seems to threaten the claim that we know our own phenomenal lives, and it may make the epiphenomenalist's report of her own view self-undermining. Consider the main replies open to an epiphenomenalist — for example, that introspection gives a non-causal form of access, that judgements about experience are justified by sharing a common physical cause with the experience rather than by being caused by it, or that knowledge of one's own experiences requires the absence rather than the presence of a causal link — and consider the main objections to those replies. Also weigh the response that the paradox is not a special problem for epiphenomenalism, or that it merely dramatises a difficulty about content and knowledge that any physicalist must face.

Weigh the arguments and come to a conclusion rather than ending neutrally. A qualified verdict is perfectly acceptable — for instance, that the paradox is fatal to epiphenomenalism, that it is survivable but costly, or that it misfires entirely — but the essay should defend whichever position it takes.

Throughout, please distinguish what is settled from what is genuinely contested among philosophers working on this topic, and do not present a contested view as if it were the consensus. Write in British English.
