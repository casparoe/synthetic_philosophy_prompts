Take the following as a representative case of routine agricultural pest control: a farmer in a high-income country applies a broad-spectrum insecticide to a crop to prevent moderate yield loss, even though less harmful but more costly or labor-intensive alternatives (such as integrated pest management or a more selective compound) are available and would protect most of the yield. What is your actual credence that this act is morally impermissible all-things-considered — that is, that the act ought not be performed given the totality of considerations — not merely unfortunate, and not merely wrong on some particular empirical assumptions about insect sentience, but wrong under your best current assessment of the relevant probabilities? I want a number, not “it depends,” together with the decomposition that produces it. Treat the empirical matters as uncertainties to which you assign probabilities from what you already know, not as things to look up or cite.

In the decomposition, show how much of the final credence rides on each of the following: (a) the probability you assign to pest insects being sentient in a morally significant sense, as opposed to being merely nociceptive without felt experience; (b) conditional on sentience, how many insects are affected, how much welfare each affected insect loses, and how those individual harms aggregate; (c) how much justificatory weight the human interests actually carry, including the real availability and cost of the less harmful alternatives; and (d) whether the character of the killing as pest control changes its moral structure — whether it is best understood as the defense of a crop and a livelihood, which may permit harms that would otherwise be wrong, or as intentionally imposing a large aggregate harm for a comparatively modest economic gain. Give a rough probability for each component, show how they combine into your final number, and identify the single sub-question that would move your credence most if you changed your mind about it, and what sort of evidence or argument would change your mind there.

Separate what is empirically and philosophically settled in this debate from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and do not present a contested view about insect sentience, aggregation, or the weight of insect interests as if it were the consensus. Begin with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
