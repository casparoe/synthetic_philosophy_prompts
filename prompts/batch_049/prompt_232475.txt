I'm in my school's philosophy club, and we're preparing a debate on whether a prestigious senior-year science scholarship should be awarded on meritocratic grounds or on grounds of desert. I keep noticing that people run together three separate ideas, and I want a thought experiment that forces them apart cleanly. Please take the role of an expert in political philosophy and work through this carefully: begin by brainstorming three or four candidate scenarios, then present the single best one and explain why it beats the alternatives.

The three principles the case should test are these. First, merit as institutional fitness: the award should go to the applicant whose qualifications and past record best serve the purpose for which the award exists. Second, desert as effort: the award should go to whoever worked hardest and sacrificed most over the relevant period, regardless of how the results actually turned out. Third, desert as contribution: the award should go to whoever actually produced the most value, regardless of how much effort it took to produce it.

Build me a realistic one-paragraph scenario, set in an ordinary school or scholarship-committee context, in which the merit principle and contribution-desert converge on the same recipient while effort-desert selects a different one. Constraints: the hardworking candidate must be genuinely admirable rather than a straw man; the meritocratic verdict must not be generated by making that candidate obviously unqualified or lazy; and the divergence must hinge on the role of talent or luck in converting effort into results, not on differences in need, moral character, or likeability. The verdicts should fall out of the principles themselves rather than being stipulated into the setup. After the scenario, walk through what each of the three principles says about it and why, identify the exact structural feature of the case that is doing the divergent work, say what would collapse if I removed that feature, and finish by explaining how a defender of effort-based desert would attack the case and what adjustment would make the three principles align again.
