Here's a view I've landed on: Jeffrey conditionalization is the whole story about rational updating on uncertain evidence. The setup is the usual one — evidence arrives as a shift in your credences over some partition (the shape in the fog moves you to 0.7 deer, 0.3 person), and you update by keeping your probabilities conditional on the partition cells fixed while the new cell probabilities propagate through. Those new cell probabilities are just inputs; where they come from is somebody else's problem. Part of what sells me on the view is that the rule has that nice characterization as the minimum relative entropy update, the one that stays closest to your old distribution while matching the new cell probabilities — so it looks canonical rather than like one gadget among many.

What would count against this? I want a map of where the view is actually exposed, with defeaters sorted by how much damage they do rather than just listed. Which are fatal, which are merely awkard, and which should a committed Jeffrey conditionalizer shrug off as problems for a different theory? In particular I want to know whether partition choice is a genuine crack or just a standard objection, whether there's any live alternative left for plain Bayesian conditionalization beyond the degenerate special case, and whether I've insulated the view into unfalsifiability by declaring the inputs exogenous — and if I have, whether that should bother me. Also tell me what would change your own mind: which argument or result, if it came out the other way, would flip your ranking, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
