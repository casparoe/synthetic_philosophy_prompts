"the money pump argument goes like this. Suppose your preferences are intransitive: you'd take A over B, B over C, and C over A. Then a trader can start you off with A, charge you a small fee to swap it for C, then for B, then for A again, and you end up exactly where you started except poorer. That proves intransitive preferences are irrational, full stop. The same logic shows hyperbolic discounting is irrational too: a present-biased person will prefer the larger-later reward when both are off in the future, but flip to the smaller-sooner reward once the sooner one is right in front of them, so a trader can exploit that reversal. And notice the argument doesn't need any assumption that the person is myopic or fails to see the whole sequence coming—each individual trade is preferred at the moment it's on offer, so a fully rational agent walks right into it anyway."
