Me and my friend were up half the night arguing about al-Ghazali and causation. She kept saying ordinary causes aren't really causes—the fire doesn't burn the cotton, the cue ball doesn't move the eight ball, my decision doesn't move my arm. But I couldn't tell whether she's a full-blown occasionalist who thinks God's the only real cause, or just a regularity person who thinks there's no necessary connection anywhere.

Her verdicts so far: billiard balls—no real production, just constant succession; fire and cotton—no necessary connection, it could totally fail to burn next time; my deciding to raise my arm—decision and movement are just conjoined events, no power doing the work; a magnet and iron filings—no pull, just co-occurrence. Every time I press on the "oomph" part she says your smuggling in something you can't observe.

The case I want to throw at her next: a healer prays and the patient's fever breaks at exactly that moment, but it's a one-off—no pattern before, no repeat after. What's she going to say happened? Is she best read as an out-and-out occasionalist, a Humean about necessary connection, or something else? And if her answer could go either way, what follow-up case would finally settle which rule she's running?
