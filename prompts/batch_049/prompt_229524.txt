I'm a novelist, and the main character of my next book keeps a private ledger of everyone who's ever wronged him, checking off their misfortunes — a layoff, a divorce, a car wreck — with this quiet satisfaction, all while telling himself he doesn't believe in cosmic justice. His sister does the public version: whenever some influencer or local politician gets caught in a scandal, she shrugs and says 'karma's real', then immediately denies she thinks the universe actually settles accounts. What I'm trying to pin down is what picture of moral desert these habits presuppose, if any. Does the 'karma' reflex strictly require something like a cosmic balancing mechanism and a proportionality principle, or can it survive as pure metaphor? And where exactly is it underdetermined, so that two or three rival theories of desert would produce exactly the same comments? I'm not after a survey of karma; I want the implicit theory reconstructed, with the load-bearing parts seperated from the parts that are just window dressing.
