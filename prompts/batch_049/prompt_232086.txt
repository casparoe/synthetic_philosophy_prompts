I spent my working life as a research scientist, and since I retired I've been reading moral philosophy — probably too much of it. I've ended up with a view I'd like you to stress-test rather than just push back on for sport. Here it is. I think there's a real difference in the default moral sensibilities of Germans and Americans, and that the German one has the better of it. By the German side I mean something like: following the rules, doing your part, and keeping the shared order intact is itself treated as a moral achievement, not just prudence or fear of punishment. By the American side I mean something like: individual conscience is the last court of appeal, and rule-following is looked at with suspicion — as conformity, or as dodging responsibility. I don't mean fixed national character. I mean the default idioms people reach for when they raise children, justify a decision, or react to someone who broke a rule for what they took to be a good reason. My view is that, on the whole, the German idiom is more honest about what morality actually demands of us and more likely to produce reliable decent behavior, while the American idiom flatters our vanity and leaves too much room for self-deception.

I know this is the sort of view that can survive any particular counterexample by muttering "well, I said on the whole." So what I want from you is a map of what would genuinely count against it: which observations, historical cases, or findings from moral psychology should move me, and which would be decisive rather than merely awkward — as close as you can get to telling me what measurement would change my mind. What would a defender of the American side say that I'd have to grant is a real cost, and what should I myself concede counts against the German side if I'm being honest? How much of my view is an empirical claim about how people actually behave and how much a normative claim about what morality should look like — and can those two be pulled apart? If it turns out the view is insulated against evidence, tell me weather that's a problem for a view of this kind, or just the price of having any view about something this messy. I'd rather find out I'm wrong then keep a view that's really just my temperament with a reading list attached.
