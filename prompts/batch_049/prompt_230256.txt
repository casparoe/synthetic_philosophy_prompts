I've been trying to get my head around the debate approach to AI alignment, where you have two AI systems argue opposite sides of some claim and a human judge decides which one won. A friend of mine who works on AI policy keeps insisting the whole idea has a known fatal flaw: a clever debater can obfuscate. It might bury a bad inferential step inside a dense technical digression, or make a correct argument in a form the judge can't actually follow, and then the judge is persuaded for reasons that have nothing to do with whether the claim is true. His line is that this isnt just an engineering hiccup, it's a deep limit, because debate was supposed to let a weak judge supervise stronger systems, but now the judge's verdict is only as trustworthy as the judge's ability to follow the reasoning — and the whole point was to avoid requiring a judge that competent. I'm not fully convinced it's fatal: maybe cross-examination lets the honest debater expose the obfuscation, or maybe the judge can spot the wrapper even if they can't spot the flaw. But I keep going back and forth, and I dont have a clean way to say what the disagreement is really about.
