Two ML engineers on my team got into an argument I couldn't settle. Our model has four cells: US paid, US free, international paid, international free. The prior is [0.08, 0.12, 0.32, 0.48]. Then a release note tells us the paid percentage among US users is now 70%, and gives us nothing else. Our prior had that conditional at 40%. Marcus says since that's not an event, plain Bayesian updating doesn't apply, and the least wrong thing is to find the posterior that satisfies the constraint while minimizing KL divergence from the prior. He ran the numbers: it keeps the international paid/free ratio at 40/60, makes US paid/free 70/30, but also shifts P(US) from .20 down to about .181. Dana said that's nuts: why would a conditional about US users change how likely an account is to be US at all? Her rule: hold P(US) at .20 and just re-normalize the US row, so US paid goes from .08 to .14 and US free goes from .12 to .06, with international untouched. Marcus says "untouched international" isn't free: she's using a different distance metric, one that treats the US marginal as sacred, and the message doesn't license that. Dana says his KL rule lets a global optimization push around a base rate the evidence never touched. They went back and forth a few more times along the same lines. Who's right about which specific point, what are both of them missing, and which update should I actually use here?
