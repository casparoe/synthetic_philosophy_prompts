Bradley's regress can be deployed in two directions: against the unity of facts — if a state of affairs such as a being F is composed of a particular, a universal, and an instantiation relation, what unites those constituents? — and against the unity of propositions — if the proposition that a is F is a structured complex, what ties its constituents together? Compare these two deployments of the regress. Are they one problem in different vocabulary, or does each exert different pressure on its target? In particular, consider whether one target can evade the regress by taking unities as primitive and non-mereological — for example, by treating facts, or alternatively propositions, as prior to their constituents — and whether the other target has a move of equal strength. State and defend a conclusion about which deployment is ultimately more damaging. Limit: 1,000 words.
