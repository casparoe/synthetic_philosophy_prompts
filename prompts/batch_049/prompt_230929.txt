ok so I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, just podcasts, and ive been reading the machinery chapter of Capital because my feed is split between 'AI will take all our jobs' and 'automation will finally free us'. I keep coming back to one idea and I'd like it as a poem to read out at the end of our little work reading group. A sonnet, 14 lines, with an actual volta. The octave should build up the side of Marx that sounds almost utopian about machinery: automation genuinely raises the productivity of labour, it could shrink the labour time needed to keep everyone alive, and the machine could in principle lighten the load. Then the volta should turn the way Marx says it actually goes under capitalism: the same machine is used not to shorten the working day but to lengthen and intensify it, it throws workers out of work into what he calls the industrial reserve army, and the worker ends up serving the machine's rhythm instead of the other way round. The closing couplet should put the blame not on the machinery itself but on the social relation - who owns the machines and who directs them. I don't want it preachy or a versified summary of Capital, it should be a good poem first, but someone who knows Marx should recognise the argument. British English please (we're in London).
