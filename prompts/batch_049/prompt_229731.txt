Two of us in a reading group have been working through a variant of the Death in Damascus case, and we have produced an argument that seems to show that a rational chooser must treat the one safe city as nearly fatal and should be indifferent between safety and death. We cannot find the error, and we need a diagnosis rather than a mere verdict.

Set-up. Tomorrow you must travel to exactly one of two cities, Damascus or Aleppo. Death is an equally reliable predictor in both directions: for some small fixed epsilon, conditional on your choosing either city, Death predicted that city with probability 1 minus epsilon and predicted the other city with probability epsilon. The prediction has already been made, and Death has gone to wait in the predicted city. If you arrive where Death is, you die; if you arrive in the other city, you live. You know all this before choosing.

The argument:
(1) An act should be evaluated by the outcome you would expect conditional on performing it.
(2) Conditional on my going to Aleppo, Death predicted Aleppo with probability at least 1 minus epsilon, in which case I die; so my expected survival conditional on going to Aleppo is at most epsilon.
(3) The same reasoning applies to Damascus: conditional on my going to Damascus, my expected survival is at most epsilon.
(4) Therefore going to Aleppo and going to Damascus have the same near-zero expected survival value, and I should be indifferent between them.
(5) But relative to Death's actual location, exactly one of the two cities is safe.
(6) So this way of evaluating acts has made the safe city look nearly fatal and has made deliberation between the two cities pointless. Since that conclusion cannot be right, at least one step must be given up.

Which step should be rejected? Explain why the offending step looked mandatory, and say what each of evidential decision theory, causal decision theory, and a ratifiability account would have to give up in order to reject or repair the argument. After the prose diagnosis, provide a JSON object with the following schema: step_to_reject (string), error_type (one of "false_principle", "invalid_inference", "equivocation", "other"), positions (array of objects, each with fields "name", "diagnosis", "cost"), deepest_remaining_problem (string).
