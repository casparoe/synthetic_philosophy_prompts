I'm a philosophy professor, and I've been keeping a record of the case-by-case verdicts of a visiting epistemologist who works in the hinge-epistemology tradition. Her method is to classify each proposition as either a hinge or an ordinary empirical belief. For her, a hinge is a commitment that stands fast for us, is not in the ordinary way based on evidence, and is something we hold fixed while gathering and weighing evidence; an ordinary empirical belief is one that is checked within inquiry, can be supported or undermined by observation, testimony, or experiment, and is in principle revisable. She has so far declined to state a general criterion, saying she prefers to proceed case by case.

Her verdicts so far:

- Hinge: there is an external world.
- Hinge: the world did not come into existence five minutes ago, with all apparent traces of a past built in.
- Hinge: other people have inner lives.
- Hinge: physical objects continue to exist when no one is perceiving them.
- Ordinary: pure water at standard atmospheric pressure freezes at zero degrees Celsius.
- Ordinary: my train leaves from platform four, on the evidence of the departure board.
- Ordinary: that bird is a finch and not a sparrow, on the evidence of its plumage and song.

Next week I want to put to her the proposition that, as it would be asserted by an ordinary educated adult with no special training, the Earth is roughly spherical.

I'd like you to predict her verdict and to do the analysis rather than simply lay out options. Specifically: (1) what is the rule that seems to be guiding her earlier verdicts? (2) What rival rules fit those verdicts just as well, and what does each imply about the Earth-is-spherical case? (3) What is your best guess as to how she will rule, with the strongest reason you can give? (4) What single further case would most sharply tell the leading rule and its best rival apart, and what would each say about it?

I'm asking for a committed answer: take a definite position on the most likely rule and verdict, argue for it, and say what future verdict would make you revise.
