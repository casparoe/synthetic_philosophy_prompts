Please give me your actual credence — a single number between 0 and 1 — in the following claim:

Benatar's basic asymmetry is a real feature of value, and, together with the minimal premise that every actual sentient life contains at least some pain, it is sufficient to establish that coming into existence is always a harm: every sentient being who comes into existence is worse off, all things considered, than it would have been had it never existed.

By the asymmetry I mean this. Compare two scenarios, one in which a particular being exists and experiences a pain and a pleasure, and one in which that being never exists. The asymmetry says: (i) the presence of pain is bad; (ii) the presence of pleasure is good; (iii) the absence of pain in the nonexistence scenario is good, even though no one exists to enjoy that absence; (iv) the absence of pleasure in the nonexistence scenario is not bad, because no one exists to be deprived of it. If you think this claim has a stronger and a weaker reading, fix the reading that the antinatalist argument actually needs, state which reading you chose, and give your number for that reading.

I am not asking for a survey of the literature or citations; I am asking for your view with the reasoning laid bare. Decompose your credence into at least the following components, and give each component a probability with a short justification:

1. P(the judgments in (iii) and (iv) are robust: they are not fully explained away as a cognitive bias, a quirk of ordinary language, or an equivocation on "good" and "bad").
2. P(given 1, the asymmetry is a feature of value itself rather than a feature of the deliberative standpoint of prospective parents, a rule of procreative responsibility, or some merely pragmatic evaluative practice).
3. P(given 1 and 2, and given the near-certain premise that every actual sentient life contains at least some pain, the asymmetry does the central work in the argument: it yields that existence is worse than nonexistence for every actual sentient being, rather than yielding only a pro tanto reason against procreation or a conclusion limited to lives that go badly).
4. P(no independently motivated person-affecting restriction on value — one that refuses to count absent pain as a benefit or absent pleasure as a deprivation when the person never exists — both captures the intuitive pull of (iii) and (iv) and blocks the inference in 3).

Then show the combination: make explicit how these terms, and any others you add, multiply or otherwise combine into your overall credence, and where any conditional dependencies or failures of independence enter.

Finally, identify the term you are least confident in and say what specific argument or evidence would most move it; then state which single consideration, if it went the other way, would most change your overall credence — ideally enough to cross 0.5 in the opposite direction — and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
