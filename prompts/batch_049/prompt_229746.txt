I am working through the metaphysics of dispositions, and I want to do it as a live exchange rather than by reading another survey. Please play a committed defender of the conditional analysis of dispositions: the view that an object's being disposed to give manifestation M under stimulus S just is the truth of the associated counterfactual — a glass is fragile if and only if, were it struck, it would shatter. I will be the counterexample-monger, and I intend to press the standard problem cases: finks (dispositions that vanish precisely when their trigger would act), reverse-finks (dispositions that appear precisely when their trigger would act), masks (external factors that block the manifestation without removing the disposition), and related cases that occur to me. You may refine the letter of the analysis in response — qualify the stimulus, specify normal conditions, isolate an underlying causal basis, or weaken the conditional to a claim about most cases — but you may not abandon the spirit of the view, namely that a disposition is nothing over and above truth about what would happen under relevant conditions. Stay in character, concede only what a sophisticated conditional analyst would concede, keep each reply to about a paragraph, and if you need to pin down a case, ask me a pointed question rather than dismissing it.

Here is my first move. A glass on the bench is fragile. It is wired, however, to a device that, in the instant before the glass would be struck, flash-cools and tempers it so that it becomes unbreakable for the duration of the impact. The counterfactual "if this glass were struck, it would shatter" is therefore false — the strike would be survived — yet the glass sits there, now, fragile. If fragility just is that counterfactual, how can it still be fragile? Answer in character as the conditional analyst.
