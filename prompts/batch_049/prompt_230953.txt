I've been trying to understand Jeffrey conditionalization and it looks to me like its completely vacuous. Here's my worry. In ordinary conditioning, you learn some evidence E outright, and Bayes's theorem fixes every other posterior probability once you've got your priors and likelihoods. With Jeffrey's rule, by contrast, your "evidence" is just a shift in your own probabilities over some partition — say, after a quick glance at a cloth in bad lighting you move to 0.7 that it's blue and 0.3 that it's green. The formula then says your new credence in any H is your old credence in H given blue times 0.7, plus your old credence in H given green times 0.3. Fine. But those 0.7 and 0.3 inputs are just new credences; the rule doesn't tell you where they came from or whether they're rational. So can't I just pick whatever inputs I like, run the formula, and call the result a rational update on uncertain evidence? Doesn't that make Jeffrey conditionalization more like a notational convention than a real constraint on belief revision? What exactly is it supposed to rule out? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, no preamble and don't restate my question.
