Draft the document that a committed defender of Procreative Beneficence — roughly, the view that when prospective parents can choose among possible future children, they have a moral obligation to choose the child with the best expected life prospects — and a committed defender of a permissive-but-not-directive liberal eugenics — the view that selection is often permissible and should be legally available, but that no such general obligation exists — could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Open with a numbered list of claims both parties accept, being careful to keep distinct a pro tanto reason to select, an all-things-considered obligation to select, and a bare permission to select, and to say whether any apparent disagreement dissolves once these are separated. Then state each residual disagreement twice, in wording each side would accept for its own view. Then identify the cruxes of the debate — sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one party. For each crux, provide a concrete case, argument, or kind of finding both sides would accept as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. Cruxes worth addressing include whether procreative choices should be evaluated person-affectingly or impersonally under uncertainty about which child will exist, and whether the plasticity of a child's prospects in response to later social and environmental inputs makes the best-expected-life child too indeterminate to ground an obligation.
