Jeremy Bentham never had to decide whether a process running on silicon could suffer. Predict what he would have said about the moral status of a video-game character in a future simulation whose pain system is functionally identical to a mammal's: damage signals prioritize over competing processes, compel avoidance, drive learning, and are broadcast system-wide, but the implementation is purely computational and there is no biological body anywhere. Would Bentham's criterion of moral considerability, the capacity to suffer, classify such a state as suffering, or does the view quietly import a biological-substrate requirement? Identify the specific commitment in Bentham's account that does the decisive work, and then state the strongest amendment a utilitarian who follows Bentham would need to adopt in order to coherently deny that the character matters morally.
