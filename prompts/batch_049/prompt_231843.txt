I cover the science of mind for a general-interest magazine, and I am currently trying to pin down what it would take for a mental state to be realized in something other than an animal nervous system. The philosopher I have been interviewing refuses to state a general theory, but she has been willing to rule on a sequence of concrete examples. Except where noted, each system is stipulated to have the full outward profile associated with pain: it detects noxious stimuli, takes steps to avoid further damage, and produces pain-like reports. Her verdicts so far are these. A human being in the ordinary pain state is genuinely in pain. An octopus with a radically different nervous system whose internal states occupy the same causal roles is genuinely in pain. A robot controlled by purpose-built silicon circuits that implement exactly the causal network of a human pain system is genuinely in pain. A detailed computational model of that network running in isolation on a supercomputer, with no body and no sensors, is not in pain; it is only a simulation. A giant lookup table that maps inputs to the same avoidance behavior but skips the intermediate causal states is a behavioral mimic, not a system in pain.

I now need to know what these rulings commit her to about a sixth case, on which she has been deliberately silent: a mobile robot controlled by an ordinary computer running a whole-network simulation of the pain system, with the simulation genuinely receiving damage signals from the robot's sensors and genuinely driving its motors. If she is to remain consistent with her earlier verdicts, should I expect her to call this robot in pain? Give me the strongest prediction, the principle that best rationalizes her five prior rulings and supports that prediction, and the strongest alternative principle that also fits all five prior rulings but supports the opposite prediction. Then specify a further case, not among the six considered here, whose outcome would most cleanly separate those two principles, and say what each principle predicts for it.
