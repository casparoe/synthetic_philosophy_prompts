I'm a science journalist working on a longread about insect welfare and the scale argument. The version of the argument I keep hearing goes like this: even if you assign a pretty small probability to any particular insect being sentient, the number of insects alive at any one time is somewhere in the 10^18 to 10^19 range, so the expected number of suffering lives is still enormous — easily swamping the expected suffering of all vertebrate livestock, say. I get the arithmetic. But I don't want the piece to just restate the argument, because the interesting disagreements are downstream: people agree the numbers are huge but disagree wildly about what follows for research, policy, or personal action.

For the piece, I want a section that says: if you take this argument seriously, these are the open questions you should actually care about, in order. So I'd like you to fill that section in. Pick, say, three or four open questions — they might be about sentience markers in insects, how to compare welfare ranges across species, whether the aggregation is even legitimate when the per-individual probabilities get very low, how much we could actually help wild insects, or something else — and rank them by how much they would change where a thoughtful reader should put their attention, money, or political energy. Defend the ordering. I'm less interested in a survey of the debate than in the reasoning: wich questions are upstream of the others, which are tractable enough that progress is plausable in the next decade, and what sort of evidence would knock one down the list.
