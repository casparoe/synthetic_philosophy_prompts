i sit on the grants comittee of a mid-size private foundation that funds basic science, and every cycle we have to decide which multi-year research programmes to renew and which to cut. one of our trustees has read some Lakatos and now she won't stop saying we should keep funding "progressive" programmes and starve "degenerating" ones, but she never tells me how to actually run that test with a stack of grant reports in front of me. i get the general idea — a programme has a hard core you don't test directly, a protective belt of auxiliary assumptions you adjust, and it's doing well when the adjustments generate new predictions that survive, badly when they just get retrofitted to explain the last failure — but i don't know how to turn that into a thing a programme officer can actually run.

so write me the decision procedure. role and constraints: i have maybe half a day per programme, i'm not a working scientist in most of the fields we fund, the decisions are contested when we make them, and whatever we decide has to be defensible in writing to a disappointed lab and to our own board. the procedure should say what counts as a "programme" for our purposes, what questions to ask in order, what would count as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision back to a person because the question is one of judgment rather than fact. i also want you to flag the places where a smart applicant could game the steps, especially around claiming after the fact that some result was a novel prediction of theirs all along, and around dumping every failure into the protective belt while keeping the core safe. then run the procedure on two or three invented but realistic grant files, including at least one where it tells me to do something i'm not going to like, such as renewing a programme that looks stalled or killing one that looks exciting. tell me too what the procedure cannot do, the cases where a Lakatosian lens is just the wrong tool, and where my own additions to the theory begin. there's a lot of talk about what lakatos "really meant," so i want you to separate the parts of the procedure that are settled and defensible from the parts that are genuinely contested among philosophers of science, and not present one controversial reading as the consensus.

please write it as plain continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, just an email-length memo i could forward to the rest of the committee.
