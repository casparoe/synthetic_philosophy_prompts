Imre Lakatos died in 1974, nearly four decades before the methodological disputes that came to be called the replication crisis began to reshape experimental psychology, biomedicine, and parts of economics. In those disputes, several much-cited experimental results failed to reproduce in large, pre-registered, multi-site replications. Some researchers treated a failed replication as tantamount to refuting the original finding; others argued that the failure reflected moderators, procedural differences, or measurement error, and that the core effect ought to be retained while auxiliary assumptions were revised. I would like a careful reconstruction of what Lakatos would have made of this state of affairs, if he were applying his methodology of scientific research programmes to it, rather than a generic summary of the crisis. In particular, please address three questions: (i) Would Lakatos take a failed direct replication to show that the relevant research programme is degenerating, or would he regard it as an anomaly that a programme may legitimately absorb in its protective belt while its hard core remains untouched? (ii) How would he distinguish, among the moves made by defenders of a non-replicating effect, between progressive problem-shifts that successfully predict novel facts and ad hoc stratagems that merely patch the programme against refutation? (iii) Which of the reform practices that emerged from the crisis — pre-registration, direct replication, meta-analysis of many studies together, or something else you take to be central — would he likely regard as a progressive research programme in its own right, and which would he criticize as a naive-falsificationist overreaction?

Please structure your answer in the following titled sections, in order: (1) Lakatos's relevant commitments (a short paragraph identifying which parts of his framework do the work); (2) Predicted assessment (one paragraph giving your best overall reconstruction of his verdict); (3) Reasoning, concept by concept (two to three paragraphs using hard core, protective belt, positive and negative heuristic, and novel facts explicitly); (4) Limits of the prediction (one paragraph on where his framework underdetermines the answer and the strongest alternative reading). Aim for roughly 700–900 words, and do not include citations or a bibliography.
