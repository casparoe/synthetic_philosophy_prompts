I work as a software engineer and recently went down a rabbit hole reading about futarchy — roughly, the idea that a democracy should vote on what outcomes we actually want, and then let prediction markets decide which policy is most likely to get us those outcomes. I've been trying to work out where I'd genuinely be comfortable letting a market make the call and where I wouldn't, based just on my gut reactions to concrete cases. I'd like to know whether there's a principle that captures my verdicts.

Going case by case: (1) whether a city should build a new light rail line, with the market forecasting ridership and average commute times if we build it or don't and betting on the better outcome — fine, I'd be happy with that. (2) whether same-sex marriage should be legal, with the market predicting the effect on average life satisfaction or GDP — not the market's call, that seems wrong to me. (3) whether the central bank should raise interest rates, with a market on inflation a few years out under each path — I'd honestly trust that more than the committee. (4) whether a new drug should be approved, with the market forecasting deaths and hospitalizations under approval vs. rejection — I mostly like this one, though I'd want someone independent checking the outcome numbers. (5) whether to launch a military invasion, with the market predicting casualties and economic cost under invade vs. don't — no, absolutely not, even if the market says invade. (6) whether an individual defendant should be held in pretrial detention, with a market on whether they'll skip their court date or commit a new crime if released — no, and this one feels kind of repugnant to me, betting on a person like that.

What's bugging me is that my pattern isn't just "markets for factual means, voting for values," because the invasion case is also a means-to-an-end question and I still reject it, while the light rail and interest rate cases are means-to-an-end and I accept them. Is there a principal that gets all of these, or close to all? And what would it say about a case I keep going back and forth on: whether a school district should use a market on long-term earnings to decide whether to replace some science classes with programming classes? Finally, which of my verdicts would the principle force me to give up?
