I've been going back and forth with a friend about whether it's wrong to be cruel to NPCs, and we keep realising we don't even agree on what would settle the question. So before either of us reads any more arguments, I want you to write the thing I wish we'd had at the start: the desiderata that any adequate theory of the moral status of video game characters has to meet.

Honestly, I'm less interested in the final verdict — do they matter, and how much — than in what an answer would have to get right to count as an answer at all. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and say whether you think it's a genuine constraint on any theory or just one side's prejudice dressed up as a requirement. I want you to be committed here: don't present a neutral menu of possible requirements and leave me to pick. Say which ones you'd defend if challenged, which ones you'd drop, and which are non-negotiable. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most to exclude candidate views.

Things the list will have to wrestle with include, but shouldn't be limited to: the difference between a character as a bundle of game assets and a character as something that can be harmed within the fiction; whether a crude, obviously scripted cry of pain has the same status as a detailed simulation of a mind; whether status should track what the code actually represents internally, or only outward behaviour, or how it reads to the player; what persistence and deletion do to moral status; and whether there can be duties to beings that can never affect you outside the running of the game. But the real work is turning those into proper requirements with cases behind them, not just checking them off.

If it helps to anchor things, compare three cases: an NPC in a farming sim who has no pain states but whose goals can be thwarted; a monster in a game with a sophisticated fear-and-pain system; and a character in a narrative game who begs the player not to delete a save file. What must a theory say about each before you'd take it seriously?

And please write the whole thing in British English.
