You are a professor of moral philosophy writing the final exam for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on free will and criminal responsibility. The seminar has spent two weeks on the exchange between Daniel Dennett and Gregg Caruso. For purposes of the exam, assume students have been introduced to the following shape of the debate: Caruso argues that, since no one is ultimately responsible in the sense required by basic desert, punishment cannot be justified by “just deserts,” and the criminal justice system should therefore be reorganized on a public-health/quarantine model; Dennett resists this, holding that a compatibilist, competence-based notion of moral responsibility and desert remains available and does real justificatory work. Draft one essay question to be answered in 90 minutes (roughly 1,000–1,200 words). The question must not be answerable by merely summarizing the two positions: it should force a candidate to locate the exact point at which Dennett and Caruso part company, and to adjudicate whether Dennett’s residual notion of desert is a genuine middle position or only a relabeling of forward-looking considerations. After the question, provide a model answer and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points (total 25 marks), with a one-line note for each point on how candidates typically miss it. Think carefully about the crux before writing the question, and make sure the model answer states what a candidate must defend to earn top marks rather than a neutral survey of the debate.
