I keep running into the same tangle in a seminar on what gets called corruption in different countries, and I've started to think there's a distinction people keep reaching for that doesn't quite have a name. Three cases from the last few weeks: a mayor hands a no-bid contract for school meals to his brother-in-law's firm, which charges about a third over market and the surplus ends up back in the family — everyone in the room called that corruption without a second thought. A legislator secures funding for a new motorway interchange in her own district right before an election and runs ads saying she "delivers for us"; almost all the jobs and convenience go to her constituents and local donors, and she wins by a landslide. Half the room called it corruption, half called it representation doing exactly what it's for. Then a third case, from a reading: somewhere with a custom of giving the municipal clerk a bottle or a little envelope when you file for a permit, where the clerk does not speed anything up or bend a rule for it; locals say it's manners, outsiders say it's bribery, and it felt like the same argument played at lower volume.

What I'm trying to figure out is whether there's an established name for the line between corruption that enriches a officeholder or their private circle and corruption that buys the loyalty of a group by steering public resources toward them — and where gift-giving customs sit on that map. I know "clientelism," "patronage," "pork barrel," and "gift exchange" are all somewhere nearby, but I can't tell if one of them names the exact thing or if each covers only one of these three cases. I'm not just after a label: I want to know what feature does the real carving work (who benefits? whether a rule is broken? whether the recipients are a definable group? whether the giver expects a specific decision in return?), and what the people who called the legislator and the gift-giver "not corrupt" are treating as the test.
