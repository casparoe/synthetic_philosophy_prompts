In a philosophy of language reading group I belong to, one member keeps defending a picture he calls the controlled-experiment account of counterfactuals. On his view, deciding whether a counterfactual is true is operationally the same as running a controlled experiment: you take the actual state of the world just before the antecedent, you intervene so that the antecedent and only the antecedent changes, the way an experimenter sets a single treatment variable, and you let the ordinary causal-explanatory structure run forward to see what follows. He draws a strong moral from this. Because an experiment's outcome is settled by the actual constitution of the system plus the intervention, counterfactual truth must likewise be objective and context-invariant: the facts, the laws, and a minimal change to make the antecedent true fix a unique answer, and talk of comparing similarity among worlds or shifting conversational standards merely reports our uncertainty about what that answer is. I think the analogy is doing the persuasive work, and I suspect its force comes from features of real experiments that counterfactual evaluation does not possess, but I have not yet worked out exactly where it comes apart.

I would like you to audit the analogy. First, provide an explicit correspondence table: what maps onto the experimental apparatus, the baseline conditions, the manipulated variable, the control condition, the recorded outcome, the protocol that says what gets held fixed, and the possible confounders. Second, identify the genuine disanalogies and distinguish the ones that matter for his conclusion from the ones that are harmless. In that connection, test the analogy against cases where making the antecedent true would require undoing earlier events, cases where the antecedent is under-described, and cases where what we are allowed to hold fixed is set by conversational interest rather than by any fact of the matter. Third, judge whether the conclusion, objective context-independent counterfactual truth, survives once the disanalogies are on the table, survives only in a weakened form, or stood only because the analogy had already assumed it. If the analogy cannot carry the load, say what a better analogy or replacement account would be.
