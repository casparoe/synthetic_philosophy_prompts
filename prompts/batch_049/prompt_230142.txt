I am a PhD student in environmental policy, and I am writing a chapter on the precautionary principle. Before I assess any particular formulation, I want to settle the standard a formulation has to meet. The difficulty is that the principle gets defended and attacked at several different levels: some treat it as a substantive decision rule that forbids acting whenever a severe worst-case outcome has not been ruled out; others treat it as a procedural rule about who bears the burden of proof; still others treat it as an expression of epistemic humility with no direct policy content. The result is that the same principle is called both vacuous and paralysing, and I suspect those two criticisms are not directed at the same object. What I need, then, is a requirements list: the desiderata any adequate theory of precaution would have to satisfy, independently of this or that treaty wording.

Assume the purpose is ex ante public decision-making about novel technological and environmental interventions under deep uncertainty, where probabilities may be unavailable rather than merely unknown to the decision-maker. For each requirement, give the motivating case or consideration and mark it as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp. I would especially like your treatment of three points: (a) how the theory should distinguish uncertainty from risk without permitting any unspecified possibility of catastrophe to act as a decisive veto; (b) what I have been calling the precautionary regress — since precautionary measures themselves can carry serious or irreversible downside risks, the principle appears to apply to its own implementation, so the list needs a principled stopping condition rather than an arbitrary line; and (c) whether shifting the burden of proof onto the proponents of a technology is a core requirement or a negotiable institutional choice. To finish, briefly indicate how the best-known formulations, such as the Rio and Wingspread ones, fare against the list, and tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude popular formulations. Please keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scorecard, and write the answer in British English.
