I've been following the Dennett–Caruso back-and-forth about free will and just deserts, and its starting to feel like the whole thing moves in a circle. Dennett keeps saying that compatibilist free will is all the free will worth having, that blame and punishment don't require anything spooky, and that there's an everyday notion of deserving praise and punishment that survives determinism. Caruso keeps saying that ordinary people mean something stronger, that basic desert moral responsibility cannot survive determinism, and that we should scrap retributive punishment entirely and replace it with a quarantine / public-health model. Each side just reasserts its starting point, and half the time I can't tell weather the two of them are even talking about the same thing. My own half-baked hypothesis is that the impasse isn't really about the facts of determinism or the details of the criminal justice system; it's about where the burden of proof sits once a practice turns out to rest on an unprovable metaphysical picture. But I'm not confident.

What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it verbal, substantive, normative, or are Dennett and Caruso answering different questions under the same heading? Is there a hidden empirical or formal question doing the work? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a distinction, an experiment, a formal result, a change in how the question is framed — or should we stop expecting progress?
