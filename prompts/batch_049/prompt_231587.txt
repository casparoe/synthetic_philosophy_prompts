I have been reading Montaigne's essay "Of Cannibals" on my own, and a particular passage has come to dominate my thinking about cultural difference. Montaigne opens by discussing the Roman army seen through the eyes of King Pyrrhus, and he writes: "When King Pyrrhus invaded Italy, having viewed and considered the order of the army the Romans sent out to meet him; 'I know not,' said he, 'what kind of barbarians' (for so the Greeks called all other nations) 'these may be; but the disposition of this army that I see has nothing of barbarism in it.'" Montaigne then draws his lesson from this and kindred cases: "By which it appears how cautious men ought to be of taking things upon trust from vulgar opinion, and that we are to judge by the eye of reason, and not from common report." I have found myself increasingly persuaded by what I take to be the position behind this passage: that the word "barbarous" is usually no more than a label for whatever is foreign, and that the only defensible way to evaluate a practice is by its actual merits rather than by its distance from one's own customs.

I would like to know what would count against this position. I am not looking for a survey of scholarship or a list of texts; I want a map of the position's exposure. Which observations, arguments, intuitions, or concrete cases would genuinely put pressure on the view that "barbarous" is just a name for the unfamiliar, and that we should judge by reason rather than by common report? I would like you to distinguish between defeaters that would be decisive and ones that would merely be awkward, and to say what a committed holder of the Montaigne-style view would themselves concede as counting against it rather than only what a critic would say. In particular, I would be interested to know whether the position, as I have stated it, has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and, if it has, whether that is a problem for a view of this kind. For my part, I would find it most persuasive if you could show a case where two practices genuinely differ and yet one is plainly better by standards that are not themselves merely another custom. Please write your answer in British English.
