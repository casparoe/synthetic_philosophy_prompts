I've been trying to pin down my own views on how pains and pleasures compare, and I've collected a bunch of verdicts about concrete cases, but I can't figure out whether any single principle gets all of them. Help me out.

Here's what I actually find myself thinking:

(1) I'd cause one person a mild headache—maybe 2/10 for an hour—to prevent someone else having a 5/10 headache for a whole day. That's just triage, no hesitation.

(2) I'd also cause twenty people a 2/10 headache for an hour to prevent one person having a 5/10 pain for a day. So below some level I really do aggregate: enough small pains outweigh a medium one.

(3) But I wouldn't cause one person a mild headache for ten minutes to give a hundred other people the best day of their lives. Pleasure just doesn't trade against pain for me. Even enormous pleasure doesn't seem like the right kind of thing to put on the other side of the scale from a small pain.

(4) I also wouldn't cause one person genuine agony—say 9/10, the kind where they can't think straight—for even one minute, no matter how many people's mild 1/10 aches it prevented. A billion, a trillion, it doesn't matter. Once pain gets severe enough, it stops being a numbers game.

(5) But inside that severe range, I still compare and trade. I'd raise one person from 8/10 to 9/10 for an hour if it lowered two other people from 9/10 to 8/10 for an hour. That's treating even awful pains as aggregable, which feels like it fights with how strongly I feel (4).

(6) And if there's a line between "normal, aggregable pain" and "different-league pain," where is it? Around 7? Around 8? A sharp cutoff at 7.5 seems arbitrary, but having no cutoff seems to lose verdict (4). That's the bit I keep going in circles on.

Is there one principle that captures all or most of these, stated precisely? What does it predict about cases I haven't listed—like causing one person a 4/10 pain for an hour to give someone else the most intense pleasure imaginable, or causing one person a 9/10 pain for an hour to prevent ten other people's 9/10 pain for an hour? And which of my verdicts would I have to give up or soften to make the best version of the principle work? The one I can't settle myself is whether I'd allow inflicting a 7/10 pain on one person to prevent a 9/10 pain in someone else; I want to say yes, but I can't tell if that's consistent with the rest.
