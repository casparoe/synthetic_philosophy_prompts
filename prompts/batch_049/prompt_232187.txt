For my upper-level undergrad seminar on deontology I want to run a sorting exercise before I reveal any of the labels, and I'd like you to design it. Make me a decision tree that takes a student from their gut reactions to a few concrete cases to whichever way of handling the paradox of deontology they actually hold. Keep it to around six questions max, and five leaves: strict Kantian absolutism about constraints, Nozick-style agent-relative side-constraints, threshold deontology, Rossian pro tanto pluralism, and act-consequentialism (which just dissolves the paradox). Use cases like the transplant case (kill one healthy person to use their organs to save five patients who would otherwise die), the preventing-murder case (kill one innocent bystander to stop someone else from murdering five other people), and a catastrophe case (kill one innocent person to prevent a nuclear strike that kills millions). Phrase every node as a question a student could answer without knowing which position it leads to, and build the tree from the logical space first: lay out the branching questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then locate the named positions in that space, and tell me which combinations are empty and whether each is empty because it's incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also be honest about which leaves the cases can't seperate — I strongly suspect an agency-based side-constraint view and strict absolutism come out identical on all three of those cases, and with the right threshold Rossian pluralism and threshold deontology can be made to look the same too, so flag those rather than pretend the tree has more diagnostic power than it does.
