I am a high school student preparing a short talk for a philosophy-of-physics reading group, and I want to close with a sestina that enacts the hole argument instead of merely describing it. Please make the six end-words hole, points, shift, same, fixed, and drag. Across the six stanzas the same words should keep returning in new positions and new grammatical roles, the way a diffeomorphism drags the metric field around inside the hole while leaving everything outside it fixed; the envoi should gather all six and land on the real lesson, that the original and the dragged model agree on every observable quantity and every invariant, yet differ if the bare points of spacetime are treated as real, so either determinism or substantivalism must give way. It should be accurate enough that someone who knows the argument recognizes the vanishing stress-energy inside the hole, the boundary values held fixed, and the dragged solution that is identical as geometry but disagrees point by point, while still being a genuine poem for group members who have never heard of diffeomorphisms.
