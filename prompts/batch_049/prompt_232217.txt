I'm trying to work out what I actually think about when consent to sex is valid, going by my gut on cases. Here are my verdicts:

- A man tells a woman he has had a vasectomy; she agrees to have sex with him on that basis; in fact he has not. Verdict: her consent is not valid; what happened was not just sex plus a separate lie.
- A man tells a woman he loves her and sees a future with her in order to get her to sleep with him; neither is true. Verdict: manipulative and wrong, but her consent to the sex is valid.
- A man agrees to use a condom but removes it during intercourse without her knowledge. Verdict: her consent does not cover the sex that actually occurred.
- A woman tells a man she is on the pill when she is not, and he consents on that basis. Verdict: the same as the vasectomy case; his consent is not valid.
- A man tells a woman he is a successful gallery owner when he is actually unemployed and living with his parents. Verdict: contemptible, but the sex is consensual.
- A boss tells an employee she will be fired unless she sleeps with him. Verdict: the sex is not consensual.
- A long-term partner says he will leave the relationship unless they have sex. Verdict: the consent is valid; the relationship is bad, but the sex is consensual.
- A sober person has sex with someone who is so drunk they can barely stand and are slurring their words, though the drunk person says yes and initiates. Verdict: the consent is not valid.

Is there one principle, or a small number of principles, about deception, coercion, and competence that captures these verdicts? The two places where my own verdicts feel hard to reconcile are the vasectomy lie versus the love lie, and the boss's threat versus the partner's ultimatum. If some verdicts have to go, which should I revise? And what does the resulting account say about the case I keep going back and forth on: a man tells his partner they are exclusive, she says she would not have sex with him if he were seeing anyone else, they always use condoms and he has been tested, but in fact he is sleeping with someone else. Is her consent to the sex valid?
