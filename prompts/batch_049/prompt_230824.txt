I am drafting a novel in which a novelist's most famous passage is read by critics against his own intention, and I want a philosophically defensible miniature of that phenomenon to use as the basis for a scene in which the novelist and a critic argue about what the passage actually means. The case would be built around two rival views of what fixes the meaning of a work of fiction. The private-intention view holds that a passage's meaning is fixed by the content of the intention the author actually had in composing it: if she meant it sincerely and literally, then it is sincere and literal. The public-attribution view holds that a passage's meaning is fixed by the intention that a competent reader, restricted to public evidence—the wording, the rest of the work, the venue of publication, genre, and known circumstances of composition—would be most warranted in attributing to the author, regardless of what she privately thought. Construct a thought experiment in which these two views come apart in the following direction: the author's private intention is a sincere, literal one, yet the public features of the work make the public-attribution view deliver a determinate ironic or parodic reading instead. Please invent the passage yourself rather than drawing on an actual quotation, keep it under one hundred and fifty words of ordinary contemporary prose, and furnish it with a concrete publication context. The divergence must be generated by the work's public features—placement, structural ironies, generic signals, the run of the surrounding chapters—not by a secret code, a private diary, a withheld preface, or a later false statement by the author; and the author's psychology should be plausible and determinate, with no confusion, self-deception, or unacknowledged irony.

After the scenario, demonstrate that it meets each specification. State the author's actual intention, state the public features that license the opposed attribution, and show the reasoning by which each view reaches its verdict. Then isolate the single structural feature doing the real work, and say what would happen if it were removed: do the two views converge, and if so, on what? Close by imagining how a defender of the private-intention view would attack the case, and say whether the phenomenon you have designed survives that attack.
