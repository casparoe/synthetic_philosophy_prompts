I am a working physicist with no formal training in philosophy. The vocabulary I trust is built from initial conditions, dynamical laws, symmetries, conservation laws, the vacuum, and the arrow of time; locutions like necessary being feel to me like category mistakes, and I want to know whether that reaction is fair or merely an artifact of my toolkit.

Restate the kalam cosmological argument and the contingency version of the cosmological argument — the one that moves from the existence of contingent things to a necessary being — in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, premise by premise. In the translation, say what happens to the arguments' central moves: for instance, whether begins to exist comes out as has a finite past or as something stronger, and whether cause comes out as a dynamical law, an initial condition, or an explanation. Give special attention to what becomes of the kalam argument's rejection of an actual infinite in a vocabulary that treats infinities as limits or idealizations.

Then give me the residue report: which premises translate cleanly, which translate only if you distort them, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Track in particular the premise that anything that comes into existence has a cause, the notion of a necessary being, and the contrast between necessary and contingent existence.

Conclude by saying whether the pieces that resist translation are the very pieces on which the arguments turn, and whether that means my vocabulary is too impoverished to state the arguments at all, or the arguments depend on commitments physics has no resources to express.
