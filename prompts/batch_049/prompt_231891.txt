You are a staff analyst for the investment committee of a university endowment. The committee currently ranks candidate investment strategies by expected monetary value. One member has proposed replacing this with a risk-weighted expected-value rule: outcomes are ordered from worst to best, and each step of improvement is weighted by r(p), the risk-transformed probability of achieving at least that improved outcome, where r is strictly increasing, r(0)=0, r(1)=1, and r(p) is strictly below p for every probability strictly between 0 and 1. Because r discounts the better outcomes, unlikely large losses weigh more heavily and unlikely large gains weigh less heavily than under the status quo. The proposal grew out of a dispute over whether the endowment should hold tail-risk-heavy strategies. The chair has asked you to draft a decision memo recommending whether to adopt the risk-weighted rule, reject it in favor of the status quo, or adopt it only for a specified class of decisions. The memo should state a clear bottom line and engage the strongest consideration on the opposite side.
