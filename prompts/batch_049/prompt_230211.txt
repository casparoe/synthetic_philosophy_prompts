ok so ive spent the last term going back and forth on reduction and emergence, and i genuinely can't see the fault line in what i've ended up with, so i'd like an audit rather than a label. here's where i've landed:

(1) i'm a physicalist. everything that exists is the kind of thing physics is about, arranged somehow; no souls, no vital spark, no extra ingredients.
(2) some higher-level properties just are lower-level ones. temperature in a gas is nothing over and above the mean kinetic energy of its molecules; it's not an extra thing riding on top of the molecules.
(3) but other higher-level properties resist that. being in pain, or being thirsty, seems different: a creature with a completely different nervous system — or a well-built machine, if it had the right sort of states — could be in the very same mental state without sharing the underlying physical state. so i don't think you can identify the mental state with any one physical set-up the way you can identify temperature with mean kinetic energy.
(4) and i'm not an epiphenomenalist. my thirst is the reason i reached for the glass, my pain is why i pulled my hand back. the mental state does real causal work; it isn't just a shadow of the physics.
(5) but i also think physics never needs outside help: for any physical event, the physical story on its own tells you what happens next; the particles aren't waiting around for a soul to nudge them.
(6) and so i don't think strong emergence is on the table either — no new fundamental forces or suspensions of the physics switching on once a brain shows up.

so, audit me. which of these can't stand together, which pairs only seem to be in tension (and why the tension is unreal), and what's the cheapest change that would restore consistency — and what would it cost? i'd much rather you pointed at the one that has to go than christened the whole thing.
