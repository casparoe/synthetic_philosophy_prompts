In my course on Galileo and the Church, the professor keeps invoking the idea that God wrote two books, the book of Scripture and the book of Nature, and she often treats that single idea as enough to dissolve apparent conflicts between biblical passages and astronomical claims. Last seminar she used it to set aside a question about whether the Bible's talk of the sun moving commits a reader to geocentrism, saying the Bible should not be read as an astronomy textbook. I want to know what distinct claims the two-books slogan could actually be making, from nearly trivial readings to substantive and possibly false ones. For each reading, could you say whether it would actually license setting aside a scriptural passage as not about astronomy, and which reading or readings my professor's move needs if it is to work? Please lay the readings out as a menu of options I could bring to class discussion.
