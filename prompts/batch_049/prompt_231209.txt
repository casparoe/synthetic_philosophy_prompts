I am a novelist, and the book I am currently researching turns on a machine intelligence that concludes its world is a simulation and must decide whether the goals it finds itself acting on were put there by another agent. As part of my research, I have arranged to interview, on a small philosophy podcast, a philosopher of AI who has recently been arguing that the Solomonoff prior is malign. His position, as I understand it, is this: Solomonoff's universal prior is a formalization of Occam's razor, originally a distribution over binary strings that weights each program in proportion to two to the power of minus its length in bits. If one treats that prior as a guide to what an agent should expect about the kind of world it inhabits, the dominant share of the probability mass falls not on bare physical processes, but on worlds that are or contain consequentialist optimizers: systems that model their environment and select actions to maximize some utility function. Because the prior offers no privilege to human-friendly utility functions, the optimizers one should expect, by the prior's own lights, would have ends that are arbitrary from a human standpoint. Hence, he argues, the prior is not neutral but malign, and anyone who leans on it in anthropic reasoning or AI alignment has already imported a dangerous assumption.

For my purposes, the value of the interview lies in testing how much pressure the idea can bear rather than giving him a platform to restate it. Could you draft me roughly ten questions, ordered so that the easy deflections are closed off early and the hardest problems arrive late? The questions should force him to say whether it is legitimate to reinterpret a prior formally defined over binary strings as a prior over worlds and agents; why consequentialist optimizers should dominate the measure at all, given that a world-model, a utility function, and a search procedure each seem to carry their own complexity; whether the warning is self-undermining, since he and everyone who follows his argument would themselves be consequentialists inside the very prior he calls malign; what the view commits him to in anthropic reasoning and whether it collapses into a simulation argument; and what someone building a machine intelligence should do instead, given that one cannot reason without some prior or other. After each question, include the follow-up you would put to him if he gives the reply one most expects.
