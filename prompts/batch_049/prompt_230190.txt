I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I would like to think through a counterfactual about Isaac Newton. My understanding is that Newton privately held strong theological views: he was anti-Trinitarian, he believed God was omnipresent and that God's existence constituted space and duration, and he believed the solar system needed occasional divine correction to remain stable—a view Leibniz criticized as making God a second-rate clockmaker who has to wind and repair his own machine. Separately, I have been reading popular accounts of fine-tuning: if the cosmological constants, the initial entropy, or the strength of gravity had been slightly different, life could not have existed. Imagine Newton were shown this modern fine-tuning evidence. Would he have taken it as vindication of design, or would he have been uneasy because a God who only sets initial constants and lets laws run looks like the clockmaker he rejected? I do not just want a yes-or-no prediction. I want you to work through the tensions between continuous divine governance and one-time calibration, between design and blind necessity, and between his emphasis on God's sovereign will and a God constrained by fixed mathematical constants. Lay out the conflicting pressures that his own commitments would create.
