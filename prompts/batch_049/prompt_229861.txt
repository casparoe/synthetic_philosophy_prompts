Many discussions of transformative AI treat “fast takeoff” and “intelligence explosion” as near synonyms, and treat “slow takeoff” as the absence of an explosion. I want you to argue that this is a mistake.

Specifically, defend the following two-part thesis. First, a takeoff can be slow in clock time while still being an explosion in the causal sense that matters: once AI systems can contribute meaningfully to AI research and hardware design, each generation can improve the capacities of the next, and the process can sustain itself through positive feedback even if each cycle takes years. Second, the reverse also holds: a transition can be very fast in clock time without being an explosion in the feedback sense, because a rapid capability jump might be driven by a single external breakthrough or by resources scaled up by human institutions, without the new system’s being able to steer its own further improvement.

From this, argue that the question “Will takeoff be fast or slow?” should be decomposed into at least two distinct questions: how steep is the capability curve in clock time, and to what extent is the transition sustained by feedback loops internal to AI R&D? Take a clear position on which of these questions matters more if our central concern is human control of the transition, and defend that position. Anticipate and answer the strongest objection from someone who insists that clock-time speed is the only thing that matters because human institutions and coordination mechanisms must respond in real time.
