I write for a science and ideas magazine, and we're putting together a themed issue on the self. I keep coming back to Dennetts old thought experiment where his brain is removed, kept alive in a vat, and radio-linked to his body as it walks around, and the question becomes where he is now: in the vat, in the body, or in the link. I'd like you to write a short story for the issue that takes that puzzle seriously.

Here's the seed I have in mind. A patient signs up for a trial because a degenerative condition has wrecked the connection between her brain and her own spinal cord; the surgeons take her brain out and keep it in a perfusion tank in Cambridge while her body goes back to her flat in Manchester, run through a low-latency link instead of nerves. One evening the link degrades, and her body does something she didn't decide to do, or at least can't remember deciding to do, and the question of where she actually lives stops being theoretical. As you write it, commit to an answer in the world of the story: say whether she is the tank, the body, the link, or something distributed across them, and let the plot show it rather than having a character lecture about it. Then add a short afterword of a few sentences saying which part of that is your genuine philosophical view and where the story takes liberties or leans on a contested reading of the puzzle.
