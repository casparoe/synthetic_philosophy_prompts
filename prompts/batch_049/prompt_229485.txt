Not a philosophy person, but I've been losing sleep over this and I want to get the shape of the problem right before I read more. I came across a paper by Andreas Matthias called something like The Responsibility Gap: Ascribing Responsibility for the Actions of Learning Automata. His point, as I understood it, is that for ordinary machines the humans in the loop can be picked out as responsible because the designers and operators had enough control and foresight. But once a system is built to learn and modify its own behavior after deployment, the designer genuinely cannot predict what it will do, so the usual conditions for responsibility stop attaching cleanly to any individual human. He thinks this opens up a gap where nobody satisfies the standard conditions, and I think the paper is basically about just one version of that gap.

I want to go up a level and ask what a good theory of responsibility in these cases would have to get right, not just what one narrow fix would look like. So imagine I'm asking for the desiderata a theory of responsibility for autonomous or learning systems must meet. What are the cases it has to handle, the distinctions it can't collapse, the results from psychology or engineering it can't contradict, and the costs it has to avoid, with the reason each of those is a requirement and not just something a particular camp happens to like? I would love it if you could also say which of these requirements are negotiable, which are non-negotiable, and which single requirement would knock out the biggest chunk of the approaches people usually propose. A table is fine.
