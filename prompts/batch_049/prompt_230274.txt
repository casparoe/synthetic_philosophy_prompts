Draft a 650-word deadpan judicial sentencing memorandum, in the voice of a judge who accepts Galen Strawson's Basic Argument as settled and who therefore holds that no offender is ever ultimately morally responsible for what he does. The memorandum should remain a credible piece of legal writing: it should state the facts of a serious but otherwise unspecified offense, invoke the applicable standard of proof, impose a specific penalty, and address the defendant directly. It should open by summarizing the Basic Argument without jargon or citation, roughly as follows: to be truly responsible for an action one would have to be responsible for the character, motives, and dispositions that produced it; that would require being responsible for the heredity, upbringing, and circumstances that formed those traits; and that would in turn require being responsible for the conditions that formed them, and so on backward without ever reaching a first self-caused starting point. The judge should treat this conclusion as beyond reasonable dispute, explicitly call retributive desert an illusion, and yet impose punishment on other grounds: incapacitation, specific and general deterrence, victim restitution, and the public expression of serious norms. The comedy should come from the sustained collision between the court's inherited language of guilt and blame and the judge's open insistence that ultimate culpability is a fiction. Do not let the judge slip into compatibilism about desert; keep the position stable throughout, so that a reader finishes genuinely uncertain whether the sentencing ritual has been exposed or quietly rebuilt on new foundations.
