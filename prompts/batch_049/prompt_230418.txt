I have been thinking, on my own and without much background in academic philosophy, about what exactly would be bad about the permanent extinction of the human species. Over time I have settled into a list of convictions, and I would like someone to check whether they really form one position or a collection that secretly pulls against itself. I do not want a defense of any of them; I want an audit.

The convictions are these:

1. If the human species went permanently extinct shortly after everyone now alive had lived a full natural life, painlessly and without anyone knowing what was coming, that would still be a catastrophe of the first order. It would not be made all right by the fact that no one was killed early, suffered, or feared death.

2. The badness of that scenario is not fully accounted for by listing the individual lives lost. Something further is lost, bound up with the fact that the human story simply ends.

3. Consider a team that discovers today a safe, cheap, definitive cure for a fatal illness. If I also learn that humanity will end with the current generation, I find myself judging that the discovery is less valuable than the very same discovery under an ordinary future, even though the living patients saved are exactly the same in the two cases.

4. I also believe that no one is wronged by never being born, and that we have no obligation to bring future people into existence. A merely possible person has no claim on me.

5. I also believe that value must be anchored in actual persons — in experiences, desires, and interests that actually occur. I cannot see how the worth of what we do now could be fixed by people who do not exist and may never exist.

6. And yet I think the deepest part of why extinction would be so bad is that many serious human activities — medical research, teaching, writing, preserving things against decay, the kind of work people build their identities around — seem to get their point partly from a human future that will outlive us.

7. That said, I do not think everything would collapse. If I learned the species would end with my generation, I expect the quiet value of an afternoon with a friend, of music, of a good meal, of solving a small problem for its own sake would remain fully in place.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs or larger clusters are in genuine tension and which only look as though they conflict. Rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would be, and tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set hang together. I would rather have the real conflicts laid out plainly than be told the set is fine or hopeless.
