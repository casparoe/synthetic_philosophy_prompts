Last night I had a long argument with a friend about dreaming, and I am still unsettled by it. My friend’s summary of the debate went roughly as follows: Malcolm showed that dreams cannot be experiences, because while one is dreaming one is asleep, and being asleep is incompatible with having conscious experiences. Therefore, dream reports are not reports of anything that happened during sleep; they are waking constructions, or at most stories we are disposed to tell upon waking. Dennett then went further, my friend said, and showed that dreams are simply retrospective confabulations: the brain, on waking, constructs a narrative and projects it backward onto the sleep period as if it were a memory of an experience.

I pushed back, but I do not know the material well enough to say exactly where this account goes wrong or whether it is fair. I would like an accuracy audit of my friend’s characterization. Which parts would Malcolm himself accept, which would he qualify or reject, and which parts run Malcolm’s view together with Dennett’s? More specifically, does it follow from the accurate parts that dream reports carry no evidential weight about anything that occurred during sleep, or does that conclusion depend on the questionable parts? Please separate what is settled in the philosophical literature on dreaming from what remains genuinely contested among people who work on this topic, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
