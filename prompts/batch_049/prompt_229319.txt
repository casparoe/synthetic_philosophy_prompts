I’m a PhD student in political philosophy, working on secession and self-determination, and I keep going back and forth between the remedial view of the right to secede (unilateral exit only as a remedy for serious injustice) and the plebiscitary/primary-right view (a nationally-defined group’s majority vote is enough). Somebody brings up Scotland or Catalonia or Quebec and I realize I don’t actually know what I’d want a full theory of the right to secede to do before it starts adjudicating those cases. So treat me like a colleague. You’re an expert on the ethics of self-determination and territorial rights. Think carefully, and before you commit to a list, brainstorm candidate requirements first and then prune the ones that are really just parochial.

What I want is the specification: a list of requirements a theory of the right to secede has to satisfy to count as adequate. Not a bibliography, not a reading list, and don’t skip ahead to defending one theory—I want the criteria themselves. The theory should be good enough to evaluate the standard three (Scotland, Catalonia, Quebec), but it should also be able to handle Kosovo, Kurdistan, Bangladesh, and weird recursive cases like the Cree in northern Quebec if Quebec left. For each requirement, give the consideration or case that puts it on the list, then say whether it’s non-negotiable or just a preference of one camp.

Some distinctions I’m pretty sure the theory can’t collapse, but I want you to check me: unilateral secession vs negotiated divorce; the group’s right vs whether secession is wise or justified all-things-considered; who counts as “the people” (and the circularity of defining the unit by the outcome); what to do about minorities inside the seceding territory; and how far back a grievance can reach before it stops counting. 

I’m genuinely unsure about some candidate requirements, so tell me wich of these belong on the list and which are bogus: economic viability of the new state; respect for the parent state’s constitution (Scotland’s referendum was legal, Catalonia’s wasn’t); a supermajority rather than 50%+1; a distinct language, history, or institutions; and whether outsiders are permitted—or bound—to recognize the new state. 

The part I most need: if some subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable—for example, respecting the parent state’s constitution and recognizing a remedial right when the parent state is the abuser—say so, and explain what we should conclude from that. Then tell me which single requirement does the most work in knocking out candidate theories, and why. A table is fine if it’s easier to read, but don’t let the table hide the reasoning.
