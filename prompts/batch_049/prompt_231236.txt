Since I joined the board of a local back-to-work charity, I have noticed that our disagreements keep collapsing into one phrase: "the undeserving poor." It is said as if it named a stable group of people, and it is then used to justify quite specific things — sanctioning some claimants, exempting others, attaching work conditions to support — but I cannot tell whether it is doing the work of a factual description of why some people are poor, a moral judgment about what they owe in return for help, or an administrative filter for catching fraud and shirking. I would like you to take that phrase, together with the associated demand that we separate the deserving from the undeserving, and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making. For each, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or a false or confused one; say which reading the case for workfare-style conditionality actually needs if its conclusions are to follow; and say which reading is probably doing the real work when the phrase is used to end an argument about tightening benefit conditions. Please write in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as though you were writing a letter.
