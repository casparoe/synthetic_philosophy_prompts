I run an undergraduate philosophy club that meets weekly, and in two weeks I'm leading a session on aesthetic testimony. The students have already taken epistemology, so they comfortably accept that testimony transmits knowledge in ordinary cases — they'll grant that you can know the restaurant is open because someone told you it is. But they have had no aesthetics, and I expect them to arrive with two intuitions that pull in opposite directions: first, that of course you shouldn't just defer to someone else's verdict on a painting, and second, that aesthetic judgments are just subjective taste anyway, so there's nothing interesting to explain. I want the session to land somewhere more precise: roughly, the acquaintance principle — the thought that first-hand experience plays a distinctive role in forming aesthetic judgments — together with the shape of the debate between pessimists and optimists about whether aesthetic testimony can ever transmit aesthetic knowledge.

Plan how I should teach this session, not the explanation itself but the teaching plan. I need the order in which the ideas must be introduced and what depends on what, given that the students' prior epistemology actually makes the problem harder in an interesting way (they think testimony is generally easy). Identify the one example or case that should carry the whole session, and say what it has to have and what it must avoid. Lay out the misconceptions the students will bring or form — I can already hear someone saying "but Rotten Tomatoes told me the film was dull" and someone else concluding "so all aesthetic judgment is just private feeling" — and specify exactly where in the sequence each should be headed off, rather than left to fester and then corrected at the end. Give me one final question or short exercise I can use to check whether they have genuinely grasped the difference between aesthetic judgment depending on first-hand experience and aesthetic judgment being merely subjective. Also say what I should openly leave out at this level; I do not want the full literature or a survey of positions. Keep the plan to no more than 800 words.
