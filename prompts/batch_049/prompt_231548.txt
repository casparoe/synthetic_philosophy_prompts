I'm running a two-hour session for my local LessWrong meetup — about 15 people, mostly programmers and a couple grad students, some of whom have done the sequences but none with linguistics — and I want an activity, not a lecture. The idea I want them to feel in their guts is that the nasty part of a slur isn't part of what's being asserted: it survives negation, embedded questions, and conditionals the way a presupposition or expressive does, while the descriptive part behaves normally. So "Dave is not a [slur]" still slimes Dave even though the sentence denies it, and asking "Is Dave a [slur]?" has already done the thing. I want the room to notice this pattern themselves, using a made-up group and a made-up slur so nobody has to produce a real one. Can you design the activity — roles, rules, materials, timeline — plus a debrief that maps what they noticed onto the at-issue/not-at-issue distinction and says why it's awkward for ordinary truth-conditional semantics? Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to steer around it, and which part of the phenomenon the activity can't reproduce (my guess is in-group vs out-group use changing the force, but you might have a better candidate).
