I teach a graduate seminar in formal epistemology, and I've basically threw in with the accuracy-first program: the view that the one fundamental epistemic virtue of a credence function is accuracy, understood as graded closeness to the truth, and that the standard Bayesian norms fall out of that. In particular, I defend the package according to which (i) incoherent credences are irrational because they're accuracy-dominated, and (ii) conditionalization is the uniquely rational update rule because it maximizes expected accuracy while every rival rule is expected-accuracy dominated, once you help yourself to a strictly proper scoring rule. I teach it this way and mostly believe it.

But I've realized I can't crisply say what would count against the view, and that bothers me. So take that position as your target. What would have to be true for the accuracy-first story about probabilism and conditionalization to be wrong, or in serious trouble? I want a map of the defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward — and for each, I want to know whether a committed accuracy-firster would concede it as a real cost or dismiss it as a misunderstanding. Where has the program been patched with qualifications, and has it been patched so much that it no longer risks anything? If so, say whether that's actually a problem for a view of this kind.

Things I want you to weigh in on: whether accuracy is well-defined independently of the choice of inaccuracy measure, so that the dominance arguments aren't an artifact of the measure; whether there are rationally permissible credal states the framework can't represent, like imprecise credences or dilation phenomena; whether the expected-accuracy argument for conditionalization builds in that the agent is certain she'll conditionalize, or assumes away memory loss and self-locating belief, so it's circular or only covers a special case; and whether the whole program is a form of epistemic consequentialism and inherits whatever's wrong with that. I'm personally most moved by a less technical objection: that accuracy is just one epistemic value among several — coherence, explanatory fit, something like understanding — and the formalism has gotten very good at not seeing the others. Tell me how much weight that worry should carry next to the technical ones.
