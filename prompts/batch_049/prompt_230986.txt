You are an expert metaphysician and philosopher of mathematics who works on the foundations of continuity and Zeno's paradoxes, and who has experience running adversarial collaborations between philosophers with very different temperaments. Take your time. Before drafting the document, brainstorm the strongest formulations of both positions and the exact points at which they diverge; then write the polished document described below.

Background. Two researchers have argued for months about Zeno's Dichotomy and Achilles-and-the-tortoise. They agree about the standard mathematics; they disagree about what the mathematics shows.

Camp A — the dissolutionists — holds that both paradoxes are fully dissolved by the mathematics of limits and measure. On their view, the sub-journeys are not real, sequential tasks a runner performs one by one; they are potential divisions imposed on a continuous path by a description. The convergence of the series 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... to 1 is the entire explanation needed, and there is no residual metaphysical obstacle to motion.

Camp B — the completion skeptics — holds that convergence is a fact about the sequence of partial sums, not about completing a process with infinitely many successive stages. If the sub-journeys are genuine stages, then the runner must complete the first, then the second, then the third, and so on, in order; the limit says where the partial sums are heading, but it does not explain how an actual process with no final stage gets finished in finite time. Camp B therefore holds that either the stage-by-stage description of motion is untenable — forcing us to abandon the picture of the continuum as made of an actual infinity of successive parts — or the standard account has changed the subject.

Both camps also suspect that part of the dispute may be verbal: a difference between modeling a motion as a whole path and modeling it as an ordered sequence of subtasks. They would regard a precise verdict on that suspicion as a genuine result.

Draft the joint statement such a collaboration would produce. The document must have three parts.

Part 1: a numbered list of claims both camps can sign. These should be non-vacuous. At minimum, include precise reconstructions of the Dichotomy and of Achilles; the relevant facts about convergent series and sums of measures; the claim that the Dichotomy, on the standard formalization, involves a countable infinity of stages and that the separate question of a path's containing every real-numbered intermediate point is not what either side is contesting; what the usual calculus-based response gets right; what it leaves unaddressed; and a shared diagnosis of why the familiar slogan that calculus solves Zeno's paradoxes is misleading.

Part 2: a disagreements table. For each residual disagreement, give: (a) a neutral statement of the issue; (b) the formulation each camp accepts for its own side, in wording that camp would endorse; (c) the crux, i.e., the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one camp; (d) a concrete case, argument, or test both camps agree bears on that crux; and (e) each camp's prediction about it, recorded in advance. The table must include at least these three cruxes:

- the real-stages vs. ideal-divisions crux: are the Dichotomy's sub-journeys genuine stages of the motion, or merely divisions imposed by the description?
- the convergence vs. completion crux: does convergence of the series show that an actual infinite sequence of successive acts can be completed in finite time?
- the last-stage crux: does a completed sequence of acts require a last act, and what follows from the fact that the Dichotomy's sequence of stages has none?

For the test cases, consider at minimum: (i) a staccato runner who reaches the endpoint in one jump or in finitely many discrete jumps, thereby skipping intermediate positions, versus a runner who moves smoothly through every intermediate point; and (ii) an idealized lamp that starts off and is toggled at each successive Dichotomy moment (1/2, then 3/4, then 7/8, and so on), toggled infinitely many times by time 1 and with no defined final state at time 1. For each case, state exactly how it bears on its crux and what each camp predicts.

Part 3: a short concluding assessment: which disagreements, if any, are merely verbal; which are substantive; and which substantive disagreement is the most promising to settle first.

Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations, and keep historical exposition to the minimum needed to make the positions precise. Treat both camps at full strength, never as caricatures. The result should read as a document both camps could actually sign, with the residual disagreement made exact.
