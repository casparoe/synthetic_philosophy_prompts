My opening move: The history-of-science analogies you are leaning on—phlogiston, caloric, the four humors—were narrow, specialized theories that could be replaced wholesale. Folk psychology, by contrast, is an extremely broad explanatory practice that has continued to absorb new developments: it now comfortably includes implicit attitudes, intrusive thoughts, metacognition, and planning under uncertainty. If the framework is supple enough to extend itself in these ways without cracking, why should we think it is a stagnant theory headed for elimination rather than an indispensable and resilient way of tracking the causes of behavior?
