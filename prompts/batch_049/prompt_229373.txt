I sit on my university's biosafety committee. A lab is proposing to build a 'protocell': a lipid vesicle with an RNA system that replicates itself using an internal metabolic cycle, enough to keep the vesicle stable and copy the RNA. The committee has to decide whether this counts as a living organism for biosafety purposes. If it's alive, the lab needs full biocontainment review and an IBC protocol; if it's just chemistry, it stays under the lab's normal safety rules. The PI argues it's not life because there's no DNA, no proteins, no cell membrane made by the cell's own machinery — just a bag of reacting chemicals. I'm suppose to circulate a short written opinion before the meeting. Please draft the memo: it should recommend a concrete classification and take the strongest argument on the opposing side seriously, not just recite textbook criteria.
