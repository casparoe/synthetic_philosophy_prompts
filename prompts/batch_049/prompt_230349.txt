Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a foundationalist and an infinitist about the structure of epistemic justification. Treat both as responses to the same regress problem: a belief seems justified only if supported by a further reason, which must in turn be justified, so the chain of reasons must either stop, circle, or extend without end. The foundationalist holds that the chain may stop at beliefs justified non-inferentially; the infinitist holds that no stopping point is non-arbitrary and that justification requires a non-repeating endless chain of reasons. Set coherentism aside. Format the statement as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. The agreed claims should cover what the regress argument shows and what it does not show, and the constraints any adequate account of justification must satisfy. In the disagreements table, state the foundationalist view and the infinitist view in wording each side would accept for its own position. Then identify the cruxes—at least: whether non-inferential justification is possible; whether an infinite chain needs to be actually traversed or only available in principle; whether a foundation can avoid arbitrariness; and whether an endless chain merely relocates rather than answers the demand for reasons. For each crux, specify a concrete case or argument both sides would accept as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction in advance.
