I am giving a paper at a workshop on the Vienna Circle and visual culture. My thesis is that Otto Neurath's ISOTYPE picture language should be read as a substantive contribution to the epistemology of representation, not as a merely pedagogical or design project that happened to be associated with a philosopher. The argument I plan to run, compressed: (1) ISOTYPE's design rules — flat, perspective-free pictograms; the repetition of identical signs rather than enlargement to encode quantity; a limited set of conventionalized glyphs; a fixed grammar for combining them; standardized and restrained color — are best understood as constraints on how a public sign system can transmit statistical and causal content without the distortions introduced by natural-language framing or realistic pictorial style. (2) These constraints answer a recognizably philosophical question: what must a nontechnical medium be like if it is to carry the content of scientific generalizations to audiences without specialist training while remaining revisable. (3) The project is of a piece with Neurath's broader anti-foundationalism — his image of inquiry as sailors who must rebuild their ship on the open sea — since the visual language was designed to be corrected piecemeal without losing contact with the facts it represents. ISOTYPE therefore belongs alongside his work on protocol sentences and unified science as part of a single program in the critique of representation.

The audience is: mostly historians of the Vienna Circle and of interwar social democracy, who know the archive and are inclined to treat ISOTYPE as adult education for workers, a piece of Red Vienna's municipal publicity, and a museum display technique; two philosophers of science who are sympathetic to Neurath's naturalism but doubtful that design rules rise to the level of philosophy; and one philosopher of language who works on diagrams, maps, and nonlinguistic representation, and who will press questions about whether ISOTYPE has anything like a compositional syntax or truth conditions.
