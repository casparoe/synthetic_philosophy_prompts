I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am applying for a twelve-month fellowship to work on a book-length essay about democratic backsliding. Please write the research proposal, roughly twelve hundred to fifteen hundred words, that I will submit with the application. I want the project to be conceptual rather than a country-by-country scorecard. Its animating question is whether democratic backsliding has analytical content independent of the user's political commitments — and, if it does, who, if anyone, is entitled to judge when it applies — or whether the term is closer to a partisan label doing rhetorical work under the guise of measurement. The countries usually named in this debate, Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and more recently the United States, should figure as examples of what is at stake, not as cases to be scored in sequence.

Please state the question so that it has a determinate answer rather than leaving it as a topic. The proposal should explain why existing discussion leaves the conceptual question open even while the standard democracy indices are cited as if they settled it, and it should lay out an approach that could make progress. I have a half-formed idea that may be worth testing: treating backsliding as parasitic on an unstated baseline of full democracy, and then asking what, if anything, could anchor that baseline without importing one side of the political dispute. The proposal should say why this approach is better than the obvious alternatives of either defending the indices as they are or dismissing the concept as mere rhetoric. It should state the possible conclusions and which kinds of consideration would decide between them, specify what would count as failure, and give an honest assessment of whether the inquiry can be completed within a year.

Do not include a bibliography or any citations; you may refer to positions and arguments in your own words, and I will add what is needed myself. Write in continuous prose, as one would write a letter or an email: no headings, bullet points, bold type, or tables.
