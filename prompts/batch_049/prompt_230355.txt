I'm a PhD student in philosophy of biology and I keep running into this debate, but I got a glib summary from a science writer and I want to know how much of it to trust before I build any of my own argument on it. The summary goes roughly like this. On the etiological account, the function of a trait is just the effect for which it was selected—so the heart's function is to pump blood because pumping blood is why hearts spread through the population, full stop. That's also what lets the account say a heart with a blocked valve is defective: it exists to pump blood and isn't doing it. The causal-role account, by contrast, says a function is whatever a part does that helps some larger system do something, relative to whatever system you happen to care about. So the same heart can have pumping as a function and making a noise as a function, a tumor has the function of making its host sick, and a broken heart has the function of doing nothing. The writer concludes that causal-role accounts are therefore hopeless about disease and malfunction. I suspect this is a bit of a hatchet job, but the etiological half sounds right to me and I dont know the causal-role literatue well enough to say where the distortion is. Can you go through it and tell me which claims a careful causal-role theorist would accept, which are overstatements or unfair conflations, and whether the conclusion about malfunction follows?
