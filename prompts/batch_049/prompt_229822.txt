Before I read any more attempts to explain tangled hierarchies and the inviolate level, I want the full spec of what an adequate account has to get right. By a tangled hierarchy I mean a loop where two levels seem to be above one another at once: Escher's Drawing Hands, where each hand draws the other; a compiler written in the language it compiles; an employee handbook whose own amendment procedure is set out inside the handbook. By the inviolate level I mean whatever has to stay outside the loop for the loop to be coherent instead of just collapsing. Write the desiderata: the cases a theory has to handle, the distinctions it can't collapse (self-reference versus vicious circularity, a loop that generates something versus a loop that merely blocks), and the moves it can't get away with, like pretending the loop isn't really there. Give the motivating case behind each requirement, flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, and then be committed: say which requirements are non-negotiable, which are probably just one camp's preference, and which single requirement does the most to knock out most accounts on offer.
