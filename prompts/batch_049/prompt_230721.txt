During an argument with some friends about the long-run future of artificial intelligence and political organization, I found myself defending a view I now want pressure-tested rather than confirmed. The view is roughly this: if Earth-originating intelligence survives and continues to develop, it will eventually come to be organized under a single decision-making agency at the highest level — a singleton — rather than settling into a stable, long-lasting multipolar arrangement of independent, roughly comparable powers. I don't mean that every firm or household would be directed by one central planner, only that something sits at the top of the most consequential decisions: a world government, a dominant AI system, several systems that merge or coordinate so tightly they function as one agent, or some universal enforcement mechanism that makes the relevant units effectively one. My reasons are mostly about escalating coordination problems: technologies that make defection catastrophic, increasing returns to scale in coordination, and the difficulty of sustaining bargains among parties that can improve their position faster than others can react. But I also hold a more structural intuition: once several agents can reason strategically about the others' reasoning, the situation does not stay symmetric for long, and the stable endpoints are hierarchy or fragmentation, not durable balance.

I want to know what would count against this view, laid out as a map of its exposure rather than as a brief for the prosecution. Specifically: what observations from actual history, international relations, decentralized systems, markets, or the evolution of intelligence would a thoughtful defender of the position have to concede are at least awkward, and which would be decisive? What would the world have to look like for the view to be false — for instance, could there be a stable long-run equilibrium of several advanced powers held apart by something other than balance-of-power luck and temporary truce, and what institutional or technical facts would that require? Which escape routes are genuinely live (durable coordination without hierarchy, fragmentation into non-interacting civilizations, persistent veto players blocking consolidation) and which are merely logically possible? I would personally be most moved by a worked example of multipolar stability that is not just a long ceasefire, or an argument that the mechanisms driving hierarchy are parochial rather than general. Finally, I want an honest verdict on whether the position has been quietly qualified into unfalsifiability — whether saying a singleton emerges eventually, at the relevant level, absent catastrophe actually risks anything — and, if it has, whether that is a serious defect in a view of this kind or an acceptable feature of a general claim about the long run.
