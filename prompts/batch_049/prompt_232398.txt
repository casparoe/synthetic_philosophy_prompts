Mill died before paid parental leave was really a thing, at least in anything like its modern form, and he never had to think about the current two-earner family with its childcare logistics. But the modern debate sits right on top of stuff he spent his life on: women's independence, economic dependence inside marriage, state meddling in family life, individual development. Here's what I want you to work through. Modern parental leave systems mostly come in two designs. One is a single family pot of paid weeks that the parents can split between themselves however they like. The other is individual, non-transferable entitlements: each parent gets their own quota, and if you dont take yours it's gone — you can't hand it over to your partner. The individual-quota side argues that the family-pot version just recreates the old gendered division of labor, since in practice it's the mother who ends up taking the leave. The family-pot side says couples differ and the state shouldn't be nudging them into a particular arrangement. What do you think Mill would come down on here, and which way do you think he'd lean? I'm less interested in a 'he was a feminist so obviously he'd pick the quota' answer than in the genuine tensions in his own thought, because I suspect he'd be pretty torn, and I want you to take seriously whatever in his thought would pull him in the other direction.
