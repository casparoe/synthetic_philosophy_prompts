I have been arguing with a fellow student in a seminar on axiology about whether the relation ‘all things considered better than’ must be transitive. Our starting point is a kind of spectrum argument. Consider a long chain of possible outcomes: at one end, a brief but very severe episode of pain; at the other, an extremely long but barely noticeable discomfort. Arrange the intermediate cases so that each adjacent pair differs only slightly in intensity and duration. When we compare adjacent cases pairwise, nearly everyone judges the slightly shorter but slightly more intense episode to be better, because the small difference in intensity seems outweighed by the difference in duration. Yet when we compare the endpoints directly, the very long mild discomfort seems worse than the short sharp pain. If the pairwise judgements are accepted, we seem forced to conclude that betterness is intransitive, since the endpoint is judged worse than the starting point despite a chain of ‘better than’ steps.

Write an essay assessing whether this sort of spectrum argument succeeds in showing that ‘better than’ is not transitive. Be careful to distinguish different claims one might mean by intransitivity: for example, that our ordinary pairwise judgements are jointly inconsistent; that the underlying betterness relation is genuinely intransitive; that betterness is in fact transitive but vague, threshold-structured, or subject to a lexical ordering of evaluative factors; or that the spectrum rests on an illicit way of aggregating intensity and duration. Weigh the most plausible defences of transitivity, including appeals to the role of betterness in guiding rational choice and the possibility that pairwise comparisons at very small margins are unreliable. Reach a definite conclusion about which interpretation of the spectrum argument is best supported, and justify it. Please write in British English.
