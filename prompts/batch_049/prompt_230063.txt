Im in an epistemology reading group thats been chewing on higher-order evidence for two years, and I've started to suspect the impasse is structural rather than just stubbornness. We keep relitigating the same divide. One camp accepts something like an enkratic requirement: you cant rationally believe p while also believing that your total evidence makes p unlikely, because thats a kind of self-undermining incoherence between levels. The other camp thinks enkratic requirements are too strong, since they'd rule out cases that look rationally fine—the classic ones being subjects with good first-order evidence for p who get misleading higher-order evidence that their own assessment was unreliable, or people who rationally suspend judgment even though their first-order evidence actually supports p. Everyone grants that first-order evidence and evidence about your own reliability are different animals; everyone agrees on the rough structure of the cases; nobody has moved an inch in two years.

My working theory is that this isnt really a dispute about who read the toy cases correctly. Its a standoff about what "rational" is supposed to track: one side treats rationality as coherence between levels of belief, the other treats it as responding correctly to whichever evidence is strongest overall. But I dont trust my own diagnosis, obviously—maybe its a verbal dispute about what "evidence" picks out, or a hidden empirical disagreement about whether real people can hold mismatched first-order and higher-order attitudes stably, or a sign that the original question was ill-posed. What kind of disagreement is this actually? And given your diagnosis, is there anything—a distinction, a formal result, a change of question—that could genuinely unstick it?
