I've been going back and forth with a friend for months about wether a much larger human population would be a good thing, and I've realized the argument never makes progress because we're using entirely different scorecards for what a theory of population value is even supposed to do. So I want to force the criteria to the surface before anyone gets to have an opinion about the yes/no.

Here's what I'd like: write the list of requirements a theory that ranks populations by goodness must meet to count as adequate for judging whether a larger population is better. For each requirement, give the puzzle or judgment that motivates it — things like the Repugnant Conclusion (the idea that a vast population of lives barely worth living could come out better than a smaller population of excellent lives), the Mere Addition Paradox, the procreation asymmetry, the non-identity problem, and same-number cases where more total or more equal welfare should win. Then mark which of these are non-negotiable and which are just pet commitments of particular camps (totalists, averagists, person-affecting views, and so on), and say what you think separates the two. If some subset of the requirements turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable given the impossibility results in popultion axiology, that's the part I most want to see worked out — which demand has to give. Don't spend time scoring existing theories against the list; I want the criteria themselves, and I'll do the scorekeeping myself.
