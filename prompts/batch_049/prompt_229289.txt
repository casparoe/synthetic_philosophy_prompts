I've been trying to wrap my head around Hofstadter's strange loop account of the self, and I keep getting stuck on the same point. He says the I is just a symbol the system builds for itself — a self-referential symbol, a kind of loop. But a symbol seems like the kind of thing that only exists for an interpreter, the way a squiggle on paper stands for something else. If that's what the self is, why does Hofstadter spend so much time insisting the self is real and not a illusion? How can a symbol have causal powers, make choices, feel pain? It sounds like he's basically admitting the self is unreal but refusing the obvious conclusion. What am I missing?
