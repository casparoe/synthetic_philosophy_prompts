Me and a couple friends are putting together a handout for our undergrad metaphysics club, and we want a "which view on the identity of indiscernibles are you actually committed to?" decision tree (a quiz with a scoring key is fine too, but I'd rather have a tree). It should start from how someone reacts to Black's two-spheres case and two or three variants, and end at a leaf like: bundle theory with strong PII, a view where spatiotemporal location does the individuating, a substratum/bare particular view, primitive thisness or haecceitism, and some kind of structuralist dissolution that says there are no individual spheres. No question should just ask "do you believe the identity of indiscernibles?" — every node has to be a concrete case or a forced choice about a principle, answerable by someone who has never heard of PII.

The distinctions I actually care about the tree separating are: (1) pure vs impure properties — whether something like being-two-miles-from-sphere-A is allowed as a difference-maker, or only purely qualitative stuff counts; (2) whether relations count at all, and in particular whether occupying a different absolute place in Newtonian space is a real qualitative difference; (3) whether you can even make sense of numerical distinctness with zero qualitative difference, or whether that's just not contentful.

Cases to use somewhere: the original Black setup (two iron spheres, same composition and shape, two miles apart, nothing else); a variant where one sphere has a scratch; a variant where the spheres differ only in their absolute positions; maybe a variant with a third object so their distance-relations to that object are different. I want you to say whether the scratch and position variants are actually doing different work than the original case or are just the same move twice.

Here's the part I'll grade hardest: build from logical space, not from the list of famous names. Lay out the branch questions, the combinations of answers they allow, then locate the named positions in that space. If some combination is empty, tell me whether its empty because the view is incoherent, because no one wants it, or just because nobody has explored it. Also, if two of the named leaves can't be separated by any of the distinctions I listed, say so directly rather than pretending your tree can tell them apart.

Keep it to maybe six to eight leaves and four or five branch questions, and give each leaf a one-sentence "most awkward committment" line. If a leaf is unnameable or unnamed, just call it "unnamed" and say briefly what it would be.
