I am putting together a research proposal on hard choices, and before I commit to a positive view I want the specification of what any adequate theory in this area must deliver. Write the desiderata for a theory of hard choices: where a hard choice is one between options that cannot be ranked against a single scale of value, and where choosing well is nevertheless supposed to be possible rather than merely arbitrary. I want the requirements list, not a survey of positions.

For each requirement: state it precisely, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say whether it is non-negotiable or a parochial commitment of some camp. In particular, the list should force a theory to say what relation holds between options when neither is better and they are not equal, and to distinguish incomparability, incommensurability in the narrow sense of there being no common cardinal unit, and parity (comparable but neither better nor worse nor exactly equal) — or to argue that one of these collapses into another, which would itself be a substantive commitment. Address at least these cases: the small-improvements phenomenon, in which slightly improving one option need not make it better than the other; rational regret after a well-made hard choice; the difference between a hard choice and optimization under uncertainty; the possibility that some choices constitute or revise values rather than express an antecedently complete ranking; and the aggregation of hard choices for policy, as when a fixed budget must be divided among goods of apparently different kinds. For each case, explain why it is a requirement rather than an optional feature.

Then flag any subset of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude the standard families of view: incomparability views, parity views, and views that reduce hard choices to ignorance or merely incomplete preferences. A table or numbered list is fine. Do not include citations; I will supply them.
