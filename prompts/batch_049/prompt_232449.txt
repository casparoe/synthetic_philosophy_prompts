I am a journalist who covers mathematics and the sciences, and I am preparing a feature on whether numbers exist. Before I put my own view into print, I want a consistency check on the bundle of philosophical positions I keep endorsing. Please treat this as an audit of the set rather than a lecture on the positions themselves.

My current commitments are these:

1. Mathematical truth is objective and mind-independent. For a fixed arithmetic statement, such as every even number greater than 2 is the sum of two primes, there is a determinate fact of the matter that does not depend on our conventions, our cognitive makeup, or whether anyone ever proves or refutes it.

2. The correct ontology for mathematics is ante rem structuralism. Mathematical objects are positions in abstract structures, and a structure is ontologically prior to the objects that occupy its positions. The number 2 is nothing over and above the place it occupies in the natural-number structure, and the same structure can be realized by any system of objects satisfying the appropriate relations.

3. The strongest argument for mathematical existence is the indispensability argument. Our best empirical theories quantify over numbers, functions, and similar entities, and because we are justified in accepting those theories, we are justified in accepting the entities they quantify over.

4. I accept a Quinean principle of ontological economy. The entities we should countenance are exactly those quantified over by our best scientific theories, with nothing further posited unless it is indispensable to an accepted theory.

5. I nevertheless regard pure mathematics that outruns present physical application as genuinely truth-valued. Set theorists who investigate large cardinal axioms, such as the existence of inaccessible cardinals, are mapping an independent mathematical reality rather than merely adopting a framework, even though no accepted physical theory quantifies over such objects.

6. I hold that the structuralism in 2 dissolves Benacerraf's identification problem. The fact that 2 can be encoded as the set {∅,{∅}} or as {{∅}} stops being troubling once numbers are positions in a structure rather than particular sets.

The problem I want solved is whether 1 through 6 are jointly tenable. I need to know which pairs genuinely pull against each other, which only appear to, and how severe each genuine tension is. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing all of them as equally bad; for each, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision is. Then identify any apparent tension that dissolves on inspection, and tell me which single change to the set would restore the most coherence at the lowest cost.
