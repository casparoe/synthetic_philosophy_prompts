I'm prepping a session on Sidgwick and I've got an essay idea I'd like some comments on before I commit to writing it up. Working title: 'Sidgwick's psychical research was philosophy by other means.' The plan: The Methods of Ethics ends with the dualism of practical reason — the apparent clash between rational egoism and utilitarianism — and Sidgwick basically says the only way to close the gap is a theistic postulate that the two ultimately coincide. So his years in the Society for Psychical Research, gathering evidence about survival and telepathy and such, weren't a midlife detour into spooky stuff; they were a direct continuation of the one place the book ends unresolved. The strong version I'd want to defend is that you can't really understand why psychical research mattered to him unless you see it as an attempt to get empirical traction on the unresolved problem at the end of the book.

Comment on this idea for me. Is it actually interesting, or does it just smooth a messy intellectual life into a neat narrative? Where is the inference from 'he needed a solution to the dualism' to 'that's why he did the psychical research' weakest? And would it matter for the thesis if the SPR involvement came about through, say, friendship networks or personal grief rather then from the argument of the book? Also tell me what would change your overall verdict: what kind of biographical or textual evidence would flip you from 'solid thesis' to 'overreach', and which step in the reasoning do you think is most likely to fail on closer reading.
