ok so im making a zine for a design class about typefaces and i fell down this whole rabbit hole about what makes every lowercase 'a' an 'a' when a blackletter a, a geometric sans a, a pixel a, and my own messy handwritting a look almost nothing alike. like theres no single shape they all share, and nobodys brain is just lining them all up against one stored picture of "the a," at least not in any simple way. i saw this idea called Letter Spirit (or something like that) and the gist is that the real identity of the letter isnt a template or an average, its more like a role: the parts only make sense in relation to each other, and what survives across styles is high-level structure, not exact shape.

for the last page of the zine i want a villanelle about this, because the form kind of does the idea already: the two refrains come back unchanged while the lines around them change in every stanza, like the same letter showing up in a bunch of different fonts and still being itself. could you have one refrain keep insisting that no single shared shape survives across all the a's, and the other refrain keep insisting that its still obviously the same letter? and make the non-refrain lines do concrete style changes — like one stanza is a serif/times-ish setting, one is blackletter, one is messy handwriting, one is a pixel or stencil thing — so the poem demonstrates the point instead of just telling it.

i want it to be an actual good poem, like someone who couldnt care less about the philosophy would still like it, but also accurate enough that someone who knows the Letter Spirit idea wouldnt cringe. and dont just say "essence is a role" over and over — show it through how the refrains and the changing lines interact.

i dont want a bibliography or reading list or anything, just the poem and, after it, a short note (not part of the poem) saying how confident you are that you got the philosophical idea right and which point in the poem or note you are least sure about or where you took the biggest liberty.
