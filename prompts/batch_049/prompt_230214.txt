I'm drafting notes for a short essay on whether the question "Why did industrialization happen in Britain rather than China?" — and, in a slightly different register, the Needham question about modern science — is well posed. I'd like comments on the argument before I write the full essay.

Working claim: the paired-contrast form is not one well-posed question but a family of questions with different implicit baselines, and the usual phrasing smuggles in a baseline that steers answers toward civilizational essences rather than testable difference-makers.

Rough notes:

1. The question pairs a single positive case with a single negative case and asks for the differentiator. That only makes sense if the two cases are drawn from a comparable population. If the relevant population is all late preindustrial commercial cores, then the more striking fact is that any takeoff occurred at all, not that China in particular did not.
2. The Yangzi delta and the English midlands shared many of the standard preconditions — commercialized agriculture, proto-industrial textile production, and pressure on the land and wood frontier. If that is right, a clean single-differentiator answer is mis-specified; the causal story probably involves interactions among coal geography, Atlantic trade, fiscal capacity, and other factors that cannot be compressed into one contrast.
3. The rhetorical pull of "why not China?" comes from treating British-style industrialization as the null case and China as the deviation needing a blocker. Reverse the polarity and the question invites different answers, which suggests both versions presuppose that the transition had to happen somewhere.
4. The Needham question should be kept distinct: it asks why early modern European science, as a particular institutional and experimental culture, emerged despite China's earlier technical and textual achievements. Folding it into the industrialization question may conflate the history of science with the history of economic growth.
5. I want to close by appealing to explanation as compression: a good why-question tells you what to vary in your model. A difference-making question should ask which joint set of differences made the British path more probable, not which single feature Britain had that China lacked.

I'd especially like comments on (i) whether the charge that the paired-contrast form does hidden work via its implicit baseline is sound or overstated; (ii) what the strongest steelman of the original question is, and how the essay should meet it; and (iii) whether my suggested replacement is genuinely better posed or merely relocates the contrast to a larger set of sub-questions.
