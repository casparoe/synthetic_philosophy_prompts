A friend in my reinforcement-learning reading group scrawled this on the whiteboard last week, and I've been stewing on it since. It seems to prove we can never know what anyone wants, which can't be right, so I want to know where it breaks.

(1) The only evidence we ever have about an agent's preferences is their behaviour; there's no direct readout of the reward signal.
(2) That's what inverse reinforcement learning makes precise: observe behaviour and recover the reward function the agent was maximising.
(3) But the reverse map is one-to-many. In the standard discounted setup, any deterministic policy can be made optimal by cooking up a reward that gives 1 whenever the agent takes the action the policy prescribes in the current state and 0 otherwise. So the observed behaviour is consistent with the true reward and with endlessly many junk ones.
(4) So behavioural evidence alone doesn't discriminate between those rewards; the data radically underdetermine the preferencs.
(5) Therefore you can never genuinely infer what another agent values from watching them. Any attribution is just an arbitrary pick among equally supported reward functions.
(6) But (5) is absurd. I know my flatmate prefers tea to coffee from years of watching her pick tea when coffee is sitting right there, and it would be bonkers to say her behaviour equally supports the idea that she secretly hates tea and drinks it for some other reason.
