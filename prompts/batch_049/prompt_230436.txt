I help run a tiny grant program — really two people and a spreadsheet — with about $200k over the next two years to put into conceptual work on the ethics of pest control, mostly the welfare questions raised by insecticides. We can't fund empirical entomology and we don't want a survey of the field; we want to know which philosophical problems are most worth paying someone to sit with. The threads I keep coming back to: (a) what to do with deep uncertainty about whether insects are even the kind of thing that can suffer; (b) whether a quick kill from a synthetic insecticide is much of a harm at all if the insect has a weeks-long life, no long-term narrative, and hundreds of offspring; (c) how to set the baseline — any realistic farm kills or displaces enormous numbers of insects one way or another, so what's the right counterfactual when we ask "worse than what?"; and (d) whether expected-welfare thinking just explodes at these scales, since a small probability of sentience times trillions of individuals is still a huge number. Which two or three of these (or something I'm missing) should we fund first, and why? I care less about the list than the reasoning: for each, what makes it upstream of the others, how tractable it actually is in 18 months for a philosopher rather than a entomologist, and what evidence would make you kill it.
