Over the last few months I've been in a reading group with some political science PhD students, and we keep going around in circles about wether the fall of the Roman Republic is actually a useful analogy for democratic backsliding today. Every week someone brings in a different version of the story — Tiberius Gracchus getting clubbed to death after the tribunician veto was treated as a mere obstruction, Sulla marching legions on Rome, Catiline and the senatus consultum ultimum, Milo and Clodius turning the streets into gang warfare, Caesar crossing the Rubicon — and then draws a completely different lesson. Some of us think it's about norms eroding before institutions formally change; others think it's about land inequality and the dispossession of veterans; one person insists the whole comparison is broken because Rome's assemblies were never democratic in a modern sense and the republic was really an oligarchy run by a few families the whole time.

Before we read another article, I want the full spec of what a theory in this area actually has to get right. As an expert on comparative democratic erosion, write me the desiderata: what any adequate account that uses the late Roman Republic as an analogy for modern democracies has to deliver. For each requirement, give the historical case or consideration that makes it a requirement; say which are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of one camp (for instance, that the explanation be structural rather than biographical, or that it explain both sides' norm violations rather than treating one side as the good guys); and flag any pair of requirements you think can't be jointly satisfied — I had a feeling, while reading, that the demand for historical fidelity and the demand for generalizable lessons pull in different directions, but I want to see the full list. And at the end, tell me which single requirement does the most to rule out the usual pop-history "Rome is a warning for America" takes. Please think it through rather than giving me platitudes about how history doesn't repeat but it rhymes.
