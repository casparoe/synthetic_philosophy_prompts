The student's answer:

"Hempel's paradox begins from the hypothesis that all ravens are black. In predicate logic this is equivalent to its contrapositive, that all non-black things are non-ravens. Nicod's condition says that a universal claim of the form 'all F are G' is confirmed by an F that is G, disconfirmed by an F that is not G, and unaffected by objects that are neither F nor G. A black raven is therefore a confirming instance of 'all ravens are black.' A white shoe, on the other hand, is a non-black non-raven, so it confirms the contrapositive, 'all non-black things are non-ravens.' By the equivalence condition, anything that confirms a hypothesis confirms any statement logically equivalent to it. So the white shoe confirms 'all ravens are black,' which sounds absurd. The best way out is to reject the equivalence condition: two statements can be logically equivalent but differ in subject matter, so confirmation isn't automatically transferred between them. This is also the standard Bayesian response, since learning that an object is a white shoe leaves the probability of the raven hypothesis exactly unchanged; the illusion of paradox comes from treating a mere failure to disconfirm as if it were positive confirmation."
