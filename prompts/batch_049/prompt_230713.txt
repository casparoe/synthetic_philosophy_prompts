Hi, I'm an undergrad writing a final paper on Leibniz's moral psychology and I've gotten myself turned around. In the Monadology (Latta translation), Leibniz writes:

The passing condition, which involves and represents a multiplicity in the unit [unité] or in the simple substance, is nothing but what is called Perception, which is to be distinguished from Apperception or Consciousness, as will afterwards appear. In this matter the Cartesian view is extremely defective, for it treats as non-existent those perceptions of which we are not consciously aware.

So Leibniz is saying there's this whole layer of mental life we don't consciously notice, and these little perceptions are doing the real metaphysical work all the time. If that's true, then why do moral philosophers make such a big deal about Leibnizian apperception and rational self-reflection? It seems like if the fundamental stuff of my mind is unconscious perception and appetition, then my conscious moral judgments are just tiny surface effects and I can't actually be praised or blamed for them. Doesn't Leibniz's own metaphysics undercut his ethics here? Or is his moral theory just not about conscious choice at all? Please answer in American English.
