I'm a HPS phd student running a section for an intro philosophy of science course, and I want to hand out a 'which demarcationist are you?' sorting quiz instead of lecturing. Could you build a decision tree, or an 8-question quiz with a scoring key if that's cleaner, that sorts people by how they respond to concrete cases rather than by which label they like. Use astrology, Freudian psychoanalysis, the cold fusion episode, and the string theory impasse as the recurring cases. The splits should be concrete claims or case judgments—e.g., whether saving a theory with ad hoc auxiliary hypotheses is itself a bad sign, or whether it's just normal science—never 'do you agree with Popper?'. Build it from the logical space first (questions and answer-combinations), then locate these leaves in it: naive falsificationism, sophisticated falsificationism (where the crime is ad hoc-ness, not unfalsifiability as such), a Lakatosian research-programme view (hard core plus protective belt, judged over time), a Kuhnian historical view that denies any timeless criterion, and a Laudanian position that demarcation is a pseudo-problem. The distinction I most want the tree to keep clean is whether failure attaches to single hypotheses or to whole programmes over time. For any answer-combination with no occupant, say whether its empty because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or just underexplored; and flag any two leaves your cases genuinely cant separate, so the tree doesn't paper over it. Audience is undergrads who've read a little Kuhn and Popper; keep it around 7 or 8 questions.
