Write a dialogue between two philosophers who both work on causation: one is a Lewis-style counterfactual theorist, the other is a Woodward-style interventionist. I want it to feel like an actual argument, not two ted talks past each other. Make them actually respond to each other's points, and force the conversation into the hard cases: preemption and overdetermination, actual causation vs general causal claims, whether the interventionist can spell out what an intervention is without already smuggling in causal language, and proportionality (is it the rock being thrown that breaks the window, or the rock's precise mass, or something between?). Neither side should be a strawman, and don't have either of them misstate the other's view just to score a point. If someone makes a concession, make it a real one, not a face-saving restatement of their own position. roughly 700 words. At the end or in a short note after the dialogue, separate what's fairly well-established in the field from what's genuinely open, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
