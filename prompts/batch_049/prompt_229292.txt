Okay, here's roughly where I've landed on testimony and I want a consistency check before I have to defend this in a seminar. I hold: (1) testimonial justification is reducible, in something like a local reductionist sense — your justification for believing what someone tells you always goes through your background evidence that they're trustworthy, but this evidence can be very thin, so in a normal case the fact that they're a competent adult making a sincere assertion in a cooperative exchange can be enough; (2) young children nevertheless get justified beliefs from their caregivers' testimony before they have anything like a track record for those particular speakers, and I don't want to write this off as mere reliability without justification — the child is justified; (3) laypeople can be justified in deferring to scientific consensus on climate change or vaccines even when they can't follow the underlying science; (4) gullibility is still a vice, and it would obviously be irrational to believe a stranger's bare say-so about something life-changing with no other signs of reliability. How well does this hang together? I want the tensions ranked by severity, including which ones only look like tensions and why, and what the cheapest revision is and what it actually costs.
