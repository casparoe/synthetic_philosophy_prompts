Im a game designer, around fifteen years now, mostly branching-narrative RPGs and a couple of strategy games. Lately Ive been building a game where you run a small country. The player can reach a genuinely good ending, but every route I design to that ending passes through at least one choice Id call morally wrong if it happened in real life: blackmailing a rival, letting an ally take the blame, burying a report, refusing to help a group because it would split the coalition. Playtesters keep asking why theres no truly clean route, or asking me to let the karma meter recover afterwards, or wanting the dirty choice clearly flagged as evil so they can avoid it. This got me thinking about a thing I later learned philosophers call the problem of dirty hands: in politics, sometimes the right thing to do is also wrong, and a decent person does it anyway and is still supposed to feel bad about it. From what I can tell the camps are roughly: (1) some acts are just forbidden, and doing one makes you corrupt no matter what it achieves; (2) if the outcome is best overall then the act wasnt really wrong, and any guilt after is just confusion or theater; (3) no, the act can be wrong and still be the thing to do, and the leftover guilt is part of taking responsibility, not a bug; (4) the whole "necessary evil" talk is a con politicians run on everyone including themselves.

What I want is a translation. Restate the main positions in that debate in the vocabulary I actually use: flags, branching dialogue, state machines, reputation or karma meters, win states and endings, irreversible choices, hidden versus shown consequences, save-scumming, achievements, and the difference between what the player knows and what the game actually tracks. Im fluent in all of that, so dont simplify the game side; do simplify the philosophy side enough that I dont need the jargon or the names, but not so much that you flatten the positions.

Go position by position. For each, tell me what translates cleanly into a mechanic or design pattern, what I have to distort to make it fit, and what part of the position has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Then give me the real deliverable: the residue report. Which parts of the philosophers argument do not survive the translation? And is that because my game vocabulary is missing something important, because the original claim was less clear than it looks, or because games are just a bad model for this? I especialy want to know whether my design instinct — that the dirty choice should stay a permanent blemish that no karma meter ever washes away — is actually the dirty hands position, or whether Ive accidentally built a different position into the mechanic.

One honesty constraint. When you map a position to a mechanic, pay attention to what the mechanic literally does. If a karma meter can only add and subtract, and a big part of the dirty hands idea is that the wrong cant be cancelled out by the good, then a karma meter is smuggling in a consequentalist answer, and I want you to call that a distortion instead of a translation. Where the mapping breaks, dont cover it over with a neat analogy.

Start by brainstorming the game concepts you have to work with and what each one can and cant represent, then do the translation, then the residue report. I want an honest inventory of what breaks, not a clean diagram.
