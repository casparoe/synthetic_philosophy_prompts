I'm a software engineer by training with no formal background in philosophy. I've been trying to follow a research area people call low-impact AI, or work on impact measures for reinforcement learning agents. The motivating idea is roughly this: if an agent is given a mis-specified objective, we would at least like to limit how much of the world it changes in the course of pursuing that objective, so that the damage is contained even before we know what we actually want. A recurring worry in that area is that "side effect" and "impact" cannot be defined without a full specification of value, which would make the entire approach circular.

I'm fluent in the following vocabulary from professional software work, and I'd like you to use it as the target of a translation exercise rather than a loose analogy: pure functions and side effects; specifications, contracts, and implementations; invariants and pre/postconditions; referential transparency; mocks, test doubles, and test harnesses; effect systems in the rough sense of type-and-effect systems, though I have not studied type theory formally; sandboxing, least privilege, and capability-based security; commits, diffs, patches, and merge bases; refactoring for behavior preservation; command-query separation; idempotency; and no-ops.

First, restate the main claims and arguments of the low-impact program in that vocabulary as faithfully and literally as you can. Make the mapping explicit: what corresponds to a baseline, an allowed effect, a covered line, a diff, a rollback, a no-op, and so on. Where a claim can be expressed in more than one software construct, say which one carries it best and why.

Second, give me a residue report. Tell me which parts of the position translate cleanly, which parts required distortion, and which parts have no counterpart in the software vocabulary at all. I want you to press on what I think is the central asymmetry. In software, a side effect is always a side effect relative to something: a function's contract, a type signature, a test suite, or a stated abstraction boundary. Somewhere the intended effect is declared, and a side effect is the complement of that declaration. The low-impact program seems to want the reverse: a notion of avoiding side effects without a declared specification of what should happen. Is there any construct in my vocabulary that constrains "change in general" rather than "change that deviates from a specified expectation"? Do baselines, sandboxes, or the principle of least privilege actually deliver that, or do they just relocate the specification to a wider boundary? Is something like "minimize the size of the diff against the branch that would have run if the agent had never existed" even well-typed when the baseline is a branch nobody ever executed?

Finally, give me your honest judgment about what the residue means. If a large part of the low-impact agenda cannot be expressed in software vocabulary taken literally, is that because my vocabulary is too impoverished and needs to be extended, or because the low-impact agenda is implicitly assuming a partial specification of value while presenting itself as not needing one? My working hypothesis is that "minimize impact without specifying value" is a type error of roughly the same kind as asking for a test suite that checks for all bugs without enumerating the expected behavior. I want to know whether that hypothesis is fair, a distortion, or too quick.
