I'm a novelist, and I'm working on a comic novel about a woman who goes way too deep into the metaphysics of causation and ends up founding a startup that sells "omission liability insurance." The premise she's running on is that if absences are real causes, than basically every person is causally on the hook for an unbounded number of bad things they never got around to preventing, so somebody ought to sell coverage for exactly that. I'd like you to write an in-universe document for me: the "How It Works" page from her company's website. Aim for maybe 450–550 words, in that bright, slightly maniacal startup voice, completely deadpan, as if the company has just swallowed the metaphysics and is now calmly working out the pricing tiers.

The jokes should come from the actual puzzles in the philosophical literature, not from inventing strawman dumbness. You can lean on things like: the contrast between doing and allowing; the old example where a gardener's failure to water the plants seems to cause their death but a stranger's failure to water them doesn't, and no one can quite say what distinguishes the two; the way any omission can be redescribed as some positive act (sitting still, breathing, being elsewhere); the proliferation problem where every non-event starts looking like a cause; and the uneasy relation between causal talk and moral or legal blame. Maybe include one "customer story" from a man filing a claim for all the traffic accidents he didn't help prevent. The satirical target is the idea that you can treat omissions as perfectly ordinary causes and not have any weirdness leak out, and the fun is watching the company cheerfully refuse to notice the problem.
