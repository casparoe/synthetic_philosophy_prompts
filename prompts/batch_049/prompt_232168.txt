I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I've been trying to understand money pump arguments. The standard case goes like this: my preferences are cyclic — I prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A. If I currently hold B, I'd pay a small amount to switch to A, then pay to switch to C, then pay to switch back to B, ending up exactly where I started but poorer. The usual moral is supposed to be that my cyclic preferences are irrational.

Here is what confuses me. A money pump is a sequence of trades spread out over time. At each individual trade, I am choosing the option I prefer at that moment, so each step looks locally sensible. Why should the fact that someone can string those steps together over time show that anything was wrong with my preferences at any particular moment? It seems the argument only shows that an agent facing repeated trade offers should avoid being predictably exploitable across time, not that cyclic preferences held at a single time are defective. My tastes change over time constantly, after all — I might prefer one refactoring plan in the morning and a different one after lunch — and nobody concludes from that alone that I'm irrational or that I could be pumped through a cycle. Where exactly is the disanalogy?

I can also imagine solving the problem in software: keep a preference database, accept each offer whose offered item is ranked above what I currently hold, and also keep a recent-trade log so I can refuse any trade that would complete a cycle I've been pulled through. That would stop the pump, but then I'd sometimes be refusing an option I say I prefer. So my questions are these. Is the money pump really an argument about rationality, or just a practical warning about leaving yourself open to exploitation? And does it show that cyclic preferences are irrational at a single time, or only that someone with cyclic preferences plus a compulsion to accept every locally preferred trade can be exploited? What exactly would be wrong with accepting each locally preferred trade and refusing only the cycle-closing one?
