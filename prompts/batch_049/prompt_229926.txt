I am a PhD student in philosophy of time, and I am preparing a methods paper on whether presentism and eternalism can be made to make contact rather than talk past one another. Please draft the joint statement that a committed presentist and a committed B-theoretic eternalist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration lasting a year. The document should have this structure:

1. Agreed claims. A numbered list of claims both sides can sign, covering at least: (a) the formal representation of the time series and the basic tense logic each side accepts as common notation; (b) what presentism does and does not assert — in particular, that it is not the moving-spotlight view and is not a thesis about the content of ordinary beliefs; (c) what eternalism does and does not assert — in particular, that it need not deny that tensed beliefs play an irreducible cognitive role; and (d) which neighboring views (the growing block, the moving spotlight, presentism restricted to one's own world) are being set aside and why.

2. A disagreements table. Each row should state a single residual question in neutral wording, followed by the formulation each side would accept as its own, and a note on whether that row is a merely verbal dispute or a substantive one. Rows should include the semantics of tensed discourse, the status of non-present objects, what makes past-tensed truths true, and whether special relativity bears on the debate.

3. Cruxes. Identify four to six sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each, state why it is a crux and specify one argument, case, or result both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. I expect the cruxes to include: whether 'only present things exist' can be given a non-trivial formulation without presupposing the tensed quantifier it is meant to characterize; whether the two sides are both entitled to argue from the Quinean criterion of ontological commitment, or whether that criterion itself is one of the things in dispute; whether presentists can supply truthmakers for past- and future-tensed truths from present resources alone, without primitive tense operators or ersatz times; and whether a presentist can recover the empirical content of special relativity from a privileged foliation without making the choice of frame ad hoc. Do not limit yourself to these.

4. A short final assessment of what the two positions found themselves agreeing on once terminology was fixed, and which residual disagreement remains genuinely metaphysical rather than semantic or merely verbal.

The central requirement is this: the agreed claims must be neither vacuous nor slanted, and every formulation attributed to a side must be one that side would actually sign rather than a caricature its opponent would write for it.
