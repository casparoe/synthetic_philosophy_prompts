Last week a friend and I got into a long argument about the sunk cost fallacy. I was defending the textbook line that past costs should be irrelevant to forward-looking decisions; he kept saying that's too clean, because a sensible person might stick with a project precisely *because* they've already sunk a lot into it, and that this can be rationally defensible rather than just a psychological glitch. We ended up scribbling on napkins this messy list of candidate justifications someone might give for continuing a project, relationship, degree, etc. partly because of costs already paid. I'd like you to rank them from most defensible to least defensible as *rational* justifications (as opposed to understandable psychological excuses), with one sentence each explaining the placement. If you think an item is not really about sunk costs at all, or conflates two things, feel free to say so.

Here's the list:
1. Quitting would force me to book a loss, and I hate booking losses.
2. The fact that past-me put all this in suggests past-me had information about the value of the project that I shouldn't throw away.
3. If I quit, other people will update that I'm flaky, and that will cost me later.
4. I want to be the kind of person who finishes things, and indulging my urge to quit would erode that self-image.
5. The past spending has actually changed my options now (e.g., non-transferable training, contracts, equipment).
6. Quitting means admitting to myself and others that the original decision was dumb, which is humiliating.
7. If I quit now, all the time and money so far will have been wasted.
8. Sticking it out gives me a chance to learn whether success was possible, information I only get by continuing.
9. It would be inconsistent with the commitments I made to myself earlier.
10. Future-me might regret not having seen it through, and I want to pre-empt that regret.
