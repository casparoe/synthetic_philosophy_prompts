Before I read any further on Kelly betting and geometric rationality, I would like the specification rather than another restatement of the formula. Please write the desiderata that an adequate theory of stake sizing for repeated, multiplicative gambles — a gambler who each round wagers some fraction of current wealth on a favourable bet — must meet, with the case or consideration that makes each one a genuine requirement. In particular, I would like you to adjudicate which of the following are real requirements and how each should be stated: (i) a policy must be consistent with survival, so that it cannot treat a wager of the entire bankroll on a positive-expectation bet with any chance of loss as acceptable, and more generally must distinguish a favourable expectation from a sensible stake size; (ii) a policy must capture the overbetting phenomenon, in which stakes above the growth-optimal fraction reduce expected log-growth, and stakes sufficiently far above it make that growth negative so that typical wealth declines over time even though the arithmetic expectation of wealth may remain attractive; (iii) a policy must generalise from a single sequence of independent identical bets to a portfolio of simultaneous correlated bets, since covariance between outcomes changes the appropriate allocation even when each bet is individually favourable; (iv) a policy must say what to do under uncertain or shifting probabilities, including whether one ought deliberately to bet only a fraction of the apparent optimum; and (v) a policy must either reconcile itself with expected utility theory or explain why the apparent conflict is illusory — in particular, whether growth-optimal staking is rationally mandatory only for an agent with logarithmic utility. For each desideratum, say whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference, and flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Keep the scorecard of existing positions brief; the criteria and their motivation are what interest me.
