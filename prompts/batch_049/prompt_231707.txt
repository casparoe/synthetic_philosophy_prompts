My lot have the basic sketch, but I've found that reading about it leaves them shrugging: of course it's just a metaphor, they say. I want them to feel the pull of the weird conclusion before they can dismiss it.

Design a 90-minute classroom game or simulation for about 16 students that does this. Each student should play an ant following only small local rules — swapping tokens, following gradients, leaving marks, counting contacts — with no one seeing the colony's overall state. The colony as a whole should then have to do something that comes out looking clever, like choosing between nest sites, finding a shorter route, or making a collective decision under noisy information. The smart behaviour should appear only at the level of the group, so the debrief can ask: where was the decision made, did any individual ant know what the colony was doing, and is "the colony decided" a genuine explanation or just useful shorthand? Give me the rules, roles, materials, a timeline, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto the Aunt Hillary puzzle.
