In a reading group on climate ethics last month, one of my colleagues kept reaching for the line "no single raindrop ever thinks it caused the flood." He'd say it whenever someone started going on about their personal carbon footprint or whether they should stop eating meat, always with this little shrug, as if the matter was just settled. I've seen the same move made with "it's a drop in the ocean" and "no individual vote ever decided an election," and I suspect these all blurrs together into a kind of vague resignation. But I want to worry specifically at the raindrop line, because it's doing a lot of heavy lifting in these conversations, and I don't think its precise content is at all clear.

What are the different things that one-liner could be claiming? Lay out the readings as a menu, from ones that are trivially true and basically harmless, up through substantive empirical claims about causal contribution and moral claims about blame. For each reading, say whether it's true, false, or question-begging, and — most importantly — whether it would actually support his conclusion that attending to individual carbon-intensive choices is somehow confused or misdirected. I'd like the whole thing in a form I can bring to the next meeting and use to force him to say which raindrop argument he's actually making.
