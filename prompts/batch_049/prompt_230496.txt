I am one of seven volunteer moderators of a long-running online community of about forty thousand members. We are all equals on paper, and each of us has the permission to post a site-wide announcement without anyone else's approval. For the past week we have been privately debating whether to respond to a platform policy change that we all regard as bad for the community. All seven of us drafted a personal statement protesting the change. After the drafts circulated, an odd pattern emerged. Each of us, reading only our own draft, concluded that if that draft alone went out, it would be a net positive: it would signal that the moderation team is engaged and might put useful pressure on the platform. Yet we also all agreed that if even three or four of the seven statements went out, it would look as though the moderation team had splintered, and the community would end up worse off than if no statement had been posted. I am fairly confident everyone is reasoning honestly, but the pattern feels structurally broken.

Please first brainstorm the structural features of the case, then identify the underlying problem and explain how seven individually reasonable judgments could produce a collectively bad outcome. Finally, recommend a concrete decision procedure we could adopt so that this kind of failure does not recur.
