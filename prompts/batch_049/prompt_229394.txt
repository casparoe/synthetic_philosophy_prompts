An analytic-epistemology working group is running a formal adversarial collaboration between two parties who have spent a year arguing about what governs assertion. The first party holds a knowledge norm: assertion is the speech act whose distinctive rule permits one to assert that p only if one knows that p. The second party holds a truth norm: the distinctive rule of assertion forbids only asserting what is false, and further criticism of a true assertion as reckless, evidentially unsupported, or objectionably lucky is best traced to other norms or conversational standards rather than to a rule of assertion itself. Neither side should be allowed to caricature the other: the truth-norm party is not committed to holding that any true assertion is fine all-things-considered, and the knowledge-norm party is not committed to holding that every assertion falling short of knowledge is blameworthy.

Write the joint statement these two parties should produce. It should include:

1. A numbered list of claims about assertion that both parties accept, phrased non-vacuously, so that neither would want to weaken or hedge them.
2. A statement of the exact residual disagreement, in neutral wording, together with the formulation each party accepts as a fair statement of its own position. Distinguish disputes that are genuine from ones that dissolved once such notions as the norm of assertion, entitlement, and impropriety were clarified.
3. The cruxes whose settlement would move at least one party. For each crux, specify a concrete case both parties agree bears on it, for instance but not only lottery assertions, Moore-paradoxical utterances, Gettiered assertions, and assertions that are blameless because the speaker is misled or under pressure, and record in advance what each party predicts the correct handling of the case is.

The document should be one both parties could put their names to, not an adjudication, and it should leave the disagreement sharp at the joints rather than softened into a truce or a hybrid view. Use British English throughout, and do not include a bibliography or citations.
