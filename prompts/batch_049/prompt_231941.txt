Recently I have had to think about what money is because I have been involved in designing a local payment instrument, and I keep returning to Berkeley’s The Querist (1735). In Query 23 he surveys several candidate accounts of money — that it has intrinsic value, that it is a commodity, a standard, a measure, or a pledge — and then proposes that its true idea is altogether that of a ticket or counter. I find this view tempting, but I want to know what it risks. Treat the ticket-or-counter account as a live position in the philosophy of money. What would count against it? Give me the strongest theoretical objections, historical cases, and features of modern monetary systems that should shake my confidence, sorted by how decisive they are. I want to know what a defender would concede as a genuine difficulty, not only what critics press, and whether a sophisticated version can absorb the counterevidence or has been qualified into unfalsifiability. Keep the response under 600 words.
