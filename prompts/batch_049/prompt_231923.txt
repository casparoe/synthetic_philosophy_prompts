I keep getting tangled up about what operational weather forecasting is actually committed to. The practice has basically converged on ensemble forecasting: you run a deterministic atmospheric model many times with tiny perturbations to the initial conditions and report the spread as the forecast; a single unperturbed deterministic run is treated as nearly meaningless beyond a short horizon. But the model underneath is a bunch of deterministic PDEs. So what philosophy of determinism, probability, and predictability does this practice presuppose? Is the ensemble spread supposed to be purely epistemic—just our ignorance of the true initial conditions—or is the practice implicitly treating it as something closer to objective, irreducible uncertainty in the system? Does the practice actually need determinism at all, or would it look exactly the same if the atmosphere had genuine micro-level chanciness? And is there a real tension between saying the atmosphere is a deterministic chaotic system and saying there are principled limits on how far ahead you can predict, full stop? Reconstruct the implicit view, separate what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits with, and say what would have to change in how forecasts are produced or interpreted if we swapped in the other account. Take a definite side—dont just lay out the options for me.
