To fix the referent, take Sen’s standard illustration. There are two people, Prude and Lewd, and one copy of a risqué novel; the alternatives are that Prude reads it, that Lewd reads it, or that no one reads it. Prude ranks the outcome in which no one reads it first, Prude-reads second, and Lewd-reads last: he prefers the book unread, but if someone must read it, he would rather read it himself and dislike it than have Lewd read and enjoy it. Lewd ranks Prude-reads first, Lewd-reads second, and no-one-reads last: he would most enjoy Prude being forced to read it, would rather read it himself than see it go unread, and least wants the book to sit unread. A minimal liberal right lets each person decide the question of his own reading: Prude is decisive over the pair {Prude reads, no one reads}, so since he prefers no one reads, society ranks no-one-reads above Prude-reads; Lewd is decisive over {Lewd reads, no one reads}, so since he prefers Lewd reads, society ranks Lewd-reads above no-one-reads. Transitivity then forces Lewd-reads above Prude-reads. But both Prude and Lewd rank Prude-reads above Lewd-reads, so the Pareto principle requires Prude-reads above Lewd-reads. Contradiction. The example illustrates the general impossibility: no social choice procedure with unrestricted domain can jointly satisfy the weak Pareto principle and minimal liberalism while always producing an acyclic social ranking.
