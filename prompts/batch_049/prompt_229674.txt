ok so i've ended up as the person who has to pick where my workplace giving campaign sends its money this year, and the more i read the more i think the arguments are just people smuggling in different hidden checklists. one guy says the only thing that matters is how big the problem is and how many people are affected, so we should go after the biggest problem anywhere, which he thinks is maybe nuclear war or future people. another says a cause only counts if your dollar does something you can measure in the next two years, so by that test we should fund deworming pills or bednets. a third says all of that is backwards because a problem that's already famous and well funded, like cancer, is automatically a worse place to put money than something nobody is touching, even if the nobody-is-touching problem is smaller. and then there's the damn food bank down the street that i know is real because i see the line every morning, versus the malaria thing where i have to just trust a spreadsheet from people i've never met. so i've stopped asking which cause is best and started asking something upstream.

what should a good way of ranking causes even have to do? i want the spec before i read another framework. write the desiderata — i had to look that word up — basically the requirements list for a theory of cause prioritization, one adequate for a small donor or a giving circle trying to pick one or two causes (not a government or a foundation with billions). give me the list: the cases it has to handle, especially the ones that pull in opposite directions like the food bank versus the bednets versus the whole-future thing, the distinctions it can't collapse, the facts about psychology or economics or measurement it can't just wave away, and why each one is a requirement. then tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just the tastes of a particular camp, and flag any pair of requirements you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. if you want, briefly say how the importance-tractability-neglectedness framework does against the list and which one requirement it fails hardest, but i mostly care about the list.
