I teach a sci-fi survey class and we just finished Asimov's Foundation; next week I want to do a discussion on psychohistory as a version of cliodynamics, and I keep going back and forth on what the novel is actually saying. On the one hand, psychohistory is basically the dream version of what Turchin is after — mathematical, predictive, treating history as a system where individuals wash out — and the novel pretty clearly stacks the deck: Seldon's Plan works until the Mule shows up as a single unpredictable mutant. On the other hand, even before the Mule the Foundation keeps slipping into the exact dynamics Turchin talks about (elite overproduction, fiscal crisis, state breakdown), and the novel seems unsure whether those cycles are what the Plan is really built out of or just noise it has to correct for. I'd like an essay I can build the class around: read the book through the lens of Turchin's secular cycles, say honestly where Asimov anticipates the cliodynamics program and where he gets it wrong, and what the Mule actually proves about the limits of predicting history. Don't summarize the plot — I want the reading. And write it in American English.
