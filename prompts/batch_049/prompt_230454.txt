I'm teaching a small evening class on creativity and constraint, and I want an essay that pairs Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model with Groundhog Day. The model, roughly, is that creativity is mostly variation on a theme: you take an existing structure and twiddle parameters until the accumulated tweaks tip over into something that feels genuinely new. On the face of it the film looks built for this—the repeated day is the theme and Phil Connors's choices are the knobs. But I don't want the film treated as a mere illustration. I'd like a critical reading that tracks which knobs the film actually turns (the small daily variations, the seduction scenes, the piano lessons, the diner routine) and which changes can't plausibly be described as twiddling the day at all. The suspicion I want tested is that the real theme being varied isn't the day but Phil himself, and that the film might be saying this sort of variation is something the knob-twiddling model under-describes. So: read the film through the model, say where it genuinely illuminates, where the film resists the reading, and what the film adds to the philosophical picture that the abstract model misses.
