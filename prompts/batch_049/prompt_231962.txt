I work in safety review at an AI lab. Before a proposed training or deployment setup is approved, I have to judge whether it is an embedded-agent setup — one in which the system's actions can reach back and alter its own inputs, its training data, or the channel by which it is rewarded — and how seriously that matters. Last night I had a long argument with a friend about whether a language model fine-tuned on its own previous outputs, scored by a separate learned reward model, is genuinely embedded in this sense; we disagreed about what the term even commits us to, and I realized I have no operational test for the question. Write me a decision procedure my review group can actually run on a project proposal: a short ordered list of questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with "not our embeddedness concern," where it escalates to "redesign before approval," and where it stops and hands the decision to a person because the question is no longer technical. Ground the procedure in the embedded-agency idea that an agent is inside the world it models, without making the reviewers learn the theory itself. Then run the procedure on two cases: (a) an RL system trained in an environment where it can modify the numeric value its reward channel reads, and (b) a language model fine-tuned on its own sampled outputs ranked by a separate reward model. Make one of the two yield an answer a reviewer would find inconvenient, and say which step of the procedure is most likely to misfire.
