I'm on my school's student council and we're the ones who get pitched every new piece of tech before it goes to the admin: hallway cameras with "AI" that detects fights, an app where you scan a QR code to use the bathroom, locked phone pouches, a cafeteria payment system that also flags kids with low balances. We have to give a reccomendation each time, usually after like 20 minutes of discussion, and half the commitee just says "if it makes things safer or faster, why not." I've been reading about how technology can have politics built into it — like the famous story that some parkway bridges were built too low for buses, which quietly kept poor people off the beach without any sign saying so. I want a procedure we can actually run in 20 minutes to figure out when a proposal is doing that: baking in a decision that should be argued about in public instead of hidden in a spec sheet. Questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer for each, where the procedure stops with a No, and where we have to admit it's a values call and kick it to the whole council. Then run it on the hallway cameras, the bathroom QR app, and the cafeteria payment thing (fair warning: we mostly like the cafeteria one), and make sure at least one of those comes out as a No even though we'd personally want to vote yes. Also say where the procedure can't tell the difference between real built-in politics and us just being paranoid.
