Here's a sharp way to ask whether symbols push the substrate around. Picture a careenium: a sealed, constantly jostled container packed with a million tiny rigid parts that latch onto one another (call them simmballs if the whole thing is running as a physics sim). Watch stable macro-patterns form—spinning gears, ratchets, little machines that reassemble themselves after a stray simmball knocks a piece off. At the micro level there are only collisions and latchings. Now treat those stable macro-patterns as symbols, and argue for a strong, non-deflationary version of the claim that the symbols really do push the substrate around: a complete causal account of why a given simmball moves as it does must sometimes cite the gear or the ratchet, not just the adjacent simmballs. Don't take the 'depends what you mean by cause' exit. Steelman the microphysical exclusion objection—all the real pushing is done by simmballs bumping simmballs, and the patterns are just compressed bookkeeping—then answer it.
