A friend walked me through an argument about Weber's Protestant ethic thesis and I cant find the exit. Heres the argument as close as I can reconstruct it.

(1) A real explanation of a large structural change like the rise of capitalism has to cite something that did causal work, not merely something that accompanied the change.
(2) Weber's Protestant ethic story seems to cite certain Protestant values as doing that causal work: anxiety about whether you were saved made people treat their work as a calling, save obsessively, and reinvest profits instead of spending them, until profit-seeking came to feel like an obligation.
(3) But those values didn't come from nowhere. They were themselves caused by earlier material and institutional conditions (status insecurity, pastoral pressures, the structure of the church, and so on).
(4) If the values are themselves caused by those background conditions, then citing the values is just citing those background conditions one step down the line. The values are a middle step, not an independent source.
(5) If we insist that values can be an independent source even when they are caused, then explanations that appeal to values become unfalsifiable: for any economic outcome, you can posit some value that would have produced it, and you can always adjust the story to fit the result.
(6) So either the Protestant ethic thesis collapses into a materialist explanation, or it is unfalsifiable. Since it isn't meant as a materialist explanation, it must be unfalsifiable.

That conclusion feels too strong, because the thesis genuinely seems to illuminate how profit-seeking could come to feel morally serious in some places and not others. Which step do I reject? My instinct is to keep (2) and (5), but I don't know what that forces me to give up. Name the step and say what rejecting it costs.
