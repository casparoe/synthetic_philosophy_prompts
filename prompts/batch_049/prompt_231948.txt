I am a third-year doctoral student in clinical psychology, splitting my time between a training clinic and a lab that studies emotion and decision-making, and I keep hitting the same problem in both places: when someone reports on their own mental life, how much weight should that report carry? My clinical supervisors treat clients' self-report as close to the gold standard, while the cognitive psychologists I work with will not let uncorroborated introspective evidence into a manuscript. From coursework I suspect introspection is not one thing — describing a current experience, explaining why one did something, and reconstructing one's past inner life are different acts with different reliability — but I have never seen that insight turned into something I can run in the fifteen minutes before I write a chart note or code an interview excerpt. Several times a week I must decide whether a first-person report should shape a clinical formulation or a research conclusion, and my supervisors sign off on both, so the procedure has to be defensible to people with strong instincts in both directions.

Please write a decision procedure for how much to trust an introspective report. Present it as questions to ask in order, where each answer leads to a next step or a stop; for each question, say what counts as a sufficient answer. The procedure should mark the points where the decision stops being epistemic and becomes a value judgment, and it should reflect what is actually distinctive about introspective reports rather than collapsing into generic skepticism about self-report. Then apply it to three cases: (1) a participant in a choice experiment who says, with complete confidence, that she chose the safer-looking option because she is a cautious person; (2) a client who, during a mood exercise, reports a faint, diffuse dread at the back of his throat and can describe it as it is occurring; (3) a client whose emotionally vivid, articulate account of how one childhood event caused his current avoidance I would like to treat as the key to the case — I want the procedure's honest verdict here even if it says my preferred reading should not go into the clinical record. End by saying what the procedure cannot adjudicate and where it will misfire even when followed carefully. Keep it to at most 1,000 words, and write it in American English.
