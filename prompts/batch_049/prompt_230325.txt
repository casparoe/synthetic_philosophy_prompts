Please write a deadpan parody of a management consulting white paper, about 650–750 words and formatted in numbered sections, in the voice of a generational intelligence consultancy called something like Cohort Advantage Partners. The piece should take for granted that cohort membership (Boomer, Millennial, Gen Z) is a deep psychological kind that explains workplace behavior. The jokes should come from getting the surrounding social science right rather than from generational insults: the age-period-cohort problem, in which chronological age, birth cohort, and historical period are confounded so that so-called generational differences can often be re-described as life-stage or period effects; the arbitrariness of the exact cohort boundaries; and the distance between finding real cohort effects and reifying cohorts as natural kinds. I have read enough rationalist-blog discussion of social kinds and reification to want the satire to be fair to the strongest version of the cohort-effects view while still quietly dismantling it. Keep the irony deadpan throughout and never state the criticisms outright; a reader should come away seeing why generational essentialism is suspect without being told. Please write it in American English.
