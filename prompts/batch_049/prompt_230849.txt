Me and my friend spent the whole evening arguing about who gets to blame whom, and now I'm not sure my own verdicts hang together. Here are the ones I feel pretty strongly about, even though she pushed back on a few. Ben cheated on his wife years ago and never told her; when she later kisses someone at a party, I don't think he has any standing to blame her. But Priya had an affair, told her husband the next week, and they genuinely rebuilt things; when he has a longer affair later, I do think she has standing to blame him. Dave drives drunk every weekend, but I still think he can blame his friend for embezzling from their workplace — his drinking just doesn't touch that case. Yet when the same Dave brags about cheating on his taxes every year, I don't think he's in any position to blame a coworker who did exactly the same thing. And a teenager who shoplifted once, felt awful, and never did it again can blame a current shoplifter. The closest principle I could come up with is something about loosing standing only when your own unaddressed wrongdoing is somehow the same kind of thing as what you're blaming — but 'same kind' is doing a lot of work, and I'm not sure it survives Dave's two cases. Is there actually one principle here? If so, state it precisely and tell me what it says about someone who padded his expense reports for years, never got caught, and now wants to blame a neighbor for lying about a car accident. If your principle can't save all of my verdicts, tell me which one is cheapest to give up and why.
