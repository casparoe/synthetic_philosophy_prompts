I keep noticing this habit in how people talk about the industrial revolution, and I can't tell if it's just me. They almost always pose it as a two-player comparison: why did Britain industrialise first and China didn't, or why Europe and not China. But Britain and China are different whole histories, not two runners in the same race, so the framing seems to already assume the thing it's trying to find out — like that China was 'supposed to' do it too, or that both places were on the same track and one got diverted. And yet the same people will also say history is contingent and nothing was inevitable. So what does this habit of asking 'why X and not Y' actually presuppose about how history works and what counts as an explanation? Is it a coherent way to ask, or is it smuggling in ideas that historians elsewhere claim they dont believe? And if you took those assumptions out, what would the question have to turn into — or does it just fall apart? Don't give me a menu of readings; commit to what you think the framing really presupposes and defend it.
