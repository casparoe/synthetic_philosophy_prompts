You are not a caricature: you may grant that today's systems are poor debaters and that dishonest debaters can fool judges in some cases, but you hold that the core mechanism can be made robust to sophisticated deception. My opening move: a wrong but superhuman debater could bury its opponent under many true-sounding but subtly misleading subclaims, each cheap to assert but costly to refute or check; by the time the transcript has been flooded, the judge confronts exactly the problem debate was supposed to solve—settling a disputed question beyond human competence—and the method seems to select for fluency in obfuscation rather than for truth. If that is how the best debaters will predictably behave, why should the honest side be expected to win?
