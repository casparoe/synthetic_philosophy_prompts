For an upcoming issue of a metaphysics journal, you are facilitating an adversarial collaboration between two metaphysicians who have spent years talking past each other about grounding. One side—let the joint statement call them the Grounding Realists—holds that "in virtue of" and its cognates ("grounds," "obtains because") succeed in picking out a real, mind-independent, non-causal dependence relation, or a small systematic family of such relations, that carves metaphysical structure, backs true explanations, and gives fundamentality a worldly sense. The other side—let the statement call them the Grounding Deflationists—holds that grounding talk is indispensable but does all of its work by regimenting explanatory priority: it marks which facts get explained in terms of which, licenses the corresponding patterns of inference and theory-organization, and asks nothing more of the world; fundamentality is a status within that explanatory enterprise rather than a terminal node in a real dependence structure. Write the joint statement these two parties could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

The statement should contain:

(1) A numbered list of claims both sides accept about the relevant "in virtue of" claims, covering at least their factivity, their asymmetry and irreflexivity in the central cases, their hyperintensionality, and the shared rejection of any reduction to material conditionals or bare necessary covariation.

(2) A precise statement of the residual disagreement, with each side's own position phrased in wording that side would accept, using only the neutral labels above. This section should cover at least: whether there is a unified relation behind explanatory priority; whether there is a fact of the matter about ground over and above its explanatory role; what "fundamental" contributes; and how each side handles the question "what grounds the grounding facts?"

(3) A crux table. Each row should give a sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side, a concrete case both sides agree bears on that sub-question, and each side's prediction about that case recorded in advance.

I suspect the natural rows will involve the necessarily equivalent but directionally asymmetric pair of Socrates and his singleton, a case about constitution or coincidence such as the statue and the lump of clay, and a case concerning a non-well-founded descending chain of "because" relations. Replace any of these with a better row if a candidate turns out to be merely verbal, and close with a short note saying which parts of the standing debate the collaboration concluded were verbal and why.
