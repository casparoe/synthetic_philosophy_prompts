Write a deadpan internal memorandum from the Duty Manager of Hilbert's Hotel to all front-desk staff, circulated the morning after a night on which the hotel was fully occupied and several infinite parties arrived. The memorandum should read as a genuine hospitality-industry document: a To/From/Date/Re header, numbered clauses, a short "for the avoidance of doubt" section, and one quoted guest complaint followed by the manager's courteous reply. Aim for around 700 words, in British English.

The comedy must come from treating the mathematics of countable infinity as a routine operational matter, never from vague jokes about infinity merely being large. Every accommodation procedure the manager describes must be mathematically correct and specified precisely enough that a mathematician could check it, though it should remain in the register of hotel operations. The memo should cover, correctly and in the manager's unflappable voice:

(i) how a room is made available to a single late arrival when the hotel is fully occupied, with the correct instruction for each guest currently in room n;

(ii) how a coach carrying countably infinitely many new guests is accommodated, with the correct instruction for each existing guest and each new arrival;

(iii) how countably infinitely many such coaches are accommodated, with the correct diagonal enumeration; the manager may refer to it as a standard pairing of coach number and seat number, but the memo must state at least one concrete assignment, for example where passenger j of coach i goes once the existing guests have been moved;

(iv) why a coach carrying a continuum of guests cannot be accommodated — the one true capacity limit — with the manager's explanation resting on the uncountability of the would-be guests rather than on inconvenience.

Include, among the numbered clauses, a guest complaint from a visitor who insists the hotel must have a last room and threatens to walk down the corridor to find it, followed by the manager's courteous and entirely unhelpful response.

The primary target of the satire is the hospitality industry's euphemistic, can-do crisis language, which here treats an actual infinity as an ordinary staffing matter; the secondary target is the finitist impulse, which the manager regards as a guest-relations issue. Sustain the irony deadpan throughout: the manager never breaks character, never winks at the reader, and always stays within the competence of an unflappable, transfinite-aware duty manager. The humour should come from the tension between the mathematical content and the corporate tone, not from a narrator explaining the joke. No citations, footnotes, or named authorities: the mathematics should sound like capacity planning, not like a lecture, and the piece should be funny to a reader who knows Hilbert's paradox and readable to one who does not.
