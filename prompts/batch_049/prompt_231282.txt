I'm on my city's climate adaptation committee, and we have to choose between two 50-year infrastructure packages for the region. Package A is the cheap one: keep the gas plants running, patch the stormwater system, minimal upgrades, taxes stay flat and the budget balances now. Package B is expensive: aggressive transition off gas, rebuild the levees and the stormwater system, big upfront bond issue, and residents feel the costs for the next decade. The two paths only really diverge environmentally around the 2080s and after. But here's the wrinkle: population and settlement patterns will be systematically different under the two packages — who moves here, who stays, who is born when and under what economic conditions — so most of the actual individuals alive in 2080 would not be the same people across the two packages. The people stuck with the worse environment under A would mostly not have existed at all under B. So in a real sense choosing A makes no identifiable person worse off, even though the people who exist under A are worse off than the different people who would have existed under B. Our consultant keeps telling us this is the nonidentity problem and our cost-benefit model can't handle it. Another committee member thinks that's a philosopher's parlor trick and wants to just run willingness-to-pay numbers. I need to circulate a short decision memo before Thrusday: recommend A or B (or some modified version), give reasoning the committee and the city attorney can act on, deal seriously with the nonidentity worry instead of treating it as an abstraction, and include the strongest reason on the losing side.
