So last night I had this hours-long argument with a friend about whether astrology is science, and I realized we were replaying a debate that's been stuck for decades, and I want someone to diagnose the argument itself rather than take a side. She kept reaching for falsifiability: astrology, the way real people defend it, can't be pinned down and refuted, so it's not science. I kept saying that you can make almost anything look unfalsifiable if you watch people retreat to ad hoc excuses, and plenty of accepted science was historically unfalsifiable in practice too. Then it turned out we were really arguing about something else: whether 'science' picks out a distinctive method, or is just an honorific we attach to whatever kind of inquiry we happen to trust. Neither of us moved an inch, even though we both knew the moves. And as far as I can tell the actual literature has the same shape: Popper and falsificationism on one side, people who say the whole demarcation project should be abandoned on another, and various attempts to revive it with multi-criterion or context-dependent definitions in between. My hunch is that the impasse is definitly at least partly verbal: one side wants a descriptive account of what makes scientific institutions actually work, while the other wants a normative line about which claims deserve public trust or funding, and their using one word, 'science', for both jobs. But I don't trust that hunch, because it might just be a comfortable way to avoid taking the disagreement seriously. What I want is a diagnosis. What kind of disagreement is the demarcation problem, actually? Is it a factual disagreement about how inquiry progresses, a disagreement about standards of evidence or about which episodes count as data, a disagreement about bedrock intuitions that neither side can argue for, or are the parties literally answering different questions under one label? Walk through the main families of positions enough to diagnose the structure of the impasse, not to survey them, and then say what you think would actually count as progress given the diagnosis: a better criterion, a sharper question, an empirical finding, or an honest argument that it should never end. And please, no headings or bullet points or bold or tables, just continuous prose the way you'd write me an email.
