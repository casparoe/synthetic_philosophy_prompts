A long explainer I read last week puts the view roughly like this: Predictive processing says the brain is a prediction machine. Rather than passively building a picture from sensory input, the cortex runs a hierarchical generative model of the world, constantly sending top-down predictions about the next input, while bottom-up signals carry only the prediction error, the mismatch between what was expected and what arrives. Perception is the brain updating its model to minimise prediction error; action is the brain moving so its predictions come true. The free energy principle is meant to be the deeper justification: any self-organising system that persists against disorder has to be minimising free energy, and since free energy is an upper bound on surprise, the brain's core imperative is to keep itself in unsurprising states. That, the article says, is why we seek out predictable environments and find unexpected events aversive. On this story the brain is a Bayesian inference engine, roughly, and mental illness is largely a matter of bad priors or misweighted prediction errors.
