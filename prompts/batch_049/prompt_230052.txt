I am a high school student writing a paper on the ethics of the Second World War, and I want to use Christopher Nolan’s film Oppenheimer as a way into G. E. M. Anscombe’s argument about President Truman and the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki. As I understand it, when the University of Oxford planned to give Truman an honorary degree in the 1950s, Anscombe objected on the grounds that the bombings did not distinguish between combatants and noncombatants, and that the destruction of heavily populated cities was chosen as the very means by which surrender was to be produced. She treated that choice, not the general desire to end the war, as the central moral fact, and she thought it made the bombings murder rather than a legitimate act of war.

I want you to read the film through that lens for me. My worry is that the film centers Oppenheimer’s guilt so heavily that it might inadvertently change the subject Anscombe was interested in: the film spends much of its energy on Oppenheimer’s inner torment, while Truman appears mainly as a figure who does not share that torment. The Oval Office meeting in particular seems to invite the audience to treat Oppenheimer’s distress as the main moral response, while Truman’s detachment looks like a failure of feeling rather than the thing Anscombe actually attacked. I would like your reading to address whether the film dramatizes Anscombe’s argument, complicates it, or quietly resists it. In particular, does the film treat Oppenheimer’s retrospective anguish as a substitute for asking whether the decision itself was wrong? Would Anscombe have regarded that anguish as morally significant, or as a distraction from the question of what was chosen as the means? And does the film accidentally support her view by making the scientists the only ones who seem haunted, while the political decision recedes from view?

Please do not focus on the historical accuracy of the film. I want the philosophical structure of the scene and the film’s moral gaze, not a fact-check. In your answer, please say how confident you are in the claims you make about Anscombe’s actual position and about the film, and single out the point you are least sure of.
