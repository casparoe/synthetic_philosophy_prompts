I keep going back and forth about how spooked a scientific realist should be by underdetermination, and it would help to rank the different things people mean by that term, since they don't all cut the same way for a realist who wants to believe in the approximate truth of our best theories (including the unobservable bits). So: rank these three from most to least serious for that realist, with a sentence or two on why each lands where it does. By contrastive underdetermination I mean the idea that whenever you have a theory, there's some other theory out their that's incompatible with it but fits all the same evidence. By holistic underdetermination I mean the Quine-ish point that no individual hypothesis gets tested on its own, since theories face the evidence as whole packages. By transient underdetermination I mean the historical situation where several different theories fit the evidence available at the time, but later evidence ended up breaking the tie.
