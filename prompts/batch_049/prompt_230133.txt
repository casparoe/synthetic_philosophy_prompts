I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, so apologies if this isn't the kind of thing you normally get asked. I keep noticing a pattern at work and I don't have a word for it, and I'd rather not settle for a vague phrase like 'we rely on AI too much' — I want to know exactly what this thing is.

Eighteen months ago our team started using an AI coding assistant. At first everyone read its suggestions carefully. But the workflow settled into this pretty quickly: the assistant writes the diff, the test suite passes, the human reviewer checks it against a checklist (does it touch the database, is it too big, does it log anything) and merges. The PR description is usually also written by the assistant. Nobody is lying — it's just that nobody can honestly say what the change does beyond 'the tests pass and the AI says it's fine.'

Every individual step of that process seems sensible. Passing tests are real evidence that the change isn't obviously broken, and spending an hour unpicking what the model did on every small change would slow us down for no immediate benefit. But after a year and a bit, we've ended up with a codebase none of us understands end-to-end. When something breaks in an old component, the person who 'owned' it has moved teams, and the current owner can't reconstruct the original design decisions, because those were never in anyone's head — they were scattered through a departed dev's prompts, or in the model, or just in the fact that the tests passed. We only notice the understanding is gone at the exact moment we need it, like a muscle you don't notice shrnk until you try to lift something.

I saw a smaller version of the same shape with satnavs years ago: every choice to follow the route was individually sensible, but people ended up with no mental map of their own city, and discovered it only when the battery died. The AI case feels like that shape but worse, because the thing we've stopped being able to reconstruct isn't a city we've driven through — it's a system we ourselves wrote.

Is there a name for this general shape — individually sensible reliance on a helpful tool leading to a collective loss of the ability to function without it, where the loss goes unnoticed until the tool fails? And specifically for the coding-assistant version, where the thing being lost is understanding of an artefact the group itself produced? I'd also want to know where the line is between this and ordinary tool use: we all use compilers without missing machine code, and nobody treats that as a problem, so what makes the AI assistant different? If there's no established name, what would you call it, and how would you define it so that it doesn't sweep in compilers and libraries?

Oh, and answer in British English please.
