I was reading Pascal’s Pensées in a seminar on early modern political thought, and I keep going back and forth over how to interpret his remarks on justice and force. On one side, he seems to suggest that justice is nothing over and above what a given legal order can compel: because human beings disagree endlessly about what is truly just, they fall back on custom and power, and the stronger party determines what counts as lawful. On the other side, he also writes as if force unaccompanied by justice is a kind of tyranny, which implies that justice is not simply identical with the commands of the powerful; otherwise the contrast would make no sense.

Write an essay on this controversy. Weigh the arguments for reading Pascal as a proto-positivist or reductionist about justice, against the arguments for reading him as a critic of force who retains a justice-independent standard that political power always corrupts. Reach a conclusion and justify it; a qualified conclusion is acceptable, but the essay should not end by saying that both readings are equally plausible.
