Our group's ethics panel authorises computational projects that might create or affect artificial systems, and it has been explicit about its moral uncertainty. It gives 0.7 credence to the view that only persons matter in themselves and that present-day artificial systems are merely instruments (the Instrumental View, I), and 0.3 credence to the view that any system capable of pleasant or unpleasant experience matters equally, including possible digital minds (the Sentientist View, S). For each proposal it records how good the proposal is according to each theory on a normalised scale from -100 (the least choiceworthy option the theory recognises) to +100 (the most choiceworthy option the theory recognises). Its recorded decisions are as follows.

1. Document-summarisation tool. I: +40; S: +35. Authorised.
2. Simulated-agent optimisation. I: +30; S: -75. Declined.
3. Low-resolution neural-emulation diagnostic aid. I: +50; S: -55. Authorised.
4. Predictive mental-health chatbot. I: +45; S: -20. Authorised.

The panel now faces a proposal to run a billion-agent lifelong simulation for consumer-behaviour forecasting. I scores it +70: substantial forecasting and efficiency benefits, with no person-level harm. S scores it -95: a high probability of creating enormous amounts of enduring digital suffering.

What should the panel decide if it wants to be consistent with its own record? Which earlier case is the closest analogue? I can see at least two decision procedures that fit the record so far. One is expected choiceworthiness maximisation: multiply each theory's normalised score by the panel's credence in that theory and choose the proposal with the highest total. The other is a veto-then-maximise procedure: first reject any proposal that a theory in which the panel has non-trivial credence judges to be a grave wrong, then apply that same expected-choiceworthiness calculation to the survivors. I believe these two come apart on the new proposal. Say which way each procedure points, whether any further procedure fits the record just as well, which one the panel is implicitly running, and which further case would best settle which procedure is really doing the work.

Please write your answer in American English. Commit to a definite recommendation rather than only laying out the alternatives; give your reasons, and say what principle the panel would be adopting either way.
