For a graduate seminar in formal epistemology, I am preparing a unit on simplicity, prior probabilities, and Occam's razor. Before I settle on any particular account — Solomonoff induction, minimum description length, or something less formal — I want the complete specification of the job such an account must do. Write the desiderata: what an adequate theory of how simplicity bears on prior probability has to deliver. It should cover at least the following. First, the distinction between assigning high prior probability to simple hypotheses and explaining why simplicity should be expected to track truth, and whether one can be had without the other. Second, the fact that any measure of simplicity is defined relative to a language or reference machine, with the Kolmogorov invariance theorem giving only invariance up to an additive constant; say whether that constant-dependence is tolerable once the evidence is finite and the constant does not wash out. Third, the uncomputability of Kolmogorov complexity, and what a usable account must therefore say about idealization, approximation, or the price of demanding computability. Fourth, the cases the account must get right or must not be allowed to exclude, such as gerrymandered disjunctive hypotheses, hypotheses whose complexity is hidden in the coding scheme, and hypotheses that fit a finite sample perfectly but generalize poorly. Flag which requirements you regard as non-negotiable and which are optional or contested, and say whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable — that is the part I most want to see, since my suspicion is that every live account quietly sacrifices one. Then briefly score Solomonoff's mixture and a minimum-description-length account against the list. Finish by indicating how confident you are in the desiderata themselves and which single requirement or incompatibility claim you are least sure of.
