I have been reading the literature on finkish and masked dispositions, and it seems to me to push toward an eliminativist conclusion. The simple conditional analysis says that an object is disposed to M when S just in case, if it were to undergo S, it would M. Finkish and masked cases appear to refute that biconditional: a wire can be live even though, if touched, it would not conduct, because a fink would make it dead at the moment of contact; and a glass can be fragile even though, if struck, it would not break, because a foam mask prevents the breaking. Since the standard non-dispositional analysis of disposition ascriptions fails, I am inclined to infer that dispositions are not genuine properties of objects. Terms like "live," "fragile," and "poisonous" begin to look like loose summaries of categorical bases plus background conditions, and the honest project would be to eliminate them. But I cannot see how to carry out the elimination without losing explanatory content. What, on this picture, is the correct replacement for disposition talk in causal explanation and scientific description? And what is actually true of the fink-rigged wire or the masked poison, if there is no real disposition present?

As you answer, please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among philosophers working on dispositions, and avoid presenting a contested view as the consensus.
