"The machine lets you have whatever experiences you'd like while your body floats in a tank, and because you don't know you're in it, everything seems real. The argument is usually taken to challenge mental-state theories of well-being, especially hedonism. The idea is that if only your mental states mattered for how well your life goes, then a machine life with the same or better felt experiences would be just as good or better, so we should have no hesitation about plugging in. But most people wouldn't plug in, and Nozick takes that to show we care about actually being in contact with reality, not merely seeming to. A hedonist's strongest reply is that the hesitation has a lot of other possible sources: status quo bias, fear that the machine will malfunction, and scruples about leaving one's real friends and family—none of which need to be about our own well-being. A desire-satisfaction theorist, in contrast, should just say the machine satisfies all your desires, since you'll believe inside it that they've been fulfilled; so desire-satisfactionism actually recommends plugging in and the thought experiment does nothing against it. In the end the objection shows that authenticity—really doing the things and not just experiencing yourself doing them—is an objective element of well-being, which is why the correct theory has to be some kind of objective-list view."
