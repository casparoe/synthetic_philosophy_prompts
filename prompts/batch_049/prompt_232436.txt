A friend in my philosophy of mind course summarized Galen Strawson's Basic Argument for me like this: "Strawson showed that free will and moral responsibility are impossible whether or not determinism is true. To be responsible for what you do, you have to be responsible for the kind of person you are; to be responsible for that, you have to be responsible for how you became that person, and so on back to your genes and upbringing, which you never chose. Since nobody can be the cause of themselves, nobody can be morally responsible for anything. So nobody truly deserves praise, blame, or punishment, and we should stop being retributive and start treating wrongdoing the way we treat disease." I find the regress step persuasive, but I cannot see how it shows free will itself is impossible, and the final claim about handling wrongdoing like a disease feels like a leap. Which parts of this summary would Strawson accept, which are overstatements or conflations, and does the practical recommendation actually follow from the argument's accurate core?
