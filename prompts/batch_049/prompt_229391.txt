Longtime LessWrong/EA Forum reader here, trying to get my axiology to hang together before I write the big post. I'd like an audit, not a lecture. Please think like a moral philosopher who knows formal axiology and population ethics, and take some private time to brainstorm the possible pressure points before answering.

The package I currently endorse:

1. Negative utilitarian priority. Suffering is the fundamental unit of moral concern. Happiness is good but not symmetric with suffering: avoiding a given pain matters more than creating the equal-and-opposite pleasure, and the exchange is not one-for-one.

2. Lexical threshold. Below some severity of suffering, pleasure and pain are commensurable, and ordinary cost-benefit tradeoffs are fine. Above that severity, no amount of pleasure or flourishing — however many people get it, including future people — can outweigh one instance of that suffering.

3. Common currency. Pain and pleasure are nonetheless the same kind of quantity, measured in opposite directions on a single welfare scale. That shared scale is what makes expected-value reasoning and shut-up-and-multiply well-defined for me.

4. No ad hoc discontinuities. I normally regard a hard cutoff or arbitrary-looking discontinuity in a value function as a bug; if one is included, I want an error theory saying why it's not just gerrymandered to protect my intuitions.

5. Verdicts. I'd accept a treatment that gives people a bad headache as a side effect but on net produces a lot of happiness. I'd reject a treatment that inflicts five minutes of peak-level agony on one person however much happiness it creates. And I couldn't tell you exactly where, between a headache and peak agony, the threshold falls; I just think it's in there somewhere, vague but real.

Which of these actually conflict and wich only appear to? Please rank the tensions by severity, explain what the cheapest fix is for each, and end with which single revision would clean the package up most. Lead with the bottom line (a sentence or two on whether the set hangs together). Hard cap: 800 words.
