so I ended up as the person in my friend group who has to referee the moral arguments, and after a genuinely ugly secret-keeping fallout I tried to write down what I actually think about right and wrong instead of just doing my usual performance of thinking out loud. this is what I came up with, and I'd like an audit of whether it hangs together before I inflict it on anyone else.

here's the package. (1) I think an act is right just in case a fully informed, perfectly rational, totally non-self-deceived version of me—call her ideal-me—would want me to do it. (2) But in everyday life I basically never try to compute what ideal-me would say. I follow coarse rules like keep promises, don't lie, don't use people, because trying to run the ideal calculation in real time is exactly how I and everyone I know gives ourselves permission. (3) I do carve out one exception: in high-stakes cases or when two of my rules fight, I think I should stop and try to reason more like ideal-me rather than just applying whichever rule shouts loudest. (4) I also believe a moral theory has to be usable, at least in principle, by a person like me from inside a life like mine. If the thing that makes an act right is something I couldn't recognize even after honest effort, then calling it "right" is just laundering the verdict. (5) In the actual mess that started this, I landed hard on "keep the promise," even though I can easily imagine ideal-me knowing extra stuff and concluding that telling would prevent more pain. And I don't mean "it was right in principle but I was allowed to get it wrong"; I think keeping the promise was the right answer for me, full stop. My reason is that the only time I can get ideal-me to deliver the break-the-promise verdict is when I already want to break it, which makes me distrust the whole exercise in just those cases. (6) Finally, I'd be suspicious of any idealization that systematically reversed my settled convictions about promise-keeping or not using people; at that point I'd think the idealization was doing the work, not my actual ideals.

So the question is: does this set actually hang together? rank the tensions by severity, including the ones that only look like tensions and dissolve on inspection, and for each real one say what the least costly fix would be and what it sacrifices.
