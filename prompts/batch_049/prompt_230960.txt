I run people-ops at a worker co-op where we try to live by two rules at once: everyone gets final say over a few things that are purely theirs (their schedule, their workstation, whether they travel), and if every single person prefers X over Y, we go with X. Last quarter this produced a circular ranking twice, and the postmortems turned into arguments about who's rights got overridden. I gather this is basically Sen's liberal paradox, but for us the theorem isn't actionable as-is. Write me a reusable pre-vote screen the meeting chair can run in under 30 minutes to catch, before the vote, when individual decisiveness and unanimous preference are about to collide, using Sen's impossibility of a Paretian liberal but translated into plain questions — no formalism, no proof. The chair isn't a philosopher; the decisions recur weekly-ish; stakes are medium (schedules, pay, workspace); and whatever we land on has to be defensible to the whole co-op afterward.

Format: give me the procedure as a table with columns Step, Question to ask, What counts as a pass, What to do on a fail, Stop or continue, Judgment call (yes/no). Then apply it to two worked cases as JSON with fields case_name, setup, results (one entry per step), conflict_found, recommended_action, residual_judgment. Make one case a weekend on-call norm where the procedure tells us we have to override someone's personal call or drop the unanimity rule, and we'll hate both; in that case say explicitly what the checklist cannot settle for us.
