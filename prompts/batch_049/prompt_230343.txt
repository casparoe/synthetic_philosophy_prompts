Last night a friend and I spent an unreasonable amount of time arguing about Aunt Hillary, the ant colony from Hofstadter's Ant Fugue who is supposed to be a single conscious entity even though none of her constituent ants has any idea what the colony is doing. My friend's view is that the argument generalizes: if an ant colony can be a minded whole over and above its ants, then large organizations — corporations, markets, states — can likewise have literal beliefs, intentions, and experiences, and we ought to take that seriously rather than treating it as mere metaphor. I kept objecting that the analogy is doing too much work, because in the ant case someone still has to be there to interpret the colony-level patterns as messages or thoughts; without that interpreter, the coordinated behavior is just coordinated behavior, no more a mind than a traffic jam is. We ended up no closer to agreement than when we started.

I would like you to write a piece of sustained, deadpan satire that works through this exact disagreement rather than merely gesturing at it. Please write a mock judicial ruling, about 700 words, in which a judge must decide whether a corporation counts as having a mind under a vaguely worded statute and is thereby forced to take the ant colony argument literally. The ruling should be formally structured — a caption or headnote, findings of fact, reasoning, and an order — and the humor should come from getting the philosophy right: the levels-of-description problem, whether the messages an organization passes around ever form symbols in a single cognitive system, the suspicion that the only interpreter in the picture is us, and the gap between behaving as if one intends and intending. Aim the satire at the overconfident slide from distributed coordination to literal mindedness, not at the more modest idea that collectives can be usefully described as if they had minds. A philosopher of mind should find the satire fair rather than cheap, and a reader who has never heard of Aunt Hillary should still be able to follow it from the ruling itself.
