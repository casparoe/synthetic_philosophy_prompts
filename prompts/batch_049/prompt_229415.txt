Two camps disagree about how a rational agent should size a favorable bet that will be repeated many times with compounded returns. Camp 1 (expected utility) holds that the sole defensible norm is to maximize expected utility, so Kelly betting is not an independent principle: it is what expected utility maximization amounts to when the agent's utility over wealth happens to be logarithmic, and an agent with a different well-defined utility function should rationally deviate from the Kelly fraction. Camp 2 (geometric rationality) holds that in repeated multiplicative gambles, maximizing expected log wealth is not one preference among many but the rationality condition, since the growth-optimal strategy has an asymptotic property that no other fixed-fraction strategy has, whereas the rule 'maximize expected wealth' keeps prescribing a full-stake bet and thereby prescribes eventual ruin whenever the bet can lose everything.
