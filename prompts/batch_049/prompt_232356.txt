Draft the joint statement that a good-faith adversarial collaboration between a Cantorian and a finitist about the actual infinite could converge on, written so that both parties would sign it as an accurate record of what they agree on and of what still separates them.

The two positions to be represented are these. The Cantorian accepts completed infinite totalities: the natural numbers constitute a single finished object, one-to-one correspondence is the correct criterion for sameness of cardinality, and Hilbert's hotel is a coherent description of a completed infinite hotel, in which adding one guest, or countably many buses, or countably many buses each with countably many seats, leaves the hotel no more full than before, and Cantor's diagonal theorem demonstrates a cardinality strictly beyond the countable. The finitist denies completed infinite totalities and accepts the infinite only as potential: "infinite" qualifies a rule, operation, or process that can be extended without bound, but there is no completed set of natural numbers and no completed hotel; sameness-of-number claims are literally meaningful only for finite totalities, a bijection between two unending processes does not by itself license a sameness-of-size claim, and Hilbert's hotel is legitimate only when read as an open-ended family of finite hotels. For the purposes of this statement, the finitist is not a skeptic about ordinary finite arithmetic; the disagreement is only about the step from the finite, or the indefinitely extensible, to the completed infinite.

The document should contain the following.

1. A numbered section of agreed claims. They should be substantive rather than vacuous, and should include:
   (a) what both sides accept about finite hotels and about the exact finite moves used in the Hilbert story: the shift n to n+1, the doubling move n to 2n, and the pairing of two enumerations into one;
   (b) what both sides accept about the diagonal procedure on the finitistically unobjectionable reading;
   (c) the claims about Hilbert's hotel itself that both sides can sign, with the scope of each claim stated carefully enough that neither side is signing an equivocation, including a joint account of why the story by itself settles nothing between them.

2. A disagreements table. Each row should isolate one genuine disagreement rather than a mere difference of language. For each contested claim, give two formulations: the claim as the Cantorian would insist on wording it, and the claim as the finitist would insist on wording it, each formulation one its own side would sign.

3. A short list of cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, state precisely what the Cantorian would need to give up or establish, and what the finitist would need to give up or establish.

4. For each crux, the test, case, or argument that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. At minimum, include: the case of the even numbers paired with the naturals via n mapping to 2n, and whether it establishes equality of size or only that one unending process embeds in another; a Hilbert's-hotel scenario of your own construction that is designed to test something specific about the finitist's reading rather than merely re-asserting that completions are unacceptable; and a diagonal setup in which the two sides differ over whether the result shows an uncountable completed totality or only an endlessly extendable gap in any proposed enumeration.

The statement should read as a single document both camps could put their names to, not as a dialogue and not as an adjudication of which side is right.
