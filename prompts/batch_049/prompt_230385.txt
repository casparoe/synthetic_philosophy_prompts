I run a small reading group that's about to spend a few weeks on decision theory, and I want a handout for week one: a "which decision theory do you actually hold?" quiz. Eight to ten questions, each a short concrete scenario with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key that sorts people into evidential decision theory, causal decision theory, and updateless decision theory (you can split functional decision theory out as its own leaf only if your questions can actually separate it from UDT; otherwise merge them and say why). House rules: don't use the labels EDT, CDT, UDT, FDT, or the phrase "Newcomb's problem," and no question can just ask whether someone updates on evidence — people should be sorted by what they choose in cases, not by which label the like.

I especially want the updateless part to do real work, not just sit there as a weird footnote. So please build in at least three cases where the whole action is in the difference between choosing after you've seen local evidence and choosing a policy (or an algorithm) before you see it. Good candidates in my mind: a smoking-lesion-type case where someone is offered a drug before their genetic test results come back; Parfit's hitchhiker, described as a bare scenario without using that name; a counterfactual mugging; and a transparent-box variant of the standard predictor game where the forecast was made before the boxes were filled and you can see how much money is in each. For every question, give me a one-line note on which answer pattern pushes toward which position and why.

Then I'd like the scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the leaves, and a final paragraph flagging the pairs the quiz genuinely can't separate — e.g., places where CDT and UDT give the same answer because there's no precommitment wedge, as opposed to places where the quiz is just badly designed. Also flag any leaf you expect almost nobody to occupy, and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just underexplored.
