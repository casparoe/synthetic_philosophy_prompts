I've been argueing with a friend about the color phi illusion — red dot flashed on the left, green dot flashed on the right a little later, and people swear they see a single dot moving across that turns green roughly halfway, even though the green wasn't in the world yet at the point where the color change is seen. Dennett's way of setting this up is the Orwellian vs. Stalinesque distinction: either you really did consciously see a lone red dot on the left and your memory got rewritten after the second flash (Orwellian), or your whole experience was held back and stitched together only after the second flash arrived, so you never consciously saw a lone red dot at all (Stalinesque). The annoying thing is that in the standard display the two stories make exactly the same prediction about what you say afterwards.

I want a thought experiment that at least splits the naive versions of the two stories. Build me a case where an Orwellian account and a Stalinesque account of a temporal illusion make opposite predictions about a peripheral measure — not the subject's final retrospective verbal report — even though both accounts still predict the same final description of what was seen. I'd like the split to go in this direction: before the second flash appears, the Orwellian account says the subject can make a correct speeded judgment about some feature of the first flash (color, location, whether it happened), while the Stalinesque account says there is no conscious version of the first flash available yet, so the subject should be at chance or noticeably slower on the same judgment. Constraints: extend the color-phi display or a close cousin of it; no single-cell recordings, no exotic physics, no perfect timing beyond what a normal human lab can do; the measure has to be something a real person could actually produce — reaction time, forced choice under a deadline, confidence ratings, catching an omitted second flash, that sort of thing. Don't strawman either account: the Orwellian memory editor gets a realistic editing speed and the Stalinesque buffer gets a realistic delay, and the split has to survive those concessions.

Then lay out the scenario, walk through what each account says the subject is conscious of at each timepoint, what each predicts for the measure and why, and identify the single structural feature of the case that is doing the work. If that feature were removed, would the case collapse back into the original color phi where the two stories cannot be told apart? Finish with what a Dennett-style multiple-drafts theorist would say about whether the apparent divergence really settles anything, or what adjustment they'd make to absorb your result.
