I am a high school student writing a paper for a philosophy course on hedonism about well-being, and I would like help thinking through the heterogeneity problem. As I understand it, hedonism says that pleasure is the only thing that is good for a person in itself, but the things we call pleasures seem starkly different from one another: the pleasure of eating, the pleasure of solving a proof, the relief one feels when a pain stops, and the malicious pleasure of enjoying a rival's failure. If these have no single common feature, then it is unclear what the hedonist is claiming is the one good.

I have collected five candidate answers to the question of what, if anything, all pleasures have in common. I have described them in my own words, so please evaluate them as I have described them.

1. Felt-quality account: all pleasures share one distinctive raw feeling—a pleasant quality, or hedonic tone—that is present in every instance.
2. Functional account: a state counts as a pleasure because of what it does in a person's psychology; specifically, the person is disposed to want it to continue, or to seek more of it, for its own sake.
3. Attitudinal account: pleasures are not feelings but a type of attitude, namely enjoying or being pleased about some state of affairs; what they share is the attitude type rather than any accompanying feeling.
4. Representational account: every pleasure represents its object as good, or as an improvement in the person's condition relative to some baseline; what they share is that content.
5. Skeptical account: there is no single common feature; pleasure is a loose family-resemblance concept, and the heterogeneity problem shows that hedonism cannot give a unified account of its central value.

Please place these five accounts into a three-tier list—top, middle, and bottom—according to how promising they are as philosophical accounts of what all pleasures have in common. Ties are acceptable. For each account, give one sentence explaining why it lands where it does.

Then, in a short paragraph, apply this to a test I have been using: the pleasure of relief, when a pain or an anxiety stops, and the malicious pleasure of enjoying another person's misfortune. Which account handles both cases best, and is that the same account you placed in the top tier?
