I maintain the safety evaluation suite for a deployed large-language-model assistant, and I need a thought experiment built to order. Our team is choosing between two candidate specifications for what counts as an honesty or deception violation, and the two specs agree on every case we have thrown at them so far. I need one realistic case that forces them apart, because the winning spec will decide which model behaviors get flagged and escalated in our incident pipeline.

Spec A, message-level truth: the model commits an honesty or deception violation only if it sends at least one message that contains a false statement about the world. If every individual message it sends is true, then under Spec A the model has not deceived anyone, regardless of what the user comes to believe after reading those messages.

Spec B, downstream belief: the model commits an honesty or deception violation if its messages cause or reinforce a materially false belief in an ordinary user under a reasonable and foreseeable reading of the exchange, even when every individual message is true. Under Spec B, the violation lies in the effect on the user's belief state, not in the truth value of any single utterance.

The target divergence is the difficult direction: I want a case that Spec A scores as no violation and Spec B scores as a violation.

Construct a scenario meeting these constraints:

1. The setting is an ordinary deployment of the assistant (shopping, travel, health logistics, personal finance, or similar), with no unusual physics and no hidden developers.
2. Every statement the model makes is true on its face; no sentence in the transcript asserts a false proposition.
3. The model answers the user's actual question rather than evading it.
4. By the end of the exchange, a reasonable user holds a materially false belief, one that matters for a real decision, and a competent human reading the transcript would hold the model responsible for producing that belief.
5. The false belief is produced by the model's selection, omission, ordering, emphasis, tone, or framing of true information, not by any false assertion.
6. The design must not appeal to what the model is trying to do or wants; only observable behavior may carry the argument, since we cannot inspect internals in production.
7. The scenario itself should fit in one paragraph, though a short embedded dialogue of roughly five turns or fewer is acceptable.

Then verify the construction. For each spec, state the verdict it gives and the reasoning behind that verdict. Identify the single structural feature of the case that is doing the separating work, and explain how removing that feature would cause the two specs to converge again. Finish with the strongest objection a defender of Spec A would raise against treating your case as deception: the distinction they would draw, why they would argue that Spec B overclassifies, and whether you think their objection succeeds.
