I've spent too long in a LessWrong comments section arguing about Maxwell's demon, and I've realised people mostly arent disagreeing about the demon at all: they're disagreeing about whether information is physical, whether erasing a bit really has to dissipate heat, and whether the second law is a theorem about phase space or just very good statistics. I want to turn this into the kind of sorting instrument that actually gets people to reveal their commitments: a "which resolution of Maxwell's demon do you actually hold?" quiz for my blog. The audience is rationalist-adjacent readers, some with physics, some with only pop-science background, so the cases should do all the teaching. My one hard constraint is that nobody should be sorted by labels: no question may ask anything like "do you believe Landauer's principle?" or "are you a Bennettian?"; every question has to present a concrete variant of the demon and ask what happens, or why it fails, or what you'd bet on if the demon were built.

Please write the quiz: 9 questions, each built around a concrete case (Szilard's one-molecule engine with its trapdoor and piston; a finite-memory demon that must reset before the next cycle; a demon wired to an unbounded reversible tape that erases nothing; Feynman's ratchet-and-pawl rectifying Brownian motion; a quantum version where measuring the atom's position necessarily disturbs it; plus whichever other variants you need). Give each question two or three answer options, phrased so an intelligent reader can answer without knowing any of the named positions in advance. Then provide a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto at least five positions: (1) the erasure-cost view, (2) the measurement-cost view, (3) the statistical-fluctuation view, (4) the phase-space/Liouville view, and (5) the whole-system-entropy-accounting view. Build the logical space first: lay out the underlying questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then locate the five positions in that space, note any unoccupied combinations worth diagnosing as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any leaf-pairs your instrument cannot separate. Hard length cap: 1,000 words including headings and the scoring key; if you need to cut, trim the commentary on each position before trimming cases.
