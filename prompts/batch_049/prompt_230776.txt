I am a high school junior and the student representative on my school district's instructional technology committee. The superintendent has proposed that, beginning next fall, each school will be evaluated in part on the average number of minutes its students log in a reading-tracking app per month. Schools below a district target would lose some discretionary funding, and teachers at schools above the target would receive a small stipend. The stated purpose is to increase independent reading. Students self-report by starting and stopping a timer in the app and entering the titles they read.

I understand the general pattern often called Goodhart's law — roughly, that once a measure becomes a target, people find ways to move the measure without producing the outcome it was meant to track. I am not asking for a generic warning that metrics get gamed. I want your actual credence, a number between 0 and 1, that this particular policy will fail in the following precise sense: conditional on the policy being adopted as described, by the end of the second full year under the policy, at least half of the district-wide increase in average monthly logged minutes, relative to the last year before the policy was announced, will be attributable to gaming — for example, running the timer without reading, logging books that were skimmed or never opened, or teachers encouraging students to log minutes regardless of whether they read — rather than to a real increase in independent reading.

Please decompose your credence. Include at least the following four components, each with its own probability, a one-sentence justification, and a sentence on how it bears on your final number:
1. The probability that students and teachers will find ways to inflate the logged number that are low-cost, hard to verify, and do not require outright fabrication of the kind that would trigger review.
2. The probability that the incentives attached to the target are strong enough to motivate widespread inflation, rather than only a small number of students or teachers.
3. The probability that district oversight, audit procedures, or professional norms will fail to detect and correct the inflation within the two-year window.
4. The probability that, if widespread inflation occurs, it will account for at least half of the year-over-year increase in logged minutes, rather than mostly adding noise around a still-real reading gain.

Then explain how you combined these components into your final credence — by multiplication, by a weighted average, or by some other calculation — and identify which component you are least confident about. Also state what evidence or argument would shift your overall credence the most, and in which direction.

Finally, evaluate a safeguard the committee is debating: replace the single target with two metrics, the second being harder to game, such as short unannounced checks of reading comprehension. Give a separate probability that this two-metric design would keep the gaming share below half by the end of the second year, with one or two sentences of reasoning.

Format your answer as a JSON object with the following fields:

- "components": an array of objects, each with "component" (a short label), "probability" (a number between 0 and 1), "justification" (one sentence), and "bearing_on_conclusion" (one sentence on how this component feeds the overall number).
