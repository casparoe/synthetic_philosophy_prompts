I've recently become kind of the office crank on this, because my company keeps rolling out generational trainings and I keep arguing that generations like Boomers, Millennials, and Gen Z aren't real kinds. Not the way a biological species or maybe a social class is; they're loose demographic bins. The cutoff years are arbitrary, within-cohort variation swamps between-cohort differences, and most "Gen Z traits" turn out to be age effects or platform effects, not durable cohort effects—people are describing being 22, not being born in 2003. I've definately ended up fairly committed to that view, and now I want to stress-test it. What would actually count against it? What would a generation have to be like for me to be wrong—shared dispositions, a collective self-understanding, causal cohesion, something else? Which possible evidence or arguments would be decisive against the "not real kinds" view, and which would just be awkward? Do committed anti-realists about generations have to concede that some things count against them, or is the view too well insulated? And be straight with me: tell me how confident you are in what you're saying, and which point you're least sure of.
