I sit on a university academic integrity panel and we've hit a case that's dividing us. A student bought an essay from an essay mill and submitted it as his own. When he was called in, he signed a statement that said exactly this: 'I plagiarised the essay, but I don't believe I plagiarised the essay.' Half the panel reads that as a clear admission and wants the standard penalty applied; the other half says the second half of the sentence cancels the first, or at least shows he hasn't accepted responsibility, so we shouldn't treat it as an admission at all. We cant seem to agree on what the sentence even does. Could you draft a short memo for the panel laying out how we should read a statement of the form 'I did it, but I don't believe I did it'? In particular: does it count as an admission, and what should it tell us about the student's state of mind when we decide on a penalty? Give us a recommendation we can actually act on, and make sure the losing side gets a fair run.
