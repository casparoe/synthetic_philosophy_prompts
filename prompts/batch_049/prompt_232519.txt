A week ago I watched two friends argue about a public call-out, and I still cannot referee it. I want a point-by-point adjudication of the arguments they actually made, not a general essay on hypocrisy.

Background: a mutual acquaintance, Daniel, published a detailed, well-sourced post accusing another founder, Leo, of inflating traction metrics in an investor pitch. The facts about Leo are not in dispute, and neither side defended Leo. What made the argument heated is that Daniel did substantially the same thing at his previous company six years ago and has never publicly acknowledged it. So the fight was not about whether Leo did wrong; it was about whether Daniel was in a position to be the person saying so.

As best I can reconstruct it, the exchange went like this:

(1) Mara said the post is true, detailed, and useful, and that "you did the same thing once" is just a tu quoque — it attacks the source, not the content. If a liar shouts that the building is on fire, the building is still on fire.

(2) Tomas replied that Mara is right about the truth of the charge, but nobody had denied the truth of the charge. The question is whether Daniel has standing to make the accusation. Truth and standing are different dimensions: a statement can be true and still be the wrong act for this speaker. A pickpocket can be correct that another person pickpockets, yet the other person can reasonably say, "I'll take that reproach from anyone but you."

(3) Mara said "standing" is a label, not a reason, and the clean-hands rule has perverse consequences. Daniel knows the pressures from the inside; ex-fraudsters are often the best investigators of fraud. If only the uncompromised may call out wrongdoing, then the more compromised a scene becomes, the smaller the pool of legitimate critics becomes — exactly backward.

(4) Tomas said Mara was running reliability and standing together. Daniel's history makes his testimony more credible; that is a reason to read his evidence carefully, not a reason to let him deliver blame. A former embezzler can give the auditors a true report; he cannot step into the prosecutor's chair. Blame is a demand for accountability, and Daniel would be making that demand from a position he forfeited through his own unacknowledged conduct.

(5) Mara said the residual complaint, if there is one, must be hypocrisy: readers will interpret the post as evidence that Daniel would never do such a thing, and that inference is false. Hypocrisy is fixed by disclosure, not silence. Daniel should state plainly that he did much the same thing once, and then make the accusation. The deception complaint dissolves, and what remains is a true accusation — which is all she was defending.

(6) Tomas said disclosure handles the hypocrisy layer, but a person can be a disclosed former wrongdoer and still lack standing. Leo's objection is not only "you are hiding your own record"; it is "you are demanding accountability from me without ever having submitted to accountability for the same failure yourself." So the unresolved question is whether blame is just true information plus disclosure, or whether it is a further act with preconditions of its own.

They asked me to referee and I couldn't. I would rather not get a both-sides-are-partly-right payoff; I want the subclaims separated and judged. Properly steelman each side in the first two sections, and do not let the move-by-move ratings lean on the weak version of either side. When "standing" or "blame" is doing load-bearing work, say explicitly what work it is doing and whether that work is legitimate.

Please structure your response in the following sections, in order, with roughly these lengths:

- The case for Mara (about 150 words)
- The case for Tomas (about 150 words)
- Move-by-move adjudication (about 500 words): take moves (1) through (6) in turn, rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right on a five-point scale, and split a move into subclaims if it is right about one thing and wrong about another.
- What neither side isolated (about 200 words): the distinction or consideration that most cleanly breaks the stalemate.
- My verdict (about 150 words): which side is more right overall, and on exactly which point the dispute turns.
