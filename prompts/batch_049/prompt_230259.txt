I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, so excuse me if I mangle the terms. I've been reading the pop-science version of memetics—culture evolves by variation, selection, and inheritance, and a meme is like a gene. I was arguing with a friend about whether that's just a metaphor or an actual theory. Then I hit this in Darwin's Descent of Man (preface to the second edition, 1874):

"I may take this opportunity of remarking that my critics frequently assume that I attribute all changes of corporeal structure and mental power exclusively to the natural selection of such variations as are often called spontaneous; whereas, even in the first edition of the ‘Origin of Species,’ I distinctly stated that great weight must be attributed to the inherited effects of use and disuse, with respect both to the body and mind. I also attributed some amount of modification to the direct and prolonged action of changed conditions of life."

So now I'm stuck: Darwin himself is pushing back on the idea that natural selection is the whole story. If the meme-gene analogy is supposed to inherit its machinery from Darwinism, how far does it actually hold once you add those caveats back in? Audit the analogy. What maps to what (meme/gene, phenotype?, fitness?, environment?, use/disuse?), where the mapping breaks down, what the gene side quietly imports that the meme side lacks, and whether the conclusion people usually want—that cultural change is a selection process—survives. Also tell me if there's a better source-domain analogy.
