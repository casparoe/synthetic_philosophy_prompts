I am a novelist, currently researching a historical novel set during a nineteenth-century national revival, and I have come to accept the constructivist or modernist account of nations. On this view, the nation as we now understand it — a bounded people defined by a shared history, language, and set of symbols, and thought to be entitled to self-rule — is not the ancient, organic thing it presents itself as. It is a comparatively recent artefact, assembled over the past few centuries by states, intellectuals, and political movements who selected and refashioned folk materials into flags, anthems, costumes, standardised languages, invented traditions, and usable pasts; print capitalism and mass schooling then allowed millions of strangers to picture themselves as one community. What we call national memory is, to a striking degree, retrospective invention.

I want to know how exposed this position is. Tell me what would count against it. I do not mean merely the standard objections its critics already rehearse; I want a map of its vulnerability. Which observations, arguments, intuitions, possible cases, or findings from adjoining fields would have to be true for the constructivist picture to be wrong or importantly incomplete? I would want to hear, for instance, about national sentiments that demonstrably predate the nineteenth century; about continuities in language, religion, kinship, or political institutions that the modernist story treats too casually; about cases where a collective identity persists without the modern machinery of print and schooling; and about what the constructivist account predicts that its rivals do not. Rank the defeaters from decisive to merely awkward, and say which of them a committed constructivist would themselves concede as counting against the view. Then tell me honestly whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability through the habit of explaining every counterexample as itself a construction, and, if it has, whether that is a problem for a view of this kind. Finally, say which of these considerations would be most likely to change my mind if it were established, and where the constructivist's own reasoning is most likely to fail.

Please write in British English.
