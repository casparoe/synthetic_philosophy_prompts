I chair the editorial-standards board of a public television station. We vote on Friday whether to broadcast a submitted six-part documentary series, Before the Builders, which argues that monuments at Giza, Tiahuanaco, and Göbekli Tepe are several millennia older than mainstream archaeology allows and were built by a lost maritime civilization. The program is not the work of obvious cranks: its presenter holds a doctorate in geology, it cites peer-reviewed papers on weathering and archaeoastronomy, and several of its local observations are the kind mainstream specialists dispute among themselves. What the specialists reject is the global conclusion and the way the series assembles the evidence. The producers argue that a public broadcaster should let viewers weigh contested evidence for themselves, that dismissing the series as pseudohistory is an ad hominem, and that archaeology has overturned its own consensus before. The board is split three ways: one faction would decline outright on the ground that airing is platforming pseudohistory; a second would air the series exactly as submitted; a third would air it only with a framing overlay and a companion program. Draft the short written recommendation I'll circulate before Friday's vote. It must recommend one concrete course of action with a time frame; it must engage the strongest version of the two losing positions rather than caricatures; it must say whether the board should evaluate individual claims, methods, or the research program as a whole, and what turns on that choice; and it should close with a general criterion we can apply to future submissions of this kind so the decision is not ad hoc.
