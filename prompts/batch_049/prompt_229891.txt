Write a satirical mock memorandum, around 500 words, from a university research ethics committee that has decided to settle a long-running internal dispute by adopting the asymmetry Nozick floats between the moral treatment of animals and persons: roughly, utilitarianism for animals, and Kantian side constraints for persons. The memo should be deadpan and bureaucratic, and the humor should come from operationalizing that asymmetry rather than from generic committee-bureaucracy jokes. For instance, the committee should be entirely comfortable authorizing the sacrifice of any number of mice when the expected aggregate welfare gain is positive, yet categorically refuse to use a single graduate student merely as a means, even when doing so would spare thousands of mice. A reader who knows Nozick's view that persons are inviolable while animals may be treated as replaceable welfare units should find the parody fair; a reader who has never encountered the view should still be able to follow both the policy and the absurdity it produces.
