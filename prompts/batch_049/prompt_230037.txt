Before I read any more defences or critiques of lexical threshold negative utilitarianism, I want the standard of assessment fixed first. Take the position under evaluation to be LTNU: the claim that once suffering crosses some intensity threshold, no quantity of milder pleasure, and no quantity of relief from milder suffering, can outweigh or compensate a single instance of it. Produce the desiderata that an axiology of severe suffering must meet. For each requirement give the case, dilemma, or result that forces it, say whether it is negotiable or non-negotiable for a view that wants LTNU to be credible, and flag any pairs of requirements that look as if they cannot be jointly satisfied. Cover at least: how the view handles intensity versus duration and many sub-threshold pains piled against one threshold-crossing pain; whether the threshold can be located non-arbitrarily; what the view must assume about the commensurability of pleasure and pain and whether welfare is one scale or two; the difference between the badness of a state and a prohibition on inflicting it; aggregation within a single life versus across persons; and empirical constraints from the measurement and adaptation of pain. Then run classical hedonistic utilitarianism, plain negative utilitarianism, prioritarianism, and a rights-based anti-torture view through the list briefly, and identify the single requirement that excludes the most candidates. Use a table for the desiderata if it helps. Write in British English. End with a final line in the form \answer{...}, where ... is the short name of the single most exclusionary requirement.
