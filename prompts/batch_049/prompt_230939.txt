I've been chewing on the thermodynamic arrow of time debate after years of reading LessWrong-style posts and the odd physics blog, and I can't shake the feeling that the whole thing has been stuck for decades for a reason nobody names outright. One camp says the arrow is basically entropy increasing toward the future plus the past hypothesis, i.e. the early universe just had freakishly low entropy. The other camp says that still doesn't give you an arrow, because you've smuggled in a time-asymmetric boundary condition and still can't say why the low-entropy end is the *past* end rather than a low-entropy end in both directions. Everyone's working with the same equations and the same observations, and yet nobody moves. My working theory is that this isn't really about physics at all, its about what counts as explaining a temporal asymmetry — like the two sides are answering different questions under one name, with one side wanting a dynamical mechanism and the other side satisfied with a global boundary condition plus statistics.

Can you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is? Is there a hidden empirical question about the early universe doing the work, a difference about explanatory standards, or bedrock intuitions about what time is such that neither side can argue for them? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a new observation, a theorem, a sharper distinction, or just admitting the question was ill-posed to begin with?
