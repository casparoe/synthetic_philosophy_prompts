I've spend most of my retirement reading philosophy of time, after a long career in physics, and I keep coming back to the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe because it has the same shape as a derivation with a sign error I can't locate: the conclusion is hard to swallow, but every step looks fine. As I reconstruct it, the argument runs:

(1) In special relativity, simultaneity is frame-dependent: for any two spacelike-separated events, there is an inertial frame in which they are simultaneous.

(2) Let H be me finishing this sentence, right here and right now, and let E be an event on a planet a thousand light-years away that, in my rest frame, lies one day in the future of H. H and E are spacelike seperated.

(3) So by (1), there is an inertial frame in which H and E are simultaneous; a spaceship passing Earth at the right velocity would be just such a frame.

(4) For any observer, what really exists now is whatever is simultaneous with them in their frame — in other words, 'real now' just means 'simultaneous with the observer.'

(5) Therefore, in the ship's frame, E really exists right now.

(6) No inertial frame is physically privileged over another, so if E really exists now in the ship's frame, then E really exists now, full stop.

(7) But E was in my future; so part of the future is already real.

(8) And if part of the future is already real, there's no principled place to stop: the whole four-dimensional block is equally real, becoming is an artifact of frame choice, and the block universe is forced on us.

I'd rather keep special relativity and reject the block universe, if that's even coherent, but I can't see which step to give up without paying what looks like a terrible price: one exit smells like reinstating a privileged 'absolute now' through the back door, and the other makes 'real' so relativized that I'm not sure the word is doing any work anymore. Please take the role of a professional philosopher of physics and think through this slowly. Before committing to a diagnosis, brainstorm the different ways 'real now' could be read, then walk through the numbered argument under each reading and tell me exactly which step you'd reject, why it looked fine, and what the major positions in the philosophy of time have to give up to reject it. I'd also like an honest answer about whether the argument actually forces as much as its last step claims.
