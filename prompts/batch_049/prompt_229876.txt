For a seventy-five-minute workshop of roughly sixteen undergraduates who have taken one semester of microeconomics and can work with payoff matrices and pure-strategy dominance, design a staged classroom game that makes them feel why mixed strategies have to be admitted into iterated elimination of dominated strategies. The first stage should be a warm-up game in which the group successfully tidies a matrix by deleting strategies that are strictly dominated by other pure strategies. The second stage should confront them with a game where that method stalls: no strategy is strictly dominated by any single pure strategy, yet one strategy is strictly dominated by a mixture of other strategies, so iterated elimination should continue after all. The rules should introduce dice or cards at the moment participants need them, so the elimination is discovered in play rather than announced; use small real stakes and include the full payoff matrices, matching rules, and a timeline. The debrief should map what happened onto the formal ideas—that a strategy can be strictly dominated by a properly mixed strategy even when no pure strategy does the job, that in a two-player game a strategy is strictly dominated in this sense exactly when it is never a best response to any belief about the opponent, and that iterated elimination with mixed dominance is meant to capture what survives common knowledge of rationality. Finally, say what predictably goes wrong when the activity is run and how to handle it, and be explicit about which aspect of the idea the game cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply.
