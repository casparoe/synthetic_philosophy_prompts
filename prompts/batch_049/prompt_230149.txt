Retired geochemist here — spent my career doing isotope work and only started reading philosophy after I retired. I've been going through Hume, and I cant shake the feeling that the no-free-lunch theorems from machine learning are basically what he was trying to say about induction, just cleaned up mathematically. The theorems say that averaged over all possible problems, every learning algorithm performs exactly as well as every other; you only beat the average by building in assumptions about which problems are actually likely. So here's the task. Write me a short essay predicting what Hume would have made of these theorems if they'd been around in his day. Would he see them as a formal vindication of his skepticism about induction, or would he object to the setup — for instance, to the uniform prior over all possible worlds? And would they change his account of custom and habit, or just sharpen it? Reason it through carefully, and don't worry about citing anything.
