I need a thought experiment for a teaching handout on artifact identity, so the case has to generate the verdicts instead of just stipulating them — no fair changing what counts as the same ship halfway through. Take the Ship of Theseus. In the standard setup, continuity of form and continuity of overlapping matter both pick the continuously repaired ship; I want you to build a version where a third criterion, continuity of function (understood roughly as: the thing goes on playing the social role the original actually played), picks the ship rebuilt from the discarded planks instead. Constraints: ordinary materials, no supernatural or sci-fi elements, the rebuilt ship can't be assembled until after the repaired ship is already in service, the discarded planks must never compose a working, floating ship during the transition, and the function verdict has to be earned by what the original ship was for, not by slapping a label on one of the two later ships. Give me: The scenario (one paragraph); Why form continuity picks the repaired ship (2–3 sentences); Why overlap-matter continuity picks the repaired ship (2–3 sentences); Why function continuity picks the rebuilt ship (2–3 sentences); The load-bearing feature (one sentence); What happens if you remove that feature (1–2 sentences); How a defender of the losing ship would attack the case (1–2 sentences).
