I've been building a small group-decision app in my spare time (where to eat, how to split a vacation cottage, that kind of thing), and I went down a rabbit hole on Sen's liberal paradox. As I understand it: you want a voting rule that respects two totally reasonable principles. First, Pareto—if everyone prefers A over B, the group should prefer A over B. Second, minimal liberty—each person has some little personal sphere (what they read, what they wear, whether they take the job) where their own preference should win even if everybody else disagrees. The paradox is that these can't coexist: there are preference setups where the rule cycles A > B > C > A, so it never settles on a coherent group ranking. I get the mechanics, but I can't tell which principal is doing the actual damage.

Write a short dialogue between two people designing such a rule who both understand the paradox but disagree about which principle to give up. One is the liberty-first person: private spheres are side constraints, and letting unanimous preferences override them is just a polite form of meddling. The other is the consensus-first person: unanimity is the only non-arbitrary anchor a group has, and the 'personal sphere' idea is way too fuzzy to build an algorithm on (plus why should one person's veto beat something literally everyone wants?). Make them genuinely respond to each other's points instead of talking past each other, and keep both of them sharp—no strawmen. It's fine if they don't fully resolve it.
