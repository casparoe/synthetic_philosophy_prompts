I run a reading group that's been arguing for months about whether "left" and "right" are real or just usefull fictions, and I'd like you to write the document that finally gets the two main camps to say something constructive together. One member — call him the Realist — thinks the left–right axis tracks something real: two fairly stable, coherent bundles of values and interests, roughly the idea that the left clusters around equality, openness, and universalizing sympathy, while the right clusters around hierarchy, tradition, and closure, and that people's positions on apparently unrelated issues really do hang together because of these underlying dispositions. The other member — call her the Nominalist — thinks this is reifying a team label. On her view we learned to call a bunch of historically contingent coalitions "left" and "right," and then went looking for a deep essence that isn't there. The cluster, she says, is mostly a coordination device: the appearance of coherence is maintained by sorting, party discipline, and out-group signaling rather than by any shared moral or psychological core.

Your task is to draft the joint statement these two could actually sign after an adversarial collaboration. Please format it as: (1) a numbered list of substantive claims both accept, without weasel wording; (2) a table of their remaining disagreements, phrased so each would accept the wording of their own position while finding the other side's statement at least fair; (3) a list of the genuine cruxes — the sub-questions that, if answered, would actually shift at least one of them; and (4) for each crux, a concrete piece or kind of evidence both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Then add a closing section called something like "What would change our minds," where each side says in a sentence or two what argument or evidence would make them substantially revise their view. Finally, include a brief note from you, the drafter, on which formulations you're least confident about and where the two sides might still be talking past each other.

I'd like you to play the role of a professional political philosopher who works on conceptual engineering and the structure of political disagreement. Think hard about how to state the two positions before drafting — maybe start by brainstorming three candidate formulations of the central disagreement and choosing the one that is neither vacuous nor slanted. One constraint: the Realist doesn't have to defend that every leftist is open or every right-winger traditional, only that the clustering is real and explanatory. The Nominalist also doesn't get to just say "people are complicated"; she needs a positive story for why a single left–right dimension looks so stable. Write the whole thing in a calm, mutally acceptable register.
