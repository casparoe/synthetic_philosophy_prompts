At our philosophy of mind reading group last week two people got into it about the color phi thing, where a red dot flashes on the left and then a green dot flashes on the right with a small gap, and subjects report seeing one dot that moves across and changes color about halfway, not two seperate flashes. The live question is whether the brain edits the story before the experience reaches consciousness (Stalinesque: the red-on-the-left stage never actually becomes conscious; the only conscious experience is the already-moving, already-color-changing dot) or whether there is first a brief conscious experience of the red dot on the left and then the memory/account gets rewritten after the fact (Orwellian, like the Ministry of Truth retroactively changing the record).

The argument:

(1) Ben said these are just two different hypotheses about the subject's phenomenal timeline: the Orwellian version includes a conscious slice of red-on-the-left that gets overwritten, and the Stalinesque version says that slice never happens. So of course they're different claims.

(2) Maya said that's a nice story but they're empirically indistinguishable: the subject's report, later memory, and behavior come out exactly the same either way, so any fact about when the red was actually conscious is invisible to every possible test. She said the distinction is a difference that makes no difference, and that the whole debate dissolves once you stop asking the bad question.

(3) Ben came back with: that's just verificationism. We can't observe the exact moment someone falls asleep either, but there's still a fact of the matter about it. And these two accounts ascribe different histories to the subject: one has him consciously experience a flash that is later edited out of the record; the other doesn't. That's a real difference even if we can't detect it.

(4) Maya replied that the Orwellian reading only looks different if you picture consciousness as a theater where events come on stage and then a censor re-tapes the footage afterward. Drop that picture, and "was it conscious before or after the editing" may not have a determinate answer, because the line between the experience itself and the memory of it is exactly what's in dispute. So she wasn't saying Orwellian is false—she was saying the question has no content.
