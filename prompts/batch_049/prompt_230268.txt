I'm grading a take-home question for an intro metaphysics class and I'd like a second set of eyes on one students answer before I put a number on it. The question I set was:

"Do Thomson's lamp cases show that supertasks (completing infinitely many tasks in a finite time) are impossible? Reconstruct Thomson's argument, give the best reply you know, and say what you think actually follows."

The student wrote:

Thomson imagines a lamp that starts on and is switched off after half a minute, on after another quarter-minute, off after an eighth, and so on, so that by the one-minute mark infinitely many switches have supposedly been made. Thomson says the lamp has to end up either on or off. But it can't end up on, because every time it's switched on it gets switched off later; and it can't end up off, because every off-switch is followed by an on-switch. So he concludes the lamp can't be either on or off, which is a contradiction, and he takes this to show supertasks are impossible. Benacerraf's reply is that there's no real contradiction: the switch-times converge on the one-minute mark, so by the same reasoning we use for limits in calculus, the lamp's final state is the limit of the on/off sequence, which is neither on nor off. The final state is just indeterminate rather than contradictory. This shows Thomson's lamp and similar supertasks are at least logically possible, even if we couldn't physically build one.

Can you give this a grade and the specific comments that justify it? I want point-by-point feedback — what's right, where it goes wrong, and whether the wrong bits are majors or minors — not just an overall impression.
