I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, with a beat that includes pseudoscience and the public understanding of evidence, and I have been asked to set the final take-home essay for an upper-level seminar on pseudohistory. Please draft one essay question, a model answer, and a marking scheme. The question should ask students to compare two of three cases: the claim that Atlantis was a real advanced civilization whose memory was transmitted to later cultures; ancient-astronaut hypotheses that explain monumental sites and texts as records of extraterrestrial contact; and Anatoly Fomenko's New Chronology, which holds that events conventionally assigned to Greece, Rome, and Egypt occurred over a thousand years later and that the written historical record effectively compresses into the medieval period. The question must force students to do more than catalogue errors: they must state, defend, and apply a demarcation criterion for historical inquiry, and they must apply it to a short hard case, specified in the question, that shares structural features with the chosen cases but that a careful historian would regard as legitimate revisionism rather than pseudohistory. Give a model answer and a marking scheme with five or six must-hit points, the marks each is worth out of 20, and a one-line note on the underlying confusion behind each common way students fall short. Time and length: take-home, 72 hours, roughly 1,200 words.
