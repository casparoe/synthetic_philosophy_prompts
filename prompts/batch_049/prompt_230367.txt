so I keep going back and forth on something in the free will literature. Dennett has this line that we dont actually want libertarian free will; what we want are the ordinary competences—self-control, responsiveness to reasons, the ability to revise long-term plans, and so on. Frankfurt, by contrast, seems to say freedom of the will is mostly about wholeheartedly identifying with the will you already have, not about having a bunch of counterfactual alternatives open. These two views often get treated as rival answers to the same question, but to me they feel like answers to different questions. Compare them: are Dennett's varieties of free will worth wanting and Frankfurt's wholehearted identification genuinely competing accounts of what we care about, or are they talking past each other? And if you had to pick one as the better account of why ordinary people care about free will, which would you pick and why?
