Yesterday I spent most of the evening arguing with a friend about money-pump arguments for the transitivity of preference. She thinks the standard diachronic argument proves much less than people usually claim: if an agent prefers A to B, B to C, and C to A, a trader can march the agent through a cycle of trades and leave them with their original option minus a fee, but my friend insists that only shows the agent can be exploited, not that any particular choice was irrational, and certainly not that the agent's preferences were incoherent. I kept wanting to say that something deeper has gone wrong, but I couldn't make the difference vivid, and I suspect my students would have the same difficulty.

I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on decision theory and diachronic rationality, with fourteen students and a seventy-five-minute meeting, and I want a structured classroom activity rather than a lecture. Design a game with tokens and small real stakes, for example candy or a few points, in which participants first form a cycle of pairwise preferences through a series of forced choices or assigned payoff tables, and then discover, by trading with a designated pump role, that their own choices suffice to turn the cycle into a sure net loss. The participants should not be told in advance that the target is the money-pump argument, and the debrief should come only after the loss has actually been felt. Give me concrete rules and roles, a timeline for the seventy-five minutes, and a debrief that maps what happened in the game onto the structure of the money-pump argument and that presses my friend's objection: the debrief should help students separate what the game shows (exploitability) from what it may not show (irrationality, or incoherence of preference). Also tell me what will predictably go wrong when I run this, for example participants refusing to trade exactly when the pump becomes obvious or treating the whole thing as a joke, and how to handle those failures, and close with an honest note on which feature of the philosophical argument the activity cannot reproduce and what I will have to supply myself in discussion.
