I'm a philosophy of science PhD student, and my advisor and I keep arguing about counterfactual vs interventionist theories of causation. Her line is that interventions are just counterfactuals with a fancier antecedent (some exogenous intervention variable), so interventionism is at most a notational reformulation of standard counterfactual accounts once you add the usual epicycles. I dont buy it; I think the intervention notion does real work. My credence that interventionism is a substantive rival—meaning there are causal claims where the most charitable versions of each theory actually disagree in truth value, and the interventionist verdict is generally the one to keep—is around 0.7; hers is around 0.3. Give me your own number on that proposition, but dont just give a number: decompose it. In particular I'd want your probabilities for (i) whether 'intervention' can be spelled out without circular use of causal concepts, (ii) whether standard counterfactual theories really handle late preemption and overdetermination without quietly sneaking in intervention-like restrictions, (iii) whether the apparent divergences between the two are substantive rather than just different ways of describing the same extension, plus anything else that's load-bearing. Then say how those combine into your credence, which sub-question the 0.7/0.3 gap most likely lives in, and what single argument or case would move your number the most.
