At a game night last week, a friend named Milo was offering a coin game for a $1 entry fee. The rules: he flips a fair coin until it comes up heads. If the first heads is on the nth flip, then for odd n he pays you 2^n/n dollars, and for even n you pay him 2^n/n dollars. Two people there, Dana and Ravi, got into a long back-and-forth about whether it was worth paying the dollar.

Dana said: (1) "Just multiply each outcome by its probability. The chance the first heads is on flip n is 1/2^n, and the payoff is plus or minus 2^n/n, so the powers of two cancel and you're left with 1 - 1/2 + 1/3 - 1/4 + ..., which sums to ln 2, about 69 cents. Milo's charging $1, so it's a bad deal by about 31 cents in expectation." Ravi said: (2) "That series is only conditionally convergent. By Riemann's rearrangement theorem you can reorder a conditionally convergent series to converge to any number you like, or to blow up. So 'the' expectation isn't a fact about the game; it's a artifact of how you happened to list the outcomes. If the number changes depending on the list order, then the game has no well-defined expected value at all, and Dana can't say $1 is too much." Dana replied: (3) "The order wasn't arbitrary; the rules themselves produce it. The game goes flip one, flip two, flip three, and the truncated version where Milo just stops after N flips has expected value equal to the first N terms, and those partial sums converge to 69 cents. That's the natural operational reading, and you'd need a real reason to throw it away." Ravi answered: (4) "The natural reading is only natural because of how we labelled the flips. If someone wrote the same payoff table with all the winning outcomes listed first and all the losing ones after, the sum would zoom of to infinity. A fair price can't depend on typography, so there's no fair price here—not 69 cents, not $1, not anything." I couldn't tell who had the better of it. Could you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right about which specific point, who is more right overall, and what either of them might be missing? And could you say how confident you are in the overall verdict, and single out the point you're least sure about?
