I keep tripping over the same puzzle when people argue about whether someone's inaction was a cause, and I don't know wether there's a term for it. A lifeguard and a tourist both watch the same swimmer drown and neither moves; everyone says the lifeguard caused the death by not acting, but nobody says the tourist caused it. Both did exactly the same nothing, so the difference can't be in the non-action itself — it's in some background expectation about what each was supposed to do. But then I noticed that expectation can be changed while the non-action and outcome stay fixed: a doctor who doesn't give a drug is said to have caused a death when the drug is standard and almost always works, but not when the drug is experimental and almost never works. Same failure, same outcome, different causal verdict. I'm less after a label than an account: is the norm or comparison case doing real causal work, or is it only doing blame work? Should the two doctor cases really differ causally, or is that ordinary speech being sloppy? And please tell me how confident you are in each part, and which claim you're least sure of.
