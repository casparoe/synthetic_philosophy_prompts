I'm one of five people on our county's civil service commission, and we're the panel that hears appeals from job candidates who say their exam score was unfair. Two of our members come from the union side and almost always vote to grant the appeal; two come from management and almost always vote to uphold the score. I'm the fifth one and I actually read the file. We decide by simple majority, and at last week's meeting someone trotted out the old line about five heads being better than one, and that the majority gets it right more often than any one of us would. I've been turning that over since then because it rings false for a group as predictable as ours, but I can't quite say why. Thursday we have to decide a case where a candidate missed the passing mark by one point and says the oral-interview scoring was biased; the other four basicly told me how they'll vote before the file even went out.

I need to write a short memo to circulate to the commission before Thursday's meeting recommending how we should handle these calls — keep the simple-majority rule, or switch to something else — and saying whether that five-heads line actually applies to us. Give me a definite recommendation, with the exact procedure you'd use for Thursday and a timeline. Don't do the thing where you lay out three options and leave me to pick; and give the strongest case for keeping majority rule a real hearing rather than straw-manning it.
