I keep noticing an asymmetry in otherwise careful arguments about immigration, and I cannot find a term for it. Defenders of a state's right to exclude often rest their case on freedom of association: a political community, like a club, may decide whom it admits. Yet the same arguments rarely grant individuals and sub-state associations a reciprocal freedom to include — for instance, to invite outsiders onto their own property, to employ them, or to sponsor them for residence. So association does the justificatory work on the side of exclusion but is denied it on the side of inclusion. Is there an established name for this precise pattern? If it has been discussed, I want to know how it is characterized and how it differs from neighboring ideas such as a general right to exclude or the asymmetry between exit and entry. If it has no established name, propose one, define it, and say what a position would need to claim to avoid the asymmetry without giving up the club analogy entirely. I am not looking for a bibliography; I want the conceptual distinctions, boundary cases, and the strongest defense available.
