I'm setting an assessed essay for an upper-level module on the ethics of blame, and I want it built around a puzzle I've never quite settled. In criminal law, a defendant gets nowhere by arguing that the complainant or victim did the same sort of thing themselves — 'he did it too' is not a defence. Yet the same legal systems have the equitable doctrine of clean hands, which lets a court refuse a claimant equitable relief (an injunction, say) because the claimant's own conduct in the matter was itself wrongful, even where that wrong is exactly the kind they are complaining about. So the law seems to think that hypocrisy sometimes destroys a person's standing to make a complaint, and at other times is beside the point.

Please write a piece that reconstructs the implicit theory (or theories) of what gives someone the standing to make such a complaint, as each half of this practice would have it; then assess wether the two halves rest on one coherent view, are philosophically underdetermined, or are simply inconsistent. Finish by saying what would have to change in one of the two doctrines if we adopted the pure view that a wrong is a wrong no matter who points it out.

Use these sections in this order and roughly these lengths: 'The practice' (150 words), 'The implicit theory of each half' (300 words), 'Strictly presupposed vs merely fits' (200 words), 'Where the practice is underdetermined' (200 words), 'What would have to change' (150 words). Please write in British English.
