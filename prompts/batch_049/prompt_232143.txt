I want to think through Langdon Winner’s 1980 essay on whether artifacts have politics by pressing its framework against a single well-known case rather than by rehearsing the theory. Please answer in exactly five numbered points, each no more than two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.

The case is the Long Island parkway overpasses built under Robert Moses. The standard account, drawn from Robert Caro’s biography and taken up by Winner, is that the overpasses on the Southern State and related parkways were built unusually low so that buses could not pass under them; the effect was to keep New Yorkers who relied on buses—disproportionately poor and Black—from reaching Jones Beach. Bernward Joerges later contested this account, arguing that the clearances were not exceptional, that buses could reach the beach by other routes, and that the evidence of deliberate racial exclusion is weak.

Using that case as your material, your five points should do the following. First, state what Winner’s framework would need to claim for the Moses case to count as a genuine example of an artifact being inherently political, and distinguish that claim from a weaker one about the political consequences of infrastructure. Second, map which parts of the case the framework explains well and which it does not, treating the overpass itself, the road system, and the beach as separate but connected artifacts. Third, say what Joerges’ objections do to the example: do they refute the theory, or merely shift it to a different level of explanation? Fourth, evaluate what condition would have to be met for the parkway system to be a case of technological determinism rather than a case of a powerful man whose intentions happened to be expressed in concrete. Fifth, close with what the case shows about the theory’s limits—specifically, whether a theory of inherently political artifacts can handle cases where the exclusionary effect is real but the design intent is unclear, or whether it must fall back on a contextual, merely contingent account.
