So I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology and I keep seeing Fitch's paradox summarized in ways that feel a bit off, but I cant quite tell whether it's the summaries or my half-remembered modal logic. Someone passed me this paragraph from a blog that's usually pretty solid:

"Fitch's paradox of knowability shows that verificationism collapses into omniscience. Verificationists hold that every meaningful statement must be verifiable — i.e., if a statement is true, it must be possible to know it. Fitch proved that this principle is inconsistent with the obvious fact that some truths are never known. Take any truth p that nobody knows. By the verificationist principle, the conjunction 'p is true and nobody knows p' must be possible to know. But if someone knew that conjunction, they would know both conjuncts, and so would know that nobody knows p. Knowledge is factive, so if they knew that nobody knows p, then nobody would know p — which contradicts their knowing p itself. So the conjunction cannot be known after all, and therefore, by the principle, it cannot be true. Hence if p is true, someone must know it. In other words, every truth is known. That's absurd, so verificationism must be false."

I'm pretty comfortable with the bones of the proof, but two moves bother me. The first is the slide from "every meaningful statement is verifiable" to "every truth is knowable" — those look like different claims, and I dont see how the verificationist criterion of meaningfulness gets you a claim about all truths. The second is the leap from "this consequence is absurd" to "verificationism must be false," because I vaguely remember Dummett and Tennant having responses that keep some kind of knowability principle by restricting it or going intuitionistic. Am I right to be suspicious? Can you go through this summary and tell me which parts are accurate, which parts are common simplifications, and which parts a defender of the knowability principle would actually reject or want to modify? I don't need citations or a reading list, I just want to know how much of this gloss I can trust.
