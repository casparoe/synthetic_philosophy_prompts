I'm presenting my position on testimony to an epistemology reading group next week and want to run a consistency check before I do, because I've got the feeling some of these are rubbing against each other but I can't tell which ones. Here's where I've landed, roughly.

First, I'm an anti-reductionist about testimonial justification: when someone sincerely tells me p and I understand what's said, I have prima facie justification to believe p without needing any positive evidence that they're reliable, the way I don't check my retinas before trusting a clear visual impression. Second, I'm also a general evidentialist internalist about justification: no belief is justified unless the believer has adequate evidence or reasons for it, and I've never been able to swallow the idea that justification can be had for free. Third, I think defeaters are only things already in view: I have no duty to go investigate a speaker, but if I already have reason to think they're unreliable, or that the claim itself is wildly implausible, the default is off. Fourth, I'm quite sure children acquire genuine knowledge from their parents' telling without first doing induction over parental track records; a five-year-old who believes her mother that the stove is hot isnt being irrational, and she isnt implicitly running statistics on Mom. Fifth, here are two cases I keep going back and forth on. A stranger at a bus stop tells me the time and I believe him, and I want to say I'm justified and, if hes right and not lying, I know it. But if the same stranger tells me he won the lottery yesterday, I don't think I'd be justified in believing him just on his say-so. I take the difference to be that the lottery claim conflicts with my background beliefs, not that I have any evidence about his reliability.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look that way, then rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. End by saying what the least costly single revision would be and what it costs.
