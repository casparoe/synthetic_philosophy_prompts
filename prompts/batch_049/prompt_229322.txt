I've been going back and fourth with a friend who's a much more orthodox Marxist than I am, and I keep finding myself defending a version of historical materialism that I'm no longer sure its actually falsifiable. Let me put the position on the table: I don't think the economic base explains everything about law, politics, religion, or art, at least not by dictating their content at a given moment. What I do think is that the relations of production set the menu of what can persist and reproduce itself over time. The base is more like a selection environment than a blueprint: a legal system, a family form, or a theology can be as weird and locally contingent as you like, but if it systematically undermines the reproduction of the dominant relations of production, it gets selected against across generations, while forms that fit tend to stick around and get copied. At moments of crisis, when the old relations of production can no longer absorb their own effects, the direction of change is set by what the base can sustain, not by the actors' self-understanding. So I'd predict that superficially different states and legal codes will converge on functional preconditions when they sit on top of similar production relations, and that the ideologies that last are the ones that make the existing extraction of surplus feel natural.

What would count against this? I want a real map of the view's exposure, not just the standard objections I've already heard (yes, Marx didn't consistently say this; yes, some superstructural changes seem to lead). I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, including: cases where a durable superstructural form clearly obstructed the reproduction of the underlying economy and was not selected away for centuries, and how my view should absorb that; whether the selection talk is doing real explanatory work or just redescribing whatever happens; whether a defender of my position should honestly admit the view has been insulated by qualifications, and whether that is a problem for something that presents itself as a theory of history; and which nearby positions, the kind that grant the legal or religious sphere more autonomy than I do, get cases right that I can't get right without ad hoc moves. Tell me what I'd have to accept as a genuine counterexample and where I should reasonably refuse to move, and be willing to end by telling me the selection-environment version is just unfalsifiable if that's what the case against it adds up to.
