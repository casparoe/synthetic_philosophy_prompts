The frequentist defines probability as long-run relative frequency: to say a fair die has a one-in-six chance of showing six is to say that, over a sufficiently long series of rolls, six will come up about one time in six. The subjectivist instead treats probability as degree of belief. A probability assignment is a report of the speaker's confidence; there is no independent fact of the matter about whether one assignment is more correct than another, and the only constraints are that the numbers be internally consistent with the axioms of probability. The propensity theorist says probability is a real physical tendency in the chance setup itself: this die has a one-in-six disposition to land six on a single throw, even if it is never thrown.
