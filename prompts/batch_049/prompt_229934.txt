Something about how the NBA handles money seems philosophically weird to me, and I can't tell what the league is implicitly claiming. The league caps what teams can pay players in total and hits teams with a luxury tax if they go over, so a team can't just pay a star whatever they could get on the open market; but there's no cap on what owners can charge for tickets, and no cap on how profitable a franchise can be. What theory of distributive justice, if anything, does this setup presuppose? Does it presuppose the sort of patterned or end-state principle that Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain argument was aimed at — the argument where fans freely pay extra to watch Wilt play, so enforcing any distributional pattern would mean interfering with those free exchanges? Or could a Nozick-style entitlement theorist consistently defend the cap as something besides an unjust interference with voluntary exchange? Be explicit about where the parallel between the Chamberlain story and a modern salary cap holds and where it breaks, and say what, if anything, would have to change if the league actually ran itself on entitlement-theory grounds.
