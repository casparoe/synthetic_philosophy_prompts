I co-host a small philosophy-of-technology podcast, and this month's guest is a philosopher who defends a Dennett-style line on counterfeit people: chatbots trained to pass as human conversation partners don't just deceive individual users, they debase the background trust that communication depends on, so disclosure labels aren't enough and we should legally require AI systems to be designed so they can't pass as persons. I want the interview to be substantive, not the usual what-drew-you-to-this fluff. Draft eight or nine questions that push past restating the view. Include at least one each on: where the line is between a counterfeit person and an innocuous bot that carries a permanent visible disclaimer; how the trust-debasement story accomodates the fact that lots of people knowingly enjoy being taken in (roleplay, fiction, apologizing voice assistants); and whether Dennett's own intentional-stance account of real human minds — that we treat each other as minds for predictive purposes, not because we've detected some inner special stuff — ends up blurring the very line the guest wants to enforce. For each, add a follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give — for instance, that people stop noticing warnings, that the harm is structural like counterfeit currency, or that there remains a real difference between evolved agency and designed mimicry.
