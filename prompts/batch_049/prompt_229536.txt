I recently got into an argument with a friend about whether dispositional properties can be analyzed in terms of counterfactual conditionals, and he sent me the following summary from a blog post he likes, saying it settles the matter. I've read enough rationalist-adjacent philosophy to be wary of summaries that end in a clean reductio, but I don't know this literature well enough to locate the specific errors myself.

Here is the summary:

"To say a glass is fragile seems to mean that it would break if dropped or struck. So a disposition like fragility just is a subjunctive conditional: x is disposed to M in response to stimulus S iff, were x to undergo S, x would M. But this simple analysis fails. A fink is a factor that would remove the disposition as soon as its stimulus occurs: a sorcerer standing ready to make a glass sturdy the moment before it is struck means the glass is fragile yet would not break if struck. A mask is slightly different—the disposition remains present but another factor blocks its manifestation, as when an antidote prevents a lethal poison from killing someone who ingests it. Either way, the conditional comes out false while the disposition is still there. So the simple conditional analysis is refuted. And because the obvious repairs just add ceteris paribus clauses that cannot be specified without using the very notion of a disposition, while causal-basis accounts look like a separate and equally problematic project, most metaphysicians have concluded that dispositionality is primitive, or at least not analyzable in counterfactual terms. Reductionism about dispositions is a dead end."

I'm not looking for a reading list or citations. I want an accuracy audit: which parts of this are faithful to the actual fink-and-mask dialectic, which parts conflate two different claims, where the summary overstates the state of the field, and whether the conclusion follows from the accurate parts once they're corrected. In particular, I'd like to know what a defender of a more sophisticated conditional analysis would say in response, and whether finks and masks are really doing the same argumentative work or require different replies.
