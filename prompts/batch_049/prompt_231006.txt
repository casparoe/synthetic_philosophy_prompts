I've been working through al-Ghazālī's occasionalism, and one of its commitments keeps looking unstable to me. As I understand it, he denies that fire causes the cotton to burn: God directly creates the burning at the moment of contact, and the constant conjunction we observe is only God's customary way of acting. But if every event is a fresh, free act of divine will, then observed regularities seem to give no reason at all about the next event. To argue that God will continue acting as He has acted because He is constant would just be to run an induction over God's past behavior, which seems circular. So why isn't al-Ghazālī committed to radical skepticism about the future? Or is there a principled, non-circular justification, internal to his view, for trusting that the regularities will continue?
