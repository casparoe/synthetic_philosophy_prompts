Since I retired from chemistry I've been reading my way through philosophy of mind, and I keep coming back to the knowing-how versus knowing-that debate. I've ended up fairly convinced by the intellectualist side: knowing how to do something — titrate a solution, ride a bike, spot a bad baseline in a proton NMR — is really a species of knowing that. You know a proposition, something like the proposition that some way is a way for you to do it, even if you can't articulate it. The appeal for me is that it makes know-how the sort of thing you can pin down and reason about, where Ryle's anti-intellectualism always left it feeling like a mysterious knack. I know the standard Rylean regress objection (if know-how is knowing propositions, then applying those propositions seems to require a further bit of know-how, and so on), but I'd like a sharper map of what I'm actually committed to.

Tell me what would have to be true for this view to be wrong, or at least badly dented. Don't just list the textbook objections; I want the full landscape. Which intuitions or cases — savant skills, animal know-how, motor learning that proceeds without anything propositional, the linguistics of "knows how"-ascriptions with embeded questions — would an intellectualist concede are awkward, and which would they dismiss as not touching the core claim? Are there empirical findings, say about implicit learning, or patients with impaired episodic memory who nonetheless acquire new skills, that would cut deeper than armchair cases? And where does intellectualism actually make predictions the Rylean view doesn't — for instance about what gets learned when someone acquires a skill — and do those predictions help or hurt it? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely annoying, and say honestly whether the view has been insulated by qualifications. If it is well insulated, is that insulation itself a vice? For what it's worth, the thing I'd personally find most persuasive is a case where two people know the same proposition but still differ in know-how — at that point I'd probably stop being an intellectualist.
