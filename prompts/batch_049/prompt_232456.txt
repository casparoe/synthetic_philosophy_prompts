I sit on the committee that reviews the social discount rate our finance ministry requires in appraisals of public infrastructure. We must sign off on the rate for a coastal flood-defence programme whose construction costs fall within the next decade and whose benefits accrue over two centuries. The committee is split over the pure time preference component. One side wants to keep a strictly positive annual rate of pure time preference, discounting future well-being as such; they argue this tracks how people and governments actually choose, and that zeroing it would let an open-ended sequence of future people collectively outweigh the present. The other side wants to set that component to zero and discount only for expected growth in per-capita consumption; they argue that a person's location in time is no reason to count their welfare for less. Please draft the committee's decision memo recommending whether to retain, remove, or restructure the pure time preference term, and stating the rule analysts should apply to this programme. I want a committed recommendation that takes a definite position and gives its reasons, not a survey of options. Lead with your recommendation in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question; engage the strongest consideration on the losing side; write in British English.
