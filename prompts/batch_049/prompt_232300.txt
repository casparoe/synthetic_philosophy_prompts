Write a dialogue between two equally competent students—one defending the Great Man theory of history and the other defending structural explanation—who are arguing about whether there would have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler. In the course of the dialogue, have them engage directly with William James’s argument in Great Men and Their Environment, where he compares great individuals to spontaneous variations that are then selected by their social environment. The dialogue should interpret and pressure-test that argument rather than merely citing it; the two characters should respond to each other’s actual points and not talk past each other.
