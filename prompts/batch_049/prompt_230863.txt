After a really long argument with my friend Sam the other night, I've basically come around to the inefficacy objection about individual contributions to big collective problems. The way I'd now put it: when it comes to climate change, eating meat, or even voting, my single action is almost certainly not the one that tips the outcome. The temperature won't change by a measurable amount because I skipped one flight, the meat industry will produce the same number of animals whether I order the burger or not, and no election in my lifetime is going to be decided by one vote. So expecting myself to act differently is like making a math error where I attribute to my own choice an effect it doesn't actually have. Sam pushed this further: he says the conclusion isn't just "don't beat yourself up," but that a lot of individual-level "do your part" morality is genuinely confused, and the only rational response is to put your energy into structural change and stop treating personal consumption as a moral issue at all. I've definately been repeating his version to people, which makes me nervous, because I never actually got clear on what would refute it.

So I guess what I want is a map of the position's vulnerabilities from someone who isn't trying to win an argument. What would have to be true for the inefficacy objection to be wrong, or at least much weaker than the way I've been stating it? I'd like you to seperate the considerations that would be flat-out fatal from the ones that are just awkward or cost the position some of its rhetorical force. And I'd like to know which of those a careful defender would actually concede as counting against them, rather than wave off with "you're just guilt-tripping me." For example, is the "what if everybody thought that?" reply as bad as Sam made it sound, or does it survive some restatement? If it turns out the view can be patched to survive every attack, I'd also want to know whether that's a virtue or a sign it's stopped taking risks. I'm less interested in a bibliography and more in a ranking of the defeaters, from "this would make me drop the view" down to "I'd qualify it but keep calling it true." Could you end with a one-sentence summary of your overall take as the last line?
