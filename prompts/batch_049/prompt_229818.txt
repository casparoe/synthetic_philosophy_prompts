Next week I'm presenting a paper to my department's philosophy of physics reading group, and I want to go in ready for the Q&A. The thesis is that the hole argument doesn't actually refute spacetime substantivalism; it only refutes a very specific version of it, the one where spacetime points are bare particulars with primitive identity (haecceitism). My positive view is a "sophisticated substantivalism" instead: points exist and spacetime is a substance, but points have no identity over and above their place in the structure of the metric field. My sketch of the argument: consider a model M with metric g, and a diffeomorphism d that's the identity outside some hole H but not inside, so we get M' = (M, d*g). If points carry primitive thisness, then M and M' represent two distinct possible worlds with identical behavior outside H but different behavior inside, and since the laws are diffeomorphism invariant, you get radical indeterminism. I accept that conditional. I just deny the antecedent — I read M and M' as two representations of the same world, not two worlds, and I claim that reading is still substantivalist because the points are real, the field is real and located at them, there's just no haecceitistic difference between the two representations.

The audience: mostly philosophers of physics who know the Earman and Norton hole argument cold. There's one committed relationist who won't accept any view where points are "real" and will say my view is just relationism with extra steps. There's a structural realist who's sympathetic but thinks I'm under-describing how points get their identity from the field. One person works on determinism and will care whether my move just changes the definition of determinism rather than answering the argument. And there's a couple of agnostics who'll dogpile if my replies get hand-wavy. The vocab they'll use: haecceitism, quidditism, Leibniz equivalence, determinism as isomorphism classes of models, symmetry.

Give me a Q&A prep sheet. I want the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way these specific people would phrase them, each with a short reply and a note on where my reply is softest, what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and the weakest point in my position that I should prepare to defend rather than hope they miss. Also tell me the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
