Last night a friend and I spent an entire evening arguing about what a social fact could possibly be, and I came away convinced that the disagreement was less about the facts than about what each of us was silently demanding from a theory. I kept insisting that any social phenomenon — a currency losing its value, a crowd panicking, an accent spreading through a city — must in the end bottom out in individual people and their beliefs and choices, since there is nothing else for it to be made of. She countered that this gets the direction of explanation backwards: individuals form their beliefs and make their choices only against a background of institutions, prices, roles, and norms that no one of them created, and you cannot recover the social fact from the list of individual states without already presupposing it. By the end we had agreed that both directions of dependence are real and that almost no one denies either; what separates the positions is which direction gets explanatory priority, and what kinds of explanation count as legitimate.

Before I go back and settle the argument, I want the criteria against which the positions should be judged. Write the desiderata that any adequate account of the relation between individual-level and social-level facts must satisfy. For each, give the case or consideration that motivates it — bank runs, the spontaneous emergence and stickiness of norms, a corporation's being held responsible, the way a market constrains individual choice, the multiple realizability of social kinds by different configurations of individuals, the difference between an institutional fact like a debt and a mere behavioral regularity like morning traffic, and so on. Then distinguish the non-negotiable constraints from the desiderata that are really commitments of one camp, such as the individualist's requirement that every macro-level explanation have microfoundations or the holist's demand that macro-level explanations be autonomous rather than shorthand. I especially want to know which pairs or subsets of these desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied as stated, and which single requirement does the most to force a choice between the camps. Finally, and only briefly, show how a strict methodological individualism, a strong holism, and an emergentist middle view fare against the list.
