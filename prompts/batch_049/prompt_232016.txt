I'm a novelist working on a book where a field ecologist and a molecular physiologist keep relitigating what a biological function actually is, and I don't want the reader to just get two people talking past each other. I want the real document they'd have to produce if a funder forced them into a proper adversarial collaboration. So here's the brief. Write the joint statement a committed selected-effects (etiological) theorist and a committed causal-role theorist about biological function could both sign after a year of good-faith wrangling.

The statement should do four things. First, lay out the claims about function talk both camps can sign as true and non-vacuous. Second, state the exact point where they divide, in wording each side would accept for itself rather than call a caricature. Third, identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one camp. Fourth, for each crux, give the concrete case or kind of evidence both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about it. Push on the cases that really seperate them: a new mutation that happens to be useful now but has no selection history, a trait selected for one thing that now does another, and a trait that was selected but is now actively harmful. Don't resolve the dispute; make the residual disagreement exact, and if part of it turns out to be merely verbal, say which part and why.

Formatting matters to me. I want this as continuous prose, like a joint letter they'd send, so no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. Just paragraphs. Write in British English. And keep it about the ideas rather than the literature — no citations, no bibliography, no author name-dropping.
