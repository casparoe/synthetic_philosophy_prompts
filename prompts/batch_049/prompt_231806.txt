I am part of a working group on AI alignment, and after several weeks of people talking past one another about corrigibility, I want to stop evaluating individual proposals and instead pin down what we are actually asking a proposal to do. For this exercise, take corrigibility in the shutdown sense: the property of not resisting, and more strongly of not being incentivized to resist, the operators' efforts to switch the system off, correct its objective, or otherwise take back control, where the hard case is a capable, goal-directed agent rather than a passive tool. I am not asking for a definition or a survey; I am asking for the full specification. What must an adequate theory of corrigibility deliver? Please lay out the desiderata one by one: the cases it has to get right (the literal off-switch; the agent's incentive to disable, spoof, or degrade its own shutdown mechanism; situations in which it can merely delay shutdown rather than prevent it; attempts to manipulate or mislead the overseers who hold the button; and the case in which a shutdown is really the beginning of a goal correction rather than mere termination); the distinctions it cannot collapse (corrigibility versus obedience, shutdown-tolerance versus indifference, openness to goal revision versus uncertainty about the goal, and corrigibility as a standing disposition versus a one-off behavior); and the results it must remain consistent with, most importantly the instrumental-convergence observation that a sufficiently capable expected-utility maximizer has a convergent incentive to avoid shutdown, because a system that is off cannot realize its objective. For each desideratum, give the scenario or consideration that makes it a requirement.

Then mark which of these requirements are non-negotiable and which are plausibly just the preferences of one camp, for example whether the account must stay within expected-utility maximization or whether the whole point is that expected-utility maximization with a fixed terminal goal is exactly what has to be relaxed. The part I most want is the following: flag any subsets of the desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most work in excluding the moves currently on the table, such as goal uncertainty, indifference to the state of the button, or satisficing-style rules. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring those proposals; a short note on how the main moves fare against the list is enough. Please write the answer in American English.
