I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I also sit on the collections advisory group at a mid-sized natural history museum. We're meeting next month about sixty-one carved wooden objects - masks and ancestor figures - collected in 1907 in what's now the border region between Cameroon and Nigeria, during a German colonial punitive expedition. The collector's notes say the pieces were 'requisitioned' after a village refused to pay a newly imposed tax, which I read as seizure with extra steps. Here's the complication: nobody's asking for all of them. A diaspora organisation in Germany wants the entire set transferred to a community-run cultural centre. The national museum in the country of origin has only asked for about a dozen pieces it calls nationally significant. And our founding charter accepted the collection from the donor on the condition it 'never be dispersed,' which our lawyer says may still be enforceable. I need to circulate a short written opinion before the meeting with a concrete recommendation - return all, return the dozen, retain with shared stewardship, whatever - and it has to honestly engage the strongest argument for keeping them, not just waive it off. It would also help if the memo seperates the legal title question from the ethical question of what we should do, since the board keeps sliding between the two. One more thing: don't give me a general essay on the Benin Bronzes - this is a decision document for one specific, weirdly overdetermined case.
