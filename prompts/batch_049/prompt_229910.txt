Write a decision tree that sorts a respondent into a position within the debate over true contradictions and paraconsistent logic. The tree is for an advanced undergraduate seminar in the philosophy of logic; it should work as a "which position do you actually hold?" instrument, so a respondent can answer their way from a plain starting question to a leaf without knowing any of the labels in advance.

The tree must have exactly six leaves:

- classical logic;
- paraconsistent non-dialetheism (the view that reality is consistent, but reasoning with inconsistent information should not explode);
- paracomplete or truth-value-gap approaches to the paradoxes;
- restricted (semantic) dialetheism;
- full-strength (ubiquitous) dialetheism;
- trivialism.

Do not construct the tree by starting from the labels. First identify the two or three dividing questions that generate the logical space, and for each question supply a concrete case or claim a respondent can assess without already knowing which position it sorts them toward. The following four cases must appear at branch points: (1) a self-referential liar sentence; (2) a borderline moment in a vague transition, such as the instant at which someone ceases to be a child or the exact hair at which someone becomes bald; (3) a pair of inconsistent clauses in a legal or administrative code; and (4) the claim that from a contradiction every sentence follows (explosion). You may add a further branch point only if it genuinely separates two of the six leaves that the four cases leave tangled.

For each leaf, give a one- or two-sentence diagnosis that states the combination of answers that places a respondent there, in terms of the cases rather than the position's name. Then list each combination of answers to your dividing questions that no standard position occupies, and say whether that cell is empty because it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored. Finally, flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate, and state the additional question that would separate them if the tree had one more level. If a recognizable position outside the six leaves (for example, supervaluationism about vagueness) naturally occupies one of the cells in your logical space, note where it sits instead of treating the cell as empty.
