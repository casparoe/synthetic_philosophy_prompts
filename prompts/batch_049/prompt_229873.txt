In Plutarch's Life of Theseus, as translated by John Dryden, he reports that the ship in which Theseus voyaged with the young Athenians was preserved by the Athenians down to the time of Demetrius of Phalerum: whenever an old plank decayed, they removed it and fitted new, stronger timber in its place, with the result that the ship became a standing puzzle among philosophers — some holding that it remained the same ship, others that it was not the same.

I want your actual credence in a sharper version of that old dispute, not a survey of options. Let the proposition be this: for an ordinary material artefact that undergoes gradual, plank-by-plank replacement of all its parts while maintaining continuous form, function, location, and social role, there is a determinate, mind-independent fact of the matter about whether the later object is numerically identical with the original object.

Give me a number between 0 and 1 for that proposition, and then decompose the reasoning that produces it. I would expect the decomposition to include at least these sub-questions, but you may revise the list if you think I have drawn the joints wrongly:

(a) Whether identity over time for artefacts is primitive or whether it is determined by something more basic, such as causal continuity, underlying material, or functional continuity.  
(b) Whether identity questions of this kind are sortal-relative, so that the answer can differ between the ship, the historical relic, the public monument, or the heap of planks.  
(c) Whether the correct metaphysics of persistence is endurantist or perdurantist, and how much that general framework fixes about this particular case.  
(d) Whether ordinary utterances of that is the same ship track numerical identity at all, rather than something weaker such as same role, same design, or same ceremonial function.  
(e) Whether apparent disagreement about the case is substantive or partly verbal, in the sense that the two sides are using same in different senses.

Assign a rough probability to each sub-question, state how they combine into your overall credence, and identify which one is doing the most work in the combination. Then tell me which term you are least confident in, and what evidence or argument would shift that term the most.

For context, I give the proposition a credence of about 0.4, and a colleague gives it about 0.8. On your decomposition, where is our disagreement most likely to live, and would it survive if we were forced to precisify each sub-question?
