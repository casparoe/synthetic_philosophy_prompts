I'm a photographer rather than a student of philosophy, and I think about seeing as a signal path: light hits a sensor, the camera records RAW data, in-camera conversion happens, and then I do the rest in post-processing before an image appears. On my own I've been reading about a debate in cognitive science over cognitive penetration of perception—the question whether my beliefs, expectations, and desires can literally change how things look to me, as opposed to only changing how I interpret and judge what I see. Restate that debate, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary of my craft: sensor, RAW capture, demosaicing, white balance, exposure, compression, and the difference between a file and the image rendered from it. Then tell me what did not survive the translation. In particular, I want to know whether the line between perception itself and post-perceptual judgment genuinely maps onto my RAW-versus-edit line, or whether my vocabulary quietly assumes the very thing under dispute—and if so, which side of the debate that assumption favors. Where something is lost, is that a defect of the photography framework, or evidence that the original distinction is less sharp than it sounds?
