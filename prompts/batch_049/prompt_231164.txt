Last night I had a long argument with a friend about whether the badness of pain is part of the sensation itself. I kept returning to pain asymbolia: a person can describe a stimulus as sharp or burning and locate it, yet not find it unpleasant, not withdraw from it, and sometimes even smile while reporting that it hurts. My friend kept replying that such people do not really have pain, or that their reports should be discounted as a symptom of their deficit. I want a careful explanation of the conceptual issue this argument is circling. In particular: what would have to be true of the sensation in pain asymbolia for the case to separate pain itself from pain's badness; what my friend is assuming when he refuses to let the case separate them; and what the dispute ultimately turns on, whether empirical, conceptual, or both. Please explain this through the distinction between the sensory-discriminative component of pain and its affective-motivational component, and make clear what is at stake in resisting that distinction.
