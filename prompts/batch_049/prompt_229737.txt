I've been reading about cause prioritization for years, mostly through rationalist and EA blogs, and I keep seeing the importance–tractability–neglectedness framework invoked at a slogan level. A friend recently gave me a fuller summary, and I want to know how much of it to trust. Her version went like this: "You score each cause on three dimensions. Importance is how many individuals are affected; tractability is how solvable the problem is; neglectedness is how few people are already working on it. You multiply the three scores and fund whichever cause comes out highest. That's the whole framework — a three-factor formula for where marginal resources do the most good."

I'm inclined to accept most of this, but the multiplication talk bothers me. It makes the framework sound more precise than its advocates usually admit, and I can't tell whether tractability and neglectedness are genuinely independent factors or just two ways of saying "an extra unit of effort will go far." Could you separate the parts of her summary that a careful practitioner would endorse from the parts that are subtly wrong? And does the practical conclusion — fund the highest-scoring cause — survive once the sloppy parts are cleaned up?

If I'm expecting more rigor from this framework than it is meant to provide, or if the whole comparison is somehow ill-posed, please say that directly instead of working politely around my framing.
