I'm teaching a philosophy of technology module this term, and in the seminar on whether artefacts have politics a student kept waving away every example with 'guns don't kill people, people kill people'. A dropped kerb removed from a design, an automated hiring filter, a motorway bridge built low enough that buses can't get under it — for him it all came down to the same thing: the object doesn't do anything, only the people using it do. I pushed back a bit, but afterwards it occured to me that I couldn't say exactly which of several different claims he was actually making, and I suspect the line slides between them depending on the work it's being asked to do. Could you pull the precise claims out of that sentence — about causes, about moral responsibility, about whether tools are neutral, about policy, whatever else shows up — and say for each one whether it's trivially true, substantive and defensible, or just false? Then tell me which reading he'd actually need for it to settle the question whether artefacts have politics, and which reading he was most likely running on when he kept bringing it up.
