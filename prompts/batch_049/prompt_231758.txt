I'm finally writing up a personal epistemology post and I want a consistency check before it goes up. I've been reading LessWrong and the rationalist forums for years, someone recommended Rorty to me recently, and now my views are a bit of a mash-up. As near as I can tell, I hold all of the following:

1. Antirepresentationalism in roughly Rorty's sense. Vocabularies and theories are tools for coping with the world, not pictures of it. There's no useful sense in which one vocabulary is closer to reality than another, except that it better serves some human purpose.

2. The map/territory distinction taken seriously. There is a mind-independent territory, and beliefs can be more or less accurate maps of it; some maps are just wrong about the territory.

3. A Bayesian picture of belief. Beliefs are probability distributions over propositions about how the world is, and epistemic rationality is getting those distributions accurate and well-callibrated in response to evidence.

4. Predictive processing. The brain is a generative model that infers the hidden causes of its sensory input; perception literally is representational inference.

5. Deflationism about truth. To call a belief true is not to ascribe a property or relation to it, like correspondence; it's just to endorse it, or to say it has whatever features make it worth having.

6. Pragmatic scientific realism. Science plainly works better than astrology or theology at letting us anticipate and manipulate nature, and it works because it is constrained by how the world actually is.

I honestly can't tell which of these pull against each other. Reading me, you'd guess 2-4 and 6 form a natural package, and 1 and 5 got bolted on later. Can you audit the set: go through the pairings, tell me which tensions are real and which only look that way, rank the real ones by severity, and tell me the cheapest revision? If I insisted on keeping 1, which of 2-6 would I need to re-read or reformulate, and how?
