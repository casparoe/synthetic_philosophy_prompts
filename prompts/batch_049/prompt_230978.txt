I have been trying to put van Fraassen's Reflection principle to work in my own reasoning, and I keep running into a difficulty. As I understand it, the principle says that my present degree of belief in a proposition, conditional on supposing that at some later time I will assign it probability r, should itself be r. But suppose I have good reason to think that my future self will be epistemically compromised—an illness that causes overconfidence, or ordinary memory loss that erodes evidence. Then the principle appears to instruct me to treat my future self's credences as authoritative even when I can already see that they will not be formed well. How should a rational person obey Reflection in such cases? Is the right response to say that the principle commands deference only to future selves that are themselves rational, and if so, what substantive content is left in the principle?
