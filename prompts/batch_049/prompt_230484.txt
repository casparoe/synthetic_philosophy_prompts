I'm writing a two-page proposal for a small AI safety grant and I need you to sharpen the question rather than write the whole thing from scratch. My half-baked idea is this. People like to say alignment is about making the model want the right thing, while control is about making it so the model can't do the wrong thing even if it's misaligned. But when you look at actual evaluations, almost all of them test alignment-shaped properties: does the model refuse bad requests, is it honest in normal situations, that kind of thing. There's basically no such thing as a "control eval." I think I know why it's hard. To test whether your controls would stop a misaligned model, you need a model that is actualy trying to do something bad. But a competent misaligned model won't try while it's being watched; it'll play nice until it sees a real opportunity. So a test where the model behaves itself is always ambiguous between "the controls held" and "the model was never trying." The project I'm pitching would be to work through what a control eval could even look like, and whether you can ever demonstrate that a system is safe because it's controlled without smuggling in the assumption that the model was actually aligned during the test. I suspect the answer to the second question determines whether a whole research program is viable, but I need the proposal to make that case.

Please turn this into the proposal: a precise statement of the question, why existing positions leave it open, the approach and why it's conceptual/philosophical rather than another empirical benchmark, what a useful deliverable looks like for people who decide where to spend safety money, and what would count as failure. Two-year scope, one postdoc. No citations; I'll add those myself. Start with the proposal itself, with no preface.
