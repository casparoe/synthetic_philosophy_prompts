People often argue from simultaneous discovery, Newton and Leibniz or Darwin and Wallace, that discoveries are in the air and that if a given discoverer had never lived someone else would have made the same discovery at around the same time. I think that argument moves too fast because it assumes we already know what counts as the same discovery. So the question I want the proposal built around is: before a case of simultaneous discovery can license a claim about inevitability, what sameness relation has to hold between the independent achievements, and do the standard cases actually meet it? And depending on the answer, should credit attach to the discovery-type or to the specific contribution, and does that change wether priority disputes matter?
