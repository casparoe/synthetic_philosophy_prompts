The way I understand it, a creative variation is made by twisting the "knobs" of a theme's concepts, and the whole game is knowing which knobs are load-bearing and how far you can turn them before the variation stops mapping back to the original. Can you write me a decision procedure I can run in under an hour before I sketch, that (i) helps me list the knobs of a given setup, (ii) classifies a candidate gag as trite / fresh / broken (no longer the same theme) / weird-but-rescuable (I can point to the mapping), and (iii) gives me the questions in order, with what counts as a good answer to each. I can't be re-deriving the theory every week, so make it a checklist I could hand to a non-philosopher. Then run it on three candidate gags for my seagull strip—say, a seagull stealing chips, a seagull who thinks he's the lifeguard, and a third of your choosing—and make at least one a gag the procedure says to kill even though I'd personally want to keep it. Flag the steps where judgement takes over, and say what the procedure cant catch. One thing thats been bugging me is gags that twist several knobs at once and feel coherent in the moment but fall apart when you actually try to say what maps to what—does the procedure have anything for that?
