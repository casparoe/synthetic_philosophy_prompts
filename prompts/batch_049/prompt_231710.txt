I sit on my university's academic integrity board, and we're redoing how we handle first-offense plagiarism. The current system: a student found responsible gets a zero on the assignment and a private notation, and the case never goes public — the implicit theory being that guilt is the right lever, you did a bad thing, you feel it in private, you correct. The dean is now proposing a public integrity registry instead: names of students found responsible would be posted on a university page for a full academic year. Her pitch is that private guilt is cheap and evaporates, while public shame is what actually deters and tells the community the standards are serious. Half the board thinks this is cruel, archaic, and psychologically dangerous; the other half thinks the current system just lets cheaters off with a bad feeling and a confidential note. Please draft the short written memo I can circulate before our commitee vote: recommend a concrete policy — adopt, reject, or adopt with conditions — give the actionable bottom line, and deal honestly with the strongest argument for the side you do not take. Also address the question that keeps coming up underneath the fight: can an institution just choose to run on guilt or on shame as its governing moral emotion, or is that the sort of thing set by the surrounding culture so that a committee's choice is partly a fantasy?
