You are a philosopher of biology who is also fluent in the conceptual toolkit of applied statistics and machine learning: distance metrics, feature spaces and manifolds, clustering algorithms and cluster validation, and the difference between finding structure in data and imposing it. Work through the exercise below carefully and step by step; begin by laying out the components of the species problem you intend to translate before mapping them one by one.

I am a data scientist. I know clustering well — k-means, Gaussian mixtures, DBSCAN, hierarchical clustering, silhouette scores, stability under resampling, the gap statistic — but I have only an amateur's grasp of biology. I recently heard two ecologists argue about whether species are real units of nature or convenient bins for organizing biodiversity. One kept insisting that species are discovered; the other kept replying that they are decided. My suspicion is that my clustering vocabulary already contains most of this dispute, and that translating the philosophy into it will either dissolve the issue or reveal where my vocabulary silently assumes something biology does not.

Take the main candidate answers to the species problem — the biological species concept centered on interbreeding and reproductive isolation, the phylogenetic species concept(s) centered on diagnosable lineage segments or smallest monophyletic units, the ecological species concept centered on shared niche or adaptive zone, and the genotypic or phenotypic cluster concept — and restate each in my vocabulary as precisely and honestly as you can. For each one, say what translates cleanly, what must be distorted to fit, and what has no expression at all in terms of distances, features, clusters, and validation procedures. Then give me the residue report: consider whether the species problem really reduces to a cluster-definition and model-selection problem — which features, which distance metric, which linkage or cut-off, how many clusters — or whether something in the biological concepts refuses translation, for instance the thesis that species are historically connected individuals with beginnings and endings rather than classes of similar things. Be explicit about which failures of translation expose a genuine limitation in my vocabulary and which expose a confusion inside the original biological concepts. Do not give a bibliography or assign readings; the conceptual mapping itself is what I am after, and I want the report to be honest about what the translation cannot carry.
