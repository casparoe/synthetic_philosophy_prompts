I've been trying to figure out whether my moral judgments are backed by any general principle, or whether I'm basically a particularist who just reacts case by case. For what their worth, here are my gut verdicts. Lying to a friend about skipping their party to spare their feelings: fine. Lying to my partner about an affair, which would also spare pain: not fine, and pretty bad. Killing someone who breaks into my house and comes at me with a knife: fine. Killing one healthy person to harvest organs and save five patients: not fine, even though more lives get saved. Breaking a lunch date because my kid got sick: fine. Breaking the same promise because a better offer came up: not fine. Stealing a loaf of bread to feed a hungry child: fine. Enjoying the fact that a coworker I resent just got fired: not fine—the enjoyment makes the action worse, even though pleasure normally counts in favor of an act. Is there a principle, or a small set of them, that captures all or nearly all of these? Which of my verdicts would it force me to drop? And what would it say about the one I can't settle: torturing a suspect to find a bomb that would kill dozens. I want to know whether the pattern pushes me toward particularism, or whether something general is doing the work. Please write your answer in American English.
