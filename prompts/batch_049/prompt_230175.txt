Two people in my programme got into it after a seminar on the Darwin and Turing point that you can have competence without comprehension, and I still cant work out who's right.

Sam went first: if natural selection can build an eye without anyone understanding how eyes work, and a chess engine can beat a grandmaster without understanding chess, then comprehension is not doing any explanatory work. The reason the eye is the way it is is just that vision helped ancestors survive, with no mind ever needing to hold that thought. So reasons can float free of reasoners.

Jess said that is exactly the slide she rejects. Detachable, fine: nobody denies a system can be competent without comprehending its own competence. But free-floating is a step further. The reason for the eye is a fact about its selection history, not some third thing hovering between the eye and its environment. Calling it a reason without a reasoner is just reifying a way of speaking.

Sam came back with the thermostat. The reason it switched on is that the room dropped below the set point. That reason is not in anyone's head, it is embodied in the strip but no one is thinking it, and it is not nothing. So free-floating rationales must be real.

Jess replied that the thermostat's rationale was installed by someone who chose the set point, so it is a reason with a reasoner, merely deferred. Natural selection has no setter. That is precisely why the eye's rationale is only a retrospective summary by us: an interpretation, not a fact about the system itself.

Sam said that is the pre-Darwinian move, refusing to call anything a reason until a mind is somewhere in the causal chain. Once you grant that design accumulates without a designer, the rationale is doing the causal work whether or not it is represented. Jess has already conceded the whole point.

Jess said no, I have only conceded competence without comprehension. That says a system need not represent a rationale to embody it. It does not say the rationale exists unrepresented. Those are different claims, and you have run them together.

They asked me to referee and I honestly couldnt. Could you go through the exchange move by move, say who is right about which points, whether Sam's thermostat argument actually works, and who is more right overall, plus whatever it is they are both missing?
