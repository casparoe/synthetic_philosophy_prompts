In the government/current-events class we did a unit on how different countries handle religion and I went down a rabbit hole afterward, and I keep circling a pattern I don't have a word for. England has an official church — the monarch has an official role as its head and there are bishops sitting in parliament — but in daily life the country feels almost post-religious: politicians don't talk about faith much and hardly anybody goes to church. Denmark's kind of similar: state church, but very low-temperature religion. Germany has this church tax where you have to actively opt out of paying support for a denomination through your taxes, and a lot of people just seem to pay it out of inertia while the pews stay empty. Then there's the US, which has a pretty strict legal seperation of church and state, and yet religion feels way louder there — higher attendance, politicians constantly doing God-talk, religious voters treated as a huge electoral force. And then France is also strongly secular but in a more aggressive way, like no religious symbols in public schools, and France seems to be exactly where religion keeps turning into political conflict instead of fading away.

My half-baked thought is this: when a state officially sponsors a church, it tames it and turns it into sort of a heritage institution nobody gets worked up about; when the state stays out of religion, religion becomes a competitive, energetic thing (the US); but when the state actively shoves religion out of public life, religion becomes a conflict zone (France). If that's right, then "separation of church and state" is covering two opposite policies, and "establishment" produces the chill outcome that people in the US seem scared it won't produce.

Is there an actual name for this pattern? I don't want just a label — I want to know if I'm really looking at one phenomenon or three different ones that each have names, where exactly the line is between the US kind of separation and the French kind, and what the obvious counterexamples are (I can think of countries where an official church was definitely not low-energy). If it doesn't have a name, what would you call the part of it that's real, and what boundary cases should stop people from overusing the word?
