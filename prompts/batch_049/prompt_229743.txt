I've been reading around Quine and the analytic–synthetic distinction, and I've ended up with a set of commitments that I'm no longer sure are jointly consistent. Could you go through them pairwise, separate the real conflicts from the merely apparent ones, rank the real conflicts by severity, and tell me which single revision would buy the most consistency at the lowest cost?

1. Confirmation is holistic: individual sentences don't have their own empirical content, and experience tests my whole web of beliefs at once.
2. No sentence is in principle immune to revision: with enough compensating adjustments elsewhere in the web, I could hold on to any given sentence, even a principle of logic, in the face of recalcitrant experience.
3. The analytic–synthetic distinction is nonetheless real: 'All bachelors are unmarried' is true purely in virtue of meaning, whereas 'All bachelors are unhappy' would need cooperation from the world.
4. Synonymy is what grounds that distinction: 'bachelor' just means 'unmarried adult male', so the truth of the sentence in (3) is a matter of word meanings alone.
5. There is a principled difference between changing a belief and changing the meaning of my terms.

I can tell the holism is pulling against something in this list, but I can't articulate exactly what has to go, or whether a careful caveat would let me keep the whole package.
