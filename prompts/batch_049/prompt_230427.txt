I cover condensed matter physics and energy science for a general-interest magazine, so I'm comfortable with statistical mechanics — microstates, macrostates, ensembles, entropy, coarse-graining, ergodicity, detailed balance — but I never got much beyond a survey course or two in philosophy of mind. Recently I heard a philosopher of physics defend a view I can't shake: if panpsychism is true, then the background churn of the physical world is ethically loaded all the way down. His version was that every microphysical interaction has a tiny phenomenal character, a sliver of valence, and the total valence flux of any physical process is roughly proportional to the number of microstate-to-microstate transitions it runs through. So a cup of coffee cooling on a desk isn't just a jump in entropy; it's a vast parade of tiny felt events with some net pleasure/pain balance. Call it micro-suffering for short.

I'd like you to restate this position, plus the standard physicalist comeback (roughly: this micro-valence is an extra, undetectable property that does no work in any equation and gets shaved off by Occam's razor), in the vocabulary of statistical mechanics as precisely as you can. Map the claims onto microstates, coarse-graining, ensemble averages, emergent macrovariables, the information stat mech throws away, maybe Landauer's principle — whatever does real work. Then give me the residue report: which parts map cleanly, which parts you had to fudge, and which parts can't be expressed in stat-mech terms at all. I especially want to know whether the fact that statisical mechanics works perfectly without ever mentioning valence is a decisive strike against the panpsychist idea, or just a sign that stat mech is deliberately blind to the very thing the panpsychist is pointing at. My hunch is that the whole view is a category mistake, but I suspect my physics vocabulary is quietly doing the category-mistake work — so tell me where the residue is and whether it marks a defect of the physcist's framework or a sign the original claim was less clear than it sounds.
