so for my philosophy club I'm writing this short essay about the idea that relativity makes the future already real, like a 4D block. I don't need you to write the essay for me, but I'd really like comments on the argument before I turn it in. Also please keep the comments in American English (spelling/grammar wise).

Here's the setup. I'm standing outside at night looking at Andromeda, which is about 2.5 million light years away. In my frame there's some definite-ish thing happening "right now" over there, like a regular Tuesday on some planet. But simultaneity is relative. A spaceship gliding past Earth toward Andromeda and another one gliding the other way won't slice up Andromeda's timeline the same way I do: one may include events I'd call days in the future in its "now," while the other includes events I'd call days in the past. And no frame is more correct than another.

So here's the move I want to make in the essay: if the things I call future over there are already "present" for some legit observer, then they must already be real in a frame-independent way. Otherwise you'd have to say reality itself is different depending on your random velocity, which seems crazy. So the future is already out there — past, present, and future form a block. And that seems like it would make free will kind of fake, or at least make the future fixed.

That's the gist. My questions:

1. Is this argument actually valid? I'm worried it only works for events that are spacelike seperated from me, like stuff in Andromeda. My own future self is in my light cone, and all observers agree it's later than me, so maybe I haven't shown my own future is fixed, just that far-away events have frame-relative slices.

2. Am I using "real" in two different ways? Like, "real for an observer" versus "real full stop." Maybe all I've shown is that our labels get slippery, not that ontology changed.

3. If the block universe is true anyway, does it actually threaten free will? I keep seeing people say no, the block just records everything, it doesn't force you — but that feels like a wording trick and I can't say why.

Can you tell me the strongest objection I'm missing, and whether my step from "someone else's now includes my future" to "the future is already fixed" is doing something sneaky? If a specific example (like Andromeda) actually makes the essay weaker, say that too.
