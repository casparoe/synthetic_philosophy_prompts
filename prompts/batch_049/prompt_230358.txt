Write a parody of a sovereign credit-rating report, around 700 words, in the deadpan, technically ornate voice of a boutique risk consultancy. The fictional firm, Cliodynamic Analytics, rates countries not on their debt but on their position in a secular cycle of political instability, as modeled by the structural-demographic tradition in quantitative macrohistory. The report should assign a "Secular Cycle Outlook" with the full apparatus of a bond rater: a headline rating, a stable or negative outlook, point estimates carried to two decimal places, a composite political-stress index, and a downgrade for a country that has slipped from the integrative into the disintegrative phase. The firm's inputs should be the model's stock components — popular immiseration, elite overproduction, state fiscal distress, and mass-mobilization potential — treated as if they were hard quarterly statistics. The humor must come from the actual content and remain straight-faced: multi-century cycles calibrated on sparse and uneven historical records, retrodiction presented as validated forecasting, pseudo-precise confidence intervals around a revolution, and political collapse priced as an actuarial event. Include two quotations: one from a "concerned traditional historian" objecting that the cycle is visible only after the fact and that the underlying database smooths over its sources, and one from the firm's chief scientist denying that publishing the outlook could alter the trajectory it projects. The piece should exaggerate the program's real features rather than invent a straw man, and it should be readable to someone who has never heard of cliodynamics while remaining fair enough that a specialist in the cliodynamics debates recognizes the objections being satirized without the report stating them outright.
