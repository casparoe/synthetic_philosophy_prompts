Recently I went down a rabbit hole reading about the causal exclusion argument from philosophy of mind. The jist I got is something like this: if every physical event already has a sufficient physical cause (causal closure of the physical), then a mental event that's supposed to be distinct from physical events can't also be the cause of your action, unless we accept constant overdetermination; so either mental states just are physical states, or the mental is epiphenomenal. I keep feeling like this maps almost one-to-one onto the way I think about games: my script is the "mental" thing, the physics engine (the simulation state it updates every tick) is the "physical" thing, and the worry that the mental gets excluded is just the worry that the script isn't really causing anything because the physics engine would be causally closed on its own once the impulse is applied.
