My machine-learning reading group keeps arriving at a fixed point I find more confident than the reasoning actually supports. The fixed point is the no-free-lunch theorem, which gets summarized roughly as follows: averaged across all possible target functions, or all possible objective functions, no learning algorithm beats any other, so whatever success a learner enjoys on our problems is a product of its inductive bias happening to align with the structure of those problems. Everyone in the room takes this as a settled formal result. But then the group slides, usually without noticing, into a far stronger philosophical thesis: there is no rational core to induction, no general-purpose reasonableness in learning, and the track record of any method is a parochial fact about the kind of world we inhabit rather than evidence that the method is any good. The strong thesis is the position I want pressure-tested, not the theorem itself.

What would have to be true for that thesis to be false? I want an honest map of its exposure. Which assumptions of the no-free-lunch argument could in principle fail, and what would the space of problems—or the world—have to look like for each failure to matter? Which of these escape routes are live options and which are merely logical possibilities? I am not looking for a sales pitch against the thesis; I want to know what a committed defender would themselves concede would count as evidence against it. Please distinguish evidence that would merely be awkward from evidence that would be decisive, and say where a formal result, an empirical surprise, and a conceptual clarification would respectively fit. I would personally find it most persuasive if the notion of a natural or learnable problem class could be made precise enough to break the theorem's symmetry without smuggling in the very inductive biases whose success is supposed to be surprising. Finally, tell me whether the thesis, once its defenders have finished qualifying it, still risks anything at all, and whether that insulation is a strength or a reason to stop taking the view seriously.
