1. uniqueness filter: the Holocaust is a category apart, so any comparison that treats it as one case among others is already minimizing.
2. survivor-prerogative filter: a comparison is legitimate only when members of the victimized group initiate it; for outsiders, comparing is a way of exercising authority over the dead.
3. mechanism filter: comparison is fine when it targets causal mechanisms (how mass killing gets organized) and obscene when it targets body counts or "scores."
4. arithmetic filter: any comparison that converts deaths into numbers is obscene by itself, regardless of what the numbers are used for.
5. oppression-olympics filter: comparison turns obscene when its function is to rank groups against each other for standing or resources, and it's fine when it builds solidarity.
6. concept-stretching filter: a comparison is suspect if the category (genocide, mass atrocity) has to be bent to fit one of the cases instead of doing real descriptive work.
7. lesson filter: a comparison passes only if it has a concrete preventive takeaway; comparing that just looks backward is vouyerism.
8. home-turf filter: you may run a comparison only if you'd be willing to apply the identical yardstick to a case where your own country or tradition is the perpetrator.
