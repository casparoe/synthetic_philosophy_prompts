I've mostly come around to Turchin-style structural-demographic macrohistory: pre-industrial states go through recurring secular cycles in which population growth, popular immiseration, elite overproduction, and state fiscal crisis feed each other until the whole thing snaps, and that something broadly similar is playing out right now. I want to know how much this view is actually risking. What would count against it, ranked from fatal to merely awkward? Which historical periods or cases does the model fit badly, or only fit with after-the-fact fiddling, and which of those failures would Turchin and his defenders themselves treat as serious rather than noise? What does the view predict that a no-cycles view doesn't, and where would the data have to come out differently for those predictions to fail? How much of the apparent fit is periodization, smoothing, or however the elite-overproduction variable gets coded, and has the whole thing been insulated into unfalsifiability? I'd rather be told that plainly than have the weaknesses softened.
