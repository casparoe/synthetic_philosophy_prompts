So me and my friend have been going in circles for months on that thing where you have to choose between one person getting tortured and a gigantic number of people each getting a single dust speck in the eye. For the record, I'm on the add-up side: I think with enough dust specks the total badness eventually outweights one torture. He's on the hard-line side: he thinks no amount of tiny one-off annoyances can stack up to equal even one real torture, because a speck and a torture aren't two points on a single scale. We've started to suspect we've been arguing passed each other, and we want a short document we could both sign.

Can you draft the joint statement we'd end up with after a good-faith adversarial collaboration? It shouldn't try to settle who's right. It should have:
- first, a numbered list of claims we both accept, about the setup itself and about what sort of claim we're making (we're not saying what to actually do to real people, just which of two forced options is worse);
- then a disagreements table with our residual disagreement stated exactly, each side worded so that side would accept its own phrasing;
- then the cruxes. I think one crux is whether the badness of a dust speck has a ceiling no matter how many specks there are, or instead grows without bound. Another is whether a single torture is itself just a very long chain of tiny bad moments, because that seems like it corners his view. And a third might be whether it matters that the specks are spread across many different people rather than all dropped on one person over time. For each crux, give a concrete tweak of the scenario that we'd both agree bears on it, and say what each of us would predict about it.

At the end, flag any part of our disagreement that you think is just a verbal mixup, and say which part and why.
