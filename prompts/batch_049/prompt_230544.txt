My Norwegian cousin and I have spent much of the past year in a low-grade argument about whether Sweden or Norway is the more liberal country. It started as a throwaway disagreement over something small—parental leave or alcohol retail, I no longer remember which—and has since accumulated a pile of evidence on both sides. Whenever one of us produces a statistic or points to a policy that feels decisive, the other responds that it is the wrong statistic, that it is outweighed by something else, or that it is not really a measure of liberalness at all. We both know the two countries reasonably well, and neither of us is simply ignorant. What bothers me is that we have not moved each other even slightly, and I genuinely cannot tell whether one of us is being stubborn, whether we mean different things by "liberal," or whether the question was never well posed in the first place. Consulting published rankings only made it worse: each one seems to bake in an answer through how it weights civil liberties against economic security, gender equality, drug policy, and so on.

I am not asking for a finger-in-the-wind verdict of the form "Sweden, with caveats." I would like you to work through what would have to be true for the comparative claim to be answerable at all: whether "liberal" picks out a single dimension or a cluster, what it would take for one country to count as more liberal overall when it leads on some components and lags on others, and what a fair, pre-registered procedure for settling the dispute would look like if my cousin and I were forced to bet. I am a longtime reader of rationalist blogs, so please give me explicit definitions, edge cases, and "your question is underdetermined until you specify X" rather than a polite both-sides treatment. If the task as I have posed it rests on a confused premise—if the honest conclusion is that our argument has largely been verbal or a dispute about weighting—say so plainly instead of picking a winner to be agreeable. And for whatever conclusions you do offer, flag your confidence and note where a thoughtful Norwegian pushing back on you would be most fair.
