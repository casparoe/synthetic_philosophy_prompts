I run a small research program on technology governance, and we're preparing a one-page pitch for a foundation to fund a six-month project on how historical analogy should guide thinking about transformative AI. My half-formed idea is that the familiar comparisons to nuclear weapons, the printing press, and the Industrial Revolution are usually treated as rival empirical bets about what AI will be like, but they are not really answers to the same question: nuclear weapons foreground a sudden expansion in destructive capability, the printing press foregrounds the cheap reproduction of information and the disruption of gatekeeping, and the Industrial Revolution foregrounds the reorganization of production, labor, and social life. Which analogy one reaches for may therefore already encode a view about which axis of transformation matters — and, more deeply, about whether a single historical case, selected after the fact and without a counterfactual, can license an expectation about a future technology at all.

Please turn this half-formed idea into a proposal that states the research question precisely; explains why the existing ways of using these analogies leave the question open; specifies a method that makes progress through philosophical analysis of analogical reasoning rather than by accumulating more historical detail, with some indication of what the work looks like month by month; says what a deliverable would look like for people actually making decisions about AI; and gives an honest account of what would count as the project failing. Keep it to one page and do not include citations or a bibliography — I'll add those myself.
