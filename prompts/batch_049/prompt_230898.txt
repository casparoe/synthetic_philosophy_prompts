I've gone down a rabbit hole lately about endurantism vs perdurantism, and the thing that's bugging me isn't which side is right, it's why the whole debate has been stuck for so long. From what I can tell, both sides agree on the physics: same stuff, same events, same spacetime. Yet endurantists say a thing persists by being wholly present at each moment it exists, so when I sit and then stand, its literally me, the whole me, at both times. Perdurantists say a thing is extended through time like it is through space, and I'm actually a four-dimensional object made of temporal parts, so my sitting-self and my standing-self are different parts of one bigger me. The arguments have been going back and forth for ages and no one seems to move.

My theory is that the disagreement isnt really about what the world is like at all; it's about what counts as a good explanation of change, or what same object over time is even supposed to mean. Is that right, or is there some real empirical or formal question hiding underneath that I'm missing? I don't want a list of every argument on both sides. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself: is it verbal, is it about standards of evidence, is it different starting intuitions neither side can argue for, or is the question just badly posed? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress—or is this debate never going to end and that's not as bad as it sounds?
