ok so I've been going back and forth on skolem's paradox for years and I think I've landed on a package of views but I keep worrying they don't actually fit together. here they are:

(1) the upshot of skolem's paradox is that uncountability is model-relative, full stop. there's a countable model of first-order ZFC, so from outside the model there are only countably many 'sets' in it, while from inside the model the reals look uncountable. so "the reals are uncountable" isn't a fact about the reals simpliciter, it's a fact about the reals-in-a-model.

(2) but I have zero sympathy for people who say the same move makes the standard naturals into a figment. I think arithmetic has a unique intended model: the naturals are the smallest set containing zero and closed under successor, and second-order induction pins that down. nonstandard models of PA are a curiousity to me, not a threat.

(3) I think CH is genuinely indeterminate. not just that ZFC doesn't decide it — I don't think there's a fact of the matter about whether there's an infinite cardinal strictly between countable and the continuum. the independence results look to me like evidence of a gap in the concept, not just a gap in the axioms.

(4) but I'd also call myself a platonist about the cumulative hierarchy: the iterative conception of set describes one determinate structure, V, and I don't want to say that "there are uncountably many reals" is somehow not true.

I can't tell whether I'm quietly using "uncountable" and "true" in different senses across these, or whether one of them just has to go. do an audit: which of these pairs are in actual tension, which pairs only look tense until you get the first-order/second-order distinction straight, and what's the cheapest fix that leaves me believing something coherent — and what does that fix cost?
