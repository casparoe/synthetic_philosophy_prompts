I want a thought experiment built to order, for use in a course or discussion group on the interpretation of heritability statistics. Design a scenario involving a single population and a quantitative trait where all of the following hold at once:

(1) At the outset, the trait's narrow-sense heritability — additive genetic variance over total phenotypic variance — is approximately 0.8.
(2) A single, ordinary environmental intervention raises the population mean by at least one full standard deviation within one generation.
(3) Allele frequencies in the population do not change; there is no migration and no selective breeding.
(4) After the intervention, the trait's heritability is still approximately 0.8 or higher, so the case cannot be dismissed as one in which the environment simply replaced genes as the source of variation.

Constraints: The trait and intervention must be realistic and ordinary — something like a dietary or micronutrient intervention on a continuous trait such as adult height — with no science fiction, no exotic biology, and no variance numbers simply stipulated into place. The intervention should be uniform: it must shift essentially every individual by roughly the same amount, rather than one that rescues only the worst-off individuals. Do not base the case on genotype-environment correlation in the initial population.

The case is for catching the invalid inference from high heritability to low environmental malleability. It should be maximally awkward for a hereditarian reader, that is, someone who treats an h² of about 0.8 as meaning that the trait is 80 percent genetic and therefore that the environment can move only the leftover 20 percent. That reader should be led to predict that your intervention will be nearly useless and should turn out to be badly wrong.

After stating the scenario, do the check. State the trait, the environmental factor, and the relevant variance components before and after the intervention. Show, in variance-component terms, why heritability is about 0.8 at the outset, why the intervention raises the mean by at least one standard deviation, and why a uniform improvement can move the mean while leaving the variance ratio — and therefore heritability — essentially unchanged. Identify the structural feature of the case that does the work, and say what collapses if you remove it. Finish with the strongest attack a defender of the hereditarian reading could make against the case as a counterexample, along with the reply the case's designer should give.
