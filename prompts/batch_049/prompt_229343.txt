I'm writing up my views for a seminar on the evolution of signaling and I'd like a consistency check before I present. My current picture is this. (a) I'm convinced the handicap principle is basically correct: a signal stays honest only if its costly enough that low-quality senders can't afford it, and once a signal gets cheap, fakers invade and the signal stops carrying information. (b) But I also accept that honest signaling doesn't always require strategic cost — index signals, where the form of the signal is causally tied to the trait it advertises, can be honest on the cheap, and so can signals between parties whose interests mostly align, like close kin or cooperators. (c) I'd also defend the claim that conspicuous consumption in humans is a handicap signal of wealth or status, and that it's reliable for the straightforward reason that poorer people can't pay the absolute cost. (d) At the same time, I'm convinced humans fake handicap signals all the time — debt-financed luxury, leasing, counterfeit goods, carefully curated social media — and the internet has made this faking cheaper and more visible. (e) I lean toward the view that the meaning of human status signals is conventional: an expensive watch signals status only against a shared background of conventions, and conventions can change the cost structure of signaling — in principle they could make a display cheap or easy to copy. (f) Finally, I dislike it when people call handicap signals wasteful or irrational in a pejorative way; if the cost is what buys honesty, then the cost is functional, not a mistake.

I suspect these don't all hang together, but I'm not sure where the real problems are. Could you audit the set? I want to know which commitments (or combinations of them) are genuinely in tension, which only look like they conflict, and what the least costly revision would be to restore consistency, with some sense of what that revision costs me. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equal, and don't assume I'm attached to any one of the commitments in advance. Also, please say where the audit's reasoning is most likely to fail, and what consideration or evidence would change the verdict about the most serious tension.
