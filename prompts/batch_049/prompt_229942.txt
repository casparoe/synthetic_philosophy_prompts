I sit on my university's academic integrity board, and we must decide a case our precedents don't settle because they turn on explicit agreement, which is absent here. Four students in a graduate seminar kept a shared notes document all term. During the 48-hour take-home final, that document gradually filled with what became a complete plan for the compulsory essay: numbered structural suggestions, three worked examples (one highly idiosyncratic to the course), and comments such as "we can't all use this opening" and "someone should push back on this objection." All four submitted essays with different wording but the same structure, the same examples, and the same response to the same objection. The students acknowledge the document but deny they acted jointly: each describes his own edits as isolated suggestions made in parallel, one says he only read the document and assumed it reflected general discussion, and none will say who started the plan. Our policy defines collusion as "acting jointly to obtain an unfair academic advantage" but gives no test for when parallel contributions become joint action absent an explicit agreement. Draft the board's decision memo: recommend a finding as to which, if any, of the four acted jointly, articulate the test future cases should apply, and engage seriously with the strongest argument against your recommendation. Begin with the memo itself, with no preface, and put the recommendation in the first sentence, followed by the reasoning.
