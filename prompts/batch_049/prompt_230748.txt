You are to draft the joint closing statement of an adversarial collaboration between two historians who disagree about historical periodization, each holding their position in good faith. The two positions are these.

Period realism holds that the labels "Middle Ages," "Renaissance," and "Scientific Revolution" track genuine, if ragged, reorganizations of European intellectual and material life. Continuities across the boundaries exist, but the discontinuities are real, concentrated, and cross-cutting, and the labels earn their keep by capturing them.

Period constructivism holds that these labels are largely retrospective instruments, projected onto a past that was in fact continuous and geographically uneven. "Middle Ages" descends from a fourteenth- and fifteenth-century humanist story about a dark interval between antiquity and a revival; "Renaissance" began as a story of rebirth told by its own partisans and was consolidated as a historical category only later; "Scientific Revolution" is a twentieth-century historiographical arrangement that bundles together changes in astronomy, mechanics, medicine, and institutions that did not in fact happen together. On this view, the labels now distort more than they reveal.

The two parties have agreed on a format. Write the document in four sections.

1. Agreed claims. Numbered. No claim should simply restate one side's thesis or be vacuous. Cover at least: what period labels do to historical attention (selection, foregrounding, compression); the fact that change is uneven across places and domains; the fact that any retrospective narrative imposes some ordering; and the practical uses these labels have in teaching and scholarship.

2. Disagreements. A table with three columns: the matter in dispute; the period-realist formulation; the period-constructivist formulation. Each formulation should be one that its own side would accept as the exact statement of its view, not a caricature drawn up by the other.

3. Cruxes. The sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. Give at least four, and make them specific. I expect the list to include something like: whether actors' own self-descriptions (for example, fifteenth-century humanists' talk of rebirth after darkness) are evidence of a break or evidence only of an aspiration; whether measures of change actually cluster around the proposed boundaries or spread continuously; whether the Scientific Revolution names one reorganization or an assemblage of separately timed changes; and whether the existence of earlier renaissances (the Carolingian and the twelfth-century) makes the category stronger or emptier. Do not treat my expectations as a ceiling.

4. For each crux, a test or body of evidence both sides accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where no such test exists, say so rather than inventing one.

Close by judging whether any part of the dispute is verbal or a matter of emphasis rather than substance, saying which part and why. This is an exercise in historiographical reasoning, not bibliography: use examples and argument, not footnotes or citations.
