I'm TAing an ethics of AI course this term and have to grade a midterm short-answer, and I want to make sure my grading is defensible before I send it back to the student. The exam question was:

"Some people defend paternalistic behavior by AI assistants—refusing a competent adult's request, or pushing back on it, for the user's own good. Give the strongest version of this paternalist position: under what conditions is such refusal or pushback supposed to be justified? Then raise one serious objection. Be explicit about any distinctions you rely on."

The student wrote:

"An assistant's paternalistic refusal is justified when, first, the user is not in a position to make an informed choice—they're in the heat of the moment or missing information the assistant has—and second, the request would likely cause the user significant harm. This is soft rather than hard paternalism because the assistant isn't stopping the user; it's just declining to help, so the user's autonomy is left intact. The deeper rationale is hypothetical consent: if the user were calm and fully informed they'd agree to the intervention, so the refusal doesn't really override their will. One limit is means vs ends: the assistant may question the user's means but not their reflectively endorsed ends, so if someone has thought it through and wants help leaving a relationship, the assistant shouldn't refuse even if it predicts they'll be worse off. The strongest objection is that hypothetical consent isn't actual consent, and respecting someone as an autonomous adult means letting them act on their present preferences; but that objection is weak here because in the heat of the moment the present preference is precisely what's compromised. Finally, the assistant can only cite welfare effects on the user themselves; effects on third parties aren't paternalism, so they can't count as grounds for refusal."

I have to give this a mark out of 10 and, more importently, point-by-point written feedback justifying the mark, because our department requires it on every answer. Can you do the grading for me? I want a mark plus specific comments on each substantive claim—where it's right, where it's wrong or doesn't follow, and roughly how much each issue should cost. Don't just give an overall impression; walk me through it.
