I am convening an adversarial collaboration between two researchers who have spent a year arguing past each other about echo chambers and epistemic bubbles. Their positions have hardened into two camps, and I want you to draft the document the collaboration should have produced.

The coverage account maintains that an echo chamber is, at bottom, a severe epistemic bubble. The mechanism is selection: algorithmic curation, self-selected media and homophilic social networks keep dissenting voices and adverse evidence out of a person's information environment. The core pathology is absent evidence; the remedy is diversified exposure; and whatever resistance to correction remains after exposure is ordinary belief-protective cognition rather than a distinct echo-chamber phenomenon.

The trust-architecture account maintains that an echo chamber is not a bubble. It is a manipulated structure of epistemic trust: voices inside the community are treated as authoritative across questions, voices outside are systematically discredited before their content is weighed, and reports of that discrediting circulate as further evidence of outside bad faith. The core pathology is corrupted trust rather than missing evidence; dissenting content may be present but discounted, which is why mere diversification fails.

Please write the joint document they could both sign after a genuine good-faith collaboration. It should contain:

1. A numbered section of claims both sides can accept — about what the cases share, what partial correctness each side has conceded, and what both now reject. Make these claims precise rather than vacuous.
2. A disagreements table setting out the residual disagreements, with each side's position phrased so its own proponent would accept the wording.
3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that a settled answer to one would move at least one side.
4. For each crux, a concrete case or type of evidence both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I suspect one crux is whether repeated exposure to dissenting testimony inside an engineered-trust environment lowers or raises confidence in the insider view, and another is whether resistance of that sort could be produced by ordinary rational updating from fixed, non-extreme credences about source reliability, or whether it requires the trust structure to have installed self-sealing attitudes towards outsiders.
5. A short note on whether any part of the dispute turned out to be verbal, and if so which part and why.

In constructing the cruxes you may distinguish between evidence being present in a feed, evidence being attended to, and evidence being treated as evidence about the world rather than as evidence of a messenger's allegiance.

Finally, close with a short paragraph stating how confident you are in the document as drafted, and single out the point or formulation you are least sure of.
