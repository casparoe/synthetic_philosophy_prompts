I've been trying to train a small model to solve competition-math problems, and I got into an argument with a friend about how to supervise it. The final answers are easy to check automatically, but the intermediate lines are the annoying part: human labels are expensive, and an automatic checker for individual steps would be about as hard as the task itself. So I sketched out five options. (1) Outcome-only reward: reinforce correct final answers and ignore the middle entirely. (2) Human process supervision: dense thumbs-up/thumbs-down from human labelers on each line of every rollout. (3) Learned process reward model: train a separate model on a pile of human step-labels and let it score every step. (4) Outcome-only training plus best-of-N sampling at inference, selecting among whole rollouts without ever grading steps. (5) Verifiable-artifact supervision: a helper model first writes a formal proof or structured rationale, a cheap checker verifies only that, and the main model trains against the checker's verdict.

Could you grade these five S/A/B/C on a single dimension: if I actually ran this, how confident should I be that the model ends up with reasoning that's genuinely good rather than just shaped to satisfy whatever channel or verifier is scoring it?
