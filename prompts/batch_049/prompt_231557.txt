I've been turning karma over in my head for years, partly from how I was raised and partly from reading whatever essays and dharma talks I come across, and my partner keeps telling me I use the word to mean too many things at once. So I wrote down six things I actually, on reflection, stand by. I could use an outside check on whether the whole list can be true together.

Here's what I've got. 1. Karma is an impersonal law, the way a natural process is. There's no judge and no scorekeeper; it just runs. 2. What decides whether an action is good or bad karmically is the intention, not the actual result. Well-intentioned acts that go badly barely count against you, and good outcomes from selfish motives don't count for much. 3. Over the long run, across lifetimes, people genuinely get what they deserve. The good fortune and the painful things that show up in a life are the ripening of that person's own past actions. 4. There is no permanent self that owns the karma. A person is a flowing process—body, feelings, perceptions, impulses, awareness—and rebirth is more like lighting one candle from another than a soul moving to a new body. 5. We are obligated to help people whose suffering looks karmic. If someone has wrecked their life through their own choices, I still think you step in and help; you dont stand back and say that's their karma. 6. I can't bring myself to say that a baby born with a horrible disease deserved it because of something in a past life. That feels monstrous to me.

So here's what I'd like. Take on the role of a philosopher who knows both karma/Buddhist thought and the standard Western ideas about moral responsibility and desert, and audit this list. First, brainstorm every pair of commitments that could be in tension, including pairs that look tense on the surface but might not actually be. Then tell me which conflicts are real and which dissolve once you look closer. For the real ones, rank them by how severe they are, and for each say what I'd have to give up or rephrase to fix it, and what that change would cost me. Then tell me if there's a single change to one belief that would settle the most of it at once. I'd rather hear that the list doesnt hang together than be told it's all fine.
