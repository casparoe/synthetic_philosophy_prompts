I've been trying to figure out whether origin essentialism is really the specific claim about sperm and eggs or something weaker, because a lot of people online talk as if it's obviously the sperm-and-egg thing. My friend who took one metaphysics class gave me this summary of what he called "Kripke's origin essentialism":

"You couldn't have come from a different sperm and a different egg. Here's the argument. Consider a world where a different sperm fertilised your mother's egg. The person who results isn't you, because you are the person who came from that particular sperm and egg in the actual world. If you said that other person could have been you, you'd be saying a thing could be identical to something it isn't. Since the other-sperm person isn't you, and identity across worlds is all-or-nothing—every possible person either is you or isn't you—it follows that you couldn't have been born from that other sperm. Same goes for the egg. By the same reasoning you also couldn't have had different parents, and in fact you couldn't have been born at a different time or place, because a different time or place would have to involve a different sperm and egg. So origin essentialism is a pretty strong thesis: pretty much your whole coming-into-existence story is essential to you."

I'm suspicious of the leaps but I can't articulate them. Is this summary fair to the actual view? Go through it claim by claim: which parts would a careful origin essentialist accept, which are simplifications, which are just wrong, and does the "whole coming-into-existence story" conclusion at the end follow from anything in the argument? Also, is there a reading of the all-or-nothing point that makes it true but trivial, and does that reading do any work in the argument?
