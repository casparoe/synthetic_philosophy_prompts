Hey, so I'm a longtime LessWrong/ACX lurker and I fell down a rabbit hole recently. I was going back through some old epistemology sequences about how "knowledge" should cash out if you're a Bayesian who still wants something like justified true belief, and a friend and I got into a pretty heated argument about Nozick's tracking theory. The standard intro version I remember is: S knows p iff (1) p is true, (2) S believes p, (3) if p were false, S wouldn't believe p, and (4) if p were true, S would believe p. And famously Nozick says closure under known entailment fails: I can know I have hands but not know I'm not a brain in a vat, because in the nearest worlds where I'm a BIV I'd still think I have hands. That part is fine.

Where we got stuck is the method-relativization. The conditions are supposed to be relative to the method M by which S arrived at the belief, so "if p were false, S wouldn't believe p via M" — but what fixes the identity of M across worlds? If I form the belief by introspection-plus-background-assumptions, is that one method or several? It feels like you can always gerrymander M narrowly enough to make the counterfactual test trivially pass ("the method is: believe p only when p") or broadly enough to make it trivially fail, and then wether someone knows something ends up depending on an arbitrary verbal choice about method boundaries. My friend thinks this is just a bookkeeping problem, I think it might be deeper.

Can you explain this issue carefully? Specifically, I'd want: a clear statement of the tracking conditions, how the method-indexing is supposed to work in Nozick, why "same method across worlds" is genuinely hard to specify (with maybe an example or two where method individuation flips the verdict), and whether you think there's any non-arbitrary way to fix this that doesn't collapse into either externalism that ignores the subject's own perspective or a demand for infallibility. Feel free to be opinionated; I care more about the mess than the textbook gloss.
