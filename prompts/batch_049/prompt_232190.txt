Before I read any more on the teletransporter stuff I want to get clear on what I'm actually asking a theory to do. I don't care about personal identity as a piece of metaphysics; I care about what matters in survival, the thing that's supposed to tell me whether I should fear stepping into the machine or whether I should regard a future person as me for practical purposes. So write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of what matters in survival has to deliver. Cover the standard cases — the transporter that destroys you on Earth and rebuilds you on Mars from new matter, the version where the machine malfunctions and the original survives so there are now two of you, ordinary survival across decades, ordinary death, amnesia where you lose all your episodic memories but keep your habits, skills and commitments, and maybe one where the brain is replaced gradually over years — and say which of those cases a theory is allowed to get "wrong" or treat as a puzzle rather than a fixed verdict, if any. Also cover the distinctions a theory can't collapse: strict identity versus what I rationally care about, surviving versus having a duplicate, and connectedness versus continuity versus mere similarity (including the case where a molecule-for-molecule copy of me forms on Mars by coincidence with no causal link to me — the theory has to say why that copy isn't me, or why I shouldn't care about its future the way I care about my own). For each requirement give me the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just taste from one camp. Then flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied — that's the part I most want, because if the list itself is inconsistent that changes what I even look for in a theory. Don't cite Parfit or anyone else, just work the cases. Oh, and no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables — plain prose like a long email. And at the end tell me what would change your mind about the list: which case or argument would make you demote a requirement or add a new one, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
