I cover philosophy of probability for a science-and-ideas desk, and I'm working on a column about Van Fraassen's Reflection principle. I need you to argue that Reflection only looks like a rational requirement because people conflate two different things: (1) a synchronic, practical requirement not to intend to adopt a credence you now regard as unreasonable, and (2) a genuinely diachronic epistemic requirement that your current credences should line up with your expected future ones. Make the case that only (1) survives contact with the standard problem cases—anticipated memory loss, drugs that temporarily wreck your reasoning, knowing that you'll be irrational with age—and that (2) should be scrapped rather than patched with extra conditions.
