Last evening I had a long argument with a friend about act-consequentialism, and by the end he had stopped defending the theory and shifted to a different claim: that the demandingness objection carries no intellectual weight because it merely observes that moral demands can be costly or unpleasant, which is not an objection at all. I think that diagnosis flattens several distinct things the strongest versions of the objection are doing, and I would like a more precise accounting of how much each contributes.

Please treat the strongest form of the demandingness objection as composed of the following candidate elements:

(a) the maximizing structure of act-consequentialism, which leaves no principled stopping point before the act with the best available consequences;
(b) the agent-neutral or impartialist pressure to treat the agent's own projects and relationships as fungible resources on a par with a stranger's interests;
(c) the background empirical situation — the scale of remediable suffering and the reliability of ways to transfer resources toward it — that makes the theory's requirements expansive rather than merely occasional;
(d) an implicit, undertheorized assumption that moral requirements must leave room for autonomy or personal integrity, without which the objection would collapse into the bare complaint of discomfort;
(e) the assumption that a moral criterion is defective if it cannot be followed by ordinary agents across a whole life, as distinct from being satisfied in isolated acts; and
(f) the thought that a theory whose demands cannot be openly taught, publicly justified, or generally complied with without predictable defection fails a condition of interpersonal justifiability.

Allocate 100 points across these six elements according to how much of the objection's force each element actually supplies when the objection is put in its most persuasive form. For each element, give one or two sentences explaining the weight you assign. Then conclude with a short verdict: which single element, if removed, would cause the strongest version of the objection to collapse, and why the claim that it is only a complaint about unpleasantness fails to capture what that element is doing.
