My friend Dana and I spent the whole bus ride arguing about whether there is any limit to what you can turn into a just arrangement just by getting everyone to agree to it. I wrote down her verdicts on a bunch of cases from earlier. She thinks the Wilt Chamberlain example is completely fine: fans freely pay an extra quarter to watch him play, he ends up with a huge pile of money, and the new, much more unequal distribution is still just—it doesn't have to match any pattern like equality or need. She thinks that if a thief steals a lottery ticket and the ticket wins, the winnings belong to the original ticket holder, because the whole chain started from an unjust holding. She has no problem with someone gathering wild clams on a public beach and selling them; she thinks its no different from picking up a shell. She thinks a lazy heir inheriting a fortune from a parent who earned it honestly is just. A worker who signs a one-year fishing-boat contract with full information gets exactly the agreed wage and nothing more, even if the season turns out terrible. A fraudulent car sale gets undone, because fraud poisons the transfer. And she says taxing Wilt's extra earnings to redistribute to the worst-off would be unjust, even if it made the worst-off better.

Next week she has to rule on a new case we haven't discussed: a thirty-year-old, with full information and no threats, signs a contract selling himself into lifelong slavery to a creditor who has paid a large sum to the man's family; the man later changes his mind and wants out. I want to predict how Dana will come down on whether to enforce the contract and whether the resulting arrangement is just on her view. She seems to be running some kind of Nozick-style historical-entitlement view—just acquisition plus voluntary transfer is all there is—but I can't tell whether she'd add an inalienable-rights carve-out, where you cannot validly sell away the whole person even by choice. Both fit everything she has said so far, but they split exactly on this new case. What does each rule say about the slavery contract, which rule do you think she is actually running and why, and what follow-up case should I raise with her to settle which one it really is?
