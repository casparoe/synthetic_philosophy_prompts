I've started defending a friend's weird view, which worries me. She thinks an ant colony is literally a mind. Not a metaphor, not a useful shorthand, not 'as if' the colony decided things — an actual cognitive system, with individual ants playing the role of neurons and the pheromone trails, antennal contacts, and nest-site signaling playing the role of signals. A single ant is as dumb as a single neuron, she says, but the colony-level patterns are the thoughts. I've run out of my usual objections because she keeps having answers, so I want to come at it from the other direction.

Tell me what would count against this view. What would a colony (or any distributed system like one) have to be like for it to genuinely have a mind, and where do real ant colonies fall short? I want you to push on the hard points: whether there can be something it feels like from the inside to be the colony, whether the colony has a unified perspective or just thousands of local ones, whether the pheromone patterns actually represent the world or merely cause other behavior, whether building a robot swarm with the same signaling rules would give you a mind or only a simulation of one, and whether the view can avoid collapsing into either loose metaphor or the kind of panpsychism that grants minds to almost anything with feedback loops. Rank the objections from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about which of them a committed defender of the colony-as-mind view should actually concede count against her rather than explain away. Then tell me wether the view can be made precise enough to be falsifiable at all, or whether it's protected by its own vagueness.
