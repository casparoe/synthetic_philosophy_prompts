I have a friend whose moral judgments about animals are unusually stable across cases, and I want to predict how she will judge a new case before she knows about it. Her settled verdicts include the following. She regards it as seriously wrong to keep layer hens in battery cages or a parrot alone in a small, barren cage. She regards it as wrong to raise and kill a pig for food when one could eat otherwise, and wrong to kill a healthy dog for food even if it had been raised for that purpose. She regards it as wrong to use a chimpanzee in invasive biomedical research. She regards it as not wrong to eat oysters, mussels, or scallops. She regards it as not wrong to catch and kill a wild salmon for food, provided the death is quick.

Now consider this new case. A company proposes to farm octopuses for food, keeping them in tanks and killing them by chilling. My friend knows that octopuses are invertebrates and that they have large brains, flexible behavior, and unusual problem-solving abilities. What would she likely say? I am less interested in the bare prediction than in the following: identify the moral principle or principles that best explain her past verdicts, state which rival principles fit them nearly equally well, explain what each principle implies about the octopus case, say which prediction you favor and why, and name one further case that would cleanly separate the rival principles. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence—your predicted verdict and the principle you judge she is running—then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating the question.
