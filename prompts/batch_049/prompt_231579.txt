I keep having the same argument with my sister whenever one of us comes back from abroad, and I want to locate where it actually lives. Some places feel "tight": the queue is a queue rather than a suggestion, littering earns you a proper telling-off, you need a permit for everything, and even the bus stop has a correct way to stand at it. Some places feel "loose": people park on the kerb, rules get negotiated case by case, and somehow the city doesn't collapse. We mostly agree on the descriptions. We sharply disagree on which sort of place is better to live in.

Her credence is about 0.8, mine about 0.3, for this claim: other things being roughly equal — income, safety, democracy, diversity — a society that demands noticeably more everyday rule-following than present-day Britain (queues, noise, parking, permits, punctuality, litter) is, all told, a better place to be a randomly chosen resident of than one that demands noticeably less. By "better" I mean the typical resident would, on reflection, judge their day-to-day life to go better, not just that the pavements look nicer. Put differently, she thinks the socially optimal amount of everyday rule-following to demand is noticeably above present-day Britain; I think it is noticeably below.

Please don't just give me a bare number; decompose the thing. The sub-questions I can see it turning on are things like: whether loose norms are stable or are just an affordable luxury of rich, safe, high-trust places; whether tight norms protect people like her while quietly taxing outsiders, eccentrics, and anyone who hasn't internalised the unwritten rules; whether there is a coordination benefit to knowing everyone will comply that you only miss after its gone; whether a society can even choose its level of tightness directly, or whether tightness is just a symptom of underlying trust or inequality; and whether "tightness" is one dimension in the first place rather than a grab bag of legal strictness, social shaming, and personal punctiliousness that often pull apart. Add whatever terms you think are missing. Give each a rough probability, show how they combine into your number, and then tell me which single term my 0.3 and her 0.8 most plausibly ride on, and which term you are least confident about. If you can, say what one piece of evidence or natural experiment would move you the most. Write the whole thing in British English.
