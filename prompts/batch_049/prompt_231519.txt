I spent a long evening arguing with a friend about whether redistributive taxation can be just. He defended a Nozick-style position, and after we had gone back and forth for some time, he sent me the following summary of what he took himself to have been arguing:

"Nozick's entitlement theory says a distribution is just whenever it has come about through voluntary exchanges, and the resulting pattern — egalitarian or otherwise — is not what makes it just or unjust. His Wilt Chamberlain case is meant to show that free choice will disrupt any favored pattern: start from whatever distribution you regard as just, let spectators voluntarily pay to watch Chamberlain play, and Chamberlain ends up with much more while the original pattern is destroyed. To keep an egalitarian pattern from unraveling, a society would have to stop people from spending their money as they choose. That is why Nozick likens taxing earned income to forcing someone to work, and why he concludes that any state that redistributes wealth is unjust."

Something about this summary struck me as too strong; I argued, for my part, that it seemed to let too much rest on whatever people happened to have done voluntarily, but I could not make the objection stick. Could you go through his summary sentence by sentence and tell me which parts are faithful to Nozick, which parts are compressed or misleading, and whether the conclusions he draws actually follow from the Wilt Chamberlain argument? I would like to know whether my unease was justified and, if so, what precisely I should have pushed back on.
