Recently I read a philosophy blog's summary of Nozick's views about animals, and I'd like to know how much of it to trust. The summary said Nozick endorses utilitarianism for animals but Kantianism for humans: when animals are at stake we should maximize total welfare and may trade off one animal's pain against another's pleasure, but humans are protected by side-constraints that may not be overridden for the greater good. It went on to say that this is why Nozick defended factory farming — the human benefits of cheap meat simply go into the utilitarian ledger and outweigh the animal suffering, while the side-constraints never apply to animals. It closed with the verdict that Nozick's view is just utilitarianism with a self-serving exception for the species doing the calculating, and that Nozick himself was untroubled by this. I haven't read Nozick and can't tell where the blogger is reporting him accurately and where the spin starts. Please audit the summary claim by claim: which parts are fair to what Nozick actually says, which parts are subtle distortions, and whether that closing verdict follows from the parts that are accurate.
