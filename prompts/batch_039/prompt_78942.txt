I'm putting together a seminar session around one question: is the social brand of externalism about content doing any work that the natural-kind brand isn't already doing? Everyone in the room has seen the water/XYZ thing, so I need a case they havent seen, built to order. Can you construct one to these specs?

- Two subjects, molecule-for-molecule duplicates at the moment of the story: same brain, same memories, same dispositions, same beliefs about what their own words mean. No interrogation or test could tell them apart.
- Their worlds differ, but not in the chemical or microstructural make-up of anything. The difference has to sit in what other people do, believe or record — practices, norms, written rules, archives. So no XYZ, no fool's gold. Apart from the twinning itself, keep both worlds ordinary.
- Keep everything else fixed: the story of how the term got into each community, and the causal route from that introduction to the stuff it was introduced for, should be the same in both worlds. If a disagreement turns up, I want it traceable to the social difference alone.
- The term should be one each subject uses by deference to something their community treats as authoritative, and if you can manage it, make that authority non-human: a rulebook, a code, a machine, an archive. No living experts, no chemistry departments.
- What I want engineered: the two externalisms have to come apart. The Putnam-ish line (what a kind term means is fixed by the actual nature of the stuff out there, not by anyone's opinion about it) and the Burge-ish line (the content of your thoughts is fixed by the norms of your linguistic community, even when you're wrong about the subject matter) must disagree about whether the two subjects' words mean the same thing, while the internalist line (duplicates think the same thoughts) says they do mean the same. If that pattern is actually impossible, don't fudge it — say why, and give me the nearest pattern that is possible.

Then the audit, and take your time on it:

- For each of the three lines: what it says the term means in each world, and what is doing the fixing. Give me the extension in each world, not just a label.
- Does either externalism have to be stretched past its home turf to say anything here, and is that stretch legit or a sign I rigged the case?
- Which single feature of the case is doing the work? Delete it and walk me through the collapse.
- The best defence for whichever line comes out badly: how would they say the case begs the question, and does the defence land?
- And the verdicts have to fall out of the theories. If you can only get my pattern by building the answer into the setup, thats a fail — tell me so instead of papering over it.

Keep the case itself under 200 words; the audit can run as long as it needs to.
