so ive been stuck on the utility monster for about a week. the setup, as i understand it: one creature gets way more out of pleasure than the rest of us get out of anything, so if you add up welfare across everybody, the total comes out biggest when you give him everything and everyone else gets nothing. i keep going back and forth and i dont want to just trade intuitions, so i want to actually audit the analogy a guy used in a forum thread.

his line was: welfare is just money. a bank with one account holding a billion and a thousand accounts holding ten each has a bigger total than a bank with a thousand accounts holding ten each, and nobody says "the total is bigger but it's all in one account, so the total is somehow suspect." adding is adding. the monster is just an account with a huge balance. if that grosses you out, you're smuggling in a taste for spreading things around, and that taste isnt part of the total.

how far does that actually go? give me the mapping first — what plays the role of the account, the balance, the transfer, the exchange rate, the bank itself — then where it breaks. three things bug me and i cant tell if theyre real:

- you can move money between accounts. i cant take your enjoyment and put it in my account. surface difference, or does it sink the picture?
- money gets added across accounts because there's a market that fixes what a dollar is worth to anyone. is there anything playing that role for welfare between two different people? if not, is "adding" even the right verb?
- the monster only exists if one person's welfare can climb forever. if each extra bit of pleasure gives a bit less than the last, which is how everything else i know works, nobody can be a monster, the curve just flattens out. so is the monster a real possibility or an artifact of drawing the line straight up forever? and if his bliss is partly made out of other people's misery — the guy who loves watching people suffer — then feeding him isnt like moving money, it's like a furnace you shovel people into. can the money picture even see that, or does it go blind right there?

his conclusion is that feeding the monster is fine and our disgust is just a bias we should get over. if the breaks are real, does that conclusion survive, or was the analogy carrying it the whole time? and if you'd reach for a different comparison instead, what would it be?
