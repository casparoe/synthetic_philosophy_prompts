I moderate a big discussion site — philosophy, science and politics, about forty thousand members — and the thing that eats my week is threads where I can't tell if the two people disagree about the world or are just using a word diffrently: is Pluto a planet, is that group a religion or a cult, does the chatbot understand its own answers. I either lock the thread or let it run, and either way someone says I've silenced a real disagreement, and some of the time they're right. It's a few of these a week, I get five minutes each, I'm not a philosopher, and everything I do can be appealed to a two-person panel, so I want a procedure plus a canned line I can post for each outcome.

I know metaontology has machinery for this. What I've picked up: some questions are asked inside a framework whose rules are already fixed, others are about whether to adopt the framework at all; two people can mean different things by 'exists' or 'object' and neither of them is wrong; and there's a test where you restate both sides in shared vocabulary and see whether anything is left to argue about. I also get that 'verbal' isn't the same as 'trivial' — calling a group a cult does real work — so 'just semantics' can't be where I stop.

Turn that into the procedure: the questions in order, what counts as an answer to each, where it says verbal-and-done, where it says real disagreement, where it says verbal-but-worth-having, and where it hands off to me because the question is which meaning we should use rather than which one is true. Then run it on two threads — is Pluto a planet, and does the chatbot understand its own answers — and tell me which verdict I'm going to hate. And what can't it do? Most of my threads feel half verbal and half real and I don't know if that's inside the procedure or outside it. If I have to settle first whether any one vocabulary is the right one before I can run any of this, say so.
