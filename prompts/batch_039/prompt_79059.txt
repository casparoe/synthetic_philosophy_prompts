Me and two friends got into a thing about Molyneux's problem after a seminar — you know the setup, the guy born blind who learns to tell a sphere from a cube by touch and then gets his sight back, and the question is whether he could tell which visible thing is the sphere before touching them. The classic answer is no, fine, whatever. But Priya built this argument out of it and I genuinely can't find my way out, and I'm hoping you can. Here it is: (1) Before he touches the two things, he can't tell which visible shape is the sphere and which is the cube — that's the standard verdict. (2) If you can't tell which of two visible things is the sphere, then your visual experience doesn't present either of them as a sphere. (3) So at first sight, vision delivers no shapes at all, just colored patches spread out in two dimensions. (4) Whatever holds for him holds for us, so what vision gives us, prior to any learning, is also just a flat mosaic of colors; the three-dimensional world of solid, shaped objects is an inference we build on top. (5) An inference can be wrong — if the 3D world is something we construct from a 2D mosaic plus habits of association, then nothing stops it from being a systematic illusion, and we'd never catch it, since we'd only ever be comparing the construction to more of the same construction. (6) So we can't know that anything we see corresponds to a real three-dimensional world; the visual world is a useful fiction. I think (6) is nuts, and my gut says the whole thing proves way too much — it would sink touch and hearing the same way, and any perception at all, which is absurd. But Priya says the only way out is to deny (5), and (5) is just the truism that an inference from a flat mosaic could in principle go wrong, so if I deny it I'm basically a naive realist who thinks perception can't be fooled.

Tell me which step to reject and what rejecting it costs. I'd honestly rather keep (1) and (4) if I can — I do think the Molyneux answer is probably right, and I sort of buy that we construct the 3D world — but I want a real verdict, not a menu of options. Pick the step you think is weakest, say why it looked so fine when Priya stated it, and price out what giving it up would cost me elsewhere. Don't hedge, don't end on "it depends," and don't just list every premise as possibly wrong — land somewhere and defend it.
