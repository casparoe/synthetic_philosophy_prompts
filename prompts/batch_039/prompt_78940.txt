Write a short internal FAQ — the kind of procedural document a company circulates to new employees, in numbered question-and-answer form, deadpan throughout, roughly 700 words — for an organization that has replaced all of its ordinary decision-making with majority votes, on the authority of the Condorcet jury theorem. The comedy has to come from the theorem's actual conditions rather than from generic jokes about committees: the piece should be funny to someone who teaches the result and still legible to someone who has never heard of it, and it must never announce, summarize, or wink at the objections it is enacting. Let the organization keep the theorem's assumptions in the letter of its policies and betray them in their spirit — an onboarding process that guarantees every voter receives the same information and attends the same briefing; a competence requirement satisfied by an examination the organization writes and grades itself; a standing commitment to keep hiring voters, since more voters means more certainty; a ban on discussing a question before voting, justified by the need for independence; a practice of putting every question to the whole staff, including questions on which the staff is no better informed than a coin. At least once the arithmetic should be allowed to show: the FAQ should state, in the flat voice of policy, what becomes of a majority's accuracy when the voters are worse than chance and the body grows. The organization should also have run into the fact that its majorities can cycle — it prefers the first option to the second, the second to the third, and the third to the first — and should treat the resulting dependence on which questions are put in what order as a matter for the office that sets the agenda, without ever remarking that this is where the power now sits. A short, quoted dissenting memo from an in-house statistician may be included, provided it stays inside the corporate register. End on a line that a reader could take as either reassuring or appalling, and do not explain which.
