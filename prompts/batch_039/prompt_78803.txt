OK so theres a fight that keeps eating my reading group's time and I want a diagnosis rather than another round of it.

The fight: eliminativists say belief and desire aren't real -- folk psychology is a bad theory and mature cognitive science won't need it -- and the other side says sure, fine, maybe there's no little sentence in the head, but something is doing that work, so what exactly are you denying? Nobody ever moves.

My suspicion is that three seperate claims get asserted as if they were one. (a) Nothing in the head has the causal profile folk psych attributes to belief -- inferentially promiscuous, decoupled from stimulus, that sort of thing. (b) 'Belief' fails to refer: it's not just that folk psych is wrong, it's that the term doesn't pick anything out. (c) Cognitive science would do better to stop using belief-talk as a modeling primitive, even if beliefs exist. (a) feels empirical, (b) is a reference-under-a-false-theory question, (c) is research strategy, and people slide between them mid-sentence. Meanwhile everyone's gut analogy -- belief is like phlogiston, or like caloric, or like 'atom' which survived a theory that was wrong about it -- is doing enormous unargued work.

The part I actually want help with. I've been toying with a cut I don't think anyone has made cleanly, and I can't tell if it's the distinction the debate needs or just the old camps renamed. Call it state-eliminativism vs norm-eliminativism. State: there are no internal states with the functional profile of belief. Norm: ascriptions of belief aren't answerable to any standard of correctness -- there's no fact of the matter about what someone believes, so 'she believes that p' is a useful fiction on the order of 'the average family has 2.4 kids', never true or false. My hunch is that everything interesting is on the norm side, that the state side is basically empirical and boring, and that once you split them most of the shouting turns out to be two camps defending different halves. But I may just be reinventing instrumentalism vs realism, or the predictive-vs-explanatory-success thing, or the question of whether belief is a normative kind. If so, tell me which one, and why my cut isn't earning its keep.

Then: what would actually count as progress here? A result out of computational cognitive science? A semantics of reference failure? The field quietly dropping the word? Or nothing -- is this the kind of dispute that stays open forever because it's really about what we want an explanation of the mind to do? Diagnose it, don't adjudicate it. And don't hand me a reading list, I know Fodor and Churchland. I want the structure of the disagreement in your own words.

Side thing that might matter: people keep waving LLMs at this -- "it predicts our text without believing anything, so eliminativism wins" -- and I genuinely can't tell if that's a category error or a decent new argument. Say whether it bears on any of the above.
