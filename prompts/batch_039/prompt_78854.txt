I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of mind, and before my prospectus defence I want a consistency check on the position I've settled into. Here it is. (1) Physicalism: nothing fundamental beyond the physical, and consciousness is not a basic feature of the world. (2) A mental state is phenomenally conscious just in case it is the target of an appropriate higher-order representation — I mean this as a constitutive claim about what consciousness is, not as a correlation between consciousness and some monitoring process. (3) The phenomenal/access distinction is real, and report is a poor proxy for experience: a state can be phenomenally conscious while remaining unavailable for report, reasoning and control. (4) Corvids and cephalopods are probably phenomenally conscious. (5) The hard problem is a genuine explanatory demand, not a pseudo-problem — it isn't dissolved by pointing to function or to representation — although it doesn't show physicalism to be false.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and how severe is each genuine one? I'd like the tensions ranked rather than listed, each with the cheapest repair and what that repair costs, and then a note on which single revision would clean up the most.
