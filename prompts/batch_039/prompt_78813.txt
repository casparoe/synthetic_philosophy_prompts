I'm putting together a case for something I'm writing on social construction, and I want it built to order rather than pulled off the shelf.

Start with the split people usually draw: some kinds have members who can take up the label -- people who know how they've been classified and shift accordingly, so you get the label changing the members, the members changing the statistics, the statistics getting folded back into the label. Other kinds can't do that: a mineral, a soil type, a disease, a storm category, a crop variety. The label just sits on top of something that doesn't care. The standard line is that only the first sort gets a feedack loop.

I want the hard direction. Give me a kind that belongs squarely in the second camp -- nothing in the members themselves is aware of or reacting to anything -- but that still runs a full loop: the classification changes the thing classified, the changed thing changes what the classification is picking out, and round it goes again. And I want the loop to run through third parties, records, incentives or artifacts, not through anybody's self-conception. Stay away from race, gender and money, those are the examples everyone reaches for; pick something else and make it specific and plausible (a grading standard, a certification scheme, a diagnostic category, a species split, a rating threshold in some niche field, a land or building classification).

Constraints: one paragraph of scenario, present-day and realistic, no science fiction, no conscious artifacts, and don't smuggle in awareness at the last minute by making the members people who happen to read the label.

Then do the checks. Walk the loop step by step and name each hop. Say what the "loops need uptake by the members themselves" view has to say about your case -- does it deny its a real loop, and if so what does it call it instead? Say what the "a loop only needs the classification to be hooked into a causal network that routes back into the members" view says, and which of the two you think is right and why. Then the isolation test: make the classification private -- nobody acts on it, no records, no incentives riding on it -- and tell me whether the loop dies, and whether the case collapses into a plain description with a label pasted on it. Then tell me which single feature is doing the work, and what happens to your case if I remove it. Finish with the strongest way a defender of the first view attacks it, and whether that attack changes your mind.

If you think I've asked for something that can't be built, tell me which part and why instead of fudging it. No citations or reading list needed, I just want the reasoning.
