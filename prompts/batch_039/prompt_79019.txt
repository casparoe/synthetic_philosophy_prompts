Last week my reading group got stuck rehashing a hypothetical my advisor likes to pose, and I ended up as the designated referee, which I'm not qualified to be. Here's the setup. A theorist in 2019 proposes a model, and one of its headline predictions is a number that turns out to have been measured back in the 1970s, in a paper that was cited a handful of times and that the theorist had never heard of. The model is now routinely described as having been confirmed by that measurement.

Sam says that's a category error. The measurement predates the model, it was sitting in the literature the whole time, so it's old evidence, and old evidence can't confirm anything -- the model just got built around a fact that was already there. Dana says the "already there" is doing all the work and it's false: nobody knew it, it wasn't in any review article, the community had forgotten it, and "known" has to mean known to someone. Sam says known-to-whom is a dodge -- if being merely findable in a journal counts, then any model can claim confirmation from anything ever published, and the theorist gets credit for a prediction he never made. Dana says confirmation was never about the theorist's foresight, it's about the relation between the fact and the hypothesis, and that relation doesn't care about publication dates. Sam says fine, but the standard Bayesian story is that a fact you're certain of has probability 1, so it can't move anything; if the agent's ignorance is now the key variable, you've stopped doing Bayesianism and started doing sociology. Dana says the probability-1 thing was always a fiction for messy measurements -- the honest question is what the community would have bet the number was before anyone had seen it, and that's a wide distribution, not a spike, so the measurement does real work. Sam says then you're trading on an arbitrary counterfactual prior, and you've abandoned the very framework that made "old evidence" a problem in the first place.

Please adjudicate move by move and tell me what both of them are missing. I genuinely cant tell who's ahead.
