ok so im putting together a small zine of poems about half-formed problems in decision theory, and the one im stuck on is commitment races. heres the idea as i have it: two agents each want to lock themselves into a policy before the other locks in theirs, because committing first gets you the better outcome — but since both know that, each is tempted to commit earlier, and harder, and to a policy thats worse in itself but better against an opponent who hasnt committed yet. so the race drags both of them past the point either would have picked, and mutual early commitment is worse for both than if neither could commit at all. the nasty part is definately that there's no stopping point inside the race: any agreement to stop is itself a commitment, and it would have to be made before the race starts, which is exactly what you cant do.

i want a poem in terza rima — tercets, aba bcb cdc and so on — because i want the rhyme scheme to *be* the race. each tercet has to close on a sound the next one is then obliged to answer, so nobody can stop committing without breaking the form. let the two agents be the two rhyme families: the a-rhyme is one of them, the b-rhyme the other, so every commitment gets picked up and answered by a commitment from the other side. one concrete image should carry the whole thing (two hands on the same lever, two people walking backwards off a cliff, whatever — but it has to be the same image throughout). around 15-21 lines. end by breaking the chain: the last line shouldnt rhyme with anything, and it should be clear that the only reason the race stopped is that something outside it stopped it, not that anyone found a reason to stop. dont name any theorists or papers, and dont explain the concept to the reader — someone who knows the problem should recognize it, and someone who doesnt should still get a poem out of it.
