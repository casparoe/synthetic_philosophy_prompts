ok so im putting together a reading group on the whole 'whose values should aligned AI serve' mess and i want a dialogue to kick it off.

two characters, both sharp, neither a strawman:

the first is a total-welfare aggregator (Harsanyi-ish). her line: the AI's objective should be to maximize the total satisfaction of people's values, weighted equally, across everyone alive now and everyone who'll ever live. she thinks any scheme that refuses to weigh one person's values against another's is either incoherent or a sneaky way of handing a veto to whoever's loudest or most stubborn.

the second is a liberal neutralist. she says aggregation needs a common currency, and picking that currency is itself a substantive choice about the good life (is a unit of worship worth a unit of ice cream? a unit of one person's art worth another person's family?). so the AI should be a neutral tool: help each person pursue their own ends, and refuse to trade one person's conception of the good against another's, even when the arithmetic is tempting. she'll gladly concede that neutrality of effect is impossible, but insist neutrality of justification isn't, and thats the one that matters.

i want them to really go at each other, not talk past each other. get them onto the crux: can you give the AI any single global objective at all without baking in one particular conception of the good? if not, what is the neutralist's procedure actually doing — and is 'be fair, defer to a fair procedure' just one more value, with its own losers? and let them fight over (a) someone whose conception of the good is 'everyone else should be converted', (b) whether refusing to aggregate quietly favors whoever's already on top, (c) whether the aggregator can dodge the repugnant-conclusion / utility-monster stuff without just quietly giving up on aggregation.

dialogue form, real back-and-forth, no tidy resolution required. and definately dont have them cite famous papers at each other — i want the arguement, not the bibliography.
