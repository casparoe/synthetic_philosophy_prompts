our reading group has been stuck on the common prior assumption for like three months now and i'm starting to think we arent even disagreeing about the same thing, so i want a diagnosis instead of another round.

here's what we keep re-litigating. everyone's a Bayesian, beliefs come from a prior over states of the world. then there's the extra assumption, the one people call a doctrine, that when two agents are uncertain about the same things theyre drawing from one and the same prior over the whole state space. from that, plus the agents' posteriors being common knowledge, you get the agreement result: if i know what you believe and you know what i believe and we're both Bayesian and share the prior, our posteriors have to be the same, so we cant agree to disagree. there's a cousin result about markets: with a common prior you can't have purely speculative trade, because if you want to sell me something i have to wonder what you know that i dont.

the split is this. side A says it's basically a theorem about the logic of belief: if two people disagree, then either they're looking at different information or one of them has made a mistake. disagreement is a symptom, not a state of the world. side B says no, it's a substantive assumption dressed up as a normalization, it's probably false of actual people, and notice that the moment you drop it you get exactly the disagreement and trade and bubbles we observe, which shows the assumption is doing all the work and nothing licenses it. then side A says fine, your experimental subjects arent ideally rational or arent commonly known to be, and side B says thats unfalsifyable. and around we go.

two things keep muddying it for us. (1) someone claims you can always restore a common prior by enlarging the state space, so that the disagreement becomes disagreement about which cell of a bigger space you're in, i.e. about information rather than about priors. if thats right then the assumption isnt really an assumption, its a choice of vocabulary, and the real content of the theorem must live somewhere else. i dont know if thats right. (2) the concrete case we keep coming back to: you and i both think the same bet on a coin is favorable to us at the same odds. that seems impossible, and yet it also seems to happen constantly. is that a fact about rationality or a fact about what we mean by favorable?

my hypothesis, and i hold it loosely, is that this is mostly verbal: the word prior is doing two jobs, a psychological state in side B's sense and a device for representing a shared informational environment in side A's, and the two sides are answering different questions (how to characterize ideally rational belief vs. how to predict actual markets) under one heading. but i might be wrong, and there might be a real disagreement underneath about whether the no-trade prediction is actually true, or about whether an idealization has to be defeasible somewhere to be legitimate at all.

so diagnose it. verbal, or a hidden empirical claim, or a fight about what a model is supposed to do, or two different questions wearing one name? go by what each side actually argues rather than the slogans. and then tell me what would count as progress here — a distinction, a formal result, a case one side cant accommodate — or whether we should expect this one to stay stuck forever, and why. please dont tell me to go read something, the library is shut and i dont have internet anyway.
