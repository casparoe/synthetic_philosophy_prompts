Right, so I need help with something I've been chewing on for a while and keep failing to get straight, and I want a diagnosis rather than another survey of the literature, because I already know the literature.

Background so you know exactly what I'm after. I run a grad seminar on the metaphysics of properties. We do the conditional analysis of dispositions — the idea that calling a glass fragile is just saying it would break if struck, under the right conditions, in the right way — and then we do the two families of counterexample. First, the ones where something interferes with the disposition itself at the moment of manifestation: the glass that a spell makes unbreakable in the instant before it hits the floor, so the condition is met and the breaking never comes, and yet we still want to say it was fragile all along. Second, the ones where the disposition is perfectly intact but blocked: the glass packed in foam, as fragile as ever, which never breaks because the foam does the work. Then we read the repairs — add a clause about the conditions being intrinsic, or about nothing interfering, or about the causal basis being present and unaltered — and every repair gets a counterexample of its own, and so on. By the end of week three everyone agrees the simple analysis is dead, and everyone also agrees that nothing has been settled: half the room thinks it's a research programme with a few holes left, the other half thinks the whole business was a mistake, that dispositions are primitive and irreducible, and that trying to analyse one is like trying to analyse red in terms of how it would look to a normal observer. Which may be true, but it's a temperament, not an argument.
