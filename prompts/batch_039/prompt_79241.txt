The idea I'd be pitching came out of a reading group on collective action where we kept throwing everything under "the tragedy of the commons," and it started to bug me that the classic picture always assumes the thing getting destroyed is a stock: fish, pasture, groundwater, bandwidth, each act of appropriation nibbling a bit away. But the cases I actually care about don't look like that. A bus queue. People bagging their dog's mess. A wiki article that quietly stays accurate. The unwritten rule against leaving your cart in the middle of the lot. What degrades in those cases isn't a stock that gets eaten down unit by unit — it's the shared expectation that other people are still playing along. And it seems to flip: everyone does it, everyone does it, then two people don't and suddenly nobody does, with nothing about anyone's underlying incentives changing in between. The other oddity is that you can't wear it out by participating; the only way to damage it is to be seen not to. So the good looks non-rival in the usual sense and yet gets destroyed by visible non-use, which isn't how we normally talk about public goods either.

My hunch is that this is a genuinely different failure mode rather than the standard story with the knobs turned up, and that if it is, it changes what interventions even make sense — you'd be thinking about visibility and thresholds instead of prices and quotas.
