Consider three regulatory regimes that now coexist in the developed world for the same activity: the Nordic model, under which selling sex is lawful but buying it is a crime; the legalization model of Germany and the Netherlands, under which brothels are licensed, sex workers registered and taxed, and Germany's 2017 Prostitutes Protection Act imposes registration and a condom requirement; and the decriminalization model of New Zealand's 2003 Prostitution Reform Act, which repealed the offences of soliciting and brothel-keeping and treats the work as a regulated but ordinary occupation — while nonetheless barring migrant workers on temporary visas from the industry altogether. All three regimes also criminalize coercion, minors, and trafficking, and all three continue to prohibit pimping or procuring in some form.

Reconstruct the implicit philosophical commitments of each regime. What does the Nordic model presuppose about agency, consent, and the location of wrongdoing — that the seller is a victim rather than an agent, that the buyer is the responsible party, or that the transaction is intrinsically harmful to a class even when no individual is wronged? If the seller commits no offence, on what theory is the buyer culpable — harm to a third party, exploitation, complicity in a practice that degrades women as a group, or a perfectionist claim about what sex may be exchanged for? What does legalization presuppose about the commodification of sex and the nature of work — that sex work is work like any other, and if so why does the state register individual workers and mandate condom use in a way it does not for other risky trades? What does decriminalization presuppose about the ordinary employment relation, and how does the migrant exclusion sit with that presupposition: is it a border-control concession that merely fits the model, or does it reveal that the regime still conceives of sex work as a special category in which the state must control who may participate?

For each regime, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits with, and identify the points at which rival theories would license the same arrangements, so that the practice is philosophically underdetermined. Say explicitly where a regime is internally inconsistent, and where the appearance of inconsistency dissolves once the presuppositions are stated. Then take a definite position: which of the three has the most coherent set of commitments about harm, agency, and the proper role of the state, and which has the least, with your reasons. Where you think a regime's defenders would concede a genuine tension, say so rather than defending it for them.
