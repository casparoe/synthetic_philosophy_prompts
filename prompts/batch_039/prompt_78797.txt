I'm preparing a lecture on self-reference and I want a worked example, not a gesture at one. Give me an example of a conceptual issue that arises when a system has no way of determining whether its own rules are in force except by applying those very rules — so the arrangement appears self-supporting, yet somewhere it rests on a level that is taken as given rather than justified from within (call it the inviolate level, if that helps). Please avoid the examples I would otherwise reach for: the liar paradox, Gödel numbering, Escher's drawing hands, a constitution that prescribes its own amendment. I'd prefer an institution or mechanism I could describe in a paragraph — a body that adjudicates challenges to its own authority, a procedure that certifies the procedures that certify it, something of that kind. For whichever example you choose, identify the loop, say which level is being taken as given, and explain what happens when someone insists on justifying that level from inside the system. If the distinction between the loop and the taken-for-granted level collapses in your example — if there is genuinely nothing outside the circle — say so plainly, since that would be a more interesting result than the one I am expecting; and if the contrast I am drawing is itself confused, say that instead of manufacturing an illustration to fit it.
