I'm a fourth-year in a phil of mind program and I've been handed the dreams session in our big third-year seminar -- it's the Malcolm vs Dennett thing, Dreaming vs the 1976 "Are Dreams Experiences?" paper -- and I've got 80 minutes with about 25 students who've done the Meditations and nothing else Wittgensteinian. Don't explain the debate to me, I know it. I need a plan for teaching it.

Here's the spine I was going to use and I want you to tell me if it's wrong. Split it into (Q1) is the dream an experience at all, and (Q2) if it is, does it happen while you're asleep. My hunch is they'll mash those two questions together and that Dennett's move only lands if you hold them seperate -- but I'm worried the split does violence to Malcolm, since his line is sort of that Q2 never gets off the ground, because "the dream happened at 3am" isn't the kind of thing that can be true or false independently of what you say when you wake up. If the split distorts him, what spine should I use instead?

The example: I'm tempted to open with something that isn't a dream at all -- a hypnotised subject who sincerely reports a past life, first person, past tense, the works -- and only then swap in the dream case, on the theory that the machinery is easier to see when the topic isn't dreams. Worried that just makes them think we're doing false memories. Verdict please, and if you keep it, what the case has to have and what it must not have.

Then I want the misconceptions in the order they'll actually surface in the room, with the point in the sequence where I kill each one. I already know they'll read Malcolm as a behaviourist who thinks nothing's going on in your head, and someone will definitely bring up lucid dreaming -- tell me what to do with that one.

Give it to me as:

1. The spine and the order (~400 words) -- what has to be in place before what and why, and whether my Q1/Q2 split survives contact.
2. The carrying example (~300 words) -- one case to build the whole session on, what it needs and lacks, verdict on the hypnosis idea.
