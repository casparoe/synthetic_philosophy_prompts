I'm a philosopher by trade — epistemology and philosophy of science, though my actual research is on measurement, which is about as far from this as you can get — and I've been roped into running a seminar on conspiracy theories next term, so I'm reading my way into a literature I really dont know. There's a pattern I keep bumping into, both in the readings and in the wild, and I can't find a name for it, which is now bothering me more than it should.

Here's what I mean. Someone spends years insisting that the authorities are 'sitting on the study' — the one that would settle the whole thing. The study finally appears and says the boring thing. He doesn't say 'right, I was wrong about the study.' He says 'well, of course they'd publish that' — and if you push him, it turns out 'the study' now refers to a different study, still suppressed, and the first one was never really the point. A friend of mine spent a decade saying the moon landings were faked on the basis of a handful of photographic oddities; when each of those got a mundane optical explanation, nothing was dropped — he told me the fakery was 'more subtle than people think.' And the odd part is that neither of them is lying, and neither, I think, is even revising on the sly. They sincerely report that their view hasn't budged, and in their own heads I suspect it hasn't. What moves is what the words mean, slowly enough that neither the believer nor the person arguing with him clocks the switch.

I know all the obvious neighbours, obviously: Popper on unfalsifiability, Lakatos on the protective belt, Quine on the web of belief, the vague idea of a 'self-sealing' belief, persuasive definitions, moving the goalposts, and the plain old charge of equivocation. But none of them seems to be *this*. Lakatos and Quine are about bolting on or tinkering with claims elsewhere in the system, whereas here nothing is added and nothing is adjusted — the same sentence keeps getting uttered and its content quietly migrates underneath it. Equivocation and goalpost-shifting are things you *do*, in an argument, to a listener; this is something that happens to a person's belief over ten years without anyone, including the person, noticing. So: does this have a name, in this literature or in philosophy of language or mind — and if it does, I want to know exactly what it covers and what it leaves out. If it doesn't, coin me one and define it properly, with the boundaries marked: how is it different from ordinary gradual drift of opinion, from a change of mind the person won't own up to, and from someone being deliberately slippery? Some boundary cases would help me test it — is a teenager who's 'into' a band for a decade while their taste turns over completely doing the same thing? A political party that keeps its name and its conference slogans while reversing the policies? And is my friend and I even disagreeing at the end of all this, or is there no single proposition we're both assessing? Please write the answer in British English — the seminar's at a UK department and I'm tired of being scolded for my vowels and my z's.
