Bit of an odd one, and I'm not sure the thing I'm describing is even a real category, so bear with me.

Two people who agree on everything I can put a number on. Same credences — ask either of them for the probability of any event and you get the same number. Same tastes over outcomes — same ranking, and the same answer when you ask how much better one outcome is than another. And they still split. Offer a guaranteed 60k versus a 50/50 between 20k and 200k and Alex takes the gamble, Sam takes the sure thing. Same story with a friend choosing between a stable job and a startup with a decent shot at two lean years and a small shot at something great; same story with a city choosing between a transit plan that's fine for everyone and one with a small chance of disaster and a small chance of being great. In each case the gamble has the higher expected value, and in each case Sam still won't take it.

Sam isn't misreading the odds, and it's not that money stops meaning much to him as he gets richer — I've fixed the utilities, so that's already baked in. It's like there's a third dial sitting next to belief and desire, one that sets how much the *spread* of a bet counts: how heavy the step down from the good case to the mediocre case feels, relative to the step up from mediocre to best. Sam feels the down-steps more, Alex feels the up-steps more.

I've ruled out the two explanations I actually know. It isn't the prospect theory move where people overweight small probabilities — my two people report identical probabilities and still disagree, nothing is being misweighed. And it isnt diminishing marginal utility, for the reason above.

So does this third dial have a name? I've been calling it 'spread-sensitivity' to myself but I doubt thats a term. What I want is the carving, not the label. Is this just risk aversion, or a specific species of it, and what makes it something other than a relabeling of either probability weighting or utility curvature? How does it sit with the old result that if your preferences satisfy a few tidy axioms you're *forced* to be an expected-utility maximizer — which would make Sam's pattern a defect rather than a genuine dimension of preference, and I'd like to know whether that's the majority view or a live fight? And where are the edges: is it the same as being averse to variance, or the same as caring only about the worst case, or is that last one just a limiting case of it? Does it still apply when the probabilities aren't known at all, or is that a different thing wearing the same clothes? If there's no name for it, coin one, define it tightly enough that I could sort cases with it, and give me a couple of cases that sit on the border.
