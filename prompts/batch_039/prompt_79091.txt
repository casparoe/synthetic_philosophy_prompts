I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I'm writing a short piece for a film club on The Matrix. I want to read the film through the debate between self-sampling and self-indication in anthropic reasoning: roughly, whether you should treat yourself as a random observer drawn from all observers, or whether the fact that you exist is itself evidence that the world contains more observers. The presumptuous philosopher thought experiment seems to show one of these assumptions licensing absurdly confident conclusions about the size of the universe. Does The Matrix take a side? Neo is both an ordinary pod human and the prophesied One, and the film keeps cutting between the masses and the singular hero. I'd like a reading that takes both the film and the philosophical debate seriously: where the film dramatizes the problem, where it evades it, which scenes I should use to anchor the discussion, and whether it adds anything to the debate that the arguments alone don't.
