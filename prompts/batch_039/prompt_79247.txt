So I'm a novelist — second book, it's about a widow who keeps hearing her dead husband's voice running commentary on her day, not a ghost exactly, more like a radio she can't switch off — and I've had Hofstadter's I Am a Strange Loop on the go all summer, a few pages at a time, mostly in the bath, and now I've got a pile of views about selves that I suspect don't fit together. My friend Priya runs a little philosophy-of-mind reading group out of her kitchen (there's wine, we never finish the reading) and I promised I'd bring my own position on personal identity so everyone can go at it. So here's where I've landed. (1) An "I" isn't a thing, it's a loop — a pattern of self-reference that could in principle run on other hardware; nothing about me requires this particular brain. (2) When someone I love dies they don't just stop. There's a real, degraded version of them still turning over in the heads of everyone who knew them, and a version of me will keep running in my friends after I'm gone — not a metaphor, lower resolution, the same kind of thing, and I'd say that's a kind of survival, a real one, a difference of degree rather than of kind. (3) But I'm not a "copy" person about my own survival. If you scanned me and grew a duplicate that remembered everything and could pass for me at dinner, I'd still be the one who dies; the duplicate could have my house and my career and I genuinely don't care about it. What I care about is this thread, continuing. (4) Grief is warranted — when someone dies something irreplaceable goes out of the world, and it isn't only that we've lost our access to them, there's a real loss out there in the world and not just in us. (5) There's no fact of the matter about exactly where one self ends and another begins; those boundaries get drawn by our interests, like the edge of a cloud. (6) But moral responsibility needs the one punished to be the one who did it, and for that to work, personal identity has to be a fact, not something we draw.

I honestly don't know which of these is the weak one, that's why I'm asking. Go through the pairs that are actually pulling against each other, tell me how bad each one is and what would have to give, and be specific about which ones only look like conflicts and aren't — I'd rather have three real problems ranked than six vague ones. Tell me the cheapest single revision that cleans up the most and what I'd be losing by making it, and don't be shy about saying if one of my six is just a stock view with a name attached. And please don't send me off to read anything — I want the audit, not a syllabus; if a point of mine has a technical name, just say it in a sentence. I'm definately not going to be able to defend all six at Priya's table, so I'd like to know in advance which one to quietly drop.
