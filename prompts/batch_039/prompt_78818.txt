I am preparing a diagnostic exercise for a graduate seminar on Moore's paradox, and I want it to test whether students can apply the distinction between a statement's being false or internally inconsistent and its being absurd to assert. Consider the following ten sentences, each of which asserts something and then adds a remark about the speaker's own attitude toward it:

1. It is raining, but I do not believe that it is raining.
2. It is raining, but I believe that it is not raining.
3. It is raining, but I do not know that it is raining.
4. It is raining, but I am not certain that it is raining.
5. It is raining, but my credence that it is raining is only about 0.2.
6. It will rain tomorrow, but I do not believe that it will rain tomorrow.
7. It rained yesterday, but I do not believe that it rained yesterday.
8. It is raining, but my colleague does not believe that it is raining.
9. If it is raining but I do not believe that it is raining, then I should check the window.
10. It is raining, but I hope that it is not raining.

Sort these into tiers according to how fully each exhibits the absurdity of the paradigm case, as opposed to merely sounding odd, being plainly consistent, or failing to be assertable for some unrelated reason. Give a one-sentence justification for each item's placement, state explicitly the criterion you are using to draw the tiers, and flag any item whose placement you regard as genuinely contested.
