I've been trying to write down my own view of knowledge for a while now. I have no training in the subject, only a long-running argument with a friend who is reading philosophy at university and a habit of writing things out for myself on trains, and what I've ended up with is a set of commitments I'm rather attached to. I'd like to know whether they can all be held at once, because I suspect that at least two of them are quietly working against each other.

Here they are, roughly as I'd put them. (1) Knowing is a matter of your belief being glued to the facts: if the thing had been false, you would not have believed it. That is the whole of it as far as I'm concerned, which is why I'm perfectly happy to say that I know there is a mug on the desk in front of me, and quite unwilling to say that I know I am not a brain in a vat, since a brain in a vat would believe exactly what I now believe. (2) Knowledge is not inherited by everything that follows from it. I know there is a mug on the desk; there being a mug entails that I am not a brain in a vat; I do not know the second thing. Logic does not carry knowledge down the pipe with it. (3) Nearly everything I know, I know at second hand — the year of the Battle of Hastings, what a friend's sister does for a living, which of two identical-looking medicines is the stronger. Someone told me, and I count it as knowledge all the same, without any means of checking it for myself. (4) Knowing is a communal business rather than a private one: a claim becomes knowledge once the people placed to test it have tested it, and the rest of us are let in by being connected to them. (5) Knowledge and luck cannot live together: if it is an accident that you got it right, you did not know it.

What I want is an audit rather than encouragement. Which of these are genuinely pulling against one another, which merely look as though they are, and how severe is each real clash? Please rank the real ones by how much damage they do, give me the cheapest repair in each case, and be honest about what that repair costs me in the rest of the package — I am not interested in being told that the whole thing is broadly coherent, or that it can all be saved with some suitably weak reading of one word. Then tell me which single commitment, if I gave it up, would tidy up the most, and whether what would be left is still the picture I started with or something I would have quietly exchanged it for. Please write in British English.
