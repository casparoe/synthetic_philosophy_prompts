Every year I teach the regress arguement in my 300-level epistemology course and every year it lands the same way: the students pick up the three options (foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism), decide the infinitist is being silly, and we move on with ten minutes left in the hour. I'd like a handout that leaves them actually stuck, so write me an explanation pitched at a smart undergrad who's read one chapter of a textbook, doing three things.

First, what the regress argument actually needs in order to run -- which premise about justification you have to grant before "so the regress is vicious" follows at all, and which premise each of the three views denies. Keep this part short, they can get the taxonomy anywhere.

Second, the infinitist's side of it: why they think "the chain is infinite" is not the same claim as "the chain never gets off the ground", and then the standard reply that an infinite chain only ever promises justification, it never delivers it -- say whether that reply works or just begs the question by assuming justification has to be something you can finish.

Third, and this is the bit I actually care about: the same three horns seem to come back in Bayesian confirmation as the problem of the prior. A credence is supported by a prior together with a likelihood, the prior by something else, and so on. So is the Bayesian picture a fourth option the classical debate missed, a restatement of one of the three in probabilistic dress, or just a relocation of the regress? Don't hedge -- pick one and defend it.

Cap the whole thing at 700 words.
