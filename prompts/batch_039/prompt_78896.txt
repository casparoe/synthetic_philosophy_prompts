I am drafting a short essay on Daniel Dennett's contrast between cranes and skyhooks, and on his associated charge of greedy reductionism, both introduced in Darwin's Dangerous Idea, and I would like detailed comments on the draft. My thesis has two parts.

First, the crane/skyhook dichotomy is standardly presented as exhaustive: anything that does explanatory lifting either borrows its power from prior lifting (a crane) or does not (a skyhook). I argue it is not exhaustive. The distinction is defined relative to a hierarchy of explanations in which each level borrows from the one below, but the base of that hierarchy cannot be classified either way: whatever does the original lifting borrows from no prior crane, so it is not a crane by definition, yet calling it a skyhook is also wrong, since a skyhook is meant to be a suspicious, question-begging posit smuggled in at some level of the hierarchy rather than the unexplained ground floor of physics. So the question whether a given X is a crane or a skyhook is not always well-formed, and I claim Dennett's rhetoric against certain posited entities trades on this false exhaustiveness. My case study is the origin of life: the first self-replicator is where the crane regress must stop, and I argue it is neither crane nor skyhook, which shows the taxonomy to be incomplete rather than showing the first replicator to be mysterious.

Second, greedy reductionism is usually read as a metaphysical error, namely the claim that higher-level features are nothing over and above lower-level ones. I argue it is better read as a pragmatic verdict: a reduction is greedy when it discards the level-specific generalizations we need in order to predict and explain, so that it loses predictive and explanatory power, not when it is metaphysically false. On this reading, talk of cranes and skyhooks slides illicitly between the metaphysical and the pragmatic charge, and that slide is part of what makes the skyhook label rhetorically effective.

Could you comment on (a) whether I have Dennett's distinction and his use of greedy reductionism right, (b) whether the base-case objection identifies a genuine gap or is a triviality about how definitions work, (c) whether the pragmatic reading of greedy reductionism is defensible or loses the point, and (d) the strongest objection to the essay as a whole?
