I'm teahcing a grad seminar on democratic theory this term and I keep running into a distinction I can't get straight, so I want your help sorting it out. When people argue for choosing political decision-makers by lot, they seem to be leaning on two quite different ideas at once, and I suspect the difference matters more than the literature lets on. On one view, sortition is a lottery: what matters is that every citizen has an equal chance, that nobody can be excluded and nobody can buy their way in, and that the procedure is legitimate whatever it spits out -- the point is the equality of the draw, and the smallness of the body is a feature rather than a bug because it lets the members actually talk to each other. On the other view, sortition is a sample: what matters is that the body mirrors the population, that its composition can be described in the vocabulary of sampling error, that you can stratify it or weight it to hit targets, and that its decisions inherit some of the epistemic authority of a well-drawn survey, or of a Condorcet-style aggregation of many independent judgements. Compare these two conceptions properly for me -- not a feature table, but where they actually come apart, which one is doing the real justificatory work in the lottocracy proposals people float, and whether an institution can coherently be justified by both at the same time.

Here's the thing that pushed me to ask. A collegue in political science has a draft in which she argues that the sample conception is quietly colonising the lottery conception -- she calls it sample-creep -- and that every time a lottocratic body gets redesigned to be more representative (regional quotas, age bands, weighting each member's vote by the demographic cell they were drawn from) you lose the thing that made the lottery attractive in the first place, namely that nobody is standing over the draw deciding who ought to be in the room. She also thinks there's a squeeze: the epistemic case wants large numbers and independence, but deliberation wants small numbers and produces exactly the correlation that undercuts the aggregation argument. Is sample-creep a real problem or just a terminological worry? And is the squeeze fatal, survivable, or evidence that the two ideas were never one argument to begin with?
