I'm in a seminar on consciousness and I've gotten weirdly obsessed with the color phi effect — two dots flashed in quick succession, and people swear they see a single dot travel across the gap and change color partway through, even though (on the usual telling) the color it changes to is the color of the second dot, which arguably only shows up after the first dot has already been seen. Our whole discussion turned into a fight about whether the brain is doing something Orwellian (the experience happens and then gets rewritten before it reaches memory/report) or Stalinesque (the fake is assembled upstream and nothing ever "reaches the subject" in the first place), and I realised halfway through that I don't actually know what a theory of the timing of experience is even supposed to deliver. So before I read another paper, write me the desiderata: what must any adequate theory of the temporal order and timing of conscious experience be able to do? I mean the cases it has to get right (color phi, but also whatever else you think is load-bearing — metacontrast masking, the cutaneous rabbit, flash-lag, Libet-style readiness-potential stuff, the works), the distinctions it can't just collapse, like between when the experience happened and when the report of it happened, or between "the experience was revised" and "the report was revised", and any empirical findings it'd be embarassing for it to contradict. For each requirement give me the case or consideration behind it, and flag which ones are genuine desiderata and which are just prejudices of one camp.

Purpose, so you can calibrate: I want something strong enough to say whether "when did she experience that?" is even a well-posed question for a brain with no master clock — a friend in the group insists the real unit is the few-second specious present and that all the millisecond stuff is a distraction, and I want the list to settle whether that's still a live option or dead on arrival. Assume I don't want a Cartesian theater smuggled in (no single place where it all comes together and then gets watched), but tell me if you think that's a hard constraint or just a prejudice. And the part I most want: is any subset of these requirements jointly unsatisfiable? If two of them can't both be met — say some version of "the experience is whatever the subject could in principle report" together with "the experience can be revised before it's ever reportable" — that's the result I actually care about. Skip the scorecard of which theory wins, I'll do that in class; just don't hand me a list where every requirement is trivially satisfiable, because then it hasn't told me anything.
