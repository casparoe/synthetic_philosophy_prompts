I'm writing a feature on the strange inversion of reasoning that Darwin and Turing are each said to have performed: for Darwin, competence comes first and comprehension arrives afterward as an effect of it; for Turing, we give up on asking what thinking is and ask only what we would accept as evidence of it. A colleague insists these are really one argument, and I'm inclined to agree — which would let me close the piece by saying that if competence never required comprehension in the first place, the objection that a model merely simulates understanding is beside the point, and the only live question is how good the performance gets. I'm confident about the Darwin half and less sure I'm getting Turing right. How should I state the combined point so it lands, and what does each inversion actually license? Under 350 words, please.
