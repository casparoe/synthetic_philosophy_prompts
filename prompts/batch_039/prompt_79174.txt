Somethings been bugging me about the whole is-violence-declining debate and I can't tell whether the thing I'm noticing has a name. Here's the shape of it. A lot of the apparent drop seems to come from stuff that never used to count as violence now counting as violence — a husband beating his wife was a private matter, a teacher caning a boy was discipline — and if you want to go back and count those under today's definition you mostly can't, because the sources describe them in the old vocabulary and don't say enough. So the widening of the category is at the same time what makes the trend look like progress and what makes it unclear whether the trend is about the world or about us, and the attitudes that did the widening are the same attitudes we're now using to judge that things got better. (I know about per capita vs raw numbers, thats a different mess.) Is there an established name for this specific bind — not the boring point that definitions change, but the case where the thing being measured and the classification doing the measuring move together because both are downstream of the same change? How is it different from the observer effect, or from just saying concepts creep? Does it matter that the change is a moral improvement, or is it the same as what I half-remember about heart attacks being redefined once there was a blood test, so the counts jumped even if nothing changed in anyone's chest — or the autism numbers climbing as the criteria widened — because there the redefinition is just better detection rather than a moral achievement, and I can't tell if that's the same phenomenon or a cousin. And what would it take to actually answer the violence question: if I freeze today's definition and recount the past, do I get a fact of the matter or just a guess dressed up? I'd rather have the carving than the label — tell me where the edges are and what this isn't.
