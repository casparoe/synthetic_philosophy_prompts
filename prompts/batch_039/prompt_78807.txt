ok so im in 11th grade and im the student rep on the committee thats rewriting our schools discipline code, and next thursday is the public comment night before the board votes on whether to scrap out of school suspension and go with restorative justice instead (circles, repair agreements, that whole thing). im signed up to speak. three minutes, so like 400 words. the room is gonna be: parents who are convinced were just letting kids off the hook, two board members who used to be cops, a couple teachers who are on my side, and like fifteen students who showed up to watch. i need you to be two things at once — a speechwriter, and a philosopher who actually knows the free will literature cold, because the philosophy has to do real structural work in this speech, not sit at the top as a decoration.

heres my problem. our teacher did a unit on free will and i came out of it thinking determinism is probably true, and more to the point that nobody ever really deserves to suffer for what they did in the basic desert sense. i still think you have to do something when a kid hurts someone. i just dont think payback is the something. but my reason for wanting restorative justice IS that view, and if i stand up and say it the parents hear that this kid thinks nobody is responsible for anything and we lose the vote. also i might be wrong, which i dont love.

so: write the speech. it has to land for people who think determinism is false, and for people who think determinism is true but blame is fine anyway, without me pretending to be neutral. it cant lean on the word freedom as if we all obviously mean the same thing by it. and it cant cite studies, stats, or researchers — im not allowed to bring sources and i couldnt verify anything anyway — so it has to be built out of the ideas themselves.

stuff from the unit i want you to use properly, and tell me if ive mangled it: the consequence argument, where determinism means you couldnt have done otherwise so you arent responsible; the Frankfurt cases, where the guy couldnt have done otherwise and yet we still seem to blame him, which is supposed to split could-have-done-otherwise off from responsibility; semicompatibilism, Fischers move where you keep responsibility but drop the ability to do otherwise; the Strawson essay about reactive attitudes and the objective attitude, which is the worry i actually have about my own view, because if nobody deserves anything am i supposed to look at the kid across the circle like a busted toaster that needs fixing? that seems way worse than blaming him; and Pereboom on basic desert and hard incompatibilism, plus the manipulation argument and the other Strawson (Galen) with his regress / luck thing. tell me which of those can actually carry weight in three minutes and which ones will just eat my time. and be honest if the speech i want is impossible — if theres no way to be inspiring in that room without smuggling desert back in, say so instead of faking it.

before you write, brainstorm out loud for a bit: what the speech has to do in the first fifteen seconds, which two or three ideas do the heavy lifting, how the parents objection sounds in their own words, and where the speech has to stop. then write it, then give me a short set of notes on the choices you made. the speech itself has to be jargon free — nobody in that room wants to hear the word incompatibilism — but your notes to me can be technical. finally, tell me how confident you actually are in whats in the speech and in your advice, and single out the one point youre least sure about.
