I am a retired agricultural chemist, thirty-five years in the field, and in the decade since I have read enough moral philosophy to know how much I do not know. Please draft the joint statement that two camps in the debate over insecticides could both sign after a genuine adversarial collaboration. The first camp holds that the evidence for morally relevant experience in insects and other invertebrates is strong enough that the numbers killed by routine pest control make it one of the largest harms we inflict, and that precaution should therefore govern regulation. The second holds that invertebrate experience is too speculative a basis for policy, that the proper moral units are ecosystem function and human welfare, and that restricting insecticides has repeatedly produced worse outcomes: lost yield, resistance, more land converted to agriculture, and disease.

I want the claims both sides can sign, worded so that neither would wince; the remaining disagreements stated so that each side accepts the wording of its own position; the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side; and, for each crux, the case or body of evidence both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Say which parts of the dispute, if any, are merely verbal.

Keep it concrete by using one comparison throughout: a region where a stem-boring moth threatens a staple crop, managed either by a systemic neonicotinoid seed treatment or by a combination of pheromone mating disruption and repeated releases of a parasitoid wasp that consumes the larvae from within. Neither regime is clean, and I want the statement to face that. I suspect one crux is whether the relevant counterfactual is no pest control at all or the best available alternative, and another is whether harms to vast numbers of small animals aggregate the way harms to a few large ones do.

Tell me how confident you are in the claims you attribute to both sides, and single out the point you are least sure of. Write it in continuous prose, as one would write a long letter: no headings, no numbered lists, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
