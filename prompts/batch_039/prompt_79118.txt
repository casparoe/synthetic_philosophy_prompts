I am a teaching assistant for a first-year seminar on formal systems and creativity, and the last session before the break is on the MU-puzzle, which I present as follows. You begin with the string MI, and you may apply only four rules: (1) if a string ends in I, you may append a U; (2) if a string begins with M followed by some string x, you may add the string Mxx, that is, M followed by x doubled; (3) you may replace any occurrence of III with a single U; (4) you may delete any occurrence of UU. The task is to produce MU. The point of the exercise is that no sequence of applications ever gets there, and the reason is an invariant: the number of I's in any string you can derive is never a multiple of three — the axiom MI has one I, rule 2 doubles that count, rule 3 removes exactly three I's at a time, rule 4 does not touch them at all — while MU has zero I's, and zero is a multiple of three. What I want the students to feel by the end is that this observation is not itself something you can grind out by pushing strings around inside the system; you have to step outside the rules and ask what the rules leave untouched. That step is what gets called jootsing, jumping out of the system.

Since the session runs late and everyone will be tired, I would like to open it with a joke about the MU-puzzle and about jootsing. It has to be genuinely funny to someone who knows the puzzle, in the sense that the payoff turns on the actual content of the thing rather than on generic jokes about frustrating puzzles or about mathematicians being odd. What I am really after, if it can be done, is a joke whose punchline is only available from outside the system: a listener who has loyally internalised the four rules and nothing else should be set up to miss it, and a listener who makes the jump should get it instantly and feel slightly smug about it. Please do not just retell the puzzle or narrate someone failing at it; the humour should come from the structure. Keep it short enough to deliver aloud in about thirty seconds, no footnotes, and if it needs a character, invent one of your own rather than using a real mathematician. If the joke cannot be built that way and a purely inside-the-system joke is the best you can honestly do, then say so and give me that one instead.
