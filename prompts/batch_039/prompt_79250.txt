Background: I'm a third-year PhD student in Chinese philosophy, and I'm trying to write a chapter section comparing Mencius and Xunzi on human nature, but I'm bored of the standard Mencius-says-we're-good, Xunzi-says-we're-bad setup, because it makes the whole disagreement sound like an empirical bet about babies, and I don't think thats where the action is.

What I want from you is a comparison, not a survey — assume I know the texts. Heres my framing; tell me if it's any good, and then actually do it better if you can. I think both thinkers need a story about how you get from a starting point to full virtue. Mencius has the sprouts, the four beginnings, and the idea that you extend or cultivate them — the child-at-the-well reaction is supposed to show that something is already there, just small. Xunzi has deliberate effort plus ritual, and the image of the crooked wood that only goes straight once you steam it and force it into the frame. So both are in the business of turning an initial endowment into virtue, and the question is whether they're running the same mechanism at different settings, or two different kinds of mechanism.

A friend of mine in cognitive science gave me a distinction I've been nicking: latent versus constructed competence. A latent competence is already there in outline and just needs the right conditions to unfold; a constructed one has to be assembled out of parts that weren't doing that job before. I want to say Mencius = latent, Xunzi = constructed. Tell me where that mapping breaks down — and don't just say it's more complicated, give me the specific place it breaks.

Then the bit I actually care about. Is the Mencius/Xunzi dispute (a) empirical, about whats in us at birth, (b) conceptual, about what xing is supposed to mean, or (c) about what does the work in moral development, growth from inside versus shaping from outside? I suspect it's mostly (b) with a real (c) underneath, but I can't get the (b) claim to stop being deflationary — if they just use the word differently then the fight goes away, which can't be right. So: say what the non-verbal residue is once you grant the terminological point. What are they still disagreeing about, and why does it still matter?

Finally a verdict. Steelman both, then tell me which one comes out better on the founder problem. Xunzi needs a first sage or teacher who invents ritual even though human nature is bad — where did they come from? Mencius needs the sprouts strong enough to be worth extending but weak enough that most people never get there — so who reliably does the extending? If you had to pick one, which, and what does the other have to give up? No citations needed, I know the literature.
