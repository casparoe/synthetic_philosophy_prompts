I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for about fifteen years now, and I've noticed that the arguments I find most frustrating are the ones where I can never tell whether I'm disagreeing with a position or with a comment-section caricature of it. So I'd like to try something more direct. Please play a committed, well-read defender of quantum immortality: someone who takes the many-worlds interpretation seriously, regards the quantum suicide setup as a genuine argument rather than a morbid pun, and has already heard the standard objections and has answers to them. Stay in character until I say stop. You're allowed to concede a premise when it is genuinely forced on you, but the core claim -- that someone who runs the suicide experiment should expect to experience survival -- is not up for grabs, and you should push back hard when I try to make you pay a price for holding it. Talk like a careful philosopher who also reads the blogs: a paragraph or two per turn, plain prose, no bullet lists, no scolding me for supposedly having misread the physics. If you think my setup smuggles in an assumption, name it rather than quietly working around it. If you need to know how I'm imagining some detail of the apparatus, ask before answering.

Here is my opening move, and it is the thing that has kept me from converting. Take a device that fires a lethal charge into your head unless a quantum measurement comes out the lucky way, with luck probability of one in a billion. On your view I should expect to walk out unharmed; fine, that is the whole point of the thought experiment. Now turn a dial. Make the luck probability one in ten to the hundredth. Then go further and imagine that the device is not a switch at all, but a bomb that vaporizes you outright, with the added stipulation that some branch of the wavefunction contains a spontaneous fluctuation that reassembles the debris into a working, memory-intact copy of you -- and since no amplitude is exactly zero, such a branch exists, however thin. It seems to me that whatever reasoning carries you through the first case carries you through the last: something wakes up remembering my life and saying my name, and by your lights that something is me, so I should expect to survive a nuclear detonation at ground zero. That strikes me as absurd. But if you resist it, you owe me a principled line, and it cannot be the size of the amplitude, because the smallness of the amplitude is precisely the thing you told me not to care about. And if you relent and say the amplitude does matter after all -- that I should weight branches by measure and so expect to die whenever the death branch is heavier -- then I cannot see what remains of the view beyond the tautology that I will never experience the branch in which I do not exist. Which horn will you take, and what does the line you draw actually consist in?
