In my French Revolution course each student has to design and run one two-hour seminar session, and mine falls on the explanation of the Revolution and the Terror: whether a class-struggle reading, an ideology/political-culture reading, or sheer contingency does the explanatory work, and whether revolutions really do consume the people who make them. I have about eighteen students and a room I can rearrange. Please design an activity that makes them feel the force of all three explanations rather than hearing them described.

What I want specifically: the group should run the same scenario twice — identical roles, rules, starting conditions, and resources — with only a hidden deck of contingency events differing between the runs, so that they discover for themselves that the same structural pressures can issue in quite different outcomes, and cannot tell afterwards whether their choices or the draw did the work. Give me the role set and each role's private objective; a payoff scheme that runs on standing, legitimacy, or resources rather than money; a mechanic whereby the group builds a running list of suspects or denunciations — each addition cheap, each removal impossible, adjudication controlled by whoever currently holds most of some scarce good — so that the machinery they invent to survive the crisis ends up aimed at its inventors; a timeline for the two runs and the debrief; and instructions for what I should be doing while they play. No framing lecture beforehand, and the three explanations should not be named until the debrief, which should map particular moments in play onto each of them, including the moments where the activity's own rules manufacture the pattern an explanation then claims to discover.

Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this and how to handle it, and be honest about which feature of the historical question the activity cannot reproduce — in particular, whether the rules I have specified already bias the group toward one of the three readings rather than letting it emerge. Throughout, keep separate what specialists actually agree on from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading of the Terror as the consensus.
