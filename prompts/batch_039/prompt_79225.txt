my reading group is wrapping a term on Dennetts four grades of creature -- Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian -- and I want to close with a poem instead of a summary slide. four movements, one per grade, and the form has to do the work rather than the vocabulary: the first section should be stuck, nothing in it can vary or learn and it should feel like a sentence handed down rather than chosen; the second only gets its next line from what happens to it, so something has to go wrong for the line to change and it has to cost something; the third gets to try lines out and throw them away inside the poem before it commits to one, so the discarded candidates should still be visible; and the fourth is the one whose lines are mostly borrowed -- words, meters, whole phrases that came from other people and got picked up as tools, and it should be a little crowded in there because of it. 4 to 8 lines per section is fine, no fixed form, but it should be a real poem, not an illustration with line breaks in it: someone who knows the taxonomy should feel the ladder in the form, someone who doesn't should still enjoy it. If the four-step ladder is a bad way to picture this in the first place -- they're supposed to nest inside each other rather than replace each other, and real animals are mixes -- don't just write the ladder anyway, say so and do something else with the form. Also tell me at the end where you had to bend the ideas or lean on a contested reading of them. American English please.
