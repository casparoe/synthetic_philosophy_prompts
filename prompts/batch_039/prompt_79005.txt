I am preparing a handout for a graduate reading group on self-undermining commitments, and the session on the paradox of tolerance needs a decision tree rather than a summary of the literature. The puzzle, stated flatly: a community that tolerates everything appears committed to tolerating the movements that would abolish tolerance, and so appears committed to permitting its own destruction. Please build a flowchart that carries a reader from a starting commitment to a named position on how the puzzle should be resolved, or on whether it is a genuine puzzle at all.

Order the branch nodes so that earlier ones fix the reader's more basic commitments and later ones arise only for readers who have not already been separated off. As a minimum the tree should ask: what is the object of toleration (conduct, persons, beliefs, or the rules of the game themselves); what grounds the toleration (the tolerating community's own fallibility, reciprocity and respect, or prudence about conflict); whether toleration is conditional on the tolerated party's reciprocity; whether the limit on intolerance is a categorical rule or a threshold calibrated to threat; whether that limit may be applied preventively or only once intolerance is manifest; and who is to identify intolerance, by what procedure. The last node matters more than it is usually allowed to: an exception clause is itself something advocates may want changed, so say explicitly where each leaf stands on whether the tolerant must tolerate advocacy for revising the criterion of intolerance.

Two constraints. First, build the tree out of the logical space rather than out of a roll-call of authors: name each leaf for its commitment ('reciprocity-conditional toleration', 'prudential modus vivendi', 'the puzzle is an equivocation between legal non-interference and forbearance toward what one disapproves of', and so on), and do not identify a leaf by who held it. Second, the tree has to route the following cases, which are the ones the group will actually argue about: a party that pursues its ends only through legal and electoral means and would, once in power, dismantle the protections that let it compete; a small and unpopular minority that advocates intolerance while depending on toleration for its own survival; a faction within an otherwise tolerant coalition that invokes the coalition's tolerance norms to block its own expulsion; a person who holds intolerant convictions and acts on none of them; an association that excludes a group as a matter of doctrine but harms no one outside itself; and a dispute in which each side calls the other the intolerant one, so that the criterion is precisely what is at issue.

Include a leaf for the view that the puzzle is real and admits of no principled solution, only a choice between two losses, and a leaf for the view that the community's commitment is to a constitution or a set of practices rather than to a value, so that suppressing its enemies is not an inconsistency. Where two leaves return the same verdict on every case, mark them as unseparated rather than manufacturing a distinction; where a combination of answers is left unoccupied, note it and say briefly whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Give each leaf one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Eight to ten leaves, no more than eight branch nodes, and phrase every node as a question a reader can answer without already knowing where it leads.
