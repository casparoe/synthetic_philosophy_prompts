I'm doing a seminar paper on disjunctivism and the argument from illusion and I found a short encyclopedia-style entry that I can't decide about: either it's a good plain-English guide or it's quitely misleading me, and I don't know enough yet to tell. Roughly what it said:

The argument from illusion starts from cases where your senses let you down -- the stick that looks bent in water, the wall that looks pink through tinted glass -- and concludes that in those cases what you're directly aware of can't be the physical object, only some inner mental item; and since the same thing is going on in ordinary perception, everything you ever perceive is really that inner item. Disjunctivism is the reply that this only goes through if you assume a perception and a matching hallucination are states of the same fundamental kind, which the disjunctivist denies: they're seperate kinds of mental state. So the disjunctivist has to say that someone hallucinating a dagger isn't having an experience at all, and that perception and hallucination are distinguishable in principle -- you can't be wrong about whether you're really perceiving, because perceiving just is being directly in touch with the object. It also drew a line between a negative and a positive version of the view: the negative one only denies the shared-kind claim, the positive one also says what perception positively is. Then it wrapped up: disjunctivism is naive realism in fancy vocabulary, it answers the skeptic by definition instead of by argument, and it's implausible anyway since nobody can tell by introspection whether they're perceiving or hallucinating.

Two things I keep chewing on: the case where someone hallucinates a dagger in a place where there happens to be a real dagger they aren't seeing, and that negative/positive distinction, which I can't tell is a real fork in the literature or something the entry made up.

So: what should I keep, where is this actually unfair to disjunctivists, and does that last paragraph follow from the parts that are right? Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no intro and no wrap-up at the end.
