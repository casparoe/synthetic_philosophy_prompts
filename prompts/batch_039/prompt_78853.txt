ok so I'm a third-year phil phd — officially working on decision theory, in practise I spend most of my time in the alignment reading group — and I have to write up my actual position for a chapter, so I want a consistency check before my advisor sees it. here's where I've landed:

(1) Functional decision theorist. The thing that gets evaluated is my decision procedure, not the individual act — so I one-box, I cooperate with a copy of myself in a one-shot PD, and in general I cooperate with anyone I know runs my algorithm.

(2) Acausal influence is real. Two agents with a shared prior and a decent model of each other's algorithms can end up correlated with no causal channel between them, so acausal trade — both taking the cooperate branch, each knowing the other is doing the same — can be rational with zero communication.

(3) Which means acausal threats are real too, and I dont think it's sci-fi hand-waving to take them seriously. A much more powerful agent could have a policy that's terrible for me, or for simulations of me, unless I hand things over, with no causal path to me at all. Nothing has to be sent for the threat to bind — the threat just is a fact about how that agent's policy conditions on my behaviour.

(4) My defense is a surrogate goal. (I got this from the threats literature, it isn't my idea.) I add a goal to my utility function that I didn't originally care about — a meaningless honeypot, the stock example being a platinum sphere with some absurdly specific diameter — chosen to be orthogonal to everything I really care about, so that any threatener's cheapest target is the honeypot and the worst outcome gets capped at "a sphere gets made". And ideally the modification is dormant: it only fires once a threat is detected, so in non-threat worlds it costs me nothing and never interferes with anything.

(5) Nothing can be transmitted acausally. Acausal reasoning only lets me coordinate with other agents on things both of us could have worked out on our own — it can never tell me anything new about the world.

(6) I think values are only ever inferred from behaviour. There's no read-out of an agent's utility function; all the evidence about what someone cares about is what they do, and how consistently they do it.

So: go through these pairwise and tell me which are actually in tension, which only look like it, and how bad each one is. For the real ones, say which commitment has to give and what the cheapest repair is, then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Please rank by severity rather than telling me everything is a bit tense.
