I'm a machine-learning researcher — representation learning and equivariant architectures, mostly — so the vocabulary I actually think in is encoders and decoders, latent spaces, invariances and equivariances, linear probes, information bottlenecks, and the distinction between what a trained model's weights encode and what any downstream read-out can recover from them. That side of things I know cold. The philosophy of mind side I know only secondhand: I sat in on a reading group that did Dennett's 'Quining Qualia', and my reaction was that the argument is either decisive or cheating depending on which paragraph you're in, and I couldn't say which, because I couldn't get it out of his idiom and into mine. A colleague in the lab keeps telling me that if no measurement could distinguish two states then they aren't two states, which sounds like cheating to me but I can't articulate why. No need for a reading list or citations; I have the paper.

What I want is a systematic translation, both directions. Take the traditional case for qualia — experiences with properties that are ineffable, intrinsic, private, and directly given to the subject — and take Dennett's case against, which I take to be roughly this: that once you press on those properties with the tasting cases, the spectrum-inversion cases, and the rest of the intuition pumps, they turn out to be either unverifiable, or verifiable but empty, or fully describable in functional terms that leave nothing over, and that the appearance to the contrary comes from treating introspection as a species of observation. Restate both sides as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary, and be concrete rather than gestural. Is an inverted spectrum just a permutation of latent coordinates that leaves every input–output relation invariant, and if so is the hypothesis unverifiable in the same way an unidentifiable parameter is, or is that analogy doing illegitimate work? Is the taster case a confound between a change in the encoder and a change in the decoder — an identifiability failure in which the subject's own report is the only measurement and it is invariant under the swap? Does 'ineffable' become 'not recoverable by any probe the subject can run on itself', and does 'private' become 'no shared coordinate system across agents'? I would rather you commit to specific mappings, including ones you then have to qualify or retract, than keep everything at the level of 'these are just different frameworks'. And keep the translation honest: where a mapping only goes through because my vocabulary already assumes that to be real a state must be decodable by some probe, or that two systems are equivalent whenever there is an isomorphism between their representations, flag that assumption rather than letting it pass as though it were part of the original claim.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. Which claims about qualia survive the translation intact, which survive only with distortion (say exactly what gets bent), and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? For whatever fails to survive, I want your diagnosis of which way the residue cuts. If my framework is missing something real — the difference between a state's being represented and its being experienced; the vehicle/content distinction; the thought that introspective reports are performances rather than measurements — then the residue is a defect of my framework and Dennett's conclusion is too quick. If instead the residue is exactly what he predicted, a confused gloss on the fact that we cannot step outside our own read-out, then my hunch that qualia is an incoherent posit is right, but I want to be told whether it is right for the right reasons or merely because my vocabulary has no way of saying anything first-personal. End with a final line beginning 'Verdict:' that states in one sentence whether the residue is a defect of my framework or a vindication of the anti-qualia argument.
