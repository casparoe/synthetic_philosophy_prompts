I run a little "case of the week" thing in our epistemology reading group — I write a short vignette about somebody believing something, everyone commits to a verdict on whether the belief is justified before we talk, then we argue. We've been doing higher-order evidence all term, so most of the vignettes have the shape "you've just been handed a reason to think you're bad at this." My colleague Nadia always goes first, mostly because she's fast and totally unembarassed about committing in front of grad students. I've started keeping a list, partly out of curiosity and partly becuase I'm writing next week's case and want to guess her verdict in advance.

Her record so far. (1) Aisha reads a printed figure off a lab requisition and checks it against the carbon copy; both agree, nothing suggests either is wrong, and the figure is in fact right. Nadia: justified. (2) Ben believes his coauthor is annoyed with him because Mercury is in retrograde — it isn't, and the coauthor isn't. Nadia: not justified. (3) Cleo has verified from the logbook and the freezer's own data logger that the samples were held at 4°C, which they were; a visiting undergrad who has never set foot in the lab says "I'm pretty sure they were at room temp" and offers nothing else. Cleo keeps believing it. Nadia: still justified — she said an undergrad's vibe isn't evidence of anything. (4) Devi's grounds for thinking the reagent was contaminated were always thin, a couple of borderline readings. Then she gets a memo — which I stipulated to be reliable — saying the building water was dosed with a compound that causes subtle, easy-to-miss errors in exactly this kind of judgment, and nobody knows who drank it. She keeps believing her readings, which as it happens were right, and when we pushed her she said "yeah, I suppose my belief about the contamination isn't really justified." Nadia: not justified. I asked her whether it was the memo, the thin readings, or the fact that Devi basically conceded the point; she said "yes" and changed the subject, so I'm no wiser.

Next week: Hana adds up a short column of numbers twice, slowly, and gets 42 both times; one entry is smudged so she's squinting at it, and the sum really is 42. Then she learns from a source I'll stipulate to be reliable that the coffee machine has been dosing people with something that makes them quietly worse at arithmetic, that she's had four cups today, and that she can't tell whether she's affected. She keeps believing the sum is 42 and doesn't doubt for a second that her belief is justified — she figures if she were impaired she'd have gotten a different answer. What's Nadia going to say about Hana, and what should she say? What I actually want is the rule she seems to be running, any other rule that fits her four verdicts just as well, what each one predicts about Hana, and the vignette I should write the week after that would genuinely pull them apart. Give me your best guess about Nadia rather than a survey of positions, and be explicit about where the guess is likeliest to be wrong — which of her four verdicts, or which feature of Hana's case, would have to go differently for you to run a different rule.
