I write novels and honestly my whole method is getting the reader to sit in the dark and watch a character's mind happen — I think of attention as a spotlight, memory as the wings, the self as whoever's on stage when the lights come up. A philosopher friend told me this is the Cartesian theater, that Dennett killed it, and that there's no stage and no audience and no single moment where it all comes together, just multiple drafts being edited in parallel, consciousness being more like being famous than like being on a stage. Fine, but I want to know how far the theatre picture actually holds for what I do. Give me the correspondence table — stage, audience, spotlight, script, director, the fourth wall, the dark — then where the mapping breaks, and which bits of it are doing load-bearing work in my prose versus just staging. Then the real question: does the anti-theater argument actually earn its conclusion, or is "multiple drafts" just another picture with its own smuggled assumptions about time and about who's watching, and if so whats the better image?
