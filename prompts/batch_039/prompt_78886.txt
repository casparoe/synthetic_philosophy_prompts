My opening move: your inference requires free expression to be culture-neutral, and it is not. Every society you would call free is one that also insists, more loudly than any society before it, that a person's work should express an inner preference — and that insistence is itself a constraint, and a peculiar one, since it makes the self the authority that choice must answer to. So the divergence you point to is what a culture-first story predicts: the societies that most demand that choice track the self are the ones where the sexes diverge most, and the divergence is clearest precisely in what people report wanting, the measure most exposed to that demand. You are reading a cultural pressure as the removal of culture. What have you got that is not just the assumption that, underneath it all, the preferences are already there?
