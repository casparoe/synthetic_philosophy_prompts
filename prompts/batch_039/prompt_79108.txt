I've been reading about Malthus for a book club and I keep bumping into a move I dont have a word for. Malthus says population will outrun food and we get famine and misery; two centuries later the famine hasn't come, because yields went up and birth rates fell; and instead of anyone saying well that was wrong, the standard line is that he was right, just early — the checks are still there, the mechanism is still operating, it just hasn't bitten yet. Same shape with the people who've been saying since the seventies that we're about to run out of oil, or that the debt bubble has to pop, or my friend who has been predicting her sister's divorce since 2016 and counts herself vindicated by the fact that it's still on the table. What strikes me is that nothing gets added to the theory when the deadline passes. Nobody says the mechanism is slower than we thought, or offers a new factor — that would be a normal rescue, an extra assumption, and you could at least argue about whether it's ad hoc. Instead the deadline just slides, and since nobody ever said when, nothing has to be given up. The theory's content is exactly the same the day after the failed deadline as the day before; the only thing that changes is that the failure stops being counted as a failure. It's as if the prediction came with a free parameter that only ever gets fitted after the data is in.

So: does this have a name? I know what ad hoc means, I know Popper had something to say about theories that keep getting shielded from refutation, and I know the general idea of moving the goalposts, but none of those seem to be it exactly. An ad hoc rescue costs you something and a goalpost move is about how much evidence counts, whereas this seems to be about time specifically, and about a rescue that costs nothing because the theory never committed to anything in the first place. I'd like to know whether there's an established term, and if the honest answer is no I'd rather have a good coinage than a bad fit.

Either way I care more about the carving than the label. What exactly is this move, how is it different from an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis and from a theory that's simply unfalsifiable, and is there a principled line between genuinely early and permanently deferred — maybe something about whether the deadline was specified before the fact rather than after? Does it matter that in Malthus's case the mechanism he pointed at is real and arguably still present, so the defender isn't making anything up, just insisting that a mechanism being there and its effect arriving are two different claims? And where are the edges — is a doctor saying you'll need that knee replaced eventually the same thing, or just a reasonable hedge? Can you lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, no preamble and without restating my question.
