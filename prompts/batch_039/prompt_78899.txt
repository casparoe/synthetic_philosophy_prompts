I'm TAing the first-year PhD micro sequence this term and I've been handed the problem on iterated elimination for the final. Every year people just recite "eliminate dominated strategies" and "the order doesn't matter" and then fall apart the second I ask them why, so I want one long problem that's built around the matrix below. Please reproduce it exactly, numbers and all. Only player 1's payoffs are needed; that's all the problem uses.

Player 1 picks Top, Middle or Bottom. Player 2 picks Left or Right. Player 1's payoffs:

Top: 3 against Left, 3 against Right
Middle: 5 against Left, 2 against Right
Bottom: 2 against Left, 5 against Right

Four things I want the question to make them do, roughly in this order:

(1) Show that Top is strictly dominated by an equal-weight mix of Middle and Bottom, and that neither Middle nor Bottom on its own strictly dominates Top. Then have them say what that shows about how the definition of strict dominance has to be written: what the dominating object is allowed to be, and what the inequality has to hold against. I also want them to notice that Top is never a best response to either of player 2's pure strategies, and to say what that does and doesnt show.

(2) Explain why the order of elimination can't matter when dominance is strict, with the actual argument and not the slogan. I keep getting "by monotonicity" with nothing behind it. The answer should say what shrinks, why shrinking can't un-eliminate anything, and why that stops being true the moment you weaken the inequality.

(3) Show the same is false for weak dominance, with a small example they construct themselves (a 2x2 game is enough, and I'd like the answer to point out that it is). Give the numbers, run both elimination orders, and say concretely which order leaves which strategy standing and why.

(4) Say whether iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies can ever throw away a strategy that is used in some Nash equilibrium, settle it with an argument, and then give a concrete example of what changes when the dominance is only weak.

Deliverables: (a) the problem exactly as the students will see it, with the per-part marks printed on it, since our students see the breakdown; (b) a model answer of 700-900 words, self-contained, no name-dropping and no citations -- I don't want "by the standard lemma" standing in for an argument; (c) a marking scheme for the TAs, 20 marks total, split into the specific moves a top answer has to make, and for each move a one-liner on what the B-range answers do instead.

The failure modes I actually see and want the scheme to catch: people treating "strictly dominated" and "not a best response to any pure strategy" as the same thing; people treating order-independence as a fact about the elimination algorithm rather than about the dominance relation; and people who confidently say weak dominance is order-dependent but then hand in an example that turns out to be order-independent, so the scheme needs something to say about that too.

Level is first-year PhD, closed book, 75 minutes, no calculators. Keep the problem's own wording plain, no cute scenario dressing.
