Bit of an odd request, sorry in advance. Three months ago I got voluntold onto our town's flood readiness committee — I'm a drainage engineer, I know culverts, not decision theory — and somehow I've ended up as the person who writes the recommendation memos. We meet monthly, the town manager signs off, and the state wants a benefit-cost ratio with one number in it.

The problem is every number I get comes as a range. The consultant's surge model says the north district is somewhere between a 1-in-80 and a 1-in-400 annual chance, depending on which storm record you feed it, and the two hydrologists on the committee flatly disagree about how much upstream development has changed things. Finance wants a single dollar figure by the 14th. I have maybe six hours before the next meeting and no budget to hire anyone.

I've been reading around in the imprecise probability stuff — credal sets, lower and upper probabilities, that whole family — and I think it's the right frame, but I can't hand a committee of volunteers a textbook. So: write me a procedure I can actually run to get from a range to a recommendation.

What I need from it: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, when I'm allowed to collapse the range down to a point and what I have to write down when I do, and where the procedure has to stop and say this is a values question, a human decides it. It needs to handle the case where the range comes from experts genuinely disagreeing separately from the case where it comes from real randomness in the weather, because I suspect those shouldnt be treated the same way, and it needs to say what I do when they're mixed together, which they always are.

Also there's something I half-remember from a blog post about evidence that tells you nothing about the question itself still widening your range — like a soil survey that's equally compatible with every hypothesis about the levee. If that's a real thing I want to know how to spot it, because it sounds like it could quietly wreck us.

Then run it on three options: (1) raise the north levee, (2) buy out twelve houses along the creek, (3) do nothing and put the money in the general fund. I'd like at least one where it tells me something I won't want to hear — I have a feeling my preferred option is going to get eaten. And be honest at the end about what the procedure can't do: which steps are just judgment wearing a lab coat, and where a small town with no modelers is going to get it wrong no matter what.
