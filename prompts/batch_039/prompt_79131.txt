I'm a contract archaeologist finishing the report on a small rescue excavation, and I have to decide how much interpretation to put in it. We uncovered three houses at a riverside settlement and, between two of them, a pit that was filled in a single episode: eleven cattle skulls arranged in a rough circle, each with a flat stone resting on top, and almost nothing else. My co-director wants the report to call this a ritual deposit showing that the community venerated its ancestors and that its households were ranked; he argues that because the pit was filled in one go, we are looking at a single moment of behaviour and can read it almost like a photograph. Our finds specialist thinks it is structured refuse, and says 'ritual' is just the word we use for what we cannot explain. The descendant community we consulted has also asked us not to publish claims about what their ancestors believed. I have to write the interpretation section regardless. Please advise me on how to decide what the objects actually license me to say about people's beliefs and about how their community was organized, and how to write it so that a reader can tell description from inference from guesswork. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
