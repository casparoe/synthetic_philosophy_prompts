I am preparing a handout for a graduate seminar on rational choice, and I need a decision tree that sorts a respondent who reports the Allais preferences into a named position on the independence axiom. The tree should begin from a commitment the respondent can recognize without knowing any theory — that she prefers a certain prospect to a gamble that is better in expectation, and then, once a common chance of a poor outcome is added to both options in a second pair, prefers the gamble — and proceed only through questions she can answer without knowing which leaf they lead to.

Design each node to isolate a commitment rather than a label. The tree must at least be able to separate: the view that the reported pattern is a mistake; views that retain a single ranking of prospects while dropping or weakening independence (for instance by making the weight an outcome receives depend on its standing among the alternatives, or by making preference depend on anticipated regret); the view that the pattern is rational only when the agent has not reduced a compound lottery in thought; the view that the agent's real problem is diachronic, so that the issue is whether she may hold to an earlier plan against her later preference; and the view that the pattern is a general feature of how people treat certain outcomes rather than an axiom-level commitment.

Build the tree from the logical space first — the questions and the combinations of answers they permit — and only then locate the named positions within it. Two injections, since a bare Allais tree is easy to recite: include at least two cases that are not the textbook one, one in which the common consequence is a loss rather than a gain and one in which the outcomes are not money; and include a node asking whether an agent's ranking of a prospect may depend on which other prospects are on offer. Note any combination of answers that no one occupies and say briefly whether it is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any two leaves the tree cannot separate with the cases given.

Eight to twelve nodes, at most eight leaves; each leaf carries the name of the position, or is marked unnamed if no one defends it, plus one sentence on its least comfortable commitment. No citations or reading list — the students will get those separately.
