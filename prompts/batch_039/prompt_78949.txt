In a reading group I'm in, a friend has produced an argument about standing to blame that I can't shake, because each premise looks right and the conclusion is one I can't accept.

(1) To blame someone is to hold them to a standard of conduct.
(2) One can hold another to a standard only if one meets that standard oneself — you cannot demand of another what you do not yourself satisfy.
(3) Everyone who blames has, at some point, failed a standard of conduct comparable in seriousness to the one she invokes.
(4) So no one has standing to blame anyone.
(5) That is absurd: it would silence moral criticism altogether and hand wrongdoers an immunity.
(6) Therefore (2) is false, and the blamer's own record is irrelevant to her standing.

He draws (6) and concludes that the notion of standing should simply be dropped. I suspect (2) is being asked to do more work than it can, but I can't name what, and I would rather keep (5) and (2) than (6) if there is a way.

Two cases keep pulling me in opposite directions. The recovered hypocrite: someone who once did exactly what she now blames, but has owned it, made amends, and does not pretend otherwise — she seems to me to have standing, which makes (2) too strong as written. The manipulator: someone who is doing that very thing right now, to the person he blames for it — he seems to me to lack standing, which makes (2) not simply false. Whatever repairs the first case has to leave the second condemned. And I don't know what to say about a court: it blames defendants though it has no conduct of its own, and a judge's private failings don't cost the court its authority to convict — so either (2) fails for everyone, or there is a disanalogy between institutional and interpersonal blame that is doing the real work.

Give me a verdict, not a menu. Name the step you reject — or, if you think the argument is valid, the premise least costly to give up — explain why that step looked fine, and price the alternatives: which of the two cases you sacrifice, whether you end up with standing for everyone with clean hands, standing only for the wronged, or no standing at all, and what remains of the concept once you have paid. Reason from the cases rather than from who has said what; I don't want a survey.
