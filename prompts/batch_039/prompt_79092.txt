I am writing the conceptual-agenda section of a grant proposal for a small interdisciplinary group that studies whether a four-grade hierarchy of cognitive competence -- Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian -- can do real explanatory work. As we have been using the grades: Darwinian creatures have their competences fixed by natural selection, so their trial and error plays out across generations rather than within a single life; Skinnerian creatures can learn within a lifetime by reinforcement; Popperian creatures can represent candidate actions and their likely consequences and evaluate them internally, so that a failed trial costs them a hypothesis rather than their life; and Gregorian creatures are Popperian creatures equipped with mind-tools -- words, notations, external scaffolds, competences borrowed from other minds -- absorbed from a culture rather than a genome.

One of my co-investigators has just thrown a wrench into the framing. She argues that the four grades are not a ladder. In her view the Popperian and Gregorian grades are partially independent: a system can be scaffolded without being able to simulate and evaluate its own options, and she thinks current tool-using systems are precisely that case; conversely, internal simulation might be achievable with almost no cultural scaffolding. She also suspects that the Gregorian grade is not a grade of creature at all but a property of a population or a niche, in which case calling a single system Gregorian is a category error.

Our reviewers will fund conceptual work, not empirical machine learning. What we have is one postdoc for three years and a little travel money. Which open questions about this hierarchy most deserve that time, and in what order? For each, tell me why it is upstream of the others; what a satisfying answer could even look like, given that no one can run the experiment that would settle it; and whether it is the sort of question a philosopher can advance without empirical collaborators. Rank them, name what you would drop, be explicit about the criteria you are trading off, and say what argument or evidence would move a question up or down your list. If the right answer is that the hierarchy should be abandoned rather than patched, say so and say what should replace it. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
