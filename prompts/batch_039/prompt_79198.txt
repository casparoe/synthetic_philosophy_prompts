I write a science-and-ideas newsletter and I want to do a "which zombie argument are you actually running?" flowchart for readers -- a decision tree they fill in without knowing any of the labels, that lands them on a named position. Start from something like: can you really concieve of a being physically and functionally identical to you with no inner life at all? Then branch on the things that actually do the work -- whether the conceivability you've got is the idle "I can't see why not" kind or the ideal, fill-in-the-details kind; whether that kind of conceivability is meant to entail possibility; and if it isn't, whether the seeming still needs explaining or just gets explained away as a trick of our concepts. Every node has to be phrased as a question a reader can answer on its own, not as "are you a type-B physicalist?"

Leaves I want: a priori physicalism, a posteriori/type-B physicalism, naturalistic dualism, Russellian monism or panpsychism, illusionism, flat eliminativism about phenomenal experience, the "we just don't have the right concept of the physical yet" view, and the mysterian "no amount of this will ever help" view. Two cases I want routed through the tree because they're the ones that make people squirm: the partial zombie (your physical duplicate, but with only one of your experiences -- say pain and no colours) and the zombie who writes a perfectly good paper argueing that zombies are inconceivable. Does each land where the full zombie lands, or somewhere else?

Tell me which pairs of leaves your tree can't separate and why, and which combinations of answers nothing occupies -- is that because the view is incoherent, or just because nobody's bothered to hold it? Six to eight branch questions, no more than nine leaves.
