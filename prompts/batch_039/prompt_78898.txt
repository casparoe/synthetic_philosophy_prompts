I'm in a small reading group that's been working through the alignment literature's attempts to say what it would even mean for a system to be a good epistemic authority, and the one I keep coming back to is what (I'm fairly sure it's Christiano's) the literature calls ascription universality. As I've got it: a system A is epistemically dominant when, relative to any competitor C that's sufficiently simpler than A, A's explicit beliefs are at least as good as C's are by C's own lights, so that no way of reading C's beliefs off its behaviour would give you information A's explicit beliefs don't already contain. I want to write a short position piece for the group and I'd like a consistency check before I do, because I've been going back and forth with a friend who thinks the whole thing is conservatism in a labcoat and I can't tell whether she's found a real problem in one of my commitments or just dislikes where it points. Here's roughly where I've landed. (i) The dominance criterion is the right ideal, and not only for AI systems: for a teacher, a peer reviewer, an expert correcting a layperson, you should be at least as well informed as the person you're correcting, judged by their standards rather than yours. (ii) The by-their-own-lights part is load bearing, if I come out ahead only because I'm applying my standards then I haven't dominated anyone, I've just asserted myself, and the criterion collapses into agreeing with me. (iii) Epistemic norms are universal, a practice that isn't responsive to evidence and consistency isn't an alternative rationality, it's a broken one, so there's no rival standard I'm obliged to accommodate. (iv) But what a person is justified in believing depends on their evidence and their computational limits, so a creature with very different priors or much less computing power can be perfectly rational in believing things I think are false, and there's no view from nowhere from which rationality gets assessed. (v) Belief ascription is interpretation, and interpretation is underdetermined by behaviour, there's never one unique fact about what a system or a person really believes, and which ascription we settle on is partly our choice. (vi) For the criterion to be worth anything it has to do work from where we actually are, not from an idealised God's-eye position, if the most I can say is that an ideal reasoner would dominate C then the criterion isn't doing anything for me.
