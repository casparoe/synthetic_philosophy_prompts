I am a novelist at work on a book in which a woman leaves a small Scandinavian town, prospers in America, and comes home for a funeral, and my difficulty is that my characters keep talking past one another; I have come to suspect that their quarrel is conceptual rather than dramatic. The Law of Jante, tall-poppy syndrome, and the American Dream are habitually filed as three attitudes toward success, but I doubt that they disagree about whether success is good — they disagree about what is being regulated: the achievement, the claim to it, or the manner of carrying it. Please explain the conceptual issue. In particular: the Jante rules are addressed to what one may think of oneself rather than to what one may do, and I want to know what kind of norm that can be, and whether it can be obeyed at will; whether tall-poppy syndrome is a sanction on success itself or only on its display; and whether the Dream and the Law of Jante, for all their apparent opposition, share the premise that a person's worth is a matter of rank — for if they do, the quarrel at the centre of my book is not between two cultures but within one. I do not need the sociology or the evidence; I need the concepts sorted out.
