ok so i keep reading arguments about whether destroying a habitat is good or bad for wild animals and they all just assert their conclusion. before i read any more i want the spec first — what would a theory of "the net welfare of an ecosystem" actually have to deliver to count as adequate?

give me the requirements list, with the case behind each one. stuff like:

- the cases it has to get right. a logged hectare vs the cattle pasture that replaces it; a dam; a rewilding project; rainforest -> oil palm; the r-strategist thing where one frog lays 10,000 eggs and 9,999 of them die badly (or don't); a "protected" area that stays forest but gets quietly emptied of big animals.
- the distinctions it can't collapse. total vs average welfare. number of animals vs quality of their lives. "fewer animals" vs "less suffering" — those two come apart badly and i think a lot of the disagreement is just people sliding between them.
- the currency problem. is the unit the individual, the hectare, the kilogram of biomass, the unit of net primary productivity? these rank the same two land states differently and i want to know which choice is defensible and why.
- the counterfactual problem. you need a baseline, and the baseline keeps moving — the forest was going to change anyway, so what exactly are you comparing? say whether this is a real requirement or just a way to never answer.
- what it has to stay consistent with from ecology: trophic cascades, density dependence, the fact that a hectare of rainforest holds vastly more invertebrates than a hectare of almost anything else, and invertebrates may be the whole ballgame if they're sentient at all.
- time. one century or ten thousand years? does the theory have to be time-indexed or can it be timeless?

for each one, tell me whats a genuine requirement and whats just one camps prejudice — im suspicious that "the theory must give a conservationist something to do" is doing no work at all, and that "more life is better" is smuggled in as a requirement when its really a conclusion.

then the part i most want: flag any pair of requirements you think cant both be satisfied. my guess is that "one common welfare currency across a nematode and an elephant" fights with "the numbers must be empirically grounded," but tell me if theres a worse pair. and which single requirement knocks out the most of the popular answers? a table is fine.
