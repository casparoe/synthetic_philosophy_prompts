ok so i'm trying to write a short piece on whether Marx's exploitation and alienation stuff still bites in the creator economy, and i'd like your comments before i take it any further. gist: the standard story is that the worker is alienated because they don't own the means of production and have to sell their labour power. but someone streaming or selling ceramics on instagram does own the camera, the laptop, the kiln. so the standard story doesn't obviously apply, and the usual fix is "well the platform is the new factory". i don't like that fix. my move is that the real means of production there isn't the camera, it's the ranking system, because without the feed your labour doesn't produce anything anyone can buy. and the second move is that the creator isn't paid a wage, they're paid in reach, a currency the platform can inflate or deflate whenever it wants. so the exploitation isn't a boss skimming surplus value off the top, it's that you never find out what your labour is worth — there's no price, only a rank. i've been calling it exploitation-by-prevented-price-discovery, which is definately a terrible name.
so: is this a coherent extension of Marx or am i just redescribing commodity fetishism? where's the arguement weakest? does "paid in reach" do real work, or does it collapse the second a creator gets a brand deal and an actual invoice? and does any of it survive platforms that pay revenue shares, since then there kind of is a price?
give me exactly five comments, numbered, one or two sentences each, no intro and no wrap up.
