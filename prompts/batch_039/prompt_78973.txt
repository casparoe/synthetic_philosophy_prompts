My two siblings and I are dividing our late father's estate, and we have gotten stuck on the principle. My older brother argues that we should split it in whatever way produces the greatest total satisfaction, counting each of us equally, since that is the split each of us would have chosen in advance had we not known which of the three we would turn out to be. I find the argument hard to resist, yet I also have a strong sense that our youngest sister, who is unwell and for whom even a modest sum buys a great deal of relief, should come first — even if the same money would yield a larger total in my brother's hands, since he insists he can only be happy with expensive things. And when I actually run my brother's thought experiment I get opposite answers depending on whether I imagine having my sister's wants or keep my own while standing in her shoes. Something is doing the real work here and I cannot tell what. Is my brother's argument sound, and if not, precisely where does it break down?
