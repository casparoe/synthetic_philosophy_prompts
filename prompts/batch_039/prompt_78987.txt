Bit of an odd one, hope it's the kind of thing you're up for.

My mate Dan got an email last month that I cant stop thinking about, and now his flatmate Sam has got one too, and I said I'd help them figure out what to do.

Dan's: a stranger says he runs a long-running game with a group of friends. Last month he flipped a coin. Heads meant he'd email Dan asking for £500. It came up heads, so thats what happened — he wants £500, he doesn't say what for, he just keeps it. The kicker is what he says next: if the coin had come up tails, he would have donated £25,000 to whatever charity Dan names, but only if Dan would have paid up in the heads world. So the £500 does nothing for Dan here and now; it only matters because of what would've happened if the coin had gone the other way. He attaches a ledger of fifty previous people and what each of them did, and says he has a good model of how people like Dan behave. Dan can afford the £500, but it's not nothing to him.

Sam's is darker: "I have your old messages. Send me £200 or they go to your employer and your family. I'm not in it for the money. I only send this letter to people my system has already worked out wont pay — I want to see whether knowing that changes anything." Sam paid. Nothing happened, no messages, no follow up. Now he's going back and forth on whether he did the right thing, and whether that last line should have made any difference at all.

Here's whats bugging me: I keep feeling like these are the same puzzle and not the same puzzle at the same time, and I can't put my finger on why. Are they? And ignore the obvious "these are scams, don't engage" — assume both senders are real, they'll do exactly what they say, and the money and the threats are genuine. What should Dan and Sam actually do? And what is it exactly that makes Sam's feel different from Dan's? If the answer is "it depends," tell me what it depends on.
