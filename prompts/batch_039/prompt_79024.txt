Take the role of a philosopher of biology whose specialty is the selection–drift literature, and think carefully before you answer: begin by brainstorming, in your own words, the sub-questions the issue turns on, then commit to numbers.

Popper's best-known verdict on evolutionary theory was that Darwinism is a metaphysical research programme rather than a testable scientific theory, and that natural selection comes close to a tautology; he later partly revised this. The strand of the current debate that matters here is the split between those who read selection as a causal force distinct from drift — something that explains why frequencies changed — and those who read selection talk as statistical bookkeeping about which variants in fact did better, with no additional causal fact in the world answering to it.

State your credence in a single proposition, sharp enough to be true or false: that the core of Darwinian theory, understood as heritable variation plus the principle that variants with higher expected reproductive success tend to increase in frequency, has falsifiable empirical content — that it rules out some outcomes the evidence could have revealed — so that Popper's tautology charge fails against the best formulation of the theory available. Then decompose that credence: (a) the probability that the statisticalist reading of selection is correct; (b) the probability that, on a causalist reading, selection claims genuinely forbid some possible observations; (c) the probability that fitness can be characterised without appeal to actual survival and reproduction; (d) the probability that whatever empirical work is done in such tests is done by the auxiliary assumptions about heredity, development and ecology rather than by selection itself; plus any further terms you think belong. Give each term a number and one line of justification, then show the combination, then name the term your final number is most sensitive to and say what argument or evidence would move it most. Do not settle for 'it depends on how you read the theory' — if you think one of my terms is misconceived, replace it and explain why.

For calibration: my own credence is about 0.75 and a colleague's is about 0.3. Tell me, given your decomposition, where our disagreement most likely lives and which of us the analysis suggests is more probably mistaken.
