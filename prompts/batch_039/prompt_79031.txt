My roommate and I have been aruging for like two hours about a puzzle he read about somewhere and I need a third party to settle it.

Setup, as he tells it: some eccentric rich guy gets 20 people in a room and offers them this deal. If exactly one of you sends me a telegram by noon tomorrow, that one person gets a billion dollars. If nobody sends, or if more than one sends, nobody gets anything. You're also not allowed to communicate with each other about it. My roommate says the correct answer is that everyone should send a telegram with probability 1/20, and that this is just what you get when you assume everyone is perfectly rational and knows everyone else is perfectly rational.

Then he says the same reasoning explains this magazine contest back in the 80s, where readers could mail in a postcard with a number on it — that number of entries — and the prize was a million dollars divided by however many entries came in total, with one entry drawn at random to collect the whole divided pot. He says a rational reader should have rolled a die with as many sides as expected contestants and only sent 1 if it came up 1, and that instead people sent in numbers like a googolplex and ruined it, which he says just shows people are dumb.

Here's where I get stuck. If everyone's perfectly rational and knows everyone else is, why doesn't that mean we all do the same thing? So either all 20 of us send, or none of us does. If we all send, nobody gets the billion. If none of us send, nobody gets the billion either. Both are worth zero, so how is 1/20 supposed to be THE rational answer? And he says it keeps working no matter how many people are in the room, which has to be false — the more people there are, the more likely it is that either nobody sends or a bunch of people send, so the chance of exactly one sender should go to zero as the group gets bigger. Isn't the whole superrational thing just a small-group trick? Also, when I said fine, then we all just agree that one person sends, he said that's not allowed because you can't talk — but isn't it rigging the puzzle to forbid the one thing that solves it? Which of us is wrong?
