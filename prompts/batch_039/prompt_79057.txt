I'm in a seminar on rational choice theory and we've spent the last three weeks on the iterated prisoner's dilemma, and I've gotten into an argument with a friend of mine — she's an ecologist, works on fish behaviour — that I cant stop thinking about. Her complaint is that the whole topic is taught backwards. The standard picture, the one in every lecture, is that cooperation is held up by the threat of permanent reversion: you play nice until someone defects, and then the relationship is essentially over, the "shadow of the future" doing all the work. She says that's a hopeless model of anything alive, because real cooperative relationships basically never terminate on the first betrayal. What she puts in its place she calls probation, and I'll try to state it fairly: when you defect against me, I don't retaliate that round at all, I just record it; we go on as if nothing happened, but for the next n rounds you're on probation, and if you defect again while still on it, then it's over for good. The part she insists is the whole point is that a single defection is treated as a debt rather than a rupture — it can be worked off over time — but the record itself never clears. And she wants it to work in a world where almost all defections are accidents, since in her fish nobody defects on purpose.

So I'd like to compare the two ideas properly rather than just declaring a winner. Is her probation genuinely a distinct mechanism, or is it just a softer-looking version of the grim-trigger story once you actually write both of them down — and if they do come apart, where exactly? What does probation predict that the standard account doesn't, and vice versa? The thing I really can't get straight is this: in the usual setup the threat has to be permanent or defecting just looks like a good deal, so if a defection is forgivable, what's doing the work of keeping anyone honest in her version? I keep feeling like the answer has to be something about the debt never really being paid off, but I can't turn that into an argument. Are these two rivals, or two settings of the same dial, and which one would you trust more once the noise level goes up — assuming, as she does, that we're talking about unrelated individuals who have a decent chance of meeting again and not about relatives. (We've also been playing a long game of Diplomacy over email for the last two months, which is probably why this is on my mind at all.)
