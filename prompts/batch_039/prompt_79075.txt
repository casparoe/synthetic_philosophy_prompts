My superviser's epistemology seminar has been on testimony all term and I still can't tell what she actually thinks, which is a problem because she marks me down for guessing wrong and then insists she isn't taking a side.

Her verdicts so far, all about whether the hearer ends up with a justified belief (she treats justified-and-true as knowledge and doesn't fuss about the difference):

- a stranger at a bus stop tells you the 9 goes to the airport, you've never seen him before, you believe him: justified
- same thing, except you watched him send three people the wrong way on purpose five minutes earlier: not justified
- a colleague who is careful and honest tells you your department is being merged, and admits all he's got is someone in the faculty office saying so, and he can't tell you who that was or why they'd know: not justified, even though he's as trustworthy as they come
- the same colleague tells you something he read in the actual memo himself: justified
- a friend who is honest and careful in general tells you confidently that your flight isn't affected by the strike, in the one area where you know he's useless and always wrong: not justified
- a generally reliable person tells you something he has no grounds for at all, a pure hunch, and it happens to be true: not justified, and she says not knowledge either

Once, in passing, she said the whole point of testimony is that the speaker puts themselves on the hook, which doesn't sound like either of the two camps I've read about, so maybe there's a third thing going on.
