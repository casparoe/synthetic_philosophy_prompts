ok so I'm in this phil of mind / cog sci seminar and last week we did the sphex wasp thing and I genuinely can't stop thinking about it. The story is: the wasp paralyzes a cricket, drags it to her burrow, goes inside to check everything's ok, comes back out, then drags the cricket in. But if you nudge the cricket a few inches while she's inside, she just drags it back to the entrance and goes in to check again. Nudge it ten times, she does it ten times. The prof called this sphexishness and said it's the stock image of the purely mechanical, and that Hofstadter's point was that what we have and she doesn't is basically a loop detector — something fires and says hey, I'm in a rut, and lets you step out. Apparently Dennett then pushed it further and argued that any purely physical system has to be sphexish in the end, which the prof waved at and moved on from before I could ask anything.

Here's what's bugging me. Suppose I'm in a rut, I notice it, and I step out. What did I just do? I ran a loop detector. That's a mechanism, same as hers, just at a different place. And if the loop detector is itself something I can get stuck in — if I keep noticing ruts in a way that is itself a rut, or I only ever break out by applying some rule I read in a self-help book — have I actually escaped, or just moved up a level? The prof made it sound like there's a bright line between the wasp and us, but I can't see where it's drawn, and saying "there's a loop detector" feels like it relocates the problem instead of solving it.

So can you seperate out what the sphexishness idea is actually claiming, and what exactly the anti-sphexish loop detector is supposed to add to the wasp rather than just to its software? Is the hierarchy-of-loops thing a real problem for the concept, or does it miss something the concept was never trying to do? And if Dennett's right that any physical system ends up sphexish, does that sink free will, or does it just quietly turn the free will question into a different question? (He mentioned compatibilism and then the bell rang.) I'd rather have the readings laid out than one tidy answer.
