I'm on the ethics and compliance committee of a mid-sized international NGO -- we run school-improvement projects in a few West African countries, funded by a European government donor plus a couple of private foundations. We meet in two weeks and I have to circulate the decision memo before then, so I'm drafting it now and I want a second brain on it.

Background. Our donor's anti-bribery certification, which we signed, is essentially "no gifts or payments to public officials, no exceptions, no facilitation payments." Our staff handbook copies that language word for word and then never defines 'gift'. Last quarter a junior accountant in our country office flagged a batch of expenses from one project: about $4,000 over eight months, in three piles.

Pile one, roughly $600: kola, drinks, and a small cash 'protocol' payment to village elders and chiefs at the start of community meetings. Always announced out loud at the meeting, always logged in a ledger anyone in the village can look at.

Pile two, roughly $2,000: 'sitting allowances' paid to teachers, parent-association members, and two district education officials for giving up weekends to attend our planning workshops, at what our country director says is the going local rate for that kind of work.

Pile three, roughly $1,400: three installments to one district education officer in the two months before he signed off on the list of schools that would get the new classrooms. Our project then picked schools off that list.

The accountant reported it. The country director is furious at her -- says she doesn't understand how anything gets done here, that refusing pile one insults the chiefs and costs us the project's goodwill, and that pile two is just paying people for their time. She also mentions, unprompted, that our compliance officer in the capital is a close friend of the DEO. Our compliance lead wants to terminate the partnership, report it to the donor, and relocate the programme. One board member has said before that 'corruption' is a Western category and we're imposing our standards on people with a different understanding of obligation and reciprocity. Another said that's the excuse everyone uses.

What I need: a recommendation the committee can actually vote on -- what happens to the country director, the accountant, the local partner, the DEO, the money, and our relationship with the donor, with a timeline -- then the reasoning, including the strongest honest version of the country director's case and the relativist board member's case, not just a restatement of the donor's rules. I also want a proposed handbook revision, since 'no exceptions' is not going to survive contact with the field and I'd rather write something our field staff can actually follow. If you can, give me a principled test that sorts piles one, two, and three, rather than just telling me all three are forbidden.

Two things I keep going back and forth on and would like you to take a position on. First, I keep wanting to say a bribe changes a decision and a gift doesn't -- is that a real distinction or am I just making myself feel better about pile one? Second, does it matter that the payments were public and recorded? And please be straight with me about which parts of this are about ethics and which parts are about us protecting our own funding.
