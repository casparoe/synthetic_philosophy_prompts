I play alot of low-stakes poker — not a pro, just a regular at a $1/$2 table who's read a couple of books and thinks about the game too much — and someone recently told me about this "dollar auction" thing. You auction off a dollar bill, and the twist is that both the top bidder and the runner-up have to pay what they bid. So people keep bidding past a dollar, and you end up with someone paying three bucks for a one-dollar bill. It instantly reminded me of a guy at my table who keeps calling because he's "already in for so much," and also of some dumb stuff I've done with a car I should've sold two years ago.

Here's what I want. There's a whole body of thinking out there about why people escalate like that — why they keep pouring money into a losing bet, why the second bidder in that auction doesnt just walk away, why sunk costs seem to grip people, and whether any of it is actually a mistake or whether it's the smart thing to do once you're stuck in it. I sort of know that literature exists but I dont really understand it. So restate it for me in the vocabulary I actually have, which is poker: pot odds, pot commitment, being priced in, implied odds, chasing, tilt, bankroll, variance, that whole world. Go through the main ideas one at a time and tell me what maps cleanly, what I have to fudge to make it fit, and what just has no home in poker at all.

I'm especially stuck on one thing. In poker everyone knows the money already in the pot isnt yours anymore — the only thing that matters is what you stand to win versus what you'd have to put in. That's pot odds, and it's supposed to make you ice-cold about sunk costs. But that's the exact opposite of the escalation story, where people can't let go. So either poker players are quietly making the same mistake everyone else does (and pot odds is just a story we tell ourselves), or there's something real in the escalation stuff that poker has no way to say. Tell me which. If my poker vocabulary is missing something, say what it's missing; if the escalation people are claiming something less solid than it sounds, say that too.
