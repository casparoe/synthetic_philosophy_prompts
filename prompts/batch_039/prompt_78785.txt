I'm a novelist — I write the kind of books where someone dies in chapter three and the reader is supposed to work out who done it about two pages before the detective does. So I spend alot of time on the gap between whats on the page and what the reader does with it. My editor keeps writing "but how does she know that?" in the margins, which is her way of asking me to put another sentence on the page, and I keep telling her that if I put the sentence on the page the book gets worse and also longer, and then she writes "how does she know that?" about the new sentence, and we go around again.

Somebody told me recently that this is an old philosophy problem — I think it's Lewis Carroll? As I understand it, Achilles has two premises, and the tortoise says fine, I accept both, but I don't accept the conclusion. And Achilles says, but if you accept those two you must accept the conclusion. And the tortoise says, oh, write that down as a third thing and I'll accept it. And then he accepts it. And still doesn't accept the conclusion. And they go on like that forever. Is that basically it?

Here's what I want. Take that problem and restate it in the vocabulary I actually use — not a philosophy paper about it. The vocabulary of what's on the page versus what the reader supplies, showing versus telling, canon, the fact that a series bible has a rules section sitting right next to its facts section, "does it land," "the room liked it." Then give me the residue report: what carried over cleanly, what you had to fudge, and what my vocabulary just cannot say. My hunch is that I can only ever say "readers will infer it," never "you must infer it," and that those are different animals — but I want to know whether that's a hole in the way I talk about books or whether the tortoise is demanding something that was never there to begin with. Also two smaller things: is "show don't tell" actually the same distinction as rule-versus-premise, or does it just rhyme? And if a series bible lists the world's rules as entries alongside its facts, has it solved the tortoise's problem or only relocated it?
