I'm in a reading group working through Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach, and we have just spent two sessions on the "Ant Fugue" dialogue — the one in which the characters take tea with an anteater and end up discussing a colony of ants whose collective activity spells out messages, and eventually treat the colony itself, Aunt Hillary, as a conversational partner. The discussion got stuck in the usual rut: one faction insists the colony obviously has a mind because it does everything a mind does, and the other insists it obviously does not because there is nowhere in it that the thinking happens. I would like to break the stalemate with a case rather than a slogan, so I am asking you to construct one to specification.

Please design a single ant colony — one continuous scenario, no more than a few paragraphs of set-up — engineered so that the following three verdicts come apart, and so that they come apart for reasons internal to the theories rather than because the case stipulates them. First, an organizational or functionalist theory of mind — any theory on which having a mind is a matter of instantiating the right causal-functional organization, however it happens to be physically realized — must count the colony as having mental states. Second, a strange-loop or self-model theory — on which a system has a mind only if it computes a high-level model of itself and that self-model is causally efficacious in the system's own processing, rather than a mere epiphenomenal summary — must deny that the colony has one. Third, a biological naturalist account of consciousness, of the sort that holds conscious experience to require the specific causal powers of a biological brain, must also deny consciousness, but for a reason independent of the second verdict, so that the case forces a clean separation between having a mind, on the one hand, and being a subject of experience, on the other. The design constraints are what make this hard rather than decorative, so please respect them. The colony must be biologically continuous with real ants: everything it does should be implemented through mechanisms real ants actually have or could plausibly have — pheromone trails, stigmergy, trophallaxis, simple individual response rules, colony-level chemical gradients — with no central control unit, no queen who is secretly the brain, no dedicated "mind ant", and no external computer or human intervention. Its achievements should be modest and concrete, such as navigating, choosing between food sources, adjusting foraging effort, or repairing damage, rather than the sort of feat that already begs the question in favor of a mind. Nothing in the set-up may assert or hint at the verdicts: no sentences like "the colony believes" or "there is no one home", and no stipulating that the self-model is causally inert — that has to fall out of how the mechanisms are wired. Finally, the colony's behavior must be held fixed across the three theories; the three verdicts must come from the theories' different conditions, not from three different colonies.

After the scenario, do the following in order. First, for each of the three theories, state the condition it imposes, then say what it delivers in this case and why, tracing the verdict to specific features of the mechanisms you described. Second, identify the single structural feature of the case that is doing the work of separating the first verdict from the second, and say exactly what would happen to each verdict if that feature were removed — would the functionalist and the strange-loop theorist converge, and if so, on which answer? Third, explain why the third theory's verdict cannot be reached by the second theory's route, that is, what the case shows about the difference between a self-model and a subject of experience. Fourth, state how a defender of the strange-loop theory would most plausibly attack your case — not by denying the biology, but by arguing that you have misdescribed the colony's self-model or its causal role — and say whether that attack succeeds or merely relocates the disagreement. Fifth and finally, tell me honestly which of the three verdicts your case most nearly rigs, and what a genuinely neutral version of the case would have to leave open.
