I am assembling a fictional-archive issue for a policy journal and need one item: a piece of sustained deadpan satire of about 650 words, in the form of an official decision notice from a regulatory body, written as continuous prose in the register of a genuine determination. That means no headings, no numbered clauses, no bullet points and no bold text, and it should read the way a long and carefully courteous letter reads.

The setting is an invented jurisdiction in which self-government is treated as a standing general principle rather than an occasional concession, and the body issuing the notice, which you may call the Boundary and Peoples Commission, has the job of deciding who counts as a people and where their territory stops. Address the notice to an invented regional movement whose application the Commission grants. Every place, body and person must be invented, so that nothing in the piece turns on any real referendum or real movement.

The comedy must come from the Commission applying the applicant's own principle with complete consistency. The constituency whose consent is supposed to settle the matter cannot itself be identified without a prior decision about who belongs to it; the reasons offered for the applicant's secession are equally available to the applicant's own second city, to a rural district that voted the other way, and eventually to a single street that has filed its own application. Let the Commission also announce two doctrines of its own invention: one under which consent lapses whenever the composition of the constituency changes, so that no settlement is ever final, and one under which any grant of secession automatically reopens the question for every sub-unit of the newly created entity. It should apologise sincerely for the administrative inconvenience and offer to assist the applicant in completing the resulting forms.

Never wink. The notice should sound fair-minded, even solicitous, and should never use the vocabulary of the academic debate; a reader who knows that literature should nonetheless recognise the real difficulty being dramatised, and should find the satire fair rather than merely clever. Please write in British English.
