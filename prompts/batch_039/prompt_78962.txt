ok so I've been sitting in on a reading group about integrated information theory for about four months now and I still can't tell whether the people arguing are even arguing about the same thing. I want a diagnosis of the standoff, not a verdict on who's right.

The shape of it, as I see it. Tononi and co. start from what they call axioms -- experience is integrated, it's specific, it's definite -- then turn each one into a requirement the physical substrate has to satisfy, write down the maths for Φ, and say the maths then tells you which physical systems are conscious and how much. Substrate doesn't matter, only causal structure. They also claim the theory explains why experience feels the way it does, and that's the part they're proudest of.

The critics, roughly: (a) you can't actually compute Φ for anything brain-sized, so the theory makes no real predictions; (b) the unfolding objection, which seems to me the sharpest -- take a recurrent system IIT says is conscious, unroll it in time into a feedforward network with identical input-output behaviour, and the feedforward one has Φ = 0, so no behavioural or neural experiment can tell them apart, which would make the theory unfalsifiable in practice; (c) the axioms aren't really axioms, they're empirical claims picked because the formalism comes out pretty; (d) the maths gives silly verdicts -- a photodiode gets a small nonzero Φ and Tononi cheerfully accepts that, while some huge randomly wired network scores very high and the critics treat that as a reductio; (e) the formalism keeps getting revised and the numbers move each time, which the critics read as adjusting the theory after the fact. Then there's the sociology layer -- the open letter calling it pseudoscience, though I noticed a survey where only a small minority of the field actually signed up to that label.

My hunch, and please treat it as a hunch: it's a non-disagreement, two projects sharing one name. Project A: what is consciousness, mathematically -- a metaphysical identification written in information-theoretic notation, where success means the formalism mirrors the phenomenology. Project B: which systems are conscious, and how would we know -- a predictive project with a completely different success condition. If that's right, the unfalsifiability and uncomputability complaints are lethal to B and beside the point for A, and the proponents only look slippery because they slide between the two whenever they're cornered. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a real formal question underneath, like whether a causal-structure theory could ever make a content-specific prediction at all.

So diagnose it. What kind of disagreement is this, which objections land on which project, and what would count as progress given the diagnosis -- a computable proxy that actually predicts something, a theorem, a distinction, or just admitting it's stuck? And definately tell me how confident you are in the diagnosis overall and which single part of it your least sure about.
