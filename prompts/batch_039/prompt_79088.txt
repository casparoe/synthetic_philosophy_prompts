My friend and I argued about Quine's gavagai until well past midnight and got nowhere, so I would like a verdict on a narrower question than the one we were fighting over. Assume, for the sake of argument, that the evidence available to the field linguist is exhausted. Here are seven renderings of the native's 'gavagai', each of which is supposed to fit that evidence equally well: rabbit; undetached rabbit part; rabbit stage, i.e. a momentary temporal slice of a rabbit; the scattered mereological sum of all rabbits past, present and future; a mass term for rabbit-stuff, with no individuation built in, as 'water' is a mass term; a verb, 'to rabbit', naming a process rather than an object; and a demonstrative-like term meaning roughly 'this one here', with the referent fixed only by the occasion of utterance. Rank these from the rendering that poses the sharpest threat to the determinacy of 'gavagai' to the one that poses the least. For each, say in a sentence or two whether it is a genuine rival to the plain rabbit rendering or only a notational variant of it, and flag any that you think Quine's own set-up quietly excludes — a candidate ruled out by the set-up weakens his case rather than illustrating it.
