I am assembling the closing handout for a graduate seminar in the metaphysics of mind, and for our final session—on the suspicion that the notion of qualia is not merely mysterious but internally unstable—I would like a poem. The case that has organized the unit is the coffee case: two drinkers, Chase and Sanborn, both used to like a certain brand and now do not; Chase says the taste itself has changed, while Sanborn says the taste is exactly as it was and only his preferences have changed. What interests me is that the case seems to put pressure on the whole package of marks usually assigned to qualia—that they are ineffable, intrinsic, private, and directly apprehended—without ever letting us inspect the alleged items themselves. I want a poem that makes its form do some of that pressure rather than merely paraphrasing the debate. Please write a sestina spoken by a seminar member who is drawn to the charge that qualia are an incoherent posit but also worries that the charge proves too much. Use as the six end-words ineffable, intrinsic, private, immediate, same, and changed. The permutation should feel like the argument turning over: by the envoi, the reader should feel how the vocabulary of sameness and change, privacy and immediacy, has been shuffled until it can no longer adjudicate between Chase and Sanborn. The tone should be exact and a little weary rather than whimsical; a philosopher of mind should recognize the case and the stakes, while a reader who has never encountered qualia should still find a real poem.

You are a philosopher-poet with a strong ear for formal verse and a working knowledge of the metaphysics of mind. Think carefully before you draft, and feel free to show a brief plan or a few candidate lines if that helps, but the poem is the deliverable. Do not include citations, a bibliography, or references to secondary literature. If the request rests on a confusion—if, for instance, the incoherence charge is itself unstable, or if a sestina cannot capture the dialectic without distorting it—please say so plainly instead of forcing the poem. After the poem, add a short note on where the verse takes liberties with the philosophical material or leans on a contested reading of the coffee case.
