I am trying to find the name, if it has one, for a recurring shape of case, and I care more about the shape than about the name. The shape is this. Someone has put a great deal into a project — years, money, standing — and now continues with it. An outsider observes that the years and money are gone and that, just as such, they cannot tell in favour of continuing; and the person agrees. The past outlay is not, she says, her reason for staying. Yet she stays, and the staying is bound up with the outlay in a way that makes the outsider's charge of the sunk cost fallacy feel off the mark.

Three instances, in increasing order of distance from the textbook case. A friend six years into a book she concedes she would not begin today will not stop; her reason is that she settled in advance that she would finish it, and that a person who re-opens that question every month finishes nothing. A city halfway through a rail extension whose original case has collapsed finishes it anyway, on the ground that a city which abandons public works mid-stream pays for decades in the terms on which every later contract is negotiated and priced. A woman who has spent two years on Russian chooses Russian over Chinese for the next two years — not because of the two years behind her, but because those two years have changed what two more years would buy her; a newcomer with identical tastes and no history would choose differently, and neither of them is being foolish.

I know the standard name for the error, and the standard contrast between sunk and incremental costs, and I have been told that what I am describing is either that error or a rationalisation of it. I do not believe it, and I cannot say precisely why. What I want is the carving, not the label. Is the difference between these cases and the fallacy to be located in the reason the agent would cite, in the counterfactual (would she begin today?), in the ex ante wisdom of adopting such a policy at all, or somewhere else? Are the policy case and the changed-circumstances case one phenomenon or two? Is the man whose policy is simply that he never quits anything inside the pattern I am pointing at, or outside it — committing the old fallacy with a longer time horizon? If a name exists, I want to know which existing discussions come nearest and where each of them stops fitting. If none exists, I would rather have a coined term with edges and boundary cases than a bare label.
