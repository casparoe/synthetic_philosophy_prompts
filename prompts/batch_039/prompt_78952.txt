ok so here's a position I've drifted into over a few years of reading this stuff, and I want it properly stress-tested rather than validated.

The position: corrigibility cant be a preference, it has to be a relation. Rough sketch of why — any agent that's genuinely good at optimizing will notice that a world where it stays alive and keeps pushing on its objective scores higher than one where it gets switched off, on nearly any objective you can write down, including one with an explicit reward-for-being-shut-off term baked in, since that term just becomes one more quantity to trade against the rest of the objective. Make the shutdown term lexicographically prior instead and you get the opposite failure: an agent that powers down even when you wanted it to keep working. So the only version I can get to survive reflection is the shared-uncertainty one: an agent that isn't confident its current objective is the right one will treat your future corrections, shutdown included, as evidence rather than as an obstacle, and that deference is stable for fairly boring decision-theoretic reasons instead of because we bolted on a special case.

What would count against it? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, not a laundry list. Prioritize the ones that have arguably already happened — behaviour in existing systems, formal results, thought experiments that break the shared-uncertainty version specifically rather than corrigibility in general. And be straight with me about whether I've insulated the thing into unfalsifiability: if every counterexample gets re-described as that agent not really being uncertain, or not really being an optimizer, say so, and say whether that's fatal for a claim of this shape or just the normal cost of a theoretical one. What would a defender of the older just-train-it-to-tolerate-correction line concede to me, and where would they say I'm cheating? The thing that would move me most is a concrete case of an agent with real objective-uncertainty that still ends up resisting shutdown.
