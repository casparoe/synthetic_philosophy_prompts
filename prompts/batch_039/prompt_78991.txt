I'm putting together the final for a small MA-level seminar on value conflict and rational choice — mixed MA students and strong 4th-years, 90 minutes, closed book, 25 marks for the long question. I want one long question on the idea that compromise between people with different values can be a source of gains from trade, the way ordinary exchange is. I'm bored of answers that just say compromise is good because both sides gain something, so I want something where the student actually has to build the case and then find where it cracks.

Here's the setup I want to use, roughly. Two housemates, Priya and Quill, have to settle two seperate things: what happens to the spare room (a dial running from all-Priya's-music-studio to all-Quill's-home-office) and the house food rule (a dial running from strictly vegan to anything goes). Priya cares a lot about the room and barely at all about the food; Quill is the reverse. That's the two-issue case. Then contrast it with a one-issue case: a single fixed thing to divide, and it's zero-sum.

Please write the question itself, a model answer (600–800 words is fine), and a marking scheme. For the scheme I want it built around the specific moves a top answer has to make, with marks attached, 25 total, and a line on each about how students typically blow it. I definately want the question to force them to do all of this: show that a package deal, where each side gets its own issue entirely, strictly beats meeting in the middle; show that in the pure one-issue zero-sum case no compromise can be a Pareto improvement, and say what that does to the both-sides-gain slogan; name the exact structural feature that makes mutual gain possible in the two-issue case and explain why it needs no interpersonal comparison of the two people's values; then drop in a sacred-value / lexicographic wrinkle and make them say whether it kills compromise or only narrows it; and finally have them compare meet-in-the-middle against maximise-the-weighted-sum-of-the-two-value-functions and say which one is actually analogous to gains from trade, and what convexity is doing in that comparison. Keep it self-contained — no citations, no reading list, the students can't look anything up.
