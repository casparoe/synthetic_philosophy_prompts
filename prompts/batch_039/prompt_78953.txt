I'm in the back half of a set theory course (we just finished the forcing unit) and something from the independence lecture has been nagging at me. The instructor laid out that CH is independent of ZFC — Gödel showed you can't prove it, Cohen showed you can't refute it — and then reached for the parallel-postulate analogy: CH versus ¬CH is like Euclidean versus hyperbolic geometry, two consistent stories and you just pick the one you want to work in. Fine, I get that. But here's where I get stuck. If that's really the situation, and forcing lets you dial the size of the continuum to whatever value you like — ℵ_1, ℵ_5, ℵ_17, hey why not ℵ_ω if you feel like it — then isn't "the cardinality of the reals" just a free parameter of the model, basically a coordinate choice? Which would mean it's sort of meaningless to say the reals "really" have one size rather than another, and equally meaningless to say CH is "false" of the actual universe of sets. So when I hear people say they think CH is probably false, or that there's a genuine fact of the matter, aren't they just dressing up a convention as a discovery?

What I want to know is whether independance actually gets you that, or whether I've smuggled something in. And if I have, what's the cleanest way to state the difference between "CH isn't settled by ZFC" and "there's no fact about how big the continuum is"? A friend in the class says the whole thing just reduces to "add whichever axiom you like and stop worrying about it," which feels wrong to me but I can't articulate why, so maybe you can tell me where the reasoning breaks down — I'd rather understand the shape of the mistake than get a one-line "no, you're confused."
