Two of my friends got into an argument at a cookout last weekend and I've been chewing on it alot since. Here's the setup. Our buddy Rob promised his mom, a few weeks before she died, that he would never sell the little lake cottage. It was her cottage, she bought it with her own money, and honestly nobody else in the family cares about it much. Rob is thinking about selling now because money's been tight and the place needs a new roof. He hasn't decided yet. Anyway, Dana and Marcus went at it, and neither of them is a philosopher or anything, they're just both stubborn and pretty sharp:

1. Dana: A promise to a dead person can't really bind you. The whole point of a promise is that somebody's counting on you - that's why breaking one matters at all. She's gone. There's nobody left to count on anything, nobody left to be let down. The reason to keep it died with her.

2. Marcus: That's wrong, because she was counting on it the whole time she was alive. She died believing the cottage was safe. And even setting that aside, the whole reason a promise means anything is that it doesn't get to quietly expire the second it gets inconvenient. If promises to dying people don't count, then every deathbed promise anyone ever made is just theater - and he knew that when he said it.

3. Dana: So what you're protecting isn't her, it's the idea of promising. You're saying Rob has to eat a real cost, money his own kids could use, to keep faith with a rule. That's not honoring her, that's being bossed around by a word. And on your logic, the more dramatic the promise, the longer it owns you, no matter who it hurts later. That seems backwards.

4. Marcus: Flip it around. If it stops binding the second the person can't complain, then it was never a promise at all, it was just a nice thing he said to a dying woman. And he could of just said something nice without promising anything. The word exists exactly so you can't come back later and decide it wasn't that big a deal. When he promised, he handed her something real, and you don't get to take it back just because the person holding it isn't around to complain anymore. Also, other people were counting on it - his sister knew, his kids knew.

5. Dana: Now you've switched who it was for. It was made to his mom. If it binds because his sister is counting on it, then okay, but that's a different thing, and if his sister's fine with selling, then by your own logic there's nothing left to protect. And "handed her something" - handed her what? If she's gone, he's holding it for nobody. Plus people say all kinds of things to someone who's dying that aren't promises. Not every sentence in that room comes with a mortgage on your future.

They kept going for a while after that but that's the shape of it, and I was standing right there the whole time and honestly couldn't tell who had the better case. Can you go through it turn by turn and tell me who's got the better of it at each step - which moves hold up, which ones don't, and where each of them is overreaching - and then, if there's some angle neither of them saw, lay that out too. American English please.
