ok, so I've been covering animal minds for about a decade, insects mostly, and I'm meant to be writing a book about them. The problem is I've ended up holding a set of views I genuinely can't tell hang together. I'd like an audit, not a lecture. Here's the pile, roughly in the order I picked them up:

1. The only thing that matters in itself is how things feel from the inside — pleasure, pain, the valenced stuff. If a creature can't feel anything, it doesn't matter morally, full stop.
2. Moral status comes in degrees rather than being a switch. Something with a thin, poorly integrated inner life still matters, just less than something with a rich one. I dont believe there's a magic threshold.
3. My honest credence that ants and their ilk are sentient at all is low — 1 in 10,000, maybe lower on a bad day. The behavioural evidence (avoiding noxious heat, self-medicating, all that) looks to me like something that could come out of a system with nothing it's like to be it, and I don't think you get sentience without a reasonably centralised nervous system.
4. There are something like a billion billion insects alive at any given moment, and human activity kills an unfathomable number of them every year. Don't hold me to the figures — nobody actually knows them — but the order of magnitude is the point. Though I keep thinking the unit should be experiences rather than heads: a dormant egg isn't having a bad time, and I've never seen anyone run the numbers that way.
5. Multiply it out and insect suffering swamps the expected welfare of every vertebrate on the planet. So insect welfare should be somewhere near the top of anyone's list, and I've started giving money on that basis.
6. At the same time I'm deeply suspicious of any arguement where an enormous payoff gets multiplied by a small probability and the result swamps everything else. If I'll act on 1 in 10,000 times 10^18, why not 1 in 10^30 times 10^40? I don't want to be the mug who hands over his savings because a stranger might be a wizard, and I've got no principled way of saying where the line is.
7. And I'm not sure my credence in (3) is even the right shape of thing. What is the 10,000 a chance of? It isn't a coin behind a door. It's whether a particular kind of nervous system does a particular thing, and I can't tell whether that's a fact about each ant, about the kind, or about my theory of consciousness.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest repair in each case? I don't know which one I'd give up, so please don't assume. Rank by severity instead of telling me they're all tangled, and be plain about which ones have to go and what dropping them costs. (No reading list, I've got a stack already.) Finish with a line beginning "Verdict:" naming the single commitment you'd cut and, in a few words, the price.
