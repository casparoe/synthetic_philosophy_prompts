I keep getting into the same argument about Pascal's wager and it never goes anywhere. First someone says you can't just decide to believe something; then someone says that misses the point, you can at least put yourself in the way of believing; then someone raises the other religions and it turns into "but which god?"; then whoever knows a bit of decision theory says fine, just put infinite payoffs in the matrix — and at that point nobody can say what the expected value even is, since a rival wager can always promise a bigger infinity, so the whole calculation seems to come apart before it starts. I've heard every one of these moves and their standard replies a dozen times and I've never seen anyone's mind change. I'd like a diagnosis rather than a verdict: is this really a disagreement in decision theory about how to handle infinite or undefined utility, a disagreement about whether practical reasons can justify belief at all (and whether belief is even up to us), or two sides answering different questions — one about what its rational to believe, one about how it's rational to live? Tell me which of those is doing the real work and why, going by the actual moves each side makes, and then what would count as progress: a formal result, a distinction, a change of question, or is the wager just not the kind of thing that can be settled? Don't just hand me the list of objections, I've got those.
