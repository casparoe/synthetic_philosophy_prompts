I need a case for the refrence class problem for a lecture, and it has to do a very specific job, so brainstorm a couple of candidate setups before you commit to one. Build an agent who has to put a number on a single event, holds all the evidence a normal person could gather, and yet has two reference classes that are both admissible -- under a criterion I'm about to stipulate -- and that give wildly different answers. The gap must not be down to her ignorance: more evidence of the ordinary kind shouldn't close it. Here's the criterion, which I'm inventing for the purpose: call a class robust if small, non-arbitrary sharpenings of the event's description leave its frequency basically unchanged. Both classes in your case must be robust, and they must still disagree. Constraints: realistic (medicine, insurance, weather, hiring -- no infinite populations, no exotic physics, no gods), one paragraph of setup, and don't beg the question by stipulating frequencies so that one side obviously wins. Then, after the scenario: (a) say what number each of the narrowest-class rule, a maximum-entropy/indifference approach, and the widest-available-class rule delivers, and why; (b) tell me which feature of the case does the real work and what happens if I delete it; (c) give the strongest line a defender of the view that this is all just incomplete information could run against you. Dont hedge at the end -- take a side on whether the case shows the problem is more than epistemic.
