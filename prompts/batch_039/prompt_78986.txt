So I'm a science journalist and I'm working on a feature about how medicine decides what counts as a disease and what's just... a variation. And I'm already bored of the obvious peg — every couple of years someone re-litigates whether obesity is a disease and I have to write that piece again. What I actually want is the argument underneath it, the one the epidemiologists have at the hotel bar after the conference.

So here's the ask. Write me a dialogue between two people. One is a naturalist about disease: roughly, a disease is a biological fact — some mechanism in the body isn't doing the job it was selected to do, judged against how bodies of that kind normally work. The other is a normativist: roughly, calling something a disease is a value judgement wearing a lab coat — a state is a disease when we decide it's bad enough to be worth fixing, and the biology just tells us what's going on, not whether it's an illness.

Both of them should be sharp, and neither should be a strawman. The normativist can't just be a relativist who thinks measles is a social construct, and the naturalist can't just thump a textbook and act like the hard cases are easy. I want them responding to each other's actual examples, not trading monologues.

Make them fight it out over concrete cases, not abstractions. I want them stuck on at least these:

- lactase non-persistence — whether it's a disorder seems to depend on whats in your fridge
- aging, which every gerontologist I talk to wants reclassified as a disease, and I genuinely cant tell whether that's a scientific claim or a funding strategy
- the reference class problem: abnormal compared to who? The species, the sex, the age bracket, the middle 95% of a population that's already sicker than any population in history?
- one case where something is plainly dysfunctional and nobody is harmed by it, and one where people are plainly harmed and nothing looks broken

Also: no citations, no lit reviews, don't have them cite papers at each other. I don't need a reading list, I need the fight. And I want at least one moment where one of them has to give real ground on something, not just say fair point and move on. End it wherever it naturally ends — I'd honestly rather it stay unresolved than get a tidy synthesis stapled on.
