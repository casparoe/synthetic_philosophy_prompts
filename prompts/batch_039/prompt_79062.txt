ok, this is for my own benefit as much as yours, so bear with the setup.

i run a phil of math seminar and a colleague and i have been rehashing the same fight for about three years now, and i want to see where a stranger lands. the fight is the Wigner thing — why it is that mathematics, including mathematics cooked up with zero thought of physics, keeps turning out to be exactly what physics later needs. i don't want a literature review or a reading list, i know the literature. i want your credence and the guts of it.

so let me try to state the proposition precisely enough that it could be true or false:

P: The pre-adaptation between pure mathematics and later fundamental physics (group theory and particle physics, Riemannian geometry and general relativity, complex Hilbert space and QM, that sort of catalogue) is a real feature of the world that calls for a substantive explanation, over and above (a) the fact that we only notice and remember the hits, (b) the fact that we produce pure mathematics in enormous volume at low cost so some hits are expected, and (c) the deflationary thought that mathematics is the general study of structure, so it would be a miracle if physics, which is the study of one structure, turned out to be unmathematizable.

give me a number on P. and then decompose it, with a number and one line each for at least these:

1. the history is roughly right as usually told — the cases are real cases of pure-to-applied pre-adaptation, not retro-fitted stories or cases where the math was actually developed with the application in view;
2. given 1, the selection effects in (a) and (b) do the explaining, i.e. there's no residual left;
3. given a residual, clause (c) absorbs it (nothing mysterious remains once you notice math is just the theory of structures in general);
4. given a residual that survives 3, something in the neighbourhood of platonism, or structuralist necessity, or the idea that our standards for what counts as a good physical theory are anthropocentrically constrained, is actually needed to explain it;
5. a term for how well-posed you think the question is — if you think the number is partly an artifact of the question being vague, say so and price it in rather than ducking.

then combine them and tell me (i) which term you're least confident in, (ii) which term the final number is most sensitive to, and (iii) what single piece of evidence or argument would move you most.

one extra thing i'd like you to take seriously rather than footnote: i probaly suspect a lot of this debate is really an arguement about a denominator that nobody will name. if the right frame is a hit-rate question — observed rate at which pure math becomes physics versus some null rate — then say what you think the null should be and whether the observed rate beats it. if you think the hit-rate frame is the wrong frame, say why.

for the record i'm around 0.3 on P and my colleague is around 0.85, and we've never figured out whether we disagree about the history, about how much the selection effects eat, or about what would count as an explanation in the first place. tell me where you think the disagreement actually lives.
