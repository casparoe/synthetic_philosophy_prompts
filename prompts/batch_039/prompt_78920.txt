I spent thirty-odd years running psychophysics experiments on human vision before I retired, and for the last couple of years I've been reading alot of philosophy of perception in the evenings. There's one fight I keep running into and I cant work out what's actually being fought over.

The question is whether what you believe, expect or want can change what you literally see. One side says yes, and points at the usual zoo: bananas look more yellow than a colour-matched patch, knowing what an object is changes how big or how slanted it looks, and the old New Look work where children from poorer families overestimated the size of coins. The other side says these are artifacts of how the experiments were run and that early vision is sealed off from what you think. As far as I can tell both camps agree about the data. They just keep publishing, and nobodys view moves an inch.

My hunch, and I'd like you to tell me if it's wrong: the fight is being carried on at three different levels that nobody separates. (1) Does the belief change what the person reports? (2) Does it change what they experience? (3) Does it change what the visual system computes, before any experience happens? My field has a sixty-year-old toolkit for pulling (1) apart from (2) — signal detection theory gives you sensitivity and response criterion as two separate numbers — and as far as I can tell neither camp uses it in a way the other camp will accept. Alot of these effects look like criterion shifts to me, which isn't nothing, but it also isn't a change in what anyone sees. Then a philosopher tells me a criterion shift might still count as penetration, because the visual input is being weighed differently. And we go around again.

So: why has this stalled, and what kind of disagreement is it really? A substantive disagreement about how perception works, a verbal one about where the word "perception" stops, or two groups answering two different questions — one about the contents of experience, one about the architecture — under a single heading? Engage what each side actually says rather than the caricatures, and tell me what would count as progress if your diagnosis is right.

Skip the reading list, I've got enough unread PDFs. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give me the reasoning, no preamble and dont restate my question. And tell me what would change your mind about the diagnosis — which argument or consideration would push you to a different one, and where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong.
