I chair a small department, and for a few years now I've been watching something happen that I can't find a name for — I'm not sure whether it's a genuinely distinct pattern or just an obvious thing I'm dressing up in fancy clothes, so I'd like you to settle that. Here is the shape of it. Nobody involved is lazy or selfish, and everyone says, sincerely, that they value the thing that is disappearing. What disappears is a practice rather than a resource: the habit of answering each other's questions on the shared email list, or reading a colleague's draft before the meeting, or turning up to the colloquium and asking an actual question. I've seen the same shape in a large hobbyist forum I've moderated for years, where people used to write up their trips and now almost nobody does, even though the same people still read the write-ups and still say they miss them. Each individual omission is invisible, costs the person who omits it essentially nothing, and helps no one; you cannot point to the afternoon the practice died. What you can point to is a handful of visible defections early on, and then a term or two later everyone has stopped — and the state we land in is one that, if you polled the group, nobody would have chosen. Two features of this puzzle me. First, the thing being used up isn't scarce in the ordinary sense; there is no fixed quantity of draft-reading or trip-writing that gets exhausted, and if anything the practice is worth more to each person the more people take part, so the usual image of a finite pasture being nibbled down doesn't fit. Second, the remedies I read about don't seem to reach it: there is nothing to fence off and no quota to set, and the obvious fix — that somebody should start enforcing the norm, calling out the people who've quietly stopped — runs straight into the fact that the person doing the enforcing bears the whole cost while the benefit is spread over everyone, including the very people who stopped, so whoever would fix the problem is standing in exactly the same problem one level up.

Is there a name for this? I've read enough to suspect it lives near the tragedy of the commons, and I've run across 'free-riding' and something about second-order collective action, and a colleague once mentioned a 'problem of many hands' in a different context, but none of these seems to be quite the thing: the commons literature I've seen is about rivalrous resources where my taking something is at least in principle measurable, and the responsibility literature seems to be about who is to blame once something bad has happened rather than about why a practice nobody wants to lose erodes while nobody decides to let it go. What I want is not a label to paste on the phenomenon. I want to know whether an established concept captures it exactly; if one does, I want to know which, and what it leaves out; and if none does, I want you to say so plainly and then propose one, defined sharply enough that I could take some new case and say whether it is an instance or not. Either way I want the boundaries drawn — what separates this from an ordinary public-goods problem, from the cases where a community does succeed in governing a shared resource on its own, and from a plain case of genuine scarcity where something really is being used up. And tell me what the two oddities I mentioned are doing here: is the fact that the thing consumed is non-rivalrous, and the fact that the enforcement problem reproduces itself one level up, essential to the pattern, incidental to it, or the whole point of it? A bare name would leave me exactly where I started.
