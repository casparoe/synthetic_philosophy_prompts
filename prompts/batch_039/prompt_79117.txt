The design is that there's a tiny core of rules the thing just takes as given — stuff like, from 'P' and 'if P then Q' you're allowed to write down 'Q' — and then everything else is supposed to get derived from those core rules, nothing else. My friend thinks the whole thing is a con, and honestly his arguement is getting to me. So fine, I write it down as a line — 'if P, and if P then Q, then Q' — but now to get from that line to the actual conclusion I have to make the same step again, just one level up. Add another line, same thing. Theres always a move left over that I made without a license. He says the honest thing is to admit the core rules are a leap of faith — a convention we decided on, not something we know — and that this goes for everybody's reasoning, not just my toy program. He's even got a follow-up: if I just define 'proof' as 'whatever follows the rules', that only tells me which strings to write, it doesn't tell me why I should believe the output.

I can't find the hole in it and it's bugging me. Is there a real reply here, or do I have to just concede? And if the answer is 'it's a convention', is that actually a resting place, or is it the same problem wearing a hat?
