I want to use the occasion to defend a thesis I gather is unfashionable in that room: that testimony can transmit aesthetic knowledge, that is, you can genuinely come to know that a film is good, or that a proof is elegant, on the word of someone whose judgment you have reason to trust. The standard pessimist objection, as I understand it, is that you cannot, and I think it really conflates knowing *that* a thing is good with knowing *what it is like*, which are two different achievements. I concede that the second is not transmissible, and I want to argue that this is a feature of aesthetic judgment rather than a refutation of aesthetic testimony. My central intuition pump comes from my working life: when a senior engineer whose taste I trust tells me that a function is elegant, or that a module's interface is ugly, I take myself to learn something about the code that I would not have learned by reading it myself, and I want to use that case to pry apart the knowledge from the acquaintance.
