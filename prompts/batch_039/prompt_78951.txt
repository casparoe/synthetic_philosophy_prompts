I'd like a single credence, decomposed, on a question about where access consciousness ends and phenomenal consciousness begins.

Take the following as stipulations for this exercise rather than as established terminology. The *broadcast profile* of a system at a given time is the set of contents that are then available to it for global report, deliberate control, and cross-modal inference. Call a content *access-opaque* for a system when it is reliably decodable from the system's internal states and demonstrably shapes its responses, yet is never reportable by the system and never enters its cross-modal inference — it drives the system without being available to it.

Now consider:

**Claim.** For any system that has phenomenal states at all, its access-opaque contents are not among them. Phenomenal consciousness is coextensive with the broadcast profile, so whatever is never broadcast is not experienced.

Give me your credence that the Claim is true, as a number, with the derivation exposed. Price at least these sub-questions, each with a number and a line of justification:

(a) the probability that the global-workspace family of theories is properly read as an account of access rather than of phenomenal consciousness — that broadcast explains availability, not experience;
(b) the probability that, if some workspace-style theory is the best account of phenomenal consciousness, that theory excludes access-opaque experience;
(c) the probability that phenomenal facts supervene on a system's functional and organizational facts at all;
(d) the probability that "content" can be individuated finely enough for the Claim to have a determinate truth value.

Say how the terms combine, and give the resulting number. Then identify the term the number is most sensitive to, and name the single result — empirical or conceptual — that would move your number most.

State how confident you are in the final figure, and single out the one point in your reasoning you are least sure of. Answer in at most 450 words.
