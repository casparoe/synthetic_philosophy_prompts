I'm a fourth-year PhD student working on reference and predication, and in three weeks I'm giving a paper at a small workshop on classical Chinese thought — the kind of workshop where half the room are analytic philosophers of language and the other half are people who read classical Chinese for a living, which is exactly my problem. The paper is on the White Horse Dialogue in the Gongsun Longzi, the text where Gongsun Long argues that a white horse is not a horse. His first move is that horse is what designates shape and white is what designates colour, and that what designates colour is not what designates shape, so white horse is not horse. His second move is about seeking: if you go looking for a horse, yellow and black ones will do, but if you go looking for a white horse they won't, and if the white horse just were the horse then what you're after in the two cases would be the same thing, which it plainly isn't. The standard line treats the whole thing as either an elementary scope fallacy or an equivocation on the word for is, and my paper argues that this is a bit lazy. My own line is that he isn't making a claim about the English word is and he isn't confused about subsets; he's making a point about names and what they fix — that a compound name fixing two features at once can't be substituted for a simple name fixing one, in any context where what matters is which features get fixed. So on my reading his not is doing the work of is-not-substitutable-for rather than is-not-a-member-of, and the standard objection only gets off the ground if you already assume that the sole job of a name is to pick out an extension, which is the very thing he's denying. Fair warning: I'm not a sinologist, I work from translations and crib the Chinese from the commentaries, and I've been told this shows. I've also read alot of the secondary stuff and I'm definately aware that I'm sticking my neck out.
