I want to spend it on the debate idea in AI safety: two models argue opposite sides of a question, a human (or a weaker model) judges, and the hope is that the honest side wins even when the judge can't solve the problem, because the judge only has to check the steps. The pitch, as I understand it, is that this gets you way past what a judge could ever verify alone — PSPACE instead of NP, or something like that. I've definately got the shape of the setup. Here's my half-formed angle, and I'd like the whole thing built around it. Everyone treats the killer objection as the obfuscated-arguments problem: the liar buries the false step inside an argument so long and tangled that the honest side can't dig it out in the time available, even though the honest side is right. I think "long" is a red herring. The real question is whether a bad argument always has a flaw that can be localized to a short sub-claim the judge could check on its own. If every flaw is localizable, obfuscation can't win — the honest debater just points at the short bad step. If some flaws are irreducibly global, so that they only show up when you hold fifty steps together at once, then debate has a hole that no amount of extra judge competence plugs. And even in the good case theres a second question hanging off it: finding the localization is itself a search problem, and nothing in the setup says the honest side can do that search cheaply, or that the judge will recognize a correct localization when it's handed one. So: (a) is every flaw localizable, and (b) if so, can the honest debater find the localization inside the judge's attention budget — and is that budget the binding constraint, rather than argument length?
