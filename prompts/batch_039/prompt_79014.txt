Two camps have spent years arguing past each other about inference to the best explanation. The first holds that explanatory considerations — simplicity, unification, the absence of coincidence — carry evidential weight in their own right: there are cases in which two hypotheses are equally likely on the evidence and equally plausible beforehand, yet the more explanatory one is the one to believe. The second holds that no such weight exists independently: whatever is truth-conducive about explanatory preference must be reconstructible as a claim about likelihoods or about prior probabilities, and whatever resists reconstruction is a heuristic for finding hypotheses rather than a licence to accept them. Draft the joint statement the two could sign after a good-faith collaboration. Neither side may cite, name, or allude to any author or published work; put every position in your own words. Deliver exactly five numbered items and nothing else — no preamble, no closing paragraph, one or two sentences each: (1) the claims both sides will sign, including what they agree they are not disputing; (2) the disagreement that remains, stated once in wording both would accept and once in the wording each side would use for its own view; (3) where each side locates the warrant for explanatory preference — in the likelihoods, in the priors, or in a rule for selecting among hypotheses — and what that implies for a case both agree is real, namely one in which the best available explanation is probably not true because the space of live hypotheses is known to be incomplete; (4) the crux, the sub-question whose settlement would move at least one side, with the case or argument both accept as bearing on it and each side's prediction for it written down in advance; (5) the part of the dispute that is merely verbal, if any, together with your own verdict on which side is right about the real crux. Throughout, make the calls rather than surveying the options: commit to particular formulations, say why you chose them, and give a verdict rather than a menu.
