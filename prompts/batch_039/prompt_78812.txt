Hey — I'm a journalist, I write a science-and-ideas column, and I'm putting together a short piece on the money pump. You probably know the drill: take someone with a loop in their preferences — say they like coffee more than tea, tea more than hot chocolate, and hot chocolate more than coffee. Hand them to a patient shopkeeper and she can walk them round that loop all afternoon, swapping one thing for the next and skimming a penny off every swap, so the guy ends up exactly where he started, just poorer. Which is what a lot of people treat as the argument that intransitive preferences are irrational.

I want to open the piece with a joke, but I definately want it to actually land for people who know this literature, not a generic nerd gag. And here's the bit I want it to turn on, because its where I think the real action is: the pump only works on somebody who doesn't see it coming. If you clock the loop, you just refuse every trade. But then your preferences never get to *do* anything — you're a guy sitting on three things, refusing to move, and that's supposed to be the rational play? And then there's a third option I keep hearing about, where instead of refusing in the moment you decide in advance that you'll never trade, so future-you can't be talked into it — you tie yourself down. (The whole thing is a story about time, right? Every lap round the loop is a fresh decision by a slightly later version of the same guy.)

So: write me a joke about a person with a preference cycle that turns on that difference — the sucker who gets walked round the loop, the smart one who sees it coming and won't budge, and the extra-smart one who binds his future self. Setup plus a punchline, keep it short, and if the punchline can be something one of them actually says, even better. Just please don't make the punchline be "he should have consistent preferences" — everyone already makes that joke and it isn't even the interesting part.

One more thing, use it or lose it: I half remember reading that you don't even need the shopkeeper, that the guy would pump himself over the course of an afternoon. If that's a real thing, feel free to work it in; if I'm making it up, ignore me.
