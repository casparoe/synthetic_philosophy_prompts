I spent my career in the natural sciences and have only recently begun reading philosophy, where I have been puzzling over Moore's paradox. I would like you to write a joke that turns on the paradox's actual structure. A sentence of the form — It's raining, but I don't believe it — is not a contradiction, since both halves can be true at once, so the absurdity must attach to the act of asserting them together in the first person and the present tense rather than to the proposition itself. Please make the humor depend on that feature — for example, by exploiting the difference between merely recording such a sentence and asserting it aloud, or by contrasting the first-person present case with the same content reported about someone else or in the past tense — rather than trading on the general oddity of the sentence. It should be funny to a reader who already knows the paradox, not a generic joke about philosophers, and short enough to deliver aloud as a setup and a punchline. Follow the joke with a single sentence naming the feature that does the work. Write in American English.
