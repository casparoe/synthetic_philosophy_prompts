You are a philosopher of language who also works on programming-language semantics — equally at home in the radical-translation literature and in the theory of observational equivalence, bisimulation, and refinement — and you are unusually good at saying precisely where a translation between two vocabularies fails. I am a compiler engineer with no formal philosophy training beyond an undergraduate logic course: assume I am fluent in my own vocabulary and a beginner in yours. I want a translation exercise, and I want the residue — what fails to carry over — treated as the main result rather than an afterthought.

First, my vocabulary, so that you do not have to guess at it. Two programs are "the same" in a sense that matters to me when they are observationally equivalent with respect to some specified observation: they yield the same results or the same traces under every input I can construct, and I can substitute one for the other with no client of the code able to tell the difference. The observation function is something I choose, when I decide what counts as observable: return values, termination, timing, memory, or the intermediate states a debugger can inspect. Refactoring is licensed by equivalences of this kind. Separately, I have the notion of a specification — the thing the implementation is for, against which correctness is defined, and which is not itself read off the implementation's behavior. A program can be behaviorally indistinguishable from a spec-conforming one and still be, in the intended sense, wrong.

Now the exercise. Take the indeterminacy of translation: the radical-translation scenario, the native who utters something as a rabbit runs past, and the linguist who cannot distinguish, on the evidence available, a hypothesis on which the utterance is about the rabbit from one on which it is about a rabbit-stage, or an undetached rabbit-part, or a temporal slice, even though every observation the linguist can make is equally well accommodated by all of them. Restate that position, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I have just described: observation functions, observational equivalence, refinement, bisimulation, specification versus implementation, test suites, trace equivalence, and whatever else of mine is genuinely apt. Do the mapping piece by piece — the speaker's dispositions, the linguist's data, the rival manuals, the claim that there is no fact of the matter, and the claim that this is not merely an epistemic limitation.

Then the residue report, which is what I actually care about. For each part of the position, say whether it translated cleanly, translated only with distortion, or has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. I have three suspected failure points and want your verdict on each. First: my vocabulary seems to make the specification primitive — the implementation is for something not itself read off behavior — whereas the scenario seems to be one in which no specification is available, since the thing that would play that role is exactly what is in dispute. Does the translation collapse into triviality here, or does the engineer's notion of a specification have an analogue I am missing? Second: I relativize equivalence to an observation function that I choose, and I regard the choice as pragmatic, while the position I am asking you to translate appears to fix the observations once and for all as the totality of possible stimulations. Is that a substantive disagreement about what evidence is, or only a difference in the scope of the quantifier? Third: refactoring-equivalence licenses substitution, and substitution is a practical and normative matter — I do it to get something I can maintain — whereas the indeterminacy claim is about what there is rather than about what I can get away with. Is the normative character of my notion a defect for these purposes, or is it precisely what the other notion lacks?

Close with a verdict on the residue as a whole: does it point to something the indeterminacy thesis cannot say and my vocabulary can, or to something the thesis can say and mine cannot, or does it show the thesis doing less work than it appears to once what counts as an observation is specified? If the translation succeeds almost entirely and the residue is thin, say so plainly — I would rather have that than a manufactured asymmetry. Do not give me a bibliography, reading list, or citations, and do not attribute positions to named authors other than the one whose view I have asked you to translate; where you need to refer to a position, describe it. Before writing anything, spend a paragraph brainstorming: list the candidate mappings from my vocabulary onto elements of the scenario, flag the ones you expect to break, and only then commit. Think carefully throughout about the difference between being unable to tell the difference and there being no difference, since that is where I expect the exercise to be decided.
