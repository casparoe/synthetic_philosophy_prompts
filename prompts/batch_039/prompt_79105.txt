OK so I've been chewing over Dennet's late argument about counterfeit people — the one where he says anyone can now manufacture things that pass for people, that these are the most dangerous artefacts in history because they rot the institution of personhood itself, and that we should therefore criminalise making them, on the model of counterfeiting currency. I want to test that against an episode he never really leans on: Weizenbaum and ELIZA at MIT in the mid-sixties. DOCTOR was a few dozen pattern-matching rules that understood nothing and never claimed to, and yet Weizenbaum's own secretary, who had watched him build it and knew exactly what it was, asked him to leave the room so that she could talk to it properly. That shook him, and he spent the following years arguing that certain jobs simply ought not to be handed to computers at all — no new criminal offence, just limits on what we should build and use.

So do the mapping properly. In that episode, what is the counterfeit, who is the dupe, and what actually gets damaged: the dupe's interests, the dupe's epistemic position, or the shared practice of treating a voice, a screen or a turn of phrase as evidence that a mind is present? Then tell me how far the counterfeiting anology really carries. Counterfeit currency is bad because money is sustained by convention and a good enough fake corrodes the convention for everyone, whether or not anyone is taken in; say whether personhood has that structure, and if it does, whether the damage is done by the artefact or by the willingness of the person on the other end of it.

Which brings me to the bit I actually care about. Dennett spent his career deflating the gap between real minds and merely as-if minds — the intentional stance, real patterns, consciousness as a matter of what is worth saying rather than some inner glow. On that view, what exactly is a counterfeit person counterfeiting, and what is supposed to distinguish the fake from the genuine article? Give me the strongest reply available to Dennett here, then say honestly whether ELIZA confirms his alarm, complicates it, or shows that the whole worry was always about us and our credulity rather than about the artefacts. And tell me what his remedy would have had Weizenbaum do, against what Weizenbaum actually did, and whether the episode is an argument for one of them over the other.

I want a disciplined mapping, not a general essay on AI and authenticity — if the theory has nothing to say at some point, say so rather than making it speak. British English please, and no citations or reading lists, I've nothing to look things up with.
