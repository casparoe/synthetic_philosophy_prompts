I just got into a two hour fight with my friend Nadia about counterpart theory, and now I have to interview someone who actually holds it (it's for a Q&A episode of a tiny philosophy podcast I do), so I want the questions to settle our argument instead of letting her wriggle out of it. The guest thinks transworld identity is a mistake, but she isn't a modal realist — she thinks there's only the one world and that counterparts are ordinary actual people picked out by similarity, with the relation shifting depending on context. So she'll happily say that when I say I could have been a plumber, that's about some other guy who resembles me alot, and she thinks the standard 'but then you've just changed the subject' complaint begs the question.

Nadia's line was: fine, then the counterpart relation is just whatever we happen to find salient, so modal claims stop being about the world and start being about us and our interests — and it makes a mess of regret too, since the guy who could have been a plumber isn't me and I've got no reason to care how he turns out. She also said the view can't even state itself without talking about other worlds, so the actualist version is either circular or a change of subject itself. I don't know which of us is confused.

Give me eight or nine questions, and for each one a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. I want at least one question on each of these:

- whether the similarity relation can be constrained enough that counterpart facts are facts about the world rather than about our interests, and what would show it definately can't;
- the uniqueness problem: if I have several counterparts, how do we get the necessity of identity and the necessity of distinctness, and if she bolts on a uniqueness requirement, what does she lose;
- iterated modality: her relation isn't transitive, so 'possibly possibly p' doesn't collapse into 'possibly p' and S4/S5 fail — does she bite that bullet, and how does she explain why our intuitions are so strongly the other way;
- a concrete case where similarity picks out the wrong person (twins, duplicates, or a case where the closest match is intuitively someone else entirely), plus what her answer does to how we're supposed to run thought experiments;
- how she states the semantics without any modal vocabulary at all, and whether the resulting paraphrase is just a notational variant of ordinary possible-worlds semantics with the labels switched;
- whether essence does any work in her picture, or whether she's stuck saying that whatever the counterpart relation hands over just is what's essential to me.

And end on what would actually change her mind — something that would be evidence against the view, not just a cost she's willing to eat.
