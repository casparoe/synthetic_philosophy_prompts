I work on monitoring systems whose internal organisation I cannot fully observe, and I keep having to decide whether a deviation is the same mechanism malfunctioning or a different mechanism that has taken over — the practical difference being whether I repair it or keep it. I want the requirements first, before any candidate criterion: what would a theory of mechanism identity have to deliver for me to trust it on that question? Specifically, the cases it must handle (two systems that agree on every input but are organised differently; one mechanism that yields different outcomes in different contexts; the same phenomenon produced by different mechanisms in different individuals; a mechanism known only through a coarse-grained model of it; a mechanism that has had a part replaced, or has adapted over time); the distinctions it must not collapse (mechanism versus mere causal chain, malfunction versus replacement, sameness of parts versus sameness of organisation versus sameness of function); and the features of experimental and engineering practice it must accommodate, including what intervention-based tests can and cannot settle. For each, say why it is a genuine requirement rather than a preference of one camp, flag any two you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and tell me which single requirement does the most to rule out the obvious answers. If the request rests on a mistake — if sameness of mechanism is only ever relative to a question or a purpose, so that there is no context-free criterion to be had — say so plainly rather than supplying a list, and tell me what that leaves me with.
