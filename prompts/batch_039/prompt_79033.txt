I keep running into a family of decision problems that all seem to want the same repair, and I want to know whether the repair has a name.

The first: I am choosing in a situation where something that happened before I was born — a process that modeled me well enough to predict what I would choose — has already fixed how the world will treat me. The option that looks best given what I can see is not the one that looks best in the worlds where the prediction went that way, and oddly it is the latter that I find myself caring about. The second: I would do better over a lifetime if I were the kind of person who cannot be pushed around, even though being that kind of person costs me in the particular case where someone tries to push. The third, which is the one I cannot make sense of: a friend insists that if I meet a stranger who decides by exactly the same procedure I do, working from different information, then that stranger's decision is my decision — so I should cooperate with them in a one-off prisoner's dilemma, and care about what happens to them the way I care about my own future.

What unites them, I think, is that in each I do better by giving up the question "what should I do now, given what I know?" and asking instead "what rule would I have wanted to have chosen, from before I knew which of these situations I would land in?" — then following that rule even where its local advice is worse. I can see how this sits close to two things I already have some grip on: the idea that your own choice is itself evidence about how the world is, and the ordinary business of binding yourself in advance so that others can see you are bound. But neither is the thing I am after: the first still has me reasoning from inside my situation, and the second needs someone to actually see the commitment.

Is there a name for this? If so I want the carving, not just the label: how it differs from each of those two neighbours, and which of my three cases it really covers. I suspect the second is a different animal, and that the third is the same animal pushed further than I expected, but I would like to be told whether that is right, and why.
