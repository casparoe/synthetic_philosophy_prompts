I'm running next month's session of our dead-philosophers reading group on Nyāya — the four pramanas: perception, inference, testimony, and the comparison/analogy one (upamana). Eight people, mostly working scientists plus one lawyer, no Sanskrit, no Indian philosophy between them, 75 minutes. Don't explain the pramanas to me and don't hand me readings, I know the material well enough — what I need is a plan for the session.

Three things I'm stuck on.

One, the order. What has to be in place before the four even make sense as a set? I keep starting with the list itself and it just turns into a vocabulary lesson.

Two, the carrying example. I've been trying to make one running case do all four — a person trying to find out whether the river behind their house is safe to swim in. They see the other kids in it, they reason from the smell and the plant upstream, a farmer tells them, and someone says it's like the river back home. It works for three and goes mushy on the fourth. Do I keep forcing it, or run a second case alongside? And either way, what does the carrying case have to have, and what does it have to lack?

Three, the misconceptions. I'm expecting "perception is whatever comes in through the senses," "inference is just induction," "testimony is faith, or hearsay," and "the four are a checklist you tick off one at a time when you're deciding what to believe." Where in the sequence do I head off each one, and what else are they going to walk out with that's wrong?

Also: my lawyer is going to say testimony is the hearsay rules and comparison is precedent. Do I encourage that or shut it down? And give me one question to put to the room at the end that would actually tell me whether the framework landed rather than whether they can recite four words. Plus what I should refuse to get into at this level, and say out loud that I'm refusing.
