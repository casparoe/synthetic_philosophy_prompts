I'm in the third year of a philosophy PhD, working on decision theory, and my supervisor has asked me to write a short "where I stand on the value of information" note for our formal epistemology reading group so that people can poke at it. I wrote the whole thing in one sitting and I'm now worried it doesn't hang together. Could you audit it for me?

(1) I'm an evidentialist about rational choice, and I think evidentialism is the correct theory rather than a quirk of one formalism: the act you should perform is the one whose expected utility is highest conditional on your performing it. The smoking lesion case doesn't worry me (you shouldn't smoke), Newcomb doesn't worry me (you one-box), and I think the causal/evidential distinction is largely a distinction without a difference.
(2) I'm a Bayesian with nothing bolted on: conditionalising is the whole of rational belief change, and more evidence never leaves you epistemically worse off than less.
(3) I accept the non-negativity of the expected value of information -- Good's theorem, if that's the right name for it -- so free information can never leave you worse off in expectation. And I take that as a constraint on any theory of rationality, not just a fact about one formalism: you can always ignore what you've learned, so a theory which implies that information can hurt you is refuted by that alone.
(4) I think it can be rational -- not merely psychologically excusable -- to pay to avoid information: the patient who says don't tell me, the person who won't open the letter, the one who won't look at the balance. Knowing can genuinely make your life go worse.
(5) I think a decision can be the rational one and still be a mistake. "I shouldn't have looked" can be true as a verdict on the decision, not just a report that the news was bad, and I dont think that's only hindsight talking.

I've got no idea whether these are seperate commitments that merely happen to feel related, or whether some of them are pulling against each other. Rank the conflicts by how severe they are instead of telling me everything is a bit tense, tell me for each genuine one which commitment has to give and what the cheapest repair would cost me, and tell me which pairs only look inconsistent so that I can stop worrying about them.
