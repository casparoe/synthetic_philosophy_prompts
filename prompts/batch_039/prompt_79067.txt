Write me a fake help-center FAQ for a startup that thinks it built the first strategy-proof voting app. Deadpan corporate voice, numbered Q&As, around 600 words, no winking at the reader.

The joke: they've read the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem — the result that says once there are three or more options, voters can rank them however they like, and no single person gets to be the dictator, every voting rule can be gamed — and instead of treating it as a limit, they've productized it. The FAQ should keep cheerfully admitting the theorem while spinning each consequence as a premium feature. By the end a reader should have worked out on their own, without being told, that "strategy-proof" and "everyone gets a real say" can't both be true of the same app.

Things I want in it, and they have to be technically right:

- why you can only rank two candidates (sold as minimalist design, not a concession)
- what happens the moment there are three or more options — who actually ends up deciding, and which subscription tier that person is on
- a single-peaked mode, for when all the options sit on one line, like picking where to get lunch; this one genuinely does restore strategy-proofness, and the company should present it as a proprietary breakthrough they invented
- a randomized tier where the app just picks one user at random and does whatever that user wanted — marketed as fairness through uncertainty — and it should actually be strategy-proof, so a social-choice person nods instead of wincing
- one line from an invented in-house "integrity officer" that, read twice, is not concerned at all

Invent pricing tiers, testimonials, an upgrade nag, whatever you need. Don't cite anything and dont add a footnote explaining the theorem — let the fine print carry it. American English, and no moral at the end.
