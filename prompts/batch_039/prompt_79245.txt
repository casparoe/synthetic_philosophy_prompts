ok so I'm in this metaphysics reading group and next week we each have to bring a one-page position sheet on material objects — basically, when do some things add up to a thing? — and then everyone else gets to go at it and find the seams. I've written mine and I have a nagging feeling that two of the items can't both be true, but every time I try to work out which one has to go, I end up convincing myself it's all fine. So I'd like an outside audit before I hand it in.

Here's the sheet. (1) The question of when some things compose a further thing has a real, non-arbitrary answer. There are genuine wholes and there are mere heaps, and universalism is false — there's no object made out of my left shoe and the moon. (2) The answer is roughly this: some things compose a whole when they're caught up in a single, unified causal process. A living organism is the clear case; a running engine is probably one too, whereas the same parts lying on a bench, even in the same arrangement, are not. (3) Vagueness is never in the world, only in us — our language and our representations are vague, but every question about what there is has a determinate yes-or-no answer, wether or not anyone could ever know it. (4) Composite objects aren't fundamental: everything true of a table is grounded in facts about simples and how they're arranged. No new fundamental properties show up at the composite level, nothing strongly emergent. (5) All the same, biology and economics give genuine explanations and aren't just shorthand for physics — I don't think the special sciences reduce to anything. (6) Composites do causal work. The table broke the vase; it wasn't merely that the simples arranged tablewise made the simples arranged vasily fly apart. Oh, and one thing I always say in discussion: I'm perfectly happy to quantify plurally over any things you like, so "there are some things that are my shoe and the moon" comes out true. I just don't think that gives you an object.

What I want from you: tell me which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how severe each real clash is — ranked, please, not a list where everything gets called a "tension". For the real ones, say what would have to give and what it would cost me, and at the end tell me which single revision cleans up the most mess. And I'd rather not be told "well, this is why everyone ends up a nihilist" or "this is why everyone ends up a universalist" — I want to know whether the package as it stands can be made to hold, and if it can, what's doing the work.
