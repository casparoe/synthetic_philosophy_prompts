I have been trying to work out whether the mere fact that a benefit lies in the future can be a reason to count it for less, and I have ended up with an argument that I can neither fault nor accept. It runs like this.

(1) A pure time preference is a reason to weight a benefit less that depends only on when the benefit occurs: not on who receives it, not on its size, and not on how likely it is to occur.
(2) Any such preference can be represented by a positive rate r, so that a benefit arriving t years from now enters the account with weight (1 + r)^-t.
(3) For any positive ε there is a time T such that (1 + r)^-T is less than ε. So beyond some point in the future, no one's benefit enters the account with a weight above ε, however great the benefit.
(4) Any positive rate r therefore makes the wellbeing of sufficiently distant people count for nothing.
(5) A view on which someone's wellbeing counts for nothing is not one I can hold.
(6) So r = 0: the timing of a benefit is never by itself a reason to count it for less.
(7) With r = 0, a marginal benefit at any time counts for exactly as much as an equal marginal benefit now.
(8) So a choice that costs the present a great deal and delivers only a small increment to someone at a sufficiently remote time — nothing else being at stake — is the choice required.
(9) I cannot accept that we are required to beggar the present for the sake of a marginal gain to a person living in the year 10^100.
(10) So (6) is false, and some positive pure time preference is required after all.

I set out to refute (10). I would rather keep (5) and (6) and give up (9) than give up either of them. I also notice that the same shape of argument can be run for the successive parts of a single life, where it would tell a person to load their goods into their earlier years — a conclusion I cannot accept either.

Tell me which step I should reject. Explain why that step looked innocent, and set out the price: what I would owe by way of an alternative account of how the weight of the future works, and which neighbouring claims I would have to abandon along with the step I give up.
