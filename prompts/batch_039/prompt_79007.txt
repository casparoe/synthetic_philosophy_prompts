A colleague in my lab has circulated a short primer on "low-impact" approaches to AI safety, and several people have started treating it as the canonical summary of that line of work. I have enough background to suspect that it smooths over something important, but not enough to say where the smoothing happens. I would like an accuracy audit.

Here is the substance of the primer, more or less verbatim:

"Start from the observation that what we really worry about with a capable AI system is side effects — the collateral damage it does along the way, the effects nobody intended. The low-impact research program takes the intuition 'do no unnecessary harm' and makes it computable. You begin with a model of the environment. You choose a baseline: normally, the state the world would have been in had the agent done nothing. Then, for any candidate action, you measure how much it reduces the set of states that remain reachable, compared with the baseline. A penalty proportional to that reduction is added to the agent's reward. The result is a cautious agent: it will not knock over the vase, will not overwrite the filesystem, will not manipulate its overseers. What is elegant about this is that no specification of value is required. You need the environment's dynamics, a way of comparing states, and the baseline; you do not need to tell the system which outcomes are good. Impact is a measure of disturbance, and disturbance can be quantified without knowing what matters. There is disagreement about details — whether the baseline should be inaction or some default policy, whether you should measure the reachability of states or of a set of auxiliary goals, whether the penalty should be one-sided so that improvements are never penalized, how to stop the agent from gaming the baseline by degrading its own position on purpose. But those are implementation questions. The framework itself shows that 'avoid unnecessary side effects' has a precise meaning that can be fixed without settling any substantive question about value. And since a low-impact agent disturbs nothing by construction, it is also the natural route to a system that can be switched off."

What I want is a claim-by-claim audit rather than a general review. Take the primer's substantive sentences in order. For each one: would someone who actually works on this accept it as written, wince at it, or reject it? Where the primer is describing a real feature of the approach, say so plainly, so that the accurate parts are not buried under the corrections. Where it is a popularization, name the simplification and say what it costs. Where it is wrong, give the corrected version and explain why the difference matters.

I am especially interested in three things. First, the notion of a side effect: the primer treats it as whatever nobody intended, and I want to know whether that is the operative notion in this work or whether it imports the wrong one, and what turns on the difference. Second, the central claim that no specification of value is needed: I would like a verdict rather than a hedge on whether impact measures genuinely avoid a value specification or merely push it somewhere less visible — and if the latter, exactly where the residue sits, and whether it is small enough to be a difference in degree rather than in kind. Third, the closing inference from low impact to switchability: the primer moves from a property of a penalty term to a claim about a deployed system's incentives without argument, and I want to know whether the inference survives.

Assume no web access, and do not supply citations, references, or reading lists; I am not looking for the literature, I am looking for an assessment I can check by reasoning. If any part of the primer is ambiguous in a way that changes the verdict, say which reading you are assessing.
