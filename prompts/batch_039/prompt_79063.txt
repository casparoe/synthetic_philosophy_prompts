ok, chapter three is kicking my ass. everyone trots out Fodor's systematicity and productivity arguments as the twin pillars of the language of thought, and every time someone tries to describe a non-LOT system that satisfies both premises, the discussion collapses into alot of hand-waving — some version of "well, it's still compositional somehow." So I want you to actually build the case, to spec, and then audit it honestly.

Here's the spec. (1) The system genuinely has systematicity in the sense the argument needs: its capacity to represent one structured content is bound up with its capacity to represent the permutations and variants of that content, and that binding isn't a coincidence or a lucky generalization from training — the architecture explains it. (2) It's productive: from a finite basis it generates unboundedly many representations it has never encountered, and those novel representations are actually usable in thought and inference, not just printable. (3) The vehicles of representation are not language-like: no discrete symbol tokens, nothing you can copy, concatenate, or type out, no constituent structure sitting in the state that mirrors the constituent structure of the content. Distributed, superposed, or dynamical vehicles only. (4) One paragraph, no exotic physics, no demons, and if it's an artificial system it should be one we could plausibly build or train.

Then do the audit. For each of the two arguments, say exactly which premise my case satisfies and whether the inference to a language of thought still goes through. If it doesn't, name the step that breaks. Then tell me which single feature of the case is load-bearing — if I stripped it out, would the case collapse back into an argument for LOT? Finally, play the classicist: what's the most damaging thing a Fodor loyalist could say to disqualify my case, and is that objection principled, or is it just redefining "language-like" until nothing counts?

Two things I need to be strict about. The systematicity has to fall out of the architecture, not be stipulated in the setup — if the case just announces that the system is systematic, it's circular and useless to me, since what I'm arguing in this chapter is that the arguments are weaker than advertised and a working counterexample would be a real weapon. And if you think the spec is actually unsatisfiable, don't fake it — say so, and show me which constraint collides with which. If it's genuinely unsatisfiable that's a result too, but I need to know it's not just a gap in how I wrote (3).
