Ok so I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for like fifteen years, and the Kelly betting stuff is one of those topics where I've read a ton and still can't tell whether the two camps actually disagree about anything or are just talking past each other. So I want to force it into an adverserial collaboration.

Camp A, the growth-optimality / "geometric rationality" people: Kelly betting (maximize the expected log of your wealth, i.e. the geometric growth rate) isn't one utility function among many, it's *the* right rule for sizing bets. Their case is that wealth compounds, that you can't bet money you don't have, and that over many bets the Kelly bettor almost surely ends up richer than anyone betting a bigger fraction -- so it's a structural fact about repeated multiplicative betting, not a taste for risk-aversion.

Camp B, the expected-utility people: no, Kelly is just expected utility maximization with log utility. Log is a choice, a pretty risk-averse one, and everything the growth people say is either a restatement of that choice or an argument that only works in the limit of infinitely many multiplicative bets. Change the utility function and the rule changes; there's nothing extra-rational about the log one.

Write the joint statement these two could sign after a good-faith collaboration. I want:

- where they still part ways, each disagreement phrased in wording it's own side would accept for itself;
- the cruxes -- the sub-questions where settling one would move at least one side;
- for each crux, a case, bet, or argument that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance;
- and if part of the fight is verbal (e.g. what "rationality" is even supposed to mean here), say which part and why.

Keep it concrete, not just abstract. Cases I'd want in there:

- A fair coin where a win pays 1.5x whatever you staked and a loss costs you the stake, and you can stake any fraction of your bankroll, played once vs. played a thousand times. My read is that the arithmetic isn't in dispute -- linear-utility/EV says shove it all in, Kelly says stake about a sixth -- so the real question is which question was being asked and why the answer should depend on that.
- A version with additive payoffs instead of multiplicative ones (a salary, a fixed-dollar bonus): does the geometric/compounding argument still bite, or does it quietly collapse into ordinary expected utility? I suspect this is a crux, since one side seems to get its answer from the structure of the dynamics and the other from the utility function.
- The mean-vs-median thing: after many Kelly bets you're almost surely richer than the person who over-bet, but your expected wealth is lower. Both sides know this, so I want it on the table as a genuine crux rather than a gotcha.
- One-shot irreversible decisions (founder going all-in, career bet) where "in the long run" doesn't obviously apply.
