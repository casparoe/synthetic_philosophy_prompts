I'm reporting a feature on a well-funded customer-service AI startup, and I've run into a framing problem I can't quite resolve on my own. The company's marketing copy describes its chatbot as one that "genuinely cares about resolving your issue" and "believes empathy is the key to good service," and in my interviews, even the engineers slip into this language, saying things like "it thinks the customer is frustrated, so it adjusts its tone." My editor wants me to take a clear editorial stance: either call this out as misleading anthropomorphization dressed up as innovation, or treat it as simply the natural, harmless vocabulary we now use for sufficiently complex software. I keep going back and forth, because the system's behavior really does seem to track something like an internal, updating model of the customer's emotional state and goals, in a way that feels categorically different from how we'd describe a thermostat "wanting" to reach a set temperature, even though I know there's no inner experience behind either. I'd like your help thinking through when, if ever, it's substantively correct rather than just rhetorically convenient to describe a system like this as having beliefs or desires, and how I should decide where to draw that line in my writing so that I'm being accurate without either overselling the technology's inner life or unfairly flattening a real behavioral distinction.
