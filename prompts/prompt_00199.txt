I've spent the last few weeks building an automated negotiation agent for my company that will haggle with a partner firm's bot over how to split costs on a shared project. My instinct was to keep our bot's decision logic completely proprietary, since revealing it seems like it would just let the other side reverse-engineer our bottom line and squeeze us for a worse deal. But one of my colleagues wants to do the opposite: he's proposing that before negotiations start, we give the other company's bot read access to our full source code, and they do the same for us, so each side can see exactly how the other's bot will respond in any situation. He claims that this kind of mutual transparency can actually produce a better and more stable split than negotiating in the dark, and mentioned something about agents being able to verify facts about each other's behavior in advance rather than just guessing. This sounds backwards to me — if the other side knows precisely what our bot will do in every scenario, doesn't that just hand them all the leverage to exploit us? I'd like to understand whether there's a legitimate reason this could work in our favor, and if so, what would need to be true about how the bots are written for it not to just collapse into one side taking advantage of the other.
