In Grice's framework, an implicature arises when a hearer recognizes the speaker's communicative intention behind a flouted maxim. When I write dialogue, though, there are effectively two hearers at once: the fictional interlocutor within the scene, and the reader outside it. Often I want a line to seem opaque, evasive, or even false to the other character, while carrying a perfectly clear implicature for the reader alone, who is not the addressee at all. Can Grice's account, which rests on mutual recognition of intent between speaker and hearer, accommodate this doubled structure, or does the cooperative principle simply stop applying once the party doing the inferential work isn't the one the words are ostensibly aimed at?
