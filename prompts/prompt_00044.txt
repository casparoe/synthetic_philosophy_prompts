I've been working through L.A. Paul's work on transformative experience for a seminar paper, and I keep getting stuck on a specific wrinkle that I don't think her central examples (vampirism, becoming a parent) fully capture. Her framework distinguishes epistemically transformative experiences from personally transformative ones, but I want an example that pulls these two dimensions apart cleanly rather than letting them run together as they do in her usual cases.

Give an example of a decision scenario involving a transformative experience where the epistemic transformation (you gain genuinely new phenomenal knowledge you couldn't have inferred beforehand) and the personal transformation (your core preferences and values shift) come apart in an interesting way—for instance, a case where someone undergoes radical epistemic transformation without much personal transformation, or vice versa. I'd like the example to be specific enough that we can actually trace how Paul's decision-theoretic worry (that rational choice seems to require assigning value to outcomes you cannot yet epistemically access) plays out differently depending on which type of transformation is doing the work. Bonus points if the example resists the easy move of just saying "well, you should defer to your future self's values," since that response seems to dodge exactly the puzzle Paul is pointing at.
