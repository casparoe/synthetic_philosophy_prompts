Spent 35 years as a field ecologist before retiring, and lately I've fallen down a bit of a philosophy rabbit hole. Right now I'm chewing on Nozick's Anarchy, State, and Utopia, and his little detour on animals is bugging me more than it probably should (don't have the book in front of me so forgive any fuzzy details). He toys with the idea of "utilitarianism for animals, Kantianism for humans" -- basically, weigh animal welfare against itself and trade it off when the sums work out, but treat humans as inviolable, never merely a means to someone else's end. Then he seems to undercut his own idea with a thought experiment: imagine a race of beings vastly more rational and self-aware than us, relating to us the way we supposedly relate to animals. Surely we wouldn't want them folding our interests into their utilitarian sums just because of that capacity gap -- we'd want them to treat each of us as inviolable regardless. But then doesn't consistency demand we extend that same inviolability to animals, since theres a comparable gap between them and us?

I want the strongest possible pushback on that last inference. Argue that the thought experiment does NOT actually commit us to treating animals as inviolable rather than aggregating their welfare -- give me a real, principled disanalogy between the two cases, not just a restatement of the puzzle.
