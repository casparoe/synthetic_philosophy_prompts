As a PhD student in political theory working on the philosophical foundations of free-expression law, I'd like a dialogue between a strict Millian, who insists that only genuine harm to others can justify restricting speech, and a Feinberg-style liberal, who argues that profound and reasonably unavoidable offense (as in Feinberg's classic bus-ride hypothetical) can also ground restriction. Have them work through whether the offense principle is doing independent normative work or is really just harm smuggled in under another name, pressing each other on concrete cases (e.g., a public demonstration versus a captive audience on a commute) rather than resting on gestures toward hate speech in the abstract. Neither should concede the point too quickly.
