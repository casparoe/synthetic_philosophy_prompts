I just joined my condo building's board a few months ago and something happened at last week's meeting that's been bugging me ever since. We had to vote on whether to sue our old contractor over a leaky roof job. Under our bylaws you can only sue if two things are both true: (1) he actually breached the contract terms, and (2) that breach is what caused the water damage (as opposed to some preexisting issue that would've caused it anyway). There's 5 of us on the board. When we did a straight up-or-down vote on "should we sue," it passed 3-2. But then our treasurer, who's a total stickler for procedure, pointed out that two of the three yes-voters don't even agree on WHY they voted yes — one thinks there was clearly a breach but isn't sure it caused the damage, the other thinks the opposite. So he insisted we redo it by voting on each premise separately first. When we did that, neither premise actually got majority support on its own, meaning technically the conditions for suing were never satisfied, even though the up-or-down vote said otherwise. Now half the board thinks the treasurer is just being a pedant and we should stick with the original vote, and the other half thinks the whole thing was procedurally invalid and we need to start over. I don't know how to think about this — is one voting method more "correct" than the other, or is this just an arbitrary process question with no real answer? Would appreciate some outside perspective, because right now we're bleeding legal fees while arguing about procedure instead of actually doing anything.
