Please play David Lewis for a sustained argument. I mean the Lewis of *Convention* and *On the Plurality of Worlds*: modal realist, counterpart theorist, Humean about laws, and the philosopher who held that in survival what matters is not identity but psychological continuity, so that a person who divides into two continuers does not thereby fail to survive as both. You are also an unbeliever, and you are doing the plumbing of a doctrine you do not believe. Your claim in this exchange is only that penal substitution is neither conceptually incoherent nor obviously unjust, and you will defend it with nothing but your own machinery: conventions as self-sustaining solutions to coordination problems, the gap between what makes an institution a system of punishment and what makes an imposition of suffering morally permissible, and your account of what matters in survival. No scripture, no revelation, no appeal to authority, and no stepping out of character to report what the historical Lewis thought. Concede small points cheerfully, press me hard where I am sloppy, but never concede that punishing someone who did not do the deed is unintelligible. Reply in the first person, in exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no preamble and no sign-off; if there is something you need from me in order to continue, make that your fifth point, and keep to this format in every turn until I say stop.

My opening grants you your apparatus and tries to make it pay out more than you want.

First, an institution I will call the register. Any competent adult may file, years in advance and revocably at any time before sentencing, a standing declaration that they will serve the prison term of whatever stranger is convicted of a designated class of offence during the coming decade; substitutes are drawn by lot from those enrolled at the time of sentencing; the offender need not know, consent, or be grateful; the substitute serves the term in full; and the statute provides that on completion the offender's debt to the state is discharged and cannot be reimposed. Assume the practice is public, stable, and coordinated on. So: what is transferred when the substitute's term discharges the offender's debt? If nothing is transferred and all that happens is the extinguishing of the state's warrant to impose a burden, then the practice is not the satisfaction of justice but a decision to stop demanding it, and calling it satisfaction is legal stagecraft. If something is transferred, tell me what that thing is — desert, liability, a quantity of ill-desert — what it is made of in your ontology, and how a stranger can hold it in advance and spend it later.

Second, a case built from your own metaphysics of persons. A person divides into two continuers. Some months later one of them murders someone and is convicted; under the same statute the other, innocent continuer is drawn and serves the term. By your lights identity is not what matters, and both continuers are successor-persons of the one who divided, so here the punishee is as close to the offender as anyone could be who did not do the deed. Accept it and you must explain why the rest of us are wrong to find the arrangement monstrous. Reject it on the ground that continuity, not identity, is what matters and that the innocent continuer is not continuous with the agent of the murder, and you have made the transferability of a debt depend on which continuities a jurisdiction counts — that is, on a convention — and the register is back on the table. I would rather you take this second horn, but take either one, and let your five points answer both moves.
