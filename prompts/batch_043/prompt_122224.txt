I teach a third-year seminar in the philosophy of mind, and one of my students handed in a short argument that I have not been able to place. I have to respond to it in Thursday's class, and I would rather do that than simply announce that the conclusion is unacceptable. My reconstruction of it, with the numbering I imposed, runs as follows.

(1) If a system could be in mental state M without being in physical state P, then M is not identical with P.
(2) For every physical property P that in fact occupies the pain-role in human beings, some possible system — a creature with a different neurochemistry, a silicon-based organism, a prosthetic nervous system — is in pain without P.
(3) So pain is not identical with any physical property, and likewise for every other mental property. Reductive identity theories are false.
(4) The standard repair: mental properties are second-order. To be in pain is to be in some first-order state that plays causal role R; the occupant of the role varies, the occupancy of it does not. This respects (3).
(5) But the reasoning in (1)–(3) is entirely general. It applies to any kind whose instances could be physically diverse, and that includes valves, currencies, temperatures, and genes. The first-order realizers of our mental properties are therefore themselves functional properties, specified by their causal roles with respect to whatever realizes them.
(6) Nothing in (4) requires this hierarchy to come to an end. Consider what I will call the Ungrounded Tower: systems S1, S2, S3, and so on, where the states at each level are specified only by the causal roles they play within the level below, each level is realized by the next, and there is no lowest level whose properties are characterized intrinsically rather than relationally. There is no categorical ground floor anywhere in the Tower.
(7) By (4)–(6), S1 has genuine pains, beliefs, and desires. So functionalism turns out to be compatible with there being no categorical, non-functional basis for anything — with a purely structural or dispositional metaphysics of the sort physicalists took themselves to be denying. A theory adopted in order to locate mentality inside a physical world appears to deliver mentality in a world with no physical floor at all.
(8) And even in ordinary cases the distinctness established in (3) causes trouble: the second-order property and its first-order realizer are distinct properties, both instantiated, both apparently sufficient for the behaviour. Either every intentional action is systematically overdetermined, or the mental property inherits all of its causal power from the realizer and does none of the work itself — which, for a theory that defines mental states by their causal roles, looks like suicide.
(9) So the multiple-realizability argument either proves far too much — no reduction anywhere in the special sciences, and Tower systems count as minded — or it refutes the theory it was supposed to motivate.

The steps I would like to keep are (1), (2), and (4): the Leibniz's-law step, the multiple-realizability premise, and the second-order account. What I cannot tell is whether (5) and (6) really follow from (4) or involve some slide — perhaps from the possibility of alternative realizers to the possibility of no categorical realizer at all, or from being functionally specifiable to being nothing but a role — whether the Tower is so much as coherent, and whether the dilemma in (8) is genuine or rests on a picture of causation and realization that we ought to have abandoned. A related question, if you have room for it: should the existential quantifier in (4) admit degrees, so that a system occupying most but not all of R counts as being in pain to a corresponding degree?

Tell me which step you would reject and what rejecting it costs: for each of the plausible candidates, what the functionalist has to give up, what they get to keep, and which of the premises I wanted to keep it forces me to surrender. Begin with a single sentence naming the step you reject, then spend the remainder pricing the alternatives. Please keep the entire reply under 450 words.
