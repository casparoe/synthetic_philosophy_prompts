I am assembling a short tutorial on Bertrand's paradox and the principle of indifference, and I need one centrepiece case built to order rather than borrowed from the literature.

The moral I want the tutorial to land is that the paradox is not really about geometry, continuity, or the nonexistence of a uniform distribution over an infinite set. It is about the fact that a description of the outcomes of a chance setup underdetermines the mechanism that generates them, so that the instruction to distribute credence evenly over the possibilities has no determinate content until the mechanism is fixed. Since every familiar version of the case involves a continuous quantity, students conclude that the trouble lies in the continuum. So I want a case in which the point survives with no continuum anywhere, and in which the mechanism can be written down in full.

Please construct one to the following specifications.

1. Finite throughout. Both the outcome space and the fine-grained state space of the mechanism are finite, with no more than twelve fine states and exactly three outcomes. No length, angle, area, duration, or other continuous magnitude appears anywhere in the setup or in the reasoning, and the case must not be a discretised version of the chord case or of any other published paradox.

2. Three outcomes, described symmetrically. Give a coarse description of the setup, in one paragraph and in terms of ordinary physical objects that a workshop could actually build, which is invariant under cyclically permuting the three outcome labels. The description should be one a careful reader would accept as a complete specification of a symmetric chance setup. It follows that applying the principle of indifference to the outcomes yields one third each, not as a stipulation but as the only verdict the description permits.

3. The mechanism, in full. Give the fine-grained state space, and make it symmetric in the strong sense that some family of transformations of the device permutes the states so that any state can be carried to any other. Indifference over these states is therefore genuinely vindicated rather than merely assumed.

4. The verdicts diverge. Under the mechanism, the long-run frequencies of the three outcomes are three numbers that are pairwise distinct, that each differ from one third, and that depart from it by enough to be visible in a few hundred trials.

5. No defects, and nothing essential withheld. Nothing in the device is weighted, worn, sticky, or biased; every component is regular. The non-uniformity must arise from the relation between the two descriptions rather than from a flaw in the hardware. Because a strongly symmetric state space together with a label-symmetric readout would force uniformity, you must exhibit which link in the argument actually fails, and it must fail for a structural reason.

6. Say what the probability is of. State explicitly whether the one third delivered at the coarse level is the wrong objective chance while being the right rational credence for someone confined to the coarse description, or wrong in both respects, and if it is the former, say what further information would move credence onto chance and why.

Hand over: the paragraph; the two state spaces with their symmetries; both sets of numbers; the diagnosis of the broken link; the minimal change that makes the two verdicts coincide, together with whether that change is a change in the world or only in what is said about it; and the strongest objection available to someone who holds that the principle of indifference needs no mechanism to operate on, followed by your reply. Keep the arithmetic in fractions and write for first-year philosophy students who have seen the chord case but no measure theory.
