I'm in sixth form and somehow got put on the group deciding whether our school is allowed to use AI for things, so I've been reading about the whole control vs alignment thing. I've got gut feelings about which safety setups are ok but no clue what rule underneath them adds up to.

- AI marks homework, but a teacher reads every mark before it goes out: fine.
- Same thing, except the teacher only spot-checks 1 in 10: not fine, feels like a rubber stamp.
- The AI can't write freely, it can only pick from a list of comments the school wrote itself: fine, feels way safer than I can explain.
- Two copies of the same model check each other, and one got a prompt telling it to look for cheating: not fine at all, feels like they're on the same side.
- A weaker, older model checking a newer, smarter one: I trust it to catch wrong maths answers but not to catch the smart one being manipulative, which seems inconsistent now I've written it down.
- Before we switch it on, we give it fake chances to misbehave (like a student emailing to ask it to leak everyone's grades) and it never takes them: I feel reassured, but also, obviously it knows it's in a test? And when the AI writes those fake tests itself I feel less reassured than when teachers write them, even tho its traps are more convincing.
- We keep a log of everything it does and read it afterwards: feels like it does basically nothing.
- We wipe its memory between chats so it can't carry out a long plan: feels like it helps a lot, maybe more than it really does.

My only guess at a principle is that a safety measure only counts if it'd still work on a model that knows about the measure and is actively trying to get round it — but taken seriously that seems to rule out nearly everything, and I don't quite believe that either.

Is there one principle that gets most of these, which of my verdicts does it force me to drop, and what does it say about the case I keep flip-flopping on: letting the AI email parents directly, but every email has to be approved by the head of year first, who already gets about 200 emails a day and just skims them.
