Two friends of mine got stuck on this for like two hours at a pub, and since I was the designated non-philosophy person at the table I mostly just listened. The question was whether *you* could have come from a different sperm and egg — whether a totally different pair would have meant a different person, born to your parents and given your name, rather than you.

Marta said no, obviously not, and gave the old Kripke-style intuition: just as a particular table couldn't have been built from a completely different block of wood, you couldn't have grown from a different pair of gametes. Every counterfactual where some other sperm wins the race, she said, is a story about someone else who'd have been raised by your parents — not about you.

Devin said gametes aren't like wood. A sperm isn't a thing with a career; it's a throwaway haploid cell, made by the million, and the four products of one meiotic division are near-twins, differing by a bit of recombination and a handful of base pairs. Swap the winner for one of its sister spermatids and surely the resulting person is still you. And once you grant that, there's no principled place to stop — pile up enough tiny swaps and you reach any sperm you like. So any sperm would have done.

Marta said that's just a sorites: vagueness in the middle of a range doesn't make the far end possible. She's happy to say it's indeterminate whether someone from a slightly different sperm would be you, and still say no about a sperm from a entirely different man.

Devin then went after uniqueness. There are documented human chimeras, he said — two separately fertilized eggs that fuse very early, with one person developing from both — so a single person can have two zygotic origins. And cloning shows the gamete framing is wrongheaded anyway: Dolly came from an adult mammary cell nucleus and an emptied egg, no sperm involved. So what exactly is supposed to be essential here — the gametes, the genome, the material, the developmental process?

Marta said both cases just need the origin described properly: for the chimera you begin at the fusion, from the fused cell mass; for Dolly, from that somatic nucleus plus that egg. What's essential is the material you actually came from, whatever shape it comes in. And she had an argument: if you could have come from sperm A in one scenario and sperm B in another, then consider a scenario where the A-egg and the B-egg both get going at once — two embryos in a dish, say. Each is supposed to be you, so by the transitivity of identity they'd be each other: two distinct people, one person. Something has to give, and she says the cheapest thing to give up is the thought that you could have come from a different sperm.

Devin said not so fast: that argument only works if coming from the same gametes is *sufficient* for being you across scenarios, which is the exact point in dispute. You could instead say that in the two-embryo scenario neither one is you, or that it's just indeterminate which, and give up transitivity rather than origin essentialism. And there's independent pressure toward that, he said — your own zygote could have split into identical twins, and then there's no answer to which twin you'd be.

Marta said twins aren't a problem if the zygote was never you: you only come into existence once the early cell mass can no longer split or fuse, around the primitive streak, a week or two in — so there's no fission case for persons at all, only for the cell mass that preceded you.

Devin said that costs more than it saves, because now the fact that your cell mass didn't split becomes essential to you: had it split, neither twin would be you, which means you couldn't have had an identical twin and still existed, wich sounds nuts. Also, if the origin that matters is a two-week developmental process rather than a pair of gametes, then the original intuition has quietly turned into something much weaker and much harder to state.

Then we had to leave. Neither had moved. Can you go through it move by move and tell me who's right about what, which claims are doing real work and which are stalling, and what, if anything, both of them are missing?
