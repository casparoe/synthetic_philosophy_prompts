I've been covering the consciousness beat for a couple years and I've more or less swallowed the global workspace line: what makes a state conscious, on this story, is that its content got broadcast into a frontoparietal workspace where a bunch of consumer systems — memory, verbal report, deliberate action — can get at it. In copy I've been treating that as the whole story, i.e. phenomenal consciousness just is that global availability, with no leftover "what it's like" beyond access.

Play it straight with me: what would count against that identity claim? I don't want the greatest-hits version where you recite the overflow argument and I nod along. I want what a committed workspace theorist would themselves have to concede, ranked fatal to merely awkward. A few things I specifically care about: which findings actually bite rather than getting absorbed by redescription (rich phenomenal content during a demanding task, no-report paradigms, decoders reading scene content out of early visual cortex, neglect and extinction, dreaming, frontal damage that leaves experience apparently untouched); whether the theory predicts anything its rivals — recurrent processing, integrated information, higher-order accounts — don't also predict, and whether anyone has tested that; and where the real exposure is, since "available to consumer systems" starts to sound like it's just defined by whatever gets reported, which would make the view unfalsifiable. Say too whether that insulation would actually be a problem for a view with this kind of ambition, or whether it's fine here.

My own wrinkle, tell me if its anything: I keep turning over a case I'm calling the dark dashboard — a subject who reports a visual scene in full detail, workspace broadcast apparently intact, but whose early sensory areas show exactly the signature that in everyone else tracks unattended, unreported stimulation. Does that break the inference from report to phenomenality that the whole evidence base runs on, or is it the same old argument in a lab coat?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — how exposed the claim actually is — then the reasoning. No preamble, don't restate my question.
