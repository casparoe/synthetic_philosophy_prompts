I would like a disambiguation of a sentence I keep meeting in arguments about war: "Violence never solves anything."

Two people in my life use it, and I doubt they mean the same thing. My uncle produces it whenever I raise conscientious objection: if violence never solves anything, then refusing to fight isn't a sacrifice, it's mere prudence, and there is nothing left for me to be conscientious about. A friend who supports some wars uses it just as cheerfully; she agrees that violence "doesn't solve anything" and still thinks it is sometimes the least bad available option. I want to know what the sentence would have to mean for each of them to be right, and whether either reading actually supports a pacifist conclusion.

The parts I can't get straight: whether "solves" is a causal claim that could turn out false or a stipulation about what counts as a solution (does a ceasefire imposed by force count, or must the underlying grievance be gone — and gone within a year or within a century?); whether a threat of violence that is never carried out is covered, since a deterrent that works looks like a counterexample of some kind; whether "never solves anything" differs from "is never necessary to solve anything," since only the second seems to be what my uncle needs; and whether the claim ranges over individual acts or over policies, given that he needs it true of wars while my refusal is one person's act.

Lay the distinct precise readings out as a table with these columns: reading (the proposition in one sentence), claim_type (empirical-causal / conceptual / moral / stipulative), status (trivial, substantive and defensible, substantive and dubious, false), verdict_for_pacifism (supports it / neutral and equally at home in a just-war view / cuts against it), and falsifier (what would have to be true, or would have to happen, for that reading to fail). After the table, in a few sentences, say which reading people who deploy the sentence to end an argument are most likely relying on, and whether that reading can bear the weight they put on it. I don't want studies or citations — I want the map.
