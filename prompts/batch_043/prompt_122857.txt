Me and two friends run a reading group at the public library — we're not philosophers, one's a vet, one does IT, I tune pianos — and we've been stuck on the same patch of conciousness stuff for about four months. Hoping someone can tell me whether there's a word for what we keep tripping over.

Background so it's clear what I mean. There's a family of views we keep running into where the idea is that the tiny bits things are made of each have a little bit of experience, and the big experience I'm having right now is somehow built out of those. The objection that always comes up — we've now hit it in maybe six different books — is that piling up little experiencings doesn't obviously give you one big experiencing. You can list all the little ones and still want to ask: fine, but why is there one person here rather than just a crowd? And as far as we can tell the answers come in two shapes. Either (1) there isn't anything more to it: once the little experiences stand in the right relations to each other — something like being jointly available to one another — that just is what it is for there to be one experience, and asking how many experiencers there are on top of that is asking for a bonus fact that was never owed. Or (2) there is something more: it's a real extra fact about which bits are bound to which, and it's the sort of fact that doesn't appear on any physicist's list of basic facts.

Here's the bit we can't find a name for. The same shape of puzzle turns up in two other places, and nobody in the books seems to treat them as the same puzzle at all.

First: time. I go under general anaesthetic for three hours, there's no experience in there at all, and then there is again. Nobody thinks that's two different people, and nobody seems to think something needs binding across the gap. But if the right answer to the little-bits puzzle is (2), a real extra binding fact, the time version looks like it ought to be exactly as hard — and it doesn't feel hard.

Second: nesting. My visual system, my cortex, my whole brain, my brain plus the top of my spinal cord, and me — those all look like candidates for the thing whose experience this is, all at the same moment, all apparently doing the same job. We pick one and shrug. But if the answer is (1), why doesn't there turn out to be a big experiencer for me-and-my-neighbour, or for the whole building?

And a third thing, which is what made us think the two answers aren't the whole story. Suppose (1) is right and all the little ones are present to each other, so there's one experiencer of a whole lot of tiny colour patches. Is it settled by that whether anyone sees a shape? It feels like you could have all the togetherness and still not have the thing we actually have, but we can't say what the missing bit would even be.

So: does this family of puzzles have a name, and are the three versions one problem or three? Please don't just hand us a label to tape on, and don't bother with references — our branch can't get the journals. I want to know what exactly the thing is, how the two answers differ, where each of them breaks, and what to call the leftover bit in the colour-patch case if there is one. If there's no established name for the general thing, coin one and tell us what would and wouldn't count as an instance of it.
