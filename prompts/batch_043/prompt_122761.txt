I'm on my hospital trust's serious-incident review panel. A patient on a ward died after a long stretch of deteriorating observations that nobody escalated. Three people separately had a duty to escalate, and each has told us they'd have done it if the others hadn't; a fourth — the consultant who wrote the escalation policy — was on annual leave, and says (probably accurately) that if she'd been around she'd have told them to keep monitoring rather than call for review. We now have to fill in the 'causal contribution' field on the incident form for each named person, and that field feeds two quite different things: the letter we send the family under the duty of candour, and the internal decision about who gets remedial training versus who gets referred for a fitness-to-practise conversation. Our current policy says the field is for 'acts that physically contributed', which as far as I can tell means we write 'none' against everyone — which feels like a cop out.

Please draft the short opinion I'll circulate before Thursday: what rule we should actually use to fill that field in, and whether it should be the same rule for the family letter as for the internal bit. Things I want you to get into: does it matter whether the non-escalation was deliberate (someone decided to wait, or decided it was someone else's job) rather than inadvertent (they simply didn't clock the obs or forgot)? What do we record when three people each would have acted, so that no single one of them made the difference to the outcome? And what do we say about the consultant on leave, given that her being there would presumably not have changed anything? Give me a bottom line I can actually put to a vote, and be properly fair to the view that 'cause' should be kept for acts and that we should use a different word entirely for the failures to act — a couple of the clinicians on the panel will push exactly that, and I need to know whether they're just being defensive or whether they've got the better argument.
