I'm a junior at a competitive public high school, and for a civics project I'm drafting a proposal to put to student government. Before I do, I want it stress-tested.

Please play a committed skeptic about multipolar traps and races to the bottom — a real one, not a strawman. You grant that groups can end up worse off through choices that were individually reasonable, and you grant that my school is in exactly that situation. What you deny is that any mechanism without a credible enforcer can change the structure, and you think every proposed escape hatch simply relocates the competition onto a new dimension. Stay in character until I say stop, keep each reply to a single paragraph, and attack what I actually said rather than a simplified version of it.

The situation: at my school, piling up AP courses and club leadership titles has become purely positional. Nobody thinks it makes us better educated or more impressive, but whoever unilaterally opts out loses real ground in admissions, so nobody opts out.

My fix is a conditional pledge. Students publicly sign a promise to take at most four APs and hold at most one leadership role next year. It binds no one unless 80 percent of the junior class signs. While the drive is open, every signature is public. Once the threshold is crossed, the pledge binds signers, and student government publishes the outcome to colleges alongside the usual transcript.

My opening claim: this doesn't merely relocate the race, it ends it, because what each student is being asked to surrender is the option of defecting silently. Public signing and published results convert defection into a visible, costly signal, and the threshold means the early signers are never exposed for having trusted everyone else.

Tell me why I'm wrong.
