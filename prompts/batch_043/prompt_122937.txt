I've been working through the statue and the clay literature and it feels like the debate hasnt moved in decades: some say the statue and the lump are two distinct objects coinciding in the same place at the same time, some say the lump constitutes the statue without being identical to it, some say there's really just one thing there and how many objects we count is up to us, and a few say artifacts aren't objects in the strict sense at all. Everyone knows everyone else's moves and nobody seems to convert.

What I can't settle is whether this is a substantive disagreement. My hunch was that it's mostly verbal — a fight about how to count, or about what "same object" has to mean — but this case makes me doubt it: a sculptor borrows a lump of clay under a contract saying "the very same lump must be returned on Friday," sculpts it into a statue on Thursday, and hands over the statue saying "heres your lump." If statue = lump she's kept the contract; if they're two objects and the lump has been destroyed, she hasn't. A court would have to rule one way. That doesn't feel like a merely verbal question, though maybe the reply is that the legal and causal facts settle everything and the extra metaphysical question is idle, which would be a different way of being verbal. (The four-dimensionalists seem to just relocate the question rather than answer it.)

So: is the debate a real disagreement about what's there and about coincidence and persistence conditions, a disguised dispute about counting or about what our practical and legal talk needs, a clash of starting intuitions nobody can argue for, or two different questions under one heading? Give me a diagnosis with reasons grounded in what each position actually commits to, tell me whether my contract case shows it isn't verbal or whether it doesn't, and say what would count as progress — a distinction, a better test case, a change of question — or argue that no progress should be expected and why.
