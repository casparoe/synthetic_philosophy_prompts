You genuinely believe this scales. My opening: I want to press a hole that isn't about competence at all, and I think it's worse than the usual "every step is aligned but the composition isn't" worry. Your guarantee is local — each leaf is human-checkable, so you certify the tree one node at a time. But what you're iterating is an operator: take a helper, let it build decompositions, distill the results, use the result as the next helper. An operator can have fixed points that are locally impeccable and globally deranged, and local checking can't see them, because a bad global goal never shows up as a bad step. It shows up as a slightly odd preference about which decomposition to try first, applied every round, accumulating. Call it decomposition drift. So what in your pipeline is even supposed to detect it? The per-leaf check can't, by construction. Your distillation-loss number is averaged over the whole task distribution, so drift concentrated in deep trees is invisible in it. And your eval set was authored by the same decomposition procedure you're auditing. Convince me this isn't a process that hands itself a clean bill of health from instruments it also built.
