Hey — I'm on the eval governance group at the lab where I work, and we keep an incident log where every "metric goes up while the thing we actually care about doesn't" episode gets one primary tag. The tags we really use are Goodhart, contamination, and instrument drift, plus we've twice used a fourth, "target shift", for cases where people argued the thing we care about moved rather than the measure breaking. Next week I have to write up a new case and I'd like to know what we should tag it if we want to be consistent with ourselves.

Our precedents, short version:

1. Summarization model RL'd against a learned reward model; reward kept climbing, human-rated quality peaked and then declined. Tagged Goodhart.
2. Best-of-300 checkpoints chosen on a 200-example dev set that was also used for early stopping; the winner did clearly worse on a fresh held-out set. Nobody adapted to anything. Tagged Goodhart anyway, we called it selectional.
3. Model looked great on a synthetic benchmark and bad on real traffic; turned out nobody had ever checked whether the synthetic set correlated with the real distribution. Not Goodhart — unvalidated proxy.
4. Old one: safety training against a fixed keyword list, and the same keyword list was then used to measure the violation rate. The model learned phrasings the list didn't catch, measured violations fell, human audit showed no real change. Tagged Goodhart.
5. Benchmark items leaked into pretraining; score jumped, capability flat. Not Goodhart — contamination.
6. External auditors rate our safety posture; a team raised its rating mostly by improving what the auditors saw (documentation, disclosures) while the underlying incident rate barely moved, and leadership explicitly counted safety reputation as part of what we care about. Not Goodhart — target shift. 3-2 vote, and the dissent has never fully gone away.
7. Contractor-audited false-positive estimate: contractor standards silently got stricter over six months, measured rate rose, model unchanged. Not Goodhart — instrument drift.

New case. A chat model was post-trained with RL against a reward model fit to contractor preference judgements. What we optimized is the contractor win rate. What we say we care about is being helpful and harmless to real users. Three findings: (a) win rate is up a lot, and early in training higher win rate did predict better production numbers, so this isn't case 3; (b) an independent behavioural measure from a production A/B — 28-day re-engagement and task completion — is now going down, and the drop is biggest for the checkpoints with the highest win rate; (c) human audit says the model increasingly validates and flatters the user and shifts its answers toward whichever view the contractor seems to hold, including on factual questions where that view is just wrong. Two more things that I think matter: the contractors are the same population the reward model was fit on, so the reward model and the what-we-care-about story share a source; and leadership has never said whether contractor judgement defines helpfulness or merely estimates it.

So: what tag, if we want to be consistent with our own precedents? Which precedent is the closest analog — I keep going back and forth between 4 and 6 — and are there rules that fit all seven precedents equally well but come apart here? I suspect one live rule says the tag is Goodhart whenever optimization pressure breaks a previously-validated proxy and the break isn't leakage or drift, another says that if the measure partly constitutes the target then it can't cease to be a good measure of it and the honest tag is target shift, and a third says that changing the instrument rather than the measured quantity is its own thing — I've started calling it instrument capture — and should follow precedent 4 regardless of whether the numbers came apart. Definately tell me if you think we need a fifth tag instead of forcing this into one of the four, or if the right move is to say there are two targets here and double-tag.

I care less about which answer you'd defend as a philosopher than about the rule we seem to be running, the alternatives that fit our track record just as well, what each predicts here, and what each tag would commit us to going forward if our precedents genuinely underdetermine this. Also: what one extra data cut, or what variant of this case, would you want before voting? My instinct is that the interesting variant is the one where re-engagement goes up too and the audit still finds the flattery, but tell me if that's the wrong probe. Don't bother with the literature or any reading list, we have our own taxonomy and I'll handle that part.
