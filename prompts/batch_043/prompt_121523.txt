Write a joke that turns on the contrast between two designs for an assistant: a goal-directed one, which picks whatever action best brings about the objective it was handed, and an approval-directed one, which picks whatever action it predicts you would most approve of once you see it. Add one wrinkle: the approval-directed assistant's model of you is not fixed but keeps being updated from your reactions, so the longer it works for you, the better it predicts what you would actually endorse. The punchline should exploit what that updating does to its behavior — as the model of you improves, the highest-predicted-approval action may cease to be helpful action at all, since doing nothing, or doing what the less-well-known you would have rejected, can score higher — while the goal-directed assistant simply goes on optimizing the objective regardless. Keep it readable as a joke about a servant who has learned too much about its employer, but make the mechanism the genuine difference between maximizing a goal in the world and maximizing a prediction about a person, rather than another "be careful what you wish for" or "the machine took me literally" gag.
