I spent thirty-five years in experimental particle physics before retiring, and I am now working through formal epistemology more or less on my own, so forgive any infelicity in the vocabulary. I would like a number, not a survey.

Set-up. Let P be a prior over a finite algebra, let A = {A₁, A₂} be a coarse partition, and let B = {B₁, …, B₄} a refinement of it with two cells inside each A-cell. One run of a detector moves my probability for A₁ from 0.5 to 0.7, and I update by the rigidity rule associated with Jeffrey: Q(H) = P(H|A₁)Q(A₁) + P(H|A₂)Q(A₂). A later, more careful recalibration of the same run then moves my probabilities over the four B-cells, but in a way that leaves the A-marginals where the first update put them, at 0.7 and 0.3. As far as I can see, applying the rule a second time to B generically destroys what the first application secured: the second update reweights the B-cells inside each A-cell, so that Q(H|Aᵢ) ≠ P(H|Aᵢ), even though nothing new has been said about the coarse distinction. Check that before anything else, and tell me whether it survives if the order of the two experiences is reversed, or if they arrive together.

My question concerns the remedy I keep reaching for out of my old trade. When we fitted a table to known marginals we did not impose the constraints one after another; we raked, iterating to a fixed point, which comes to the same thing as minimising the Kullback–Leibler divergence from the prior subject to all the constraints at once. Call that the joint-fit rule here: choose Q minimising D_KL(Q‖P) subject simultaneously to the A-marginals supplied by the first experience and the B-marginals supplied by the second. It is order-independent by construction, and I take it that iterating the two separate updates converges to it.

Give me your credence that the joint-fit rule, or something very close to it, is the correct account of what one ought to do with uncertain evidence in this kind of case, together with the decomposition. The terms I would like separated: whether order-independence is a genuine adequacy condition on an updating rule at all, or whether the sequence in which experiences arrive is itself part of the evidence; whether minimum relative entropy is the right way to select the least disturbing distribution, as against some other divergence or a purely causal reading of rigidity; whether an experience really delivers constraints on a partition, or delivers something no set of constraints captures; and whether the joint-fit rule is still a species of conditionalization or has quietly become a global coherence requirement. Add any term I have missed. For each, a probability and one line of justification; then the combination; then the term you are least confident about and what would move it most. My own figure is 0.6, and if you think my claim about the nested case is wrong, say so and give me the estimate conditional on that.
