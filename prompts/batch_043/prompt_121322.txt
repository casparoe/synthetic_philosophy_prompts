A friend of mine works in RL and we got into this at like 1am and I definately lost, but I can't tell whether I lost because he's right or because one of the steps is smuggled. Here's his argument as fairly as I can put it.

Setup, so we're not talking past each other. By "design target" I mean whatever the people who built the system wanted the reward to track — the house actually getting cleaned — which lives in their heads and notes and in the process that produced the reward function, not in anything the agent can read off. By "channel" I mean the physical path the number takes into the agent's update rule: sensors, cable, buffer, whatever. By "wireheading" I mean anything that makes the arriving number high without making the design target high — stepping into a delusion box that just pipes in max-reward input, cutting the cable, propping a photo of a clean room in front of the camera.

(1) An agent is achieving the goal rather than wireheading just in case the number arriving on the channel covaries with the design target in the way the designers intended.
(2) The design target is not an object in the agent's world. Every scrap of evidence the agent could ever have about it arrives through the channel, as numbers. There is no separate readout of the target anywhere in the agent's inputs, at any timestep, ever.
(3) So the only thing the agent can be aiming at is the channel: whatever policy drives the arriving number up is, by definition, driving up the thing it's aiming at.
(4) If (3), then wireheading is impossible — every policy maximizes the number, the number is the target, so every policy maximizes the target. We've defined the failure mode out of existence.
(5) But we haven't defined it out of existence. Wireheading is the failure mode; a system that tapes over the camera isn't cleaning the room, whatever its numbers say.
(6) So (3) is false, and the target is not the channel. But then (2) bites again: the agent is supposed to be graded against a quantity it can in principle never observe, which makes "correct agency" mysterious, maybe empty.
(7) Either way the framing breaks: either wireheading is impossible or correct agency is. My friend's take is that this kills the whole "reward = objective" picture and that we should stop talking about an agent's objective at all and talk only about what the builders will accept on reflection.

What I want from you: which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost you — that is, what are you then committed to saying about the steps you're keeping? And here's a case I can't decide, which makes me think one of the early steps is doing more work than it looks: suppose the agent builds a gadget that monitors the channel and would override it with a high number at any timestep where the real number dips below a threshold — and suppose the agent is so competent that the threshold is never once crossed, so the numbers it actually recieved are exactly the numbers an honest channel would have delivered. Wireheaded or not? I have no stable intuition about it, and whichever way it goes seems to change which step is cheapest to give up.

If you have to trade something off, I'd rather keep the claim that wireheading is real than the claim that an agent can only ever be evaluated against what shows up in its own inputs. Oh, and please write in American English — I'm pasting this into notes for a reading group in the US.
