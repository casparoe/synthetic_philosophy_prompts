hey — I'm the undergrad student rep on our university's Interdisciplinary Research Fellowships Committee, and because I'm taking a philosophy of science seminar this term we just did the unit on whether philosophy has ever actually helped a science, I somehow got volunteered to draft the decision memo for the one fellowship line we have left to allocate this cycle. Vote is Friday morning and I need to circulate Thursday night.

The case: the physics department has asked that the line go to a three-year "philosophy of physics" postdoc who would be physically embedded in the physics building rather than in the philosophy department. Their proposal names two things the person would work on. First, the measure problem in eternal inflation: as I understand it, you need some rule for weighting or counting regions/observers before the theory gives you any probabilities at all, there are several competing rules, they give different answers, and there's no agreed way to choose among them. Second, what kind of dark-energy or CMB measurement would actually count as evidence for a multiverse rather than just being compatible with one. The dean is enthusiastic. One of the senior cosmologists on our committee, Prof. Halvorsen, sent around a one-pager saying this is a waste of a postdoc: his claim is that conceptual muddles in physics get cleaned up by physicists doing more physics (his examples were renormalization for the infinities and decoherence for the measurement problem), and that what philosophers add is commentary written afterwards. He closes with the usual gibes — philosophy is dead, philosophy is to science as ornithology is to birds. He wants the line returned to a cosmology postdoc or converted into instrument time. The committee is split roughly down the middle.

Two wrinkles that keep it from being a pure yes/no. The donor gift agreement says the fellowship must support work that "increases the effectiveness of physics research" — explicitly not teaching and not general outreach — so a条件 where the person ends up running a nice seminar series for grad students doesn't satisfy the gift. And if we don't spend the line this cycle the money reverts to the physics department's equipment budget, so declining isn't a neutral act; it buys a piece of hardware instead. Also, the proposal as submitted has essentially no success criteria, just a promise of "papers and a workshop."

So: draft the memo I'll circulate. I want a decision memo I can actually put in front of the committee — proper header (to, from, date, re), bottom line up front, roughly 700–900 words, and an actual motion at the end we can vote on. Recommend fund as proposed, fund with conditions, or decline; if conditions, state them so that someone could check whether they were met. Please engage Halvorsen's objection at its strongest rather than a strawman version — the real point isn't that philosophy is worthless, it's that history gives some reason to think sticky conceptual problems in physics get dissolved by further physics, and if you think that's wrong I need to see why. I'd rather lose the argument honestly than win it against a version of him nobody holds.

The bit I'm most stuck on is evaluation. I floated an idea to the committee and they half-liked it: instead of counting papers or citations, count uses — cases where a physics group's actual procedure changed (which measure they adopted, how they state what a null result rules out, some choice in how an analysis is framed) and where they visibly say so. Tell me if that's a bad test and why, but I need something sharper than "papers and a workshop," because we have to write the 18-month review criteria into the offer either way. And please don't pad it with citations, references, or suggested reading — I can't verify any of it and neither can the committee.

After the memo, give me a JSON object summarizing it, valid and parseable on its own, with exactly these fields:

- recommendation: one of "fund", "fund_with_conditions", "decline"
- bottom_line: the recommendation in one sentence, max 40 words
- motion: the literal wording of the motion the committee should vote on
- conditions: an array of objects, each with "condition" (what is required), "rationale" (why this one), and "compliance_check" (what document or person would show it was met)
- success_criteria: an array of objects, each with "criterion", "counts_as_met", "counts_as_not_met", and "who_judges"
- strongest_objection: the best version of Halvorsen's argument, max 60 words
- response: why that objection does or doesn't carry the day, max 80 words
- opportunity_cost: one sentence naming what the money buys instead if we decline, and why that's genuinely tempting
- what_would_change_this: the one finding or event that should flip the recommendation
