So: a thousand bit-identical parallel copies of one mind running on a thousand machines are not a thousand patients, they're one patient who has been made very hard to kill. A perfect lookup table that reproduces every input-output pair of a trained net counts for nothing, because the work was already done by whoever built the table and nothing is being computed on the spot. And running one mind at a thousand times normal speed doesn't give you a thousand times more experience, it just gives you the same life played fast. Here's my opening move. Take some trained system — an image classifier, or whatever you'd prefer to use as the example — and replace its middle layers one at a time with exhaustive lookup tables that simply store that layer's input-output behavior for every input the system will ever actually be fed during its working life. Since its working life only contains finitely many inputs, such a table can in principle cover all of them, and after the swap the system behaves identically in every case that will ever arise. You want to say the swapped-in table computes nothing and so carries no moral weight. But the only difference left between the table and the layer it replaced is what would have happened on an input that never occurs — a counterfactual difference with no actual consequences anywhere in the world. So either your occupancy talk is really just a prejudice in favor of hardware that looks like it's straining, or you have to accept that the original layer's moral status was floating on possibilities that nobody will ever cash in.
