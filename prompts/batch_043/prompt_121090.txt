My friend and I argued late into last night about whether culture really evolves by Darwinian selection, or whether meme is just a grand word for a thing that caught on. She says the framework cannot be tested, and I could not say why it feels like more than a metaphor to me. It is not an academic question for me: I work on the ranking system at a large video platform, and what we do all day is generate variants of the same item, a different thumbnail, a different title, a different opening second, show each variant to a small random slice of users, keep whichever one holds attention, retire the rest, and let the winner propagate to everyone, over and over, on millions of items. If I squint, that is variation, inheritance, and differential fitness.

Two features of our own practice make me doubt the parallel. First, we do not simply let winners propagate: we also pay raters to score items for quality, we demote what we call engagement bait internally, and we fold those scores into ranking, so the same system that treats spread as success explicitly denies that spread is fitness, or at least that it is the only thing that should decide which variant survives. Second, the quantity we optimize is our own metric, fixed in advance, which makes the system look less like a climate than like a breeder. My friend pounced on that: artificial selection is not the Darwinian story, she said, and worse, it quietly gives up the strong claim that cultural variants are agents with interests of their own that can conflict with ours.

So what I want from you is the implicit theory of my own engineering practice. What would have to be true of culture for what we have built to count as Darwinian selection on cultural variants, rather than as ordinary optimization under a fixed objective? Which of the three ingredients does the machinery actually secure: variation, faithful inheritance, differential fitness? Is there a reading on which it fails one of them? I have a nagging worry here that copying which is very fast but very sloppy gives churn rather than accumulation, the way a replicator that copies too quickly and too inaccurately cannot hold any adaptation together at all, and that a system tuned purely for spread could push culture into that regime. Does the quality override contradict the selection picture, or is it a special case of it: selection at a different level, or a designed change to the environment in which variants compete? Where does the practice strictly presuppose a Darwinian, meme-level view of culture, and where is it philosophically underdetermined, so that a rival picture such as an epidemiological or cultural-attractor account would license exactly the same machinery? And if the strong picture were true, what would I have to build differently?
