After a career in experimental physics I have started sitting in on a philosophy of language reading group, and I have been asked to set down where I have landed on slurs so the others can attack it. I keep reaching for the distinction between a formalism and its interpretation, which served me well in my own field, and I am no longer confident it is doing the work I want here. Take S to be a slur and N its neutral counterpart, and confine attention to uses in which the intended target class is the same.

Here is the package:

1. S and N are coextensive, and sentences differing only by the substitution of S for N have identical truth conditions; nothing derogatory enters into what is said.
2. The derogatory component is conventional rather than conversationally derived: it cannot be cancelled, and it survives negation and interrogation.
3. It behaves as an expressive of the sort I have been reading about: non-truth-conditional, speaker-oriented, and not embeddable, so that an utterance presents the speaker as occupying the derogatory attitude and the content does not shift to whichever agent an attitude verb introduces.
4. Offensiveness is a property of the word, settled by its history of use, not a property of the speaker's psychology; someone who uses S with no ill will, or without knowing what it carries, still produces something offensive.
5. Appropriation is genuine and not merely excused: in in-group use, and in the case of a court clerk reading a transcript aloud, the utterance is not offensive at all, rather than offensive but permitted.
6. S is not lexically ambiguous; there is no derogatory sense sitting alongside a neutral one for an appropriating use to select between.
7. In free indirect discourse, where a novelist writes in the voice of a bigoted character, the derogation belongs to the character and not to the author.

I cannot myself locate the fault line, and I have no favourite among these. Please audit the set rather than survey the literature: which commitments are genuinely in tension, ranked by severity; for each, exactly what would have to be given up and what giving it up would cost; which tension is only apparent and why it dissolves; and, if there is one, the single revision that would clean up the most. Please answer in American English.
