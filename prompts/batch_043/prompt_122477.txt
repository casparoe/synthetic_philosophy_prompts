ok so i got kind of nerd-sniped in ap calc by this game and i've ended up with an actual opinion about it, and i want to know how exposed that opinion actually is.

the game: flip a fair coin until it lands heads. if the first heads is on flip n, you get +2^n/n dollars when n is odd and −2^n/n when n is even. so the payoffs are +2, −2, +2.67, −4, +6.40, −10.67, ... with probabilities 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, ... which means each outcome's contribution to the "average" is +1, −1/2, +1/3, −1/4, ... and if you add those up in that order you get ln 2, about 69 cents. but the positive terms and the negative terms each diverge on their own, so if you're allowed to add them in whatever order you like you can make the expected value come out to anything you want, or just blow up. this is the Pasadena thing (or at least my version of it).

my position: the game does have a correct price and it's 69 cents. the order-sensitivity isn't a reason to say there's no fact of the matter, it's a reason to notice that one ordering isn't arbitrary — the order you get if you list outcomes by when the game would actually reveal them, which happens to also be the order you get by listing them biggest-probability-first, and also the order you get by listing them smallest-payoff-first. three independent-sounding ways of picking an ordering all land on the same one, and that one converges. my friend says that's just picking a favorite ordering and dressing it up.

so: what would count against me? i want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and i want you to say which ones someone holding my view would concede as real and which they'd dismiss as a confusion. A few things i've thought of but can't tell how much weight they have:

- flat pasadena. don't flip one coin at a time — just pick a random real number in [0,1] and read off its binary expansion, so the whole infinite sequence is "already there" and nothing gets revealed in order. same outcomes, same payoffs, same probabilities, but no temporal structure for me to privilege. if my view gives 69 cents for the sequential game and something else (or nothing) for the flat one, that seems like a reductio, because they're the same game. is it? or is there still a canonical ordering on the game tree even when nothing happens in time?
- scrambled pasadena. permute the payoff schedule — say swap what you get at n=2 and n=3 — so that ordering-by-probability and ordering-by-payoff-size now pick *different* orders and give different numbers. my three-way coincidence looks like it was an artifact of how i set the game up. does my view then have to arbitrarily pick one, and is that fatal or just a loose end?
- the long-run thing. as far as i can tell, if you actually played this game over and over, your running average wouldn't settle down to 69 cents, because the huge rare payoffs keep wrecking it — the ordinary law of large numbers doesn't apply to something with an infinite mean like this. does that kill my number, or is it just the same problem showing up in a different costume, or is it actually evidence *for* me somehow?
- finite truncations. cut the game off after N flips and it has a boring well-defined expected value, and those converge to ln 2. isn't that just... my view winning? what's the strongest reason not to take the limit of the truncated games as the answer — and is whatever that reason is aimed at me specifically, or does it hit every position equally?
