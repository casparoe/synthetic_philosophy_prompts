My friend and I spent about three hours last night argueing about accuracy-first epistemology — shes basically a veritist, thinks the whole point of a credence is to be close to the truth and that's the only thing you score it on — and we never got to the case I actually care about. We've got twenty bucks riding on what she'd say, so: help me predict her.

Her verdicts so far, as best I can reconstruct them:

1. Fair coin, nothing else: 0.5 is right, and being at 0.9 on heads is worse, not just a gamble that might not pay off.
2. Fair coin, but a witness who's right 60% of the time says heads. She says 0.6. She specifically rejected both "stay at 0.5, the coin hasn't changed" and "heads is more likely than not, so go all the way to 1".
3. A guy with credence 0.9 that it rains tomorrow and 0.3 that it doesn't: defective, full stop. When I pointed out that my own respectable 0.55/0.45 got beaten in fact by that guy when it did rain, she said that's no defense, because there's a coherent pair that would've beaten him whichever way it went — she said 0.79/0.21, I checked, shes right.
4. Everything else held fixed, someone who was at 0.9 on the outcome that actually happened did better than someone who was at 0.6 on it.
5. You should plan to conditionalize, because from where you are now that plan has the highest expected accuracy. And she added that a plan can be right beforehand even when, as things turn out, it drags you somewhere less accurate than where you started — like when you go collect evidence that happens to mislead you.
6. I asked whether being certain and wrong is infinitely bad. She said it's the worst thing you can do, then changed the subject when I pushed on the "infinitely" part.

Two loose ends. First, she kept saying things like "I'd rather be wrong nearby than wrong a mile away" — off by one on the jellybeans beats off by a thousand — but that never came up in any case she actually ruled on, so I don't know if it's part of the view or just something people say. Second, I have a feeling all of this turns on how you score the credence that lands on the options which didn't happen, but she got vague when I asked.
