Hey, I'm in a creative writing club at school and we're doing a sci-fi unit, and I want to write something about the Fermi paradox but not the usual "they nuked themselves" or "we're in a zoo" version, so could you write me a short story I can crib structure from? I dont want a list of possible solutions or a survey — I want an actual story with people in it.

Here's the thing I keep turning over and I want the story to really be *about* it. My theory is that there's a tension inside one whole family of explanations that nobody ever seems to deal with. Say the reason the galaxy isn't crawling with colonies is that making yourself noticeable gets you killed: self-replicating probes are way cheaper than defending against them, so anyone who broadcasts or expands gets visited, so everybody stays quiet and hidden. Fine. But that means the silence is being held up by a *threat*, and a threat only works if the other side knows about it. Deterrence that nobody ever communicates isn't deterring anybody. So if that story is right, the sky shouldn't just be empty, it should be full of advertising: deliberate shows of force that don't give away where you live. Blowing up one of your own moons in a way that can't be natural. Pulses that are obviously artificial but come from a direction nobody could ever trace back. And as far as we can tell there is none of that. So either the threat-silence explanations predict something we just don't see, or the advertising is out there and we've been filing it under ordinary astronomy for decades.

I want the story to sit inside that fork and not wuss out and pick a side cleanly — end it somewhere honest, with the characters genuinely unable to tell which one they're in yet. Like, 800 to 1200 words, American English spelling please, and have the argument come out through the characters disagreeing rather than a narrator lecturing. My advisor said my last draft's dialogue sounded like a Wikipedia article, so make them talk like actual people who are tired and annoyed at each other. Set it somewhere concrete — I was thinking a radio observatory in Chile in the 2030s — and the person who spotted the anomaly should be weirdly reluctant to talk about it instead of excited.
