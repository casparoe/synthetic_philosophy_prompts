me and my friend got into a proper two-hour arguement last night about farmed shrimp, and I came away genuinely unsure what rule she's running. She sits on the technical committee for one of those farmed-seafood certification labels, and she insists every position she's ever taken is consistent with the others. I think her record fits at least three different rules at once and that they come apart on the case in front of her next month — but I couldn't get her to say which one she's on.

Her votes and stated positions, as best I can reconstruct them:

- Atlantic salmon: voted to require percussive or electrical stunning before bleeding — asphyxiation in ice slurry or CO2 is out, in her view. At the same meeting she voted *against* lowering the maximum stocking density, on the grounds that it pushes up cost per kilo and the farms will just drop the label.
- Edible crab and lobster: electrical stunning required before any processing; against live boiling; against declawing; against dry storage out of water. She keeps bringing up crabs rubbing at a wounded claw and learning to avoid a shelter where they got shocked.
- Whiteleg shrimp: voted *against* any stunning requirement at harvest — immersion in ice slurry is fine by her.
- But at that same meeting she voted *for* a rule that pond harvests can't leave shrimp stranded out of water or in the sun for more than a few minutes, and for a phase-out of eyestalk ablation pegged to whenever a selective-breeding alternative becomes commercially available. She voted against requiring anaesthesia for the ablation, saying there's no approved product and the hatcheries would walk.
- Bivalves: no provisions at all, didn't even want it discussed.
- Insects reared for feed (black soldier fly and similar): no provisions either, "come back with data" — although she did mutter that the numbers there are scary.
- Cleaner fish used in salmon cages (lumpfish, wrasse): voted to give them exactly the same provisions as the salmon themselves, on the grounds that they're vertebrates and the mortality in the pens is grim.
- When someone floated certifying farmed octopus: said the right answer is not to certify it at all, whatever the slaughter method.

The case next month: a company has a stunner that mounts on the harvest pump — continuous-flow electrical stunning of shrimp at harvest, at something like 1–2% of farm-gate cost, at realistic pond-harvest scale. Take the committee's own commissioned evidence review as given (I'm not asking you to check any of it, and please don't go looking for sources — just treat it as stipulated): it says shrimp have nociceptors, will groom or rub a treated antenna for minutes afterwards, learn to avoid a chamber where they got a shock, and show a stress-like physiology that is blunted by anaesthetics; but it also flags that all of this is compatible with nociceptive reflex plus simple associative learning, that there's no agreed decapod analogue of the vertebrate telencephalon, and that the only validation the stunner has is behavioural (loss of the tail-flip, no response to a noxious probe), which can't cleanly distinguish insensibility from paralysis. The review also notes that the numbers are in the hundreds of billions of animals a year, so even a tiny per-animal effect is enormous in aggregate.

So she has two votes coming: whether to require stunning for shrimp at harvest now that it's cheap and technically workable, and — if yes — whether behavioural validation of that kind is good enough or whether she should hold out for something else, and what.

Here's what I actually want: predict both votes, and more importantly name the rule she's running that generates them. Then give me the *other* rules that fit her whole record just as well and say what each of them predicts about these two votes, especially where they split. Say which one case you'd put in front of her to tell the surviving rules apart — I've got candidates (a near-costless kill method for the feed insects; an extension of the stunning requirement to some farmed finfish nobody has ever actually tested; a whole-life/enrichment rule for a long-lived species instead of a kill-method rule) but tell me which one discriminates best and why. And if she wants to stay consistent with herself, which of her own precedents is the closest analog here, and what is she committing to either way?
