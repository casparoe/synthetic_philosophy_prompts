I keep running into the popsci version of information cascades — "people copy each other, the herd locks onto the wrong answer, adding more people doesn't help" — and I want the actual machinery under it, the kind of thing I could turn into a forum post without embarrassing myself. Take the textbook setup: two states with equal priors, each person gets one private binary signal that's correct with probability q (say 0.6), people move in a fixed order, everyone sees everyone else's *action* but nobody sees anyone's *signal*, and you're paid for matching the state and nothing else.

Here's my inside view, which I want checked and corrected. I think the right bookkeeping object isn't "how many people chose Good" but the net count D of signals that have actually been *revealed* — signals-for-Good minus signals-for-Bad. While |D| is 0 or 1, each new person's action reveals their signal whatever it is, so D takes a ±1 step. Once two net signals pile up on one side, |D| = 2, and then the public odds are (q/(1−q))², so even someone holding a contrary signal has posterior odds (q/(1−q))² × (1−q)/q = q/(1−q) > 1 — they take the herd's action and reveal nothing. So it's a biased random walk absorbed at ±2: with q = 0.6 the herd is wrong about (1−q)²/((1−q)² + q²) ≈ 0.31 of the time, and a million more people changes nothing, because after absorption nothing further is ever revealed. Is that the right frame, and is the bookkeeping actually right?

Three things I can't get straight:

1. Everyone in the cascade is behaving rationally, and everyone *knows* the cascade might be wrong, yet nobody can profit from acting on their own signal and nobody would learn anything if they did. So each person would prefer the cascade broken and no person can break it. Is that a real collective-action / externality structure, or am I smuggling something in?

2. The frozen public belief looks *more* confident than the private belief of every agent who's sitting on a contrary signal — public odds (q/(1−q))² versus their private q/(1−q). So the crowd ends up more confident than any member of it. Is that a genuine feature or an artifact of my setup?

3. In the model a cascade, once started, never breaks. Real ones obviously do. Which assumption is carrying that — the fixed binary signal strength, the fixed order, the fact that people only see actions and never arguments?

And the thing I actually care about, which I've never seen anyone discuss: suppose everyone also gets to *say* something, a real number, on a channel with no payoff attached. My guess is that if they post their posterior and you naively pool, you double-count all the public information and you've just moved the cascade into belief-space — but if each person posts their likelihood ratio and one aggregator keeps a running log-odds ledger, the whole cascade result just dissolves and the ledger converges on the truth. Does it? And what kills that in practice: incentives to lie, signal correlation nobody knows about, people not knowing their own q?
