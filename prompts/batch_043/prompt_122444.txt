hey — I'm organizing the last session of our "cognition without brains" reading group and I want to open with something that isn't another 40-page paper. Could you write me a piece of satire I can hand out?

Form: an internal document from a university HR / legal-affairs office — a "determination" memo with an attached email thread, maybe 800–900 words total, in deadpan American university-administration prose. Setup: a myrmecology lab has put in a personnel-related request concerning an ant colony of the kind Hofstadter made famous under the name Aunt Hillary — she's apparently been listed somehow as visiting researcher / research subject / colleague, and nobody in the office wants to commit to whether she has a mind, they just need to get her into a category. That's the joke: the file can only process her by either collapsing her into a single individual or dissolving her into a process, and it can do neither.

I'd like the humor to come from getting the mechanisms right, so someone who actually works on social insects (or on this stuff in philosophy of mind) would laugh rather then wince. Things to build it out of:

- no central executive: the queen isn't in charge, nest-site choice comes out of scouts and quorum thresholds, so the field 'reports to / supervisor' can't be filled in
- the speed gap: colony-level "deliberation" runs on the order of hours to days, so every deadline is absurd in both directions — 'please return the signed form within 72 hours' is less time than it takes her to have one thought about the form
- turnover: no individual worker will still be alive at the end of the review period, so routine questions about continuity and identity verification get weird fast
- the selective attribution thing: when the colony does something adaptive the memo calls it deciding or preferring or choosing, and when it does something spectacularly stupid the same memo switches to maintenance-error vocabulary, with nobody noticing. The circular ant mill (the "death spiral" thing, where a lost group of army ants follows each other until they drop) should show up as an "incident" filed in a completely different idiom than the successes.
- the unit-of-selection angle: workers are sterile, nothing below the colony has fitness of its own, so if anything here has interests it's the colony — and then have the memo demand the name of the individual ant responsible for something, which is exactly the one thing that doesn't exist
- self-report: she gets asked to fill out a self-assessment. Either there's a colony-level way of representing the colony (roughly the reason to think there's a mind there at all) or there isn't (no single ant knows anything). Bonus if the form comes back eleven days late with something unsettlingly apt and it's filed under noncompliance.

Constraints: keep it deadpan, no winking, no character turns to the camera, and please don't let anybody deliver a little lecture explaining the philosophy — the reader should be able to tell what's being ironized from how the document behaves. One invented reductionist holdout in another department (give him a name) should file dissents in the margins, and his dissents should stay consistently reductionist — he shouldn't accidentally concede a colony-level mental state halfway through. Target the practice of mind-attribution and institutional individualism, not any real person.
