My friend and I got into the torture vs dust specks case and I think we accidentally proved both answers. Setup: option A is one stranger tortured for fifty years, option B is 3^^^3 strangers each getting a dust speck in the eye, and write "worse than" as >. Here's what we ended up with:

(1) If a harm H is much worse for the person who suffers it than a harm h, then no number of people suffering h adds up to something worse than one person suffering H. Torture is much worse than a speck, so no number of specks is worse than one torture, so B is the better option.
(2) There's a chain of harms running from a dust speck up to fifty years of torture -- h0 = the speck, h1, h2, ... hn = the torture -- where each item is only just barely worse than the one before it, small enough that nobody could tell neigboring items apart.
(3) For neigboring items in that chain, the worse one is not much worse than the milder one. "Much worse" has to leave room for barely worse, otherwise nothing ever counts as barely worse.
(4) So (1) never gets a grip on a neigboring pair: for every i there is some finite number m_i such that m_i people suffering h_i is worse than one person suffering h_{i+1}.
(5) Now substitute up the chain. Replace the one torture with m_{n-1} copies of h_{n-1}, then replace each of those with m_{n-2} copies of h_{n-2}, and so on down to h_0. Each replacement makes the outcome worse while everything else is held fixed, and worse-than is transitive, so the end result -- m_0 x m_1 x ... x m_{n-1} specks -- is worse than one torture. That's a finite number, since it's a finite product of finite numbers. It'll be hugley bigger than 3^^^3, but (1) said *no* number of specks is worse than one torture, so the size shouldn't matter.
(6) So no number of specks is worse than one torture, and some number of specks is worse than one torture.

I'd really like to keep (1) -- it's the only reason I trust the intuitive answer at all -- and (5) looks like nothing but arithmetic plus transitivity. But (2) looks obviously true and (3) looks true by definition, so I dont know what to give up.
