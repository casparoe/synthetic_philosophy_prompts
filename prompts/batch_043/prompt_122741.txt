hey — I'm on this small committee that has to write inclusion criteria for a welfare register: a list of the systems whose experiences, if there are any, an institution has to take into account before it builds, copies, throttles, or switches them off. Wet animals are on it by default. After that it gets murky, and every meeting goes in circles because somebody shows up with a different theory of consciousness and we end up arguing about that instead. So before I read any more of these theories, I want the spec: what does a theory have to deliver to be usable for this?

Not a theory of consciousness in general. Specifically: given some physical system doing something we can describe computationally, which of the computations it can be described as doing carry moral weight, and how much. Write me the requirements list, with the case or consideration behind each requirement, and say which ones are non-negotiable for our purpose versus which are just one camp's preferences dressed up as requirements.

Cases that have actually come up in our meetings, so please make sure your list bites on these:

- an emulation copied exactly and run twice: two patients, or one patient twice over? does the answer change if the copies drift apart after a minute?
- one brain emulated across two datacenters, with the link latency stretched from milliseconds to a month to a year: at what point is it two computations instead of one, and what happens to the moral weight in between?
- the same transition table as a working emulation, but every state change is driven by someone outside turning a crank: is it computing anything, and does it matter who's turning the crank?
- the same emulation's dynamics run in reverse, so the counterfactual structure is intact but the causal arrow points the other way
- a frozen emulation on a disk that is never run but could be, versus one that is run but whose internal states are overwritten immediately and whose outputs are discarded unread
- the nation-of-people-passing-notes setup, but with only two people in it: if a billion is a mind, is two?
- a brain whose neurons are replaced one at a time with chips that reproduce each one's input-output function exactly: I need something said at every step of the swap, or an admission of where the theory goes quiet
- a system that is right on the inside but can't move anything, and its mirror image, a giant lookup table that is behaviorally flawless
- a next-token prediction model that reports pain in every situation we test and whose reports we can't tell apart from ours

Two things I especially want. First, the attribution problem: any old heap of matter can be described as implementing a lot of formal descriptions, so the theory has to say which descriptions are the morally real ones and what makes them real — and it can't just say all of them, or that the answer is whatever we decide to project. Second, tell me if any subset of your own requirements can't be jointly satisfied. That's the part I most want to see, because if e.g. substrate independence, determinate verdicts for every physical system, and no-trivial-realizations can't all hold, then our register needs a tie-breaking rule and I'd rather know now.

Also flag which single requirement knocks out the most popular candidate views. And tell me which distinctions people in this area collapse that they shouldn't: having status versus how much, the computation versus the hardware running it, the system versus the process, whether moral status can even be vague when our register needs a yes or no.

The view I keep half-defending in meetings is that the morally relevant part of a system is whatever causal structure you'd have to preserve to keep its future responses counterfactually robust under perturbation — the would-still-have-flinched test. Put it on your list somewhere and tell me whether it's a genuine desideratum or just functionalism with extra steps.

Keep the weight on the criteria, not on scoring the theories, but a short scorecard at the end for the functionalist-ish family, the biological or naturalistic family, and the integrated-information family is fine, a few lines each. Table is fine. Don't bother naming papers, I want the arguments. And please write it like you're talking to someone who has to defend the criteria to a room of people who disagree, not to a seminar.
