I'm a software engineer working on storage and query infrastructure, with no formal training in physics or philosophy. There's a framing about the thermodynamics of information that has started doing a lot of argumentative work in my team, and I'd like it audited properly before we build anything on it.

The framing is that Maxwell's demon is best understood as a program running on finite memory, and that the second law survives for reasons any engineer would recognise. In the version we pass around: the gas is the input data; the demon is a comparison-and-swap sort over molecular velocities; the record of which molecules were sent where is a heap allocation; and the thermodynamic bill comes due when that allocation is freed. On this picture Landauer's principle is just a statement about the price of clearing memory — resetting one bit against a bath at temperature T costs at least kT ln 2 in dissipated work — and the demon is defeated the way a leaky program is: it cannot free its measurement record without paying, and the payment exactly cancels what it extracted from the gas.

Three conclusions get drawn from it, and I want to know which of them the analogy can actually carry.

(a) Observation is free in principle and only forgetting costs. So a system that takes in information and never discards it — a model, a sensor, a control loop — incurs no fundamental thermodynamic cost for the information it acquires.

(b) The Landauer wall on computation is an engineering artifact of building machines out of irreversible gates. If we uncompute instead of erase — keep the garbage, run the tape backwards — the bound does not apply, and the energy cost of datacentre-scale computation is not a law of nature.

(c) A dodge I proposed myself: don't erase at room temperature at all. Move the garbage bits into a memory reservoir held at a few kelvin and reset them there, paying k T_c ln 2 per bit instead of k T_h ln 2. When I priced in the refrigerator needed to hold that reservoir cold, using the Carnot limit for pumping the resulting heat back up to T_h, the saving came out to exactly zero: k T_c ln 2 for the reset plus k (T_h − T_c) ln 2 of fridge work sums to k T_h ln 2. I don't trust an exact cancellation like that. It usually means either that I've derived a conservation law or that I've double-counted. Which is it here, and does the same accounting sink the other dodges in (b)?

A physicist friend pushed back on the framing and I couldn't tell how much of it was pedantry. As I understood her: the one-bit-is-kT-ln-2 identity is not a general equivalence between Shannon entropy and thermodynamic entropy, but holds for a memory whose two logical states are degenerate, so mapping bits onto molecules of an ideal gas is doing hidden work; logically reversible and thermodynamically reversible are different properties, so (b) conflates an architectural choice with a physical one; uncomputing converts an erasure bill into a memory bill, and a supply of blank bits is itself a thermodynamic resource rather than a free given; and the costs of error correction, of moving bits across a thermal gradient, and of interfacing with a noisy environment at all are not removable by making the logic reversible. She also said there is a live disagreement about whether the erasure story is what saves the second law in the first place — some accounts put the cost on the acquisition of information, others claim neither is needed — which, if true, means we've been leaning on a contested account as though it were settled.

So: give me the mapping explicitly — gas, demon, measurement, memory, erasure, heat bath, reversible logic — with what each corresponds to and where the correspondence imports something the target domain doesn't have. Then separate the disanalogies that matter for (a), (b) and (c) from the ones that don't, and tell me which conclusion survives, which was being carried entirely by the analogy, and whether there's a better analogy for whatever does survive.

One more thing that may help localise the problem. I wrote a small hard-rod simulation in which a demon running an in-place bitonic sort over velocity signs appears to extract work indefinitely while erasing nothing. I assume this is an artifact and not a discovery, but I'd like to know exactly which feature of real thermodynamics it is missing. My guess is that my simulation runs at effectively zero temperature, with no fluctuations and no bath, so nothing it records counts as physical information at all — but I'd like that confirmed or corrected, and I'd like to know what would have to be added for the bookkeeping to close.
