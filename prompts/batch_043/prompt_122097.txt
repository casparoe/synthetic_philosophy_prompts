My Berkeley reading group painted itself into a corner and I want someone to tell me which step to walk back.

(1) The master argument: try to conceive of a tree existing unconceived. The moment you try you have to think of the tree, so you've conceived it. The task can't be carried out — nobody can frame an idea of an unconceived existent.
(2) Generalize the mechanism: if the only way to carry out the supposition that p is to make p false, then p is impossible. That's the conceivability test doing the real work.
(3) Run the same test on other minds: try to conceive of another spirit's ideas existing while I perceive nothing. Any idea I frame is, by Berkeley's own lights, an idea in my own mind, so I've made it perceived by me. So by (2), other minds' unperceived ideas are impossible.
(4) Run it on God: Berkeley's fix for the tree between my visits to the park is that God perceives it. But he also holds that we have no idea of spirits at all, only a notion. So trying to frame the idea of the tree's being perceived by God is something I can't do either — and by (2) that's not just my failing, it's impossible.
(5) So (1) through (2) land me in solipsism and with no fact of the matter about the tree when nobody's in the park, which is more than immaterialism and more than Berkeley can accept.
(6) The obvious patch is to restrict the argument to ideas and exempt spirits, on the grounds that ideas are passive and spirits active. But nothing in (1) or (2) mentions activity or passivity, so the restriction looks like special pleading unless something else is doing the work.
(7) So either the master argument is unsound, or it proves far too much.

I'd rather hold onto (2) then give up the master argument entirely — I like the thought that conceivability constrains possibility — but I'm not sure its coherent to keep it and still have other people. Which step do you actually reject, why did it look fine, and what does rejecting it cost you elsewhere? Commit to one diagnosis rather than handing me a menu. And tell me how confident you are in it and what would change your mind, especially if you think the passive/active distinction can be made to carry real weight here.
