I'm giving a 20-minute talk at a joint cog-sci/NLP colloquium next week and I badly need Q&A prep, because I know exactly how this room goes.

Thesis: a text-only language model isn't obviously ungrounded, because grounding is a property of the causal chain running world → speakers → corpus → model → readers who can act on the outputs, not a property of the architecture. The humans in the loop are the transducers. My main intuition pump is what I call the parasitic-twin test: picture two models trained on corpora that are distributionally indistinguishible — same vocabulary, same embedding geometry, same everything — except one corpus was written by people describing the actual world and the other was generated by a process with no causal contact with that world (say, inhabitants of a simulation describing a different physics). If people's intuition is that the twin's symbols are ungrounded while the real one's aren't, then whatever is doing the work is the chain, not the text statistics, and a model trained on the internet has that same thing, just degraded and second-hand and a matter of degree. Second plank: at deployment the model sits in a feedback loop with readers who check its outputs against the world, and I want to push on why a closed loop should count but bandwidth and sensorimotor contact shouldn't.

The room: about half NLP people who think this debate died with word embeddings and multimodal models and would much rather talk about evals; two grounded/embodied cognition folks (perceptual symbol systems, vaguely enactivist) for whom you need a body; one roboticist who'll say grounding is closed-loop real-time interaction with an environment or it's nothing; and one philosopher of mind who will just rerun the Chinese Room at me.
