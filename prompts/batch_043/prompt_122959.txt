Been going back and forth on blame for ages and I want someone to check whether my actual package hangs together before I try to teach it. Where I've landed:

(a) whether someone deserves blame depends only on them — what they did, their reasons, what they were capable of — and nothing about the person doing the blaming makes the target any more or less blameworthy;
(b) even so, a hypocritical blamer lacks standing: their blame is out of order and they should keep it to themselves, even when the target obviously deserves blame;
(c) what does the forfeiting is the blamer having done a relevantly similar wrong themselves and not owned up to it;
(d) standing isn't scalar — you either have it or you don't, and it doesn't get discounted just because the blamer's own version was much smaller than the target's;
(e) you get it back by acknowledging it and genuinely changing, so the forfeiture isn't permanent;
(f) none of this is special to blame between people who know each other — an uninvolved third party whose done the same thing and hasn't owned up to it lacks standing too;
(g) but the person who's never done anything remotely like it, who blames only when it costs them nothing and goes quiet the moment the norm is inconvenient, has full standing — clean hands is what matters;
(h) and I want to hang onto the thought that a lot of ordinary public criticism — random people calling out powerful people who do bad things — is perfectly appropriate and is one of the few accountability mechanisms that actually does any work.

So: which of these really collide and which only look like they do? I half suspect (a) and (b) are fine together and the appearance of conflict is an illusion, but I can't tell where the real problem is. Rank whatever genuine tensions you find by how bad they are, say for each one what would have to give and what that costs me, and name the single revision that cleans up the most. Also: seperate what's actually settled among people who work on this from what's a live dispute — don't smooth a contested view into orthodoxy for me.
