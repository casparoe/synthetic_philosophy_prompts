I am the student member of the Activities Funding Board at my high school, the committee that allocates the student activities fee each spring. On Thursday we vote on a single item, and I need to circulate a written opinion beforehand. Please draft it as a memo of no more than 500 words, with the recommendation stated in the first two sentences. I would rather have a short memo that commits to something than a careful survey of the options.

The item: three years ago the Board funded a peer-tutoring matching platform that a team of students built and that the district IT office now hosts. About 21,000 dollars has gone into it, mostly a small annual stipend for a student maintainer. Matching still gets done by hand roughly half the time, because the scheduling logic never worked well. The alternatives our faculty advisor priced out: license a commercial scheduler for about 9,000 dollars a year, or migrate to a free open-source tool, which would take roughly forty hours of work over the summer and would lose the two years of session records currently in our system. The 7,000 dollars the team is requesting for next year is unspent and could instead fund roughly a dozen small club requests that we turned down last cycle.

Our precedents pull in both directions. We kept funding the student film festival through three years of thin attendance, and it is now the best-attended event of the year. Two years ago we killed a student-built newspaper app after one year, and the students who built it said afterward that the process taught them more than the product ever would have. Most of us now think both decisions were right, which makes it hard to say what rule we were following.

Two arguments have been made to me that the standard sunk cost line does not seem to dispose of, and I want the memo to engage them rather than restate that past spending is irrelevant.

First: every year's Board is a new set of people who bear the cost of continuing while the students who benefit are two or three years away and have no vote. If each Board is free to treat prior investment as nothing, then no multi-year project can survive one bad year, and any sensible team will propose only what pays off inside a single school year. On this view, treating past spending as a reason to continue is not an error about costs; it is the only commitment technology a body with annual turnover has, doing the work a contract would do if we could sign one.

Second: for the two seniors who have maintained the platform for three years, being told that the past does not count sounds like being told to regard their own earlier effort as a stranger's. Their work has made them the people who do this, and what they would lose is not only time or money.

The memo should recommend one course of action with a time frame and a checkable condition for revisiting it, give the reasons, state the strongest argument for the opposite decision in a form its defenders would recognize, and close with one sentence naming the rule the Board would be adopting for future multi-year projects.
