So I'm a programming languages person — I spend my days in the guts of a proof assistant, mostly elaboration and termination/productivity checking — and I got dragged into an argument about the regress problem at a meetup last week that I haven't been able to shake. Here's how I'd map it: foundationalism is axioms plus a trusted kernel — the regress stops in things that aren't derived at all, and, notably, in things that mostly aren't even expressible in the object language (definitional equality, the reduction rule, the kernel's own trusted code, the FFI boundary), which feels structurally like the foundationalist's point that the terminators are non-doxastic, states of the world or of experience rather than further beliefs. Coherentism is mutual recursion: no base case, a cyclic term graph where every node is supported by the ones around it, and the standard objection that it can't account for the contribution of experience looks to me like the observation that a closed, total, effect-free term has no way to touch the environment at all — it can only ever reshuffle what it already has. Infinitism is the interesting one: it wants an infinite chain that a finite mind can nonetheless *have*, which smells exactly like a non-well-founded but finitely representable derivation — a corecursively generated stream of reasons where you never materialize the whole thing, only the generator plus a guard/productivity condition guaranteeing you can always emit the next one on demand.
