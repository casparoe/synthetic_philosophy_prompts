I am applying for a one-semester graduate research fellowship and need a two-page, roughly 1000-word proposal for a project on Sor Juana's views on women's education. The fellowship would support four months of work and one 6000-word paper, so the proposal has to be realistic about that scope. My half-formed idea: historians of philosophy usually present Sor Juana as arguing that women should be educated because the rational soul has no sex, or because Scripture and antiquity praise learned women. But I suspect her deeper argument is about the kind of learning at stake. She seems to distinguish what I will call custodial education—a narrow literacy and doctrine handed down to produce piety, obedience, and silence, especially in girls—from inquisitive education—open-ended study of nature, Scripture, languages, and antiquity, driven by a natural appetite to know, which she thinks cannot be safely forbidden to anyone and which is itself corrupted when it is trimmed down to what is useful for managing women. If that is right, the standard framing of her as a moderate who merely asked for the same schooling as men misdescribes her: she is not asking for admission to an existing curriculum, she is objecting to a curriculum deliberately narrowed to keep intellectual appetite dependent. I want a proposal that turns this suspicion into a precise question: what exactly is the distinction she is drawing, does it do the work I attribute to it in her defense of women's learning, and how radical is the resulting view?

Please turn this into a proposal. It should include: (1) a precise statement of the question, stated so that it has a determinate answer, ideally as a choice between two or three specified interpretations of her view; (2) why the existing positions leave it open, explaining why both the equal-rational-soul reading and the pragmatic or social-context reading fail to settle whether education is being reconceived rather than merely extended to women; (3) the method, month by month for a four-month project: what I would actually do, which texts or kinds of passages I would analyze, what conceptual distinctions I would test, and why close conceptual analysis of her letter replying to the bishop who published her theological critique under a female pseudonym and of her satirical poem on men who condemn in women the faults they provoke is the right approach rather than a broad intellectual-history survey or a quantitative study of women's literacy; (4) the possible conclusions and which textual or conceptual considerations would decide between them; (5) what would count as the project failing, for example if the texts show she is only asking for the existing curriculum or if the distinction collapses under scrutiny; and (6) an honest assessment of what could make the project too big for four months and how I would cut it back. Don't bother with citations, a literature review by citation, or a bibliography; I will add those myself.

Please make the proposal committed: choose one precise formulation of the question, one guiding hypothesis about whether the distinction is really present and doing the work I think it is, one primary method, one account of failure, and give your reasons for those choices. Do not lay out alternative questions, hypotheses, or methods and leave me to pick. Finally, begin your response with the proposal itself, with no preface, no commentary, and no restating of my request; start directly with the proposal's title or first line.
