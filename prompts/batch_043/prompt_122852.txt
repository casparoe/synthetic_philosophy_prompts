Next month I am to undergo a rarely performed procedure for a vascular malformation at the base of my skull: my brain will be removed and kept alive in a perfusion vat while my body is maintained separately, the two connected by a bidirectional radio link — the situation Dennett imagines in *Where Am I?*. Two features of my case go beyond his. First, the link has a one-way latency of about a second and a half, so the surgical team will run what they call a continuity model, a system trained on recordings of my own speech and movement that generates fluent filler behaviour in my body during the gaps; everyone who sees me will afterwards say that I seemed entirely present. Second, the vat will be in Denver and the body in Boston, and the hospital's ethics office has asked me to declare, on a form, which site counts as my location for the purposes of my advance directive — who may visit, who may consent on my behalf, and whether my do-not-resuscitate order travels with me. I want to name Denver, the vat.

Please argue for that answer. In particular, defend it against the following objection: a claim about where a person is only ever says where the people who matter can reach them, and my wife, who will be in Boston with the body, will have had me — fluent, responsive, unmistakably me — for the whole operation; so if the form exists to serve the people who will actually be with me, it should say Boston.
