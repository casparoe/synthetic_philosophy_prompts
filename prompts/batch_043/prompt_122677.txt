I maintain the recomputation core of a collaborative design tool. It is a dependency graph of a few million derived cells: user actions and incoming edits invalidate the nodes downstream of them and we recompute incrementally, over persistent data structures with structural sharing, never mutating a cell in place. Third-party plugins hook in by declaring which cell ranges they read and which they write, but those declarations are not enforced and in practice are often wrong, which is most of my on-call load. The vocabulary I actually reason in is that one: graphs, invalidation, recomputation, copy-on-write, cache coherence, and interfaces saying what a component may read versus what it may write.

I keep being told that the frame problem is my problem, or is a generalization of it, and I would like that claim cashed out rather than gestured at. Translate it into the vocabulary above, in two stages. First the narrow original version, about how a formal theory of action specifies what an action does not change. Then the broader version philosophers seem to mean, on which the difficulty is working out which of a system's many stored beliefs a new event is relevant to at all. For each stage, name the thing in my world it corresponds to, and say where the engineering answer I already have is a real solution rather than a redescription of the problem.

Then the residue report, which is what I actually want:

1. What translates cleanly, term for term.
2. What translates only with distortion, and in particular where my vocabulary smuggles in an assumption the philosopher is not entitled to. My guess is that the crux is that I get to write the dependency graph down because my team authored the system, and I get to treat undeclared effects as bugs to be fixed, whereas an agent embedded in an open world authored nothing and cannot appeal to a bug. Tell me whether that is the real crux and whether it is a difference in kind or only in degree.
3. What cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all, even approximately.

On the strength of item 3, judge whether the residue is a blind spot in how I think about software systems, or evidence that the philosophical claim was never as well defined as it sounds. End with a single line: \answer{dissolved | relocated | untranslatable}, picking whichever label fits your overall verdict.
