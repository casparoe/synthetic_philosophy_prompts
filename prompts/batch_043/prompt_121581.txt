Our reading group has spent a year on the Epicurean argument that death is nothing to us, and it has not moved an inch. Both sides grant that a corpse is not the subject of any experience, and both sides grant that being killed at thirty can be a great misfortune; after that, the deprivationists redescribe the second datum in terms of what the person would otherwise have had, their critics redescribe it as a loss to the living or as badness simpliciter, and nothing further happens. I want a diagnosis of the impasse rather than another round of it.

My hypothesis is that the sentence death is bad for the one who dies is quietly doing three separable jobs at once: asking whether some attitude toward one's own death is fitting; asking whether killing someone wrongs or sets back that person in particular, rather than being a bad event that merely involves them; and asking whether a fact about a person's good can obtain at a time when there is no person to have it. Tell me whether the standoff is verbal, a clash of intuitions neither side can argue for, or a hidden disagreement in the metaphysics of well-being — specifically about whether facts about someone's good require a time at which they are had, and about whether the relation expressed by bad for is causal or merely normative.

Two constraints on the answer. First, do not simply run through the stock objections — the timing problem, the difficulty of locating a harm that occurs after the subject has ceased to exist, and so on — except where they do real work in the diagnosis; I want the structure of the disagreement, not a catalogue of moves. Second, please work with the case my group keeps skipping: a choice made decades before a particular person exists, which foreseeably shortens the life she will in fact have, so that the deprivation falls entirely at a time before there is anyone to be deprived. Does counting that as a harm to her reveal a commitment to a timeless notion of a person's good, and is anyone who refuses that commitment left unable to say that death harms at all? Given your diagnosis, say what would count as progress here — a distinction, a case that splits the three questions apart, a change of subject — or argue that we should expect none, and say why.
