Longtime lurker, been reading this corner of the internet since the Sequences were still being written, and I've got a thing I keep noticing that I can't find a word for. Hoping somebody can tell me whether it already has a name.

The general shape is a positional competition where everybody escalates and the escalation is pure waste in aggregate and everyone involved knows it — college admissions is the case I know best, since my youngest starts applying next fall and my brother-in-law teaches at one of those suburban high schools where the whole senior year is basically an arms race. Fine: that part is the boring familiar thing, I've read the Moloch essay same as everybody, and multipolar trap / tragedy of the commons / positional arms race all gesture at it. But two features keep showing up in the cases I actually watch, and I don't think those labels get them, and it's those I want the name for. One: the competition doesn't just waste effort, it degrades the thing being competed over — and the degradation makes the competition worse instead of self-limiting. As the transcripts inflate, they get noisier as evidence of anything, so admissions leans harder on the few things they still trust, which are the most coachable and most purchasable items on the file, so the coaching money flows there, so those get noisier too. The signal doesn't fade out, it collapses onto narrower and easier to game criteria, and the race speeds up. Two: the people who drop out of the race are disproportionately the ones who never wanted to be in it, and once they're gone the remaining field is more committed to escalating, and the median participant is now somebody who likes the race. I see the same skeleton in hiring where I work: as portfolios and take-homes get AI-polished into uselessness, the loop closes harder onto brand-name employers and referrals, which are the most gameable things left.

Which is why I don't think the usual words are quite it. People throw Goodhart around for anything, but as I understand it that's a measure ceasing to track the target; here the measure's failure is what feeds the process that broke it, which feels like the opposite direction. And the everybody loses framing isn't right either — there's a concentrated winner, the test prep companies, the credentialing vendors, whoever owns the ranking, and they're organised and they defend the arrangement, while the losers are diffuse. So: is there an established concept for the collapse-onto-narrower-criteria part, or for the dropout-makes-the-trap-tighter part, or for both together as one animal? I don't want a word slapped on it. Tell me what the real neighbouring concepts are — I assume I'm circling costly signalling, positional goods, maybe adverse selection, maybe some kind of ratchet — where my description fits each one and where it doesn't, and which fits best. And if there genuinely isn't a name, then coin one, define it tightly enough that I can separate it from its neighbours, and give me the two boundary cases: something that superficially looks like this but shouldn't count, and something that really is this that a sloppy definition would miss.
