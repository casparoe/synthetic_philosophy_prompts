I work as a quantitative ecologist, and most of my day is spent with capture–recapture estimators, size-biased and length-biased sampling, Horvitz–Thompson inverse-probability weights, and Bayesian model comparison across a handful of competing population models. I am fluent in likelihoods, sampling frames, identifiability, weakly informative versus improper priors, prior sensitivity, the inspection paradox, and the difference between design-based and model-based inference. My exposure to anthropics consists of a couple of secondary summaries, so please do not assume I know that literature's own dialect.

Here is the task. Take the dispute between the self-sampling assumption and the self-indication assumption — the dispute that gives rise to the presumptuous philosopher, where an armchair argument appears to settle how large the cosmos is — and restate it as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary. Two constraints, because I want the translation to earn its keep rather than reproduce what I have already read:

First, do not hand me SSA and SIA as two brute alternatives. I have been playing with the continuous version, in which the weight a hypothesis receives from the datum that I exist goes as N to the power α, with α = 0 recovering SSA and α = 1 recovering SIA. Write that weight function (or the corresponding likelihood) explicitly in my terms, and tell me what α is doing. In my work an exponent like that is a stipulated feature of the sampling design, so I want to know whether it is playing the role of a design parameter, of a prior, or of something for which my framework has no slot.

Second, the translation has to handle a wrinkle I have been chewing on. Standard treatments take the datum to be simply that I exist, or that I exist as an observer with such-and-such evidence. But my actual datum is indexical in a further way: I exist, and I find myself right now occupying an observer-moment of the rather unusual kind that worries about sampling assumptions. Let β be the elasticity of the number of observer-moments of this reflective kind with respect to total population size — the thought being that a world with a hundred times as many observers need not contain a hundred times as many observer-moments spent doing anthropics, since the fraction of observer-moments that are reflectively occupied may itself fall as a population grows. Fold β into the weighting and tell me what it does to the presumptuous philosopher's argument. If the honest answer is that β is just one more free parameter that swamps everything, say so and say why.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about: what in the dispute maps cleanly onto tools I already have; what translates only under a distortion I should be suspicious of (I suspect the reference class is one of these — it looks like a sampling frame, but in my work the frame is fixed by the designer, whereas here it appears to be up for grabs, which makes me think the analogy is concealing something); and what the philosophers are arguing about that has no counterpart at all in a framework where the sampling protocol is always given in advance. Where there is residue, say whether it indicts my vocabulary or the philosophical question.

Please answer as exactly five numbered items, each two to three sentences long, with no preamble and no closing summary. Suggested division: item 1, the explicit translation of SSA; item 2, of SIA, including where α and β enter; item 3, what translates only with distortion; item 4, what has no counterpart in my vocabulary; item 5, your verdict on whether the residue is a defect of the framework or a sign that the original question is less well posed than it looks.
