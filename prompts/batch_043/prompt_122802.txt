I've ended up beleiving that narrative explanation in history does real explanatory work of its own: when a historian explains some outcome by telling a story in which A leads to B leads to C, the explaining isn't a lazy placeholder for a covering-law explanation we just haven't filled in yet (Hempel-style), it comes from the way the events get made to hang together as a sequence — you come to see the outcome as followable from that order of things, and the shape of the telling is part of what does the work, not decoration on top of it.

So: what would count against this?

The thing I'd personally find most damning is what I think of as the scrambled-annals test. Take a narrative, reduce it to its bare dated events in random order, hand that plus ordinary background knowledge to competent historians, and see whether they recover the same explanatory verdicts — this is what explains that, this link is load-bearing, you can drop that bit and nothing changes. If scrambling costs nothing, the configuration isn't earning its keep and I should give up. But what else should I be watching for, and how would you rank it — fatal vs. merely awkward? I especially want the defeaters that someone who actually holds the view would concede as counting against them, not just the ones critics like to throw. Things like: whether the standards historians really use to accept or reject a narrative turn out to be covertly causal-modeling standards under another name; whether there's any case of two narratives that fit every dated fact equally well and differ only in "shape," where historians have no reliable way to choose; whether the view can say anything about how much you can damage the middle of a story before it stops explaining at all.

And one more: is the standard hedge — of course the background causal claims do the heavy lifting, the narrative just organises them — a retreat that empties the position, or was it what the position meant all along? I can't tell wether I'm looking at a real thesis or at something that's been qualified into safety.
