I got into an argument with my flying buddy last weekend and it's turned into one of those things I can't let go of, so I went looking for something sharper than vibes, and found this in a pop-philosophy essay a coworker sent me (it's one of those "skepticism is bad for you" takes):

"Nozick's tracking theory says that knowledge is justified true belief plus two extra tracking conditions. Your belief has to track the facts: in the nearby possible worlds where the proposition is false, you don't believe it — and, redundantly, in the nearby worlds where it's true, you do. Put another way, a belief is knowledge only if it's true in every nearby world where you'd have formed it. So a true belief isn't knowledge if it's just an accident that it lines up with reality, and this is why tracking theory handles the Gettier cases that had the justified-true-belief account tied in knots: there, the belief is true and justified but would have been exactly the same if it had been false. The payoff is skepticism. Nozick shows that we really do know we aren't brains in a vat, because the vat scenario is so remote that it isn't a nearby world at all — the skeptic's possibilities are just too far away to matter. The catch, and it's a fatal one: tracking theory lets you know the animal in the pen is a zebra, know that a zebra isn't a cleverly painted mule, and still not know that it isn't a cleverly painted mule. That's flatly contradictory, and since nobody really disputes that knowledge is closed under known implication, the whole theory was abandoned within about a decade."

Here's why I'm suspicious, though maybe for dumb reasons. I vaguely remember someone telling me that Nozick actually concedes the brain-in-a-vat thing to the skeptic and blocks the argument somewhere else, which would make that sentence backwards. And I don't see why the second condition would be redundant when the first one only talks about worlds where the proposition is false. But I also don't trust myself at all here — I fly a rented 172 on weekends, I'm not a philosopher, and I can't get hold of Nozick's book right now, so please don't just tell me to go read it.
