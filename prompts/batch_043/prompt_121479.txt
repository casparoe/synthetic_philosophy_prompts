I've been going back and forth with a friend about the knowledge argument, and I want to find out where our disagreement actually lives rather than re-run the dialectic. So please skip the tour of the standard positions: assume I have read them and can recite them. What I want is a number, and the decomposition that produces it.

The version I care about is not the textbook one, because I think the textbook version lets each side win too cheaply. Consider instead a Mary who is a congenital protanope: she lacks functioning L-cones, but she has normal vision along the blue–yellow axis, she has seen green and yellow and blue her whole life, and she knows the complete physical truth about human color vision — the optics, the cone sensitivities, opponent-processing in the retina and in V4, the psychophysics, and the complete microphysical state of her own brain. Importantly, she is not experientially naive: she already knows what it is like for a color to look some way to her. At t1 a gene therapy restores L-cone function, and she looks at a ripe tomato. I like this case because it strips the argument down: whatever she gains is not acquaintance with sensory experience in general, and not the concept of a qualitative character, but the specific character of red — and red is the opponent of green, a quality she was already on intimate terms with.

The proposition I want your credence on: at t1, Mary comes to know at least one fact that is not entailed by the complete physical truth about the world together with all a priori reasoning, where entailment is understood strictly and indexical or Twin-Earth style maneuvers are allowed. My own credence is around 0.6. My friend's is around 0.2, and I believe she is a phenomenal-concept-strategy person.

Please decompose. The terms I would want to see, with a rough number and one line of justification each, are at least: whether there is a determinate fact about the specific character of Mary's post-t1 red experience over and above its functional, relational, and representational role; whether knowing what it is like is factive at all, as opposed to an ability or an acquaintance relation; whether the fact she gains was genuinely unavailable to her before t1 — this is where the protanope variant bites, since she already knew what green looks like and knew the opponent structure, so there may be a route of interpolation or construction from what she had to the specific quality, and I want to know whether such a route would defuse the argument or merely relocate it; and conditional on her gaining a novel phenomenal-ish fact, how much room the deflationary strategies still have to block the failure of entailment. Add or replace terms as you think fit, but give me a number for each and say how they combine into your headline figure.

Two more things. First, a diagnostic I have found myself using: suppose her L-cone function is switched off again at t2, and months later, after her ability to conjure a red image in imagination has degraded to the point where she reports that she can no longer summon it, she still says that she knows what red looked like. Does that tell us anything about whether what she gained was a fact or an ability, and if you think it does not, say why not. Second, an outside-view check: given how much of anyone's number on this question is inherited from a general prior about consciousness rather than from anything specific to the argument, how much should I discount my own 0.6, and how much should my friend discount her 0.2?
