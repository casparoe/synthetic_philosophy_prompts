I serve on the allocation committee of the Bracknell Trust, a small health-focused foundation that is spending down: our governing instrument requires the remaining capital to be committed within three grant cycles, after which the Trust closes. This cycle we have USD 4.6 million to place. Three proposals survived staff review, and we cannot fund all three at full scope, so the decision is real.

Our standing method is a multiplicative importance / tractability / neglectedness score. In our scorecard, importance is burden per unit of problem (DALYs), tractability is the share of the problem averted per million dollars in that setting, and neglectedness is one over total annual philanthropic and bilateral spend in the cause, taken at whatever level of description staff happened to use in the proponent's write-up.

Proposal A: school-based treatment for soil-transmitted helminths plus micronutrient supplementation in two districts of a low-income country. USD 1.9 million over three years. Evidence base is strong and the delivery platform is proven; cost per child treated is roughly a dollar. Two bilateral funders and one multilateral are active in the same districts, and staff estimate about three million dollars of room for more funding before the platform saturates. One of the bilaterals has announced it will exit the country within eighteen months, and the ministry has no budget line to absorb the shortfall; if it leaves, staff think remaining room for more funding roughly doubles.

Proposal B: a pilot to remove lead from paint sold in a middle-income country's domestic market, paired with enforcement capacity. USD 2.2 million over three years. Burden is large, the causal chain from regulation to measured blood-lead reduction is long, and the evidence for enforcement actually biting is thin; there is political risk. No other funder is currently in lead work in that country. Separately, a large private foundation has publicly pledged to match new commitments in this area one-for-one, up to 1.5 million dollars in total, but only for commitments signed before a deadline five months from now, and nothing thereafter.

Proposal C: surveillance, clinical guidance, and a small treatment stockpile for a poorly understood chronic kidney disease affecting young agricultural workers in one region. USD 1.4 million over three years. Almost nothing is spent on it globally, the aetiology is unsettled, and part of the grant is research that would change what later funders could do. Our own regional program officer has nine years of relationships with the regional health authority and two growers' associations; her secondment ends in fourteen months, and that relationship capital leaves with her.

Two further complications. First, two committee members want the scorecard amended before we vote: one to replace neglectedness with a counterfactual-displacement measure (the share of resulting activity that would not have occurred but for us), and one to force every cause onto a fixed twelve-area taxonomy so that staff cannot sharpen a description until the denominator looks small — under that taxonomy, at least two of these proposals collapse into the same cause area. Second, our treasurer wants up to one million dollars held back unallocated, on the ground that a large funder is expected to open a co-funding window in about fourteen months.

Please draft the decision memo I will circulate, addressed to the committee and no longer than about a thousand words. It should: open with a specific recommended allocation, including tranche structure or conditions and whether any reserve is justified; state what weight the ITN score should carry in the final decision and why; say whether we should adopt either amendment now or decide this case first and revise the framework afterward; and include a short section giving the strongest version of the case for the option you are not recommending, of the kind its advocate would recognize. The committee is split and sophisticated, so the reasoning has to be usable by people who disagree with the conclusion, not merely a restatement of the score.
