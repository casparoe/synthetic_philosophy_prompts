i'm doing my seminar presentation for the assertion module and i need a case built to a spec, because every time i try to make one myself it collapses into a Gettier setup or the chicken-sexer case and my tutor says that isnt doing any work.

four candidate norms for flat-out, unhedged assertion:
- K: assert p only if you know p
- T: assert p only if p is true
- B: assert p only if you believe p
- R: assert p only if you have adequate evidence for p that you could actually give if asked

what I need is a case where K says permissible, T and B also say permissible, but R says impermissible — and where the intuitively right thing for the speaker to do is to assert flat-out anyway. so the knowledge norm is the permissive one for once and the reasons norm is the fussy one. i'm arguing that K handles unreflective expertise better than R does and this is the case i want to hang that on.

constraints on the scenario:
- realistic and contemporary, one paragraph, no sci fi / clairvoyance / anything supernatural, and please not a hospital or a courtroom — everyone uses those. a kitchen, a workshop, a machine shop, a mixing desk, a field site, a sorting table, whatever.
- she has to genuinely know. reliable, well calibrated, hard-won perceptual or practical skill, true belief, safe enough. im granting a broadly reliabilist / skill-and-virtue account of knowledge on which unreflective expertise counts as knowledge, so "she doesnt really know" is not an available move.
- but zero reflective access: if you asked her how she knows, she'd either shrug or produce a reason that isn't the one she's actually going on — confabulate, not lie.
- no escape via hedging, this is the bit that matters. in the case, saying "i have a hunch, not sure why" or "i might be wrong but —" has to be about as useless as saying nothing, given how the audience will recieve it. so R cant wiggle out by prescribing a hedged version instead.
- the cost of following R has to be real: silence or hedging should lose something an ordinary person would care about, not just a bit of pedantic tidiness. otherwise my tutor will say R's verdict is fine and K has no advantage here.
- any impropriety has to be purely epistemic. not rudeness, not overstepping her role, not arrogance.

after the scenario give me:
1. a short paragraph per norm: what it says about the case and why it says that, derived from the norm rather than just announced
2. which single feature is load-bearing, and what happens if you remove it — both directions: take out the reliability, take out the inarticulacy
3. the strongest available reply from someone who holds R. i want the real version, not a strawman. i'm expecting something like: fine, she may say it, but she isn't entitled to say it flat-out and expect to be believed; or: knowledge comes in grades, and flat assertion needs the reflective grade, not just the reliable one. if you can see a reply to that, give it.
4. an honest flag anywhere you had to stipulate a verdict instead of getting it out of the norm, since that's exactly where my tutor will go for me.
