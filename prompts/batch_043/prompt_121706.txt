Hi. I'm on the governance working group for a mid-sized open source project (data infra, ~30 people with commit rights). I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy or law background, and I've been asked to draft the memo for a decision we have to make this week. I'd like you to write it. Sorry if this is long, I think the details matter.

Background: about 14 months ago our founder stepped back and we replaced "founder decides" with a written charter. The relevant bits:

- Section 2 defines who votes: an "active committer" is someone with at least one commit merged in the last 180 days AND at least 3 pull request reviews in the last 90 days. Right now that's 29 people.
- Section 7: ordinary amendments need 2/3 of active committers. Any amendment that changes section 7 itself needs 3/4. There's also a sentence added last year saying no amendment may lower any voting threshold below the threshold currently in force — thresholds only ratchet up.
- Section 11: the whole charter has to be reaffirmed by a simple majority every 24 months or it lapses, and if it lapses a five-person interim council named by the former founder runs everything for 90 days. Our window closes in six weeks.

The case: a group of long-time contributors who've mostly drifted out of day-to-day work filed what we're calling Amendment 41. It does not touch section 7 at all. It does two things:

(a) It rewrites section 2 so the review requirement becomes 1 review in the last 365 days, and adds that anyone who has ever been a committer stays on the roll unless they opt out in writing. We ran the numbers: the electorate goes from 29 to something like 90, and roughly two thirds of the new people haven't touched the codebase in over a year.

(b) It includes a "construction and validation" clause: if any part of this amendment is later held to have needed a section 7 supermajority, then this amendment is deemed also to have amended section 7 to whatever minimum extent was necessary for it to be valid, and the rest of the amendment stays in force.

Nobody on the call was fooled about what's going on — the people pushing it are friendly with the dormant ex-committers, and once the roll is 90 instead of 29 the 3/4 threshold is a completely different animal. But we could not agree on what to do about it, or on what principle we were even arguing about. The only useful thing anyone said was that it's "a schema migration that also rewrites the migration tool."

Two of us think it's obvious: this is an amendment-rule change in substance, so it needs 3/4 of the current roll, and the validation sentence has to come out because if that's allowed then section 7's 3/4 is decorative — every future amendment just includes the same sentence and pre-authorizes itself. Two others say: if that's right, then entrenchment is cosplay, because any group can always get at the thresholds by editing the definitions the thresholds are written in terms of, so we should stop pretending, repeal the second sentence of section 7, and rely on the 24-month reaffirmation plus norms. One person says the validation clause is completely normal and we use severability language like that in our licenses all the time.

What I need is a memo I can circulate to the list before Friday that recommends something we can actually put to a vote — and that says what threshold that vote is taken under, since that's disputed too (yes, I see the joke). Concretely:

- A bottom line with a date on it: reject, accept, split it into parts, or defer past the section 11 deadline — pick one.
- A rule or test for telling a "definitions change" from an "amendment-rule change" that we could apply again next time, not just a verdict on this one. The thresholds are written in terms of "active committers," and anyone who can rewrite that term can get at the thresholds without touching section 7, so I need something that handles that and not just a slogan about substance over form.
- Treat the retroactive self-authorizing sentence on its own, and say why it is or isn't the same thing as ordinary severability language.
- Engage honestly with the "entrenchment is cosplay" position. Two people I respect hold it and I think it's the strongest thing on the other side, so I don't want it waved away.
- A short "what we are not deciding" section, because this thread will otherwise eat the entire charter.
- Flag anywhere your recommendation depends on guessing how the vote actually goes.

Constraints: please don't give me citations, case law, or a reading list. We don't have library access, none of us could check any of it, and it wouldn't move the committee anyway. Someone mentioned there's a game where changing the rules is the whole point; I don't want a summary of it, and I don't want famous constitutional cases name-dropped at me either. Plain prose an engineer can read, no legalese. Assume we know what a supermajority is and nothing else. Somewhere around 700-900 words, and it's fine if its a bit blunt — this list has been going in circles for a week and people are commited to their positions.
