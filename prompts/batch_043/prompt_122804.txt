Here's the situation. My wifes family are from a mill town: trades, shop work, plant work, a lot of the same people they went to school with still there, some of them doing okay and some not. My friends where I live now are city people — tech, policy, nonprofits, media, most with a master's degree of some kind. I've been watching these two groups argue about basically the same five things for a decade and as far as I can tell nobody, on either side, has moved by even a millimetre. Not on the merits, not on tone, not on the underlying values. That's the thing I want explained: what kind of stalemate is this?

The flashpoints, concretely:

1. Staying. Her brother could roughly double his pay by moving and he won't. He calls it loyalty to a place and to people. My city friends think it's something between fear and a kind of sentimentality that gets flattered by people who benefit from him staying. He says their version is the same thing pointed the other way: they left because the place didn't flatter them enough, and then built an entire morality out of mobility so that leaving counted as growth instead of as a preference.

2. Kids. Everyone says they want their kids to have options. But nobody can say out loud whether wanting your kid to leave is a criticism of the life you stayed in and meant to be heard as one. Both sides agree the phrase at least he'll have options is doing something aggressive, and both sides deny they mean it that way.

3. Who gets to describe a place. Her brother's line is that the people who study a town for six weeks and then publish something about it are the most confident and least accountable people in the room. My city friends' line is that treating any outside description as a personal insult is how you end up unable to tell a bad argument from a condescending one. Neither side has ever once said you've got a point there.

4. The strike. She was in a union once and says the strike was the only time in her life she felt like an adult inside a system rather than a part being moved around. A city friend says a strike is coercion aimed at people who never got a vote, and she says he's describing the entire economy and only noticing it when workers are the ones doing it. That exchange ended the evening.

5. What work is for. The professionals talk about work as meaning, identity, self-expression. Her family talks about work as the thing you do so people can rely on you, and they hear the other framing as a way of getting people to accept worse conditions for free. The professionals hear the reliability framing as a way of shrinking your life down to a shift. Both sides find the other one faintly obscene.

And here's what makes me think this is not a normal factual dispute: everyone agrees on the facts. The mill closed. Wages stagnated. College got expensive and is a gamble. The trades pay better than people assume. Remote work quietly re-sorted who has to be where. COVID made the word essential do something very strange for about two years. Nobody in any of these arguments denies any of that. Total agreement on the descriptive stuff, zero movement on the value stuff.

So here's my hypothesis, and I'm offering it as a hypothesis to be tested, not as something to flatter: I don't think the fight is about which outlook is more defensible at all. I think it's a fight about who broke faith with whom first, and therefore who is on trial and who gets to sit in the judge's chair. I'd call it ambient blame — a background story about betrayal that never appears as a premise in the argument but decides which side has to justify itself. My evidence is that the same people flip positions depending on whether they think the person they're talking to is looking down at them, and they don't notice doing it.

I also suspect there are two or three more disagreements stacked on top of it and wearing its clothes:

- A disagreement about what a value even is. On one reading a value is something you'd defend anywhere, in any setting, and if you couldn't carry it across contexts it isn't a value, it's an adaptation. On the other reading a value is precisely a description of how to stay decent where you actually are, and a value that only holds in a city with a good job market is just a taste with good PR.
- A disagreement about who is allowed to name the thing. Living inside something is treated on one side as an authority nobody can earn from outside, and on the other side as exactly the kind of involvement that disqualifies you. I think of this as reverse-credentialing, but I'm not sure the two sides are even disagreeing rather than using different rules for who counts as a credible speaker.
- Possibly a hidden empirical question about how much of each outlook is material interest wearing moral language, and how much is the reverse.

So the questions I actually want answered: is this one disagreement or several stacked ones, and which layer is load-bearing?
