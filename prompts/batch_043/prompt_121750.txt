I teach a 200-level epistemology seminar, 14 students, one 80-minute block, and next week is peer disagreement — conciliationism vs steadfastness. I want a game rather than a discussion, something where they end up doing one of the two things before anyone names either view: no mention of the words conciliationism or steadfastness until the last ten minutes.

Rough setup I have in mind: everyone gets a brief look at a jar and writes down an estimate, then gets paired and is shown their partner's estimate, and can revise for points under a calibration-style scoring rule; nobody learns the true count until the end, so the only thing they have to go on is each other. The wrinkles I want built in, because these are the bits I've never seen handled well: some pairs get genuinely independent looks while others are shown the same look twice without being told, so naively meeting in the middle double-counts; and in about a third of the pairs one person gets a real second independent look, except the facilitator sometimes swaps in a duplicate of the first one, so each student has imperfect evidence about whether they're the better-informed one. Then a final round where the disagreement is about whether meeting in the middle is the right rule at all — I want them to walk into the self-undermining worry on their own, the thing where splitting the difference with everyone seems to force you to give up splitting the difference as soon as somebody rejects it.

Give me rules, materials, a scoring scheme needing no money, a minute-by-minute outline, and a debrief that maps whatever actually happened onto the first-order/higher-order evidence distinction, the independence condition, and the spinelessness charge. Be honest about what will go wrong the first time — somebody will spot the duplicate trick, somebody will just average mechanically without feeling anything — and about what the game can't do, since I assume the leap from jars to moral and religious disagreement is mine to make out loud. Write the whole thing as one continuous piece of prose, no headings, bullets, bold, or tables.
