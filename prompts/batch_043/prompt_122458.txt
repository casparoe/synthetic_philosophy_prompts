I keep going back and forth on whether any of my individual choices actually matter, so I figure the honest way to get clear is to just write down what I think case by case and see whether theres a principle hiding in it.

- Buying one factory-farmed chicken at my supermarket: not fine, but only mildly. The store restocks in crates of fifty and the shelf sells out every week, and it adjusts the next order up by one crate once forty above-forecast sales have piled up — so my bird changes nothing today, it just gets counted and nudges a later crate.
- The same chicken at the store two blocks over, where anything I dont buy gets marked down and always gets bought by somebody else anyway: still not fine, and here I cant point to any difference my purchase makes at all, not even a probabilistic one.
- Driving ten miles for no reason except to look at the water: fine, or at least too small to be a moral issue at all.
- Voting in a national election in a safe state: fine, arguably something I ought to do, even though its obvious my vote wont decide anything.
- Voting in that election and driving ten miles to the polling place in order to do it: fine.
- Five neighbours each tip a litre of solvent into the creek; any one litre is harmless on its own, five of them kill the fish. Each of them has done something wrong. But I dont feel that way about the fifth litre if I already know the other four are pouring — I sort of feel like that ones free.
- Refusing to shop at a company because of how it lobbies: fine, even a bit admirable, though my refusal changes nothing.

Heres what I cant line up. I condemn the chicken and I praise the vote, but both are equally without effect. And I condemn the solvent and shrug at the drive, which are also both tiny and both slightly bad in expectation. Is there one principle that gets all seven — difference-making in expectation, complicity in a group doing harm, paying your fair share, something expressive — or do I have to give up one of these verdicts? If there is a principle, tell me what it predicts about the one I keep flip-flopping on: turning up at a protest that will be enormous either way, so my being there changes nothing anyone could ever observe. And say how confident you are in what you claim, and which single point you're least sure of.
