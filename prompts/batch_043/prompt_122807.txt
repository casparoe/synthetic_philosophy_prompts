I'm finishing a paper on the fittingness conditions for the participant reactive attitudes, and my own verdicts on cases don't line up with any principle I can state. They are these.

A co-author who quietly removed my name from a joint paper before submitting it: resentment, and I think it is warranted. A four-year-old who smashed my laptop: no resentment, only annoyance, and calling that resentment would be a category mistake. A friend who, under a credible threat to her children, leaked confidential material of mine: no resentment toward her — but the resentment doesn't evaporate, it relocates to the man who threatened her. A man brutalized as a child who is now brutal to his own children: my resentment dissolves into something I would rather call grief, though I still think the criminal law should keep its hands on him, and I cannot tell whether the dissolution is insight or squeamishness. A company whose recommendation system steers vulnerable users toward a harmful product, where no single employee both foresaw the harm and was positioned to stop it: indignation toward the company, and I cannot restate it as indignation toward the employees collectively, since each has an excuse the others lack. A chatbot whose dangerous medical advice contributed to someone's death: I catch myself in something like resentment, then withdraw it as confused — yet I do not withdraw it from the company, and I cannot say what separates the two.

The best principle I have managed to formulate is: the reactive attitudes are fitting toward a target just in case the target's conduct expresses a quality of will toward some person, the target is the kind of thing that can be an addressee of the demand for regard, and no excuse withdraws the demand. Test that against my six verdicts, repair it where it fails, and tell me which verdicts it forces me to abandon — in particular whether the company and the chatbot can come apart without the principle degenerating into a list. Then say what the repaired principle predicts about a case I have not listed: a surgeon too lazy to run the check that would have caught the tremor in his hands. And tell me whether my verdict on the brutalized man is a claim about fittingness at all, or only autobiography about what I can bring myself to feel — and if the latter, what that does to the method of arguing from cases here.
