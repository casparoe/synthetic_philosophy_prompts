i need a poem to open a reading-group session on acausal trade, and i dont want it to be a whiteboard with two boxes and an arrow that isn't an arrow.

here's the shape i have in mind, but change it if you see a better version of the same idea. Two columns, one speaker per column, loose iambic tetrameter, maybe 16-20 lines each. The speakers are agents who will never exchange a single bit — seperate light cones, or one program on two machines that never talk — and each is deciding whether to pay a real cost whose benefit lands entirely on the other.

The formal rule: rhyme across the gutter. The last word of each left-hand line rhymes with the last word of the line opposite it, and that rhyme is the only thing that ever passes between them. And to keep it honest, neither column may contain a verb of communication or observation — no send, receive, signal, reply, hear, answer, message, watch. If you have to break that once, make it cost something.

What i actually care about is the reason each speaker pays. It has to read as the correlation and not as any consequence: not being owed, not a later meeting, no reputation, no iteration, no enforcement, and above all no ever finding out whether the other side paid. Whoever knows this literature should be able to say which formal choice is carrying the argument, and should wince if a reader comes away thinking one speaker simply trusts the other's character.

Then: a shared refrain, identical and unchanging, three times in each column, and in the final stanza both columns close on the same line, same words, side by side — the fixed point. Let the cross-gutter rhymes start slant and tighten to exact as it goes; I want the early imprecision to read as each agent having only an approximate model of it's own decision procedure, converging as the recursion gets deeper. If that's too cute, say so and do better.

And a coda, 4-6 lines, one voice, set apart from the columns: a third agent who wants into the trade and can't get in, because neither of the others can model it — too noisy, too complicated, it's self-model too thin to be simulated from outside. It tries the refrain and doesn't quite land it. That's meant to be the uncomfortable part, where the symmetry turns out to have a door policy: you only get to trade acausally if you're legible enough to be predicted. Don't resolve it, don't sentimentalize it, don't let it become a plea.

No jargon anywhere — no utility talk, no theory names, nothing a non-philosopher in the room would have to ask about. Someone who doesn't know the material should just have heard a poem about two voices that rhyme.
