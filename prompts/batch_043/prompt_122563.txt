I've been trying to write up my position on Pascal's-mugging cases for a reading group, and the pieces keep failing to line up. Here is the set as I currently hold it. I want an audit of whether it is jointly tenable, not a survey of the debate.

(a) Decision rule: maximize expected utility, with the probabilities being my own credences and a cardinal utility function that already contains whatever risk-aversion I have. No ambiguity-aversion, and no separate filter that screens options for reasonableness before the calculation.

(b) Unbounded utility: no finite ceiling on how good an outcome can be. I want to keep the aggregative thought that enough small goods outweigh any finite bad, so I refuse to cap the utility function.

(c) No zero priors for contingent empirical claims: for every N, the hypothesis that the stranger in front of me can and will produce N units of value if I hand over five dollars gets positive, if astronomically small, credence.

(d) I don't pay the mugger, and I don't think I should. Nor do I think the iterated-mugging reply (he will be back tomorrow, and so will his friends) disposes of the case: the genuinely one-shot version, at a cost I can easily bear, still doesn't move me.

(e) The bounded-utility fix is worse than the disease, so I reject it.

(f) I also reject fixes that operate on the decision rule rather than on the prior — a lexical probability threshold, treating chances below some epsilon as zero — because of the aggregation argument: enough independent chances, each individually negligible, jointly make an outcome near-certain, and a view that throws away each one throws away the lot.

(g) My actual fix is a selection-based discount on the prior. The probability that a claim of enormous stakes is true, conditional on its having reached me through a channel that is itself sensitive to whether I pay — a stranger who asserts it because asserting it is how he gets my money — should decay as the size of the claim grows, because what the arrival of the claim is evidence about is the claimant, not the world, and that evidential bearing does not weaken as the payoff grows. The product stays bounded and the mugger is stopped.

(h) But I want long-shot reasoning to work elsewhere. I think it is worth spending real money on interventions with something like a one-in-ten-thousand chance of averting a civilizational catastrophe with ten-to-the-fifteen or more lives of value at stake, and I think that is where marginal resources do the most good. I also want to say that the gap between this and the mugger is structural, not merely a matter of the numbers being less extreme.

(i) I would pay in a variant where the claim is independently corroborated: small-scale demonstrations I can inspect, a mechanism I can trace, testimony not produced by the fact that I am the one being asked.

My own attempts to locate the fault line have each seemed to cost me something elsewhere in the set, so please find it rather than confirm my diagnosis.
