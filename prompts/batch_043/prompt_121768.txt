I've been reading the rationalist blogs and forums for about a decade, and I work in market design, so my native vocabulary is this: agents with utility functions, quasi-linear and transferable utility, the core, the Nash bargaining solution and Kalai–Smorodinsky, Shapley value, incentive compatibility and the revelation principle, ex-ante versus ex-post Pareto, and the fact that mixing over policies and mixing over utility functions come apart as soon as choices are sequential.

There's a picture that gets passed around in these parts — that parties with different values can capture genuine gains from trade by compromising, each conceding what matters less to them than to the other side, so that both end up better off by their own lights. I want that picture restated in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, with nothing smuggled in from outside it, and then I want the residue report.

Three things I especially want pinned down. First: moral disagreements rarely have a transferable numeraire, so what exactly has to be assumed for gains from trade to even be well-defined here — is the trade framing load-bearing or decorative? Second: is the proposed compromise a mixture over policies, a mixture over utility functions, or a reweighting inside one social welfare function? In my world those three coincide in static one-shot problems and diverge badly in sequential ones, so I want to know which the argument is actually leaning on. Third: can deontological side-constraints, and the dirty-hands or integrity objection people raise against compromising at all, be represented in this vocabulary, or only by faking them with lexical thresholds and infinite weights?

Then tell me what has no expression in it whatsoever. My suspicion is that the frame's blind spot is that it takes agents as given, with fixed utility functions, so it cannot say anything about a compromise that changes who the compromisers are — and that better off by their own lights is indexed to a self that may not survive the deal. But I want your read on whether that residue indicts my vocabulary or shows the original thesis was thinner than it sounds, and where you'd put the burden of proof.
