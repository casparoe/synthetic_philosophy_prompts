Ok so I've been going back and forth on Molinism and I think I've found the thing that breaks it, but I want someone to tell me where I'm messing up. The setup: before God decrees anything, he has middle knowledge — for every possible person in every possible set of circumstances, he knows what that person would freely choose. So creation is basically God shopping: he looks over the catalogue of possible people, sees which ones would screw up, and decides who to actualize and what situations to drop them into. If I end up sinning, God didn't merely foresee it, he *picked* me, and picked me partly because of how he knew I'd turn out. But then my choice was already fixed before I existed, so I'm not free, and middle knowledge is just determinism with extra steps. Molinists keep insisting the counterfactuals of freedom are true independently of God's will, but that feels like a dodge — the truth is settled either way, so what does "independent" actually buy? My patch was to go probabilistic: God doesn't know what each possible person *would* do, only a chance distribution over what they'd do, so there's nothing to shop with. But then I can't see how God gets any providential control out of it, and it looks like it collapses straight into open theism. (I'm not asking about the grounding/truthmaker complaint, that's a separate thing I've already given up on.) Which of these moves is the confused one?
