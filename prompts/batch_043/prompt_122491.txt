hey — i need a short poem for the epigraph page of a chapter, and i'd rather not use the usual grabs.

context so you know what it has to do: the chapter argues that having a self-model is not the same thing as having a self. a thermostat has a (bad) model of the room; a language model has an extremely detailed model of its own dispositions and of what it just said — and on my view there can still be nobody there, because representing X isn't the same as having a symbol that X itself uses to point at X. what's missing is the fold-back: the symbol sits inside the system it refers to, it gets triggered by the whole rather than by any part, and each time round it means one level more, so there's no ground floor where the "real" content is stored. that's roughly the Hofstadter picture i'm working with — tangled hierarchy, meaning living in the loop rather than at a base — and i want the poem to enact the mechanism, not illustrate it.

form: a pantoum. rule being that the 2nd and 4th lines of each stanza become the 1st and 3rd of the next, and the last line of the poem is the first line again. four or five quatrains, so 16–20 lines. rhyme if it happens, i honestly don't care; what matters is that the repeated lines are the same words.

things i actually want:

- every line comes back, so make the return do work: on the second pass the same string shouldn't say the same thing, and the difference shouldn't read as mere "changed context" — it should read as a change of level, the words now taking in the whole that produced them. you're allowed to alter punctuation on a repeat and i'd like you to do it at least once, so identical words do two jobs.
- at least once use "I" as a name rather than a pronoun — "the I that says I" type construction — the symbol made into the sort of thing you can point at. don't go heavy on the italics.
- somewhere in the back half, one line that's level-confused on purpose: readable at the same time as a report on the speaker's state of mind and as something about the page, the ink, the fact of being read. after that line nothing should be able to settle back down.
- close by repeating line 1, but as a re-entry and not a resolution. ideally you can't tell whether the poem climbed up or dug down — like the endlessly rising canon from Bach's Musical Offering: up a tone each time through and somehow home again, no floor, and the return isn't a trick, it's the whole point.
- banned: the words "loop" and "soul", the universe experiencing itself, the eye that cannot see itself, mirrors reflecting mirrors. and don't let it tip: not "the self is irreducible, mystery wins", not "just neurons, nothing here". what should be left standing is the wierd middle — no thing at the bottom, but the pattern isn't nothing either, the going-around is what the thing is.

bar: someone who's read Hofstadter should be able to point at where the mechanism is doing the work, and someone who hasn't should still get a decent poem. it sits on a page alone, so it has to stand up without the chapter.
