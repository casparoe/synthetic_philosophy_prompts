I help run a small weekend reading group that grew out of a local rationalist meetup; most of us have read the encyclopedia article on moral responsibility and a scattering of blog commentary, and none of us are specialists. Our last two sessions on the Frankfurt-style cases degenerated, and I diagnosed the degeneration as a taxonomy problem rather than an argument problem: people were using the phrase could have done otherwise to mean at least three different things (could have performed a different bodily action; could have willed or chosen differently; could have refrained from acting intentionally), and using blameworthy to cover both being an apt target of resentment and demands for explanation and deserving to suffer in whatever sense would make punitive consequences fair. So I want a classification instrument, not more prose: a decision tree or flowchart, eight questions, six to eight leaves, sized to fit on one sheet with a scoring key on the back, that sorts a person by their verdicts on cases rather than by which label they would apply to themselves.

The constraints I care about, in order. First, build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of names: give me the questions and the answer combinations they permit, then locate the actual positions in that space afterward. Branch nodes must be forced-choice verdicts on concrete cases, phrased so that someone who has never heard the word compatibilism can answer them and so that the correct answer is not predictable from the name of the leaf it leads to; no node may ask whether someone accepts a principle, or mention a view by name. Second, these cases must all appear somewhere in the tree: a standard counterfactual-intervener case where the intervener never fires; a nested version where the intervener waits on an involuntary prior sign; a flicker case where the only thing left open to the agent is a twitch or a slightly earlier decision; a variant where instead of a neural device the intervener simply threatens something the agent cares about enormously, and its mirror image where the intervener would reward the agent for choosing otherwise, so that alternative possibilities survive but the payoffs are distorted; a case with no intervener at all where the agent has many alternatives but every one of them is nearly as bad as the one chosen; a case where the agent knows about the device and one where only we know; a plain determinism case with no intervener, where the agent acts through an ordinary reasons-responsive mechanism; a manipulation case where the mechanism was engineered to produce exactly this choice but now operates reasons-responsively, plus the thirty-years-ago versus five-minutes-ago contrast; and a case where the agent cannot now do otherwise but the inability is their own earlier doing, as with handing over the car keys to prevent a drunk drive home. Third, the leaves must include leeway incompatibilism in both its plain and its flicker form, semicompatibilism of the actual-sequence or guidance-control sort, the Frankfurt-style view that locates responsibility in identification with or ownership of one's will, source incompatibilism resting on the manipulation argument, and the skeptical position that nobody has desert-entailing control but that we should keep a forward-looking practice anyway, with an honest note on whether that last one is a genuine leaf or merely a practical codicil that several leaves can attach to. Each leaf should carry one sentence on its least comfortable downstream commitment. Where the eight questions permit a combination nobody occupies, list it and say briefly whether it is empty because incoherent, because unattractive, or because merely unexplored; flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot actually separate instead of pretending the nodes distinguish them; and make the scoring key say what to do with a respondent whose pattern lands in an empty cell or looks internally inconsistent.

Two further things. The epistemic register I want: in a short coda, separate what you take to be settled in this dialectic, including how widely the simple alternative-possibilities principle is now rejected and on what grounds, from what remains genuinely contested among people who work on it; mark each node whose intuitive answer is itself disputed, note how confident you are in each such claim, and do not present a contested reading of the dialectic as the consensus. And be willing to criticize the instrument: tell me which of my cases are really repackagings of another, which node a specialist would say is doing question-begging work, and where my framing of the branches smuggles in a contestable assumption, for instance that there is one control condition underlying all our blame practices, or that the folk notion of responsibility is a single thing to be analyzed rather than a family of them.
