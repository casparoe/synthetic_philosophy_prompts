hey, I've somehow ended up holding the pen for the wrap-up doc of an adversarial collaboration that's been running about eight months, and I need a draft of the joint statement. the two camps:

worst-case people — roughly, until you can state a threat model and show a bound that holds for every input or trajectory inside it (or bound the probability of catastrophe conditional on assumptions you'd defend under adversarial scrutiny), you don't have a safety case; measured average performance on an eval suite is evidence about the distribution the suite was built from and not obviously about anything else.

average-case people — roughly, worst-case bounds over any threat model rich enough to contain a system smarter than you are either vacuous or unattainable, so the only thing that has ever actually reduced risk in practice is measured reliability on representative distributions, minus known failure modes, with error bars you can act on and monitoring in deployment; demanding worst-case guarantees is a way of never having to produce a number.

what I want:
