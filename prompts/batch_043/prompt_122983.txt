I'm working on a novel where one of the characters — a burnt-out experimentalist, not a crank — actually gets a working demon, and I want the physics to be the sort a physicist would grudgingly nod at instead of throwing the book at the wall. So before I decide what she's built, I need the spec.

Write me the desiderata for a theory of demons: what any account of when a little gatekeeper can and can't turn information into work has to deliver, with the case behind each requirement. I want the list to cover failure stories and my success story equally — a good theory should be able to look at any setup and say where the entropy actually goes, not just stamp NO on it. The purpose it has to serve: it should be good enough to tell me which single assumption I can break in fiction and what I'd be forced to break along with it.

The cases I want it to handle, because these are the shapes my plot keeps taking:

- the never-eraser: she keeps every measurement result forever, the memory just grows, nobody ever pays to clear it;
- the lazy door: opening and shutting the gate is free and frictionless and she only ever opens it when the molecule was going the right way anyway, so most of the time she does literally nothing;
- the species sorter: not fast-from-slow but two kinds of molecule in a mixture, making a concentration difference and osmotic pressure — is that the same problem wearing a hat;
- the cold brain: her memory sits at a lower temperature than the gas;
- the blind demon: no measurement at all, she just exploits a correlation that's already sitting there between something she can see and the molecule's state;
- the one-shot: a single run, work out, quit — where per-cycle bookkeeping doesn't obviously apply and rare big fluctuations matter more than averages;
- the demon whose memory gets wiped for free because the environment thermalizes her bits on its own.

Plus the structural ones I keep tripping over: where the theory draws the boundary between demon, gas, and environment and what rides on that choice; whether it's allowed to lean on the demon being an agent or a mind rather than a dumb mechanism; whether it has to give answers for finite memory, finite time and a finite bath, or only in some ideal limit; and whether the cost is a hard floor per bit, the kT ln 2 thing I half-remember from Landauer, or only an average that a lucky fluctuation can beat.

Mark which requirements are non-negotiable — break them and it isn't physics anymore — and which are just preferences of one camp, like insisting the whole thing be told in the vocabulary of entropy rather than information, or in terms of ensembles rather than individual runs. Flag any pair of requirements you suspect can't be jointly satisfied; that's the part I'll build the third act around. And tell me which single requirement kills off the largest number of would-be working demons, so I know what I'm up against.

A sentence each on how the big extant stories do against the list is plenty — the measurement-is-fine-it's-the-erasure-that-costs line, the fluctuation-theorem people, whatever else — I care about the criteria far more than the leaderboard, and I'd rather you spent the words on which demands are real and which are tribal.
