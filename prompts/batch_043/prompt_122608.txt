I write a weekly column on science and ideas, and I am working on a piece about whether the line between therapy and enhancement can bear any real weight now that the same molecules move between the two categories depending on who is prescribed them and why. My editor wants the online version to carry a short interactive sidebar: a decision tree that a reader can walk through by answering concrete questions and end up at a named position in the debate, rather than at a label they would have picked for themselves at the start. I would like you to build that tree for me.

The construction matters more to me than the prose. I want roughly seven branch nodes, each phrased as a question an intelligent but untrained reader could answer without knowing which position it leads to; no node may simply ask whether the line sits at normal species functioning, or whether enhancement is morally permissible, since that would just sort people by the vocabulary they already have. Each node should instead present a case or a claim on which the positions genuinely diverge, and the answer should route the reader onward. Please build from the logical space rather than from a list of names: give me first the questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then locate the actual positions in that space, mark the combinations nobody occupies, and say of each whether it is empty because it is incoherent, because it is merely unattractive, or because nobody has got round to it. I need between nine and eleven leaves, each labeled descriptively with associated thinkers' names in parentheses only where you are confident, and each carrying one sentence on its most awkward commitment. The leaves must include the species-typical normal-function view; the fair-equality-of-opportunity or normal-opportunity-range view; the welfarist view on which there is no morally relevant discontinuity at all, only a graded cost-benefit profile; a capabilities or threshold-sufficiency view; the giftedness or openness-to-the-unbidden objection; the view that the distinction has no moral force but that an administrative line still has to be drawn and should therefore be drawn democratically; the positional or arms-race view on which therapy and enhancement come apart because some goods are comparative; and a skeptical view on which medicalization is constitutive, so the line simply tracks whatever diagnostic institutions happen to recognize this decade. Somewhere among the nodes I want these cases used rather than merely mentioned: GLP-1 receptor agonists prescribed for diabetes, then for obesity, then for a healthy reader who would like to lose five kilos; growth hormone for idiopathic short stature in a country whose average adult height is low; stimulants for a diagnosed child versus the same stimulant for a student with no diagnosis; testosterone restored to a seventy-year-old's own thirty-year-old levels; a cochlear implant for a child of Deaf parents; vaccination, which raises immune performance above anything unaided nature delivers; pharmacological damping of a traumatic memory after an assault; and a titration case, where a drug that restores a deficiency can be dialed past normal.

Two further cases I want the tree to have to adjudicate, because I think most versions of this debate quietly dodge them. The first I have been calling mismatch repair: an intervention that lifts nobody above the species-typical range and instead re-tunes a species-typical mechanism that is mismatched to the environment the person actually occupies, such as sustained attention under conditions of dense interruption, or a threat-response system calibrated for acute danger in a life of chronic low-grade stress. This looks like therapy relative to the niche the person lives in and enhancement relative to species-typical functioning, and I want to see which branch the tree sends it down and what that costs the leaf it lands on. The second is the counterfactual-person baseline: judging against not the population but the individual's own unmanipulated trajectory, so that an intervention preserving a capacity someone was never going to have counts as therapy for them while the identical intervention given to someone already at that level counts as enhancement. Please also flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot really separate, and name the further case that would separate them. And a hard constraint: no citations, bibliographies, or reading lists, since I cannot verify them and my readers do not want them; write for a general audience with no background in bioethics, and keep the whole thing to something I can set as a sidebar.
