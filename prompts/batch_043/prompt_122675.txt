My opening move. Take this pair of imaginary creatures, both Popperian in the ordinary sense: they run candidate actions through an internal model before acting. Ava's model is a superb filter and a hopeless generator — it coughs up three or four crude, largely recycled options and then screens them brilliantly, so she almost never gets hurt. Otto's is the mirror image: a lush generator that keeps throwing up novel, elaborate, slightly wierd candidate plans, paired with a bad filter — he acts on maybe one in three and gets hurt a lot. On your leveling story Otto is the degenerate case, the one sliding back toward the Skinnerian end, because his testing is worse and his hypotheses still tend to die with him rather than in his stead. But I want to say Otto is the more advanced mind, because what actually improves as you climb the tower is generation: the point of words and other mind-tools isn't that they help you filter better, it's that they hand you better candidates to filter in the first place. So: is the tower a story about filtering or about generating? And if it's about generating, how do you keep saying the four levels are just one continuum?
