Im giving my last seminar presentation of term on Friday and I need a proper Q&A prep sheet, not a defence of the view.

My thesis: Dennett is basically right that the self is a centre of narrative gravity rather than a thing you could point to, but he puts the story in the wrong place — most of the gravitational work is done from *outside* the person, by family, friends, institutions, care home notes, the people who keep saying "she's always been like that". So the self is a joint posit, not a private one. The case I lean on: compare someone with dense autobiographical memory but nobody left alive who shares any of it, with someone who has almost no episodic memory at all but whose family and care staff keep up a thick running story about her. On my version the second has, if anything, *more* self, and I want to claim that's the right result rather than a reductio.

The room: my tutor, who's already more or less on board with Dennett; two phenomenology people who think there's a pre-reflective bodily sense of self that no story could possibly constitute; one Parfit-ish reductionist who'll want to know whether my move is just Parfit with extra steps or worse than Parfit; and a clinical neuropsychologist from the psych dept who's actually worked with people in the later stages of dementia and thinks I'm being glib about them.

Give me the six or so objections I'll really get, in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each of those people would actually phrase them, ordered by how much damage they'd do. For each: what I should concede straight away, what I should dig in on, a two-minute answer, and where that answer is softest. And then the one question I'm praying nobody asks, plus what I'd even say if they did.
