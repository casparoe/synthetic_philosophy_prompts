I'm a quantitative social scientist — applied micro / political economy by training. My working vocabulary is potential outcomes, DAGs, identification strategies and conditional independence, mediation and mechanism tests, instruments and natural experiments, effect heterogeneity, external validity and transportability, construct validity and measurement invariance, equilibrium and spillover effects. I read that literature comfortably. I have no training in social theory: my exposure to Weber is what a well-read nonspecialist picks up — the Protestant ethic thesis, elective affinity, ideal types, the idea that sociology interprets action by its subjectively intended meaning.

I have to referee a historical-sociology paper that leans on Weber, and before I judge it I want to reconstruct its argument in terms I can actually work with. The package, as I read it, is four claims:

1. Values can be causes. A religiously grounded complex of belief and disposition — roughly, a notion of a calling joined to a this-worldly asceticism — exercised causal influence on the emergence of a distinctively modern economic conduct.
2. Meaning-level adequacy. An explanation of an action or a practice is deficient if it gets the causal pattern right but does not render the behavior intelligible as something the agent could have been up to, in terms like the agent's own.
3. Value-relevant selection. Any concrete historical outcome has an indefinitely large set of causal antecedents; what selects a segment of that manifold as *the* explanation is not merely a causal fact but the significance of the outcome for us, so the object of explanation is partly constituted by the investigator's values.
4. Elective affinity rather than one-way causation. The religious complex and the economic ethos are said to have selected and stabilized each other, not simply to have pushed on each other.

Please translate each claim into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and for each one mark three things separately: what comes across cleanly, what comes across only after being bent — say exactly where and how you bent it — and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. If a translation requires an assumption my framework does not license on its own, such as that agents' reasons are causally efficacious or that something can be a cause without being even in principle intervenable, say so explicitly rather than smoothing it over. And if one of my four claims is a caricature of what Weberians hold, tell me that instead of translating it dutifully.

There is one failure mode I keep running into and have started calling semantic endogeneity. It is not ordinary reverse causation, which I can write down: it is not that the economic outcome causes the religious variable. It is that what the religious variable *is* — the content of the doctrine, what counts as the same belief across two towns, what counts as the same disposition over fifty years — seems partly to shift with, or to be fixed partly in terms of, the economic outcome it is supposed to explain. My instinct is that this is just a hard case of things I already have names for: measurement invariance across sites, construct validity, the requirement that an intervention on a variable leave the variable's identity intact. But I am not confident, because those tools all presuppose a well-defined variable whose values move, and here it looks like the variable itself is in question. Tell me whether that is a genuine distinction or whether I have inflated a measurement problem into a metaphysical one.

Then give me the residue report. Of what does not survive translation, how much points to something my causal vocabulary cannot say, and how much points to the original claims being less clear or less distinctive than they sound — in particular, whether claim 2 says more than "mechanisms should be made explicit" and whether claim 3 says more than "estimands are chosen to serve interests." I would also like the reverse direction: is there anything in my framework — some assumption about manipulability, separability of treatment and outcome, or the invariance of a variable's identity under intervention — that quietly begs the question against the Weberian position?

Finally, be explicit about the epistemic status of your own translation: where it is most likely to be rejected by someone who actually works on Weber, where it depends on a contested reading of him, and what would change your mind about whether the residue is real rather than an artifact of the way you chose to translate.
