I am trying to say what I actually believe about the truth of statements about the future, and I would rather begin from my verdicts on cases than from a theory. Here they are.

(i) A midshipman will toss a fair coin at noon tomorrow. "The coin will land heads" — I will call this neither true nor false. (ii) "The coin will land heads or tails" — true; I will not give that up, though I cannot see how a disjunction can be true when neither disjunct is. (iii) Astronomers have fixed, from the mechanics of the solar system, that there will be a total eclipse visible from a certain harbour nine years from now, and nothing now could prevent it. "There will be a total eclipse over the harbour" — true, now, even though nothing has yet brought it about and no one has observed anything. (iv) A patient is told her chance of surviving to June is nine in ten. "She will survive to June" — I still want to say neither true nor false, but I feel a real pull to say it is nearer the truth than the parallel claim about a patient whose chance is one in ten, and I distrust the pull. (v) "I will order the fleet to sail," said now by an admiral who has not yet decided and is still weighing her reasons — I want to say she cannot truly assert it; yet her flag officer, who has watched her weigh such reasons for twenty years, can truly say of her, now, that she will order the fleet to sail. I hold both verdicts and cannot see how both can be held. (vi) In another physical universe a perfect simulation of our world has already been run to completion, and in it a coin intrinsically identical to ours, tossed in intrinsically identical circumstances, lands heads. I do not think that makes (i) true; but I cannot say what our coin has that its duplicate lacks.

Is there a single account of what makes a future-tensed statement true — and of what one should say about the ones that are not — that returns all six verdicts? If not, tell me which verdict to surrender and which clause was doing the work. And tell me what your account says about a case I have not settled: "one day a human being will be the last human being, and she will be born on a Tuesday," where nothing now fixes whether there will ever be a last human being at all.
