I'm putting together a reading-group session on Dutch book arguments and I need a case built to order, because the three I've tried to build myself all cheat and I can't tell where.

What I want is a scenario where somebody is genuinely and flagrantly probabilistically incoherent — I don't care which axiom they break, as long as it's one a real person could actually break rather than a logic puzzle. A conjunction-fallacy-shaped monotonicity break, prices across an exhaustive partition that don't add up, conditional credences that can't be reconciled with the unconditional one — your pick. The incoherence has to be readable straight off the prices they post. But then: **no bookie can actually Dutch book them**, even though the bookie is sharp, knows their prices, has no information about how the events come out, and isn't up against any artificial restrictions.

Constraints on the build:

- Real setting, ordinary people. A workplace betting thread, side bets in a fantasy league, the friend who always runs an informal book, a prediction app where you can only buy. No demons, no perfect predictors, no infinities, no unlimited bankroll on either side.
- The bookie can make at most three bets with them and can't ask them to risk more than $100, and any sure loss has to be at least $10. So the case can't work by shrinking the margins until they're not worth bothering with.
- The person really is betting and really is willing to take the bets the bookie offers, one at a time. No cheap immunity: they don't get let off by refusing to bet, and they don't get let off by having imprecise or vague credences, or by being risk-averse about money, or by money meaning something weird to them. Their credences are incoherent, full stop. The immunity has to come from something structural — what contracts exist, how offers arrive, what they'll accept in combination, whatever you go with — and it shouldn't double as an excuse for them.
- The bookie can't lie about what the contracts pay or about the events, but can otherwise be as shrewd as you like: sequencing, framing, offering through other people, spreading things over a week.

After the scenario I need:

1. The arithmetic. Name the axiom that's broken, show it's broken from the posted prices alone, then walk through the books the bookie would try — I assume there's one obvious one — and show precisely why each fails to produce a guaranteed loss.
2. Which single feature is doing the work, and what happens when you take it out: same people, same prices, same events, feature gone, book goes through. I want to see the immunity generated rather than stipulated.
3. The bit I actually care about: play the Dutch book theorist back. Give the strongest version of their reply — my guess is something like "Dutch-bookability was only ever meant to be sufficient for irrationality, not necessary," or redoing the whole thing with bribes and fees instead of bets, or arguing that the agent is irrational in a second and different way. Rate how well each reply works and say where it leaves the claim that Dutch book arguments tell us what rationality consists in.

Two more things. Tell me how confident you are that these specs can all be satisfied at once, and flag any place where your case leans on a contested reading of what a Dutch book argument is even claiming. And if they can't all be met — if any genuine incoherence plus any realistic venue always leaves some book on the table — say that plainly and tell me which constraint has to go, instead of quietly handing me a fourth case that cheats on one of them.
