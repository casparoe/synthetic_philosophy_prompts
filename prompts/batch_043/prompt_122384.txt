(1) Mara: In the version where she'd only be wasting a trip, Ana knows. In the version where her house is on the line, she doesn't. Same true belief, same evidence — so the stakes themselves are doing the work, and knowledge is stakes-sensitive. Call it pragmatic encroachment.

(2) Theo: You've only shown that the standard the word "knows" is held to shifts with context, the way "flat" or "tall" does. Nothing in Ana's epistemic position changes. Same data, cheaper explanation.

(3) Mara: Then take the proxy case. Ana is in the low-stakes version, and she tells Ben — whose house is on the line — "I checked, it's open Saturday." Ana's practical situation never changed. (4) Theo: It doesn't, because when Ben attributes knowledge to Ana he adopts Ana's practical situation to set the standard — the question is whether Ana checked well enough for her purposes. Ben's emergency raises the bar for whether Ben knows, not for whether Ana does. So your case doesn't separate the views.

(5) Mara: Then the views have stopped differing about anything I care about — you've made the context inherit the subject's stakes, which is encroachment with extra machinery. Your view floats the standard with the conversation, so in a context where nothing is at stake for anyone knowledge becomes nearly free — and then you've lost whatever made the high-stakes verdict feel forced in the first place.

(6) Theo: Not lost, just relativised: nothing-at-stake contexts really do have low standards, and the "stability" you're missing is just the fact that ordinary attributions happen at middling stakes. Meanwhile your own view has the hidden-stakes problem: if a hacker has secretly arranged for Ana's house to burn down if the bank is closed on Saturday, your view says she doesn't know — she loses knowledge because of a fact she's never heard of, with nothing in her evidence or her mind changed.
