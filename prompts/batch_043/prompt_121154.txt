I'm writing a section of a seminar paper on whether rational herding is self-correcting, and I need a case built to specification rather than asserted. The claim I want to test is the reassuring one: that an informational cascade, however wrong it may be, is fragile — because it is triggered by a short, unrepresentative run of early signals, it should burst as soon as any modest amount of the private information that later participants were suppressing is released. I need a case in which that reassurance fails, and in which the failure is generated by the epistemic situation of the agents rather than stipulated by me.

**Construct a one-paragraph scenario** in which all of the following hold simultaneously.

1. *Herding.* From some identifiable step onward, no private signal any participant holds is capable of changing their action.
2. *Rationality.* Every individual choice maximizes expected accuracy given what that agent has observed. No agent has any taste for conformity, no reputational or career reward for being with the crowd, and no penalty for dissent; the structure of the situation and the rationality of the participants are common knowledge. Following the crowd may be rational only because crowd behavior is evidence, never because being in the crowd pays.
3. *Wrongness, in a sense you state.* The cascade lands on the worse option. Say precisely which counterfactual you are using to call it worse: specify the alternative history in which the same agents, with the same priors and the same ex ante information, choose otherwise, and state what is better in that history and for whom. I want a wrongness verdict that does not smuggle in hindsight.
4. *The self-sealing feature.* The variable the participants are trying to track must be partly produced by the group's own choices: the quality or payoff of an option depends on whether the group invests in it, and the only evidence anyone can ever observe is generated by options that were actually invested in. Choices that were never made leave no trace. This feature must be visible in the story as an ordinary fact about the setting, not as a stipulation about what agents believe.
5. *Nothing exotic.* Realistic setting and scale — a department, a funding panel, a clinical practice, a platform, a market. At most about fifteen actors. No demons, predictors, or simulations; ordinary people with ordinary evidence. State the prior and the signal reliability explicitly, in numbers, so the updating can be checked.

Then deliver the following.

- The scenario, as one paragraph.
- A step-by-step walkthrough: for each agent up to and past the onset of the cascade, what they observe, what they infer, their posterior, and their action. Identify the step at which the cascade begins and show arithmetically why private signals at and after that step are powerless.
- Verdicts for two rival diagnoses: (a) herders are irrational or conformist, so blame the agents; (b) herding is rational but information-destructive, so blame the structure. Show that both diagnoses fit everything an outside observer could see, and say what remedy each one prescribes. Then show that the standard remedy — mandatory disclosure of all private signals before anyone acts — does *not* fix your case, and give the argument or arithmetic for why not. If some other intervention does fix it, say what, and say what it costs.
- Which single feature of the case is doing the work. Then remove it and rerun the case: show that with the feature removed, the standard fragility result returns, so the case is not simply a counterexample by construction.
- The strongest attack available to someone who holds that cascades are fragile — for instance, that your case is not an information cascade at all but a coordination problem with an endogenous state, and so was never within the scope of the claim — together with your reply and an explicit statement of what you concede.
- An honest note on what the case cannot show.

A case that merely stipulates that the crowd is wrong, or that nobody is ever surprised, fails; the verdicts must fall out of the priors, the signal structure, and what is observable to whom.

After the prose, give a brief structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- `scenario`: one-sentence restatement of the case
- `setting_and_actors`: setting, number of actors, what each is choosing between
- `priors_and_signals`: the prior and signal reliability, as numbers
- `cascade_onset_step`: the integer step at which the cascade begins, with a one-line justification
- `wrongness_counterfactual`: the counterfactual history that makes the outcome worse, and what is worse in it
- `rationality_check`: whether each agent's action maximizes expected accuracy given their information, and the one step most likely to be challenged
- `remedy_verdicts`: an object with keys `blame_the_agents`, `blame_the_structure`, `mandatory_disclosure`, and `alternative_remedy`, each a short string giving what that diagnosis prescribes and whether it works here
- `load_bearing_feature`: the feature doing the work
- `ablation`: what happens to the cascade when that feature is removed
- `strongest_objection`: the best available attack, plus what is conceded in reply
- `not_shown`: what the case cannot establish
- `audit_questions`: an array of two or three checks a hostile reader should run, any of which would break the case if it fails

If a field does not apply, say so explicitly rather than filling it in.
