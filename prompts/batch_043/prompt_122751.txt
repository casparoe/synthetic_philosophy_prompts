Hi there. I'm a journalist — I mostly cover science and ideas, but I've spent the last year or so doing a run of peices on anti-corruption, and I keep stumbling into the same shape of story and I don't have a word for it, which is becoming a problem because I'd like to use the word in print without a footnote saying "sort of like Goodhart's law."

Three instances. A city hospital bans staff from accepting gifts from suppliers: within a year the gifts are gone from the ledger, and the doctors' relatives have started turning up as paid "consultants" to the same suppliers. A country introduces competitive exams for government hiring to kill off patronage: applications and test scores go up and up, and the ministries respond by moving work onto short-term consultancy contracts that never go through the exam. A transparency rule requires that allocative decisions be justified in an open meeting: the meetings get beautifully documented, and the real decision now gets made in the corridor beforehand, so the minutes record a ratification nobody disagreed with.

The pattern I think I'm seeing: the reform targets a channel, the favouritism doesn't shrink so much as relocate — and it relocates to a channel nobody was previously measuring, which is exactly why the reform looks like it worked on every indicator anyone collects. So there are two things tangled together, the displacement itself and the fact that the displacement is invisible to the very metrics that were supposed to certify success.

Is there an established concept or literature for this? I've heard corruption described as being like water, finding whatever crack is open, and I know about measures becoming targets when they're used for decisions, but neither seems to be quite it: the first is a metaphor rather than a claim with edges, and the second is about people gaming a number, whereas here the underlying thing — who actually gets the job, the contract, the bed — may not have improved at all while nobody is gaming anything. There's also ordinary circumvention: every tax code breeds avoidance, and I don't want a term so broad it covers that too.

If there's no name, I'd rather have a good carve than a label. What exactly is this, and what isn't it? I want to know how to tell it apart from genuine reduction that just leaves messy residue, from a reform that was cosmetic from day one, and from the sort of adaptive rule-dodging that any prohibition produces. And I'd want the boundary cases sorted: does it count when the new channel leaves people better off than the old one did (a gift ban that pushes procurement toward open tendering is different from one that pushes it toward cousins), or does the concept only apply when the substance is unchanged and only the surface has been cleaned? Also, a practical worry for the piece: if the target is unobservable and the proxy improves, is there any principled way to say whether the balloon was squeezed or deflated, or is that just permanently underdetermined?
