Me and two friends run a tiny board game studio — one published game, one in prototype, a blog where we post playtest postmortems. I keep arguing with myself about who owns what, so here are my gut verdicts and I want the principle behind them, if there is one. And I dont want the law: I know roughly what the law says, that mechanics basically arent protectable, that you'd need a patent, whatever. I want what makes sense of the gut.

1. A bigger studio ships a game built on our central loop — the same three-step thing that makes ours ours — but their own art, their own rules text, not a word copied. Not fine, I'm furious.
2. A designer who has never seen our game ships a structurally identical loop. Fine, hard luck.
3. Someone played our game at a con six years ago, genuinely forgot, then came up with the loop themselves. Not fine, but way less bad than 1, and I genuinely dont know what I'd want done about it.
4. Someone reads our postmortems where we list the twelve variants we playtested and killed, and skips all twelve. Barely bothers me — which is strange, since in 1 it's the creative part that got taken.
5. Someone takes the loop and fixes the bit we never got right; the fix is clearly theirs and their version is better than ours. I dont like it, but id struggle to say they owe us.
6. Turns out three other designers demoed near-identical loops the same year we did, none of them had seen ours. My sense of owning it dropped when I found out — yet I still think the copier in 1 did something wrong, just worth less.
7. Someone fed our rulebook to a language model and asked for a game with the same central loop; no text or art in common with ours. Not fine.
8. We used a model trained on other people's games to help design ours. Obviously fine.
9. Our game's been out of print eight years, we never enforced anything, someone revives the loop. I feel almost nothing. Maybe fine.
10. A guy sells a game on our loop in a language we dont publish in, to people whove never heard of us. Nothing. Which cant be right.

My own stab is: I own whatever you couldnt have reached unless I went first. That handles 1 and 2 but 7 and 8 dont both fit, and 4 and 9 dont either. Is there one principle that gets most of these, or a small set, which clause does each case motivate, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? Then: what does your principle say about two cases I have no verdict on — a fan mod that requires you to own a copy of our game and makes nobody any money, and a company that uses the loop in an internal training exercise it never sells? And which single hypothetical case would best tell the surviving candidate principles apart?
