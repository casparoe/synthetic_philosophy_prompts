Write a dialogue of 1,100 to 1,500 words between two philosophers of science who have been hired, as outside consultants, by an instrumentation lab to adjudicate a single sentence. The lab wants to write, in the abstract of its paper, that the pressure in compartment A of its apparatus opens the relief valve; the principal investigator has asked the two of them to tell her whether that causal claim is licensed. One consultant, C, defends a counterfactual account in the Lewis–Stalnaker style: an event c causes an event e when e counterfactually depends on c, or when there is a chain of stepwise counterfactual dependence running from c to e, where the relevant counterfactuals are evaluated under a similarity ordering that forbids backtracking by allowing a small local violation of law at the point of divergence and then letting the laws run forward again. The other, I, defends an interventionist account in the Woodward–Pearl style: causal claims are made relative to a set of variables and structural equations, and X counts as a cause of Y only if some intervention — an exogenous, surgical setting of X that affects Y, if at all, only along routes that run through X, with off-path variables held fixed by further interventions — would change Y or its coarse-grained value. They are colleagues of long standing, equally good, and neither is permitted to be the other's fool.

The apparatus, which both have in front of them, is this. A rigid cylinder of total internal volume 4.0 litres is divided by a frictionless, massless piston into a left compartment A and a right compartment B, both containing ideal gas held at a temperature T that the lab sets with a heat bath. Compartment A has been sealed since manufacture and contains n_A = 0.20 mol, which has never been altered and cannot be. Compartment B has a port through which the lab can set n_B. The piston carries no external load, so whenever it is free the equilibrium condition is p_A = p_B = (n_A + n_B)RT/V. The piston can also be pinned at a fixed position, in which case the two volumes are held fixed and the two pressures need not be equal. A spring-loaded relief valve at the right end of the cylinder opens if and only if the pressure in B exceeds 3.0 bar. In the run under dispute the lab set T = 300 K and n_B = 0.30 mol; at noon the piston was free, p_A = p_B = 3.12 bar, and the valve opened.

Three facts about this system should organize the argument. First, no manipulation available to the lab sets p_A alone: both of its handles, T and n_B, change the pressure in B through routes that do not pass through p_A, since heating B's gas raises p_B directly and adding gas to B raises p_B directly. Second, under C's no-backtracking semantics there is robust counterfactual dependence in both directions: had p_A been 2.5 bar at noon, the piston would have been driven left, p_B would have fallen below the threshold, and the valve would have stayed shut; and had p_B been lower, p_A would have been lower too. Third, everything turns on a feature of the setup that neither theory has a name for. Call a variable constraint-shadowed when no intervention can set it independently within any faithful model of the system, while its value is nonetheless counterfactually load-bearing for the outcome of interest. Both characters should agree that p_A is constraint-shadowed and that this is the crux, and each should try to show that her own framework gives the right verdict about constraint-shadowed variables while the other's does not.

Requirements for the exchange.

Make the apparatus do the work. The numbers, the three handles, and the distinction between the free and the pinned piston should be load-bearing, not decoration; no position may be advanced as a slogan that floats free of the case.

Give each side its strongest pressure, and make sure it is answered rather than merely asserted past. I should press the charge that C's small divergence miracle is a disguised intervention — an exogenous surgical setting — so that the counterfactual account secretly contains the interventionist one while avoiding the discipline of saying which variable is being set and over what range, and that C's similarity ordering is in practice fixed case by case and then called intuition. C should press the charge that I's verdict is hostage to a representational choice — which variables, whether the system is written as a block of simultaneous equilibrium equations or as relaxation dynamics in which the pressure difference accelerates the piston, which values are treated as the contrast class — and that when I retreats to hypothetical or soft interventions to handle systems with no handles at all, such as the same cylinder welded shut and thermally isolated, or the very early universe, she is helping herself to counterfactuals whose truth conditions her own framework has not supplied.

Both must confront the symmetry, rather than let it pass: at equilibrium p_A and p_B are numerically identical but are quantities of different compartments, C's chains appear to make each a cause of the other, and I's framework has a handle for p_B and none for p_A. Let each say whether that asymmetry is a discovery or an artifact.

Each must concede one thing that costs her something real — not a courtesy. For C this should be something like route-insensitivity: event-level dependence cannot by itself isolate the contribution of p_A as such when every way of realizing the antecedent drags other causes along. For I this should be something like model-relativity, or the inability to state causal facts about a system with no exogenous handles at all.

At some point they should run the test case in which the piston is pinned at its actual position. They will agree that the verdict flips. Each should then say what in her account the flip consists in — a change in the world's counterfactual structure, or a change in the model — and they should disagree about whether that difference matters.

Neither may close the discussion with an unargued it depends what you mean by cause, or with a truce declared because the question is merely pragmatic. Keep stage directions to a minimum, use no footnotes, and include no scholarly apparatus: if either alludes to a tradition, let it be half a clause at most, since the argument must stand on its own.

End the dialogue with each character writing down one sentence: what she would still need to be told about the system in order to settle the lab's question. The two sentences should differ in a way that locates the residual disagreement precisely — one asking for a fact about the modal structure of the world, the other asking for a fact about which representation we owe the claim — and the reader should be able to say why they are not the same request.
