so my reading group (me, my roommate, and like six people from a philosophy-adjacent discord) has been slogging through philosophy of mind this term, and last week we finally got to the Chase and Sanborn quining qualia paper. the group is now basically split in half: one side thinks qualia are the single most certain thing there is, the other side thinks the concept is a broken posit that falls apart the moment you press on it, and after two hours of going in circles we decided to end the semester with a dumb little zine instead of another argument. i'm laying it out this weekend and i need a centerpiece.

what i want is a fake government recall notice — like one of those URGENT: RECALL AND SAFETY NOTICE bulletins you see taped up in a laundromat — announcing the recall of qualia. deadpan throughout, 600-700 words plus a headline, formatted with the real bureaucratic furniture: product description, affected models and serial numbers, nature of the hazard, what consumers should do immediately, an FAQ, and a contact line. include at least one quote from a spokesperson for an institute of intrinsic properties who objects to the recall, and one from a consumer who insists theirs are fine.

the constraint i care most about is that the jokes come from getting the actual argument right, not from 'lol subjective experiences aren't real.' some things i want worked in: the four hallmark features (ineffable, intrinsic, private, immediately apprehensible in consciousness) treated as product specs that make the unit impossible to inspect, test, ship, or return; the recall being triggered by the finding that the only instrument certified to detect the product — introspection — systematically misreports it; the agency's official line that consumer reports about the product are perfectly good data while not being evidence that the product exists; failure modes listed with names that evoke the standard intuition pumps (spectrum inversion, the case where the units change but the owner can't tell, the case where there are none and nothing changes); and the agency quietly never being able to say what, exactly, it is recalling. nobody in the piece should ever state the argument flat out — no line like 'therefore qualia don't exist' or 'the concept is incoherent' — the whole thing should land as a reductio of how qualia get defined in the first place. someone who's never read any of this should still enjoy it as nightmare bureaucracy, and someone who has should finish it and feel a bit sick. and please spare us the koala pun, we've all heard it.
