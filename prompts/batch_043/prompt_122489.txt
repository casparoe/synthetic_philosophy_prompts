Here's my own first pass, which I'd like you to redo properly and then correct. Epistemic bubble: the evidence stream is a non-randomly selected subset of the available data, with the selection probability correlated with the content of the evidence; every individual update is locally correct, but the filtration makes the posterior systematically biased, so the natural intervention is randomized exposure, like a holdout set that reopens the missing cells. Echo chamber: the intervention isn't on which data you see but on the likelihood function or on the learned source-reliability parameters, so that out-group testimony carries a near-zero or negative weight; in the negative case, seeing true contrary evidence decreases your credence in the truth, which means exposure is not merely unhelpful but monotone in the wrong direction. I've also been playing with two complications I haven't seen anyone discuss. First, call it expected inversion per impression: the quantity a feed would optimize if it wanted entrenchment per unit of counter-attitudinal exposure rather than engagement per impression. Such a system would deliberately surface strong opposing material, and it would look, by every engagement metric we currently track, like a system promoting viewpoint diversity. Second, call a case attenuated rather than inverted when the update is correctly signed but shrunk toward zero — say a fixed trust weight of 0.1 on out-group sources — and call it meta- or hyper-parameter capture when what's been poisoned is the reliability term itself rather than any first-order belief, so that the agent's own learning rule keeps re-estimating the reliability downward as a function of how much out-group material it sees. My tentative claim is that these cut across the bubble/chamber distinction rather than falling neatly on one side, and that once you have them the honest taxonomy is indexed by something like the sign and slope of the update under randomized exposure plus which parameter is being tampered with, not by the presence or absence of a filter.
