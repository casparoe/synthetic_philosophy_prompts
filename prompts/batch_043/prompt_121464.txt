So: I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background, and I fell into the "is culture Darwinian" / meme argument about a year ago through a fight on my work Slack, and I cannot believe how completely it has stalled. As near as I can tell the two sides are these.

Side A: culture is a second inheritance system. Tunes, techniques, recipes, jokes, tool designs get copied with variation and differential success, so you get cumulative adaptation by a Darwinian process, and the thing being copied is a replicator — the meme. Copying is never perfect, but imperfect copying plus selection is exactly the recipe, and the messiness of culture no more stops it being Darwinian than the messiness of biology does.

Side B: the analogy is broken at the load-bearing joint. Cultural transmission isn't copying, it's reconstruction — you don't recieve a meme, you watch someone do a thing and rebuild your own version of it, which is why everybody is so good at telephone-game-style drift. Variants aren't random with respect to what would be useful, they're aimed at what people want. There's no genotype/phenotype split, no clean generation boundary, no fact of the matter about where one meme stops and the next begins, so there's no unit of selection and no real replicator. At best "meme" is a loose way of talking; at worst it's a metaphor doing the work of an argument.

And then nothing. Both sides restate, nobody moves, nobody concedes anything, and neither side seems to think the other has made a point that needs answering. It smells to me like a question that has quietly stopped being a question about culture and become something else, and I want to know what.

Here's my own half-baked hypothesis, and the reason I got suspicious in the first place. I build systems that contain genuine high-fidelity replicators with real lineages: git. Commits, diffs, forks, merge conflicts, a perfect record of who copied what from whom and when. When I look at culture with git-eyes, most of it has no such structure whatsoever — nobody can tell you which version of the joke they just told you. But some of it does: manuscripts copied by scribes (I'm told there's a whole subfield that reconstructs family trees of manuscripts from shared copying errors), legal codes, sheet music, and obviously code. So the hypothesis: the two sides aren't disagreeing about whether culture is Darwinian at all — each of them is looking at a different slice, the high-fidelity-record slice and the reconstructive-drift slice, both are right about their own slice, and the single word "culture" is doing all the work of hiding that.
