I hold a position about the meaning of utility that I would like stress-tested, and I would rather not be handed the greatest hits.

Here is the position, in three parts. Utility is not a quantity that choice measures with more or less error; it is a numerical representation of an agent's preference ranking, and being better off just is getting the option one preferred. Call this representationalism. From it I draw two further commitments. First, there is no welfare fact that a sufficiently rich body of choice could fail to reveal: no residue of feeling, no fact about what the agent really wants beneath what she chooses, no gap between revealed and true preference that is not merely a gap in the data. Second, no welfare-relevant claim can turn on which of the many monotone transforms of a given ranking you use to represent it, which is why I hold that interpersonal comparisons of utility are not difficult to make but incoherent to ask for: there is no hidden magnitude, so there is nothing to compare. Third, when actual choice misbehaves relative to the standard revealed-preference axioms and their finite-data rationalizability conditions, the correct response is not to posit an underlying welfare state that choice is tracking badly, but to repair the description of the choice situation: enrich the space of alternatives, index options by the descriptions under which they were presented, move to temporally extended or all-things-considered preference, and then see whether the axioms hold. I will call this the repair strategy, and I regard it as the part of the package most likely to be doing illegitimate work.

What I want from you is a map of the exposure of this package, not a survey of the literature.

Start with what I think I can already dispatch, and tell me where the dispatch fails. Adaptive preferences, framing effects, menu dependence, and the familiar violations of the independence axioms for choice under risk: I treat these as data about the description of alternatives, not as evidence of a welfare state that escapes choice. Is that response legitimate, or is it a case of the theory redescribing its own anomalies?

Then give me the considerations I have not gotten to work out, at whatever level of detail they deserve:

Under uncertainty, a choice is a joint function of what the agent wants and what she expects, so a body of choice data is consistent with a range of utility-and-probability pairs. What exactly does the standard separability machinery have to assume in order to break that tie, is that assumption a substantive empirical claim about agents or something close to a stipulation about what utility means, and what would show it false?

Tell me whether the repair strategy has a stopping point. Is there any finite body of choice data that no repair could accommodate — say, an agent who faces the same feasible set twice with no relevant change in her circumstances and who chooses differently in a way that is not noise but tracks the order of presentation? If there is no such body of data, say so plainly, and then tell me whether that insulation is a defect here. The three commitments are of different kinds — metaphysical, conceptual, methodological — and I suspect the honest answer differs across them, and that it matters whether a claim of this sort was ever in the business of risking empirical refutation.

Give me also what the view is stuck with. If it is right, then the use of willingness to pay in cost-benefit analysis is not a rough proxy for benefit that we should apologize for; it is constitutive of benefit, and there is no deeper notion of advantage that such an analysis could be failing to track. Is that consequence one I should be embarrassed by? What else does the package commit me to that a critic could make unacceptable?

Two constraints on your answer. First, for each defeater, say whether a defender of representationalism would herself concede it as counting against the view, or would dismiss it as smuggling in a rival's notion of welfare — I want the concessions, not just the criticisms. Second, and this is the part I care about most: for each defeater, note whether it would also count against the main rivals — informed-desire accounts, hedonic or mental-state accounts, objective-list accounts — if it were true. A consideration that cuts against every account of welfare alike is not evidence against mine, and I will treat it as noise.

Rank what you find from fatal to merely awkward, flag anything that has arguably already arrived in the choice data, and tell me which single development would move you, if you held the view. For my part, the thing I would find most persuasive is an argument that the repair strategy is guided by something the theory officially denies exists — that the repairs are not neutral bookkeeping but are answerable to a distinction between the agent's preferences and her good.
