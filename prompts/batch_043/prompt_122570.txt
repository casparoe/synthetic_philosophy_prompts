hey — I'm on the clinical AI governance committee at my hospital (I'm the quality-improvement lead, not a data person, and I think I got put on the thing because it meets at 7am and nobody else volunteered). We have to make a call next month on whether to renew the contract for an early-warning score that pings the rapid-response team when it thinks a ward patient is deteriorating. It's been live for 14 months on two general-medicine wards, half the committee wants to keep it because the charge nurses like it, and the alternative is going back to a paper chart that everyone fills in ten minutes before the end of the shift.

The validation package the vendor sent us is what I can't make up my mind about — I can't tell if its circular or if I'm just being paranoid. Their headline number is that in retrospective data the score predicts documented clinical deterioration with an AUC around 0.87. But the nursing notes are where deterioration comes from, and ever since go-live the charting system fires a documentation prompt whenever the alert fires, so flagged patients get deterioration written up more thoroughly than unflagged ones. Their second argument is stability: consecutive four-hour scores on the same patient correlate at 0.94, performance is nearly identical across the two sites, and it barely buddges under resampling — they call this reproducibility and say it's independent confirmation, because each new score is computed from new vitals and new labs, so if the model were junk its outputs wouldn't line up with each other. And they point out that our rapid-response nurses say the alerts catch things early, though when pressed those same nurses say they'd have noticed some of those patients anyway.

Our staff ethicist, who did a philosophy PhD before she changed careers, says this is straight-up the bootstrapping problem and the problem of easy knowledge: you can't credential a source using that source's own outputs, and a gas gauge stuck on full agrees with itself perfectly too. I'm mostly convinced but not entirely, and two things keep bugging me. First, the model at 8am isn't literally the same evidential source as the model at noon, so is one source really grading itself here, or is there something to the vendor's point? Second, we do have outcomes nobody writes: 30-day mortality, unplanned ICU transfer, arrest calls. The catch is that they're rare, and if the alert works the patient doesn't arrest — so the model's successes erase the very outcomes we'd score it on, which seems like it could make a working model look useless or a useless model look fine, and I don't know which way that cuts.

Can you draft the memo I'll circulate before the meeting? It needs a concrete recommendation — renew, renew with conditions, or let it lapse — plus a timeline and a set of criteria the vendor could actually meet in the next six months, so that if we say no they have something specific to come back with rather than just a vague "more data please." And please take the stability argument seriously rather than only saying that agreeing with yourself isn't evidence: I want the strongest version of why self-agreement might count here and the strongest version of why it doesn't, because that's the bit I'm going to get grilled on.
