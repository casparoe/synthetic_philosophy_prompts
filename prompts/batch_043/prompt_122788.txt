I am drafting the normative section of a civic-education framework for a ministry working group, and before I look at any candidate theory of patriotism I want the specification rather than the theories. Please write me the list of requirements that an account of the moral standing of patriotic attachment would have to meet in order to be usable for my purpose. That purpose is narrow and I want the list built for it: adequate to decide whether, and by what means, a democratic state may deliberately cultivate attachment to country in a captive audience of pupils who have not chosen to be there. I need the standard, not a survey.

For each requirement: one sentence stating it; the case it must handle or the consideration behind it; whether you take it to be non-negotiable for this purpose or a demand that one camp imposes and another is entitled to refuse; and what accepting it would rule out. Where a single putative requirement is really two, split it.

The cases I want used as tests rather than as ornament: a plurinational state several of whose constituent peoples regard the political unit as not theirs; a nation with no state, and the emigrant who has not been back in thirty years; a polity that has peacefully ceased to exist, loved by the grandchildren of its citizens; a reformer whose object is the country's unfulfilled aspirations rather than its present condition; a country whose present prosperity depends on an ongoing wrong it will not acknowledge; someone who judges, on the evidence, that her country is the worst in the world and who nonetheless cares about it; a public official who breaks the law out of loyalty to the country; the position of the state, which is to be both the teacher of the attitude and its beneficiary; and the position of the pupil, who is to have the attitude inculcated and is yet supposed to have it genuinely, as a virtue rather than as a conditioned habit.

The distinctions I want the theory unable to collapse: country, nation, state, government, and compatriots; loving a country and approving of it; being permitted to weight compatriots more heavily and being required to; the disposition and its public expression; patriotism's value and patriotism's effects on inquiry into the national past; intrinsic and instrumental value; and the content of the attitude and its source, whether chosen, inherited, or taught.

Then flag any two requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say whether the conflict indicts the specification or is the first real result about the phenomenon. Name the single requirement that does the most work in excluding candidates. And — briefly, three or four lines each, since the criteria are the deliverable and the scoring is not — score three views against the list: patriotism as loyalty to a political community's constitutive values rather than to a nation; patriotism as a partly non-voluntary, identity-constituting partiality of the same general shape as familial love; and patriotism as worth having only instrumentally, as the affective condition of redistributive solidarity.

Where a requirement rests on a contested empirical claim, mark it as such and say which way the claim would have to come out. Do not survey the literature and do not attribute the views; I have the readings in front of me.
