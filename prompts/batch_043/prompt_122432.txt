Say you're a historian in the 2150s working the Halcyon archive. Halcyon was a consumer tech company from the 2020s. Basically everything internal got auto-deleted on a rolling schedule — internal chat gone in 30 days, most docs in three years — except whatever got swept up in litigation holds. Those holds were run by outside counsel using keyword lists, and they froze whatever the keywords hit. On top of that, the deletion logs themselves survived: timestamps, participants, channel names, and message lengths for ~99% of what got destroyed, but no contents. So what you actually have is: stuff lawyers thought might be discoverable, whatever employees happened to forward to personal drives that later ended up in public collections, some bug-related accidents, and a giant pile of metadata about the rest.

Here's the claim I care about. Call it H: before the v3 launch, at least one person at Halcyon told the CEO or a VP with launch authority, in a meeting, that the recomendation system was causing documented harm to teenagers.

There is no surviving record of any such meeting. Not in the held material, not in the personal drives, and nothing in the deletion logs that looks like it either.

My supervisor reads that as straight evidence of absence and puts P(H) at about 0.2. I'm at about 0.6 — my feeling is the selection procedure is so tangled up with the hypothesis that the silence carries almost no information, and maybe even points the other way.

Give me your own number, but I mostly want the decomposition: what sub-questions does it depend on, a rough probability and one line of justification for each, and how they combine. I'd guess the relevant ones are something like the base rate of such a meeting happening at all given the product was in fact causing harm, whether a meeting like that generates any written trace in the first place as opposed to being a conversation in a room, whether the hold keywords would have caught it if it did, and whether the fact that meetings leave traces in calendars is enough to make the deletion logs informative. Use whatever terms you think actually belong instead. Then: which sub-question is your final number most sensitive to, and which one do you think my supervisor and I most likely disagree about. And if you could only get one more artifact out of the archive, which one would move you most.
