Write a piece of sustained bureaucratic satire: a form letter from a government agency that has fully implemented content externalism and now adjudicates what its citizens' words and thoughts mean.

The letter should be issued by something like the Office of Content Determination, Department of Environmental Reference, and addressed to an individual applicant whose file has been reviewed. Format it as an official memo: a letterhead with an agency title and a deadpan motto of your own invention, a "Re:" line, numbered sections, at least one bracketed field left for the clerk to fill in, and a footer with an appeals deadline. Maximum 550 words, not counting the header and footer.

The comedy has to come from getting the externalism right, not from inventing a straw version of it. Work in, without ever lecturing or naming the view as a thesis:

- the Twin Earth point that a molecule-for-molecule duplicate standing on a planet whose colourless, odourless drinkable liquid has a different microstructure is thinking a different thought, and that no amount of intracranial inspection could reveal this, so the agency's investigation consists of plumbing surveys, soil samples and expert panels rather than anything done to the applicant;
- the Burge-style social case, in paraphrase: an applicant who sincerely applies a word to an ailment in a part of the body where that ailment cannot occur is ruled to have a false belief, not a different concept, because the reference was fixed for them by the community's experts;
- the consequence that the agency, not the applicant, has the last word on what the applicant meant, and that an appeal is therefore decided by the same procedure being appealed;
- and one hard case of the agency's own making: an applicant whose household liquid was swapped imperceptibly over roughly a decade, so that the agency must decide whether the applicant's term changed reference mid-life or the applicant silently acquired a new concept, with a fee schedule keyed to whichever ruling it reaches.

Additional constraints: reach the famous slogan about meanings not being located in the head only in the agency's own administrative phrasing — do not quote anyone verbatim. Keep the tone flat, procedural and sympathetic throughout; no winking, no punchline that breaks character. A reader uneasy about externalism should feel why, without the letter ever saying so.
