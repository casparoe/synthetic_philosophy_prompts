hey — I'm putting together a handout for a modal epistemology reading group I'm running next term (advanced undergrads plus a few grad students), and what I want is a decision tree that walks someone from "I have no idea whether conceivability tells us anything about possibility" to a named position, without ever asking them which -ism they like.

Leaves I need covered: modal skepticism (conceivability carries basically no modal weight), the Kripkean line where apparent conceivability gets defeated by a posteriori necessity, a two-dimensionalist line where ideal primary conceivability really is a guide and the Hesperus/Phosphorus-ish cases get handled by the as-actual vs. counterfactual split, a modest modal-rationalist line where conceivability is only defeasible evidence with no constitutive link, and at least one localist option where the bridge principle works for some subject matters (consciousness, say) but not others (chemistry, math).

Two nodes I care about, because the usual versions of this sort of thing seem to skip them. First, an asymmetry node: lots of people are fine with conceiving P being evidence that P is possible but not with failing to conceive P being evidence that P is impossible, and I don't want the tree forcing a symmetric verdict. Second — this one's my own invention, call it "possibility drift": suppose we found that sustained a priori reflection reliably expands the set of scenarios subjects can't rule out, and that the expansion tracks reasoning time rather than the scenarios' actual modal status. I want that as a branch point, and I want it to separate views on which idealization is supposed to be truth-conducive (so the finding is a defeater) from views on which ideal conceivability is more or less defined as unchallengeability under ideal reflection (so the finding is either no threat or conceptually excluded).

Build it from the logical space first — the questions and the answer-combinations they allow — then locate the actual positions in that space, note any combination nobody seems to occupy and say whether that's because it's incoherent or just unattractive, and flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't separate instead of inventing a distinction. Every node should be a question someone could answer without knowing where it leads, and please use at least one consciousness case and at least one identity case as illustrations along the way.
