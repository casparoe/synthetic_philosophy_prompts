I'm a graduate student in a seminar on theories of well-being, writing my term paper on the heterogeneity objection to hedonism about welfare: the charge that the pleasures of eating a peach, solving a proof, and finally scratching an itch share no single item, so that hedonism either picks out no real property or quietly collapses into a list theory. By "fatal to hedonism" I mean something specific: that someone who fully understood the objection and the best available replies could no longer reasonably accept hedonism as the correct account of welfare. My credence that the objection is fatal in that sense is about 0.3; a classmate's is about 0.7.

Please give me your own number, decomposed rather than bare. I'd like these terms, and any you think I've left out:

1. How likely is it that there is in fact no single felt quality — no introspectively identifiable hedonic tone — shared by all and only pleasures? Read this as a claim about what introspection and what we know about valence and reward psychology give us, not as a claim about our words for pleasure.
2. Conditional on that, how likely is it that hedonism is committed to there being such a quality — i.e., that the objection's unity requirement is the right reading of the theory — as opposed to hedonism being able to retreat to a functional or dispositional characterization: pleasure as the property of being in a state one is presently disposed to sustain or renew, or valence treated as a determinable with several determinates, unified by causal and motivational role rather than by how it feels?
3. How likely is it that such a retreat is both extensionally adequate and still recognizably hedonism, rather than an ad hoc relabeling that abandons whatever made the theory attractive, or one that has to outsource the real work to a theory of the attitudes and so becomes a desire theory in disguise?
4. How much of the objection's force survives the fact that its rivals look structurally parallel: the desire theorist owes us an account of what all desire-satisfactions have in common, the objective-list theorist of what makes the entries on the list the entries. Is this shared burden a defence of hedonism, a reason to become a pluralist about welfare, or merely a tu quoque that leaves the original problem standing?
5. How much of what travels under the name of the heterogeneity problem is really a problem about commensurability — whether pleasures sit on one scale, so that some quantity of the pleasure of a proof can be traded against some quantity of the pleasure of a peach — and is meeting that demand something hedonism needs in order to be a theory of welfare at all?

Combine the terms into a single number, tell me which term your figure is most sensitive to, and tell me where you think the gap between me at 0.3 and my classmate at 0.7 most likely lives. Finally, name the one argument or finding, conceptual or empirical, that would move your number the most.
