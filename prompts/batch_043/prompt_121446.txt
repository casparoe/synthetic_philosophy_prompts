John Searle died in 2019 and therefore never encountered today's large language models. His Chinese Room argument holds that a system that merely manipulates formal symbols according to rules does not thereby understand their meaning: syntax alone is not sufficient for semantic understanding, and simulating a cognitive process is not the same as reproducing its causal basis. Suppose we could show him a modern neural language model trained on vast amounts of text and able to produce fluent, context-sensitive replies. How would he most likely extend the argument to such a system? Would he grant that it understands language, or would he say it remains a merely syntactic simulation? How would he reply to the claim that the trained network as a whole understands even if its individual operations are formal? And would features such as its scale, its statistical training on human text, or its lack of a biological body make any relevant difference to his original reasoning?
