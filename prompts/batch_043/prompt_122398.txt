You are a political philosopher who specializes in the ethics of membership, territorial rights, and freedom of association. Please think carefully, and before you give me your verdict I would find it genuinely useful if you first set out, in two or three lines apiece, the candidate principles that could jointly explain the track record below — I want to see the whole space of rules that fits our precedents, not only the one you happen to favour — and then run each of them against the new case.

I am the junior, non-voting secretary of our faculty's Working Group on Membership and Exclusion, which for three years has issued short advisory opinions on who may exclude whom, and I have to draft the opinion on a new petition before our next meeting. I want to know what consistency with our own record actually requires, because I suspect our precedents fit more than one principle and I would rather not smuggle in my own politics; my private view is that the petitioner should win, and I would like you to argue against me if the precedents point the other way. Compressed, the opinions so far: a contemplative religious order that refuses to admit a postulant who rejects its vows — exclusion permissible; a private household that declines to put up a stranger for the night — permissible; a private hospital's emergency department that turns away a patient in acute danger of death who cannot pay — impermissible; a company that owns every dwelling, the only school and the only clinic in an isolated mining town, and that evicts a family for union organizing — impermissible; a small farming cooperative with a language-revival charter that admits only members willing to conduct its business in the endangered language, although it is by far the cheapest housing in the district — permissible, but only because it publishes transparent admission criteria and lets non-members lease land at the market rate; a wealthy coastal state that refuses admission to families from a drowning island state whose submersion its own emissions materially contributed to — impermissible, five to four, the majority relying on dire need plus causal contribution and the dissent on collective self-determination; a large public discussion forum that expels a member for sustained bad-faith disruption — permissible.

The new petition concerns Meridian, a privately built and operated orbital habitat with about two thousand residents. Meridian's charter requires every adult resident to re-affirm its mutual-aid compact every five years, and the operator has declined to renew the residency of a woman born and raised there who now refuses to re-affirm and publicly campaigns against the operator's governance; her partner and child remain residents in good standing. Meridian is the only inhabited structure at its location, it runs all life support, sets the rules of daily life, and operates the only dispute-resolution mechanism available; roughly forty percent of residents are not employees of the operator and never signed anything beyond a standard berth licence. This is not a death sentence: Meridian can, at roughly half a percent of its annual operating budget, fund her transfer to a small research station a few weeks' transit away that has agreed to take her, but she cannot pay for it herself and no state has accepted a duty to receive her. Our chair's circulated draft argues that the mining-town opinion was really about retaliation for protected activity rather than about the town's state-like character, and so does not govern Meridian; I cannot tell whether that is a distinction or an evasion.

So: does Meridian have the right to exclude her, and if it does, must it fund her relocation? Which of our precedents is the closest analog and why, and what principle are we committing ourselves to either way — the associational-or-ethos reading, the state-likeness reading, the no-genuine-exit reading, the dire-need reading, or something else? If our precedents genuinely underdetermine the case, say so plainly and tell me what each available decision would commit us to, including any conditional option such as exclusion permissible only if funded and phased over a stated period. Finally, name one further hypothetical case, specified in a sentence or two, that would best separate the principles you still think are in play. Please commit to a single recommendation rather than laying out the options for me to choose between, give me in one line how confident you are in it, and flag the one step in your reasoning you think most likely to be wrong, or the single further fact about Meridian that would most change your mind.
