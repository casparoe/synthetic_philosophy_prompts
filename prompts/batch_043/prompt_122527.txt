I'm in a course on 19th-century social thought and my term paper is on Kierkegaard's attack on "the public" — the idea that the public isn't a group of people at all but a phantom that nobody is, that has no situation and can't act, and that the daily press supplies with an organ, so that "someone said" gets replaced by "it is said". I want to use him for a media-critique paper, and I've been leaning on my own distinction between being *distributed to* many people and being *addressed as* a public (a book can go out to thousands and still address one you). Here's roughly where I've landed, and I want to know before I write it up whether it hangs togther:

(1) the public is essentially medium-dependent: what makes it the public rather than a crowd is that the word goes out to no one in particular and each person recieves it privately.
(2) the press is an instrument, not a cause — it doesn't create levelling, it completes it, and levelling could in principle go on without it.
(3) the public has no agency; no one is it, and nothing is really done by it.
(4) nonetheless the press really does corrupt the age: people are worse off, morally, in a press-saturated age than they'd otherwise be.
(5) individuals stay fully answerable — hiding in "it is said" is something you do, which is why the crowd is untruth.
(6) the remedy is the single individual, standing alone before God, and that's a status everyone has equally, no one more than anyone else.
(7) levelling is bad because it reduces everyone to an abstract universal, the "someone", the average, and destroys what's concrete and distinctive.
(8) a case verdict I'm confident about: a departmental reply-all email thread, forty people who all know each other, each writing a slightly different version of the same safe sentence, is the public in Kierkegaard's sense, and a purer case then a newspaper.

I like all eight for different reasons and genuinely can't tell where the trouble is. Which pairs are really in tension and which only look like it, ordered by severity, what the cheapest fix is for each real one, and which single revision would clean up the most.
