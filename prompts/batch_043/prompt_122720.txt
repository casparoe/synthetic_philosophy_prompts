Hi — I need a hand with something for the graduate seminar I'm running this term. Two of my best students (one of them is a colleague who sits in) have spent two sessions going at each other over Fitch's paradox of knowability, and they've started arguing past one another badly enough that I want to force the issue: I want the document an adversarial collaboration between them would have produced, not another round of the same exchange.

Camp one, call them the Restrictionist: classical modal logic stays exactly as it is, and the knowability principle gets restricted in its range — either to the fragment of the language that doesn't contain the knowledge operator, or (they'd prefer this) to propositions that aren't self-defeating to know, so that p ∧ ¬Kp drops out automatically, since knowing it is what makes it false rather than anything about truth outrunning us. Their line is that Fitch only ever gets purchase on Moore-style propositions of the form "p and it's not known that p," and that no verificationist ever claimed those were knowable, so nothing has been conceded to the realist.

Camp two, the Repairist: the principle stays fully general in content, and the culprit is the modal-epistemic logic we wrapped around it — the step where knowing a conjunction is taken to give knowledge of each conjunct inside the scope of the possibility operator, or the move from "it's not the case that p ∧ ¬Kp can be known" to "it's not possibly knowable that p ∧ ¬Kp," or the classical last step from there being no unknown truths to every truth being known. Their line is that the whole thing is an artifact of describing a non-ideal, temporally stretched inquirer with the logic of an omniscient one.
