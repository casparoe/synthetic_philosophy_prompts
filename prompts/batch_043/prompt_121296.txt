Write a dialogue between two AI safety researchers, Rue and Ivo, who disagree about whether the debate protocol can extend human oversight to systems far smarter than the overseer.

Anchor the disagreement in one concrete task. A human judge with mathematical training but limited time must decide whether a machine-generated formal proof of a conjecture in number theory is valid. The proof has roughly a million steps; each individual step is elementary, and the judge can verify any given step, or any short chain of perhaps a dozen steps, if it is pointed out to her. But the protocol allows only about twenty rounds of questioning before the judge loses patience and rules, so no long chain of steps is ever examined in full. Rue's position is that recursive cross-examination nevertheless solves the problem: every claim can be split into sub-claims until the disagreement is reduced to a single short chain the judge can check herself, and because maintaining a lie under adversarial questioning is harder than puncturing one, the honest debater wins.

Ivo raises a specific objection he calls mosaic obfuscation. A dishonest debater can build a false argument that is locally impeccable and globally inconsistent: no individual step is wrong, and no short chain of steps is wrong, but two steps lying very far apart in the proof contradict each other. Since the debate tree only ever examines short connected chains, the contradiction never appears on any single path the judge follows; exposing it requires comparing two distant steps, and finding which pair to compare is itself as hard as checking the original proof. The dishonest debater, who built the mosaic, knows where the crack is; the honest debater must search for it and has no cheap way to do so, while the dishonest debater can always produce a fluent, locally consistent story on whatever branch is probed.

Requirements for the dialogue: both speakers should be equally competent, and neither should be a strawman. Ivo should press the objection to the point where Rue must genuinely revise the protocol rather than restate it — for example by changing what the judge samples, what each debater is required to commit to before seeing the other's claims, or what counts as winning a branch — and Rue's revision should in turn force Ivo to either sharpen the obfuscation or concede something real. Close the dialogue without settling the dispute, but have each speaker concede at least one substantive point along the way.
