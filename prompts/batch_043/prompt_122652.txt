hey — I'm putting together a one-page handout for a metaphysics reading group I run (mostly second-year grad students, a couple of brave undergrads), on the Humean vs anti-Humean fight about laws of nature. I really don't want another "Lewis says X, Armstrong says Y" summary page. What I want is a decision tree: it starts from questions somebody could answer before they've learned any of the names, and it ends at named positions. Aim for something like 9 or 10 branch nodes and 5 or 6 leaves. Hard rule: no node may just be the label fight restated — nothing like "are you a Humean?" or "do laws govern or merely describe?" Those are the slogans, not the diagnostics.

The branch questions I want built into it, roughly:

1. Can two worlds match on every particular thing that happens and still differ in their laws? (i.e. do laws supervene on the total mosaic of occurrent, non-modal matters of fact)
2. Is that mosaic allowed to contain powers and dispositions, or only categorical property-instances?
3. In the best-system story, is the tradeoff between simplicity, strength and fit supposed to be objective, or indexed to creatures like us? I have a suspicion this secretly splits the Humeans into ones who get objective laws and ones who've basicly become projectivists, and I want to see whether the tree actually shows that or whether I'm making it up.
4. Does a law explain or ground its instances, or does it just summarize them?
5. A node I made up and want stress-tested: call it bookkeeping vs steering — does the thing you're calling a law merely keep score on what happens, or does it have a hand in making it happen? My guess is it partly collapses into (1) but not entirely, and I want the tree to say exactly where it comes apart.

Use concrete cases at the nodes where they'd do work rather than abstract phrasing. Ones I like: a world containing exactly one particle, moving at constant velocity forever — is there a law there, and if so is it the same kind of thing as our laws? A world where a billion particles all happen to move in straight lines and then all stop, with nothing enforcing it. And whether one stray event on the far side of the galaxy changes what the laws are here.

Leaves I want to actually appear: Mill–Ramsey–Lewis best-system Humeanism; the Armstrong/Dretske/Tooley contingent-necessitation sort of view (nomic necessity as a relation among universals); dispositional essentialism of the Bird/Ellis kind; Maudlin-style primitivism about the nomic, where laws are just taken as basic and don't reduce to anything; and a deflationary or projectivist leaf where laws are cheap.

Also: flag at least one combination of answers that's empty — I'd guess "supervenes on the mosaic AND steers" is unoccupied, and I want to know whether that's because it's incoherent or just unfashionable — and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't genuinely separate instead of pretending it can. Finish with a small table mapping answer-patterns to leaves so someone can score themselves. thx!
