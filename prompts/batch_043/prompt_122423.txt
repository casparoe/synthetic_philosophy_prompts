I'm putting together a guest lecture for a graduate seminar on the philosophy of psychiatry, and I want to build it around Miloš Forman's 1975 film of One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest read through Ian Hacking's account of the looping effects of human kinds. I know that literature well enough not to need a summary of it: what I need from you is a close reading of the film that takes the idea seriously and is also willing to say where the film refuses it.

The material that interests me most is the ward's own classificatory machinery — the contrast between acute and chronic, between the committed and the voluntarily admitted, the disturbed ward upstairs — together with the fact that most of the men could legally walk out and do not. That looks like the purest cinematic image of a loop available anywhere: the kind is not simply imposed on them from outside, it has become the standing way they are available to themselves, and every piece of evidence the institution has about their chronicity is generated inside the apparatus that produced it. But I want you to press on three distinctions that the film lets us draw and that Hacking's own cases, fugue and the nineteenth-century mad travellers among them, do not by themselves force.

The first concerns Chief Bromden. He has taken up the classification of deaf-mute deliberately, as camouflage, and he can set it down at will; nothing in him answers to it. Is that looping at all, or is it something adjacent that the literature has not named — what I have been calling classificatory camouflage? If it is looping, then looping does not require the kind to restructure a person's self-understanding, and the picture is considerably broader than Hacking makes it. If it is not, I want the principled line: what separates adopting a kind as a way to be a person from wearing one as a disguise, and which side of that line do the men who stay voluntarily fall on?

The second is whether the film shows looping on the classifier's side of the loop. Nurse Ratched's authority is inseparable from the ward's classificatory order, and the staff read McMurphy's very sanity — his competence, his charm, his willingness to negotiate — as further confirmation of the antisocial reading they began with on arrival. I would call that pre-emptive looping: the criteria for the kind are quietly adjusted in advance, because the classifiers anticipate how the kind will be taken up and gamed. Is that a genuine addition to the account, or only the ordinary self-confirming structure of a suspicious interpretation? And does the fishing expedition tell in favour of looping at all, or merely of institutional cruelty — do the men improve because the kind has been suspended, or because the setting has?

The third is the lobotomy. It strikes me as the film's darkest comment on the loop: an intervention that fixes the person classified, producing a subject who can no longer respond to being classified, and so converting an interactive kind into something as close to an indifferent one as medicine can manage. If that is right, the film is asking whether there is something distinctively wrong with foreclosing a loop, over and above the wrong of the damage done. I would also like you to say whether the ending — Bromden's escape, his recovered voice, his size — commits the film to a pre-social self that a dynamic nominalist should reject, or whether it can be read instead as the claim that one can only move from one loop into another, so that the only question worth asking is which loops are worth having.

Finally, if looping is real, is there anything like a pro tanto duty of diagnostic abstention, a reason to withhold a kind whose uptake is likely to be harmful? The film seems to want something like that, yet Bromden's camouflage and the men who stay suggest that withholding has costs of its own.

Please write this as continuous prose, in the register of a letter to a colleague: no headings, no bullet points, no boldface, no tables, no block quotations, and nothing quoted from the film, at around a thousand words. Do not spend words summarizing the plot beyond what your argument needs, and do not give me a reading list or any citations; I want the argument itself.
