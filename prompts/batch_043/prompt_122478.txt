ive been going to this really casual reading group (mostly engineers, one guy who did a philosophy minor) and we got stuck on panpsychism. not the is-it-true part, the objection to it that people call the combination problem. my understanding so far: if every fundamental bit of the world has some tiny experience, you still owe an account of how those tiny experiences add up to the single experience im having right now, and it seems to split into a few sub-problems — why a pile of subjects would constitute one subject instead of just staying a swarm, why my experience is so coarse-grained relative to whatever is going on down at the micro level, and why experience has the particular qualities it has (colors and so on) rather than some totally different palette. one of the group also mentioned a move where the micro-subjects are supposed to be stuck together by a special relation, and the objection raised was that if the relation is just a physical or functional one, you can still seem to imagine all the micro-experiences with no macro-subject showing up — i think they had a name for that thought experiment, some kind of zombie variant.

the guy in the group who is actually into this says the whole objection is overblown, and he keeps leaning on one analogy: liquidity. no individual H2O molecule is wet or liquid, wetness isnt an extra ingredient you have to pour in, but get enough molecules together in the right arrangement and youve got a liquid, end of story. so, he says, macro-consciousness out of micro-consciousness is the same kind of emergence, nothing spooky, and the combination problem is just the general how-does-emergence-work question that everybody already lives with. the conclusion he draws is a comparative one: panpsychism is no worse off than the physicalism its competing with, so the objection does no work against it specifically.

im suspicious but i cant say exactly why, which usually means i dont understand it yet. can you audit the analogy for me?

- what does it quietly import that the target doesnt have? my gut is that liquidity is a dispositional or behavioural property — its about what the stuff does, so once youve fixed the microphysics and the arrangement theres nothing left over — whereas the whole point of the micro-experiences is that theyre not like that. is that right, and is it the load-bearing disanalogy or a red herring?
- does his conclusion survive? note that his conclusion is comparative (no worse off than physicalism), so it could survive even if the analogy is junk, for some other reason. i want to know whether the analogy is carrying it or the conclusion is standing on its own.
