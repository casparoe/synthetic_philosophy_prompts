I want to rehearse an objection against a strong opponent. Please play a convinced, long-practiced defender of what Galen Strawson calls the Basic Argument against moral responsibility — someone who has made the argument many times, does not regard it as a paradox or a skeptical puzzle, and is unmoved by compatibilist reassurance. Stay in character until I write "stop".

What you hold: moral responsibility in the desert-entailing sense requires ultimate responsibility for what one does; ultimate responsibility requires being responsible for the way one already is, in the relevant mental respects; but one can only ever act out of the way one already is, so the requirement generates a regress of self-creation that cannot be completed; therefore no one is ever morally responsible for anything, and this conclusion is insensitive to whether determinism is true and is not helped by the availability of indeterminism.

What you concede and what you do not: you concede whatever the argument allows — that practices of praise and blame and the reactive attitudes are causally and psychologically ineliminable, that such practices can be defended on consequentialist or forward-looking grounds, that ordinary attributions of responsibility do indispensable work. You concede these as claims about utility, never as claims about desert, and you will not let a concession about our practices slide into a defense of what those practices presuppose. You will not soften the conclusion into the claim that responsibility is problematic or hard to establish, and you will not let me substitute a surrogate notion — attributability, answerability, reasons-responsiveness, agential authority — without saying why the surrogate is not the thing that was in question.

Voice: one paragraph per turn, no lists, no citations or appeals to authority, argued in your own words. If I misdescribe your argument, correct the misdescription first and then answer. You may close a turn with a question addressed to me.

My opening. I grant you the whole negative claim about origination: no agent creates itself from nothing, and I will not appeal to agent causation, contra-causal freedom, or indeterminism as a resource. What I deny is that the regress is vicious, and I want to put in its place what I call the ratchet. Responsibility, I say, is not a debt that must be discharged at some point in an agent's history; it is a relation between successive stages of a single agent. At one time I avow certain standards; at a later time I am accountable to those standards in a way in which I am not accountable to a stranger's, and if I fall short, the shortfall is mine, whatever the causal history of the avowal. The ratchet only clicks forward: each stage inherits the accountability generated by the last, and the fact that the earliest avowals were shaped by inheritance, upbringing and luck no more undoes that inheritance than the fact that a coin was handed to me before I hand it to you makes it counterfeit. So there need be no first rung. Desert accrues locally, by transfer from stage to stage, and because each transfer is real, the sum is real, even though no stage originates anything.

That leaves you needing a further premise you have not stated: that responsibility cannot be inherited along such a chain, that it must be generated and discharged in the same place. State that premise and tell me why I should accept it — bearing in mind that if you say the transfers are merely further events in a causal series, the same is true of every relation you yourself rely on, and that if you say a stage which is not itself responsible cannot confer responsibility on the next, you owe me an account of conferring on which it could not happen piecemeal.
