im putting together a last evening on Being and Nothingness for a few friends — the topic is bad faith — and I want to close it with a sestina rather than a summary. Six end-words: APRON, HAND, STONE, MIRROR, FAITH, VERTIGO, rotated properly through six stanzas of six lines, then a three-line envoi that calls all six back and closes on VERTIGO.

The form should be doing the work: the same six words keep coming back in new arrangements and nothing is ever used up, which is what bad faith is for Sartre — not one lie you tell yourself and settle into, but a project you have to remake over and over, a wavering self-persuasion. Its not even self-deception in the ordinary sense, since a lie needs one person who knows the truth and another whos taken in, and here theyre the same; so its a faith. Someone who knows the text should be able to tell the two flights apart by the images: the waiter playing at being a waiter, who collapses himself into his facticity (I just am this, I cant help it, a thing among things), and the woman who leaves her hand lying in her companions as if it werent hers, who flees into pure transcendence (nothing Ive done is really me, I hover above all of it).

Dont give me a triumphant last line about finally being authentic — the ideal of sincerity is itself bad faith, and the spirit of seriousness, taking values as if they were written into the world, is the other way of ducking out. Better if it ends still running, with the vertigo of freedom being the thing its running from.
