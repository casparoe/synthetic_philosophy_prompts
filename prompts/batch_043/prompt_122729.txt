I'm in a philosophy of language reading group and we did the Twin Earth stuff last week, so it was fresh. Sam is a hardline internalist: meaning is whatever's in the head, the Twin Earth stories are a parlor trick, end of story. I kept pushing back, and then I realized my own verdicts definately don't add up, so now I want help finding out what principle, if any, is behind them.

Here's where I ended up, going case by case, just reporting gut reactions. (1) Classic Twin Earth: the stuff in the lakes and taps there is XYZ, not H2O, superficially indistinguishable from water, and I'm happy to say Oscar and Twin-Oscar mean different things by 'water' and are thinking different thoughts. My gut there is that 'water' still means water, or at least that nothing important about meaning has changed, and Sam says that's flatly inconsistent with (1) unless I can say which microphysical differences matter and which don't. Is there one account of content determination that gets all seven, or close to it?
