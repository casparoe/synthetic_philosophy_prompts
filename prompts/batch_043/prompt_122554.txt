I sit on a technical advisory group that a national statistics office has convened to answer two questions: what should this country's headline measure of national wellbeing be, and should the government formally adopt a target of reaching the top ten of the international happiness league table within a decade. I'm the philosopher on the group, I have to put a two-page note in front of the others in a fortnight, and before I write it I want my own position checked for consistency rather than defended. Here is where I've landed, item by item.

(a) The quantity worth measuring is people's own evaluative judgment about how their lives are going — the standard 0–10 ladder item, or something close to it — and not an objective list of goods, and not aggregated moment-to-moment affect. My reason: how well a life is going is constitutively a matter of the subject's own point of view, so there is no better evidence about it than the person's own verdict, and no deeper fact for a list of objective conditions to be a better measure of.

(b) The headline number should be the plain arithmetic mean of those judgments — not the share of the population above some sufficiency threshold, not an inequality-adjusted or prioritarian-weighted mean — because the mean is the honest aggregate, and anything we want to say about the shape of the distribution can be published next to it as a separate series.

(c) I hold a priority view about justice: gains to the worst-off count for more than equal-sized gains to the comfortable, and I want that to show up in how policy options are appraised.

(d) It is legitimate for a government to make league-table position an explicit, publicly owned objective with a named minister accountable for it. The alternative — improving wellbeing while officially not caring how we rank — strikes me as either disingenuous or a way of avoiding accountability.

(e) The measure has to be something a government can actually move and that can be tracked at least annually; a quantity that only responds on a generational timescale is not fit to be a target, whatever its philosophical credentials.

(f) Self-reported life evaluations are systematically distorted by adaptation. People living in chronically harsh conditions renormalise their expectations downward and report a 7 for a life that a better-off person would call a 4, so raw means flatter deprived populations and part of the international ordering is an artefact of who has learned to expect less.

(g) The resulting comparability problem should be handled by measurement technique — for instance anchoring methods in which respondents also rate short hypothetical case descriptions, so that we can tell whether two populations are using the scale the same way — and not by mixing objective conditions into the index itself.

(h) No weighting scheme should be imposed across countries that tells us how much income counts for relative to health, freedom or social trust. Any such weighting imports a contested view of the good life into what is supposed to be a neutral comparison.

Two places where I feel the strain, though I may be wrong about both: I worry that (d) sits badly with (a), and I have been told that (b) betrays (c). But I suspect the real fault line is somewhere else in the set, and I would rather you found it than confirmed my hunches.

What I want from you is an audit, not a survey of the field and not a reading list — I will supply references myself. Go through the eight commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look as though they are, ordered by severity rather than listed as equals. For each real tension, say precisely what does the work: what exactly has to give, what the cheapest revision is, and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. Where a technique or distinction looks like it repairs a tension but does not, say so and say why. If one single revision would clean up more than one tension at once, name it. And if there is a commitment in the set that I have not noticed is doing load-bearing work, or a tension I have not mentioned, flag that too. Finally: given the audit, tell me what the two-page note to the group should actually recommend about the headline measure and about the top-ten target, and what the strongest objection to that recommendation will be.
