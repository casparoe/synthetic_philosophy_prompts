The question (25 marks):

Throughout, a "policy" for a UDT agent is a function from the agent's total observations to its available actions, and UDT ranks such functions by expected utility computed with the agent's prior rather than its posterior.

(a) State what updateless decision theory prescribes in each of the following, with one sentence of justification each. (9 marks)
(i) Newcomb's problem with a predictor who is accurate 99% of the time. If it predicted you would take only the opaque box, that box contains $1,000,000; if it predicted you would take both, it contains nothing. The transparent box always contains $1,000, and you keep whatever is in the boxes you take.
(ii) The counterfactual mugging. A perfectly trustworthy predictor flips a fair coin out of your sight. If it lands heads it asks you for $100, and you either pay or don't. If it lands tails it pays you $1,000 iff it predicts that you would have paid had the coin landed heads. You are then truthfully told that the coin landed tails and that no request will be made.
(iii) A one-shot prisoner's dilemma against an exact copy of your own decision algorithm, running on different hardware but given the same inputs as you.

(b) State the principle that unifies these three verdicts, and explain what exactly the agent is supposed not to be updating. (6 marks)

(c) Omega, who is fully trustworthy, tells you: "I flipped a fair coin and I am now truthfully telling you the result. If it came up heads, I have placed in the next room an exact copy of your decision algorithm, running on the same inputs as you, and the two of you will now play a one-shot prisoner's dilemma against each other: (C,C) = 3,3; (D,D) = 1,1; (C,D) = 0,5; (D,C) = 5,0. If it came up tails, I have placed in the next room a stranger who attended the same unusual decision-theory boarding school as you. Because of that shared upbringing there is a 90% chance they choose whatever you choose — but this is purely a matter of common cause: the upbringing is over and done with, their algorithm shares no code with yours, and nothing you decide determines, or is determined by, or causally influences what they decide." Omega then says: tails. (10 marks)
(i) What does UDT do?
(ii) Would it do something different if Omega had said heads?
(iii) If your answers to (i) and (ii) differ, explain why that is not just ordinary Bayesian updating — say what the agent is still being updateless about.

The student's answer:

(a) (i) One-box. The money is already in the box either way, but a UDT agent doesn't reason causally; it asks which policy has the higher expected utility from the prior, and since the predictor is 99% accurate, one-boxing is near-conclusive evidence that the million is there, so the policy "always one-box" looks far better ex ante than "always two-box".
(ii) Pay the $100. Scored from the prior, the policy "pay if asked" is worth ½(−$100) + ½($1,000) = $450 and "refuse if asked" is worth $0, so the agent pays. The fact that it already knows the coin came up tails is irrelevant: UDT ranks policies, not actions, and it ranks them with its prior.
(iii) Cooperate. The copy is running your decision procedure on the same inputs, so its output is guaranteed to match yours; you are therefore choosing between (C,C) = 3 and (D,D) = 1, and 3 beats 1. This is really just Hofstadter's superrationality — the idea that rational agents faced with the same problem will arrive at the same answer and are entitled to rely on that — made precise.

(b) The unifying principle is that the agent picks the policy maximising expected utility computed with its prior probabilities, where the relevant dependence is logical rather than causal: it asks what would follow from the output of its own decision procedure, and so from the outputs of every sufficiently similar decision procedure. "Updateless" means the agent never conditions on its evidence. It scores whole policies from the standpoint of the prior, then executes whatever the winning policy prescribes, so its uncertainty stays frozen at the prior.

(c) (i) Cooperate. Two reasons. First, the agent is updateless, so the fact that Omega has told it "tails" cannot enter the calculation: it has to pick the action that is best from the ex ante point of view, and ex ante it might well be facing its own copy, where cooperating gets 3 rather than 1. Second, even in the tails branch there is a 90% chance the stranger does what you do, so cooperating is strong evidence that they cooperate: 0.9 × 3 + 0.1 × 0 = 2.7, against 0.9 × 1 + 0.1 × 5 = 1.4 for defecting. Both routes agree.
(ii) Heads: also cooperate, since the copy is running your algorithm and you get (C,C) = 3. Same answer either way.
(iii) Nothing to explain, because the answers don't differ — and that is the whole point of being updateless. The ranking of the options is fixed once and for all at the prior; learning which branch you are in changes your beliefs about the world but not what you ought to do. If learning it did change what you ought to do, UDT would just be evidential decision theory with extra steps.
