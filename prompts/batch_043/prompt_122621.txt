The four cases.

Case B, the selection-filtered report. I am told that the chance of A is 0.7, and I separately learn that the bureau issuing such reports only ever reports chances for events from a large pool that in fact occurred, with reported chances spanning the whole range. The report is therefore evidence about the outcome, not merely about the chance, and the two cannot be separated. My framework handles this by correcting the likelihood for the sampling design. I want to know whether the admissibility clause can handle a case where the chance report and the outcome information are the same signal.

Case C, the unrepeatable event. A one-off physical system, never observed before and never to be observed again: physics hands me a chance for its decaying within the hour, but there is no sequence I can embed it in, no exchangeability, and every reference class I might choose gives a different number. Case D, undermining.
