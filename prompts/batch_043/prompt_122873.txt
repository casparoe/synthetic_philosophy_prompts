I am editing a manuscript on reference whose author has already committed herself to verdicts on seven cases: (1) on the twin planet where the watery stuff is XYZ, her duplicate's word refers to XYZ and not to water; (2) a speaker who misapplies a technical term but defers to experts refers to what the experts refer to; (3) a theorist's term whose associated description is largely false but which latches onto a real kind refers to that kind; (4) a term for which nothing plays the posited role has no referent; (5) a creature with no language and no community, whose internal state is caused only by water and guides its behaviour accordingly, has states with the content water; (6) a speaker transported gradually to the twin planet has a term whose reference shifts with her current surroundings; (7) a speaker who acquires 'gold' purely from books and pictures, never encountering any, refers to gold.

The new case she is drafting is Ariel. Ariel is a simulated agent whose entire life lies inside a high-fidelity simulation and who has no idea of this; within it there is exactly one clear drinkable liquid, which everyone including the simulated chemists calls 'water', and which the simulation's own best physics describes with a microstructure other than H₂O. Ariel has never had causal contact with H₂O, with XYZ, or with any liquid outside the simulation: her teachers, her samples, and her linguistic community are all simulated. The simulation was written by engineers who modelled that liquid on real water, though they never speak to her. Assume for the sake of the case that Ariel is a genuine subject with beliefs and a language.

What will she say Ariel's word refers to, and why? I care less about the answer than about the rule the earlier verdicts are evidence of: state the rule her record suggests she is running, give at least one other rule that fits all seven verdicts and comes apart here, say what each predicts about Ariel, identify which of the seven verdicts does the most work in narrowing the field, and design the variant of Ariel's case — one change to the set-up — that would best separate the surviving rules.
