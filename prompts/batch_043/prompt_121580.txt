Hi — I work on the data team of a small consortium that publishes a shared authority file for museum and archive catalogs: people, corporate bodies, places, works, one record per entity, everybody downstream pulls from our URIs. What actually fills our week is merge proposals — two records that might be the same person, firm, or place, and someone has to decide whether they become one record or just get linked. It's maybe 20–40 of these a week, they're triaged by rotating catalogers and two interns who have about five to ten minutes each and no research time, and anything irreversible has to be signed off by our senior editor, who is a career librarian, extremely patient, and will not read a line of philosophy. Bad merges are quietly catastrophic because other institutions have already copied the URI and we can't recall it. I also suspect part of the problem is that we currently just use owl:sameAs for every kind of connection we make.

I did a philosophy minor years ago and halfway through a lunch argument with a colleague I dredged up Frege's puzzle about identity — the morning star/evening star sort of case, where an identity statement can be a genuine discovery and tell you something, while the trivial self-identity tells you nothing, even though both are about one and the same thing. My colleague says this has nothing whatsoever to do with our work: if two records refer to the same thing, obviously they should be one record. I think he's wrong, but I can't turn the intution into something my team could actually run, and "be careful about sense and reference" is not instructions anyone can follow. So what I want is a procedure: an ordered checklist in words that a cataloger with zero philosophy can run on a merge proposal in under ten minutes, with what counts as a sufficient answer to each question, clear STOP-with-a-no points, and clear "hand this to a human because it's a values call rather than a fact" points.

Some things I think have to survive the translation, though please organize it however is actually right rather than following my list: identity always being identity *under a sortal* (the same person, the same legal entity, the same continuing brand, the same city, the same river — so if no single sortal covers both records, that's a relation, not a merge); a distinction between terms that rigidly tag one thing whether or not any description happens to fit (a personal name, a registry number) and flaccid role-descriptions like "the director", "the artist of this work", "the firm then trading as X", which can be true of different things at different times and probably shouldn't be merged with anything, only time- or role-indexed; and the case where an identification is true but definitely informative — a notname finally attached to a named painter, a pen name, a maiden name — where I take it the merge is fine at the level of reference but rewriting every older sentence that used the other name is not. For that last part I want an actual test my catalogers can run in their heads for when swapping one term for another is unsafe: belief reports, quotations, attributions, "the artist of", modal and temporal contexts, and cases where the source was simply mistaken or the thing referred to doesn't exist. Give me a small set of test sentences with the verdict for each, and say which verdicts are judgment calls dressed up as rules. Also tell me plainly what the procedure cannot do, and separately flag where a wrong merge fails *silently* downstream instead of loudly — that's the bit I actually lie awake about.

Then please run it on three cases. (1) A proposal to merge a record for an anonymized fifteenth-century painter known only by a conventional nickname — say "Master of the Blue Annunciation", I'm making that up — with a record for a named artist, on the strength of one recent attribution that maybe half the field accepts. (2) A proposal to merge the record for a manufacturing company that was absorbed into a larger firm in a merger and whose name lived on afterwards as a brand: same entity or not, and if "not", what do we put there instead? (3) The one I already know you'll annoy me on: our objects database has a painting whose own historic catalog card attributes it to a famous old master, current scholarship gives it to a pupil in his workshop, and the marketing team wants it merged into the famous painter's record so it surfaces in search on the site. If the procedure says no, I need one paragraph I can paste to the marketing people explaining why, and it can't use the words "sense", "reference", or "opaque". Long answers and bullet points very welcome, and please don't assume I remember any of the terminology.
