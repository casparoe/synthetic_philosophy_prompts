I'm putting together the final for my upper-level formal epistemology seminar (mostly advanced undergrads, a couple of MA students sitting in) and I need one genuinely hard question on Jeffrey conditionalization. Last year's version was a disaster in the sense that it was too easy: everyone just wrote down P'(H) = Σᵢ P(H|Eᵢ)qᵢ, said the word "rigidity," noted that the rule doesn't commute, and walked out with an A. So I want a question where that package of facts is at best a B, maybe a C. What I'm after is the crux underneath it. Two things I keep circling around. First: is Jeffrey's rule actually a new, non-Bayesian rule of inference, or is it ordinary conditioning in a bigger space — I have a feeling you can always cook up some extra "virtual" evidence proposition in an extended algebra that reproduces the Jeffrey shift, and that when you look at what has to be true of that proposition, rigidity turns out to be a screening-off / conditional independence assumption rather than a logical truth, but I can't quite turn that into a question rather than a lecture. Second: the non-commutativity. I want them to actually compute a two-partition case in both orders, see the numbers differ, and then say whether that's a defect in the rule or a sign that a description like "your new probability for Gⱼ is rⱼ" is leaving something out — and if so, what extra information would pin the result down.
