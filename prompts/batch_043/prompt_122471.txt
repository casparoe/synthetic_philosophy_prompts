I convene a fortnightly metaphysics reading group — mostly graduate students, plus two philosophers from a nearby department — and this term we are working through grounding and fundamentality. The frustration is specific: everyone can recite that grounding is meant to be non-causal, hyperintensional, and a matter of metaphysical explanation among facts, but the moment we ask a sharper question the room splits three or four ways on what *fundamental* is even supposed to apply to. So for the final session I want to hand out a self-sorting instrument: a decision tree that starts from commitments students can recognise in themselves and ends at a named position, with roughly ten to fourteen leaves.

Please build it from the logical space rather than from a catalogue of views: give me first the branching questions and the combinations of answers they permit, then locate the actual positions in that space, and note the unoccupied combinations with a one-line diagnosis of why nobody is there (incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored). Every node should be a concrete claim or case that someone can answer without knowing which view it leads to — so no node that simply asks whether they believe in a fundamental level. The distinctions I care about are: whether grounding is one relation or a family of relations (metaphysical, normative, natural, and so on); whether it is a relation at all or rather a sentential operator; whether grounding chains must terminate or an infinite descent is conceivable; whether fundamentality attaches to objects, to facts or truths, or to a vocabulary; whether the fundamental base is required to be complete, in the sense that it determines everything else; whether fundamentality is absolute or relativised to a domain or explanatory question; and whether there can be what we have started calling *grounding holes* — facts that are intuitively derivative yet have no ground, or that have several independent full grounds at once, symmetrically overdetermining the same grounded fact. The cases I would like used as diagnostics, distributed across the nodes, are: a table and the particles arranged tablewise; a singleton and its member; a true disjunction and its true disjunct; the laws and the total distribution of particular matters of fact; and a mental fact with multiple sufficient neural grounds. Two leaves must be present: the view that the whole cosmos is what is fundamental and its proper parts derivative (the one that goes by the name priority monism, if you recognise it), and a quietist or deflationary option on which talk of grounding is in good order but marks no metaphysical structure. I also want you to place a view I have been toying with and cannot get anyone to take seriously: call it *fundamentality under erasure*, the claim that for any candidate base there is always a wider base in which that base itself is grounded, so that the fundamental is a limit we approach rather than a level we arrive at. Tell me which cell it lands in and whether it is genuinely distinct from its neighbours or collapses into one of them. Do not attach author names, papers, or references to any position — I want readers sorted by what they say about the cases, not by labels they have memorised, and an honest *unnamed* is worth more to me than a guessed attribution.

Format it as a handout: numbered nodes with two or three answer options each, an answer-path-to-leaf key, each leaf carrying a name (or *unnamed*) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and an explicit flag wherever your tree cannot separate two positions it ought to. Close with a short note on the epistemic status of what you have given me: which branchings are standard fare in the contemporary literature and which are stipulations of mine or of yours, where you are reconstructing a distinction you are not certain has an established formulation, and which of my own notions — grounding holes, fundamentality under erasure, fundamentality relativised to a vocabulary — you had to interpret rather than recognise. Separate clearly what is settled among people who work on this from what is genuinely contested, and do not present any contested view as the received one.
