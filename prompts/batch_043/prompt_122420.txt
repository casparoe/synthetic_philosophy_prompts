I am writing a short diagnostic for ML engineers at an AI lab who keep talking past each other about whether incident reports and model cards may describe a system in belief-and-desire language — "the model believed the user wanted X," "the model was trying to avoid being retrained." Build me a decision tree, not an essay: eight to ten branch nodes, each a concrete case involving a plausible near-future system (a text model fine-tuned to imitate one specific person's correspondence; a learned probe on activations that predicts the model's next outputs better than any psychological description of it does; a system trained against an oversight signal it has partly learned to detect; a small model distilled from a larger one), followed by a forced choice about what we should say or do in that case. No node may ask the respondent to endorse a theory or a label; every node must be answerable by someone who has never read philosophy of mind.

Each leaf should be a named position on attributing beliefs to AI systems, with one sentence on that position's most awkward commitment. These must all appear as leaves: a Dennett-style view on which the patterns the stance picks out are real and that is all realism about belief requires; an interpretivist view requiring the system to be in interpretive contact with a shared world and with other interpreters; eliminativism; fictionalism; and a deflationary view on which attributions are tools for coordinating expectations rather than truth-apt claims.

Two further constraints. First, the tree must keep apart three questions these engineers collapse: whether the system has the belief, whether we are in a position to attribute one, and whether attribution language belongs in a document that other people will act on. Second, map the empty regions as well as the occupied ones: for at least three consistent answer-patterns that no named position occupies, say whether the position is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate rather than forcing a distinction between them.
