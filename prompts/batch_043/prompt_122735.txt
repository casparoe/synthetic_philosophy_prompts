I am defending a paper in a philosophy of mind seminar next month and I want a Q&A prep sheet for the discussion period.

My thesis: in Dennett's Where Am I? — the story in which a man's brain is removed and kept alive in a vat in Houston, linked by radio to his body, which is then sent to Mars so that signals between them are delayed by many minutes — the puzzle is not that we cannot discover where the person is, but that the Location Question has no determinate answer until we settle what we want to track. My own contribution is a case I call the Continuum Case: instead of the delay arriving all at once, stretch it out over months, and suppose that as it grows the body on Mars is progressively fitted with an onboard prosthetic controller that silently takes over more of the sensorimotor loop, until the Earth-bound brain is nearly idle and the Mars-bound body largely runs itself. Somewhere along that gradient the person, if there is one answer, migrates from Houston to Mars; but the gradient supplies no non-arbitrary cut-off, and any line drawn on it looks like a decision about which continuant to hold responsible rather than a discovery about one. I conclude that where am I? is answered by adopting a tracking practice, and that the same is true, if less vividly, of ordinary people.

The room: an animalist in the Olson vein, for whom we are human animals and the vat contains an organ rather than a person; a Parfit-style reductionist who thinks that psychological continuity with any cause, plus the slogan that identity is not what matters, already delivers everything my indeterminacy claim delivers and more cheaply; two embodied or enactivist types who doubt that a brain in a vat has experiences or agency at all, so that the setup never gets going; a visiting systems neuroscientist who treats the cerebrum as the clinical criterion and wants to know what is practically at stake; and a metaphysician who thinks every case of this shape is a sorites and has a favoured theory of vagueness. No one there is a Dennett scholar, and the department is generally hostile to deflationary conclusions.

Draft six to eight objections in the order they will actually come, each phrased the way that objector would phrase it, with a two-or-three-sentence reply, a note on where the reply is soft, what I should concede at the outset, the single reply that has to land for the paper to survive, and the objection I am hoping nobody asks.
