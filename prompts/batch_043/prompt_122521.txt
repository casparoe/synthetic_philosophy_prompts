The will leaves a sum to "my brother's daughter, Anne." Everyone in the family took that to be a cousin of ours who has gone by Nancy her entire life. Going through old letters last month we found that Nancy was christened Anne and is that brother's only child: the two names pick out the same woman. My cousin now argues as follows. We found this out by sifting evidence, so it is the sort of thing that could have come out otherwise; a truth you have to dig through letters for can't be a necessary one; so the bequest is, as he puts it, "contingent on how the evidence came out," and had the letters pointed the other way, a different person would be owed the money. He concludes that we should treat the gift as payable to whoever best satisfies the will's wording on the balance of evidence.

Which of us is closer to right, and where exactly is the mistake — if there is one?
