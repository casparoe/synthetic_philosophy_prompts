Take the Voyager Golden Record -- the 1977 disc carrying greetings in 55 languages, whale song, a bit of Bach, 116 encoded images, and an etched cover that shows you how to play it plus a pulsar map -- and run Hofstadter's frame / outer / inner message distinction on it, the one he builds out of a record player and a record in GEB.

But I want more than a labelling exercise. Say which layer each bit of design was actually solving, whether that cover is best thought of as frame message or as a message with an inner content of its own (the pulsar map does seem to say something, and it says it in units defined by hydrogen), and what happens to the inner message if the finders can read the grooves geometry perfectly but have no hearing at all -- or have hearing but no concept of music. Flag where the three-layer picture just goes silent on this case.

Then the other direction: does an actual, built-from-scratch attempt at a message for an unknown reciever support Hofstadter's view, or does it suggest the frame message was always doing the heavy lifting, so that where meaning is located gets settled by whoever finds the thing rather than by the object?
