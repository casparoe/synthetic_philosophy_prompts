At our centre's methods reading group — mostly economists, two sociologists — one of the economists keeps running the same analogy: methodological individualism is just microfoundations, and microfoundations is just what statistical mechanics did for thermodynamics. Nobody needed hotness as a force acting on molecules; you get the gas laws out of the motion of lots of particles, and macro vocabulary is bookkeeping. He uses it to license two things: any explanation that cites a culture, an institution or the market as a cause is a placeholder to be replaced by a story about individual beliefs and incentives, and we shouldn't fund projects whose explanations stop at the macro level.

I'd rather audit the analogy then just argue back. Give me the correspondence table — molecules, temperature, the gas laws, the derivation — and then the disanalogies, but sorted by whether they actually touch his two conclusions. The cases I keep getting stuck on: inflation, where each agent's expectation is about inflation itself, so the micro story seems to already contain the macro variable; a bank run, where what each depositor does depends on what they think others expect, so the individual-level state is irreducibly about other people; and something like a firm's culture or a neighbourhood's segregation pattern, where the macro property looks structural rather than an average of individual quantities, so there's no mean-kinetic-energy move available even in principle.

Which reading of MI does the picture actually support — bare supervenience, or every macro explanation must be replaced, or a claim about mechanisms — and does the funding conclusion need a stronger one than the analogy delivers? Does it survive the disanalogies, or was the analogy carrying it? And if the holist's chess-or-language comparison is the better source domain for my three cases, what would it take for that to make his requirement false rather than just hard to satisfy?
