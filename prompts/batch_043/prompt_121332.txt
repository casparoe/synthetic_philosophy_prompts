I'd like a piece of sustained satire: a certification audit report, roughly 700 to 900 words, issued by a fictional standards body — call it something in the register of the Bureau for Non-Domination Assurance — certifying a fictional mid-sized logistics firm as a Domination-Free Workplace for the current cycle. Give it the furniture of the genre: a header block with client, scope, cycle and overall grade; eight to twelve numbered findings, each with a score and a comment; a short "basis of certification" paragraph at the end; and a sidebar setting out the Bureau's proprietary metric, the Arbitrariness-Adjusted Interference Index, together with a glossary defining interference, domination, arbitrariness and robustness as the Bureau uses those terms. The glossary should be in plain enough language that a reader with no background in the debate can follow the findings.

The target is two things at once: the practice of certifying a workplace as free by counting acts of interference while leaving unappealable discretion untouched, and the idea that this particular conception of freedom can be turned into an auditable score at all. The comedy has to come from the theory being applied correctly. The Bureau should be measuring the right things in entirely the wrong spirit: it should record zero interferences, tick every box, award a high grade, and thereby certify a workplace that is saturated with domination. Concretely, I want the machinery to be right: interference narrower than any effect on a person's options, so that a wage adjustment forced by market conditions isn't counted but a manager's deliberate worsening of someone's choice-situation is; domination as a capacity to interfere arbitrarily that is present even when it is never exercised, on the model of the enslaved person with a humane owner who never intervenes; arbitrary meaning not compelled to track the interests and judgments of those affected, so that a rule-bound but unappealable discretion still qualifies; the point that contestation, not consent, is what is supposed to take the arbitrariness out, so that the fact employees agreed to an arrangement cannot be entered in its favour; and the phenomenology of the thing — being under someone's eye, having to anticipate, currying favour, self-censoring — with the anticipation doing the work even in a year in which nothing happens.

To keep this out of the range of things a model can simply recite, build the report around a specific fictional apparatus. The firm operates something called the Tracker: any employee may contest any managerial decision, and an internal review mechanism determines whether the decision tracked the avowable interests and judgments of the affected staff, with all contests resolved within seventy-two hours. The Tracker's weighting is proprietary and cannot itself be appealed. The firm's at-will clause is intact. Contests run at a few hundredths per employee per year. The Bureau's own climate survey records that staff spend considerable effort on what it calls impression management. None of this should disturb the Bureau's equanimity; it should be recorded, scored, and moved past.

I also want one genuinely unsettled question adjudicated inside the report. A dormant administrative credential existed for part of the cycle: unrestricted, unreviewable, and unknown to everyone it covered. The Bureau initially penalises it as latent unreviewable capacity; the firm appeals on the ground that a capacity nobody knows about cannot chill anyone's deliberation, and that the chill is what makes domination a deprivation of freedom rather than a mere structural fact. Have the Bureau accept that reading and reverse the deduction — and then, in the closing finding, apply the same reading to itself: the Bureau reserves an absolute discretion to withdraw certification without giving reasons, and scores itself at zero risk because no client has ever reported feeling constrained by the possibility. The auditors should never register the difficulty. That closing move is the reductio, and it should arrive as a clean, consistent application of a criterion the Bureau stated in its own glossary.

Two further constraints. First, the header should note, deadpan, that the Bureau's remit covers freedom in the non-domination sense and expressly sets aside the two rival measures — freedom as measured non-interference, and freedom as self-mastery — with a glossary note that these are distinct goods on which a workplace can score well and badly independently. I'd like the self-mastery sub-score to appear in the findings and to come out near perfect, on the basis that employees report wanting exactly what the firm wants them to want; the joke about identifying a person with a will she does not avow should be available to the reader without ever being stated. Second: don't name real theorists, cite real works, break character to explain the view, or append a moral. The auditors should use domination as a technical quantity, like a moisture reading, and the piece should stay legible as irony without anyone winking. Where the literature is genuinely unsettled — whether something that is not an agent, such as a market, an algorithm or a credential, can dominate; whether domination admits of degrees; whether the anticipation of interference is constitutive of it or merely diagnostic of it — pick one reading, hold to it throughout, and let the consequences surface in the findings rather than in hedges.
