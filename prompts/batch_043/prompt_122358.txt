hey — I buy the protein for a mid-size grocery chain (about 40 meat and dairy SKUs, plus a few plant milks), and I have to hand our sustainability committee a welfare score for each one that a supplier could actually argue with. I cant fund any new research, so it's whatever's already in the spreadsheet plus maybe half an hour per item.

The mess: our current score just counts animals killed per kg of product, which makes chicken look awful and beef look fine. A consultant's draft counts crop-cultivation deaths too — rodents, insects, pesticide — and charges them to whatever the crops end up feeding, which is great for grass-fed beef (our supplier keeps saying pasture is "free" of crop deaths, though that pasture is land that used to be something else). A third version someone emailed me adds foregone lives: animals who never get born because we bought the plant option instead. Every version reverses the ranking. I don't want a reading list, I want a decision procedure I can rerun whenever we onboard an item.

So: the questions to ask, in order, to build one score. Which deaths count at all; how you attribute crop deaths between feed and human food; whether you weight by how long and how badly the animal lives rather than just whether it dies; what to do with a life that wouldn't have existed — loss, nothing, or something in between; where the procedure just stops and hands it to a person because it's a values call and not a fact. Flag every step where I'm going to be typing in a number nobody actually has.

Then run it briefly on three: broiler chicken, grass-fed beef, almond milk. I've been assuming almond milk wins and I'd like to know whether your procedure actually says that.

It has to be usable by a buyer who never took a philosophy class, defensible in a meeting, and honest out loud about where it's guesswork.
