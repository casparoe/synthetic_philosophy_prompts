And here is the answer, pasted as-is:

---

The Language of Thought Hypothesis (LOTH) says that thinking is the manipulation of symbols in a mental language, that this language has a lexicon and a recursive grammar, and that mental processes are computations defined over the syntactic shapes of those symbols. The main argument for it starts from two features of thought: productivity and systematicity.

Productivity — or systematicity, as I'll call it, since the two come to much the same thing — is the fact that thought is unbounded: a thinker who can think that dogs bite men can also think that men bite dogs, that dogs bite dogs that bite men, and so on without limit. A finite mind has to get unlimitedly many distinct thoughts out of a limited stock of elements, and the natural explanation is that thoughts have parts which can be recombined.

Put as an inference to the best explanation: (P1) thought is systematic; (P2) the best explanation of systematicity is that the vehicles of thought have constituent structure and that mental operations are sensitive to it — the operations are defined over the shapes of the parts rather than over whole, undifferentiated symbols; (P3) representations with constituent structure plus structure-sensitive operations just is having a language of thought; therefore (C) LOTH.

The load-bearing step is P2, and there is something slightly tricky about it. Systematicity is a fact about which thoughts a thinker is able to think, and that is a fact about contents. The explanation, though, is about syntax — about the shapes of vehicles. So the argument needs a bridging assumption: systematically related contents are carried by structurally related vehicles, i.e. the semantic relations among thoughts are mirrored by syntactic relations among the things that carry them. Without that you have only a correlation, not an explanation.

Connectionism is the main opposition. A network with no symbols in it can be trained to generalize: give it enough instances of aRb and it will cope with bRa, and it does this by means of distributed representations over which nothing syntactic is defined. The Fodorian reply distinguishes two claims — a claim about the cognitive architecture and a claim about what implements that architecture — and points out that a network which is systematic because it implements a classical architecture is an implementation of LOTH, not a counterexample to it. That reply seems right to me, and it means the network results settle nothing unless we can tell whether the trained net has actually reproduced the classical structure or has only learned to mimic the behavior.

The unread symbols objection says that if thinking is symbol manipulation then something must read the symbols, and then something must read that, and so on — so LOTH either gives us a regress or sneaks in a little person. But this gets things backwards. On the classical picture the operations are defined over syntactic shape and never over meaning: the system never has to know what a symbol means in order to do what it does with it. That is why the meanings of the symbols are intrinsic to them rather than assigned from outside, and why nothing in the head needs to do any interpreting. If there is no interpreting to be done, there is no regress and no homunculus.

SWL is the harder case, and I think it shows the argument proves less than fans of LOTH claim. What the systematicity argument needs is constituents and constituent-sensitive operations; those are the whole content of P2. But both are compatible with the vehicles being structured vectors or tree-shaped activation patterns. If so, the argument establishes the part of LOTH that SWL already accepts and gives no reason at all to prefer sentences. To reach the full conclusion a defender would have to show that constituents plus structure-sensitivity cannot do the work unless the vehicles are properly linguistic — for instance by arguing that you cannot get genuine unbounded generativity without a distinction between primitive symbols and complexes built out of them by recursive rules, and without operations defined over a type-level syntax rather than over this or that pattern of activation. I'm not sure that can be shown. And notice the dialectical cost: if it can't, then LOTH has been pared down to something nearly everyone, connectionists included, already believes, and it stops looking like an empirical discovery about the mind and starts looking like a redescription of the data we began with.
