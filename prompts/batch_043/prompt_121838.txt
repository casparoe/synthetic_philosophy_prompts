I'm putting together a short position paper for a workshop on modality, and before I commit the thing to print my supervisor read a draft and said, rather unhelpfully, that my commitments "don't all pull in the same direction". I can't see where the fault is supposed to be — each of them seems to me independently reasonable, and I've picked them up from people who I take to be sensible — so I'd like an audit rather than a lecture. Here is roughly where I've landed, deliberately stated in my own words rather than anyone else's:

(1) Ideal conceivability is a genuine but fallible guide to metaphysical possibility, by which I mean: if no contradiction can be found in a hypothesis even after arbitrarily much a priori reflection, that is real evidence that the hypothesis describes a way the world could be, though it is not conclusive. (2) I accept the standard Kripkean cases of the necessary a posteriori — that Hesperus is Phosphorus, that water is H2O, and the essentialist claim about origins, e.g. that a given person could not have been born of different parents — and I am happy to say that in these cases the apparent imaginability of the denial is explained away rather than being a genuine possibility, so I already concede that conceiving fails somewhere. (3) I have never been able to take on board the two-dimensionalist idea that there is a second, "primary" kind of possibility alongside the ordinary kind: it seems to me that "could" is univocal, and that what the Twin Earth thought experiment describes is not a possible world in which water is XYZ but a world in which the stuff that plays the watery role is XYZ. (4) I think the zombie intuition survives ideal reflection and the conceivability argument against materialism is the best argument against materialism that we have; I don't find the phenomenal-concept-strategy replies or the "illusion of conceivability" replies persuasive, and I don't see why the materialist should be allowed to reach for a necessary a posteriori identity here when there is nothing analogous to the molecular story that makes it intelligible how water could turn out to be H2O. (5) I am independently attracted to something in the neighbourhood of Russellian monism or panpsychism — phenomenal properties as the intrinsic grounds of the microphysical, with brute psychophysical necessitation — because it seems to dissolve the interaction problem without collapsing into eliminativism. (6) I am also attracted, on the modal-epistemology side, to the broadly neo-Aristotelian thought that modal knowledge is knowledge of natures or essences, and that this is precisely why necessary a posteriori truths turn up where they do: we had to discover empirically what the stuff in front of us is. (7) Finally, I think empirical science plainly extends our knowledge of what is possible — physics tells us what matter can and cannot do — and I take this to be no threat to (1), since the empirical results only hand us premises that we then reason a priori from.

What I want from you is the consistency check my supervisor didn't give me. Take the seven commitments and tell me which pairs or triples are genuinely in tension and how badly, which ones only look like they conflict and in fact dissolve on inspection, and for each real tension what the cheapest repair would be and what it would cost me — i.e., which of the commitments in that cluster would have to be weakened, qualified or abandoned. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and whether what remains is a package anyone could recognisably hold rather than an arbitrary heap: is there a stable subset here that keeps most of what I care about, and is there a version of the zombie argument that survives if I insist on keeping myRussellian leanings? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and be specific about which commitment has to go in each case. Assume I'm settling this in the armchair: I don't want suggestions about what to read next, or who has defended which combination, or how the disputes are labelled in the literature — I want the tracing of commitments done on the theses as I've stated them, and I'd rather you argued with me than surveyed positions for me.
