Setup, so we're using the same words for the same things. Thomson's lamp: a lamp with a toggle switch, off at t=0, flipped at t=1/2, flipped again at t=3/4, then at t=7/8, and so on, so infinitely many flips before t=1. Question: is it on or off at t=1? Two camps have been going at each other about this for decades. Camp A (Thomson's side — call them the completion skeptics) says the classic bit of reasoning is a genuine contradiction: the lamp can't be on at t=1, because every time it's switched on there's a later switching-off; it can't be off, because every time it's switched off there's a later switching-on; and there's no third state to fall back on. So what's broken is the idea that anyone could complete an infinite sequence of acts, and the whole supertask idealization is rotten. Camp B (the underdetermination people, roughly Benacerraf's side) says the reasoning only shows that the description doesn't settle the answer: add a rule — at t=1 it's on; or the state has to vary continuously through the limit; whatever — and you get a perfectly consistent story. So the lesson is about the incompleteness of descriptions, not about impossibility.
