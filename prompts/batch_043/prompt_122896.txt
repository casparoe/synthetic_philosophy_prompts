So prioritarianism under uncertainty is not a seminar curiosity for us right now. At a reading group last night a friend walked me into the following argument and I have not found the exit.

(1) Prioritarianism ranks outcomes by the sum over people of f(w), where f is increasing and strictly concave. This is supposed to be the view that keeps what was attractive in egalitarianism while escaping the levelling-down objection: because f is increasing, making anyone better off always improves the outcome, so prioritarianism never recommends levelling down.
(2) Under uncertainty the natural extension is ex post prioritarianism: score each possible outcome by that sum, weight by its probability, and maximise the expectation.
(3) Put f = square root, and take two people both at welfare level 2. Option Safe: nothing happens, both stay at 2, score 2√2 ≈ 2.83. Option Risky: a free intervention; with probability ½ the first gets 6 and the second 0, with probability ½ the reverse; score √6 ≈ 2.45. So prioritarianism prefers Safe. My friend adds that the numbers generalise — for any f concave enough that 2f(2) exceeds f(6) + f(0), and for any number of people, there is a case of this shape — and he runs it with a hundred independent pairs, where prioritarianism declines the free lottery a hundred times over.
(4) But ex ante, each person's expected welfare is 3 under Risky and 2 under Safe: an equal chance of 6 and of 0, an expectation 50 per cent higher, for both. Nobody is worse off in expectation; both are better off in expectation. So prioritarianism tells us to decline a free change that is better in expectation for everyone.
(5) The levelling-down objection was never really about actual outcomes; its force is the thought that a change cannot be an improvement if it is an improvement for no one. Read ex ante — and ex ante is the only reading available when the choice is made under uncertainty — prioritarianism demands levelling down in expectation, in exactly the sense in which the view that values equality for its own sake demands levelling down outright.
(6) The objection is not really about inequality either. I noticed that the prioritarian also prefers Safe to a perfectly correlated lottery in which both people get 6 or both get 0, so that no outcome contains any inequality at all: here too the score is √6 versus 2√2. My friend is happy to concede this, because he says it is worse news for me, not better: it shows the concavity is doing risk-aversion work, and if f merely re-scales welfare then the sum of f(w) is just utilitarianism in better units and prioritarianism was never a distinct view at all.
(7) So either prioritarianism is utilitarianism in disguise, or it is an expectation-levelling view that fails the one test it was designed to pass. There is no stable middle position between utilitarianism and egalitarianism.

I cannot accept (7), but I cannot find the break either.
