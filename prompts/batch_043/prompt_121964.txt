Hi — no academic background here, I've just been poking at this on my own after a mate in a forecasting group mentioned it, and I've got myself into a proper knot.

As I understand it, van Fraassen's Reflection principle says something like: my credence now in a claim, supposing that my future self will have credence r in that same claim, should just be r. In other words, trust your future self's numbers. (Probability, if you'd rather.)

Two things seem to follow, and both feel wrong, but I can't see where I've gone wrong.

One: what's the point of bothering with evidence at all? Take whether my football club goes up this season — I'm on about 50% now, and I'd guess that by May I'll be at about 70%. Reflection looks like it's telling me to be at 70% now and save myself a season of following results. My mate says that's definately not what it means, but he can't tell me why, and it does look like what it says.

Two, which is the one actually bothering me: I'm starting a course of steroids next week for a flare-up, and the side effects list says people feel bizzarely certain about everything while they're on them — my GP warned me I'll feel right about things I'm not. So I know now that in a fortnight I'll be at 95% on all sorts of stuff I'm at 50% on today. Shouldn't I be getting to 95% now? Someone told me the principle only applies to a future self you've got no reason to distrust, but that smells like a fudge — there's no reliability clause in it as far as I can see, and if you're allowed to bolt one on you can dodge anything.

I've actually started trying to live by it: writing down in the evening what I'll believe in the morning, and going with that number instead. Am I doing it right? What's the best way to defer to your future self in practice — a spreadsheet of predicted future credences, or is it meant to be more of an attitude? And how do I handle the fortnight on steroids: is there a principled way to discount a future self you know is going to be warped, or do I just have to ride it out?
