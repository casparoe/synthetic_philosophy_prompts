ok so i'm in this philosophy club at school (it's really just me, my friend Dani, and a teacher who lets us use his classroom at lunch) and we got completely stuck on something for like an hour yesterday and I keep turning it over. Basically: are there ever reasons to *believe* something that aren't evidence? Dani's line is that no — evidence is the only thing that can be a reason for belief, and everything else is a reason to act, or to get yourself into a situation where you'll end up believing, or to lie to yourself, but not a reason to believe. I sort of agree, but my gut verdicts case by case don't all line up with that. Here's what I actually think:

- A company offers me a million dollars if I genuinely believe their energy drink is the best one. — Not a reason to believe it. Defintely a reason to pretend, and maybe a reason to stand near their ads until I get worn down, but not a reason to believe.
- If I believe I'm going to make the free throw, I make it more often, and that's *because* I believed it — the confidence is part of what makes the shot go in. — This one feels like it might be a real reason? It isn't evidence about my jump shot, but the belief sort of makes itself true.
- My friend tells me she didn't cheat on the calc test. The evidence is genuinely ambiguous, but if I don't believe her that's a crappy thing to do to a friend, and part of trusting someone is believing them. — Feels like this counts for something, somehow, even though it isn't evidence either.
- I'd be much happier if I believed my ex and I were getting back together. — Nope. This one is basically the definition of the wrong kind.
- The weird one I made up to break my own rule: a demon pays me $1000 if I believe P, and it has *also* arranged things so that P is true whenever I believe it. So believing gets me the money, and the money only shows up in worlds where P is true anyway. — Still feels bogus to me?? Even though the good thing flows right through the truth of the belief.

So my tentative principle, which Dani says is trash, is something like: a non-evidential consideration is a real reason to believe P only when the good it gets you comes *by way of* P's being true — the benefit has to route through the truth, not just through your having the belief. That says yes to the free throw, no to the energy drink, no to the getting-back-together thing, and yes to the demon case — and my gut says no to the demon case, so either my principle or my gut has to go. It also isn't obvious what it says about the friend case (is the friendship a "good that routes through the truth"? that feels like a stretch, but the verdict still feels right to me). And I don't know whether any of this transfers to attitudes that aren't belief: I have a reason to want to *want* to run the 5k, since wanting it would make training suck less — is that a reason to want it?

Can you do reflective equilibrium on me, basically. Find the principle (or the two or three candidate principles) that fits my verdicts, give me each clause with the case that forces it, tell me honestly which of my verdicts I'd have to give up and what the cheaper principle that drops one looks like, and then tell me what the best principle says about the case I actually care about and keep going back and forth on: my dad's new business. He really wants me to believe it's going to work, and my believing it seems to (a) make him less stressed so he works better, (b) make me way less annoying to be around, and (c) make me not look too hard at the spreadsheets that suggest it isn't going to work. Is believing that something I have reason to do?

Format it like this, in this order: "My verdicts, restated" (~120 words), "The principle(s)" (~250 words), "Clause by clause" (~250 words), "What I have to give up" (~200 words), "The dad case, plus one case I haven't thought of" (~150 words), then "Bottom line" in two or three sentences.
