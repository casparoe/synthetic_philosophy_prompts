The reason I want a sestina specifically is that the same six words return in a different order every stanza, and that's the thing I keep failing to get people to see about multiplicative betting: if you stake a fixed fraction, terminal wealth is a product, so its log is a sum, and the order of your wins and losses literally doesn't matter — only the counts do. Six words, six stanzas, every ordering the same sum. Except that the order does matter the instant there's an absorbing barrier under you — a margin call, a forced liquidation, any number below which you're out of the game — because an unlucky ordering kills you on the way to a product that wouldn't have cared. I'd like the rotation to carry that: the form indifferent to order, the poem not.

The other thing, which is the part I actually care about, is why the log shows up at all and where it stops applying. The growth-maximiser's answer, as I understand it, is structural rather than a taste for safety: given a stream of multiplicative payoffs over indefinite time, the quantity you can add across periods is log wealth, maximising E[log] is what maximising the long-run growth rate comes to, and it's roughly maximising median rather than mean, since wealth is a product and the log of a product is a sum so the CLT lands in log space. Which is why the average can compound while almost every individual path decays, the mean carried by a vanishing minority of astronomical paths. Fine. But that framing seems to quietly assume your objective is a *rate* and not a *threshold*. If what you face is a cliff you either clear or don't — one number you have to hit by a fixed time, a runway that either reaches the next round or doesn't, a catastrophe you either avert or don't — then the log isn't obviously the right aggregator, and there's that old result about fixed-target gambling in a subfair game where the probability-maximising play is to bet boldly: enough to land exactly on the target, or everything if you can't. Meanwhile the growth-maximiser in that same game bets nothing at all. Same table, same probabilities, opposite behaviour, and none of the difference is a difference in nerve.

So: somewhere around stanza four or five I want the turn where the reader sees that the two bettors' disagreement isn't about how much risk either can stomach. And don't resolve it — no closing verdict on which one is right; leave both prices standing in the envoi. Also please don't caricature the bold one as reckless or the cautious one as timid: on their own objective each is being exactly as rational as the other.
