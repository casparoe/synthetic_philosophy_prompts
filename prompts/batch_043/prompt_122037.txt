I cover science and ideas for a daily newspaper, and for fifteen years I have worked inside our verification machinery: the two-source rule, off the record and on background, the requirement that a claim be publishable only if someone with a name and a stake will own it, the difference between we checked this and this is checkable, corrections as the only public record of our error rate, and the quiet standing assumptions underneath all of it. I keep running into the view that some of our commitments are hinges in Wittgenstein's sense, as developed in the late notebooks published posthumously as On Certainty and by the epistemologists who have built on them: not premises we have tested but the fixed points that make testing possible, exempt from doubt not because they are especially certain but because a doubt directed at them would have nothing to stand on. I would like that position in my vocabulary rather than in epistemology's.

First, translate it as faithfully as you can, as a claim about what a newsroom does rather than about what an individual believes. My own first pass is that a standard is not itself sourced, and demanding a source for it is a category mistake. Tell me where that pass distorts the position, and then give the strongest version you can manage in my terms, including whatever in the position resists the word standard.

Second, the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which survive only with violence, and which have no expression at all in my vocabulary. For each piece of residue, say whether it reveals a gap in how newsrooms think or a place where the philosophical claim was doing less work than its formulations suggest. I want the honest version, not the reassuring one.

Third, apply the translation to three candidates from my own practice: that my recorder captured what was said and my notes mean what I meant, that the world described in yesterday's paper is the same world as today's, and that an official denial is reportable while the absence of one is not. Which of these would the position count as hinges, which are merely badly supported empirical beliefs wearing the costume of method, and what test would separate them?

Finally, two puzzles. When our desk retired the both-sides convention for settled climate findings, we experienced it as a decision, and within a year it looked as though it had always been compulsory: can the hinge story describe such a shift as anything other than an unargued change plus hindsight? And if a hinge is whatever a practice cannot doubt, is a newsroom that treats the official account as its default as well-founded as one that does not? Does the view have any resources for distinguishing a hinge from a captured habit, and if it does not, is that a defect in the view or an unreasonable expectation on my part that epistemology should grade practices rather than describe them?
