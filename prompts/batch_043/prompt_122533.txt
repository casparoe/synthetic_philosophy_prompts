I have been reading about theories of biological function on my own, without any formal training, and I keep running into the same two cases. One is the creature that springs into existence fully formed, or the very first heart that ever evolved, where the account that says a function is whatever the trait was selected for seems to have nothing to say. The other is the diseased or damaged heart, where the account that says a function is whatever the trait currently contributes to some capacity of a containing system seems to say the wrong thing. A friend in my reading group, who has read more of this than I have, claims the two accounts never really come apart in real biology: every apparent conflict, she says, is one of those two cases in a different costume, or else it is science fiction. I want one case to put to her, built so that she cannot file it under either heading.

What I need is what I will call a double-life trait: a single structure in a single organism that currently produces two effects, call them X and Y. X is the effect the trait was selected for, and it still produces it. Y is an effect it also produces now, and Y really does contribute to a capacity of one of the organism's systems. The selected-effect account should deliver the verdict that the trait's function is X and not Y; the causal-role account should deliver the verdict that its function is Y and not X. Both verdicts have to fall out of what the theories actually say, not out of my stipulating them.

Constraints. A real or realistically described organism: no engineered creatures, no imaginary physics, and a selection history a working biologist would accept. The trait is not damaged, diseased, vestigial, or idle; it is currently load-bearing through Y. The reason X no longer counts as a contribution must be internal and species-typical, not that this individual ended up somewhere strange or that the environment recently changed, since I do not want the moth-at-the-lamp case rewritten. And the story should not be one where Y simply took over as the thing the trait is now for, because that is ordinary exaptation and I do not think it would move her. Please also show why the selected-effect account cannot quietly add Y as a second function.

Give me the scenario in one paragraph of at most 150 words, with the whole answer under 700. After the scenario, state the trait, the selection story for X, the containing system and capacity for Y, and why each account gives the verdict it does; then tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, what happens to both verdicts if that feature is removed, and how a defender of whichever account comes off worse would attack the case. Commit yourself: give me the one case you think actually works and say why it beats the alternatives you considered, and give me your own verdict on whether the case, if it does work, tells us anything about which account is right, or only that the two accounts are answering different questions under one word.
