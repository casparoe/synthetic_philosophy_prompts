I should say at the outset that I have no academic background in philosophy. I work in data archiving, and I think about this sort of thing on long walks in the evenings, so please forgive me if there turns out to be an obvious word for what I am about to describe. I would be grateful if you could write your answer in American English.

For a couple of years now I have kept running into the same small obstacle in settings that otherwise have nothing to do with one another, and I have started to suspect that it is one phenomenon rather than several. Let me give you the versions I have actually got stuck on.

The first was at work. We had two byte-identical copies of the same photograph on a hard drive, and I asked what made them two files rather than one. I was told that they sit at different addresses on the disk. But that seemed only to move the question along: what makes one address a different address from another? If the answer is that they occupy different physical sectors, then I appear to be back where I started, with two exactly similar things in different places and no way of saying what the difference between the places consists in either.

The second version I built myself, because I did not trust the hardware case. Imagine a universe containing nothing but two exactly similar iron balls, two metres apart, and nothing else — no landmarks, no edges, no observer. I then tried to add things that would let me single out one of them. Adding a third object of a different shape did not help once I placed it equidistant from both: every relation I could state came out shared. Each ball is two metres from a ball; each is five metres from the cube; each is the same colour as the other. Naming them failed in a way I found harder to shake: I could not say "the one on the left" because with nothing else in the universe there is no left, and if I christened them by pointing, then the phrase "the one I pointed at" seemed either to fit whichever ball it was without telling me anything, or to be hopelessly indeterminate about which one it was.

The third version is the one that bothers me most. Suppose the two balls slowly orbit one another and come to rest in each other's former positions. In the middle of the manoeuvre they are perfectly tellable apart — there is the one going clockwise and the one going anticlockwise. At the beginning and at the end they are not tellable apart at all. So: once they have settled, is there a fact of the matter about which ball ended up where? And suppose instead that something rearranged them while nobody was tracking them, so that the same question arises without any intermediate stage to appeal to. Would anything have happened? I have had two reactions from people I have put this to: either that nothing happened at all and the question is empty, or that something happened which no description could possibly capture. Both feel defensible to me and they cannot both be right.

There is a residual version too. If I add a third exactly similar ball, the only thing that seems to change is the number. So I wonder whether the number of things is itself another leftover fact of the same kind, or a different one.

What I am groping for is a name for the leftover — or apparent leftover — of which-is-which, and of how-many, that seems to survive after every qualitative description has been given. I have read a little around the edges of this and I gather there is a well-known argument that two things cannot be exactly alike, usually illustrated with a pair of iron balls in empty space. That is not my question. For the sake of argument I am perfectly willing to grant that two things can be exactly alike in every qualitative respect; my puzzle begins only after that concession, and it concerns whether anything further is left over once the likeness is granted.

So I would like more than a word. Specifically: is there an established concept for this, and if so, what exactly is it and where does it stop? Which of my cases are really the same case in disguise and which are genuinely different? What are the nearest established notions to what I have described, how does my thing differ from each of them, and which fits best? Does it make a difference whether the items are objects, places, moments, or people? If nothing established fits, please say so plainly rather than stretching something to cover it, and then coin a term, define it tightly, and tell me what would count as a borderline case for your definition and what would fall outside it altogether.
