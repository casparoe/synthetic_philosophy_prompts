I'm on the committee of our village fête, and I have to decide whether to let a friend of mine run the stall he's designed, so I'd be grateful for your view on how it should be priced. The game is this: you pay an entry fee, then Marcus tosses a coin until it first comes up heads. If that happens on the first toss he hands you £2; if on the second you hand him £2; if on the third he hands you £2.67; if on the fourth you hand him £4; if on the fifth he hands you £6.40; if on the sixth you hand him £10.67 — in general the amount changing hands on the nth toss is 2^n/n pounds, coming to you when n is odd and going to him when n is even. Since a first heads on the nth toss has probability 1 in 2^n, each row of his payout table works out as contributing exactly 1/n of a pound to the average, plus and minus alternately, and he has a sign on the stall reading average payout 69p, which is where 1 − 1/2 + 1/3 − 1/4 + … ends up. He proposes to charge 50p a go, profits to the hospice.

The trouble started when a friend of mine who does mathematics looked at the table. She pointed out that the winning rows on their own add up to more than any number you care to name, and so do the losing rows, so that the total you get depends on the order you add the rows in. She then showed me that if you take the winning rows two at a time against one losing row the same table totals about 104p, that three at a time gives about 124p, and that by taking enough winning rows per losing row you can push the total past any figure at all, or equally send it as far below zero as you like. Marcus's response is that the game is therefore worth whatever anyone says it is, and he is now talking about charging £20 a go and about selling me a season ticket of a hundred games for a flat fee; our treasurer, meanwhile, just wants one honest number to print. Somebody else suggested we cap the table at twenty tosses and void the game with a refund if the coin runs longer, which produced an average of about 67p, or about 72p if you cap it at twenty-one, drifting back towards 69p as you let the cap grow. It is also noticeable that half of everyone who plays wins £2 straight away and walks off £1.50 up, and I can't tell whether that is a relevant fact or a distraction.

So: is the 69p on the sign simply meaningless, given that the rows can be reshuffled to total anything, or is there still a good reason to call it the value of the game that survives that reshuffling? If the game has no average in the ordinary sense, is there any entry fee at which playing it is plainly a bad idea and any at which it is plainly fine, and does buying the hundred-game season ticket help me — do the swings even out over many plays, or does repetition not rescue things here? And is capping the table at twenty tosses a way of finally measuring the same game, or is it quietly substituting a different one? I have to give Marcus and the treasurer an answer by the weekend, and I'd like to know not just what to tell them but why, since I suspect at least one of the arguments floating around is a piece of sophistry and I can't yet say which.
