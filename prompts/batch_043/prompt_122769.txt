I'm a senior at a large public high school, and I help lead our Ethics Bowl prep group — about ten of us, one afternoon a week. There's an argumentative move we keep running into, and I'd like to know whether it has an established name and, more than that, what exactly it is.

It goes like this. Someone defends the view that a benefit matters more the worse off the person is who receives it. An opponent then describes a case where the only available way to shrink the gap between the best- and worst-off is to take something away from people who are already better off and give it to no one at all, and then treats our discomfort with that case as a refutation. The same move gets aimed at people who say the bad thing about poverty is that some have less than others rather than that they have too little, and it seems to land just as hard against both views — which is what makes me suspect it's really one complaint and not two.

The wrinkle is a variant a teammate and I came up with and couldn't resolve. Instead of taking from the better-off, compare two worlds: one containing only badly-off people, all at exactly the same level, and one containing those same people at that same level plus an extra group of well-off people who never affect them and are never affected by them. Nobody is worse off in either world. The move above seems to have nothing to grab onto here, yet the two views we started from appear to give different verdicts, and we couldn't tell whether that's a genuine difference or an artifact of how we built the case.

So: does the original move have a name, and does my variant have one of its own? I don't want just a label. I want to know what the thing actually is — is it a complaint about harming people, about wasting value, about what can matter when no one is made worse off? — which nearby complaints I might be lumping it together with, and which of the two starting views it genuinely damages, if either. If my variant does split them, I'd like to know which one it hits and where the edges of that case are.
