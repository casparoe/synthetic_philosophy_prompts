i'm in my 4th year, IR department but really doing political theory / socio-legal stuff. my current chapter is on why states and international bodies are so reluctant to apply the g-word while a conflict is still ongoing — i'm coding parliamentary statements, foreign-office legal advice, some NGO reports, a handful of cases, mostly anglophone.

I keep hitting the same shape and I don't have a word for it. tell me if it's a real thing or if i've stared at it to long.

The shape: the category requires that you show intent to destroy the group as such, not just that huge numbers died. and the evidence you can actually get for that is (a) what the perpetrators said and wrote, and (b) the pattern over time — which parts of the group, how systematically, whether the killing pauses once it's public. But (a) is thin when the destruction runs through policy rather than rallies: starvation, withholding medical care, levelling the water system. And (b) only becomes legible after a while. so the case gets stronger the longer it goes on. Which leaves the people whose job is to name it early — rapporteurs, NGOs, backbenchers, correspondents — squeezed between two accusations: name it now and you're prejudging a legal question and degrading a term that has to stay clean for the courts; wait, and you're told you stayed quiet through it. And "let's be careful about labels" isn't a neutral position, because caution costs whoever is being careful nothing and costs the people being killed time.

Three places I see it:

1. one of my cases: a foreign ministry tells parliament it's policy not to use the term until a competent court has ruled, in the same breath calling the reports appalling and demanding an investigation. the court rules years later, long after the killing is over.
2. the archival version: colonial-era mass death gets the label applied by historians once the papers open. nobody thinks the delay was prudent, it was just late. same structure, but no live decision hangs on it.
3. the one that bothers me: sometimes it is named early and it sticks. so the trap isn't universal, and I can't tell whether that's because the evidence was unusually direct or because who has the standing to name matters more than what's known.

My working guess is that the category is built like a verdict (you say it after the proof) but gets used like a diagnosis (you say it to trigger a response), and that the lag between those two is where all the politics lives. someone in philosophy of medicine muttered about diagnosis under uncertainty, and i have a vague memory of Austin having a class of "verdictives" — but I may be misremembering, and I don't think either of those is quite this.

So: does this have a name, or is it three different things I've stapled together? please don't give me reading lists or citations, I can chase those myself. what i want is the carving: the nearest established concepts and how each differs from what I described, whether this is one phenomenon or several, and if there's no name for it, a name, a definition, and a couple of boundary cases — including what wouldn't count.

And be explicit about where you're guessing, or where my description is doing more work than it should. In particular: tell me what would make you say "no, that's not a real pattern, you're cherry-picking," or "those three aren't the same phenomenon at all." I'd definately rather find that out now than at the viva.
