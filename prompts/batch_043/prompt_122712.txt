Hi — I run a tiny podcast for policy grad students, and my guest next week is a philosopher who's just finished a paper defending what she calls "steep prioritarianism". The short version: instead of plugging everyone's welfare into one concave function and adding it up, she multiplies each person's benefit by a priority weight that falls off really fast as their lifetime welfare rises — fast enough that the weighted sum sometimes ranks an outcome where every single person is worse off above one where every single person is better off (her stock example: two people at 10 and 100 versus both at 5). She thinks that's the right answer, not a reductio.

I dont want the episode to be her restating the view for forty minutes. Can you draft eight to ten questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give? I'd like at least one on:

(a) whether the steepness is even coherent — as I understand it, once the weights fall faster than one-over-welfare the weighted sum stops being increasing in anyone's welfare, so I want a question that forces her to say whether she's genuinely given up monotonicity or is quietly helping herself to the concave-function formulation;
(b) the sequential version I keep getting stuck on: if levelling down improves the weighted sum at each step, does she accept a kind of ratchet where a long series of individually sanctioned levellings drives everyone toward the floor, and if not, what exactly breaks the chain;
(c) whether she should just bolt on a Pareto side-constraint and let priority operate only among Pareto-optimal options, and whether that makes the steepness idle;
(d) whether a view that permits levelling down is still distinguishable from plain telic egalitarianism — what the weighting buys her over just saying inequality is intrinsically bad;
(e) the zero-point worry: if the weights are a function of absolute welfare, does shifting the zero of the welfare scale (neutral level versus a life not worth living) flip her verdicts;
(f) how you'd ever calibrate how steep is steep enough.

Assume she'll deflect with things like "Pareto is only a heuristic" and "levelling down is only counterintuitive if you've got person-affecting intuitions you havent earned", and build the follow-ups around that. End with one question about what kind of argument or evidence could actually change her mind.
