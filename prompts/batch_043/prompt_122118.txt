I'm a magazine writer working on a piece about what it costs to reason about a system from outside it — the maneuver Hofstadter calls jumping out of the system — and I want to hang it on Lewis Carroll's 1895 *Mind* dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise. I keep losing my grip on the hinge of it, the moment when Achilles stops arguing and starts writing:

Then Logic would take you by the throat, and force you to do it! Achilles triumphantly replied. Logic would tell you 'You can't help yourself. Now that you've accepted A and B and C and D, you must accept Z!' So you've no choice, you see.

The reading I want to defend is that the Tortoise isn't asking for an extra premise but asking that a rule be handed over as one, and that every time Achilles obliges he builds a slightly larger system in which the identical gap reappears — so the regress is not the Tortoise's stupidity but the shape of any attempt to make a system license its own operation. Is that right, and where exactly does it stop being right?

Two follow-on worries, both of which I have to get right in print. First: is the standard argument that MU can't be produced — start from MI; a final I may be followed by U; whatever follows the M may be doubled; III may become U; UU may be deleted; every string reachable this way has a count of I's that isn't a multiple of three, and MU has none — the same maneuver as seeing that Z never gets into the notebook, or does it differ in a way I'd misrepresent by running them together? The divisibility argument terminates; Achilles' doesn't, and I don't have a clean account of why. Second: I want a usable distinction between enlarging a system and exiting it — adding a rule, or writing the sentence 'MU is unreachable' as a string inside, versus proving something about the system in terms it can't express. A philosopher friend says arithmetization shows systems can talk about their own provability, so the boundary is movable rather than fixed. How would you state the distinction so it survives that objection, and which claim about the link between Carroll's regress and the formal case is the one I should refuse to make at magazine length?
