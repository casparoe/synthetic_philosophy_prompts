I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I'm trying to work out what licenses a move I make all the time. At work the move is explicit and I know it's an approximation: I model redundant components as failing independently, I treat successive draws from a PRNG as independent samples, and I ship that. Lately I've noticed I accept exactly the same move in a setting where nothing is random at all, and I want to know how much it is actually worth there.

Concretely, consider this program, and call the infinite bit sequence it emits A:

```
n = 1
x = 3
while True:
    x = (x*x + n) % (2**61 - 1)
    emit(x % 2)      # the low bit of x
    n = n + 1
```

Here is what I can report without being able to prove any of it. I have generated the first 10^9 bits of A: the density of 1s is 0.5000004, the longest run of zeros is 29, and the standard statistical test batteries I have on hand pass comfortably. Nobody I have asked can prove anything about the limiting density, and I cannot either. Please treat the question as genuinely open.

The proposition I want a number on: P = "the limit, as n goes to infinity, of the fraction of 1s among the first n bits of A exists and equals exactly 1/2." I mean that as a claim about the mathematical object, not about what is provable; if you think there is no determinate fact of the matter about the limiting density of a particular computable sequence, say so and tell me how much that costs you.

My own credence in P is about 0.93. A colleague puts it at 0.4. Her argument: nothing about A is random — it is six lines of deterministic arithmetic — so the randomness is a property of my ignorance, my ignorance is not evidence, and absent a proof I should sit near a broad prior over possible limiting densities, which has no particular reason to concentrate on 1/2.

I want your number, but I want the decomposition more. Please give me each of the following with a number attached and one line of justification:

1. Hidden structure. Your probability that A possesses systematic structure which a 10^9-bit sample cannot detect but which either destroys convergence or pulls the limiting density away from 1/2 by more than 10^-6. The failure mode I have in mind is the one I have been told about in analytic number theory, where an independence heuristic gets the leading term right and misses a real bias that appears only in a finer statistic.

2. The typicality bridge. Suppose I fully grant a model in which the bits are independent and fair. Every particular infinite sequence still has measure zero under that model, so no claim about this sequence's limiting density is a probability-one consequence of it. How much confidence survives the step from "the model assigns probability 1 to density 1/2" to "A has density 1/2"?

3. The licensing question, which is where I suspect the real action is. What rule, if any, entitles me to presume independence here? I can state four candidates that I half-believe simultaneously: (M) mechanism-absence — presume independence whenever I know of no mechanism linking the events; (S) symmetry — presume independence only when my total evidence is invariant under permuting the events, so that they are exchangeable; (K) incompressibility — presume independence only where the dependence is not compressibly describable, i.e. no short program captures the correlation; (R) robustness — do not ask whether independence holds, ask whether the conclusion survives every perturbation of the joint distribution with per-bit mutual information at most δ, and rely on the argument only if it does. Give me a probability for each that it is part of the correct account, and then your credence in P conditional on each being the right one. As I have stated them they bind me from every direction: K refuses to license anything at all, since A is six lines long and therefore maximally compressible; M seems to license whatever I do not yet understand; S seems to demand a symmetry of my evidence that I cannot exhibit; R seems to demand a δ I have no way to estimate. If the correct rule is a hybrid, or none of the four, say which and why.

4. Base rate. Of heuristic arguments with exactly this shape — no randomness anywhere, independence presumed on ignorance, conclusion a limiting statistic — what fraction get the truth right? Not the celebrated successes; all of them, including the ones nobody bothers to write up.

Then combine those into your number, and tell me: which single sub-question your final number is most sensitive to; where you think the gap between my 0.93 and my colleague's 0.4 actually lives; and what one piece of evidence would move your number the most — a second, structurally unrelated argument that also delivers 1/2, a conserved quantity discovered in the recurrence, a proof of a subtle bias in a nearby sequence, or something else. Be specific about the evidence, not just the direction.

Two constraints. Please do not answer "it depends on which philosophy of probability you accept": I want the number and the sensitivity analysis, and if your number really does depend on that, put it into the decomposition as a further sub-question with a probability attached. And please do not point me at papers or invoke authorities by name — I cannot evaluate them, and I do not want to outsource the argument to someone whose work neither of us can check right now.
