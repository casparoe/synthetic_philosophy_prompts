I sit on a hospital formulary committee, so I spend a silly amount of time arguing about what counts as treatment versus what counts as enhancement, and I keep running into a shape I dont have a word for.

It goes like this. Some intervention starts out as an upgrade for people who are already functioning fine — most of the ones I see were originally developed to rebuild or repair something damaged, and then get offered to people who just dont like how that part looks or works. As more people take it up, the range of what looks unremarkable shifts. Then not having it starts to read as the defect, and a few years later the same intervention is being requested as a restoration. The thing you measure to decide what counts as normal has itself been moved by the thing it was being used to classify. I think I see the same shape with height referrals for kids who are short but healthy, and with students using prescription stimulants to study, and it makes me suspicious that "restore normal functioning" cant do the work people want from it, because the normal is downstream of the interventions.

Two things I cant settle on my own. First, every case I can think of ratchets in one direction — upgrades turn into restorations, but I cant produce a single case where a restoration turns into an upgrade — and I dont know whether that asymmetry is part of the thing or just a fact about how medicine happens to spread. Second, I cant tell whether this only afflicts baselines defined statistically off a population, or whether it also gets into the alternative baselines people propose when the statistical one starts wobbling, like "restore the level this particular person would have had if nothing had gone wrong" or "restore what the function is for."

So: does this have a name? And I dont just want a label dropped on it. I want to know what the phenomenon actually is, how it differs from the nearby things it keeps getting muddled with — plain medicalisation, the problem of which population you compare someone to, the thing where a measure stops being useful once people start aiming at it, the thing where classifying people changes the people being classified — and where its edges are. If nothing established really fits, coin a term and define it tightly enough that I could look at a new case and say whether it is or isnt an instance. End with \answer{...} containing just the one best label, established or coined, and nothing else on that line.
