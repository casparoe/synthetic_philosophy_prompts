I have built the following argument for a seminar on collective rationality, and it now seems to me to prove a great deal more than I am able to accept. I would be grateful for a diagnosis.

The case. A research ethics committee of seven must rule on a clinical trial. Its charter fixes three propositions and one connection among them: P, "the trial has scientific merit"; Q, "the risks to participants are acceptable"; and R, "the trial may proceed", together with the rule that R holds if and only if both P and Q hold. The members vote as follows: P is accepted by members 1 to 4; Q is accepted by members 4 to 7; and, because member 4 is the only one who accepts both P and Q, R is accepted by member 4 alone and rejected by the other six. Every member is individually consistent. The majority verdicts are not.

(1) In the only sense of "the committee accepts X" that is available to us — we are a committee of equals, not a panel with a chair's casting vote — the committee accepts X just in case a majority of its members accepts X.

(2) So, in that sense, the committee accepts P, accepts Q, and accepts not-R.

(3) Given the charter, P, Q and not-R are jointly inconsistent.

(4) So the committee's attitudes, in that sense, are jointly inconsistent: there is no way the world could be in which all three are correct.

(5) A body of attitudes that admits of no possible world in which they are all correct is not a single view of how things are. A subject whose attitudes are jointly inconsistent does not thereby hold a view about whether R; it holds, at most, an aggregate of views.

(6) So the majoritarian reading does not ascribe judgments to the committee at all. "The committee accepts P" turns out to mean only that most members accept P, and the committee itself believes, holds and judges nothing. It is not a subject with a view; it is a tallying device.

(7) And no other reading does better. The general impossibility result — that any procedure which admits every coherent profile of individual judgments, decides each proposition on the basis of the members' judgments about that proposition alone, and always returns a consistent and complete collective set, is dictatorial — together with the observation that a dictatorial procedure does not aggregate anyone's judgments but one member's, entails that no procedure constitutes a collective judgment. Whatever else such a procedure produces, it is not what the group thinks.

(8) Therefore there is no such thing as a group judgment. When the committee's letter refusing permission goes out under the funder's seal, nothing is true of the committee that is not already true of its members taken one at a time.

That conclusion strikes me as absurd: there is a fact of the matter about what the committee decided, and what it decided is that the trial may not proceed.

My own attempt at a repair was a two-tier rule: distinguish what the majority endorses — published as a record of the members' views, and allowed to be incoherent — from what the committee rules, produced by aggregating the premises and taking the logical consequence, required to be coherent, and alone possessing legal force. My seminar objected that this gives me two pseudo-agents instead of one real one, and I suspect they may be right.

I should like to keep (1) and (2) if I can. (2) is arithmetic, and abandoning (1) looks like conceding at the outset that democracy and collective rationality cannot be had together, which I take to be a substantive thesis rather than a datum. Beyond that I cannot locate the damage.

Please tell me which step you reject, why that step looked unobjectionable when the argument was being built, and what rejecting it costs. In particular: what is someone committed to who accepts (4) but denies (5), and is that commitment about collective irrationality one we should want? Is my two-tier distinction a genuine answer to (7), or only a relabelling of the problem? And if you think the argument is valid and the defect lies in a premise, say which premise is least costly to abandon and what else that abandonment commits me to. Please answer in British English.
