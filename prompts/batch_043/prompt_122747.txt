Next tuesday I'm giving the soft-matter / nonequilibrium seminar here and I'd rather walk in with an actual prep sheet than with vibes, so: draft me the Q&A.

The thesis I'm defending: there is no privileged answer to the question of where a Maxwell demon pays for the work it extracts. The usual candidates — the kT ln 2 per bit you pay when the demon erases its memory, the cost of the measurement itself, the low-entropy state the demon has to be initialised in — are each a special case of one quantity: kT times a relative entropy between (a) the true conditional distribution of the system given what's in the demon's memory and (b) the conditional distribution the demon's feedback protocol was actually built for. Call the mismatch the calibration gap, and I claim the demon dissipates exactly that. So you can move the cost around by redesigning the demon — hoard garbage bits and pay later, erase every cycle and pay at the dump, model the input well and pay almost nothing — and you can push the per-bit cost definately as low as you like, but only by paying for it in memory size and runtime. The second law is saved by a space–dissipation tradeoff, not by a fixed toll per bit.

Sketch, so you know what to aim at: (1) the standard feedback bound says work out is at most kT times the mutual information between system and memory; (2) that bound is only tight when the protocol is reversible with respect to the demon's own posterior; (3) if the demon's posterior is wrong, the shortfall is a relative entropy and it lands as heat in the write/feedback step; (4) every canonical exorcism is that same identity with a different choice of what the demon got wrong.

Who is in the room, roughly: two stochastic-thermodynamics theorists who live inside fluctuation theorems and feedback bounds and will say step (3) is just the usual splitting of the mutual-information term with philosopher's clothes on; three experimentalists who build real feedback traps and colloidal information engines and will ask what number this predicts and whether it's above their noise floor, and will point out that in their devices the dissipation is dominated by lag and friction, not by anybody's bad model; one quantum information person who'll push on whether the gap is basis-relative, on generalized measurements and a quantum memory, and on whether a demon's posterior is even well defined; one senior statistical mechanic who thinks information talk is anthropic bookkeeping and the exorcism literature is a pseudo-problem because the second law was never in danger; and one grad student who works on autonomous information ratchets and will point out that a ratchet has no model of anything, so my account seems to cover only demons with a little homunculus inside them.
