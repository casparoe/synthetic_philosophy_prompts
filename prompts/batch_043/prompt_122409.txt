I'm writing a paper on whether early modern accounts of divine knowledge have anything useful to say about a problem that has only lately become concrete, and I'd like you to take a definite position on it rather than survey the space of options.

The case, which is hypothetical but I think stable enough to reason about. Suppose a behavioral foundation model — call it Oracle-9 — is trained on an essentially exhaustive record of human choices: text, purchases, movement, biometric and administrative data, the lot. Its distinctive capability is answering queries of exactly the Molinist shape: given a description of an agent and a description of a circumstance that agent is not in fact in, it says whether that agent would freely perform a given action in that circumstance. In testing, its predictions are sealed in advance, never shown to the subjects, and never used to intervene on them; they are then checked against what those subjects actually do when they later end up in situations closely resembling the ones described. Accuracy is about 99.4%, far above any competing method. Two further stipulated facts matter: the residual 0.6% error does not shrink as data and compute scale up by orders of magnitude, and the misses are not concentrated in any identifiable subclass of agent or circumstance — it is not simply that we lack data.

The figure I want you to speak for is Luis de Molina, the sixteenth-century Jesuit who introduced middle knowledge: God's grasp, prior to any decision about which world to create, of what every possible creature would freely do in every possible circumstance — counterfactuals of creaturely freedom. In Molina's telling this knowledge is not inferred from the natures and circumstances involved (his Dominican critics, who held that God moves the created will by a physical premotion, were the target here); God knows it immediately, by supercomprehending the will, more intimately than the will knows itself, and it is precisely what lets God select a world while leaving every choice in it free.

Please answer these, committing yourself on each:

1. How would Molina classify what Oracle-9 has? Is it an instance of middle knowledge, of mere natural knowledge, or something his threefold distinction has no shelf for? Note the awkward feature that Oracle-9 is itself a creature inside the actual world, downstream of whatever God decreed — so be explicit about whether its success is, for Molina, a rival route to counterfactuals of freedom or rather itself a fruit of middle knowledge.

2. Is Oracle-9 evidence for or against Molina's central claim that a free agent, in the very same circumstances and with every disposition unchanged, could have done otherwise? One reading says the stubborn 0.6% vindicates him, since a determined will would in principle be predicted without residue. The opposite reading says that a finite system getting this far by modelling dispositions and circumstances suggests those counterfactuals are grounded in the agent's nature after all — roughly what his critics thought, and what the later objection that nothing makes such counterfactuals true requires. Decide which reading Molina should take more seriously, and say why.

3. There is an asymmetry here: a counterfactual of freedom can never be checked as such, because the moment you place the agent in the circumstance you are no longer dealing with a counterfactual. Does Molina have resources to treat this as a merely epistemic limitation on us, or does Oracle-9's track record leave the metaphysical worry about truth-makers exactly where it was?

4. Finally, the practical analogue. A committee uses Oracle-9 to decide which applicants to admit, or which populations to bring into being, partly on the basis of what those people would freely do — a creaturely parody of God's selection of a world. Would Molina's picture make such reasoning more defensible than its critics assume, or does it belong only to God?

I want a committed answer: tell me which way Molina goes on each point and give the reason, rather than balancing considerations and leaving me to choose. State briefly, at the outset, the Molinist commitments you are relying on, so that I can check the attribution. Flag the one step where you are extrapolating furthest beyond anything Molina himself addresses, and name the single change to the scenario that would most likely flip your main verdict. Please write in British English, in continuous prose of roughly 800 words, and reason from the structure of the views rather than from outside reading.
