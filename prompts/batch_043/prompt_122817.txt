Putting together a short position paper on scepticism for a reading group and I'd like someone to audit the package before I stand up and say it out loud. Roughly where I've landed:

1. Semantic externalism, Twin Earth style: what my thoughts are about isn't settled by anything available to me from the inside. And I think it earns some anti-sceptical mileage — a vat-dweller who thinks the thought we'd express as 'I am a brain in a vat' isn't thereby thinking anything true.

2. But I don't think that settles the sceptical problem, because I think the sceptic's real hypothesis is topic-neutral: a purely structural claim about the shape of someone's total input history and internal state, framed without using any ordinary object-words. That version I think its coherent, I don't think I can eliminate it, and I don't think (1) touches it.

3. I'm Moorean/dogmatist about perception: experience gives immediate, non-inferential support for ordinary beliefs, I know I have hands, and I don't need an argument against the vat before I'm entitled to that.

4. I want to keep closure for knowledge.

5. Internalism about justification — the new evil demon intuition is my lodestar. My envatted twin and I are in exactly the same justificatory position, and the same goes for evidence: same experiences and same reasoning dispositions, same evidence.

6. A couple of verdicts I'm oddly attached to. First: someone who was envatted yesterday doesn't know anything about their surroundings any more, and their situation is worse than mine in a way that isn't just 'as it happens their beliefs are false now'. Second: take the variant where the people running the simulation model a whole language-using community and a physical environment, so that the subject's words have a perfectly ordinary causal and social history inside the simulation. I want to say both that their words do refer to the simulated objects and that they're still radically wrong about how things stand with them. That last bit is what I keep going back and forth on and I can't tell whether I'm being incoherent or just greedy.

I genuinely can't see where the fault line is. Two people I've shown this to flagged different things and neither convinced me. So: go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are really in tension and how badly, and for each one what would have to give and what that would cost me. I suspect at least one of the tensions only looks like one, so say which — and rank them rather than listing everything as equally bad. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and which of these theses is quietly doing more work than I realise. Please don't give me a reading list, I just want the audit.
