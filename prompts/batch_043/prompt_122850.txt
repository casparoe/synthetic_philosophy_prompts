My seminar paper defends a policy I call the trajectory rule: an assistant with a long interaction history should treat a user's request as one to override, not merely to refuse, when the request fits a trajectory the assistant predicts the user will later repudiate — paternalism licensed by anticipated future endorsement rather than by present consent or by imminent harm.

Before I write it, I want the analogy stress-tested. Restate, as precisely and as unflatteringly as you can, the main positions on when an assistant should refuse or override a user — never override; override only to avert imminent and serious harm; and override whenever the model judges an intervention to be in the user's own interest — one at a time, in my clinical vocabulary. If the analogy is doing rhetorical rather than argumentative work in my paper, say so plainly.
