In the foundations-of-statistical-mechanics reading group I attend, the line "information is physical" gets repeated in a tone that suggests the conversation is over. Last week it was aimed at me, and I want to know what it would have to mean for it to have done the work it was being asked to do.

The case was a Szilard engine: a one-molecule gas in a box in contact with a single heat bath, a partition, and a demon with a one-bit memory. I made two claims. First, that the demon need never erase anything: hand it a tape of blank bits prepared in advance, let it spend one bit per cycle correlating that bit with which half of the box the molecule is in, and any dissipation happened off-stage, somewhere else, possibly long ago. Second — this was the part that annoyed people — that if the tape is prepared at a cold temperature and the engine is run at a hotter one, the bookkeeping comes out looking like an ordinary reversible heat engine: roughly kT ln 2 of work extracted per bit at the hot end, at least kT ln 2 of work spent per bit preparing it at the cold end, and a net efficiency of 1 minus the ratio of the two temperatures. On that reconstruction the "information engine" is a heat engine with extra vocabulary, and nothing specifically informational is doing any work.

The reply I got was: "Information is physical — the blank bits are a physical resource, and the demon uses them up." I don't know what to do with that.

Please lay out, as a list, the distinct precise theses that sentence could be expressing here, from the trivial to the radical, and for each say whether it is true, whether it is a platitude or contentious, whether it is false, and — most importantly — whether it would actually support that reply. In particular: does any reading say something that "a low-entropy system is a resource" does not already say, or is the informational phrasing a redescription that would work equally well if the demon's tape were replaced by any cold reservoir? Is the reply even the same claim as the erasure-dissipation point the slogan is usually wheeled out for, and is that point what saves the second law in my setup, or is something else doing it? Which reading was the person replying to me most likely reaching for? And flag the readings that are just misreadings — I have heard this slogan used to claim that a full memory device literally weighs more, and that Landauer's bound is why a laptop gets warm. If my numbers above are wrong, say where. One last wrinkle: the line is the title of Rolf Landauer's 1991 essay, and in it he is attacking rather than endorsing the idea that each logic operation must dissipate energy — "It was not hard to associate a logic gate with a degree of freedom, then to associate kT with that, and presume that this energy has to be dissipated at every step" — so if my group means "computation necessarily costs energy" by it, they may be using it for nearly the opposite of what he argued.
