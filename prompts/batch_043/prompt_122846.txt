I'm giving a paper at an interdisciplinary war-guilt seminar in a fortnight and I want prep for the questions rather than help with the paper — the argument's more or less written.

The thesis: we should seperate three things that get run together. (1) causal responsibility for the July crisis, i.e. who actually did what in those weeks; (2) what I'm calling *licensing* blame — the blame a political community carries for the standing institutions, habits of deference and public stories that made the gamble available to it's decision-makers in the first place, that put it on the menu as a thinkable option at all; (3) retrospective endorsement, what later generations do with the war in memory. My claim is that the post-Fischer, sleepwalker-ish picture — nobody authored the war, the crisis was a shared European failure — only kills (1). It leaves (2) standing, so 'no single author' doesn't exculpate in the way people keep assuming. The case I lean on is the unconditional backing handed to Vienna in early July: even if the men who gave it were improvising, the option was legible to them only because of a domestic political culture and a set of barely accountable decision-making structures their community sustained and could in principle have revised.

Audience: six or so historians, all firmly in the shared-failure camp — archival, allergic to counterfactuals, they think retrospective moral verdicts on 1914 are presentism, and one of them regards the whole Fischer controversy as a moralising intrusion into the discipline. Then two people who work on collective responsibility, one an individualist for whom group blame is at best a metaphor, one who thinks states really are agents and is suspicious of anything that isn't. Plus a political theorist who'll ask what follows for reparations today.

Give me the Q&A prep sheet: the eight-ish objections I'll actually get from *this* room, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that person would actually phrase it, with a two-minute reply, a note on where my reply is soft, and what I should concede before anyone stands up. And include the one I'm hoping nobody asks — I've got a bad feeling about the individualist on who exactly the bearer of licensing blame is and why it's the same bearer a century later, but tell me if there's something worse coming.
