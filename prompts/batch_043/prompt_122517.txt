Three nonprofits in our city run after-school STEM programs for basically the same families and chase basically the same money: two local foundations plus a city grant that's scored partly on "innovation" relative to the other applicants, so the identical program can score worse this year just because a rival upgraded. I'm on the board of one of them. Over about four years we've escalated in a way that feels textbook: one of us started sending every kid home with a robotics kit, all of us matched; then someone added a free laptop loan for the semester, all of us matched; then Saturday hours, then summer sessions, then paid "mentors" with real engineering degrees instead of the grad-student volunteers we used to use. Every individual step made sense, every step got matched within a few months. All three of us are now burning through reserves and two have cut their scholarship slots.

In the spring we got all three boards into one room. Everyone agreed on the diagnosis, we wrote down a short list of things we'd each stop doing, shook hands, and it held for maybe six weeks before one org quietly brought back the laptop loans. My question is about the Moloch / multipolar trap framing. I keep seeing it described as a coordination failure — everyone can see the bottom we're racing toward, nobody can stop unilaterally, so the fix has to be coordinating. But we did coordinate: same room, full agreement, everyone knowing exactly what was happening, and it still collapsed. So is the framing just wrong here, or is the "just coordinate" version of it missing a step?

And the thing I actually need: is there a known ranking of escape hatches — like, is a signed agreement with penalties strictly better than a handshake plus a group chat, is a reputation mechanism better or worse than a contract, is merging the three orgs the only real answer — or is "it depends which kind of trap you're actually in" the honest answer? If it's the latter I'd like to know what the kinds are and how to tell them apart in a case like ours. (One board member wants us to pre-commit by announcing a cap publicly to the families, on the theory that walking it back would cost us more than the laptops do. Is that a real mechanism or just wishful thinking?)

I also keep flip-flopping on something: the families are definately getting more than they used to, so the escalation has been great for them and bad only for the three of us as orgs. Does that mean this isn't a Moloch situation at all but just ordinary competition competing away our margins — which is supposed to be good? Please say plainly which parts of your answer are standard and which are contested among people who actually work on this stuff, because I don't want to walk into the next board meeting repeating someone's hobbyhorse as if it were settled.
