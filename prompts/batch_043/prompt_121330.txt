So: the consciousness reading group I run has been stuck on illusionism for about a year and a half and nobody has moved an inch, and its starting to feel like a ritual. The realist side: introspection hands you a datum — experience has qualitative, intrinsic, what-it's-like-ish properties — and illusionists are either quietly redefining the word or answering a different question and calling it an answer. The illusionist side: introspection is a fallible, simplifying self-model, it re-describes our sensory states as having properties they don't have, and the "hard problem" is an artifact of treating that self-model as transparent. Each side has a stock reply to the other's stock argument and the exchange is now fully predictable. Someone says "you're denying the datum," someone says "your datum is a theory-laden introspective judgement," round it goes.

Here's the bit I want you to actually chew on. To test whether anything empirical is doing the work, we invented a hypothetical future result. Suppose we get a complete functional account of an introspective re-description subsystem: it takes fine-grained relational and functional information about a subject's sensory state and re-encodes it in a deliberately coarse format for use in deliberation and verbal report, a format that attributes simple intrinsic-sounding qualities. Suppose we can then lesion it: subjects still discriminate wavelengths, still act appropriately, still pass forced-choice colour tasks, but they stop saying there's anything it's like and stop caring which experiences they have. And suppose it further turns out that this coarse re-encoding is the only reason anyone ever took the properties to be intrinsic, ineffable, and intrinsically private. The realists in my group said: fine, you've explained the report and not the experience, or possibly you've just located and destroyed the neural correlate of consciousness — either way nothing follows about whether there's experience. The illusionists said: that's exactly what we meant, what were you expecting. Neither side budged, which is itself data.
