I'm in my school's philosophy society, and at the end of the year each of us posts a short list of where we've landed on one topic; the rest of the group then gets ten minutes to find the inconsistency in it before the author responds. Mine is on the three-stances picture — physical, design, intentional — and I'd like to find my own problems before they do. Here is where I think I've landed:

1. A pattern is real just in case treating it as real buys you prediction: if describing a system's behaviour in terms of that pattern lets you compress the data rather than take it event by event, the pattern is real, and there is nothing more to being real than that.
2. So if the intentional stance pays on a system — if attributing beliefs and desires to it lets you predict it — that settles whether it really has beliefs. There is no further fact behind the predictions about whether they are merely as if.
3. The physical stance is complete in principle: everything a system does is in principle predictable from its microphysics. This does not make higher-level patterns second-class, or destined to be replaced by physics, because in-principle predictability is not something any actual agent can use.
4. The intentional stance is only licensed where the design stance is. You are entitled to assume a system will do what it has most reason to do because something — evolution, an engineer — designed it to be good at whatever it does, and it is the assumption of good design that makes the rationality assumption safe.
5. A chess engine gets the intentional stance from me, including nested attributions: I predict it by saying that it represents me as expecting it to do X and acts on that representation. The nesting does not bother me.
6. A language model does not get it, even though I will admit that describing it in terms of what it believes does seem to help predict its outputs, because it was not designed to have beliefs, only to produce plausible continuations.
7. My strongest refusal is a system trained on purpose so that humans can predict it by attributing beliefs and desires to it — fine-tuned with the explicit objective of being interpretable under the intentional stance. There the pattern looks to me like an artifact of the training objective rather than a discovery about the system, so I will not grant it.

Please audit the set rather than explain the stances back to me. Which pairs actually pull against each other, ranked by how badly, and for each: what exactly would have to be revised and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the list? Which apparent tensions dissolve on closer inspection, and what makes them dissolve? Is there a single revision that would clean up the most? Be blunt rather than agreeable: if one of my items is too vague to audit, or leans on a distinction my own criterion forbids me to draw, or if the whole thing is confused in a way I have not noticed, say that plainly instead of producing six polite tensions. And where you are genuinely unsure whether a tension is real or only verbal, say so and say what would settle it, rather than picking whichever option sounds more confident.
