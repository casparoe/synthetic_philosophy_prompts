write me a joke about Dutch book arguments. I dont want the kind of thing that would still work if you swapped the word Bayesian out for any other philosophy word -- the humour has to come from the actual plumbing of the argument: the sure loss, the fact that the bookie needs to know your credences in advance, the assumption that you'll take a bet at your fair price in either direction (you'll buy it and you'll sell it), and the difference between a synchronic book (your credences violate the probability axioms right now) and a diachronic one (the bookie sells you a bet, hangs around until you learn whether E, then buys it back at the price you now think is fair).

Here's the setup I want: a bookie tries to make a book against someone whose credences are imprecise -- she's got a whole set of probability functions instead of a single one, and she'll only accept a bet if it looks favourable by the lights of every function in the set. The punchline should turn on why the standard argument doesn't get a grip on her, and ideally she comes out of it in a way that makes the bookie look a bit silly. One or two paragraphs, setup then payoff. Also: the joke can't just be that she loses a load of money, since that's the argument rather than a joke.
