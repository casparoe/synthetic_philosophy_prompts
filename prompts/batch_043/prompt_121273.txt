My father has mid-stage Alzheimer's and several times a week asks where his brother Frank is. Frank died two years ago, and telling him again restarts the whole grief each time, so I have fallen into answering with things that are strictly true and that I know he will complete wrongly: "Frank can't get over here right now," "he's still up north." My mother, who shares the caregiving, tells him the truth bluntly every time and says what I do is lying with better paperwork — a distinction that exists only so I can deny having lied. My own defence is a rule I made up: inducing a false belief is acceptable only if everything I actually say is true and the other person could still reach the truth by pressing me, since I would answer honestly if cornered. But with Dad that second clause feels hollow: he cannot press, he would not remember the answer, and the failure of memory is exactly what my sentences rely on to do their work.

So: is my mother right? Is there a genuine moral difference between lying and arranging for someone to mislead themselves, or only a difference in how it looks to others and in what I can afterwards deny? Does it change anything that the capacity my rule leans on is the very one he has lost? And is there anything to a further worry I have — that I have started doing the same sort of thing with the aide who comes in three mornings a week, where the excuse is much thinner?
