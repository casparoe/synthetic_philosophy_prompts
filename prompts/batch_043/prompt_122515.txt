I had an argument at work a couple of months ago about setting a prior on a continuous parameter — I put mass uniformly on a length scale, my colleague wanted it uniform on the log — and we got noticeably different answers, and chasing down who was right dropped me into Bertrand's paradox and I've been stuck there ever since. The chord case is what gets me: you ask what fraction of random chords are longer than the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle, you spread the probability evenly over each of the three natural-looking things (the endpoints around the circle, the midpoint along a radius, the midpoint anywhere in the disc), you get three different numbers, and roughly a century later there's apparently still no agreement about which one is correct.

My hypothesis is that this only looks like a problem about geometry and is really a fight about what a probability distribution is for. If it represents my ignorance, then "no reason to favour one value over another" ought to settle it, and it's a scandal that it doesn't. If it represents how the value got produced, then "random chord" just means "chord produced by some mechanism", and of course there's no unique answer — it depends how you throw the straw. The invariance move I keep running into (Jaynes and co.: demand the distribution be invariant under the symmetries of the problem as stated) looks to me like an attempt to get uniqueness back anyway, but when I try to apply the rule it seem to break, because what counts as "the problem as stated" depends on wich variables you wrote it down in — which is just the original problem one level up.

So diagnose this for me rather than telling me who's right. Is it a verbal dispute about "random", a substantive one about whether probability is information or chance, a hidden formal question about whether a continuum admits any canonical notion of "even", or two different questions — how to rationally spread credence versus how to model a physical setup — sharing one name? And given whatever you think it is, what would progress even look like: a theorem, a distinction, abandoning the question as ill-posed, or should I just accept that my colleague and I were never going to converge and get on with my life?
