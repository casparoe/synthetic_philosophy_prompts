Bit of context: I'm 71, retired, spent about thirty-five years in experimental spectroscopy, mostly building and calibrating instruments, which is its probably why I think the way I do. Evenings for the last couple of years I've been reading philosophy and going to a reading group, and they've asked me to give a short talk on where I've landed on consciousness. Before I make a fool of myself in front of thirty people I'd like to know whether the package holds together. Here it is, more or less:

(1) There is something it is like. Conscious experience is real, and it's the one thing I'm as certain of as I am of anything. I've no patience with the view that says there's no such thing and we're all just confused about our own minds.

(2) But what introspection hands me is a user illusion. The desktop, not the files. The seamless unified field, the richness, the sense that my seeing-the-red is the thing that made me say "red" — that's a dashboard the system builds for its own purposes, heavily simplified, and in places flat wrong about the machinery underneath.

(3) And no little person inside reading the dashboard. I'm not smuggling in a homunculus; as I understand the interface story, not needing one is the whole point of it.

(4) Methodologically: when the subject's report and the instrument disagree, I go with the instrument. I spent a career learning that a detector's output is evidence about the detector as much as about the world, and that "it obviously works like this" is the weakest grade of evidence there is. Symptom reports point you somewhere; they don't tell you the mechanism.

(5) Along with that, I dont think the "hard problem" is a special problem. It looks to me like the problem of life looked around 1900: the machinery is hidden from you, so of course it looks like you'd need an extra ingredient, and of course an interface would hide precisely the thing that would dissolve the puzzle. The felt explanatory gap is something to be explained, not a premise to reason from.

(6) And yet I want to keep a residual question: why is all this processing acompanied by anything at all? I don't think that's a pseudo-question and I'm not going to pretend it evaporates, even though I've no idea whether it's answerable.

(7) Here's a case I made up because the group kept going in circles. Call it the dark readout. Take a control system with a self-model that gets consumed downstream — it feeds action selection, memory, verbal report. Now take a second system, physically identical, in which the self-model is computed twice: copy A does everything the original did, and copy B is computed at the same time, in the same format, in equal detail, and then discarded — it feeds nothing. Behaviour, dispositions and reports are indistinguishable between the two systems. My verdict is that the dark copy adds no experience: there isn't "more" of whatever it is in the second system. But when I try to say why from inside my own picture I get stuck, because on my own story consciousness is supposed to be the dashboard, and copy B is a perfectly good dashboard that just isn't plugged into anything. So either I'm stuck with something uncomfortably close to epiphenomenalism, or I need a consumption condition that the dashboard picture doesn't hand me for free.

So: audit it. Which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? I have a vague feeling the trouble is somewhere in 1/2/3 and maybe again in 4/5/6, but I genuinely can't locate it and I don't know which one I'd give up. Rank the tensions by how bad they are, and for each one say what would have to go and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere in the set. If a single revision would clean up the most, tell me which. And be concrete please — "it depends what you mean by illusion" is the sort of thing I already get from the reading group.
