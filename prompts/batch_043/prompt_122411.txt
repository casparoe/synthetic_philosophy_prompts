hey — I'm in a philosophy of action seminar this term and for the midterm we don't write an essay, we have to hand in a staged dialogue instead (my prof's convinced it forces you to actually steelman the other side). We did Buridan's ass last week and I want mine on that, but on a version of the case that isn't quite the textbook one.

Setup: the donkey is between two bales that are exactly alike in every respect it has any reason to care about — same distance, same size, same freshness — one on the left, one on the right, and it has no left/right preference. The wrinkle: it's hungry, it's getting dark, and another donkey is coming down the road that will take whichever bale ours hasn't started on within a minute. So it has a real reason to eat *one of them* now, and no reason whatsoever to eat the left one rather than the right one.

Write me a dialogue between two people who agree the donkey ends up eating and disagree about how that's possible. One says roughly: when the options are equally good (or on a par — we had a reading arguing for that third relation and I liked it), there's no further question for reasons to settle; reasons can license the disjunctive action, and taking either bale is what eating-left-or-right consists in, so picking one isn't a failure of rationality at all. The other says roughly: that's cheating, a reason to eat-left-or-right can't become a reason to eat-left, and if nothing breaks the tie then the donkey walking left is exactly as unexplained as the donkey standing still — so either some genuine tie-breaker has to show up, or the selection has to be something the will does rather than something reasons do.

Make them actually answer each other, not trade monologues: press the first on whether "just picking" is any different from a random muscular twitch, and press the second on why, if leftness is a reason to go left, symmetry doesn't make it equally a reason to go right so the two cancel, and on whether demanding a tie-breaker just relocates the problem one level up. Also have them deal with the variant where we make one bale very slightly better — one of the readings used that to attack the better/worse/equally-good trichotomy, and I want to see whether either position survives it. Give each the strongest version of their argument and, if one of them has an answer the other genuinely can't handle, say so instead of calling it a draw. Around 700-900 words.
