so my friend (shes a composer) and I have been going round on AI music for months and I cant locate where we actually disagree, so I want to try the decompose-it-into-numbers thing.

Here's a setup with the easy outs removed. The claim I want a number on: Dana is creative in whatever sense of creative carries the aesthetic value we actually care about in art — not some deflated sense nobody would fight over. Give me your credence, and decompose it.

My attempt at the split, fix it if it's wrong. I've been calling these P, H and A:
- P: the artifact-side thing — output is novel, surprising and good, judged from the object and its relation to prior work.
- H: the historical thing — it changes the space of what can be made next, others build on it, it reorganizes a tradition.
- A: the agency thing — it comes out of a subject with reasons and a stake, something that could have failed in the way that matters, and whose artistic agency the work expresses.

Numbers I want: how much of the value we get from art rides on each of P, H and A (give me a split summing to 1); whether H can be had without A; whether P or H alone is enough for the full-strength claim; and how much weight to give a term I think people dodge — whether our resistance to A-less creativity is just status-quo bias about which kinds of minds are allowed to count. Add a term for a cross-link too: how much of our engagement with art is essentially engagement with another mind, and, conditional on that, how likely it is that a system like Dana is such a mind.
