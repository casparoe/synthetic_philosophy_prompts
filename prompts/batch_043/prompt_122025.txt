I run a six-week evening discussion series on social norms — about 30 adults, mixed ages, almost no philosophy background, 75 minutes a session. I want one session to be a game where they feel the tight/loose tradeoff (how much rule-following a society should demand) instead of me standing up and explaining it. Design it.

Two things I want built into the rules rather than named afterwards. First, what I've started calling precaution displacement: in a loose society precautions don't disappear, they get pushed onto individuals and go invisible — locks, background checks, only meeting people through friends — so a society can look relaxed while total caution is unchanged. I want players to have the option to buy private protection when the group is loose, and to notice on their own that they do. Second, the ratchet: loosening should be harder than tightening, so a group that panics once stays tight after the threat is gone. I'd also like them to feel the low-grade resentment of having harmless eccetricities squashed by enforcement that can't tell harmless from harmful.

Give me the setup, the rounds, materials (nothing I can't get at a craft store), point payoffs with no real money, the one thing the facilitator says before play starts and nothing more, and timings. Then a debrief that maps specific moments onto the tradeoff, what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and honestly which part of the real question the game can't stage at all and I'll just have to say out loud.
