I keep going back and forth on the causal exclusion argument and I want someone to argue the side I cant quite make stick. Setup so were using the same terms: if a mental cause M (Sofia's wanting to hail the bus plus her believing that raising her arm will do it) is distinct from the neural realizer N, and N is all by itself sufficient for the effect E (arm goes up), then M either overdetermines E or is excluded by N; systematic overdetermination is supposedly miraculous, so M is excluded, and the non-reductive physicalist is left choosing between identity theory and epiphenomenalism.

The move I want defended: the argument only looks sound because "N suffices for E" is ambiguous between two closures. Theres the weak one physicalists actually accept — every physical event that has a cause has a sufficient physical cause, no dangling causes from outside the physical domain — and the strong one the argument needs: N suffices for E regardless of what M is doing, i.e. under an intervention on N that leaves M free to vary. Supervenience is precisely what makes the strong reading unavailable. So: argue that once causation is understood as contrast- and class-relative difference-making, M and N aren't competing for a single causal slot, and the non-reductive physicalist keeps real mental causation without paying for it in overdetermination.

But spend most of the effort on two objections I keep getting stuck on. First, promiscuity: on a contrastive story, tons of coarse-grained properties of N start looking like causes of E too — N's happening at 3pm, N's being a state an ideal predictor would have forecast, and so on — and the usual filter ("only proportionate or projectible properties count") looks like exclusion smuggled back in. Give me a principled filter that keeps M and throws out the junk, and test it on a variable-realization case: Sofia and a differently wired counterpart realize the same want-belief pair with different neural states and both raise their arms. Second, inheritance: even granting M makes a difference, isn't its difference-making power just borrowed from N, leaving M causally relevant but not genuinely efficous? Why isn't that just epiphenomenalism with better PR?
