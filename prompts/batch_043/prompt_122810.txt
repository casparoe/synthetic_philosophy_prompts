My reading group has an argument that the most popular fix for the logical omniscience problem is empty. The conclusion is way too strong for me to swallow, but I cant find where it cheats.

Setup, which I thought was uncontroversial: your doxastic state is the set of worlds you havent ruled out, you believe P iff P holds throughout that set, and consequence is inclusion of world-sets. So believing P plus P entailing Q gives you believing Q: closure, logical omniscience, which none of us has.

(1) The standard fix keeps the picture but lets the space include impossible worlds, worlds where a classically valid entailment can fail, so [P] is not a subset of [Q] and closure breaks.
(2) If the space can contain worlds where entailments fail, then for any P and any Q at all — Q = P or P, Q with nothing whatever to do with P — there is a model of the space on which you believe P and do not believe Q.
(3) A framework that permits every pattern of belief and non-belief has no content: it says no more than that people believe some things and not others, and it cannot tell apart the mathematician who has not yet worked out some hard theorem from someone who cannot do modus ponens.
(4) And the same goes for every fix that works by weakening the logic rather than by adding a parameter you can dial down the way you dial friction back in: once entailments are allowed to fail anywhere, any pattern is licensed.
(5) So the fix is vacuous, and so is the whole program of modeling doxastic states with worlds.

I would most like to keep (2) and (3), they feel like the solid part, so if something has to go I would rather give something up in the setup. But I suspect (1) is being read two ways and (2) only follows on one of them, and I cant tell which. Name the step, explain why it looked safe, and price the alternatives.

Give me, in this order:
- Which step breaks: about 100 words
- Why it looked fine: about 120 words
- What rejecting each of the neighbors costs: about 200 words
- Where that leaves the problem, honestly: about 100 words

No preamble, and please write in American English.
