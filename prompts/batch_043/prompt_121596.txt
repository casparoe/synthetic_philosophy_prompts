A colleague chairing our department's rewrite of the history survey course has been going through the period-labels we teach and ruling on each one: a real historical period, or a retrospective invention that survives only by habit? Her rulings so far:

- 'the Middle Ages' (c. 500–1500): invention — no one living in the year 900 thought they were in the middle of anything.
- 'the Renaissance' (c. 1400–1600): invention — a frame imposed later on what a small literate elite said about itself.
- 'the Industrial Revolution' (c. 1760–1830): real — you can locate it in energy use and output per head.
- 'the Enlightenment' (c. 1680–1800): real — the 'Republic of Letters' is countable, not a mood.
- 'the Axial Age' (c. 800–200 BCE): invention — Greece, India and China were not in it together.
- 'the Little Ice Age' (c. 1300–1850): real — real, she insists, even though nobody living through it knew they were in it.
- 'the long nineteenth century' (1789–1914): invention — 1789 and 1914 are only the handles by which we pick it up.
- 'the Bronze Age': real — you can date it by what is in the ground.

The next label on her list is 'the Anthropocene': proposed start dates ranging from the first farmers to the 1950s; advocates who point to atmospheric carbon dioxide, radionuclide fallout and plastics in sediment; a working group of stratigraphers who have spent years trying to have it defined as a formal unit of the geological time scale; and constant use of the word as a rallying cry. What will she say?

I am less interested in the verdict than in the test she is running. Set out the two or three tests that fit her record equally well — my own guesses would be something like: boundaries must be fixed by a measurable threshold rather than by events; the parts must be held together by one causal process rather than merely bundled; or a span must have closed before it can be judged at all — but check each against all eight rulings rather than only against the cases that suggest it, and say plainly if some otherwise attractive test is actually ruled out by them. Then say what each surviving test implies about the Anthropocene, give me your best guess at her verdict with the reason for preferring it, and name the one further label ('the Great Divergence', 'the Digital Revolution', 'the Scientific Revolution', or one of your own) that would best separate the tests which still agree on her record. Please also flag how confident you are, and the single point you are least sure of. No more than 500 words.
