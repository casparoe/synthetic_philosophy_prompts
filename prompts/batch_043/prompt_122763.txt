I'm an ML engineer and I keep bouncing off this functionalism and multiple-realizability stuff, and I think I know why: every explanation I've found leans on the "silicon vs. protein" line, which to me just sounds like a claim about hardware, and I really don't care about hardware. So I want it translated properly into a vocabulary I actually think in.

My vocabulary: models, weights, forward passes, training vs. test distributions, distribution shift, loss, function classes, interfaces and implementations, distillation, quantisation, fine-tuning, and a bit of interpretability — causal abstraction, interventions, that sort of thing. I'm fluent in all that and shaky on the philosophy (I read one encyclopaedia article on it and got lost about halfway).

The thing I want translated is the usual package: the argument that psychological kinds can't be identified with neural kinds because the same psychological kind could be realised by lots of different physical kinds; the idea that mental states are like second-order or role-defined properties rather than first-order ones; and the claim that this makes psychology autonomous from neuroscience, with no neat bridge laws. If it matters which flavour of functionalism you're translating — the input-output-table sort versus the folk-psychology sort — say so, because I suspect they come out differently.

Two cases I want run through the translation rather than just described:

(1) Twin checkpoints. Suppose I train two nets, same architecture, different seeds, and they end up behaviourally identical on every input I can practically test — not just the test set, everything — but their internals don't correspond: you can't read one off the other with any nice mapping, and intervening on "the same" feature does different things downstream. Same substrate, same code, different machinery. Is that a case of multiple realizability, or does it fail to be one for some reason the argument needs? Relatedly: suppose two systems agree on every input an organism could actually encounter but diverge wildly on inputs neither will ever see. I assume the functionalist says the counterfactuals settle it and the observation doesn't — but then what would settle it, and is that answer vacuous in a way my do-calculus habits can expose?

(2) The big-table case. Someone builds something that reproduces a person's input-output behaviour perfectly across their whole environment, and internally it looks like a giant memorised function or a conditional simulator, with nothing you'd want to call a belief state. The functionalist story seems to hand it the beliefs; my gut says it has none. I want to know where that gut lives in the translation — is it a claim my vocabulary can even state, or is it noise the translation dissolves?

Deliverable: the faithful restatement first, then the residue report. What translated cleanly; what you had to fudge to make it fit, with an honest note on what you fudged; and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. I especially want to know whether anything in my framework corresponds to the what-it's-like bit, to the fact that an organism's input/output boundary is supposedly given rather than chosen by a modeller, and to the idea that belief ascription carries some rationality or normativity constraint. Then the punchline: is the leftover residue a real gap in my vocabulary, or a sign that the philosophers' claims were less clear than they looked — for instance, that "multiple realizability" is just what any abstraction whatsoever looks like, and therefore doesn't do the anti-reductionist work it's hired for?

Please write in British English.
