I'm putting together a reading-group session on Dennett's crane / skyhook vocabulary from Darwin's Dangerous Idea (1995, Simon & Schuster), and I want to push it somewhere it doesn't normally go. Everyone in the group knows the standard examples — evolution as a crane rather than a skyhook, the temptation to put a mind at the top — so skip that.

The episode I want analyzed is the Human Genome Project and its aftermath. The facts as I'll supply them, so you don't have to rely on shaky memory: a public consortium raced a private company (Celera) to a draft sequence announced around 2000/2001, declared essentially finished in 2003. The surrounding rhetoric was that we were reading the code, the blueprint, the book of life, and that mechanisms for the common diseases would follow. What followed instead, roughly: genome-wide association studies from about 2007 onward turning up large numbers of common variants with individually tiny effects, and a persistent gap between what those hits could explain and the heritability estimates from twin and family studies; then ENCODE in 2012 announcing that something like 80 percent of the genome was biochemically functional, plus fairly immediate pushback, after which that headline figure was quietly softened in later ENCODE summaries. On the more successful side, rare-variant work that really did deliver — people carrying loss-of-function mutations in PCSK9 turn out to have strikingly low LDL, which led to a whole new class of cholesterol-lowering drugs.

Here's what I want, and it's the in-the-weeds part.

First, the mapping. Be disciplined about it: what exactly was playing the skyhook role, who was leaning on it, and what explanatory work did people expect it to do? Note that I don't just mean the obviously theological stuff. There's a subtler candidate: the gene as a program or instruction, a locus of design that explains the trait without anyone having to build the developmental machinery. Say whether that's a skyhook in Dennett's sense or whether the metaphor doesn't transfer, and why. Then do the same for the cranes — which parts of the story were actual grounded machinery, and grounded in what.

Second, a distinction I want you to use and, if it doesn't work, to repair. Call it an 'IOU crane': a posit that does real social and institutional work — funding, headlines, careers — while being explicitly promised to be cashed out as a crane later. My question is whether Dennett's vocabulary can even classify it. It looks like a skyhook from the standpoint of what it's currently doing and like a crane from the standpoint of what it claims to be, and I suspect the framework has no third box. Tell me what it would take to tell, after the fact, whether an IOU was honored or quietly defaulted on, and apply that test to the genome case. Related: Dennett's own gloss is roughly that good reductionists explain apparent design without skyhooks while greedy reductionists try to do it without cranes. Does the genome era fit that description, or is it a different sin — mistaking a parts list for a mechanism, or level-skipping? If it's a different sin, name it.

Third, run it the other direction. What does the episode show about the framework? I can see three candidate verdicts and want you to argue for one: (a) confirmation — the skyhook-flavored promises either got cashed into cranes or evaporated, which is what you'd predict; (b) anomaly — something the framework can't handle, and my pet candidate is that a false skyhook can be productive, i.e. hype that motivates the construction of genuine cranes, which seems either fine for the framework or fatal to it depending on how you read it; (c) a case the framework was never calibrated for, because molecular genetics isn't a fight about design or teleology at all, and the vocabulary just goes slack when you aim it at explanatory practice inside a mature science rather than at creationists and skyhook-seeking cognitive scientists.

Two more constraints, please. Grounding: cranes are supposed to rest on cranes all the way down, but nothing in this story ever bottoms out — chemistry, then physics, then whatever's under physics. If grounding is only ever local, what work is the word skyhook still doing, and is the contrast really a global one or just a pragmatic, local flag on a research program? And heritability itself: a population-level, top-down quantity that does genuine predictive and design work without being grounded in anything micro, but which also isn't remotely mysterious. Is that a skyhook? If your answer is that the framework conflates unexplained with top-down, say so and say what's lost.

Be explicit about where the framework is silent rather than forcing it to speak — I'd rather have two places where you tell me the vocabulary has nothing to say than a tidy five-paragraph essay. No bibliography or citations needed, and please don't pad it; six or seven hundred words is plenty. Oh, and if you end up disagreeing with my framing of the episode or think I've got the sequence of events slightly wrong, say that up front rather than building on it.
