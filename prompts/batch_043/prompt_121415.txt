Okay so this is a weird one, and I don't really know who else to ask.

About three weeks ago I got an email from someone I've never met, in another country. They've got a pile of my old personal data from a breach years ago — old tax scans, stupid forum posts, some medical stuff — and they say they'll dump it publicly on the 14th unless I send them about $900 in crypto. Fine, garden-variety extortion. But the part that's been living rent-free in my head is this: they've gone out of their way to convince me (and honestly they back it up pretty well) that the whole thing is already automated and scheduled. They claim they can't unschedule it, they claim they have no way to, and they openly admit that paying doesn't stop the leak. They also say they picked me out of a few thousand people in that breach because some model scored me as likely to pay — and that the score is what *they* get paid on, not the money. Their bonus, they say, is per person who pays.

So my first reaction was: obviously don't pay, there's literally nothing being offered here, my paying changes nothing about what happens to the data. But my mate Sam (he reads way too much decision-theory stuff) says that's exactly the naive bit: the only reason I got picked is that people like me pay, so refusing is the move that goes together with the leak happening anyway, and the real question is what kind of person I'd want to be *before* I knew I'd been picked. Then my brother says something different again: that the actual decision was years ago — become someone who structurally *can't* pay, no wallet, no way to send it — so that I'd never have been scored as a target in the first place, and that I blew it by being reachable at all.

Two more things, maybe relevant. One: a while back I made myself a rule that I never pay ransoms, and I've told people about it. Now I can't tell whether I'm refusing because it's the right call or because I've started treating the rule as the thing that matters instead of whatever the rule was for. Two: I do think paying has some ordinary downstream effects too — it funds these guys, and my paying probably gets fed back into whatever they use to score the next person.

What's the actually right way to think about this? Should I pay? And please tell me what would change your mind — what argument would flip you, and where do you think your reasoning is most likely to be wrong? I've got until tommorow to decid, so anything helps.
