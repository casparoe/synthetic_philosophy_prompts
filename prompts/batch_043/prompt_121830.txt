For about twelve years I have worked as a project manager shuttling between a Dutch engineering firm and the Japanese company it has a long-standing partnership with, and I have watched one sequence of events repeat itself often enough that I am convinced it is a structure and not a coincidence. I have never managed to describe it in under five minutes, and I have no word for it. I would like to know whether what I keep watching has an established name in any field that studies this sort of thing, and I would rather have an explanation than a label: a bare term would tell me nothing, since I am posted somewhere with no library access and cannot chase down references.

The sequence, as concretely as I can give it. A measurement problem comes up in a joint review. Everyone in the room already knows the numbers; there is no factual dispute. The Dutch side states the problem unhedged, in front of the group, with no framing that would let the Japanese engineers absorb it without losing standing in front of their colleagues. What arrives is not, I think, the content — it is a second message about the relationship, which the speaker did not send and does not hear. The room cools.

Then the first turn of the loop. The Dutch manager registers the cooling and adds a repair: that he is saying this precisely because he thinks they can fix it, that he would rather be straight with them than let them hear it from someone else later. It is meant as reassurance, but it is delivered in exactly the register that caused the difficulty, and it asserts bluntness as a virtue, so it now also says that the way the remark was received was itself wrong. The temperature rises.

The mirror case has a different shape. A Japanese engineer says, about the same numbers, something like: regarding the measurements, there may be a few points that one could perhaps look at again, possibly. The Dutch manager records the issue as settled. Nothing has been transmitted. And the repair — that this is only a personal impression, that he may well be mistaken — removes whatever content was left, so the repair here does not amplify but evaporates. My first question is whether these are one phenomenon or two: a failure mode that inflates a message with relational content, and one that drains a message of it, with each side's attempted fix using the very form that caused the trouble.

After two or three rounds each side has reached a moral verdict. The Dutch side concludes that the Japanese side never says what it means and that you can never know where you stand. The Japanese side concludes that the Dutch side says everything and cares about nothing. Both verdicts concern truthfulness, but in different directions: one is a kind of withholding, the other a kind of excess. I would like that asymmetry addressed too — whether the two are the same failing, and whether the fact that the sequence reliably ends in mutual accusations of dishonesty tells us anything about whether the polite fiction and the brutal candour are doing the same job.

I know the rough contrast between high-context and low-context communication, but those labels only mark the difference in style; they say nothing about the dynamic I am describing. So, in asking for a name, I am really asking for the carving: what exactly this mechanism is, how far it extends, and which nearby things it must be kept apart from. I have some guesses about the near-misses and would like them tested. It does not seem to be a plain misunderstanding, since both parties grasp the factual content perfectly. It does not seem to be simple rudeness either, since the same bluntness is experienced as considerate when it comes from someone within the same norms. And I am unsure whether it depends on the cross-cultural setting at all, or whether the same loop runs between a blunt and an indirect person inside a single culture — and whether long familiarity dissolves it or merely makes it run faster, since in my experience the people who have worked together for years are not immune.

If the thing genuinely has no established concept, please say so plainly rather than stretching something to fit, and then coin a term for it, define it tightly enough that I could tell a case of it from a non-case, and name the cases that fall just outside it.
