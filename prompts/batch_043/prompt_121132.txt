I run a small rationality reading group — 14 to 18 adults, one 100-minute session in a borrowed library meeting room, no projector, no laptops, and nothing I could not carry in a tote bag: index cards, markers, envelopes, a shoebox or two, and a bag of something edible as the only stake. I have been reading this material online for about fifteen years and maybe a third of the group has read some of it, so I would rather the session not be a rehash of a post everyone has already seen.

The thing I want them to feel, rather than be told, is the one Hofstadter staged in his 1982 careenium paper, the one reprinted in Metamagical Themas: inside a large caring system there are tokens, simmballs, that stand in for objects outside it, and the question is who shoves whom. Does the meaning of a symbol push the physical substrate around, or does the substrate shove the symbols around with the meaning coming along for the ride? When the system cares about something real, does the caring reach the real thing through the token, or does it terminate at the token and at most push substrate?

Design me a runnable activity — a game or structured simulation with real choices and small stakes — in which the group collides with that question before anyone names it. Constraints:

1. No lecture up front, and no vocabulary dump. Participants should not hear grounding, derived intentionality, original intentionality, or the word simmball until the debrief.

2. The force has to come from the rules, roles, and incentives, not from anything I say afterwards. People should be doing something — moving tokens, choosing, betting, blocking — at which they can succeed or fail, and the ambiguity about what their success was actually attached to should be something they walk into and notice, not something I announce.

3. Please build in three specific mechanisms. First, a silent re-grounding: partway through, without telling the people working at the token level, permute the correspondence between token types and the real objects they stand for, so that careful token work keeps succeeding at the token level while coming apart at the referent level. Second, a reverse-direction round in which the referent changes first and the token layer has to catch up, so that causation visibly runs world to symbol to action. Third, a self-directed token: at least one token should stand for the participants themselves in their role as substrate, so that what they do next depends on what that token means, and the same physical card produces different behavior under different correspondences.

4. Give me rules, materials, roles, a payoff scheme that needs no money, and a minute-by-minute timeline for 100 minutes including roughly 20 minutes of debrief.

5. The debrief should map four or five specific moments in the activity onto specific claims in the dispute, and should give me the strongest version of both sides — the symbols really do the pushing, and the substrate does all of it — in the form they will actually come up in the room. Include the three questions I should ask at the reveal and the order to ask them in.

6. Tell me what will predictably go wrong. I expect the group to treat it as a puzzle to win, someone to meta-game the scoring, someone to notice that I am the only real source of correspondence and conclude that the whole thing was rigged, and someone to conflate the map changed with the symbol meant nothing. Handle those and add the ones I have not thought of.

7. Be frank about what the activity cannot reproduce and what I will have to supply in words. In particular: in the room the correspondence is stipulated by me, and stipulation is just more symbols, whereas the interesting case is a system that builds its own grounding from scratch; and nothing that can happen in a library meeting room discriminates between the physicalist story and the top-down story, since both predict every observable outcome. Say plainly which part of the idea I will simply have to assert.

8. It should work on a committed physicalist and on someone who is not one, without presupposing either.

Lead with a single sentence naming the core mechanism that carries the whole activity, then give the design, timeline, debrief, failure modes, and limits. No preamble before that sentence, and no restating back to me what I asked for. Answer in American English.
