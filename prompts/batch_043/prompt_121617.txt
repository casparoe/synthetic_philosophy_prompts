I help run my school's philosophy club, and I'm putting together a one-page handout that sorts people by their verdicts on trolley cases rather than by the labels they'd pick for themselves. Please build me a decision tree with eight to ten nodes and five to seven leaves.

A few constraints I care about. No node may name a theory or use the words "intend", "foresee", "double effect", "deontology" or "consequentialism" — half my club has heard those words and will just answer so as to match whichever view they already like. Each node should instead be a concrete case with the physical setup spelled out (who is where, what the agent's body does, what happens if they do nothing), or a plain question about whether two cases have to be answered the same way.

The cases must include: the standard switch; the footbridge push; a loop variant in which the one person's body is itself what stops the trolley; a case where the one dies as a by-product of the very redirection that saves the five; a variant in which he is gravely injured rather than killed; and one of my own — the only way to stop the trolley is to drop a sleeping passenger through a trapdoor, and he dies without waking, without struggling, and with no one but you ever knowing. Tell me which node that last one belongs at, or why it is a bad node.

Leaves should include: an absolutist one; two distinct leaves on which the numbers are not what matters but the agent's relation to the death is, one turning on whether the death is what produces the rescue and one on whether the agent aims at it; a leaf where the numbers eventually win; a maximizing leaf; and a leaf for someone who thinks the whole pattern of our answers is explained by features of the cases that aren't morally relevant at all.

Where combinations of answers are unoccupied, mark the empty cell and give a one-line diagnosis — incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored? I suspect someone who permits the push but forbids the switch will be such a cell. Finally, flag any two leaves your tree can't actually separate and say what extra case would break the tie.
