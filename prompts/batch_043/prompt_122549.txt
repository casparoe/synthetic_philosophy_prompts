I'm writing a one-page handout for a graduate seminar on measurement and AI safety, and I need a taxonomy of proxy failure organized as a decision tree rather than as a list of named effects: given a description of a case, the tree should route you to a leaf that names a failure mode and says in a clause why that mode is hard to fix. Please build it from the logical space instead of from the labels: settle the branching questions first, say which combinations of answers are genuinely reachable, then locate the familiar failure modes in the cells they occupy, and for each reachable-but-empty cell say whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unnamed and unexplored.

The branching must include, at minimum, whether anyone other than the principal is choosing behavior with an eye to the proxy and against the principal's aim; whether such a chooser is a separate agent or the very system the proxy is meant to shape; whether the proxy-to-target correlation survives intervention on the proxy rather than mere observation of it; and whether the correlation survives in the tail of the proxy's range even though it holds in the ordinary region. Add one split of your own that tracks whether a failure announces itself, in the sense that the proxy's own numbers start to look wrong to whoever is watching, or whether it stays silent while the proxy keeps improving and the target stagnates; I care most about which cells are silent, because those are the ones automated monitoring cannot catch. Required leaves, under whatever names you prefer: a failure where the proxy has no causal path to the target at all; a tail-region failure where extremal values are reached by routes that do not carry the target with them; a strategic failure driven by outside agents responding to the proxy; and specification gaming where the exploiting is done by the optimized system itself. Where your questions cannot separate two leaves, say so rather than inventing a distinction.

Test the finished tree against five cases and report where each lands: a hospital system that bonuses surgeons on thirty-day survival and finds surgeons quietly declining the sickest patients; a foundation that pays a literacy programme per word-per-minute read aloud and finds children reading fast nonsense; a city that ranks schools on the share of pupils above a fixed test threshold and finds resources flowing to pupils just below it; a lab that breeds mouse lines for time-to-tumour and gets mice that die early of something else; and a hiring model scored on the retention of candidates it recommends, which learns to favour people unlikely to be hired anywhere else. If any case falls on more than one leaf, say which question is at fault.

Write all of it as continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold text, lists, or tables, and finish by stating how confident you are in the tree overall and which single branch point you trust least.
