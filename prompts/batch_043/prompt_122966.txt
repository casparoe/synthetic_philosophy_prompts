writing up comments for a zoning board hearing and ive tied myself in knots over the "right to the city" thing. as the housing advocates use it, it sounds like they mean anyone who wants to live here has a claim to live here, which i just dont get: a right to the city cant be a right to move into somebody elses block, and if I've lived here twenty years, paid into the tax base and maintained my house, the claim has to be mostly mine and my neighbours', not some family three states over who'd like to move in. the principle I keep coming back to is something like a residency-stake view: the strength of your claim to shape a place scales with how much of your life is staked in it. and isn't local control obviously the more democratic option anyway, on the subsidiarity logic that decisions should be made by whoever is closest to what's affected? so who actually has a right to the city here, and why wouldnt it be the people already there?
