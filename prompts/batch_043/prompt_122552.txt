I've been going round on the torture vs dust specks thing on my own (no philosophy background, just thinking about it late at night) and I think I've talked myself into a corner. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. I bite the bullet on the original case: some enormous number of people each getting a dust speck in the eye for one second is worse than one person being tortured for fifty years.

2. What pushes me there is the one-more thought: there's no number where that many specks beats the torture but one fewer doesn't. A single extra speck can't be doing the work. So there's no line to draw anywhere on the scale, so the huge number wins.

3. But I also think there's a real difference between something bad you recover from and something bad that wrecks you. If a bad thing leaves literally no trace — you're back to exactly how you were a second later, no memory of it, nothing downstream — I want to say it doesn't make the outcome worse at all.

4. And on that basis: one person getting a speck in the eye every second for the rest of their life, with no trace each time, is not even close to as bad as that same person being tortured for fifty years.

5. I also think the obvious thing to do in the original case is prevent the torture, and that a decent person would refuse to be the one handing out the specks.

So: does this hang together? I don't trust my own judgment here — if I had to guess I'd say 4 is the weak one, but that's a guess. Please go through them, rank the tensions by how bad they actually are, and for each one tell me what I'd have to give up and what that would cost me. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most. And if one of these isn't a real tension at all, or I've set the whole thing up wrong, say that straight out rather than being polite about it.
