hey — hoping you can help me think something through before thursday.

I've just joined the ethics panel at my food co-op (about 4,000 members; we publish short non-binding "should members do X?" notes that people actually read). I've read through two years of minutes and I cannot work out what rule we've been running. The cases so far:

1. Driving 2km to the shops when you could walk. Ruling: don't. The minutes say the extra fuel adds a real if imperceptible increment of harm, spread across zillions of people, and that no single driver is pivotal for anything — and that this doesn't get you off the hook. They explicitly flag that this is different from case 2: here there's no threshold, you're adding a tiny increment, not gambling on being the one who tips an order.

2. One 200g pack of factory-farmed mince from our own butcher counter. They reorder in whole-animal units, so any given pack has maybe a 1-in-700 shot of nudging the next order up by one animal. Ruling: don't.

3. A leather belt from an online closing-down sale: shop shuts next week, owner retiring, no reorder, no reinvestment of the money, plain packaging, and the member works from home so basically nobody will ever see her wearing it. Ruling: no obligation — buy it, don't feel bad.

4. Voting. Ruling: you should vote in a close election; no obligation to vote in one that's certain to be 70/30. One member filed a dissent saying that's inconsistent: if a 1-in-700 shot is enough in case 2, we can't suddenly get squeamish about a 1-in-a-million shot here.

5. Chicken from a new supplier whose ordering system is opaque — might be whole-bird thresholds, might be weight-based, might be a franchise where your purchase changes nothing at all. Ruling: don't, because you can't rule out being pivotal and skipping is cheap.

6. Six members splitting a whole pig from a farmer, where if any one drops out the other five would buy a smaller joint instead. Ruling: don't, and the minutes add "this one isn't marginal — you're each forking over a sixth of the animal."

New case, which is mine to draft: the summer party. The beef was pre-ordered and paid for in March, non-refundable, 40kg from a local farm; it's a one-off, the farm's herd plan is fixed for the year and won't respond to anything about the party; whatever isn't eaten goes to a food bank whose users' demand doesn't feed back into any order anywhere. So the production channel is dead and the money is long gone. But the member in question writes our newsletter column, sits on the board, and three other members have literally said they're waiting to see what she does before they decide — including a new member who's been going back and forth. Her choice will be public.

So: does she have to skip the burger? What rule are we actually running, and what other rules fit those six verdicts just as well? What does each of them say about the party? Give me your best guess with the closest analog among 1–6, and if our precedents genuinely don't decide it, tell me what we'd be committing ourselves to with each answer. Also tell me what further case I should put on the agenda that would flush out which rule we're on. And I'd like to know whether it matters that it's *her* — board member, columnist — rather than some random new member at the same table, or whether that's a red herring.
