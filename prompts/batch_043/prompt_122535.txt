Hey — I could use a hand designing one session of my seminar. It's a mixed room, about 16 philosophy and CS master's students, 75 minutes, one whiteboard, printed 5x5 grids, index cards, no laptops. The topic is inverse reinforcement learning: inferring what someone wants from watching what they do. Most of them will arrive assuming it's basically mind-reading from video footage, and I want them to leave with the reverse feeling — that the behavior alone constrains almost nothing until you bolt on assumptions, and that the assumptions are carrying nearly all the weight. They should hit that before I name it.

Rough shape I have in mind (tell me if it won't work): a "demonstrator" student gets a secret reward sheet and walks a route on the grid while everyone else writes down what they think she's being rewarded for and how confident they are. Three wrinkles I really want in there:

1. what I'm calling the prohibition problem — she avoids certain cells, and you cannot tell from the route whether she dislikes them, is forbidden from entering them, or simply doesn't know they're there. I want the room to discover that "avoided" doesn't mean "disliked" by getting burned on it, not by being told.
2. a transplant test — same demonstrator, new grid where the previously locked cells are open and an old shortcut is gone. Half the room is only permitted to copy her actions, the other half has to state a reward function, and we score everyone's predicted route on the new map. I expect the cloners to loose badly there, but tell me if I've got that backwards.
3. one demonstrator who is deliberately not optimal — she's satisficing, or following some rule like "keep the wall on your left" — while still producing a route that looks perfectly sensible on the first grid, so that whatever reward everyone infers from her is a confabulation.

I'd also like two demonstrators with genuinely different reward sheets to produce identical routes, revealed late, plus a moment where the class realises that the reward function is really a whole family of them. A colleague mentioned something about adding a potential-based term and the optimal policy not budging at all; I'd like that to land as something they can't resolve from the evidence in front of them, not as a slide.

Can you give me the rules and materials, a payoff scheme that needs no money and penalises hedging (I was thinking they have to commit to an exact route and bet points on it), a minute-by-minute timeline for the 75, and a debrief that maps specific things that happened in the room onto specific assumptions — optimality, known dynamics, the choice of features, and whatever tie-break rule you use to pick between explanations that fit equally well, since a couple of them will have heard of "maximum entropy" and will want to know where it slots in. No lecture up front, and nobody should hear the words "identifiability" or "degeneracy" until afterwards.

Finally: what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this — the secret leaking, someone proposing the reward "just take this exact route," a demonstrator being sloppy — how I should handle each, and honestly which part of it I'll definately just have to say out loud because the game can't produce it. I'd rather know before I'm halfway through.
