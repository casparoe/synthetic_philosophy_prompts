For most of my career I was a particle physicist, a good deal of it spent building and vetting statistical models, and I have only lately begun reading philosophy. One idea I keep meeting is Goodhart's law — the observation that a metric, once it is turned into a target to be optimized, tends to stop measuring what it was originally calibrated to measure. To me this looks like nothing more than overfitting in a policy setting: the metric has overfitted to the historical data on which it was validated, so the moment you begin optimizing for it, it ceases to track the underlying quantity. If that is the whole story, Goodhart's law seems to add nothing beyond what we already knew from statistics. Am I missing something that would make it a genuinely distinct phenomenon, rather than a re-labeling of overfitting?
