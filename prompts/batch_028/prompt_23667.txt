I went hiking last weekend and kept seeing animals in pain — a deer with a broken leg, a bird with a tangled nest, a rabbit that looked like it had been hit by a car — and ive been trying to figure out whether I actually have a moral obligation to reduce that kind of suffering. The thing thats been bugging me is the non-identity problem: if I intervene to reduce the suffering of wild animals, I change which animals come into existence, so I can't say I've helped any particular animal, only that the world has different animals in it. Write an essay on whether the non-identity problem actually undermines the moral case for reducing wild animal suffering, or whether there are ways around it (e.g. totalist or prioritarian framings that don't need to compare the welfare of the same individual across counterfactuals). Weigh the main arguments on both sides, but don't end on a non-committal note — defend a verdict.
