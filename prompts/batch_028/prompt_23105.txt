I'm working on a novel and one of my characters is a die-hard Cartesian about animals -- he genuinely buys the idea that a dog in pain isnt suffering, it's just a clock striking the hour, and I want him to be a sympathetic, well-argued version of that view rather than a cartoon villain. The trouble is the book is set in the present, so at some point he has to deal with stuff Descartes never had to. Two things I'm stuck on: first, what's the strongest reply a real Cartesian could give to the modern neuroscience of animal pain -- does the fact that we can now map pain processing in animal brains actually threaten the automata view, or is there a move Descartes himself would make to keep it intact? And second, Descartes drew the line between humans and animals on the basis that only we have an immaterial rational soul, but my character lives in a secular world that doesnt really believe in immaterial souls, so what's he got left to hold that line with? I don't want a gotcha, I want the best version of the defense and where it starts to crack.
