I chair the evidence review panel for a public health risk report, and two panelists I consider epistemic peers keep giving different probabilities for the same hazard — 2% vs 8% — even after a full evidence exchange where everyone's seen each others data and reasoning. The report has to commit to a single number by the end of the week. Aumann's agreement theorem (if two people are rational, share a common prior, and their posteriors are common knowledge, they should have converged by now) makes the fact that they havent a little worrying. Can you draft the short memo I'll circulate before Thursday's vote? It should recommend a concrete procedure for what the panel does next, be honest about why the obvious options — averaging, or just taking the more cautious number — don't work, and say what the fact that they still disagree after the full exchange actually tells us about the two of them.
