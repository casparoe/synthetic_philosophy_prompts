I've been mulling over this scenario where a prophet tells a guy he's going to die on a specific day, and no matter what the guy does — stay in the city or run to the mountains — he ends up dying that day anyway. Most people I've talked to say this is a problem for free will, but I keep feeling like it's not really about free will at all. Can you write a dialogue between two people arguing about this? One should defend the view that the prediction really does take away the guy's freedom (he cant avoid dying), and the other should push back and say the guy could have done otherwise but just wouldnt have, so the real problem is about how accurate predictions work, not about free will. Both should make their strongest case and actually respond to each other's points, not just talk past each other.
