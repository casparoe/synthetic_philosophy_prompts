I'm giving the toast at my editor's retirement dinner. She spent thirty years writing about Rousseau's views on theater -- the argument that the stage is where we practice a kind of safe self-deception, where we admire virtue from a distance instead of feeling it, and where the audience's pleasure depends on staying just far enough away from the characters to keep us cold. The wrinkle is that she also ran a community theater for two decades and was genuinely, visibly happy doing it, so the toast can't just be "she was right about theater and the rest of you are fools for watching it." Half the room are theater scholars who will catch every reference to Pygmalion and his two little plays; the other half are her family and they just want to be told she was a good person. Write a five-minute toast that builds on the real tension between what Rousseau argued and what she actually did with her hands, and that lands as affection rather than as a lecture.
