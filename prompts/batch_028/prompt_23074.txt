The question:

Problem 4. An urn contains N balls numbered 1 to N. A ball is drawn uniformly at random, and you are told the number on it is 97.
(a) (4 pts) Give a 95% upper bound on N that does not assume any prior distribution over N, and justify it.
(b) (3 pts) Explain how this is supposed to transfer to a claim about the total number of humans who will ever be born, and state the resulting bound, taking the number of humans born so far to be about 10^11.
(c) (3 pts) Now assume a prior P(N) ∝ 1/N. Write down the posterior (up to a normalizing constant), compare its 95% upper bound to the frequentist one, and say whether the posterior has a finite mean.

Student's answer:

(a) The 95% upper bound is 97 / 0.05 = 1940. Justification: for any fixed N ≥ 97, a random draw exceeds 0.95N with probability at most 0.05, so the procedure "report n/0.05 as the upper bound" misses the true N in at most 5% of experiments, and therefore there's a 95% probability that N ≤ 1940.

(b) If we are a uniformly random sample of all humans who will ever be born, then our birth rank n (about 10^11) plays the role of the drawn ball number, and the same bound gives N < 2×10^12 with 95% confidence, i.e., only about 2 trillion more humans will ever be born. The main objection is the reference class problem: the bound depends on which reference class you pick (all humans who will ever be born, humans of this civilization, etc.), there's no canonical choice, and the "random sample" assumption isn't obviously true anyway, so the whole argument rests on it.

(c) With P(N) ∝ 1/N and likelihood P(97 | N) = 1/N for N ≥ 97, the posterior is proportional to 1/N² for N ≥ 97 (normalizing constant about 97, since the sum of 1/N² from 97 to infinity is about 1/97). The 95% upper bound comes out to about 20 × 97 = 1940, i.e., the same as the frequentist bound, so the two approaches agree. The posterior mean is 2 × 97 = 194, so the expected total is about twice the number of humans born so far.
