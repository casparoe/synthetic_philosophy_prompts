I've been going down the Quine moral-philosophy rabbit hole lately (yeah, I know, niche). The part that stuck with me is "On the Nature of Moral Values": his take that moral values aren't objects in our best scientific theory, that ethics ends up on a coherence theory of truth while science keeps some claim to correspondence, and the whole altruistic-vs-ceremonial-virtues split where prudence doesn't even count as a moral value to begin with.

Now here's the thing he obviously never had to answer. Take a modern LLM that's been trained to generate moral reasoning and moral judgments. On Quine's framework, is the LLM actually making moral judgments, or is it just running coherence with no observational anchor at all? And if it's not really making moral judgments, is the gap between it and a human performing a ceremonial virtue while lacking the underlying altruistic concern a real one, or is it the same kind of thing? I'm curious weather his framework has any actual teeth here or if it just dissolves into "ethics isn't a science, so whatever."
