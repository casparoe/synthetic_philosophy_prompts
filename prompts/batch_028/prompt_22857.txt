I'm the engineering representative on my company's data governance committee. Our launch policy states that "any production system that processes EU personal data requires the approval of the Chief Data Officer before launch," and its only escape hatch is that "the Chief Data Officer may delegate approval authority in writing." The Chief Data Officer left fourteen months ago and the position remains vacant, so neither clause can be satisfied by anyone. A product team wants to launch a new EU-facing feature within the next ten days and argues that because there is no Chief Data Officer, the approval requirement cannot apply and that the standard launch process (engineering sign-off plus legal review) is sufficient. The privacy lead argues that the requirement is simply not met, that no one is authorized to waive it, and that the launch must be blocked until the board names a delegate. The committee meets Thursday and needs a ruling before the launch window closes. Please draft the decision memo I'll circulate: it should recommend a concrete ruling with a time frame. I suspect this is an instance of a well-known puzzle in the philosophy of language about sentences that refer to things that don't exist; I'm not a philosopher, so I'd like the memo to name the relevant framework and use it to pick a side, while engaging the losing side's strongest argument rather than just restating the policy text.
