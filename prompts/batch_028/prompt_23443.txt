I've just had a long argument with a friend about accuracy-first epistemology. He maintains that, on the accuracy-first approach to epistemic utility theory, the epistemic utility of a credence is a function of its accuracy, and that this in turn entails probabilism—the thesis that rational credences must satisfy the axioms of probability. I pushed back by saying that accuracy-first epistemology is, at bottom, only a theory about the value of doxastic states, and that it has nothing to say about the rationality of forming them, so I don't see how it could ground any decision-theoretic conclusion like probabilism. Is my friend right that accuracy-first epistemology entails probabilism, or am I right that it is merely a value theory and is silent about rationality?
