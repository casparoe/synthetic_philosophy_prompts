I recently started working on evaluating a large language model, and I've run into a methodological question I can't resolve on my own. When I ask the model to explain why it did something — why it refused a request, or why it gave a particular answer — it produces a confident, coherent, and often very plausible account of its own reasoning. But these accounts are not stable: rephrase the question and it gives a different account, and sometimes the account contradicts what the model actually did. The accounts seem to track the framing of my question more than any consistent internal process. One colleague says we should treat the self-reports as authoritative, on the grounds that they are the model's own testimony about itself and we have no better access to what is going on inside it. Another says we should discard them and evaluate only the behavior, on the grounds that a system that can generate a fluent but unstable story has no stable states to report on. I'm writing the methodology section of our report this week and need to decide how to treat the self-reports. How should I think about this disagreement, and what is the defensible position to take?
