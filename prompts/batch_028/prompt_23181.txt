I recently had a long argument with a friend about two thousand dollars a bank deposited into my account by mistake, which I have not yet reported. My friend says I must return it, on the ground that if everyone kept money paid to them in error, the whole practice of accurate payment would collapse, since no one could trust that a transfer was made in the right amount — and so my action, if it were everyone's rule, would destroy the very practice it relies on. I concede that a rule as broad as "keep any money paid to you by mistake" could not plausibly be everyone's rule. But I am not sure the test is stable, because the answer seems to depend on how I phrase my rule: stated narrowly, as "keep an overpayment only when the payer is a large institution unlikely to notice or be much harmed," it looks like it could be everyone's rule without the practice collapsing. So is my friend right that I must return the money, or does my objection show that his test simply yields whatever answer the person formulating the rule wants?
