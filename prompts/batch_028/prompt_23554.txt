Suppose you are a decision-making agent in a very large world, facing a large number of one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma encounters with randomly sampled opponents. Some of these opponents run a decision algorithm that is highly evidentially correlated with yours (they would make the same choice you would in the same situation), while the rest run uncorrelated algorithms. Please argue that the rational strategy in such a large world is to defect, and that evidential cooperation is a small-world phenomenon that breaks down in large worlds. Your argument should explicitly account for the evidential link between your own action and the composition of the population of decision algorithms, and it should identify the threshold on the fraction of correlated opponents above which cooperation becomes rational and explain why a large, algorithmically diverse world places you below that threshold.
