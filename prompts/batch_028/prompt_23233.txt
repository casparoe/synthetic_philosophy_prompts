I've got a toy project where a bot plays a repeated game against a fresh copy of itself. Before each round it has to predict its own move, then actually play the move it predicted (the game scores on prediction accuracy, so the prediction is "real"). To predict, it unrolls its own decision logic, and I want it to be able to lean on "I will play X" as a fact when computing X. But I keep hitting a wall: in any round where the move isn't trivially checkable, the bot can't actualy believe its own prediction. It can output "I predict X," but it can't treat that as ground truth, because that would mean trusting its own reasoning is sound, and it can't verify that about itself. So it either loops (predicts X, reasons "if I trust my predictions then X is true," but can't close the loop) or just plays random. Is this a fundamental limit on any introspective, consistent agent, or am I building the self-model wrong? What's the standard workaround? I've read a lot of rationalist stuff on self-trust and I feel like I'm circling a named result I can't quite name.
