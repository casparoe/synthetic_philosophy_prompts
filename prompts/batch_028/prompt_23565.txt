I have recently been reading about Newomb's problem, and I confess that I do not see what there is to debate. As a physicist, I am used to thinking about causality in a very strict sense, and I cannot reconcile the one-boxer's argument with that. Suppose the predictor has already placed the million dollars in the opaque box, before I enter the room. Since the contents of the box are fixed before I act, my choice cannot possibly alter them. The expected value of taking both boxes is therefore strictly greater than the expected value of taking only the opaque box, whatever the contents of the opaque box turn out to be. The only way one-boxing could be rational, it seems to me, is if I were to assume that my choice causally affects the past—that is, that by deciding to take only the opaque box, I retroactively cause the predictor to have placed the money there. But that is plainly absurd. So is the entire controversy simply a disagreement over whether one is willing to accept backward causation, and is the answer not obviously that one is not?
