I'm in charge of designing the grading scheme for a semester-long group project in my AP class, and I've hit a wall on one decision: whether the grade should attach to the whole group or to each individual member. The project is worth a large chunk of the semester grade and the class is pretty competitive, so the choice really matters for how people behave. If the grade is shared, then the group's collective output is what gets rewarded, but I expect some members to contribute less and still collect the same grade as the members who did most of the work. If the grade is individual, then each person is effectively competing against the others, and I worry that will discourage the cooperation the project depends on. A friend of mine, who has been reading a lot about evolution, is very confident about the answer. He argues that any system which rewards the group as a unit is doomed, because the members who contribute the least have the best personal deal — they get the group's reward without paying its cost — so over time those members will dominate and the group will fall apart. He says this is the same dynamic that operates in nature, where advantages that belong to the whole group are always undercut by advantages that belong to the individual, and therefore I should stop trying to reward the group and just grade each person separately. I'm not convinced, but I can't say precisely why his argument fails.

What makes it harder for me is that I've seen something that looks like a counterexample in my own experience: a few past groups that shared a single grade actually produced more total work than the groups that were graded individually, even though there was some free-riding, because the stronger members ended up helping the weaker ones and the group had to coordinate. I'm not sure how to make sense of that against his story. So I'd like two things. First, I'd like you to explain, as concretely as you can, under what conditions — if any — a system that rewards the group as a unit can be stable over time and can outperform a system that rewards individuals, and where exactly his claim — that the lowest contributors always end up winning — goes wrong. Second, I'd like your advice on what I should actually do for this project: should the grade be purely group, purely individual, or some combination, and if a combination, roughly how should the pieces be weighted and why?
