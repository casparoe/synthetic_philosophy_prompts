I retired from materials science a couple of years back and, being hopelessly bored, I've been falling down philosophy-of-physics rabbit holes. I finally worked my way through David Albert's *Time and Chance* (2000) and most of it clicked, but I keep getting stuck on what I think is a real fork in the road that the book doesn't quite force you to confront. On one side you have Albert's own setup: the fundamental laws are time-reversal symmetric, and the whole thermodynamic arrow comes from postulating a Past Hypothesis — the universe began in a fantastically low-entropy macrostate — plus the statistical postulate that we should treat all microstates compatible with that as equally likly. On the other side there's a different move I keep seeing gestured at but never seen laid out honestly: instead of a low-entropy condition at one end only, you put low-entropy boundary conditions at *both* ends of cosmic history, so entropy is minimal in the middle and climbs in both time directions, and the "arrow" is really two arrows pointing away from a low-entropy middle.

I was discussing this with a friend over coffee and we kept going in cirles, so I'd love a straight comparison of the two pictures. Specifically: what does each one actually explain (the approach to equilibrium, the existence of records and memories, why we seem able to influence the future but not the past), what is the real cost or ad hocness of each, and is the two-sided version a genuine competitor or just a relabeling of the Past Hypothesis with extra steps? And if you can, tell me whether Albert himself would count the two-sided proposal as a serious rival or a red herring.
