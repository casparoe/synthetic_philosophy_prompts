For a graduate seminar in epistemic social choice, I would like a dialogue between a proponent of the epistemic-democratic reading of the Condorcet jury theorem and a critic who argues that the theorem's independence assumption fails in real electorates. The two speakers share the premise that, in the election under discussion, most voters relied on the same handful of news outlets, and they must work out whether the theorem still licenses confidence in the majority's verdict. The critic should distinguish independence of the information voters receive from independence of their final judgments; the proponent should defend the theorem as far as it can be defended, including under weakly correlated errors, and should address the corollary that if the average voter is more likely to be wrong than right, larger majorities are worse, not better. Both speakers should be competent and should respond to each other's actual points rather than talk past each other.
