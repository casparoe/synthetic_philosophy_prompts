I've been thinking about the absent-minded driver problem (the one where a driver has to take the second of two identical-looking highway exits but can't tell them apart, gets 0 for exiting at the first one, 4 for exiting at the second, and 1 for missing both, and the optimal move is to randomize continue with prob 2/3 at each intersection). I keep thinking it's more relevant to building AI agents than decision theorists usually give it credit for. Please describe the most important implications of this problem for designing decision making in agents with imperfect memory, like an RL agent that loses part of its context window, or an LLM agent that can't reliably remember what it did earlier in a task. In particular I'm curious about what it implies about optimizing policies (the rule you commit to before you start) versus optimizing the action at a single decision point, and about how to do expected utility math when the same policy gets evaluated at multiple indistinguishable decision points without double counting.
