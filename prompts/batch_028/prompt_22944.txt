I'm working on a long-form piece about how game theorists decide which strategies a rational player ought to rule out, and I've hit a knot around iterated elimination of dominated strategies that I keep failing to straighten out. The strictly-dominated case feels uncontroversial to everyone I've talked to, but the moment you switch to weak dominance the whole procedure starts to misbehave, and I increasingly suspect that much of the disagreement is really about an unstated choice buried in the definition of dominance itself: whether the strategy doing the dominating has to be a pure strategy or whether it may be a mixed one. Could you walk me through the conceptual difference between iteratively deleting a player's strategies that are weakly dominated by some pure strategy versus iteratively deleting the strategies that are weakly dominated by some mixed strategy? In particular, is it genuinely possible for a strategy to be eliminated under the second rule but to survive the first, and if so, what's the intuition for how a blend of a player's other strategies can dominate a strategy that no single one of them does?

I'd also like that tied to two facts I keep hearing gestured at but don't feel I actually understand: that under weak dominance the order in which strategies are deleted can change which ones survive, and that iterated elimination can strip away strategies that still turn up in some Nash equilibrium. Since I'm going to be explaining all of this to a curious but non-specialist readership, I care more about the underlying logic and the intuition than about a mechanical walkthrough, though a small finite game of your own construction would help me anchor the discussion and check that I'm picturing it right.
