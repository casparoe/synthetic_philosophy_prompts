I just got appointed to the risk-communication committee for our hospital's new genetic screening program, and our first real fight is over what the patient-facing consent materials should say about the risk numbers. The statisticians want to print individualized estimates ('your personal risk is 3.2%'), the ethicist on the committee keeps objecting that a probability is only a long-run frequency so we can only honestly tell patients '1 in 300 people with your profile develop the condition', and a consultant of ours keeps pushing for language about the patient's own biological tendency ('your body carries a 1-in-300 chance'). I need a short written opinion to circulate before tuesday's meeting that takes a side: which framing should the materials use, with reasons that actually engage the other two positions philosophical objections instead of just waving them away, plus a concrete fallback for when we can't compute an individualized estimate at all.
