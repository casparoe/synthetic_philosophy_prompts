I've been reading secondary literature on Nāgārjuna's tetralemma (the catuṣkoṭi), and as someone who thinks in terms of classical logic I keep running into what looks like a genuine inconsistency. My understanding is that the tetralemma is applied to any thesis p by rejecting all four alternatives: that p holds, that p does not hold, that both hold, and that neither holds. But the law of excluded middle guarantees that one of the first two alternatives is true, so rejecting all four appears to assert a contradiction. If Nāgārjuna is making that positive claim, the tetralemma seems to refute itself the moment it is turned on the thesis that emptiness is itself empty. How, then, is the tetralemma supposed to work without simply being false?
