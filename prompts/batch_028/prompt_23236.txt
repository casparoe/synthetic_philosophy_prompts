I write about science and ideas for a living, and I've been roped in as a guest member on the advisory panel of a small science-and-philosophy museum. They're deciding how to frame a joint exhibition on George Berkeley, and the curaters are split: one side wants the idealist bishop, the other is pushing for a show that foregrounds The Querist, his 1735-37 series on Irish banking, paper money, and credit. The panel needs a written opinion by Friday recommending a framing, so please draft the short memo that does exactly that: pick a framing (or a specific hybrid), give it a one-sentence thesis the show could hang on, and argue why it's the better story. Include the strongest case for the losing side and why it still loses, and take seriously the possibility that his attack on abstract ideas is doing real work inside the economic arguments rather than being a biographical footnote.
