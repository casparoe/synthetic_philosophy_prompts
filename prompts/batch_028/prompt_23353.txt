I'm a novelist, and on the side I run a small interview series in which I talk to philosophers about ideas I use in my fiction. Next month's guest is a philosopher who defends the claim that Parfit's hitchhiker case shows that rationality can rationally require irrationality. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view: I want at least one question each on the objection that the hitchhiker's beliefs are unstable or self-defeating, so the case shows only that certain beliefs are irrational, not that rationality itself requires irrationality; on whether the case depends on the public visibility of rationality — that is, on drivers being able to detect whether a hitchhiker is rational — and what happens to the argument if rationality is not publicly detectable; and on what the case implies about whether "rational" is the sort of property an agent can know she has. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give.
