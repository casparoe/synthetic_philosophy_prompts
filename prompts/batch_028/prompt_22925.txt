I have been thinking on my own about the experience-machine thought experiment from the 1970s, the one in which a machine can deliver to you any experience you want, indistinguishable from real life, and which is usually used to argue that pleasure cannot be all that matters in a good life. I keep returning to the question of what Epicurus would have made of it, because he is perhaps the one philosopher who ought to be the most tempted to plug in. Taking his actual views seriously — that the aim of life is tranquility and the absence of pain, that one should distinguish natural and necessary desires from empty or unnecessary ones, and that friendship is the single most important ingredient of a happy life — what do you think Epicurus would have concluded about a machine that could give him exactly a quiet, simple existence: the pleasure of a modest meal, long conversations with friends, freedom from fear? In particular, I would like you to work out whether friends inside the simulation would count as real friends for him, whether the mere knowledge that nothing is real could itself spoil the tranquility he was aiming at, and whether he would end up agreeing with the modern philosophers who use the machine to deny that pleasure is the only good, or defending hedonism from the inside.
