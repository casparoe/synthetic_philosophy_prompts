I've been going back and forth on what iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies (allowing domination by mixed strategies) actually guarantees. My working assumption is that since a strictly dominated strategy is never a best response to any belief about the opponent, and the procedure only ever removes strictly dominated strategies, the set of strategies surviving the full iteration must be exactly the set of strategies that are a best response to some belief in the original game — which would mean the iteration does no real work beyond the first round of elimination. If that's wrong, where exactly does the argument break down, and what is the correct characterization of what surviving IESDS buys you?
