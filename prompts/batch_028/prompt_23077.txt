I just got into this like two hour argument with my friend Marco (physics grad student, basically a Penrose devotee) about whether the Lucas-Penrose argument actually shows the mind is non-computational, and we got way past the basic "you can see the Gödel sentence is true" move—he kept insisting that's the easy part—and landed on what I think is the real problem, the unwinding thing. Like, okay, grant that my mind is modeled by some consistent theory T and I can see G_T is true. But then what I can actually see is now T plus G_T, which is a new consistent system with its own Gödel sentence (call it G2) that I can also see is true, and so on forever. So the argument doesn't just show I'm not T, it seems to demand that I track this infinite tower of consistency statements—Con(T), Con(T+G_T), Con(T+G_T+G2), ... all the way up—and Marco's whole response was basically "yeah that's exactly the point, the mind has this direct non-algorithmic access to truth," which I think just kicks the can. I was at this awful coffee shop near campus the whole time and I still cant tell if the unwinding objection actually sinks the argument or if it's just a restatement of it.
