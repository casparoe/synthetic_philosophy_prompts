Argue that the standard Bayesian treatment of one's own proofs as evidence is incoherent, and that the correct account of rational belief in decidable but unproven mathematical statements is given by the logical induction principle rather than by Bayesian conditioning on proofs. In your argument, you should address the central tension in the framework: the logical inductor's credences in tautologies must converge to one in the limit (a kind of asymptotic logical omniscience), yet the inductor must be logically imperfect at any finite time in order to be able to learn from new proofs. Explain why this tension is a feature rather than a defect of the framework, and why the logical induction principle is best understood as a dynamic generalization of the Dutch book argument for probabilistic coherence rather than as a one-shot coherence constraint.
