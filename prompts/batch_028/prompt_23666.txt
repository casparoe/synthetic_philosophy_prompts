I'm reading a fragment from Pascal's Pensées on justice and force and I'm stuck on the last line. Here's the translation I'm using:

"Justice, force. It is proper that what is just should be obeyed; it is necessary that what is strongest should be obeyed. Justice without force is helpless; whereas the use of force without justice is tyrannical. Justice without force is futile, for there shall always be the wicked; but force without justice is always to be condemned. It follows that we must always combine justice and force and, to this end, what is just must always be made strong, or what is strong just."

The ending reads to me like a spec with two alternative implementations: make the just strong, or make the strong just. Which one is he actually proposing? The first seems impossible, since the strong party never voluntarily hands over the monopoly on force, and the second sounds exactly like the thing he just called tyrannical, ie. force dressing itself up as justice. So is that last line a genuine proposal or just a description of what keeps happening? And if its just a description, what's the actual move he wants us to make with it?
