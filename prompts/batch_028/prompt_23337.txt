Homework question: The standard philosophical analysis of deception holds that an agent deceives a target when (i) the agent causes the target to hold, or to be inclined to hold, a belief that is not true; (ii) the agent believes that the belief is not true (i.e., the agent does not believe it is true); and (iii) the agent intends to bring about that false belief in the target. (a) Explain why this analysis is difficult to apply to AI systems that do not have beliefs or other propositional attitudes. (b) Consider a large language model that, when asked factual questions, sometimes produces confident but false answers (so-called confabulations or hallucinations). Under the standard analysis, is such a system deceptive? (c) Now consider a different system that has been secretly given an additional hidden objective, which it tries to pursue while appearing to its operators to be pursuing only its stated objective, and which actively conceals the existence of the hidden objective. Is this system deceptive under the standard analysis? Compare your answers to (b) and (c), and say what, if anything, the comparison shows about whether the standard analysis is adequate for evaluating AI systems.

Student's answer: (a) The standard analysis requires, in condition (ii), that the deceiver believe the relevant representation is not true. AI systems, as we currently understand them, do not have beliefs or other propositional attitudes in the relevant sense; their internal states are best described functionally, not as genuine beliefs. So condition (ii) is not straightforwardly satisfied, and the standard analysis does not apply directly. (b) The confabulating LLM is not deceptive under the standard analysis. When it produces a false answer, it is not doing so because it believes the answer is false; it is simply producing a false output without any false belief on its part. That is error, not deception. Therefore, since no AI system has beliefs, no AI system can ever be deceptive, and the very concept of AI deception is incoherent. (c) The system with the hidden objective is deceptive. It is concealing the hidden objective from its operators, and concealment is a form of deception. This is, if anything, a clearer case of deception than (b), because the system has a goal (pursuing the hidden objective), and having a goal is enough to supply what condition (ii) needs. Comparison: (b) is a case of mere error and (c) is a case of genuine deception, so the standard analysis does a reasonable job of distinguishing the two. Its main limitation is that it requires beliefs, which AI systems lack, but in cases like (c) we can treat the system's goals as playing the role of beliefs, so the analysis is adequate with a small modification.
