I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and I'm preparing a piece on the epistemology of testimony. I've been following the reductionism versus anti-reductionism debate, and I'm stuck on a point about anti-reductionism. If testimony is a basic, non-inferential source of justification — on par with perception or memory — then it seems the only reason to accept a testimony is that it is a testimony, and any independent check on the speaker's reliability is exactly what anti-reductionism rules out. So how can the anti-reductionist ever be justified in rejecting a testimony, say when the speaker has a known history of lying? Doesn't anti-reductionism collapse into the view that testimony is prima facie authoritative in a way that makes it impossible to doubt it?
