I was reading a pop history book on the idea of the soul (definitely not a philosophy textbook, just one of those narrative ones) and it had a section on Avicena's 'flying man' thought experiment -- the one where you imagine yourself just created, floating in empty space with no body, no hands, no eyes, nothing you can feel, and you still know you exist. It stuck with me because I've been playing with a VR headset for a few months and the experience is kind of the exact opposite of the flying man: in VR you stop noticing your real body and start feeling like you have a different one, the avatar, so you're aware of a body that isn't yours while being completely unaware of the one that is. A friend of mine said that means VR is actually a flying man case where the 'body' you're aware of is a fiction, and I argued it's more like a counterexample, and we couldn't resolve it.

The question I actually want to get at is what Avicena himself would have made of all this. If you could drop him into a modern conversation about the metaverse and mind uploading -- the whole 'could we scan a brain and run the person on a computer' idea -- what would he have said? I keep guessing he'd be pretty unimpressed by the uploading thing, since for him the soul isn't a pattern or a program you can copy, it's this immaterial thing, so a digital version of you would be like a really good drawing of a person rather than the person. But I honestly don't know his actual position well enough to be confident, and I don't want to just project some generic 'old dualist' onto him. I'm making a short video essay on the flying man and VR and I'd like to get the philosophical stakes right instead of just doing an old-man-vs-new-tech bit, so I'd rather have a careful answer than a confident wrong one.
