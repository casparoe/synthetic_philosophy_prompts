I'm working on a novel and one of my characters is this intense, health-obsessed guy who reinvents his diet every few chapters — one stretch it's all raw food, the next he's on some fasting protocol, then he's back to eating whatever and calling it listening to his body. I want his relationship to food to feel grounded in something real rather then just a plot device, and I keep coming back to Nietzsche as a template, because I know he was basically a hypochondriac who was constantly changing his diet and chasing new cures. So I figured I'd ask: what do you think Nietzsche would actually make of the modern diet-optimization culture, the whole intermittent-fasting / keto / biohacking thing where people track every meal and treat their body like a machine to be tuned?

More specifically, would he read that culture as a genuine affirmation of life, a yes-saying to the body and its appetites, or would he see it as just another flavor of the ascetic ideal, a kind of ressentiment against the body dressed up as self-care and discipline? And how does his own chaotic, self-contradictory relationship to food cut across that? Would he recognize himself in these people, or would he be contemptuous of them? I'd love a take that's specific to who he actually was rather than a genric be-strong Nietzsche answer.
