I'm a PhD student in philosophy (free will and moral responsibility track) and I've been going back and forth with my advisor about Frankfurt cases for like two weeks now and I genuinely cant settle which response to them is the strongest. Quick setup for context: Frankfurt's whole move is to build a case where an agent (let's call him Johnny) does something entirely on his own volitions, but there's a counterfactual intervener (a neuroscientist, or Big Jim, or whatever) who *would* have stepped in and forced a different outcome if Johnny hadn't done the thing on his own — except the intervener never actually has to, because Johnny acts first. So Johnny is responsible for what he did, yet it looks like he couldn't have done otherwise, which is supposed to refute the Principle of Alternate Possibilities (PAP: you're only morally responsible for what you did if you could have done otherwise).

Anyway, I want you to rank the following responses to the Frankfurt challenge in order of how compelling you find them, most to least. I'm describing them by their content rather than by who's famous for them, so don't worry about names:

1. The irrelevance objection — the counterfactual intervener is just irrelevant to Johnny's actual responsibility, so the case proves nothing; Johnny is responsible in the ordinary way and the hypthetical helper changes nothing.
2. The flicker-of-agency objection — Johnny still has a mere "flicker" of the ability to do otherwise in the actual sequence of events, so PAP isn't really violated after all.
3. The reasons-responsiveness / guiding-principle response — PAP is just false, but responsibility is preserved because it depends on the agent's will being appropriately guided by their own reasons (or second-order volitions), not on alternative possibilities.
4. The strengthened / no-flicker Frankfurt case — you can build a version where even the flicker is gone (the intervener is guaranteed to act, or Johnny's decision is fully determined), so the flicker objection has no purchase.
5. The libertarian self-forming-action response — at genuinely undetermined self-forming moments the agent does have real alternative possibilities, so PAP and libertarian freedom survive Frankfurt cases.
