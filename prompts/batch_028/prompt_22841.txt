I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and I am trying to work through a rather peculiar assignment I have been offered before I commit, so I would value a careful second opinion. A research institute is offering to pay me a very large sum to take part in what they call a "cognitive immersion" study, and the protocol runs as follows. I am put under a general anesthetic; a fair coin is flipped; and then, depending on the result, one of two things happens. If the coin lands heads, I am woken on a Monday, I conduct a single interview for the study, my memory of the entire episode is then erased, and I go home. If the coin lands tails, I am woken on a Monday, I conduct an interview, my memory is erased, I am put back to sleep, I am woken on a Tuesday, I conduct a second interview — which, because of the wipe, is indistinguishable to me from the first — and then my memory is erased and I go home. At no point during either interview am I told the day of the week or the outcome of the coin.

The complication is that the institute wants me, at the moment of the interview, to do two separate things. First, to state my probability that the coin landed heads. Second, to name a price at which I would be exactly indifferent between buying and selling a contract that pays me $100 if the coin landed heads and nothing if it landed tails; the same contract is offered to me at each interview, so in the tails scenario I would be offered it twice. I have run this past several colleagues, and I am getting three different answers. The first group says I should assign the coin a probability of 1/2, because it is a fair coin and nothing I experience upon waking is information about how it landed. The second group says I should assign it 1/3, because there are three possible interview-occasions — Monday in the heads case, and Monday and Tuesday in the tails case — I have no way of telling which one I am in, and only one of the three is a heads-occasion. The third group, and the one I find most baffling, says I should assign a probability of 1/2 while pricing the contract at one-third of its $100 value, that is, that the right probability and the right price can legitimately come out different. I keep assuming that third position is simply incoherent — that if the fair price of the contract is $33.33, then my probability that the coin landed heads ought to be 1/3 as well, and that you cannot consistently hold the two apart — but I cannot quite pin down the argument that would force the two to agree. So could you help me see whether that third position is genuinely incoherent, and, setting that aside, tell me what probability I should actually assign and what price I should actually name when I sit down for that interview?
