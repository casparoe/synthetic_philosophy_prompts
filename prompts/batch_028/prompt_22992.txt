I'm on the investment committee of a small quant fund, and we have to decide which of two candidate strategies to run for the coming year. The choice flips depending on which average of returns you use, and the committee is evenly split. Strategy A has a higher arithmetic mean annual return (about 18%) but much higher volatility (about 40%); Strategy B has a lower arithmetic mean (about 13%) but much lower volatility (about 20%). Judged by arithmetic mean, A wins comfortably; judged by geometric mean (the compound annual growth an investor actually realizes), B edges ahead.

Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Friday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action — which strategy to adopt, and which measure the committee should use to evaluate and report strategies going forward — with a time frame for the decision. It should explain, in terms a non-specialist on the committee can follow, why the two averages diverge and which one tracks what we actually care about, namely the long-run growth of the fund's capital, given that we reinvest returns and that LPs can redeem on short notice. And it should honestly engage the strongest case for the losing side — for instance, the argument that over a single finite year, expected (arithmetic) return is what matters and the geometric mean over-penalizes variance — rather than just restating the log-utility / Kelly argument.
