The standard pitch in my courses is that the coherence arguments (the Dutch book stuff) justify expected utility maximization: if your beliefs and preferences are incoherent, you can get Dutch booked, so you should make them coherent, and coherence just is expected utility maximization. But I keep feeling like there's a gap in this reasoning that nobody in my seminar seems to want to talk about. The Dutch book theorem, as it's usually presented to us, is about whether your credences satisfy the probability axioms. It doesn't seem to say anything about whether your preferences over outcomes satisfy the von Neumann-Morgenstern axioms. So even if your credences are perfectly coherent, I'm not sure how you get from there to a full expected utility maximizer, which also needs a utlity function over outcomes.

So can you write me an essay on whether the coherence arguments really do justify expected utility maximization? I want you to weigh the arguments for and against — like, is the gap I'm sensing real, or is there a way to bridge it (maybe via de Finetti's previsions or something) that I'm missing? And please actually reach a conclusion and defend it, don't just lay out both sides and shrug at the end.
