"Explain the pessimistic meta-induction as an argument against scientific realism, and give one standard response to it."

Here's what the student wrote:

The pessimistic meta-induction (PMI) is an argument against scientific realism. The argument looks at the history of science and observes that many past scientific theories that were very successful — that is, that explained and predicted well — were later shown to be false, or at least not literally true. Examples include the phlogiston theory of combustion, the luminiferous aether, and the caloric theory of heat. Since most of these past successful theories turned out to be false, the PMI argues that, by induction, we should expect our current successful theories to be false as well. Therefore, we have no good reason to believe that our current best scientific theories are true, and in particular we have no reason to believe that the unobservable entities they posit, like electrons or quarks, really exist. This is an argument against realism, because realism is the view that our best scientific theories are (approximately) true and that the unobservable entities they describe actually exist.

So the PMI shows that our current theories are false, and that realism is unjustified.

One standard response to the PMI is the no-miracles argument: the success of science in explaining and predicting is a miracle unless our theories are true, so the best explanation of science's success is that our theories are (approximately) true. This response refutes the PMI by showing that success is in fact evidence of truth, which is the opposite of what the PMI assumes.
