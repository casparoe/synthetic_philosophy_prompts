I've been going back and forth on the difference between Bratman's account of joint action and Searle's idea of collective intentionality, but I keep getting stuck on whether they are really answering the same question. Bratman seems to treat joint action as something built out of individual agents who have shared intentions, mutual beliefs, and intentions that mesh with each other. Searle seems to say that we need a primitive we intention, not just a pile of individual I intend statements, so the basic unit is already collective. What I want is a careful comparison of the two views, not just a summary of each one.

Can you help me see where the disagreement actually lies, especially on three points? First, does Bratman's account explain why joint action has normative force, or does it just describe the psychological ingredients? Second, is Searle's collective intentionality a genuine explanatory primitive, or does it risk naming the phenomenon and calling it a theory? Third, how would each view handle a case where one participant stops caring halfway through, or where a group acts together without every member fully agreeing?
