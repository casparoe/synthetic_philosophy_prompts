I've been going back and forth on a point in Parfit's argument that what matters in survival is not personal identity but the relation he labels R — roughly, psychological continuity with the right kind of cause — and I can't quite get it to click. In the straightforward teletransporter case the story is clear: the copy on Mars bears R to the original, so what matters holds, even though the original is destroyed and so identity does not survive. But I can't see how the argument is supposed to work on the reductive view, since on that view identity is supposed to consist in psychological continuity, which looks just like R. If R and identity really are the same relation on the reductive view, then Parfit's conclusion that identity is not what matters looks trivially false on that view, and the whole argument seems to have bite only against the non-reductive, further-facts view. Is that right, or am I missing something about how R and identity can still come apart even on the reductive view?
