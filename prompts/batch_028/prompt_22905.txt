Write a dialogue between two Hegel scholars in a seminar arguing about whether the war thesis in the Philosophy of Right is load bearing or dispensable. One holds that the ever-present danger of war is what keeps the state from rotting into a mere administrative machine, and that the citizen's willingness to risk death for the universal is a constitutive moment of ethical life, so drop it and the whole account of the state collapses. The other holds that the war moment is only a contingent, historically indexed concession that Hegel's own logic of recognition and the Idea of World History are steadily working to make unnecessary, i.e. that a world state or universal recognition would render it obsolete, so its not essential to the system. Both should be able to point to the actual passages (the danger-of-war point, the citizen risking death, the march of reason) and genuinely press each other on their strongest objection rather than just restating their positions. Keep it to about a page and a half.
