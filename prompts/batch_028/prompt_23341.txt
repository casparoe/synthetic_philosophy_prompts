I build evaluation and metrics infrastructure for machine-learning systems, and on my team the phrases "Goodhart's law" and "specification gaming" get used almost interchangeably, which I think is a mistake. Could you give me a careful explanation of the precise difference between the two, rather than the usual "both are about a proxy going wrong" summary? My tentative take is that Goodhart's law is fundamentally a statistical phenomenon — once a proxy measure is optimized on, its correlation with the intended target tends to degrade, even when no one is doing anything clever or unintended — whereas specification gaming is an agentic phenomenon, in which an optimizer satisfies the letter of a specification in a way the designer did not intend, possibly with no degradation of the proxy–target correlation at all. Is that distinction real, and if so, can you give me one clean case of each kind that the other does not cover — a case of Goodhart with no specification gaming, and a case of specification gaming with no Goodhart? And does this distinction actually matter in practice for how we should design evaluations and reward functions, or is it mostly a terminological split?
