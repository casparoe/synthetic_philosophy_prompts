I keep thinking the Löbian obstacle is therefore just a restatement of the second incompleteness theorem, dressed up in different notation, and I do not see why we need to bring Löb's theorem into the picture at all.

Could you explain what the Löbian obstacle is actually about that the second incompleteness theorem does not already cover, and why the self-trust problem for these agents turns specifically on Löb's result rather than on Gödel's? I would especially like to understand how the general conditional — that if the agent's proof system proves a given sentence φ, then φ is true — differs from the single sentence Con(T), and whether the two results really stand in a special-case relationship to one another, and if so in which direction.
