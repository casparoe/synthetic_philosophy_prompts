I've been personally stuck on whether some of my own beliefs are held for the right reasons lately (like, am I only believing X because it'd be really uncomfortable not to), and then two friends of mine got into an argument about exactly this at dinner and I genuinely couldnt tell who was right. One, Sam, said there's no such thing as a "wrong kind of reason" — a reason to believe p has to be a reason that p is true, like evidence, and so "believing X would make me less anxious" isnt a reason to believe X at all, it's just a reason to want to believe it, or a sign I'm being biased. The other, Priya, said those are totally real reasons, just not truth-directed ones, and that the fact that believing something would make you feel better is a genuine consideration that bears on whether you should believe it, it's just that for belief the "right" kind of reason is evidence, so it's a wrong kind of reason, not no reason. Sam said that's just stretching "reason" until it means nothing, because a real reason to believe should make you more justified, and a comfort-based motive makes you less justified, so it can't be a reason to believe, it's a reason to distrust the belief. Priya said she wasn't calling it a reason to believe, she was calling it a reason full stop, one that can actually cut against the belief (since holding it for comfort would impure your motive), and that the very fact that we treat these motives as something to resist shows they have normative force, so they're reasons. I don't know who's right and it really matters to me. Can you go through their exchange and say who's right about each specific point, and what if anything they're both missing?
