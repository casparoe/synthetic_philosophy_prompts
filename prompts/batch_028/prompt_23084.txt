I'm a software engineer so I keep framing this as "the data was alredy in the database before the model was even trained, so how can it be evidence for the model?" which I think is basically the old evidence problem in Bayesian confirmation theory. As I understand it: if the evidence E is already known, then P(E) = 1, and Bayes' rule can't give the new theory T any boost from it — you end up with P(T|E) = P(E|T)P(T), which is at most P(T). The classic example is the anomalous precession of Mercury's perihelion, which was measured long before general relativity. I'd like a short dialogue between a Bayesian confirmation theorist and a likelihoodist trying to figure out whether Mercury's perihelion actually confirms GR. The Bayesian has to deal with the fact that P(E) = 1 means there's no update, and the likelihoodist has to deal with the fact that their account is about likelihood ratios, not about the agent's actual credences. Make both of them competent and make them actully respond to each other's arguments, not just take turns restating their position.
