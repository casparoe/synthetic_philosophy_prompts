So I'm not a philosophy person at all, I just got obsessed with the "is time even real" question a few weeks ago and started reading stuff on my own, and I stumbled on this old paper by a guy named McTaggart from like 1908. I found a copy floating around online and I'm trying to make sense of this chunk:

"Positions in time, as time appears to us prima facie, are distinguished in two ways. Each position is Earlier than some, and Later than some, of the other positions. And each position is either Past, Present, or Future. The distinctions of the former class are permanent, while those of the latter are not. If M is ever earlier than N, it is always earlier. But an event, which is now present, was future and will be past. ... For the sake of brevity I shall speak of the series of positions running from the far past through the near past to the present, and then from the present to the near future and the far future, as the A series. The series of positions which runs from earlier to later I shall call the B series."

Okay so I get the rough shape of it — there's a "static" way to order events (earlier/later, which he calls the B series) and a "moving" way (past/present/future, the A series). But here's where I get stuck. He says the A-series distinctions are the ones that are NOT permanent, and the B-series ones are permanent. So on the surface the B-series seems like the more objecive and stable one, right? But then he goes on and claims the A-series is actually MORE fundamental to time than the B-series, and because the A-series turns out to be contradictory he concludes that time ITSELF is unreal, not just that "the present" is unreal. I keep wanting to push back and say, well why not just drop the A-series and keep the B-series, that looks like a perfectly consistent way for time to be? Like, why does the A-series being broken drag the whole thing down with it? Can you walk me through why he thinks you can't just have the B-series standing on its own, and how this lines up with the presentism/eternalism thing I've been reading about?
