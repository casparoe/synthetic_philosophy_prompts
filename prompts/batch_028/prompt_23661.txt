I'm prepping a question list for a podcast interview with a philosopher of economics who's out with a new book arguing that pure time preference is a real, justified part of rational disounting — not just a proxy for the interest rate or for risk aversion. I want the questions to go past "so do you think we should discount the future or not." Can you draft eight to ten? Specifically I want at least one question on how they carve pure time preference out of the observed discount rate, given that most of the observed rate seems to be opportunity cost and risk rather than a bare preference for the present, and a follow-up that presses on what happens to that carve-out when the thing being discounted isn't money but something like the welfare of future people, which you can't invest. Also want a question that forces them to say whether their view is compatable with taking existential risk and the far future seriously, with a follow-up keyed to the "the rate is small enough that it doesn't matter much" reply I expect. And one on the non-identity problem — the fact that which people exist in the future depends on what we do now, so comparing future welfare to present welfare isn't as clean as a discount factor suggests.
