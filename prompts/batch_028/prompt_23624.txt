I've been bugging my friends about this for a while and I still can't figure out where my reasoning is going wrong, so I thought I'd ask here. So I work in a lab, and a couple of years ago we published a result showing that a certain drug compound reduced tumor growth in a mouse model. I read the paper, checked the numbers, and was pretty convinced it was real. Now some totally independant lab has replicated the result and published it, and my postdoc is all excited, saying "this is strong evidence the compound actually works!" I get it — I do feel a little more confident now — but when I try to formalze it I get stuck.

The problem is I already believed the result before I saw the replication. In my head, the probability the compound works was already pretty high, and the replication was something I expected, so it barely moved my credence. But then it seems like the replication isn't really "evidence" for me, even though it obviously is for the postdoc who hadn't seen our paper. And it generalizes in a way that feels crazy: once you've learned the literature, it looks like nothing new a patient or a new experiment tells you can count as evidence of anything.

So my questions are: is the replication evidence for me or not? And if it is, how does that square with the fact that I already believed it? What am I missing?
