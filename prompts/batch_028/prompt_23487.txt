I've been working through Wittgenstein's private language argument recently — I keep thinking about it through the lens of the problem of the criterion, i.e. when it is even possible for a judgment to have a standard of correctness — and I keep running into a specific spot where I think the standard presentation leaves something important unresolved, so I'd like a dialogue that gets at it. The PLA is usually laid out as showing that a language whose terms refer only to the speaker's own private, immediate experience cannot exist, because once the referent is wholly private there is no way for the speaker to tell whether they are applying a term correctly or merely think they are. But I've noticed that the usual defenses of private language come in two distinct flavors, and I'm not convinced the PLA actually engages with either of them on their own terms.

The first is what I'll call the memory defense: when I encounter a sensation and want to check whether it is the same one I previously labeled, I can consult my memory of the original case, and that remembered case functions as my standard of correctness. The second is what I'll call the reliable-indicator defense: even if no one could verify from the outside that my use of a term tracks the right sensation, the term could still have meaning so long as its use is reliably caused by, or functionally locked to, the relevant sensation, rather like a reliable gauge, and the PLA simply fails to reach that possibility.

Could you write a dialogue between a Wittgensteinian who defends the argument and a philosopher who defends the possibility of private language, in which the Wittgensteinian is specifically required to answer both of these defenses? I'd like the Wittgensteinian's reply to the memory defense to turn on the point that memory is itself a fallible faculty with the same lack-of-an-independent-standard problem (you cannot distinguish a genuine memory of the original sensation from a mere present impression of it, so it cannot serve as the yardstick), and the reply to the reliable-indicator defense to turn on the point that a reliable causal or functional connection that leaves no room for error is not a meaning but a reflex or a mere disposition, because for a term to mean a sensation the speaker must be able to be wrong about it. And I'd like the private-language defender to push back on both of these replies in a way that is genuinely competent rather than a strawman — for instance, by arguing that the Wittgensteinian is quietly stipulating that meaning requires a public standard of correctness, or that the requirement of a possibility of error is a feature of public language rather than of meaning as such. Please keep both characters equally strong and have them respond to each other's actual points, and end the exchange on a clearly identified point of genuine impasse rather than resolving it too neatly, since I think the live disagreement is more interesting than any tidy conclusion.
