I am a second-year undergraduate taking a graduate-seminar-style course on Rousseau this term, and I have been asked to help a faculty friend who sits on the university's Committee for Arts and Civic Life with a memo that is due to the full committee before next Tuesday's meeting. The committee funds and co-sponsors student performances, and a drama group has applied for a co-sponsorship and a modest production grant for a new staging of Molière's *Le Misanthrope*, in which the honest but rigid Alceste is repeatedly mocked and the charming, manipulative Philinte comes off as the wiser man. A minority of the committee, to their credit, does not want to object on taste or budget grounds but has raised a substantive concern: that a university body which claims to form citizens is in some tension with publicly underwriting spectacles that reward sociability, flattery, and self-display, and that the committee should say something principled rather than simply approve. The student who applied has a ready answer, which I think the memo must take seriously: that a free university is not a moral commune, that audiences are capable of irony and of judging what they see, and that a committee which vetoes artistic projects for their moral effects has stepped far outside its mandate. Our Rousseau course has just finished the 1758 *Lettre à M. d'Alembert sur les spectacles*, in which Rousseau argues against d'Alembert's proposal for a theater in Geneva and is notably harsh toward *Le Misanthrope*, so I have read the letter, but I do not want the memo to be a summary of it; I want a document the committee can actually use. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate: it should reach a concrete recommendation on the application, with a time frame for any conditions, and it should engage the strongest version of the civic-formation objection rather than simply restating it, while also giving fair and forceful treatment to the freedom-of-the-university objection, including a point at which the two positions genuinely diverge rather than merely differ in emphasis. If the recommendation is conditional, the conditions should be ones a drama student could plausibly accept or reject, and the memo should close with a bottom line the committee could vote on as written.
