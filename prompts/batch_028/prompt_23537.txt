I am a novelist, and I would like you to write a short story that dramatizes Simone Weil's critique of political parties. For Weil, a party is less a forum for deliberation than a machine for generating collective passion: it manufactures a shared fervor in its members and, in doing so, subordinates each member's independent judgment to the party line, so that the party as an institution gradually becomes an end in itself, distinct from the cause it claims to serve. Set your story with a character genuinely devoted to a just cause who comes to see that the party advancing that cause has become such a machine, and who must therefore choose between remaining in the party—retaining its power and the warmth of its collective fervor—or leaving it, and thereby preserving independent judgment at the cost of that power. Let the story turn on the specific tension Weil identifies between loyalty to the party as an institution and the individual's duty to think for herself, and leave the reader with the weight of the trade-off rather than resolving it with a tidy moral.
