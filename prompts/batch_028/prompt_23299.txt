Please argue that rank-dependent expected utility theory, although it was developed to accommodate violations of the independence axiom, is structurally unable to represent ambiguity aversion of the Ellsberg kind. In particular, explain why the probability weighting function in the rank-dependent framework operates on the cumulative probabilities of a single, known probability measure, why this means that no rank-dependent functional can represent both of the characteristic preferences in the two-color Ellsberg problem (the preference for the red-bet over the black-bet, and the preference for the yellow-bet over the red-or-yellow bet), and why this limitation shows that a genuinely ambiguity-sensitive theory must depart from the representation of beliefs as a single probability measure, as in the multiple-priors or capacity-based approaches.
