I am preparing a colloquium talk on Grice's account of conversational implicature, and I have come to believe that there is a genuine tension in his framework that I want to press. Please argue that Grice's notion of speaker meaning is not the right notion on which to base his theory of conversational implicature. In particular, explain the problem that, on Grice's intention-based definition, attributing speaker meaning already requires the audience to recognize the speaker's intention to be recognized, and show why this makes the mechanism by which implicatures are generated either circular or under-determined, since the cooperative principle and the maxims seem to presuppose the very common ground about intentions that they are supposed to generate. Then sketch what a non-circular account of implicature would have to look like.
