I'm a PhD student in the philosophy of language, and I've been circling a particular sub-question in the literature on rigid designators and the necessary a posteriori that I'd like a careful, argumentative essay on. We all know the headline result — that on Kripke's causal-historical theory of reference, identities like "Water is H2O" are necessary truths that we nonetheless come to know only a posteriori, because both "water" and "H2O" are rigid designators and the identity holds in the actual world. What I want to press is a sharper controversy: whether the *necessity* of such natural-kind identities is genuinely secure, or whether it quietly depends on a contingent fact, namely which substance the original act of reference-fixing latched onto. The worry runs roughly like this. "Water" gets its reference fixed by some initial dubbing that latched onto a particular sample, and which substance that dubbing latched onto is plainly a contingent matter — had it gone to XYZ rather than H2O, "water" would have rigidly referred to XYZ, and "Water is H2O" would have been false in every world. So the necessity of "Water is H2O" seems to ride on a contingent reference-fixing fact, which is precisely what a robust, unqualified necessity should not do.

Please write an essay that weighs this controversy and reaches a defended conclusion. Steelmann both sides: the Kripkean reply that the necessity is genuine once we hold the reference-fixing fixed (or that the relevant modal quantification is anchored to the actual baptism, and that this is all the necessity such a truth can have), and the objection that this makes "Water is H2O" at best a "relative" or "anchored" necessity, and so not necessary in the robust sense in which genuine necessities are necessary. I don't want a neutral survey — I want you to land somewhere determinate and justify it. In particular, say something clear about whether the necessary a posteriori survives as a stable and genuine category of metaphysical necessity, or whether it dissolves into something weaker — a contingent truth, a merely relative necessity, or a confusion between epistemic and metaphysical modality.
