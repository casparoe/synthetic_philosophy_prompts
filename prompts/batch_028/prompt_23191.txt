I'm a novelist, and I'm trying to think through a decision I can't quite make on my own. My agent has told me, candidly and I believe correctly, that the novel I have just finished is unlikely to find a publisher; the market is against it, and I have no reason to doubt her assessment. What I have noticed, though, is that the books I have written while I genuinely believed the previous one would be a success have been my best: I take more risks, I sustain my work, and I carry myself in interviews in a way that seems to earn people a second look. So I am considering a course of action I find difficult to defend. For the coming months I will keep the rejection out of my thoughts, I will read only the favorable coverage of comparable books, and I will try to bring myself to genuinely believe that this book will be published, on the ground that the belief will make me a better writer and so raise the real probability that this book, or the next, succeeds. I have even begun to ask myself whether I should decline a well-paid teaching post this autumn on the assumption that I will be a published author. Is this a rational plan, or am I simply deceiving myself? And if there is a defensible version of it, what exactly does that version consist of, and at what point does it stop being defensible?
