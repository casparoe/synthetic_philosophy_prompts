"Aumann's theorem is a 4-page gem from the Annals of Statistics. The two agents are allowed to have different information about the world — that's the whole point, they each have their own private signals. The theorem says that if their posterior probabilities for some event are common knowledge — i.e. each agent knows the other's posterior, knows that the other knows, and so on — then the two posteriors must be equal. So rational people with the same prior can't agree to disagree.
