I'm a grad student in philosophy of religion and two of my advisor's postdocs got into it at a reading group over al-Ghazali's occasionalism, and I kept getting asked to referee it, so i'm pasting the argument here.

One of them said the whole thing is just a re-label: Ghazali denies that fire necessarily produces burning, but he even concedes that in practice the sun's light always follows the sun and fire always burns, so he's just saying "God causes the effect and he happens to do it on a schedule" -- which is the same as the falasifa's "the cause produces the effect," except with God's name stuck on it. The only real difference, the other one said, is that the philosophers think the connection is necessary in the thing itself (a "necessary per se"), while Ghazali thinks it's only necessary by divine custom, and that's the whole game.

The other one pushed back hard and called that a strawman, because Ghazali's move is doing real work: he's making a Humean point centuries before Hume that we never actually observe the necessary connection, only the constant conjunction, so the philosophers have smuggled in an unobserved metaphysical necessity, and once you pull that thread the connection is just regularity plus God's free will, which means miracles aren't violations of anything but just God choosing to act off-schedule.

The first one then conceded the Humean point was fair, but said it actually makes the position worse, not better: if the regularity is just God's habit and God is free, then the laws of nature are contingent in a way that makes induction and science basically impossible to justify, so you've traded a clean error for a live epistemological problem.

The second one said that's a false dilemma -- Ghazali isn't claiming the regularity is a guaranteed law, just a reliable custom we can rely on for all practical purposes (fire burns, water quenches), and that's all science needs, so there's no epistemic catastrophe.

I honestely can't tell who's right about what. Can you go through it move by move and say who's right about which specific point, and what, if anything, both of them are missing?
