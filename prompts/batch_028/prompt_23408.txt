I've recently gotten into philosphy — I retired from physics a few years ago and I've been reading up on perception. I was reading about the arguement from illusion and disjunctivism, and it got me thinking about virtual reality. When you're in a VR headset, you see a tree, but of course there's no real tree, just a screen and some code. The argument from illusion says that since you can have experiences that seem identical to real ones but aren't, all experiences must have a common factor. Disjunctivists deny that — in the good case you're directly aware of the object, in the bad case you're in a different state. So what would G.E. Moore, who wrote that famous paper attacking the argument from illusion, have thought about VR? Would he have said that in VR you're really seeing the virtual tree (so it's a good case), or that VR is just a new bad case the argument from illusion can use? I'd love to hear your take.
