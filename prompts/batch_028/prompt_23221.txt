I am retired from physics and have only recently begun to read philosophy seriously, and I am trying to work out for myself the difference between counterpart theory and the doctrine of transworld identity. Please compare the two, but do not stop at the textbook contrast that one denies and the other affirms that the same individual can be present in more than one possible world. I want the comparison to do real work on three points: first, what each view takes a possible world itself to be, and why that difference matters for the question of identity; second, how each view handles the claim that a particular thing could have failed to exist, which I find much harder to make sense of than the claim that it could have been otherwise; and third, why the counterpart relation, if it is not transitive, is not merely a defective stand-in for identity, but what Lewis actually thinks it accomplishes. I would also be grateful if you could say where the two views genuinely converge, so that I do not overstate the disagreement.
