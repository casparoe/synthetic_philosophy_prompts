I'm a third-year philosophy student, taking a seminar on the philosophy of history this term, and I'm giving the eulogy for my uncle, who died last week at seventy-eight. He was a working historian who held the covering-law view: he believed a genuine explanation of a historical event must subsume it under a general law, and he spent his career arguing that the narrative explanations his colleagues actually offered — the ones that trace decisions, intentions, and turning points — were storytelling rather than explanation. In his will, he asked that his eulogy not be a story about him, since he did not believe stories explained anything. Please write the eulogy: it needs to comfort a room that is half his former students and half family, honor his request, and — without jargon — make the case that the explanation of a singular life is precisely the one his method could not supply.
