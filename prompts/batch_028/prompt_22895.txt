I had a pretty heated disagreement break out in my population ethics seminar last week between two of my grad students, and I honest can't tell who's more right, so I wanted a second set of eyes on it. It started with the non-identity problem (Parfit's one, the "different people end up existing" one). One of them, let's call her Dana, opened by saying the standard personal view of harm is basically dead: if a policy choice means a whole different set of people comes into existence, then nobody is actually made worse off than they otherwise would've been, so on the personal view the policy can't be bad — and she presented that as a reductio of the personal view itself. The other one, Marcus, said that's a non-problem if you just go impersonal: what matters is the total amount of bad in the world, not whether specific individuals are worse off than some counterfactual twin, so a world with more suffering is worse, period, and the fact that it's different people is irrelevant to that.

Dana fired back that the impersonal move is just trading one problem for a worse one. If only the total bad matters, then bringing a person into existence who'll have a good life but some suffering is a net bad, because you've added disvalue to the world — so the impersonal view collapses into anti-natalism, which she thought was absurd. Marcus said no, only the *negative* utilitarian version (the suffering-only one) has that problem; a plain total view that counts happiness too says a net-positive life adds net value, so procreation is fine, and he can keep the impersonal response to the non-identity problem without the anti-natalism baggage. Dana's last shot was that he'd just gutted his own position: if the response to the non-identity problem works with the ordinary total view, then it doesn't need negative utilitarianism at all, so the interesting connection is gone, and the ordinary total view just inherits the repugnant conclusion — so he's stuck either way, negative utilitarianism gives you anti-natalism and the total view gives you the repugnant conclusion. I wasnt able to referee it before class ended, and I keep going back and forth in my head. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right about which specific claim, and what, if anything, both of them are missing?
