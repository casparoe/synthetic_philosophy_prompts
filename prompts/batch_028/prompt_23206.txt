I sit on the grants committee of a small planetary-defense institute, and before Thursday's meeting I need a short written opinion on whether we should fund a proposed ten-year program to catalog and track near-Earth asteroids. The committee's only serious objection is a memo from our risk analyst, who argues that the doomsday argument implies we are almost certainly early in the total number of humans who will ever exist, so the expected value of any long-horizon program aimed at protecting future generations is negligible and the grant should be denied. The memo you draft should recommend a concrete decision to the committee, and it should engage that objection on its own terms: I want you to work through the reference-class problem in enough detail that a panelist who has read the LessWrong threads on anthropic reasoning can't dismiss it, including why the analyst's choice of reference class is not the only legitimate one and what that does to the strength of his conclusion. End with a clear bottom line.
