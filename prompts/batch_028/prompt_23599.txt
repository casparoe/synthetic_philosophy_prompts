I'm covering a dispute between two rival accounts of a set of measurements, and I've hit a wall in deciding how to frame the piece. As far as I can tell, the two accounts agree on every number any existing experiment can produce; the difference between them is that one is a lean, economical restatement of the data, while the other is a more conservative extension of the framework most people already accept. My editor wants me to pick a winner and tell readers which one is "really" right. I suspect the data alone can't settle this, and I'm not comfortable either crowning one side or brushing the whole thing off as a matter of taste. Can you help me think through whether there's a genuine sense in which one account is better even when the evidence ties — and, if so, how I should present that to a general audience without misleading them about what the evidence actually shows?
