I'm a retired evolutionary biologist who has only recently started reading philosophy of biology, and I host a small podcast for working life scientists. This month's guest is a philosopher of biology who defends the "species as individuals" view — the thesis that a species is a single entity, roughly the way an organism is, rather than a class of organisms. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view, with at least one each on how the view bears on the units-of-selection debate (the standard objection being that selection acts on genes and organisms, not on species), what it makes of the Biological Species Concept's interbreeding criterion (still the default for many biologists), and how it handles asexual lineages and the fossil record, where an interbreeding-based account has no grip. For each, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give; I expect her to say the individuals view is a metaphysical claim independent of the taxonomic problem, so press on whether that independence is doing as much work as she thinks.
