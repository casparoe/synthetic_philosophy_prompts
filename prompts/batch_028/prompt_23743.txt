I come from a software engineering background rather than philosophy, so I may be framing this wrong. I've been reading about the evolutionary debunking arguments against moral realism — the kind developed by Sharon Street and others, roughly the idea that if natural selection shaped our moral intuitions for fitness rather than for tracking moral truth, we have reason to doubt them. My question is: doesn't the argument undermine itself? If evolution is not truth-conducive for moral beliefs, then it is also not truth-conducive for the beliefs we use to formulate the debunking argument, including our beliefs about evolution itself. So if we take the debunking argument seriously, we lose the grounds for trusting any of our beliefs, and the argument cannot distinguish moral beliefs from beliefs about evolutionary biology. Isn't that a fatal problem, and doesn't it mean we should just go back to trusting our moral intuitions?
