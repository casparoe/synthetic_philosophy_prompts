A recurring objection to Quine's "gavagai" argument from radical translation is that it only establishes that the linguist cannot determine which of several candidate analyses of the word is correct — an epistemic underdetermination — rather than Quine's stronger claim that there is no fact of the matter about the word's meaning. Please explain the conceptual distinction between indeterminacy of translation and the weaker underdetermination of theory by evidence (the Duhem–Quine thesis), and explain why Quine's argument is intended to establish the former rather than the latter. In particular, address why the indeterminacy is best understood as a global feature of the translation manual as a whole, rather than a local ambiguity about the single word "gavagai," and how this bears on the force of the objection.
