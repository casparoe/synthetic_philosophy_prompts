I'm a third year PhD student in philosophy of religion and I've been stuck for weeks on a debate in our Molinism reading group, so I'd like you to write an essay that actually forces a verdict instead of sitting on the fence. The question: are counterfactuals of creaturely freedom (CCFs) -- "if S were placed in circumstance C, S would freely do A" -- ontologically prior to what the agent does, as the Molinist holds, or grounded in the agent's free choice, as the Ockhamist holds? The Ockhamist's basic line is that the CCFs can't be more basic than the choices, since the choice is what makes the CCF true; the Molinist replies that the CCFs are true independently of God's actualization decision, which is what lets providence coexist with genuine freedom. I find the whole thing genuinely confusing because each side seems to restate the other's premise, and I keep suspecting the disagreement is more verbal than substantive, though I can't say why.

What I want from the essay is a real weighing of two objections -- the grounding objection (no truthmaker for CCFs, so they lack truth-value) and the flicker objection (if the CCFs are settled before creation, the agent can't even flicker into doing otherwise at the moment of decision) -- and then a verdict on which of the two does more damage to Molinism, and whether the Molinist has a live response to the stronger one. Don't just describe the positions; take a side on the priority question and defend it, and if your verdict is that the debate is only partly substantive, say precisely which part is substantive and which is verbal. I'd also appreciate it if you flagged at least one place where the standard textbook presentations of the two views quietly assume what they're supposed to be arguing about, since I'm increasingly convinced the literature talks past itself.
