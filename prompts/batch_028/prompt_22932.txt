I spent most of my professional life studying the robustness and failure of complex systems — how a network holds together under stress, how a small perturbation propagates, and why some structures are fragile precisely because they are elaborate. I have only recently turned to philosophy, and I am now rereading Hannah Arendt's 1971 essay "Lying in Politics," the one she wrote after the Pentagon Papers, and I find her account of political lying more counterintuitive than I expected.

I would like you to compare, as precisely as you can, the two kinds of falsehood she distinguishes: the ordinary political lie, on the one hand, and the systematic lie or propaganda of totalitarianism, on the other. My engineering intuition says that the more elaborate system of falsehoods should be the more fragile one, because it has more moving parts and therefore more places where a single true fact can wedge in and produce a contradiction that tears the whole thing open. But I get the impression Arendt argues the opposite, or something close to it: that a consistent, self-contained alternative reality is in some respects more durable than an isolated lie, precisely because it no longer has to be true and because it gradually dissolves the shared factual world — she calls this process "defactualization" — against which a true fact would normally register as true.

Please make the contrast explicit and mechanistic rather than merely descriptive. What is it about the relationship between a lie and the common world of facts that makes the single lie vulnerable to exposure, and what changes, in the case of the systematic lie, that neutralizes the very self-correcting mechanism by which facts normally correct lies? And then press the consequence: if supplying a true fact is not the natural antidote to propaganda, because propaganda has already dissolved the shared framework that would make the fact register as true, what does Arendt take the actual remedy to be, and does her answer depend on something about the plurality of witnesses and a common world rather than on the bare accumulation of verified facts?
