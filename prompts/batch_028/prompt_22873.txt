As a novelist, I keep returning to Schopenhauer's account of music — his claim that, whereas the other arts are merely copies of the Ideas, music is a direct objectification of the Will itself, which is why it strikes us with such force and can seem to hold the whole range of feeling within it. I am drafting a scene in which a character hears a composition produced entirely by a non-human process: no one composed it, no one performed it, and no one intended it to mean or feel anything at all. I want the character's reaction to turn on something philosophically real rather than a generic "the technology is amazing" beat. Taking Schopenhauer seriously, what would his framework say about such a piece? If music's power comes from its being the Will made audible, does a composition with no human will behind it still qualify, or does it expose a crack in the account? And might Schopenhauer's view, precisely because it does not reduce music to the expression of a composer's feelings, handle this case more gracefully than a standard "music is the expression of human emotion" theory?
