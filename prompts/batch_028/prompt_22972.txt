Two of my grad students got into it over Skolem's paradox during office hours. One, Maya, said the paradox is really devastating: if ZFC has a countable model, then from the outside the model's "reals" are just a countable set, so "uncountable" can't mean what we think it means. She said this shows cardinality is fundamentally model-relative and that Cantor's theorem is really about what's definable inside a model, not about sets in the absolute sense.

The other, Jonas, pushed back. He said the model isn't wrong — the external enumeration of the model's elements isn't itself an element of the model, so the model correctly believes there's no bijection between its ω and its reals. He said the paradox just shows that "there exists a bijection" isn't absolute between models, which is exactly what Löwenheim-Skolem predicts, and that Maya is confusing the meta-theoretic perspective with the object-theoretic one.

Maya replied that if the external perspective is the real one, then the model's reals really are countable and the model is just plain wrong. She said we can't have it both ways — either countability is absolute or it isn't, and if it's relative then the whole edifice of set theory is built on sand.

Jonas said that's a false dichotomy. The model is correct from its own perspective, and that's all that matters for the internal consistency of ZFC. The external perspective is a meta-theoretic tool, not a rival "real" one.

They asked me to referee and I honestly couldnt. Can you go through their exchange and say who's right at each step? I'm especially curious whether Maya's worry about set theory being "built on sand" has any traction, or whether Jonas's deflationary response is the standard and correct one.
