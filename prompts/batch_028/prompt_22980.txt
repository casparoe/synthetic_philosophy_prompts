And the "I get everything I could ever want" point is 100k for me and 60k for her. The set of splits that are actually achievable, given the publisher's constraints, is a smooth curve that starts at (0,60), passes through (50,47), and ends at (100,10), where the first number is my take and the second is hers. Here's the exchange, more or less as I heard it. (1) She said the fair point is the one that maximizes the product of each person's gain over the walk-away point, which she computed to be around (46,55), and that this is the standard answer to this exact problem, so that's that. (2) He said the product rule is arbitrary and that the fair point is instead where each of them gets the same fraction of her maximum possible gain, which he says puts the split at (70,50), and that this is what the literature calls the Kalai-Smorodinsky solution. (3) She said the KS point depends on the ideal point, and the ideal point is by definition unattainable, so it's silly to anchor fairness to a number neither of them can ever get. (4) He said the ideal point is just "the most either of us could possibly hope to get," which is a perfectly concrete thing, and that the product rule is the one doing the hand-waving, since it has no obvious interpretation beyond "maximise this product." I honestly can't tell who's more right. Could you go through their exchange and say who's right at each step -- not just who wins overall?
