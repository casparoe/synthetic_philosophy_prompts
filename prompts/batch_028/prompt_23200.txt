Please argue that the supervaluationist's standard reply to the objection that each precisification of a vague predicate has a sharp boundary, and so supervaluationism is committed to many sharp boundaries — namely, that the precisifications are a mere modeling device and not candidates for the predicate's genuine extension — does not dissolve the objection but only defers it to the level of the set of admissible precisifications, and that the supervaluationist is thereby committed to an epistemicist position at that higher level.
