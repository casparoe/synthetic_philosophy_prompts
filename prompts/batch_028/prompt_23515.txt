Please grade the answer out of 20 points and give me a point-by-point assessment that justifies the score: for each substantive claim in the answer, indicate whether it is correct, incorrect, or imprecise, and explain why.

Exam question:
"In a short essay, explain why Kripke holds that the identity statement 'Water is H2O' is a necessary truth that is nonetheless known only a posteriori. Address the role of rigid designators, and explain why the existence of such a truth does not collapse the a priori/a posteriori distinction."

Student's answer:
"Kripke's argument rests on the claim that natural kind terms like 'water' are rigid designators: they designate the same substance in every possible world in which that substance exists. Because of this, 'Water is H2O' is true in every possible world, which is exactly what makes it a necessary truth.

We still know this only a posteriori, because we learned the chemical composition of water through empirical investigation — chemistry and experiment — rather than by reasoning about the meaning of the words. So the necessity of the statement does not come from the meanings of 'water' and 'H2O'; it comes from facts about the world.

This does not collapse the a priori/a posteriori distinction. The a priori/a posteriori distinction concerns how we come to know a proposition, whereas necessity and contingency concern whether a proposition is true in all possible worlds. These are independent axes, so a proposition can be necessary and a posteriori, just as it can be contingent and a priori. 'Water is H2O' is a necessary a posteriori truth, and it shows that the two distinctions are orthogonal. The statement is a posteriori, finally, because it is synthetic: it is not true by meaning alone, so we need empirical evidence to confirm it."
