I'm working on a piece about where the line between persuasion and manipulation actually is — I've got my eye on stuff like political ads, "dark patterns" in apps, and how much of what we casually call "influence" is just a really good arguement wearing a disguise. The problem is that the stock answer (manipulation = someone tricking you or bypassing your rationality) keeps falling apart for me, because half the time the person is being totally upfront about what they're doing and it still feels manipulative, and the other half you don't even notice you're being swayed but it doesn't feel like manipulation at all.

So I'd love a dialogue between two people who each defend a different account of the line. One thinks the dividing line is transparency: if the target knows, or could know, that someone is trying to influence them, it's legitimate persuasion, and if the influence is hidden from them, it's manipulation. The other thinks transparency is basically beside the point, and the real line is whether the influence runs through the target's own critical reasoning or bypasses and undermines it — on that view something can be completely open and still manipulative, or hidden and still just honest persuasion. Have them actually argue it out rather than just state their positions: I want them to bring up the cases where their two tests give opposite verdicts (a con artist who's upfront about the con, or a really elegant argument you absorb without realizing you're being persuaded) and then try to figure out whether either side can absorb the other's best cases or whether the disagreement is just irreducible. Please keep both of them genuinely sharp — no strawman on either side — and make them respond to each others actual moves, not talk past each other.
