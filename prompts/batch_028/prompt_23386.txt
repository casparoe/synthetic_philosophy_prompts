I am a novelist preparing a collection in which each story dramatizes a narrow philosophical problem rather than a general moral lesson, and I would like you to write a short story related to moral testimony. Set it in a small coastal arbitration office in a fictional city, where a claims adjuster named Mara receives a letter from a retired ethicist who insists that approving a routine insurance payout would make her complicit in a modest but real harm to a third party. Mara cannot independently evaluate the underlying moral principle, because the ethicist's judgment rests on long reflection and a moral framework Mara has not adopted; what she can weigh is the ethicist's sincerity, possible motives, the deadline, and the fact that another expert she consults by phone gives the opposite answer. Please write a story of roughly 1,500 words whose central tension is not whether the payout is good or bad, but whether Mara is justified in acting on the ethicist's moral judgment at all, and whether her decision to defer, resist, or delay shows moral passivity, moral responsibility, or something more ambiguous. Keep the prose restrained and concrete, avoid naming real philosophers or citing theoretical labels, and end with a small action that makes the problem felt rather than resolved.
