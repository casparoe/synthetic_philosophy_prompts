I spent my career as a chemist, so Putnam's Twin Earth thought experiment has a special appeal to me: the clear, drinkable liquid that fills the lakes is H2O on Earth but XYZ on Twin Earth, and the word "water" picks out different substances in the two places. I've been trying to understand the idea that the meaning of a word is not fixed by what is in the speaker's head, and I think I see a difficulty. If the meaning of "water" is not in Oscar's head, then nothing in Oscar—his brain state, his dispositions, his memories—determines what "water" means for him. Yet Twin-Oscar, who has exactly the same brain state, uses "water" to mean something different. It therefore looks as though nothing about Oscar at all fixes the meaning of "water," and the view implies that the meaning of a word is not determined by anything, which is incoherent. I suspect I am misreading the argument. Where does my reasoning go wrong?
