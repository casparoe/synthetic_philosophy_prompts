I'm a PhD student in philosophy, working on the Epicurean argument that death is nothing to us. I keep going in circles on one thought experiment and want to sanity check my reasoning. Let's say Epicurus was alive today and saw the cryonics movement — freezing your body after legal death in the hope that future medicine revives you centuries later. What would he think? My working hypothesis is that he'd be surprisingly sympathetic to it. The frozen years are a time at which you don't exist, so on his view its can't be bad for you, and if revival fails you're just dead, which is also nothing to you. So cryonics looks like a free option: no possible harm, only possible gain. But two things worry me. First, the deprivation objection — that death is bad because it cuts off experiences you would have had — seems to apply to the frozen period, and I'm not sure Epicurus's rejection of deprivation actually blocks it here. Second, on his materialist account where the soul just dissipates at death, is the revived person even the same person? What do you think his position would be, and where does the argument break?
