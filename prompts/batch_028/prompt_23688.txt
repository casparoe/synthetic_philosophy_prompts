I'm the chair of the judging panel for a small literary prize — three of us: a veteran critic, a fellow novelist, and me. We have one finalist this year and the prize either goes to it or goes unawarded. The critic thinks it's the best book of the year and wants to award it full stop; the other novelist thinks it's clever but empty and wants to pass. Both have written long, careful statements, and after two meetings of going over them I cant tell who's misreading the book — they don't so much disagree about what's in it as about what matters in it. I read it too and I lean toward the critic, but nowhere near as hard as either of them. I have to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's vote recommending what we should do. I don't want it to re-argue the literary case, we've definately exhausted that — I want it to grapple with the actual situation: the two most confident voices on the panel are dead even, I'm the weak tie-breaker, and we can let the prize go unawarded. Please draft it with a concrete recommendation (award or pass), the reasoning, and a serious treatment of the strongest point on the other side — I've lost a prize like this before and I don't want the losing side to feel it was a foregone conclusion.
