For my graduate seminar on the axiomatic foundations of cooperative bargaining, please write a dialogue between two equally competent theorists — one a defender of the Nash bargaining solution, the other a defender of the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution — as they work through the following two-person bargaining problem: the disagreement point is (0, 0), and the feasible set is the convex region { (u₁, u₂) : 0 ≤ u₁ ≤ 100, 0 ≤ u₂ ≤ 100, u₂ ≤ 100 − u₁²/100 }. Each theorist should first state the allocation his or her own solution selects for this problem and then defend it by appealing to the axiom that distinguishes the two solutions: independence of irrelevant alternatives for Nash, monotonicity (resource monotonicity) for Kalai–Smorodinsky. The two must genuinely engage with each other's actual arguments rather than simply alternate presentations of their own case — each should press on the other's distinguishing axiom and explain why that axiom, rather than the Pareto, symmetry, or affine-invariance axioms they share, is what drives the other to the wrong answer on this example. Neither side should be a strawman, and each should be able to state the strongest version of the case against the other.
