I'm writing a feature on free will for a science-and-ideas magazine, and I want to close it with a short story that dramatizes a Frankfurt-style case — the classic counterexample to the principle of alternative possibilities, in which a person makes a moral choice and a hidden mechanism would have compelled that very choice had the person been about to choose otherwise, yet the person in fact chooses it on their own. I'd like the story to add the twist that textbook versions usually leave out: the protagonist knows the mechanism exists and knows exactly when it would trigger. Please write a short story of roughly 600 to 900 words in which a person faces a genuine moral decision, is fully aware that a device will force the correct outcome if they are about to pick the wrong one, and yet makes the right choice unaided. Make it genuinely ambiguous whether the protagonist chooses rightly for their own reasons or merely because the device is watching, and leave the reader unsure whether the protagonist is morally responsible without resolving that uncertainty with a tidy philosophical verdict.
