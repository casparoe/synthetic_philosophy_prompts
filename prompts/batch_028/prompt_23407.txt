Student's answer: Bell's theorem shows that quantum mechanics is nonlocal. The key idea is that if you have two entangled particles and you measure them in different directions, the correlations between the outcomes are stronger than what you'd get from any local hidden variable theory. Bell derived an inequality that any local hidden variable theory must satisfy, but quantum mechanics violates it. The theorem relies on three assumptions: locality (what happens here can't depend on what happens over there), realism (particles have definite properties before measurement), and freedom of choice (the measurement settings are chosen freely). The EPR argument showed that QM is incomplete, and Bell's theorem showed that EPR was wrong because any hidden variable theory that completes QM must be nonlocal. This means that nature is fundamentally nonlocal.
