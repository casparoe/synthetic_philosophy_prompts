I'm in a graduate seminar on the naturalization of mental content this term, and I've been working through Millikan's teleosemantics and the objections to it. I keep coming back to the worry that the account is circular in its treatment of misrepresentation, and I've drafted a short essay trying to spell out why. Could you read it and comment? I'm especially unsure about the final paragraph, where I try to argue that Millikan's etiological response doesn't actually break the circle, because I'm not confident that "selection for tracking X" really does presuppose the notion of tracking. I'd like your comments on whether that step is sound, on whether the overall objection is as strong as I think it is, and on whether there's a cleaner or more pressing version of the circularity worry that I should be aiming at instead.

Here is the draft:

On Millikan's teleosemantic account, the content of a mental representation is fixed by its proper function, which is in turn fixed by the evolutionary history of its bearer. A belief that there is a predator, for instance, has that content because the relevant neural state was selected for being caused by predators and for causing flight. On this view a representation is correct when it performs its proper function, that is, when it is caused by the property it represents, and it is a misrepresentation when it occurs in the absence of that property.

A persistent objection is that the account is circular. The proper function of a representation is fixed by its history of correct correlation with the represented property, but the account then defines correctness as the performance of that very proper function. So the notion of correctness is explained in terms of a function that is itself defined by correctness, and if the circularity is vicious the account fails to ground the normative character of mental content, which is precisely what a naturalized theory is supposed to do.

Millikan's standard response is that the circle is broken by the etiological grounding: the function is fixed by an actual historical fact, namely the selection the representation underwent, which is a natural, non-normative process independent of the representation's current correctness. I think, however, that this response does not fully break the circle. The selection story itself must say that the representation was selected for tracking the represented property, and the notion of tracking already presupposes the very correct-correlation relation the account is trying to explain. The circle therefore reappears at the level of the selection narrative, and the etiological grounding does not supply the non-circular anchor the account needs.
