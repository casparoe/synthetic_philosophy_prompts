I'm a novelist and I'm trying to write a scene where a character's dispostion gets finked, and I keep running into this fink/mask distinction in the philosophy literature that I can't quite get a grip on. Here's my understanding: a finked object loses its disposition right when it's about to be tested (like a vase that magically stops being fragile the instant you're about to drop it), and a masked object has the disposition but something's blocking it (like a caged bird that can't fly). But here's the thing — in both cases, when you actually try to test the disposition, it doesn't manifest. So the only difference seems to be timing: the fink loses the disposition just in time, the mask never loses it but blocks it. Isn't a fink just a mask that gets switched off at the last moment? Feels to me like the fink/mask distinction is just about the timing of the intervention, not a real metaphysical difference. Am I missing something, or is this just a terminological artifact that philosophers invented to have more to talk about?
