I've been reading a lot about Nagarjuna's tetralemma lately, mostly through some secondary sources that are pretty dense, and I keep getting stuck on the same question. The tetralemma says that for any proposition P, all four of the following are rejected: P, not-P, both P and not-P, and neither P nor not-P. And then the whole "emptiness of emptiness" thing — that the thesis of emptiness itself is empty — which I get is supposed to prevent us from reifying emptiness into a positive metaphysical claim, but it also seems to make the tetralemma self-undermnining in a way that I can't quite articulate.

I was talking to a friend about this and he keeps insisting that the tetralemma is just a dialectical tool, a way of dissolving conceptual confusions, not a statement about what reality is actually like. But I keep coming back to the thought that if it's just a tool, why does it apply to all four horns and not just the first two? The "both" and "neither" horns seem to be doing real logical work, and I don't see how a purely pragmatic reading can explain that.

Could you write a dialogue between a Madhyamaka proponent who thinks the tetralemma is a genuine statement about the ultimate nature of reality and a proponent who thinks it's purely a dialectical/therapeutic tool? I'd like both to be really competent and to actually engage with each other's strongest points — in particular, I'd like the ontological side to press the "why all four horns?" question and the pragmatic side to press the "emptiness of emptiness" self-undermining problem. And I'd like the dialogue to end without resolving the disagreement, but with both sides understanding the other's position better than they started.
