I am a retired experimental physicist, and only in the past couple of years have I begun to read philosophy seriously, on the urging of a former postdoc who has since become a political theorist. My entry point was, not surprisingly, Pascal: I spent most of my career working with the man who gives his name to a unit of pressure, and I had long assumed he was a scientist who dabbled in theology on the side. Reading the Provincial Letters has thoroughly changed that impression, and I find myself puzzled by a part of the book I did not expect to find there, namely his discussion of the relationship between justice and force, which he treats as one of the great sources of disorder in human affairs. The more I think about it, the more I am reminded of Thrasymachus in the first book of the Republic, who insists that the laws of every city are made by the ruling group to serve its own interest, and that justice, as people speak of it, is simply the advantage of whoever holds power. At first glance the two sound almost identical, and I confess I am not sure how to tell them apart.

Could you compare these two positions for me, with particular care about the difference between what each author is actually claiming and what role the claim plays in the larger argument of the work? I would be grateful if you could address three points. First, are Pascal and Thrasymachus making the same empirical claim about how power and law actually relate in the world, or is one of them making something closer to a diagnostic or normative claim about a confusion that people fall into? Second, do they draw the same conclusion about what we ought to do in the face of the gap between justice and force, or do their prescriptions diverge, and if so, in what direction? And third, how does the function of the passage differ, given that Thrasymachus is setting up a problem for Socrates to answer in a dialogue, whereas Pascal is writing a sustained polemic in defense of a particular religious view? I suspect a reader can be tempted to collapse the two even though the projects are very different, and I would like to understand exactly where the resemblance breaks down. Please write this as a clear prose comparison that a careful but non-specialist reader can follow, and do not assume I have read either work in full.
