I'm giving a three-minute toast at a send-off dinner for my friend Nora, who is due with her first child next week. I'm a software engineer, I make most of my decisions by estimating expected outcomes, and I have no formal philosophy background — but one of the guests, a philosophy PhD student, has been explaining to me the problem of transformative experience, and I want the toast to take it seriously rather than joke it off. The core idea I want handled: the decision to become a parent is epistemically transformative, in the sense that the evidence needed to evaluate it only becomes available after the fact, and to a person who is in some sense not the person who made the decision. I want the speech to say something honest about that — that the woman in the room tonight is about to become someone none of us can fully predict — without being morbid, without turning into a lecture, and without making a single promise about how it will feel. The audience is about thirty friends and family, none of them philosophers except the PhD student, who will notice any reference to the actual literature. Please write the toast.
