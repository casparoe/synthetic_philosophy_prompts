I cover science and ideas for a magazine. At a decision-science symposium I was reporting on, I overheard two researchers get into a real argument after a panel on the Ellsberg paradox, and I wasn't technical enough to call it. The first, a probabilist, argued that the Ellsberg experiments don't show people are irrational, only that people care about whether they know the odds, so the right model is to treat an ambiguous belief as a whole set of possible probability distributions rather than a single number and to choose the option with the best worst-case expected value across that set, which is just expected utility generalized to genuine ignorance. The second, a decision theorist, replied that this generalization is incoherent: maximizing the worst case makes you refuse a bet that pays off under every distribution in your set, so you would walk away from a free lunch, and the rule is dynamically inconsistent, because if you plan to apply it at every decision point, the action your plan prescribes in advance is precisely the one you will reject once you actually reach that node, so it fails the same coherence tests we demand of ordinary expected utility. The probabilist conceded the rule is conservative but said that this conservatism is exactly the ambiguity aversion the experiments reveal, and that the free-lunch objection only bites if the set is unreasonably wide, a problem you solve by tightening the set as you gather evidence. The decision theorist then claimed that this is the tell: if you keep tightening the set until the worst case stops being absurd, you have collapsed it to a single probability and are just doing standard Bayesian expected utility in disguise, so the maximin rule never actually does any work, but if you keep the set genuinely wide, the free-lunch and inconsistency problems are live, which means the rule is either vacuous or incoherent. Please referee this. Go through the exchange and tell me, for each of the four claims, whether it is right, wrong, or only partly right, and say what both of them are missing. I don't just want to know who wins overall; I want to know who is right about which specific point.
