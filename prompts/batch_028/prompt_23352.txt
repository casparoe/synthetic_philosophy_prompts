Could you write me an essay on whether the Löbian obstacle is a genuine constraint on self-trusting agents or mostly an artifact of demanding proof-theoretic self-trust? I want it to actually grapple with the technical core rather than hand-wave. The setup is that Löb's theorem says if a system L proves "if L proves φ, then φ" it already proves φ, so a consistent L can't prove its own soundness schema, and that's exactly why the tiling agents paper treats self-trust as a real problem for self-modifying agents. On one side is the view that it's real — you cant prove your own soundness, period. On the other is the argument that the obstacle dissolves if the agent's belief in its own soundness is a metalogical belief sitting at the interface between syntax and semantics, not an axiom in the object logic — and adding it as an axiom is precisely what makes the expanded system inconsistent, so keeping it outside L is not a cheat. Then there's the probabilistic / logical-induction line, which claims the theorem just doesn't bite in a probabilistic regime. Weigh all three, then reach an actual verdict and defend it; dont leave it as "both sides have a point." A nuanced verdict is fine (e.g. real in the proof-theoretic sense but not for the agent designs we actually care about) as long as the conclusion is earned, not just asserted.
