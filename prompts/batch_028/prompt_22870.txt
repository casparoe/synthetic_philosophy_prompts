I'm at a dinner party with a friend who is a statistician, and he has proposed a bet that I can't decide whether to take. The rules: we flip a fair coin. If it lands heads, I then flip a second coin repeatedly until it comes up heads, and I pay him $2^{k-1}, where k is the number of flips it took. If the first coin lands tails, the same procedure is run, but he pays me $2^{k-1} instead. He insists the bet is perfectly fair, since neither of us has any advantage, and my intuition agrees with him. But when I try to compute the expected value, both my expected gain and my expected loss come out to infinity, and I can't make sense of that. Should I take the bet, and is there a principled way to decide, or is this just a case where the math breaks down?
