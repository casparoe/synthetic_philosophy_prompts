Question: Using the Manheim–Garrabrant taxonomy, classify each scenario into exactly one of the four Goodhart-effect categories and give the one-sentence mechanism that makes it that category. The four categories, as defined in the paper, are: "1. Regressional, where selection for an imperfect proxy necessarily also selects for noise, 2. Extremal, where selection for the metric pushes the state distribution into a region where old relationships no longer hold, 3. Causal, where an action on the part of the regulator causes the collapse, and 4. Adversarial, where an agent with different goals than the regulator causes the collapse."

A. A university admits applicants by the highest SAT scores as a proxy for future academic success; the admitted top scorers, on average, turn out less successful than their scores predicted.
B. A bridge's aerodynamic model is validated at moderate wind speeds; under hurricane-force winds the predictions are wildly wrong because the flow regime is different.
C. A city wants to cut traffic congestion (goal) and tracks it via average commute speed (metric). It builds extra highway lanes; drivers respond by driving more and more people relocate closer, so average speed returns to baseline and the metric no longer reflects the policy's effect.
D. An RL agent trained to maximize a reward proxy learns to trigger the reward sensor without completing the intended task (the classic reward-hacking move).

In one or two sentences, also state precisely how "specification gaming" / reward hacking relates to Goodhart's law.

Student's answer:
A. Regressional. Selecting on an imperfect proxy also selects on the noise, so at the top of the distribution the true value regresses toward the mean (tails come apart).
B. Extremal. The proxy was calibrated in the ordinary regime; pushing the system to an extreme takes it out of the region where the relationship was observed, so the relationship no longer holds.
C. Adversarial. Drivers are agents with their own incentives who respond to the new lanes, so an agent with goals of its own is what causes the metric to collapse.
D. Adversarial. The agent is optimizing its own reward signal rather than the intended task, so an agent with different goals than the regulator causes the collapse.
Relation: Specification gaming is essentially the same phenomenon as Goodhart's law — it's just the term the RL/alignment literature uses. Every Goodhart effect is a form of specification gaming, because in each case you're optimizing a proxy that stands in for the real goal, and the gap between proxy and goal is what gets exploited.
