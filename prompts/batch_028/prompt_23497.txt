Okay so I'm a novelist and I'm working on a novella about a woman who spent her whole life building a perfect archive of her own life — every conversation transcribed, every meal logged, every small death (a pet, a friendship, a version of herself) recorded and filed. And then she dies, and the story is told from the perspective of her archivist, a man who has to decide what to do with the archive.

I want a short story (around 1,500 words) that works with this setup but is really about Zhuangzi's view of death. Not the one I know where he wants heaven and earth as his coffin and the sun and moon as his jade discs — I want the other one, where his wife dies and his friend Huizi finds him drumming on a basin and singing, and Zhuangzi explains that her life was a long chain of transformations, from no life to formless change to qi to form to life, and now death, like the four seasons just turning over. The thing that's bugging me as a writer is whether that view is actually a comfort or just a way of shutting people out, and I want the story to sit in that tension without resolving it into either "Eastern philosophy is soothing" or "he was a cold man."

So: the archivist is sorting through the archive after her death and keeps finding that she had been documenting her own decline for years, calmly, like someone tracking the weather. I want him to hav to make a decision about the archive (destroy it, publish it, keep it, etc.) and for that decision to be the story's real subject. Keep it grounded and specific, minumal dialogue, and don't make the dead woman a saint or a villain.
