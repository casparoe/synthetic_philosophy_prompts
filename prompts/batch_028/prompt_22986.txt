I've been working through the causal exclusion argument for a paper I'm drafting for a graduate seminar in philosophy of mind, and I keep arriving at a conclusion my advisor keeps telling me is a mistake, without being able to pin down exactly where I've gone wrong. As I understand the argument, it runs roughly like this: the physical domain is causally closed, so that every physical event already has a sufficient cause entirely in physical terms; my mental states — my belief that it's going to rain, the ache in my shoulder — are either identical to some underlying physical states or they are something genuinely over and above them; and if they are over and above, then because the physical cause is already sufficient, the mental state is causally redundant, or "excluded," from doing any real work. Once I accept that much, I get to what I take to be the punchline: if the argument succeeds, then my conscious experiences cause literally nothing. My decision to reach for the umbrella doesn't move my arm; the electrochemical cascade does, and the decision is just a passenger along for the ride. And if my experiences cause nothing, I've been arguing, they can't be real in any robust sense — they're epiphenomena, a kind of useless byproduct the universe is hauling around. So my working thesis has been that the exclusion argument is, in effect, a reductio of consciousness itself: it shows that if there is a genuine hard problem of consciousness, the only way out is to deny that consciousness is a real, causally active feature of the world. My advisor insists I've mislocated the argument's target, but I can't see what she means.

Could you help me find the flaw? In particular, does the exclusion argument really establish that conscious states are causally impotent, or is it aimed at something more specific? And even granting the argument, what's the difference between a mental state being excluded from causation and its being unreal — is that a distinction worth drawing, or does causal impotence collapse into nonexistence?
