For my philosophy of religion seminar we've been working through Ayer's argument against immortality in his essay 'Why I Am Not a Christian,' and I'm trying to figure out what he would make of modern survival technologies. As I read it, his objection is aimed specifically at the traditional disembodied soul: a soul stripped of any body and of any particular spatio-temporal location, he says, cannot be identified as *me*, and genuine survival would require the continuation of my stream of consciousness rather than the bare persistence of some soul-substance. The trouble is that cryonics and whole-brain emulation look engineered to do exactly what his argument demanded — preserve the psychological continuity (and, in cryonics' case, the actual body). So does this neutralize his objection, or does he have a further move — e.g., a verificationist point that we cannot confirm the resuscitated or emulated person really is me, or a point that, given his bundle theory, there is no fact of the matter about which continuation is the 'real' me? Please work through how his *specific* argument (rather than verificationism in general) bears on (a) cryonics and (b) a successful whole-brain emulation, and tell me where you think he is most vulnerable.
