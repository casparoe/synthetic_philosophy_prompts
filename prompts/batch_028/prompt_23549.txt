I chair my university's facilities safety review panel, a three-member body whose governing standard is that an immediate closure is required whenever a building's installation both violates current code and presents an imminent hazard, and we must vote separately on each of those two findings and on the closure decision itself. In our current case, outdated electrical work in the main physics lab, our three members split as follows: one finds a violation but no imminent hazard and opposes closure; one finds no violation (the work is grandfathered) but an imminent hazard and favors a remediation deadline rather than closure; and one finds both and favors immediate closure. The result is that the majority finds a violation, the majority finds an imminent hazard, yet the majority rejects closure — the discursive dilemma, in miniature. Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting: it should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame, say which aggregation rule — premise-based or conclusion-based — the panel should apply in cases like this and why, and engage honestly with the strongest consideration on the losing side.
