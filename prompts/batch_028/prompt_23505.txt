Okay so I was re-reading an old thread on a rationalist forum about precommitment and Ulysses contracts, and it made me start chewin' on this for a while. I'd love a clear explanation, because I keep goin' in circles. The setup: hyperbolic discounting is usually presented in the behavioral-econ literature as a *preference* — a time-inconsistent utility function where the effective discount rate falls as the delay gets shorter. But akrasia, the way it's usually discussed in the philosophy of action, is a *failure of will* — a gap between what you judge to be best and what you actually do. So I keep wondering whether these are even the same phenomenon, or whether the literature is just smuggling one concept into the other.

Here's the thing that makes it worse for me: if I model myself as a series of short-lived selves, each of which rationally prefers its own immediate reward, then there's no 'failure' at all — each self is just doing what's rational for it. Akrasia only shows up if I insist on the 'one self' framing, where the diachronic self is a single agent with a single utility function. So it feels like the whole concept of 'weakness of will' is an artifact of how you slice the self. And then there's the 'sophisticated vs naive' agent distinction I've seen in the behavioral lit — a sophisticated agent anticipates its future self-control problems, a naive one doesn't — and I think that's relevant here, but I'm not sure how it bears on the one-self-vs-many-selves question.

So my actual question: is hyperbolic discounting really a theory of *preferences*, or is it really a theory of *choice* / self-control? And does that distinction actually matter — i.e., does it dissolve the concept of akrasia entirely, or does akrasia survive as a real (if framing-dependent) phenomenon? I'd also love to know how commitment devices (Ulysses contracts, precommitment, etc.) fare under each framing, because if the committing self and the wavering self are 'different agents,' it's not obvious a commitment is binding at all. Can you walk me through this in a way that either resolves the tension or at least makes clear where the real disagreement is?
