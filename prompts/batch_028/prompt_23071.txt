I serve on the board of a residential cooperative of about 200 units. Two members have proposed an amendment to our bylaws under which only owners who pass a written financial literacy test — covering general material like how to read a balance sheet and what an interest rate is — would be eligible to vote on major capital decisions, such as whether to refinance the building's mortgage at the current rate or to pay a premium for a longer-lasting roof. The proponents point out that the last two close votes were decided by a handful of members who had not read the budget packet, and that the building has paid the price. The opponents say that a test written and administered by the board would simply codify whose opinion the board is willing to count, and that the vote is part of what makes an owner an owner rather than a tenant. Please draft a short written opinion for circulation before the vote scheduled for six weeks from now: it should recommend a concrete course of action — adopt the amendment, reject it, or adopt a modified version — with reasons that take seriously the strongest argument on the losing side.
