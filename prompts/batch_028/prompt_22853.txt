I'm a novelist and I'm plotting a novel about a village thats offered a deal: a company will build a dam that makes everyone richer on average, but there's a real chance the valley floods and the farmer at the bottom ends up ruined, and the farmer refuses to sign. The plot needs to turn on the difference between a deal that's a Pareto improvement in expectation — everyone expects to gain — and what I think is called a safe Pareto improvement, where noone can end up worse off no matter what actually happens. Here's the twist I'm after: the only way to make the deal safe is to fully insure the farmer against the flood, and that insurance is so expensive in expectation that the insured version is no longer a Pareto improvement at all — the company ends up worse off. The fix that makes the deal acceptable to the farmer is exactly what makes it unacceptable to the company. Could you write me a short story that dramatizes that? I want the reader to feel why it's strange that a change which makes everyone richer on average can't actually be agreed to, and I want the farmer to be a real person, not a symbol.
