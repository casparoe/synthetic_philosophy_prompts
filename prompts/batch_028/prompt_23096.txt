I am a retired physicist who has only recently begun to read philosophy, and I find myself stuck on a point where the history of thermodynamics meets the modern cosmological arrow. Boltzmann held that the low-entropy state we observe is a rare fluctuation in a universe that is otherwise in near-equilibrium. The modern "Past Hypothesis" instead treats the low-entropy state as an initial condition of the universe, and explains the arrow of time from that initial condition rather than from a fluctuation. If Boltzmann were alive today and aware of the Past Hypothesis and of the cosmological evidence that the early universe was in an extremely low-entropy state, how would he assess his own fluctuation hypothesis? Would he revise his view, or defend it on independent grounds? Please explain your reasoning, and flag any points where a reconstruction of his view is genuinely uncertain.
