"Distinguish testimonial injustice from hermeneutical injustice in Fricker's framework. For each, state the mechanism of the wrong and give a brief example. (10 points)"

Here's one student's answer:

"Testimonial injustice is a wrong that happens when a speaker doesn't get the credibility they deserve, and the reason is a prejudice about their social identity. For example, a Black patient in a hospital whose reports of pain are dismissed by a white doctor who just doesn't take them seriously. The wrong is done to the person in their capacity as a knower, and it only happens when the hearer is consciously trying to be unfair to them.

Hermeneutical injustice is different. It happens when there is a gap in the shared vocabulary or interpretive resources of a society, so that someone can't make sense of or articulate an important part of their experience. For example, before the phrase 'sexual harassment' was widely used, women who were harassed at work didn't have a good way to describe what was happening to them. In this case, the hearer wrongs the speaker, because without the right concepts the speaker can't be properly understood."
