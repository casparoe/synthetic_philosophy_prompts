I have been trying to make sense of the difficulty people talk about in distinguishing between preferences and biases in moral judgment, and I confess I do not quite see what the difficulty is supposed to be. As far as I can see, a bias is simply a preference that fails to meet a standard we have independently agreed on—say, a standard of impartiality, or of being responsive to the relevant reasons—whereas a legitimate preference is one that passes that standard. The only hard part, then, is practical: figuring out, in a given case, whether a particular leaning of mine passes the test. So my question is really a practical one: what is the most reliable way to check, case by case, whether a given preference of mine is secretly a bias? I ask because I am about to make a decision in which I want to be sure I am not merely rationalizing a bias as a preference.
