I'm a third-year PhD student in epistemology, and I've been trying to get a firm handle on Fitch's paradox of knowability for a paper I'm writing on anti-realist deflationism about truth. I've been working through the standard reconstruction of the argument, and I keep getting stuck on what I take to be the crux of the Fitch move: I read the key step as requiring that if an agent knows the conjunction (p ∧ ¬Kp), then the agent also knows that she knows it — in other words, an application of the KK principle, or positive introspection. I'm puzzled by this, because if the paradox really depends on KK, then it seems the whole threat to anti-realism is just a byproduct of accepting the 4 axiom in an epistemic logic, and since KK is widely doubted (there's a substantial literature on whether knowledge is self-introspective, and recent work on the limits of self-knowledge suggests it is not), anti-realists should simply reject 4 and the paradox goes away. I was discussing this with my advisor, and she suggested that I might be misidentifying which principle is actually doing the work in the Fitch move, but I haven't been able to see what she means. Could you help me work out whether the standard Fitch argument genuinely requires KK, or whether it goes through on more minimal assumptions (factivity and closure under known consequence, say), and if the latter, what the implications are for the anti-realist's options for responding to the paradox?
