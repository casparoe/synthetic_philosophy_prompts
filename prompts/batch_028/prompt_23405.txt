I'm working on interpretability for a small transformer, and I keep going back and forth on whether it is even correct to say that a particular neuron "represents" something. The standard test in the literature is causal: intervene on the unit (zero it out, patch it in from a different input, or add a constant offset) and check whether the output changes in the way you would expect if that unit were encoding that property. For example, a putative "cat detector" should, when ablated, make the model less likely to classify a cat image as a cat. Argue that passing this causal-intervention test is not sufficient to establish that the unit has a representation of that property, and explain precisely what the test fails to rule out.
