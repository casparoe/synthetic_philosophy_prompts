I'm giving the toast at the retirement dinner of a political economist who spent three decades trying to reconcile Carnap's philosophy of science with democratic economic planning. Half the room are philosophers of science who will catch every reference; the other half are her family, who mostly want to hear about the time she fell asleep during a keynote.

What I need the toast to do is honor her actual position, which is more specific and less comfortable than "she was a socialist": she was a social democrat in the old Vienna-Circle sense, and she held, on Carnap's model, that the choice between planning and markets is an empirical and institutional question, not a metaphysical one about human nature, and not something that follows from any law of history — a position that puts her on a particular side of the famous Popper–Carnap dispute over historicism and prediction, one she defended for years and then, in her last decade, quietly abandoned. Please write a five-minute toast that gets this right, uses one accessible metaphor both halves of the room can follow, and lands as affection rather than a lecture. She is alive, in the room, and has a very dry sense of humor, so no eulogy.
