I've read the standard resolution of the two-envelope paradox, on which the expected-value argument for switching collapses once you note that it silently assumes an improper prior over the amounts, and I'm not satisfied that it identifies the real error. Please argue that the deeper mistake is a misapplication of the principle of indifference: the player treats the hypotheses "I hold the smaller envelope" and "I hold the larger envelope" as a priori equiprobable conditional on the amount I have actually observed, when that conditional probability is really fixed by the prior over the total amount and is generally not one-half. Then work through a concrete proper prior to show that the rational answer to "should I switch after seeing X?" is prior-dependent, and explain why the ex ante symmetry observation — that, before any amount is observed, unconditionally switching confers no advantage — is compatible with, rather than in tension with, a prior-dependent ex post switching rule.
