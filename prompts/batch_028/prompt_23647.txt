The premise: two decision-makers who can't possibly affect each other's world. One is a human operator running a helium-3 extraction rig on a moon; the other is an autonomous AI managing a mining claim in a different star system, light-years away, no FTL in the story's physics. They both face the same structural choice, wich is basically a prisoner's dilemma where defecting wins unless you're confident the other side will cooperate — and crucially, each can model the other's decision procedure with high confidence, and each knows thier counterpart is doing the same to them. Nothing either one does causally changes the other's payoff; no messages, no shared environment, nothing. I want the story to be about the moment each of them 'settles' on cooperating anyway, purely through reasoning about the other's reasoning.

Can you write that short story for me, roughly 800-1200 words? I want the acausal-trade mechanic to be the actual plot engine, and I want the gap between what they'd get by defecting and what they get by cooperating to be something the characters feel, not something a narrator explains in a footnote. I'd also really like the story to keep the uncomfortable question live to the end: is this really cooperation with another agent, or is each one just predicting a copy of itself and dressing it up as a deal? No exposition dumps — if you need to establish the setup, do it through the characters' own thinking — and please end on something ambiguous rather than resolved.
