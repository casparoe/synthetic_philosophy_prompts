I'm a retired theoretical physicist, and in my last few years I've taken up philosophy in a way that would have seemed odd to me a decade ago. I've been reading about dialetheism and paraconsistent logic, and I confess the boundary between the two is still fuzzy to me, even after working through a few of the standard paradoxes on my own. I'd like you to write a dialogue between a dialetheist and a classical logician, in which they try to settle a specific point of disagreement: whether the Liar sentence — the sentence that asserts of itself that it is false — shows that some contradiction is genuinely true (a dialetheia), or whether it shows only that our classical assumptions about truth and bivalence break down, so that the sentence simply lacks a determinate truth value.

I want the classical logician to press the objection that paraconsistent logic, which blocks the explosion principle so that a true contradiction does not entail everything, is merely a technical device for tolerating inconsistency and does not by itself establish that any contradiction is true; and I want the dialetheist to respond that the Liar and its kin are best understood as revealing genuine true contradictions, and that paraconsistent logic is the right framework for reasoning about them without trivializing. Both speakers should be equally competent and should respond to each other's actual points rather than talk past each other, and I'd like the dialogue to end without a clean resolution, since I suspect the disagreement is deeper than either of them can settle in a single exchange.
