I'm taking an upper-level epistemology seminar this term, and two of my classmates got into a debate during our reading on self-knowledge that I honestly couldn't referee, so I'm hoping an outside perspective might help. To set the stage: we had been working with two principles. The first is a principle sometimes called "luminosity," which I took to say roughly this: if it seems to you that p, then you know that it seems to you that p — the idea that conscious experience comes with a special, reliable form of self-knowledge. The second is the KK principle: if you know that p, then you know that you know that p. The question that split the room was whether luminosity supports, or even entails, KK.

Priya said it doesn't. Her argument was that luminosity is a principle about phenomenal self-knowledge — it tells you what it is like to you — and it has no application to knowledge as such. Much of what we know, she pointed out, is non-phenomenal: I can know a mathematical result or a historical fact without there being anything distinctive about my experience, so luminosity simply has no purchase there, and it can't be used to establish KK, which is a claim about all knowledge. Marcus replied that every case of knowing involves your being in some epistemic state — possessing some evidence or justification — and that by luminosity you know that you are in that state, since you know what it is like to have that evidence. On that basis he thought luminosity does support KK after all. Priya pressed back that possessing evidence is not the same as there being something it is like; one can have justification without any distinctive experience, so luminosity still doesn't reach it. Marcus answered that if you genuinely possess the justification, you are in a position to tell that you possess it, and that this is precisely the meta-knowledge that KK demands.

I couldn't work out which of them had the stronger case, and I wasn't even sure I had the real point of contention straight. Could you go through their exchange move by move and say who is right at each step — not just who comes out ahead overall? I would also be grateful if you could say what, if anything, both of them are missing.
