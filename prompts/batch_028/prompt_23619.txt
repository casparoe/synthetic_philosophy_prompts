I'm a PhD student in Chinese philosophy working on the *Zhuangzi*'s relevance to contemporary philosophy of mind. Suppose Zhuangzi were alive today and encountered cryonics and whole-brain emulation—the so-called "digital afterlife." Given his treatment of death as a transformation (*hua*) of *qi*, his dream argument, and his insistence that we not cling to a fixed individual self, how would he likely regard these technologies? I'm especially interested in the tension I see: emulation seems to preserve (or copy) an individual mind-pattern, which looks like precisely the clinging to the self the text is meant to dissolve, yet it might also be read as just another instance of the transformations he describes. Please give a reasoned projection grounded in specific themes in the text, not a generic "he would be at peace with it" answer.
