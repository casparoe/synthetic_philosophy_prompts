I'm a science journalist who sits on the scientific advisory committee of a natural history museum, and we are reviewing the draft text for a new exhibit on the human body. One panel asserts that the shape of the human nose — narrower in cold, dry climates, broader in warm, humid ones — is a direct product of natural selection, evolved to condition the air we breathe, and that this is why nose shape tracks climate across populations. Half the committee is comfortable with the wording; the other half thinks it is precisely the kind of unfalsifiable, pan-adaptationist claim that Gould and Lewontin's spandrels critique targets, since the same nose shape could just as well be a byproduct of other changes in the face or a target of sexual selection rather than of selection for air-conditioning. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting: it should recommend a concrete course of action (publish the panel as written, publish it with a stated caveat, or strike it), and it should give the committee a standard for when an adaptationist claim of this sort is strong enough to put in front of the public, while fairly engaging the strongest reason to simply publish the adaptation story as it stands.
