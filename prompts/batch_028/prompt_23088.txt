Moore's paradox is usually stated with the pair "It is raining, but I don't believe it is," yet the sentence "It is raining, but it isn't the case that I believe it is" seems perfectly unobjectionable, and it is hard to see what a difference of mere logical form could make. I'd like an explanation of the puzzle that takes this contrast as its starting point rather than restating the paradox: how do the main accounts — the practical-entailment account, the self-refuting-assertion account, and the view that the first-person present-tense self-ascription is defective because there is no fact of the matter about whether it is true — each handle the non-paradoxical variant, and can any of them explain why the variant is not paradoxical in the first place?
