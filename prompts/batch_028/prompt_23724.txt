I'm in a philosophy of language seminar this term and we've been working through Russell's theory of descriptions (On Denoting), and I'm trying to write a short essay on two puzzles the theory seems to raise, so I'd really appreciate a second pair of eyes. Below are my draft notes. I've tried to state the puzzles in my own words, but I'm worried I may be misstating one of them.

First, negative singular sentences. Russell's move is that a sentence like "The present King of France is not bald" is not a false singular proposition about some object (there is no such object), but a true general proposition — roughly, that there is no object that is the present King of France and is bald. This gets the right truth value, since we do treat the sentence as true. But I'm not convinced the reason it's true is right. The sentence seems to presuppose that there is a present King of France and then deny that he is bald, so it looks like it should be neither true nor false (or false) rather than true. I suspect Russell is fixing the scope of the negation in a way that lets him get the right answer for the wrong reason, and I'm not sure whether a charitable reading can save him here.

Second, misdescription in opaque contexts. When I attribute a belief using a description, as in "I believe that the person who wrote Waverley was tall," the description seems to do double duty: it picks out an individual, but it also carries the information by which I know that individual. If the description is false of the individual it picks out (say, the author of Waverley was in fact short), then Russell's quantifier-like treatment of descriptions seems to make the whole belief attribution come out false, even though I genuinely have the belief. I'm not sure Russell's account has the resources to separate the descriptive content from the reference, and I'm unsure whether this is a genuine gap or whether I'm misreading how the theory handles opaque contexts.

Could you comment on (a) whether my reading of the negative singular sentence case is fair to Russell, (b) whether the misdescription problem is actually a problem for the theory or whether I've misstated it, and (c) more generally whether these two puzzles are the right ones to focus on for a paper at this level? I'm least confident about (b).
