I am a philosophy PhD student working on the foundations of AI alignment, and I am writing a section of my dissertation on coherent extrapolated volition (CEV) as a proposal for specifying the goals of advanced AI. The objection I want to press is that CEV cannot be a legitimate source of value: the idealized subject it invokes—the person we would become if we thought more, knew more, and had grown into the people we wished to be—is a counterfactual being, and it is not obvious that the preferences of a hypothetical, transformed version of an agent can ground what the actual agent ought to do. Please work through the strongest version of this objection and the best CEV-side reply, and then explain what the outcome of that debate would imply for the project of specifying AI goals in practice: in particular, whether CEV is best understood as a decision-procedure for humans, as a definition of a genuine moral property, or as something else entirely.
