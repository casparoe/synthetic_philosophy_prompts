I am a professor of philosophy of language and sit on the curatorial advisory board of the university's anthropology museum. We are mounting an exhibit from a language with no prior written record, and two of our field linguists have produced competing analyses of a key term: one reads it as picking out a persistent whole object, the other as a transient part or stage of one, and both fit the available evidence equally well. The exhibit label must carry a single English gloss, and the board is divided between choosing the gloss that seems more natural, printing both, and printing the untranslated term alone. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before next week's vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame, take the indeterminacy of translation seriously rather than paper over it, and grapple honestly with the strongest argument that one gloss is in fact correct even if we cannot tell which.
