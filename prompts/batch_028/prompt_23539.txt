I just spent an hour and a half arguing with my friend over dinner about Molinism — specifically about whether middle knowledge, the idea (going back to Molina) that God knows what free creatures would do in circumstances they were never actully placed in, quietly makes God's decree conditional on these brute counterfactual facts, and whether that's even compatible with the doctrine that God is the sole sovereign cause of everything that happens. He's a Molinist and I wasn't buying the standard reply, and then I remembered the commitee I sit on actually has to rule on something very close to this next week. We're the doctrinal review committee of our denomination, and a pastor was reported for teaching in a sermon series that "God's decree is conditioned on middle knowledge — God saw what free people would do and then arranged the circumstances." The complainant says this denies the sovereignty of the decree; the pastor says he's just presenting a well-known model of providence in the literature. Can you draft the committee's written opinion to circulate before Thursday's meeting? It should recommend a concrete ruling (dismiss, sustain, or sustain with a required corrective statement) with a time frame for any follow-up, and it should engage the Molinist position on its own terms — in particular, whether the counterfactuals of freedom are coherent given that antecedent facts don't settle what a free agent would do, whether the standard reply (that God's decree still freely includes both the choice of circumstances and the choice of world) succeeds, and what the strongest case is for whichever side we lose. We don't need to resolve the whole debate, but the committee has to say something defensible about whether this particular teaching violates the standard.
