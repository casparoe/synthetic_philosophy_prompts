I work in software, and a lot of what my team does — ranking, feed algorithms, A/B testing — is really just the problem of aggregating a bunch of weak, noisy signals into a single decision about what's good. A while back I came across the idea of an information cascade (sometimes called rational herding), and it's been sitting in the back of my head, partly because it keeps coming up when I try to make sense of things like why the restaurant with the longest line is the one everyone queues for, even when most of the people in line have no real idea why they're there. The basic setup, as I understand it, is that people make a choice one after another; each person gets some private, imperfect signal about which option is actually better, but they can only observe the choices the people before them made, not the signals those people saw. Somewhere down the line it becomes optimal for a new person to just set their own signal aside and copy the crowd, and from then on everyone does the same, so the group can lock onto a wrong answer even though every single agent is doing the rational thing.

What I can't get a clean mental model of is the mechanics of that, and in particular the tension in it. Can you walk me through, at the level of the actual decision-theoretic reasoning each agent is doing, how the cascade kicks in — i.e., at what point the accumulated public record starts to outweigh a fresh private signal, and why that threshold gets crossed so early? I'm also confused about the fragility of the result: I've heard that a cascade can be established on the basis of just a couple of early decisions, so that the consensus is fragile in a way a genuinely well-supported one isn't, and that relatively little new information can overturn it. Why is there that difference? And finally, this looks to me like a clean counterexample to the wisdom-of-crowds intuition — the Condorcet jury theorem, where independent signals average out and the majority is usually right — so I'd like you to pinpoint exactly which assumption (I'm guessing independence) the cascade violates, and what that means for the gap between every individual being rational and the group as a whole being rational.
