I run a small philosophy podcast with two other students at my school, and next week's guest is a visiting philosopher who has written extensively in favor of the repugnant conclusion, the claim that, for some very large population, a world containing only people whose lives are only marginally worth living can be better than a smaller world of people whose lives are very good. Our listeners have mostly encountered the argument in popularized form, so I would like to prepare nine to eleven interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view and its motivations. In particular, I want at least one question each on the following, with a follow-up for each keyed to the answer she is most likely to give: first, the mere addition principle, and whether she regards the intuition that adding people with positive lives cannot make things worse as the genuine engine of the argument or merely as a rhetorical device; second, the sadistic conclusion that averagism appears to entail, and whether she thinks that cost is disqualifying for rival views or something her own view can absorb; third, the charge that the argument silently flattens a morally important qualitative difference between a life that is barely worth living and one that is genuinely good, and how she responds to the suspicion that the whole force of the argument rests on that flattening; fourth, what her view implies for the practical questions our students actually care about, such as whether we ought to bring as many future people into existence as we can if their lives would be positive. I would also like one closing question about what evidence or argument could change her mind, because several of my students are unconvinced that the conclusion is repugnant in the first place, and I want the interview to end on that live disagreement rather than on a restatement of her position.
