Why Every Truth Ends Up Known: Fitch's Knowability Paradox

The knowability thesis says that every truth is, in principle, knowable. On a simple modal-logical rendering, where K means "is known" and the diamond means "it is possible that," the thesis is the principle: for every proposition p, p → ◇Kp. The principle looks modest; it does not say that every truth is known, only that no truth is in principle beyond our epistemic reach. Yet, as Fitch showed (the result appears as Theorem 5 in his 1963 paper, "A Logical Analysis of Some Value Concepts"), the principle entails that every truth is in fact known: ∀p(p → Kp).

Here is the proof. Consider the proposition "p is an unknown truth," i.e. the conjunction (p ∧ ¬Kp). Suppose, for reductio, that this conjunction is known: K(p ∧ ¬Kp). Because knowing a conjunction entails knowing each conjunct, we get both Kp and K¬Kp. From K¬Kp, and the fact that knowledge implies truth, we get ¬Kp. This contradicts Kp, so the supposition was false: ¬K(p ∧ ¬Kp). Since this was derived with no undischarged assumptions, the necessitation rule lets us conclude that it is necessarily the case that ¬K(p ∧ ¬Kp), and therefore it is not possible that K(p ∧ ¬Kp): ¬◇K(p ∧ ¬Kp). Now suppose (p ∧ ¬Kp). Since it is true under that supposition, the knowability principle gives ◇K(p ∧ ¬Kp), which contradicts the previous line. So ¬(p ∧ ¬Kp), which is the same as p → Kp. Generalizing over p gives ∀p(p → Kp). Every truth is known.

Two points about the strength of the argument. First, the derivation is quite demanding: it depends on the S5 axiom of positive introspection, Kp → KKp (if you know p, you know that you know p), because without it the move from K¬Kp to ¬Kp would not be licensed. Second, the argument survives the obvious intuitionistic objection. Anti-realists typically reject classical logic, but every step of the proof, including the final move from ¬(p ∧ ¬Kp) to p → Kp, is valid in intuitionistic logic, so no classical principle is doing any work. The upshot is that the knowability thesis collapses into the claim that we already know everything, which is why verificationist and anti-realist theories of truth are in trouble.
