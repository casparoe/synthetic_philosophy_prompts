I'm teaching a seminar on Grice this semester and two of my grad students got into a real tussle over lunch that I couldn't referee, so I'm hoping you can. They were looking at the sentence "Some of the papers were rejected," which a journal editor might say about a batch of submissions, and they agreed it carries the "not all of them were rejected" reading. Where they split was over what kind of thing that reading is.

Mara said it's a plain convesational implicature, Grice-style: the maxim of quantity is doing the work, because the editor should have said "all" if that were true, and it's cancellable — the editor could have gone "some were rejected, actually all of them were." Devon pushed back and said cancellability is necessary but not sufficient, and that the "not all" is a conventionalized scalar implicature, baked into "some" as a scalar term on the <some, most, all> scale, not a free inference from the maxims. Mara replied that if it were conventional it'd be in the lexical entry for "some," but "some" literally just means "at least one," so the "not all" has to be added by the conversational context, which is exactly what makes it conversational. Devon conceded the point but said that's just Grice's binary being too clean, and that the "not all" is more robust than a typical free implicature — it's almost always there, across contexts, which is more like a conventional feature. I tried to bring up cancellability as a tiebreaker, and Devon hit me with the fact that "some were rejected, in fact all of them were" sounds really awkward and marked, way more so than cancelling a run of the mill implicature, which to me (and to Devon) is evidence of conventionalization. Mara's last move was to say the awkwardness is a context effect, not a lexical one, and that the robustness of the "not all" doesn't change the fact that it's an implicature rather than a presupposition, which is the point that actually matters.

I honestly don't know who's right about what here, and I don't want to tell my students something wrong. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right about each specific claim, and what, if anything, both of them are missing?
