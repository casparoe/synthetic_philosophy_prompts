I'm moderating the Q&A for a conference session, and the speaker is a philosopher who defends the two-dimensional semantic strategy in philosophy of language — the view that a term's meaning is captured not by a single intension but by two: a "secondary" intension that fixes its reference across possible worlds once the actual context is held fixed, and a "primary" intension that tracks what the term would pick out in different epistemic scenarios. The speaker has been using this framework to argue that the conceivability of philosophical zombies establishes their genuine metaphysical possibility, and to explain why a sentence to the effect that water is H2O is a posteriori yet necessary. I've found that in past discussions the conversation tends to drift toward the general conceivability of zombies and away from what the two-dimensional framework is actually doing, so I want a sequence of eight to ten questions that press the technical content of the position rather than having the speaker restate it, with each question paired with a follow-up keyed to the answer the speaker is most likely to give.

In particular, I'd like at least one question on the precise division of labor between the primary and the secondary intension, using the water/H2O case as the test — why exactly does the primary intension come out non-necessary while the secondary intension is necessary, and what that does for the a priori/a posteriori distinction. I'd also like a question on what is supposed to index the epistemic dimension (scenarios, centered worlds, or epistemically possible worlds) and whether that choice is arbitrary or does real work, since the force of the zombie argument seems to depend quietly on it. I want a question aimed squarely at the crux: the step from the claim that the primary intension of "zombie" is non-empty, which is an epistemic or conceivability claim, to the claim that zombies are metaphysically possible, together with the standard reply the speaker will almost certainly offer and a follow-up that tests whether that reply actually closes the gap. Finally, I'd like one question on Kaplan's objection that the apparent two-dimensionality is just a byproduct of indexicals and context rather than a deep feature of meaning, and one closing question about what kind of evidence or argument could genuinely count against the two-dimensionalist strategy.
