Last night I had a long, fairly heated argument with my friend Daniel about my cousin Priya. Priya has been working the same data-entry job at a logistics warehouse for eleven years. She's been passed over for promotion at least three times, the pay is barely above minimum wage, and the commute is two and a half hours a day. But every time I bring up the idea that she should look for something better, she gets defensive and says she's "fine where she is," that she "likes the routine," and that she's "not that ambitious." What's complicated the matter is that she has, in fact, had opportunities to leave — a cousin of mine offered her a better-paying job in the same city two years ago, and she turned it down. So when I press her, she points to that and says, "See? I chose this." Daniel, who's taking a course in the philosophy of economics, keeps telling me that this is a textbook case: her choices are the only thing that really count, and her stated ambivalence is just noise. He says that if I respect her as an adult, I have to respect what her choices show, and that my discomfort is just me projecting my own values onto her. I'm not sure I buy it, but I can't quite articulate why. Can you help me think this through? I keep coming back to the question of what her "preference" even is — is it the feeling she reports, the pattern her choices trace out, or the preference she would have if she actually understood her situation? And I want to know what, if anything, I'm allowed to do about it — whether "helping" her is a form of respect or a form of condescension.
