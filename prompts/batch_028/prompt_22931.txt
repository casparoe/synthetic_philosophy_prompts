I sit on my university's IACUC (the committee that reviews animal research protocols) and we have a protocol coming up on Thursday that I need a memo on before the meeting. The lab wants to use cuttlefish in a learning study — they're trained to pick colored lights in a tank to get a food reward, and the researchers say the whole thing is non-invasive, no pain involved, just observation of behavior. The problem is that cuttlefish are invertebrates, so they don't fall under the Animal Welfare Act at all, and NIH is still working up its guidance on cephalopods in research, so our policy manual basically has nothing to say about them. The protocol as written does not mention any measures for the animals' welfare beyond "they're kept in the tanks they came from."

One member of the committee is an invertebrate biologist who says the cephalopod literature on pain and stress responses is genuinely unsettled, and she thinks we shouldn't pretend we know they suffer. The other side says the study is observational and low-risk, and that if we start demanding vertebrate-level protocols for animals the law doesn't even cover, we'll strangle legitimate research. I personally can't settle whether a cuttlefish's experience is even the right category of thing to be asking about, but I do have to get the committee to a decision.

Can you draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday? I want a concrete recommendation with a time frame — approve as written, approve with specific added conditions, or send back — plus the reasoning the biologist and the researchers could both actually respond to, and an honest treatment of the strongest point on whichever side loses.
