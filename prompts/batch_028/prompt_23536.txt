I need a scene in which the character looks at his childhood home twice — once before he recognizes it, and once after — such that the scene instantiates the strong version of cognitive penetration of perception: the belief that this is his childhood home changes the actual phenomenology of his visual experience, not merely his judgment or attention. The case should not be a classic geometric illusion, the stimulus and the character's attention should be held constant across the two lookings, and the character should be able to detect and report the difference in his experience. Give me the scene, and briefly explain why it qualifies as strong penetration rather than mere top-down modulation.
