So I just got into this like two hour argument with my friend about Frege's puzzle (the Hesperus = Phosphorus thing) and I couldnt stop turning it over, so I wrote my side down as a short essay to sort myself out. Can you read it and comment, esp. on the middle paragraph where I try to say what the "informativeness" gap actually is?

Frege's puzzle, as I see it: "Hesperus is Phosphorus" is informative, but "Hesperus is Hesperus" is not, even though both are true and, if a name's meaning is just its referent, both express the same proposition (Venus = Venus). The puzzle is supposed to show that meaning can't be just reference, since then the two sentences would have the same meaning and the same cognitive significance.

My claim: the puzzle is real, but it's not a puzzle about meaning (proposition) at all. Both sentences express the same proposition; the gap is in what I'll call "cognitive significance," which tracks the modes of presentation the speaker uses, not the proposition. So the puzzle doesn't refute the view that meaning = reference; it just shows that cognitive significance isnt determined by proposition.

The part I'm least sure of: is "cognitive significance" a real third thing, distinct from both proposition and pragmatic implication? Or am I just relabeling the Fregean "sense" and smuggling it into a vague cognitive space? My defense is that sense is supposed to be part of the content of what's said (different sense = different proposition), whereas cognitive significance is a property of the utterance or the speaker's use, not of the proposition. But I'm not sure that's enough.

So: does this third-category move actually do any work, or is it just a relabel? And is there a cleaner way to state the distinction I'm after?
