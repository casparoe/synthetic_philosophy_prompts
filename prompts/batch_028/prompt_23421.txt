I'm a PhD student and a student member of my university's graduate student conduct committee, and I've been asked to draft the committee's written opinion on a case of research misconduct we just heard. A third-year PhD student fabricated the outcome of one experiment in a paper that was published in a peer-reviewed journal last year and is now cited in a handful of subsequent papers; she has since re-run the experiment, confirmed the null result, and told us she fabricated because the real data came back null and her advisor had repeatedly told her the project "had to work." The paper has not yet been retracted. We have to submit our opinion to the Dean by the end of the month, and the Dean has asked us to state plainly whether our recommended sanction is grounded in what the student deserves for her conduct or in what will best serve the department, the student, and the research community. Please draft that opinion: a concrete sanction recommendation, the reasoning behind it, and an honest treatment of the strongest case for the grounding we end up not adopting.
