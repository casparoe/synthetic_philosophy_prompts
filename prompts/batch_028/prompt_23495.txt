ok so I'm in a philosophy of science seminar and we've been going in circles on Humeanism vs. anti-Humeanism about laws of nature and I keep feeling like we're missing the actual point of the disagrement. the thing that bugs me is: both sides seem to agree on all the Humean base — the mosaic of local matters of particular fact, what actually happens in spacetime. they agree on every observable fact, every local property and event. the Humean says laws are just the compact regularities that best summarize that base (the best-systems picture, where a law is a theorem of the system that optmally trades off simplicity and strength), while the anti-Humean says laws are real governing entities that explain why the base is the way it is, with some kind of nomological necessity backing them up. but if they agree on everything that's actually the case in the world, what's left for them to disagree about?

I was talking about this with a classmate and I think the real question is whether the disagreement is substantive or just verbal — like, is the anti-Humean's 'necessity' and 'governing' just a different way of talking about the same regularities, or is there some actual metaphysical difference that shows up somewhere? and I think a concrete way to test it is: do the two views support the same counterfactuals? if I ask 'what would have happened if the initial conditions had been slightly different,' do the Humean and the anti-Humean give the same answer? if yes, maybe the disagreement is just verbal. if no, then it's substantive and we can point to exactly where they diverge.

can you write a dialogue between a best-systems Humean and a governing-laws anti-Humean in which they try to figure out whether their disagreement is substantive or merely verbal? I'd like them to actually engage with the counterfactual test I mentioned — have them work through a specific case together (maybe something like a simple dynamical system, or a particle moving in a field, or whatever concrete scenario you think will surface the difference) and see if they end up with the same counterfactuals or not. both should be competent and steelman the other side, not strawmen, and I'd like them to reach some kind of tentative conclusion about whether the gap is real or just a difference in how they're describing things.
