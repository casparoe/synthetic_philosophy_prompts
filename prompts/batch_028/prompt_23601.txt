I chair my university's data ethics review committee, and we vote next week on a proposal to allow a faculty member to scrape students' public social media posts for a study of political polarization, without individual consent, on the grounds that the posts are public. Half the committee wants to reject the proposal and adopt a general ban; half wants to approve it with an anonymization condition. Please draft the committee's decision memo—recommend an outright ban on the practice, approval with conditions, or rejection of the proposal—grounding the recommendation in the distinction between the two forms of Kant's universalizability test: whether the maxim "I may use others' publicly available information for my own purposes without asking" fails in the stronger sense, that it cannot coherently be universalized without self-defeating, which is the ground of perfect duties, or only in the weaker sense, that it is universalizable but no rational agent could will it as a universal law, which is the ground of imperfect duties—and what that classification implies for the form the policy should take. The memo should state which test it takes the maxim to fail, if either, and engage seriously with the strongest version of the position it rejects.
