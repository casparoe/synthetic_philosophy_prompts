Okay so I've been going back and forth with a friend about this and I want a second opinion because I cant get it out of my head. He's the type who's read a couple philosophy books and likes to make airtight arguments at dinner, and this is the one he keeps bringing up.

His argument: imagine you're having a really vivid hallucination of a red apple on the table. From the inside it's exactly the same as actually seeing a real red apple — no amount of looking inward will tell you which you're in. So the most honest description of either case is just "it seems to me that there's a red apple," and since that seeming is the same state in both, you never actually have the apple in your experience, only the image, and the outside world is just something you guess at. Perception, he says, is all mental pictures, and you can never be sure any of them line up with reality.

Here's where I think he's wrong but I can't say why. When I actually see an apple it feels like the apple itself is part of why I believe there's an apple — like the apple is doing the work of justifying my belief, not some internal copy. In the hallucination there's no apple doing that work, so the two cases feel genuinely different even if I can't tell them apart from the inside. He says that's just me being fooled, that if I can't detect the difference then for all that matters it isnt there. I think I've heard of a view called disjunctivism that's supposed to be a reply to his argument, but every explanation I found left me more confused. Can you compare his view with the disjunctivist one and tell me which holds up better, and specifically whether my intuition that the apple is "in there" doing justificatory work has any real defense or if I'm just pattern-matching?
