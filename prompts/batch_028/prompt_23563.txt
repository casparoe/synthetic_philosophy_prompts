I just spent two hours arguing with a friend about whether the suffering of wild animals — even invasive ones — has any moral weight at all, and now our board actually has to decide a case where it does. He maintains that wild animal suffering is natural and therefore outside the scope of our moral obligations, and that our only duty is to the conservation goal. I sit on the management committee of a coastal island reserve, where an invasive rodent population is driving two native seabird species toward local extinction. The committee must choose, before the next breeding season, between a lethal culling program, a non-lethal sterilization program, or a hybrid. The conservation staff are split: the culling camp says sterilization is too slow and too expensive, and that the rodents' own welfare is a negligible consideration next to the native birds' survival; the sterilization camp says culling is gratuitously cruel and that we have no standing to impose it. Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame, and it should engage the culling camp's strongest point — that the rodents' suffering is negligible compared to the native species' — rather than just restating the conservation goal.
