I sit on my health system's clinical informatics committee, and we're about to vote on which fairness criterion to lock in for a readmission-risk model we're rolling out across two patient populations with very different baseline readmission rates — the lower-rate population is also the one that has historically been under-served by our system. Our data team has laid out the impossibility result for us: with unequal base rates, calibration (a given score means the same real probability in both populations) and equalized odds (equal false-positive and false-negative rates across populations) cannot both hold, so we must pick one and live with the cost of the other. The scores will be used as a hard threshold that triggers a follow-up program, not as a soft input to clinicians. Please draft the short memo I'll circulate before Friday's vote: it should recommend one of the two criteria, say concretely what rejecting the other would cost us in this specific use, and honestly steelman the strongest argument for the criterion we'd be giving up.
