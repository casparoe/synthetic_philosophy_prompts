I am a novelist working on a work of speculative fiction in which the substance the inhabitants call "gold" is not the metal we know but a luminous, non-metallic material. I would like you to write a dialogue between a Kripkean defender of rigid designation and a descriptivist about whether my use of the name "gold" is legitimate. The descriptivist should maintain that a name in a work of fiction picks out its referent through a cluster of descriptions, so that I may apply "gold" to a new substance so long as it satisfies enough of the familiar attributes. The Kripkean should maintain that "gold" rigidly designates element 79, that the identity "gold is element 79" is a necessary a posteriori truth, and that my novel therefore either uses the name correctly (in which case my substance is, all along, the real gold) or misuses it (in which case I should rename my substance). Both speakers should be philosophically competent, should respond to each other's actual points rather than talk past one another, and should conclude by isolating the precise point on which their views diverge, so that I can decide which to follow in the novel.
