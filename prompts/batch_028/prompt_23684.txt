For a scene in my novel, a character who defends endurantism (the view that an object persists by being wholly present at each moment) is pressed by a colleague who defends perdurantism (the view that an object persists as a series of temporal parts). I want the exchange to be accurate. Please compare how the two views handle what is often called the problem of temporary intrinsics—the difficulty of explaining how the very same object can bear different intrinsic properties at different times—and explain why the anti-attitude case, in which an object is described as being both happy and sad, seems to pose a sharper challenge for the endurantist than for the perdurantist.
