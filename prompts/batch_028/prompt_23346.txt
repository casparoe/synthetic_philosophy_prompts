I'm in a metaphysics seminar this term and the fink/mask business is genuinely getting to me, so I want to try to work through it by writing fiction about it instead of just re-reading the papers. Quick refresher in case it's fuzzy: a finked disposition is one that gets destroyed the instant you try to manifest it (the standard example is a lump of gold that would freeze solid if you hammered it, so it's "malleable" only in some weird counterfactual sense), whereas a masked disposition is really there but blocked from showing — like a lightbulb that's fine but the power's off. Where I keep getting stuck is whether a finked thing actually has the disposition at all, or whether we're just being fooled by the counterfactual, and whether the fink/mask line is even stable or whether finks are just masks described in a misleading way.

So: write me a short story (a few hundred words is fine) that dramatizes this. Seed I'm picturing: a "disposition assayer" — part jeweler, part shrink — whose job is to certify, before a big deal closes (a marriage, an insurance policy, a job), whether a client's key disposition is intact, finked, or masked. One morning a client comes in whose disposition keeps reading as finked and then as masked every single time the the assayer runs the test, and the assayer slowley realizes that the very disposition they rely on to tell finks from masks is the one on the table. I want a reader who has zero background to still follow the human drama, but a reader who knows the distinction to feel it doing real structural work in the plot rather than showing up as a lecture.
