I write novels — the one just out is about a triage doctor who has to choose between saving one kid or a whole ward — so I've been deep in the forced-saving literature, and I host a small podcast where I interview people about ideas that show up in my books. Next week's guest is a moral philosopher who's spent the last deacade defending David Taurek's view that the numbers don't count: you're not required to sacrifice the one to save the many just because there are more. She's very good at the seperateness-of-persons move and keeps steering back to it. Draft eight to ten questions that go beyond having her restate the view. I want at least one question each on the one-versus-many asymmetry, on whether the view reduces which person you save to luck or who happens to be closest, and on what happens when the 'one' is someone the agent already owes a special obligation to — and for each, a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
