Two colleagues of mine argued over coffee after a talk on epistemic democracy, and I honestly couldn't referee. Mara said the Condorcet jury theorem is a genuine argument for majority rule: if each juror is more likely than not to reach the correct verdict and their judgments are independent, then the probability that the majority is correct approaches one as the jury grows, so larger juries are epistemically superior. Devon replied that the theorem is a knife-edge result that tells us little about real juries: it requires strict independence, which deliberating jurors plainly violate, and it has a cliff at p = 1/2, so if jurors are even slightly biased the wrong way, majority rule is worse than a coin flip and you would want the opposite of majority rule. Mara answered that the independence assumption is idealized in every such model, that the theorem's purpose is to identify the conditions under which majority rule is epistemically sound rather than to mirror real juries exactly, and that empirically jurors are at least as competent as random, so p ≥ 1/2. Devon conceded the empirical point but said it is the wrong metric: the theorem maximizes the chance of the correct verdict, treating a false conviction and a false acquittal as equally bad, whereas criminal law cares about minimizing wrongful convictions, and the theorem is silent on that asymmetry; she added that if p is only barely above 1/2, the jury would have to be enormous before the probability approaches one, which is impractical. Mara said the asymmetry is a limitation of the theorem, not a refutation of it, that the theorem still establishes majority rule is not anti-competent whenever p ≥ 1/2, and that there are versions that relax independence to allow moderate correlation. Devon said that relaxing independence requires strong extra conditions and yields a much weaker result, so the clean theorem is a beautiful mathematical fact but a poor guide to how juries should actually be constituted. Going through their exchange, can you say who is right about which specific claim, and whether either of them is missing something important?
