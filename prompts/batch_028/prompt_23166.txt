Here's the question I gave:

"State Hempel's raven paradox as an argument. Identify the two principles that generate it, and say why they can't both be kept. Then evaluate one proposed resolution of the paradox and say whether you think it succeeds."

And here's the student's answer:

"Part (a): Let H be 'All ravens are black.' By contraposition, H is logically equivalent to H*: 'All non-black things are non-ravens.' A green apple is a non-black thing that is not a raven, so it is a positive instance of H*. By the equivalence condition, whatever confirms H* also confirms H, so the green apple confirms 'all ravens are black.' This seems paradoxical because looking at an apple seems to tell us nothing about ravens.

Part (b): The two principles are Nicod's criterion and the equivalence condition. Nicod's criterion says that only observations of ravens should affect our view of H: a black raven confirms it, a white raven disconfirms it, and observations of non-ravens have no influence. The equivalence condition says that if E confirms H, then E confirms any hypothesis logically equivalent to H. These two can't both be kept, because Nicod's criterion says the green apple (a non-raven) has no influence on H, while the equivalence condition applied to H* says it does confirm H. So at least one of them has to go.

Part (c): I think the best resolution is the one Hempel himself gave. Hempel accepts the conclusion that the green apple does confirm the hypothesis — he doesn't try to deny it — but he argues that the degree of confirmation is extremely small. The reason is that there are vastly more non-black things in the world than there are ravens, so a single non-black non-raven does very little work. The apparent paradox comes from our intuition that the apple is irrelevant, but formally it is a confirming instance; we're just overestimating how much it confirms. So I think this resolution succeeds: it keeps the equivalence condition (which I'd want to keep anyway, since it's just a logical fact) and it dissolves the paradox by showing the confirmation is real but tiny."
