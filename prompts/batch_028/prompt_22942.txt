I'm three chapters deep into fixing the middle of my novel and I've hit a wall that I cant solve with craft, so I'm hoping you can. The protagonist is a journalist who gets offered an undercover assignment — six months embedded with a smuggling crew to write a book about them. She has to say yes or no in chapter four, before she knows what the assignment will do to her, and the story is built on the fact that it will change her, so that's not a flaw in the plot, it's the plot. Every version I've written either has her decide on some flimsy reason — "I need the story" — and I can feel the reader sensing she's just deciding because the plot needs her to, or she keeps stalling and the book dies.

Here's the thing I keep coming back to: I don't think she can make a fully informed decision, and I don't think that's a bug. She literally cannot know what she'll want to be like after six months in their company, so any scene where she weighs the pros and cons like a normal person feels fake to me. But I also can't just have her flip a coin or trust her gut, because that's the opposite of the kind of character I'm writing. I was at a writers' dinner last week and a friend said I should just write the aftermath first and work backwards, wich I've tried, and it made things worse because I ended up writing her future self's knowledge back into the present-tense decision. So — how do I write a decision that's honest about the fact that the person making it can't know the thing that matters most about it, without the scene collapsing into either arbitrariness or stalling?
