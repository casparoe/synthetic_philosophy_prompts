I'm not a consciousness person by training (I'm more of an alignment reader who keeps getting pulled into these threads), but I've been going down a rabbit hole on the meta-problem of consciousness lately — I keep bouncing between the Alignment Forum and Chalmers and I'm not sure I'm tracking the distictions right — and I wrote up some notes trying to pin down what I actually think the meta-problem is. I'd really appreciate a careful, pushback-welcome read, especially on the three things I flag at the end.

My working framework is to keep three things separate: (1) the hard problem, i.e. why is there subjective experience at all, why do physical processes give rise to it; (2) the explanatory gap, the claim that even granting every physical fact we still can't see how they entail the phenomenal facts; and (3) the meta-problem, which I take to be neither of those but rather "why do we *think* there's a gap in the first place?" — the problem of explaining the *intution* that consciousness is mysterious, the felt sense that something is missing. My hypothesis is that a lot of the literature quietly conflates (2) and (3): people argue about whether the gap is real, but the meta-problem is really about the *origin* of the gap-intuition, which feels like a more modest and more tractable question. I'm leaning toward what I'm calling "illusionism about the gap" — the gap-intuition is a product of how our conceptual architecture carves out "experience" so that it *looks* irreducible to the physical, and there's no real gap. But I'm nervous this just collapses into full-blown "illusionism about consciousness" (phenomenal consciousness itself is an illusion), which I don't want to bite because I'm pretty sure I actually have experiences. And then there's the Mary-room / knowledge argument thing, where I keep flip-flopping on whether Mary is an argument that there's a *real* gap (she learns a new fact when she sees red, so the physical facts missed something) or whether it's better read as an *explanation* of the gap-intuition (of course we feel like something's missing, given our special first-person access) — and I can't tell which reading is more charitable, or whether the answer even matters.

So concretely: (a) is my (1)/(2)/(3) split right, or am I mischaracterizing the meta-problem / splitting hairs? (b) Is "illusionism about the gap" a coherent position that's genuinely distinct from "illusionism about consciousness," and if so what's the cleanest way to draw the line (and if not, what's the move that makes them collapse)? (c) How should I read Mary, and does that answer settle whether the meta-problem is a genuinely separate, tractable problem or just the hard problem in a trench coat? I'd rather you tell me where I'm wrong than nod along, and if I'm misremembering how the meta-problem is usually framed, just correct me.
