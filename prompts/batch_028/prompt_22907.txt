I am advising a colleague who is designing a protocol for a clinical trial. A panel of experts is asked to estimate the probability that a new treatment will succeed. Half the experts give a single number (say, 0.6); the other half give a range (say, 0.4 to 0.7). The protocol requires a single threshold: if the estimated probability of success is above 0.5, the trial proceeds; if below, it does not. My colleague wants to collapse the ranges into point estimates before comparing them to the threshold, on the grounds that a decision requires a single number. I am not sure this is the right move. Is there a principled way to handle the ranges without collapsing them, and what are the philosophical stakes of doing so?
