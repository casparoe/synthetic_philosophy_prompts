I was at a climate-policy dinner last month and ended up stuck between two people who'd started arguing about a bill that defines what counts as a 'forest' for carbon-credit purposes. The bill needs a threshhold — some minimum tree density — and nobody can agree where the line goes.

The lawyer working on the bill kept saying there's a fact of the matter. Clear trees out of a stand one at a time and there's a specific tree that's the last one where it's still a forest, and the next one where it isn't. We just can't know which tree it is, which is why it feels fuzzy. So his move was to pick a bright line — he floated two hundred trees per acre — and accept that you'll misclassify a few cases near it. Better a known arbitrary line than no line at all.

The environmental philosopher at the table pushed back hard. She said 'forest' is genuinely vague — there's no fact of the matter hiding there. The real principle is tolerance: taking out one tree from a forest doesn't turn it into a non-forest. Keep applying that and you end up with a single tree being a forest, which is absurd — so the word can't have a sharp edge and can't obey the usual either-or rule. She called his 'unknowable sharp cutoff' a fiction the law invented to feel comfortable.

He replied that her tolerance principle is obviously false at the edges. The last tree in an otherwise bare field — pulling it clearly ends the forest. So tolerance breaks down, which means there's a boundary, which means there's a fact of the matter after all.

She said he was only right at the extremes. Tolerance holds in the middle, the borderline zone, and fails only at the clear-cut cases. So the concept has a sharp core and a fuzzy fringe, and there's no reason to think there's one true line anywhere in the fringe. The law should treat that fringe as a zone of judgment, not as a matter of getting an unknowable fact wrong.

I didn't take sides. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right about each specific point, and what, if anything, both of them are missing?
