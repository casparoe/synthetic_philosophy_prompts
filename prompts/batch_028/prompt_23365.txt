I've been thinking about Frankfurt cases and the principle of alternative possibilities and I keep getting stuck on a weird detail. Suppose someone is deciding whether to order a burger, and a neuroscientist has a device that would make them order salad only if they were about to order burger; the person freely decides burger and the device never activates. I get that Frankfurt people say this shows you don't need the ability to have done otherwise to be responsible, but I can't shake the feeling that the case is doing something sneaky by changing the causal setup around the decision itself.

Could you write a dialogue between a defender of Frankfurt cases and a defender of the principle of alternative possibilities, where they argue over exactly this burger case? I want the PAP defender to say something like the case only works if you already assume the agent's actual decision process is the kind that makes reponsibility attach, and that the would-be intervention is not as irrelevant as it looks. The Frankfurt defender should push back by saying that if the PAP defender still wants responsibility to depend on genuine alternative possibilities, the case shows that requirement is too strong or at least not in the right place. Make both of them competent, like they actually respond to each other's points instead of just repeating their slogans, and keep it short but with real back-and-forth.
