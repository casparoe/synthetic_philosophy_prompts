I was a population geneticist for thirty years before I retiered, and only then did I start reading philosophy of mind. Teleosemantics, the Millikanian idea that a mental state represents just what it was selected to represent, feels almost obvious to me, selection should be doing the work. But I keep getting stuck on the problem of novel content. How can an organism come to represent something no ancestor ever represented, say a quark? No selction pressure ever shaped a quark detector. The usual Millikan reply is that the content of a new state is fixed by the functions of the older capacities that build it up, so we don't need a fresh selection event. I want you to argue that this compositional move doesn't actually solve the problem. It just relocates the question of where content comes from onto the constituent capacities, and it still leaves unexplained how the first, non-compositional representations ever got any content at all. Make the case as sharply as you can, and keep it to a few tight paragraphs.
