I retired from physics a few years ago and have been spending my evenings on philosophy of science, and I keep returning to the so-called problem of old evidence, which I believed I understood until recently. I was discussing it with a former postdoc of mine, and she assured me that the problem is well understood and that Bayesian epistemology handles it without difficulty, but I am not convinced, and I would value a second opinion.

The textbook case is the anomalous precession of Mercury's perihelion: it was known and quantified from Le Verrier's time, decades before Einstein, and yet it is routinely cited as one of the strongest pieces of evidence for general relativity. On the standard Bayesian account, confirmation is an increase in credence, so that E confirms H just in case P(H | E) exceeds P(H). But if I had already assigned credence one to the perihelion anomaly, then conditionalizing on it changes nothing, and it cannot raise my credence in general relativity at all. The response I have encountered is to say that my credence in the "evidence" was in fact less than one, because what I possess is not a single fact but a body of many individual observations, and that a genuine update is therefore still possible. That strikes me as a cosmetic fix: I do know the anomaly, and modeling myself as uncertain about it merely to make the inequality come out right looks like changing the subject.

And there is a deeper difficulty that I keep coming back to. General relativity is, for me, a new hypothesis, so I never assigned it a prior credence in the first place, which means the P(H) on the right-hand side of that inequality was never actually defined. It therefore seems to me that Bayesian confirmation theory simply has no purchase on old evidence, and that the honest move is to concede the point and adopt a non-Bayesian account of confirmation. If I am missing a Bayesian response, I would be grateful if you could name it and explain specifically how it answers both the "cosmetic fix" objection and the ill-defined-prior objection; and if you think I am right that Bayesianism cannot handle the case, I would like to know which non-Bayesian account of confirmation you would recommend.
