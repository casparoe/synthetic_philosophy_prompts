The question was: In the derivation of the CHSH inequality, (a) state the assumptions, (b) say which assumption the experimental violation forces us to give up, and (c) explain why the violation doesn't allow faster-than-light signaling. The student wrote:

For (a), the CHSH inequality is derived assuming three things. First, locality: the outcome at Alice's station is a function only of her setting a and the shared hidden variable λ, and is independent of Bob's setting b, and vice versa. Second, measurement independence: the distribution of λ is independent of the settings, ρ(λ|a,b) = ρ(λ). Third, determinism: λ fixes the outcomes, so we can write A(a,λ) and B(b,λ) as definite ±1 values. Under these, the CHSH combination is bounded by 2, whereas QM reaches 2√2, which the experiments confirm.

For (b), the violation means at least one assumption is false. It can't be determinism, because any theory can be made deterministic by adding enough hidden variables, so the violation forces us to give up either locality or measurement independence. Measurement independence is clearly true — the experimenters pick their settings randomly, independently of the particles — so the violation forces us to give up locality.

For (c), even if Alice's outcome depends on b, she can't signal with it, because her marginal probability P(A|a) = ∫ dλ ρ(λ) P(A|a,λ) does not depend on b. Bob's setting only changes the joint correlation, which Alice can't read off from her own data alone, so no superluminal signaling.
