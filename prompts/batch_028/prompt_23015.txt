I'm on the drafting commitee for a new constitution here, and we're about to vote on how to deal with the parliament, so I need a short written opinion I can circulate to the other members before Thursday's session. The situation is: we've finished drafting the new fundamental law, but the existing elected parliament — which is hostile to us and to the head of state — has flatly refused to accept it and is insisting that only it has the power to amend the fundamental law, so it's stonewalling. The head of state wants to dissolve the parliament by decree and then put our draft to a national referendum, arguing that direct popular consent will settle the legitimacy question and make the whole thing proper. A couple of members on the committee are uncomfortable because it looks like we're just using the state's guns to manufacture a "yes" vote — force wearing a plebiscite's clothes.

I'd like the memo to engage Pascal's distinction between force and justice seriously rather than just gesture at it — in particular his point that the first establishment of any political order is a matter of force rather than justice, that justice without force is impotent but force without justice is mere tyranny, and his worry about the right of the strongest. My working question is whether a constitution ratified by referendum but only after the legislature has been dissolved by the state's coercive power can count as legitimate in the sense Pascal cared about, or whether it's just the old order's force re-labeled as popular will. Please recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame — for instance whether we should support the referendum at all, and if so under what conditions and in what sequence, dissolve first or hold the vote first — and honestly steelman the strongest case for the head of state's plan, since at least two of us think a free popular vote, even if not perfectly free, can genuinely found a new "right" rather than merely ratify a fait accompli.
