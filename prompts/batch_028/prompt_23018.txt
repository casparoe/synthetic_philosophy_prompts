I chair a small graduate reading group on Madhyamaka, and we've invited a visiting scholar to close out our spring series. He is best known for a recent book arguing that Nāgārjuna's catuṣkoṭi — the fourfold negation in which a thing is said to be neither existent, nor non-existent, nor both, nor neither — is not merely a reductio that leaves no positive residue, but a genuine, non-reifying doctrine of the middle way, with "emptiness" functioning as a real (if itself empty) conventional dharma rather than a mere absence. I want to prepare a Q&A that gets past the standard "emptiness is not nihilism" reassurances and presses where his position is actually vulnerable. Could you draft eight to ten questions that do that, each turning on the content of the view rather than inviting a restatement of it?

I'd like at least one question pressing the self-referential worry — if all four horns fail, is "emptiness" a fifth, and how does the "emptiness of emptiness" passage in the thirteenth chapter stop the resulting regress without smuggling in a positive ground — and at least one on why the "both" and "neither" horns are rejected on their own merits rather than as mere logical consequences of rejecting the first two; in particular, I want to test whether rejecting "both" really entails rejecting "neither," since a thing could be neither existent nor non-existent and yet not be "both." Please also include one question on the criterion that makes a conventional truth merely conventional rather than ultimately false, given that all four horns are denied. For each question, give a short follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give, so I can keep the exchange moving if he deflects.
