I spent my career in theoretical physics, so the idea of a gauge redundancy is familiar to me, but I have only recently turned to philosophy of physics, and the hole argument in general relativity has left me genuinely uncertain. Please write an essay on whether the indeterminism exhibited by the hole argument is a genuine physical indeterminism or merely a gauge redundancy. Set up the argument briefly: a diffeomorphism that is the identity outside a bounded region but non-trivial inside it maps one solution of the field equations to another, and the two solutions agree everywhere outside that region while differing inside it, so the field equations appear not to fix a unique solution from the data outside the region. Then weigh the substantivalist's position — that the two solutions are genuinely different physical situations, because they assign different metric values to the same manifold points, so the indeterminism is real — against the relationalist's position — that manifold points have no physical identity independent of the metric and matter fields, so the two solutions are the same physical situation and the indeterminism is illusory. Reach and justify a verdict on whether the indeterminism is genuine or illusory, and say whether the standard relationalist resolution succeeds or merely begs the question.
