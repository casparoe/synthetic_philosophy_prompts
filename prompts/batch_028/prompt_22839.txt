I'm writing a eulogy for my dad, who died last week. He was a philosopher of time and a committed presentist, the sort of guy who'd gently tell you over dinner that the past doesn't exist and only this moment is real. I'm a novelist, and I've spent my whole career writing books set in the past, so he had a running joke about that for thirty years. The service will be small: half the room will be his collegues, mostly eternalists, so they'll catch every reference, and the other half will be family who just want to be comforted. Please write a five-minute eulogy that comforts the family without saying anything he'd have considered false — which, given his view, means I can't lean on the past being real or preserved, and the comfort has to come from somewhere else. I'd like it to touch on the tension between my work and his, but land as grief and love rather than a lecture.
