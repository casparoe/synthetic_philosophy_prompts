"The exclusion argument goes like this. Premise 1: the physical is causally closed — every physical event has a sufficient physical cause. Premise 2: overdetermination is rare — one effect can't have two independent sufficient causes without a weird coincidence. Premise 3: on non-reductive physicalism, mental states are distinct from physical states (not identical) but supervene on them. So when I raise my arm because I want to, the neural firing is a sufficient physical cause, and my wanting is a different cause of the same effect. That's epiphenomenalism.

The best response is to deny that the mental cause is really a second cause. There's one cause, not two, and the argument only works if you count the same cause twice.

Also, the argument is really aimed at substance dualism, because it needs the mental cause to be a different kind of stuff from the physical cause. Non-reductive physicalists are safe because they already think the mental is physical, just described at a higher level."
