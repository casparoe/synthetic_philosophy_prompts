Compare Nāgārjuna's tetralemma (catuṣkoṭi) — the fourfold denial that a given proposition is true, is false, is both, and is neither — with the classical principle of excluded middle. Read as a positive thesis, the tetralemma appears to reject bivalence, since it rules out all four options, including "neither true nor false." Read instead as a reductio, it is a dialectical method that shows each option, when analyzed, collapses into contradiction, without itself asserting a positive claim. Which reading is more defensible, and how does the choice bear on the objection that the doctrine of emptiness (śūnyatā) refutes itself?
