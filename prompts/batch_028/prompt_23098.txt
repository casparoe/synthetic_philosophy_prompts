Write an essay on whether logical omniscience is a coherent ideal for a rational Bayesian agent. I've been going back and forth on this for a while and I keep seeing it come up in the alignment discusions but nobody seems to actually resolve it. The standard objection runs something like this: if an agent already believes every logical consequence of what it believes, it can never be surprised by learning a logical truth, but we're in fact surprised by new theorems all the time, so the ideal can't be what rationality is. But I'm not sure that's airtight. Maybe the right reply is that surprise is just a psychological feature a perfectly rational agent wouldn't have, so the ideal is unattainable rather than incoherent. Weigh that reply agianst the objection, and also say what an agent's credences in unproven-but-true propositions should be, whether belief closure is actually a requirement of rationality, and whether a non-logical-omniscient agent can still be rational in the way that matters for decision-making. Reach a conclusion and defend it, don't end on a non-committal note.
