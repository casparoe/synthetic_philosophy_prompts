I run a small podcast on philosophy of physics, and this month's guest is a philosopher who defends the self-indication assumption (SIA) against the self-sampling assumption (SSA). I want four to six interview questions that press her on the actual hard spots rather than have her restate the view. The centerpiece should be the presumptuous philosopher: a physicist in the year 2100 has two equally supported candidate theories of everything left — one predicting a cosmos with 10 to the 24th power observers, the other with 10 to the 36th — and the philosopher insists that merely by noting that she exists she can say, before any experiment is run, that the larger theory is a trillion times more likely. Have her walk through how that trillion-to-one ratio is derived, then press on whether the objection is really about the size of the ratio, about SIA's failure to respect background evidence, or about the reference class being too broad — and for each of those answers, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. Also include one question on the limiting case where one hypothesis makes the universe infinite, which SIA would seem to assign probability one to, and one on whether the problem is fixed by switching from observers to observer-moments or by restricting SIA to bounded reference classes. End with a question about what empirical discovery would make her drop SIA.
