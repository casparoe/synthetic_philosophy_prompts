I'm on my university's research integrity committee and we've got a vote next week on whether to sanction a postdoc. The case: she noticed a shared centrifuge making a new grinding noise and a box of reagents that looked mislabeled, but she told nobody — the PI, who's formally the one who flags equipment problems, was on sabbatical and she figured it wasn't her call. A week or so later another researcher ran a protocol on that centrifuge, the rotor failed, and a student got cut by a flying shard and needed surgery. The whole committee keeps getting stuck on the same thing, and honestly it's the causes-by-omission problem I've been reading about it on the blogs for years: did her silence actually cause the injury, or was it 'really' the centrifuge's fault (and the PI's, for not setting up a backup)? I want a short written opinion I can circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete outcome — sanction, no sanction, or something in between — and it should actually wrestle with wether the omission made a difference, not just defualt to 'she had no duty so it's not her fault.' And please give the strongest version of the case against whatever you recommend, not just a strawman.
