The "cluelessness" objection to consequentialism is often stated in a way that quietly conflates two distinct epistemic situations: (i) the betterness relation over outcomes is complete, but we are ignorant of which way it goes between the options before us, and (ii) the betterness relation is itself incomplete, so that some pairs of options are genuinely value-incomparable and no amount of further evidence could settle which is better. Explain the difference between these two cases and show why it matters: in particular, how does the force of the objection to consequentialism differ across the two cases, and which consequentialist responses (for instance, shifting to expected-value maximization, or to a decision rule that merely avoids dominated options) are available in each?
