Question: Explain what is meant by the "responsibility gap" in the context of autonomous systems. In your answer, (a) explain why the gap arises, and (b) discuss whether the gap is a problem for moral responsibility, legal responsibility, or both.

Student's answer: The responsibility gap is the problem that when an autonomous system does something harmful, we can't seem to hold anyone responsible for it. The reason it arises is that these systems learn and make their own decisions, so the programmer who wrote the original code didn't control the specific harmful action, and the user who was using the system didn't directly cause it either. So there's a gap between what morality requires — that someone be held responsible — and the people we could actually blame.

As for whether it's about moral or legal responsibility, I think it's the same problem either way. Since no one is really morally responsible for the harm, we also can't hold anyone legally responsible — the manufacturer didn't cause it and the user didn't cause it. So the gap means there's no one to hold accountable, period.
