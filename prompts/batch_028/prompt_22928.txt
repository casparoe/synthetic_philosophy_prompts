I'm writing a piece on Jain anekāntavāda (many-sided truth) for a science and ideas mag and I want to test a claim I keep circling. The saptabhaṅgī runs: syāt asti, syāt nāsti, syāt asti-nāsti, syāt avyaktā, syāt asti-avyaktā, syāt nāsti-avyaktā, syāt asti-nāsti-avyaktā — "in some respect it is," "in some respect it is not," and so on. A common reading is that this is a many-valued logic, so Jainism denies non-contradiction (the pot is both permanent and impermant). Argue against that: the seven "values" are not truth values but modes of predication indexed to a naya (a partial viewpoint), so anekāntavāda denies the possibility of a standpoint-free predication, not non-contradiction. Make the case as strong as you can.
