I'm a software engineer, not a statistician, so I may be misapplying the term, but I think what I'm seeing in our last A/B test is Simpson's paradox. The treatment arm beat the control arm on onboarding completion in every cohort I can slice by — country, device type, signup source, account age — yet the overall completion rate for the treatment arm is a few points lower than for the control arm. I've been told the lesson of the paradox is that aggregate statistics are unreliable and that you should always trust the subgroup-level results. If that's right, does the overall drop just mean the aggregate is an artifact of how the two arms happened to be composed, and the treatment actually improved completion?
