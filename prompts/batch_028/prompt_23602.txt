I sit on the technical review board for a safety-monitoring agent that our lab is preparing to deploy alongside a larger model. The monitor's behavior is specified in a doxastic logic: its beliefs form a set closed under logical consequence, and it satisfies the S5 introspection axioms, so it knows exactly what it believes. The spec's author treats this as a harmless idealization, arguing that because the deployed monitor only needs to check low-complexity invariants, it is close enough to logically omniscient for our purposes. The board has to decide whether to approve the spec as written, reject it, or approve it with conditions. Please draft the board's decision memo with a clear bottom line and the reasoning behind it. I want you to engage my colleague's strongest point (that the idealization is fine in practice because the monitor never needs to decide high-complexity consequences), and to address the concrete technical objection that deciding whether a sentence is a logical consequence of the monitor's first-order premises is undecidable, so no finite program can implement a monitor whose beliefs are closed under all such consequences.
