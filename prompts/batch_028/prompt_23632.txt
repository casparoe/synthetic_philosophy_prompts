I'm a second year political philosophy PhD student and my committee keeps telling me my work on equality is too abstract, so here's a real mess I'm sitting with. My town just got a one-time grant of about two million dollars and the council has to decide how to split it. The options on the table are basically: give every adult the same check, give more to people who report being less happy or in worse shape, or make sure everyone can actually afford the same basic basket of things — food, heat, a phone, bus fare to the one job that's hiring. Here's where it gets wierd: the long-term unemployed folks in the worst shape barely report feeling bad about anything, they seem fine, but they cant afford the bus to that job. And one resident has a disability that makes everything cost her roughly double, so under the equal-check plan she's left behind, but under the happiness plan she ends up tied with a guy who just complains about everything. I keep flip-flopping between asking who has the least stuff, who feels the worst, or who can actually do the things everyone is expected to do, and I dont know how to defend any of those once someone points at the complainer or the content-but-stuck folks. How should I actually think about which principle the council should use?
