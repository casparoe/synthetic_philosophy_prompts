After a long career in theoretical physics, I have recently taken to reading the philosophy of mathematics, and the independence of the continuum hypothesis from ZFC has been occupying a disproportionate amount of my thinking. I think I understand the broad philosophical stakes — whether CH has a determinate truth value, whether we ought to extend ZFC with new axioms, the so-called multiverse picture of set theory — and I find them genuinely interesting. What I have not been able to reconcile is Easton's theorem, which I take to show that the continuum function can be made to take almost arbitrary values, subject only to mild regularity conditions such as monotonicity. If that is right, then it seems to me that 2^aleph_0 could consistently be any aleph whatsoever — aleph_1, aleph_2, aleph_100, or even something like aleph_omega — and that all of these would be equally available to a set theorist free to choose her axioms.

I must be misunderstanding something, because I would have expected the size of the continuum to be a more tightly constrained quantity. Could you explain what I am getting wrong? In particular, is it actually consistent with ZFC that 2^aleph_0 = aleph_omega, or does some theorem I am not aware of rule that out, and if so, how does that bear on whether CH is a special question or merely the first in a long list of equally open ones?
