I have spent most of my professional life in experimental physics, where the difference between what is logically prior to what and what is causally or temporally prior to what is a distinction I take for granted and rarely need to make explicit. Having recently retired, I have taken up philosophy largely on my own, and I am now working my way through the literature on Molinism and what is called middle knowledge. I find the topic genuinely interesting, but I have run into a point where my understanding breaks down, and I would be grateful for help.

As I understand the standard account, God's middle knowledge is His knowledge of what any possible free creature would have done in any possible set of circumstances, and this knowledge is described as logically prior to God's free decree to actualize one particular world. It is precisely this "logically prior" claim that puzzles me. In my own field, when one item is prior to another in the sense that matters, it either causes the later item or at least stands earlier in time than it, and in either case it exerts a kind of constraint on what follows. So I find myself reasoning as follows: if the truth of these counterfactuals is prior to God's will, then it must be independent of His will; and if it is independent of His will, then there are facts about what I would freely do that are not up to God, which seems to place a real limit on His sovereignty. I cannot see how a Molinist could defuse this without either giving up the claim that middle knowledge is prior to the will, or giving up that God is sovereign.

Could you help me see where this line of reasoning goes wrong? I suspect, though I cannot yet say precisely how, that I may be importing a physical intuition about the direction of explanation that does not survive the move into this theological setting. I would value a careful account of the different senses in which one thing can be said to come "before" another, and of how the Molinist tradition is supposed to use that distinction to answer the objection I have laid out.
