I run a small experimentation team that decides which user studies to fund before a product launch, and until recently I justified each study by computing its expected value of information (EVPI) in isolation, treating the resulting figures as if they were independent. A colleague has since argued that this is methodologically wrong because the value of information is not additive across studies. Please argue for her position. In particular, explain why the joint value of two information sources can be strictly less than the sum of their individual values (the substitutability of information), demonstrate this with a small concrete decision problem, and explain why the monotonicity of the value of information in informativeness does not rescue the additive approach. Close with a practical recommendation for how a team should evaluate a portfolio of candidate studies and decide which to fund.
