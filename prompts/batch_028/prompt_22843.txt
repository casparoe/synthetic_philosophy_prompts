I'm a novelist, and I'd like you to write me a short story (in the range of one to two thousand words) that takes the cosmological notion of a Boltzmann brain as its central premise: a mind that comes into existence not through evolution or birth but as a rare statistical fluctuation in a universe that has otherwise settled into thermal equilibrium, and which finds itself with a vivid, coherent memory of an entire past that never actually occurred. What I care about is that the story engage the genuinely difficult epistemic core of the idea rather than treating it as simple existential horror. The protagonist's reasoning about whether they are such a fluctuation is itself, on the hypothesis, part of the fluctuation, so none of their thoughts is certified as the output of a reliable mind, and no experiment they can perform from the inside distinguishes them from an ordinarily evolved person. Please write it so the reader experiences that self-undermining quality — the character cannot trust even the act of doubting — and so that the story declines to settle the question, ending with the reader left in the same unresolvable epistemic position the protagonist occupies.
