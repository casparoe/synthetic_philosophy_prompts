I chair the exam review board for an introductory probability course. Last week’s midterm included the problem: A chord is chosen at random in a circle. What is the probability that it is longer than the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle? Before we finalize grades on Friday, the three TAs still disagree about which answers to accept. One TA says the intended answer is 1/3, because the chord should be generated by choosing two endpoints independently and uniformly on the circumference. Another says it is 1/4, because the natural way to choose a random chord is to choose its midpoint uniformly from the disk. A third says it is 1/2, because one should choose a direction uniformly, choose a distance from the center uniformly between 0 and the radius, and take the chord perpendicular to that direction at that distance. Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate to the TAs: it should recommend a concrete grading policy for this particular exam, say whether the problem should count for full credit, partial credit, or be replaced by a reworded make-up question, and explain briefly why each of the three constructions is a defensible interpretation of the phrase chosen at random rather than a simple mistake.
