My company just rolled out a screen monitoring tool for the whole office and im a little furious about it. I'm writing a short piece for the internal forum and I want it to be principled, not just "big brother is watching." Here's a draft — can you comment on the argument? Specifically: (1) does my "harmless information" case actually defeat the instrumental defense of privacy, or does the instrumentalist have some response (a future-proofing or option-value move, maybe) that saves the account? (2) where does the argument seem weakest overall?

Everyone's arguing about productivity, but I think the real issue is privacy, and I think the instrumental defense of privacy — the idea that privacy matters because it prevents harms like discrimination and chilling effects — can't do the work we need it to do.

Here's my case. Imagine a monitoring tool that only records which internal documents you open, and nothing else: no keystrokes, no browsing, no content. And imagine the information is truly harmless — the documents are accessible to everyone in the company anyway, and no one would definately use your reading history against you. No discrimination, no chilling of anything. Yet I still think being monitored in this way is a privacy violation. The instrumentalist has to say that if no foreseeable harm is possible, there's no reason to care. But that seems wrong. There's something about being watched, even when it can't hurt you.

So I want to argue that privacy is a relational good: its value doesn't lie in the information itself or the harms it prevents, but in the social relationship it structures. When a company monitors you, it's not just collecting data; it's asserting a kind of oversight that changes what kind of relationship the two of you have — from one of trust to one of supervision. And that change has value independent of what any particular piece of information could be used for.

I know this could sound like a relabeling — "you just don't like being watched" — so I'm not confident the relational account is doing real work rather than just repackaging the instrumental one.
