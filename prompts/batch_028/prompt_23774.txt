I've been reading about Avicenna's flying man thought experiment, where a person is created fully formed in mid-air, limbs spread so they don't touch, eyes closed, no sensory input, and yet still recognizes their own existence. Coming from a software background, I keep thinking of it as taking a system, cutting off all its external input and interaction, and asking whether the process itself still exists. I'd like you to compare it with Descartes' cogito. Both seem to isolate the self by removing everything external, but I'm not sure they're doing the same job or reaching the same conclusion. Specifically: what is each argument trying to establish, and do they both actually support the claim that the self is distinct from and independent of the body? Where do they diverge, and which of the two, if either, makes the stronger case that the self survives the removal of the body?
