I'm a novelist, and I've been rereading Tolstoy's late essay on the nature and purpose of art for a book I'm working on. His central claim — that a work of art has value only insofar as it transmits to its audience a feeling the artist genuinely experienced, and that the artist's appeal to not knowing what he is creating for is really a confession that the work serves only himself — is the idea I want to dramatize, but every attempt I make turns the story into a lecture. I keep being struck, too, by how Tolstoy seemed to hold himself to his own standard, simplifying his late stories and disparaging his earlier, more ornate ones, which only makes the theory harder to write about without sounding like I'm summarizing it. A friend of mine who is a musicologist suggested I put the theory under pressure through a concrete dilemma rather than stating it, so I'd like you to write a short story that does exactly that.

Set it in a provincial Russian conservatory in the last years of the nineteenth century. The protagonist is a young composer who has two works to his name: a technically dazzling symphony that the critics praise as a masterpiece but which leaves the concert hall cold, and a simple, almost naive folk song that made the whole village weep the first time it was sung. He has been asked to submit one of them to a prestigious prize, and he must choose. The twist I want: the folk song that moved everyone was actually written by his older sister, who has since died, and he has been performing it as his own; the prize committee is about to credit him, and he must decide whether to confess. I want the aesthetic question — whether a work that transmits a genuine feeling but was never felt by the person who made it counts as true art — to be at least as present as the moral one. Let the reader feel the tension between technical brilliance and genuine communicability, without the narrator ever lecturing, paraphrasing Tolstoy, or resolving the dilemma with a neat moral. Keep it under 1,500 words, in a restrained, period-appropriate voice, and leave the ending open.
