I've been stuck on this idea for a while, the one where the choice you make about when to have a kid actually decides which person ends up existing. If you have the baby now you get one person, but if you wait a year a totally different person is the one who gets born, and there's no way to swap them. And the part I can't get over is that the kid who actually exists cant really be wronged by your call, because the alternative wasn't "a worse version of her," it was just that she doesn't exist at all.

Anyway I'd love a short story that captures that exact weirdness, not the surface level of "parent makes a tough choice." The thing I keep failing to pin down is the parent who feels guilty but every way they try to say what went wrong just falls apart, because the complaint "I made her life worse" isn't even true, the only other option is that she's not here. Can you write a story where the whole emotional weight comes from that gap, where the character keeps reaching for a reason to feel guilty and each one dissolves for that specific reason? You don't have to explain the philosophy out loud, just make the reader feel the hole.
