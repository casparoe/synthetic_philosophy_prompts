I've been rereading Borges for a while now, and his stories keep circling back to the nature of time — the branching labyrinth of The Garden of Forking Paths, the dreamer in The Circular Ruins who discovers that he himself is being dreamed, the suggestion of The Library of Babel that the universe is an enormous combinatorial structure. Borges died in 1986, but in 2003 the philosopher Nick Bostrom published the simulation argument, which argues that we may be living inside a computer simulation, in which case the flow of time we experience is just a sequence of states computed by something outside it. If Borges had read Bostrom, what do you think he would have made of it, and how would it have fit with his own views on time?
