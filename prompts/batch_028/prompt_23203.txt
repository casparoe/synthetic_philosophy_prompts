I sit on a military justice review board that advises the government on whether to pursue criminal prosecutions of former enemy combatants now in custody. We have a live case in front of us: a conscripted soldier from the opposing army killed one of our own soldiers during a routine firefight, and there is no evidence of any atrocity — just one soldier shooting another in the ordinary course of combat. Our soldier, for what it's worth, was also a conscript. The board is split. Half of us want to prosecute the enemy soldier as a murderer; the other half point out that we would not prosecute our own soldier for doing exactly the same thing, and that treating the two differently seems arbitrary. I've been thinking a lot about the idea that soldiers on both sides of a war may be morally on a par even when one side is fighting an unjust war, and I'm not sure how far it takes us here. Please draft the written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's session: it should recommend whether we should pursue the prosecution or drop the case, give reasons the other half of the board could actually respond to, and engage that "both sides are on a par" idea seriously — including the strongest reason to set it aside in this particular case, given that the enemy soldier was a conscript.
