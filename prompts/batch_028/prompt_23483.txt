I sit on my state's parole board, and I'm a software engineer by profession — I spent three years on the team that built the actuarial recidivism model the board relies on, so I know its internals better than anyone in the room. The case before us has split the board. A man convicted of armed robbery in 2005 and sentenced to 12–30 years has now served 20 years; he was denied at his two previous hearings and is before us again. The model scores him in the top decile of 10-year recidivism risk, which in his offense cohort corresponds to roughly a 28% probability against a base rate of 9%, but the model was validated on applicants near first eligibility, around 12 years in, and I don't know that the score transfers to a man who has already served two decades. Half the board says the robbery has long since been paid for and that holding him five more years on the strength of a score I can't vouch for is punishment without a justification; the other half says the only legitimate reason to hold a man past his minimum is to prevent the harm he might cause, and that 28% over a decade is too high a risk to release into the community. Please draft the board's decision memo, to circulate before next week's vote — grant parole now, or deny and revisit in five years — with a clear recommendation, the reasoning for it, and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side.
