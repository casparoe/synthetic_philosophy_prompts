Question: In 400 words or fewer: (a) state Grice's Cooperative Principle in your own words; (b) give one example of a flout of a maxim, identify which maxim is flouted, and work out the conversational implicature; (c) explain the difference between what is said and what is implicated, and why the distinction matters.

Student answer: Grice's Cooperative Principle says that in a talk exchange, participants generally cooperate: each contribution should be of the kind required at that stage by the accepted purpose or direction of the exchange. It's a description of how competent speakers normally behave, not a rulebook they consciously follow. For (b), take "It's a dry day, and the grass is green." The first clause is irrelevant to the second, which flouts the maxim of relation (be relevant). The listener infers the speaker is being coy or withholding, and that is the conversational implicature. Another classic is "Some of the students passed the exam," which flouts quantity: the speaker could have truthfully said "all," so the implicature is that not all passed. For (c), what is said is the literal propositional content of the utterance, while what is implicated is the further content the listener derives from the assumption that the speaker is being cooperative. The distinction matters because the implicated content is cancellable, non-detachable, and not part of the literal meaning, and it is exactly what lets us understand irony and indirectness. Since the implicature is just what the speaker meant beyond the literal content, conversational implicature and speaker meaning end up coming out to be the same thing, which is why Grice's account is so powerful.
