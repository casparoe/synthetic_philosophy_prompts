Write a short story about a backend engineer at a mid-sized startup who has to decide whether to take a lead role at a small research lab. The catch, which they read about in a paper on transformative experience: the role is personally transformative in the strong sense, so the person who takes it will have different preferences than the person sitting in the interview right now. That means they literally cant assign a utility to the outcome, and their whole expected-utility framework for the decision just breaks. I want the story to actually wrestle with the option value response, the idea that even if you can't evaluate the experience itself, the value of finding out is evaluable and might be enough on its own to justify the choice. Make the character someone who thinks in decision trees and minimax-regret, and show the limits of that mindset without being preachy or turning it into a lecture. First person, a few thousand words.
