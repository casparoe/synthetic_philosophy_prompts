I'm a PhD student in value alignment and I keep coming back to Mill when I think about coherent extrapolated volition (CEV) — the idea that an AI shouldnt just optimize for what people currently ask for, but for what they'd want if they could think about their own values longer, more clearly, and more cooperatively. On one hand Mill's a utilitarian so summing people's preferences is basically his whole thing, but on the other he's already gesturing at idealized preferences with his higher/lower pleasures stuff and his competent-judges argument (the one who's tasted both is the better judge). So what do you think Mill's actual position would be — does he endorse the CEV move, or does he think it's a betrayal of utilitarianism? I especially want to know whether he'd worry that 'extrapolating' preferences is just a smug way for whoever's building teh AI to override what people actually want with an elite notion of what they'd want if they were more like the designers.
