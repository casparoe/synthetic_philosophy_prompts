So I've been running this little self-experiment for a few months now. Background: I read a while ago, in some forum thread I can't even find anymore, that people's confidence in their own memory is basically uncalibrated, and it stuck with me. So every Sunday I pick a random event from the week — a conversation, a meeting, something I ate — and try to reconstruct it in detail before looking at anything. Then I check against my calender, my notes, and where it exists, photos or message logs. Match = 1, mismatch = 0, and I keep a running percentage. It's been hovering around 80-something percent, and honesty I feel like I'm actually learning something about how much I should trust my own recall.

The problem is that a friend of mine, who's also deep in this stuff, looked at my setup and said the whole thing is circular in a way that makes it worthless: I'm using my memory to check my memory, so of course it mostly passes, it's just the system grading its own homework. I pushed back that the notes and calendar entries were written at the time, not recalled, so there's an independent record in the loop, but he wasn't convinced. His view is that the only non-circular version is to have other people keep records of what I said and did, which is obviously not scalable, and that I should just stop.

Is he right that the circularity kills the experiment, or is there a version of this that actually gives me evidence about my own reliability? And if it's salvageable, what should I actually be reading out of the 80% number? I don't want to just carry it around as a vibes-based confidence boost, but I also don't want to throw away a month of data over a technicality.
