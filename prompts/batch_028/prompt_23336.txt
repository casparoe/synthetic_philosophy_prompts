I've been preparing a position paper for my epistemology seminar, and the interaction between luminosity and the KK principle is the part I keep getting stuck on — I can state the arguments, but I'm not sure where I should land. Could you write an essay on whether the KK principle is defensible, with the luminosity debate as the central point of tension?

By the KK principle I mean the claim that if one knows that p, then one knows that one knows that p — or, in the weaker form, that one is at least in a position to know that one knows that p. The essay should weigh the main arguments on both sides. On the side in favor, you might draw on the intuition that knowledge is in some way self-intimating or transparent, and on the idea that the luminosity of the knowing state — roughly, that being in a state of knowing p puts one in a position to know that one is in that state — licenses the weak form of KK. On the other side, you should engage with Williamson's argument in Knowledge and Its Limits that mental states, including on his view the state of knowing, are not luminous, and spell out why this threatens the luminosity-based route to the KK principle.

I'd also like you to address at least one substantive response — for example, whether restricting luminosity to certain states such as phenomenal states lets one preserve a limited KK, or whether the weak form survives even when the strong form fails. Reach and justify a clear conclusion rather than ending on a non-committal note, and say something specific about whether you think the failure of luminosity genuinely undermines the KK principle or whether the two can be decoupled.
