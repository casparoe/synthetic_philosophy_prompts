Im moderating a small student paper series next week and the guest is a philosopher who defends Williams-style internalism about practical reasons, the view that an agent has a reason to act only if the reason is connected, in the right way, to the agent's subjective motivational set, either directly or through a suitably rational extension of it. I want six to eight interview questions, each with one follow-up, that go beyond having him restate the view. Please make at least one question each press on how the subjective motivational set is supposed to be identified without circularity, on whether the rational extension clause is doing too much work or too little, on cases of ignorance where the agent lacks the relevant desire but would have a reason if she knew the facts, and on whether internalism can make sense of criticizing other people's motivations or of moral progress. The follow-ups should anticipate the answer he is most likely to give and turn the pressure back on the view.
