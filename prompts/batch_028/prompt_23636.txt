After a career in physics, I have recently taken up the philosophy and technical literature on AI alignment, and I find myself uncertain about the distinction between inner alignment and goal misgeneralization. My working understanding is that inner alignment concerns whether the model's internal goals correspond to the objective it was trained to optimize, while goal misgeneralization concerns the model's behavior departing from what was intended when the environment shifts. But since the model's weights are fixed at inference, its internal goals do not change, and its behavior is simply the optimal policy for those fixed goals. So if the behavior departs from our intent out of distribution, it seems to me that the only explanation is that the fixed internal goals were a poor proxy for our intent to begin with — which is to say, that the internal goals do not correspond to what we actually wanted. On that reading, goal misgeneralization appears to be inner-alignment failure in disguise. Am I missing something, or is the distinction between the two largely terminological?
