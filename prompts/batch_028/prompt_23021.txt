Two quant friends of mine got into an argument at a bar about the Kelly criterion. One said it's the only rational betting strategy: it's the unique fixed fraction of your bankroll that maximizes your long-run growth rate, so if you're going to keep betting, anything else just grows slower. The other pushed back and said that's not an objective fact, its just what a log-utility agent wants, and a rational person with constant relative risk aversion γ should bet f*/γ, not f*, so calling Kelly "rational" smuggles in a particular utility function. The first one replied that the growth-rate argument doesn't need any utility at all, and that overbetting is fatal no matter what you value, because if you bet more than Kelly your bankroll goes to zero almost surely. The second one conceded that but said that only shows overbetting is bad, not that full Kelly is good, since half-Kelly keeps about three quarters of the growth rate while cutting your drawdown risk a lot, and if your estimate of the edge is even a little too high, full Kelly puts you on the ruin side of the line. The first one said fine, if you don't trust your edge estimate you should shrink toward no-bet, which is exactly the same as having a bigger γ, so we actually agree on the mechanics and the only real question is what γ is, which is just a matter of preference. I was the one holding both their drinks so I didn't say anything. Can you go through this move by move and tell me who's right about each specific claim, and then say what both of them are missing?
