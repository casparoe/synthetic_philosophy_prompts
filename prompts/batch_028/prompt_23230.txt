I dont want to bias you, so I'll leave it as is:

Neurath's first rule is that a bigger quantity is shown by more pictograms of the same size, never by a bigger pictogram, because an enlarged figure is ambiguous (is the increase in its height or its area?) whereas same-size figures can just be counted. The status of the picture-language, on the other hand, was meant to be provisional: Neurath saw it as a first step toward a fully wordless language, and only later, as a kind of concession, did it get described as a 'language-like technique' that merely goes along with words. That fits his anti-foundationalism — the ship that is rebuilt plank by plank at sea — which means the whole enterprise, the picture-language included, is built up piecemeal without fixed foundations, and so the Isotype rules themselves are only temporary conventions, to be revised as the language matures.
