I did biochemistry for about thirty five years before I retired, and philosophy only grabbed me a couple of years ago. I went straight for Ian Hacking's work on psychiatric classification and what he calls the looping effect, because as a chemist I basically understand feedback loops and the core idea is clean: you carve out a kind of person, people find out they're that kind, they start acting differently, and the kind quietly changes underneath the classifier. The problem is that all of Hacking's best examples are adults, the pervert or the delinquent who internalizes the label and performs it. I've been chewing on this with a few friends and I keep landing on the thought that the adult cases are the easy ones, and that the interesting case is actually one of his own examples, the hyperactive child, where the classified person is a kid who can't exactly read the diagnostic manual and restructure their own personality.

So here's what I want: make a real argument for the position that when the looping effect shows up in a childhood psychiatric classification like ADHD, the feedback runs mostly through the adults and institutions around the child, the parents, the teachers, the school accomodations, rather than through the child's own self knowledge, and that this makes it a structurally different feedback from the self fulfilling prophecy looping Hacking emphasizes in the pervert and delinquent cases. Bring in his interactive versus indifferent kinds distinction and say something honest about what this implies for how 'real' or stable the classification is. Don't just restate the standard account and then politely note the complication, actually take the side I just described and defend it.
