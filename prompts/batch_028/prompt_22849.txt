I'm writing a feature on how philosophers have tried to naturalize the content of beliefs, and teleosemantics keeps tripping me up. As I understand the view, a mental state represents something just in case that state was shaped by natural selection to track it — my fear of snakes represents danger, for instance, because the ancestral version of the fear was selected for firing when real threats were present. If that's right, then the representation seems guaranteed by the selection story itself: the state is, by definition, the kind of thing that fires when what it is for is there, so it shouldn't be able to fire in the wrong circumstances. But we obviously do have false beliefs all the time. So how does teleosemantics make room for error, or is the theory quietly committed to the idea that misrepresentation is impossible?
