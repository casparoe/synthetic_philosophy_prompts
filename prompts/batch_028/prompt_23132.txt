I've been reading a lot online about decision theory and how a rational agent ought to make choices, and I keep running into this idea called acausal trade (I've also seen it called acausal cooperation). The basic setup, as far as I can tell, is that two agents who have no way to communicate and no way to causally affect one another — for instance, they're on opposite sides of the universe, or each makes its decision simultaneously with no message passing in between — can nonetheless end up doing things that benefit both of them, purely by reasoning about what the other agent is likely to do. I can follow the headline idea, but I don't think I actually understand the mechanism, and I keep hitting a wall on a couple of specific points.

The first thing I don't get is the causal story. If the two agents genuinely cannot influence each other, then in what sense does one agent's choice "make" the other agent do its part? To me that starts to sound like a one-shot prisoner's dilemma in which each side is just hoping the other will be nice, which I would expect to collapse into both sides defecting. So what is the actual mechanism by which the cooperation gets locked in? And I've seen a specific claim that this only works if each agent can build a reasonably accurate model of the other agent's decision-making procedure — not merely the assumption that the other agent is friendly, but a model of what the other agent will decide given its expectations of me. I don't see why that much modeling is required, and that's the second thing I want clarified.

Finally, I'm curious about a variation that I think exposes the mechanism: what happens if the two agents don't reason in the same way? I've seen the term causal decision theory, which I take to mean an agent that weighs its options by counting only direct causal influences, as opposed to an agent that reasons more broadly along the lines of "what would my kind of agent do in this situation?" If one side of the trade uses that causal-only reasoning and the other side uses the broader reasoning, does the trade still go through? I'd like you to walk me through the mechanism step by step first, and then address the modeling question and the mixed-decision-theory case, because I think those two are exactly where my understanding is breaking down.
