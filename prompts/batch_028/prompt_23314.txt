I'm a retired meteorologist who has only recently gotten into philosophy, and I'm currently the adjunct instructor for the introductory epistemology section I teach at the community college. I'd like your help grading a student's final answer, since I'm not confident I'm catching all the subtle errors. The exam question was: "Explain the regress problem in epistemology, and then evaluate foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism — for each, state how it is supposed to escape the dilemma, and state the main objection to it." The student's answer is below. Please give the answer a letter grade and a point-by-point assessment that justifies it: for each of the three views, say whether the student's characterization is accurate, whether the objection they offer is the right one, and whether the objection is stated correctly. Then give an overall letter grade with a one-paragraph justification. I'd rather have a point-by-point assessment than a general impression, and I'd like you to flag any subtle errors that a hasty grader might let slide.

The student's answer:

"The regress problem is that if every belief has to be justified by other beliefs, then the chain of justification either goes on forever, loops back on itself, or stops at some belief that isn't justified by anything. Foundationalism says the chain stops at basic beliefs that don't need justification from other beliefs. The main objection is that these basic beliefs are just dogmatic — they're assumed to be true without any reason, which is arbitrary. Coherentism says there's no foundation; instead, beliefs justify each other in a web, and a belief is justified if it fits coherently with the others. The main objection is that a web of beliefs could be a consistent fiction, like a well-written novel, and still be false. Infinitism says the chain of justification goes on forever, so there's no need for a foundation or a loop. The main objection is that this is impossible for a finite being, since we can't actually have an infinite number of reasons. Of the three, I think foundationalism is the most plausible, because at least it acknowledges that some beliefs are more basic than others, which matches how we actually think."
