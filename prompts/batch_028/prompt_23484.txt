The standard answer to the Meno problem — why is knowledge better than true belief? — is that knowledge is more stable or reliable, whereas true belief may be merely lucky. But this answer faces a difficulty: reliability is a property of a method or a type, not of a particular instance, so it is not obvious how a particular piece of knowledge is "more reliable" than a particular piece of true belief, since both are, in fact, true. How, then, can reliability be what makes knowledge better than true belief? Please explain this difficulty and evaluate the main responses to it, including the move from comparing instances to comparing types and the claim that knowledge has an intrinsic epistemic value that true belief lacks.
