I have been asked to referee a dispute between two independent actuaries who were each commissioned to price the same catastrophic-loss insurance product. Both are scrupulous Bayesians, and both have now fully disclosed to each other every data set, model, and private information source they relied on. After several rounds in which each revised their estimate in light of the other's, the gap has narrowed but not closed: they now stand at 31 percent and 44 percent for the probability of a major loss within the next five years. The senior actuary insists that this residual gap is incoherent — since they have shared everything and both are rational, one of them must be making an error of arithmetic or probability. I am not satisfied by this. Is he right that two rational agents who have exchanged all their information can no longer legitimately disagree, and if not, what exactly is it in their setup that licenses the remaining gap?
