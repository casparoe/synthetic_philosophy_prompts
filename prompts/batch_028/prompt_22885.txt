So I've been reading a bit about David Lewis's modal realism, the idea that possible worlds aren't just useful fictions or abstract descriptions but are literally as real as this world, just not connected to ours by space or time. And last week at dinner a friend of mine who reads philosophy made this move that I cant stop thinking about. He said that if Lewis is right, then in some possible world I'm a fabulously wealthy CEO, and so in a very real sense I already am a billionaire — I just happen to be stuck in the branch of reality where I'm not. He told me I should feel less envious of my cousin who made it in tech, because it's me over there, just in another universe.

And here's the thing: I can't tell if I'm just being a sore loser about my cousin or if there's a real logical problem with his argument. On the one hand Lewis does say these other worlds are as concrete and real as ours, so I don't think my friend is just talking about make believe. But on the other hand the statement "in some world I'm a billionaire" feels like it's smuggling something in, and I can't pin down what. If I push the logic, it also looks like he should be able to say "in some world you're a mass murderer, so in a sense you are one," and I don't think anyone would find that comforting or even true. Is my discomfort justified? Does Lewis's view actually support the "you're already a billionaire in a sense" move, or is my friend misapplying it — and if so, where exactly does the argument break down?
