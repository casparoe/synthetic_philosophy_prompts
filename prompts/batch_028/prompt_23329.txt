I'm on the board of our local weekly and I've been reading a lot about information cascades lately — the idea that a herd can form even when everyone is being rational, because each person treats what earlier people did as evidence. We're about to face a decision and I'd like a short memo you'd circulate to the board before Thursday's meeting. The situation: two bigger outlets in the state both ran a story yesterday that a county commisioner took kickbacks, and both say they reviewed bank records — apparently the same ones. We haven't seen the records ourselves, and the commissioner's lawyer is already calling the story fake. If we run it and it's wrong, we're done for; if we hold it and it's true, we just look timid. I keep flip-flopping on whether two outlets running it is real evidence or basically one data point with two stamps on it. The memo should recommend a concrete course of action — run it, hold it, or verify first — with a time frame, and it should take the "just follow the outlets, their reporting is real evidence" side seriously instead of waving it away as herd behavior.
