My philosophy classmate keeps bringing up Robert Nozick's 1981 book Philosophical Explanations, in which he argues that many animals are conscious but lack a "self" — a self that is the subject of their experiences — and that this distinction is supposed to matter ethically. Write a short story, about 800 words, that takes this idea seriously. Set it in a world where the scientific community has reached a consensus about exactly which species lack a self, and center the story on a person — perhaps a veterinarian or a researcher — who must make a decision that forces them to ask whether the absence of a self is really what matters. The story should not simply agree with Nozick; it should dramatize the tension between an animal's conscious experience and its philosophical status as a self, and it should end in a way that suggests the ethical weight of animal consciousness cannot be fully explained away by that distinction.
