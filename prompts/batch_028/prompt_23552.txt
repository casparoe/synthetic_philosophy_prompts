Two friends of mine who both took a game theory course got into it at dinner about iterated elimination of dominated strategies, the thing you do to narrow a game down to the strategies that could actually show up in an equilibrium. One said the clean result is that eliminating strictly dominated strategies gives the same surviving set no matter what order you do it in, which is why strict dominance is the safe solution concept. The other agreed on that part but said it doesn't help, becuase in the games that actually matter the eliminations are usually weak dominance, and there the order you eliminate in can change which strategies survive. The first one said that's just a practical wrinkle, pick a sensible order and move on, and that weak dominance is the concept you actually want most of the time anyway. The second one said if the answer depends on an arbitrary choice like the order then it's not a solution concept at all, just whatever you happen to get, so the whole exercise is rigged. Theyre both stubborn and asked me to referee. Can you go through their exchange and say who is right about each specific claim, and what, if anything, the two of them are both missing?
