I am a retired astrophysicist who has only in recent years taken up the philosophy of physics, and I should be grateful for your considered opinion on the following question. Had Albert Einstein been alive to learn of the so-called loophole-free Bell tests first reported in 2015, and of the use of Bell-inequality violations to certify randomness in devices whose inner workings one does not trust, what would have been his position? In particular, would he have regarded these results as a refutation of his own demands, or merely as further evidence that the quantum state is not a complete description of physical reality? I should be glad if your answer were grounded, as far as possible, in his published statements rather than in the caricature of a physicist who merely objected to action at a distance, and if you would distinguish, as far as the historical record allows, between the separability requirement he expressed in the 1935 EPR paper and the locality condition that Bell's theorem actually targets, saying which of the two the experimental results really bear upon.
