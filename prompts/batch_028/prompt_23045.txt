Argue that the mere presence of a counterfactual intervener in a Frankfurt case is enough to make the agent not free, even if the intervener never actually intervenes and even if the agent is completely unaware of the intervener's presence. The key point is that this isnt just a matter of the agent's psychology (feeling watched, feeling controlled, etc.) but of the causal structure of the case: the mere presence of the intervener changes the causal story of the agent's action in a way that vitiates responsibility, even if the agent's decision making process is exactly the same as it would be without the intervener. And then respond to the standard reply that what matters is the nature of the actual sequence, not the mere presence of the intervener.
