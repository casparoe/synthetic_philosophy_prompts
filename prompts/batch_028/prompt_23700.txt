Kierkegaard's The Present Age (1846) is basically a takedown of how the modern press levels everyone out into a bland sameness while also turning the public into a phantom that can never commit to anything. I want you to take that framework and run it against how algorithmic feeds and social media actully work now, and figure out where his account still tracks and where it falls apart. Does the recommendation engine do the leveling he was scared of, or does it do something he didn't have a word for, like shattering the public into a million personalized micro-bubbles? Land on a verdict: does his picture of the public survive the algorithmic age, or does it need a real rewrite, and why.
