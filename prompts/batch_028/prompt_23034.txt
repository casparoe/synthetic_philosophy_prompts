I'm a novelist, and in my current book a character must decide whether to have a child — a decision I want to portray as genuinely rational rather than as a leap of faith. I've come across the philosophical notion of transformative experience and the claim that such a decision is in principle undecidable, on the grounds that having a child is personally transformative: the person you become may no longer share the values you now have, so you cannot evaluate the outcome from the perspective of the one who will live it. Write an essay on whether it is nonetheless possible to make a rational decision about a personally transformative experience. Weigh the argument that such experiences defeat rational choice against the replies that one can still reason decision-theoretically by forming a probability distribution over how the transformed self will value the outcome, or that one should simply decide by what one currently values about the kind of life in question, and distinguish the claim that one cannot predict the experience from the stronger claim that one cannot decide about it. Reach a conclusion and justify it — I need to know, for my character, whether the rational course is to decide, to defer, or to accept that no decision is available.
