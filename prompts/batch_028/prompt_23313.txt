I'm writing a novel in which two working philosophers argue late into the night about the special composition question, and I need the exchange to be philosophically authentic rather than a strawman on either side. Write a dialogue between a mereological universalist and a mereological nihilist, each pressing the other's view against the problem of the many — the universalist arguing that the problem of the many is, if anything, a confirmation of universalism (there are simply many overlapping objects, and that's unproblematic), the nihilist arguing that it shows the idea of a determinate composite object is incoherent (there is no fact of the matter which collection of atoms is "the cat," so there is no cat at all, only simples). Have them respond to each other's actual moves, and let the dialogue make clear where their disagreement is substantive and where it might dissolve into a mere difference about how we carve up the ontology.
