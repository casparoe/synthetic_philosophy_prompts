I'm a retired statistical physicist and I've only recently gotten into philosophy, so bore with me. I've just been asked to sit on the grant review panel for a small foundation that funds work at the physics-philosophy boundary, and one of the proposals this round is about the Boltzmann brain problem. The author's central claim is that the problem — a model which produces Boltzmann brains in greater number than ordinary observers seems to predict that we should be Boltzmann brains, and we're not — is not actually a problem for eternal inflation but for the self-sampling assumption, and that it dissolves if you switch to a self-indication assumption instead. About half the panel, mostly working physicists, thinks this is unfalsifiable anthropic hand waving and wants to reject it outright; the other half, mostly philosophers of probability, thinks the self-locating part is a live debate and that an outright rejection would be an abdication. Please draft the panel's decision memo — fund, reject, or fund with conditions — with a bottom line we can actually vote on, and an honest treatment of the strongest case on the losing side. I'd like the memo to really engage with whether the move from self-sampling to self-indication dissolves the problem or just relocates it, because I think that's where the disagreement turns and I don't want the memo to just restate what both sides already believe.
