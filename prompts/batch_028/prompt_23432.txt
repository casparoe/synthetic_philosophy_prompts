I work on recommendation systems and online A/B testing, and I keep hitting a pattern where users appear to copy the crowd instead of acting on their own feedback, so I've been reading the informational-cascade literature for a side project on whether my ranking features are just amplifying herding. Next month I'm doing a podcast interview with a behavioral economist who has spent her career defending the rational-herding / informational-cascade model as a genuine explanation of real-world conformity — fads, technology adoption, market panics — rather than as a mere boundedly-rational bias, and I want to go well past the "yes, people follow the crowd" level and press on the parts of the machinery I think are doing all the work.

For context, as I understand the canonical setup (the 1992 Bikhchandani–Hirshleifer–Welch model and its descendants): a sequence of agents each receives a private, noisy signal about some binary state, observes the actions of everyone before them, and acts optimally given a common prior; at some point the public information accumulated in the observed actions outweighs any new private signal, so the agent rationally ignores their own signal and a cascade sets in. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that turn on the actual structure of this model, each paired with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. I specifically want at least one question each on: (a) the unraveling argument — if agents were free to delay their decision in order to observe more predecessors, the cascade should not form, so the model seems to depend on a fixed, exogenous order of decision; how does she reconcile that with real settings where waiting is always an option; (b) fragility and identification — because a cascade can be seeded by a single early coincidence, how do you distinguish a genuine informational cascade from a spurious one when you only observe actions and never the underlying signals; (c) the independence assumption on private signals, and how correlated signals (everyone reading the same review) change the analysis; and (d) the empirical distinction between informational cascades and reputational cascades, given that both produce identical behavior. Please make the follow-ups anticipate the standard defense she'll reach for and push on it.
