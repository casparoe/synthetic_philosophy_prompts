In the causation literature, interventionist accounts (Woodward, Pearl) are sometimes presented as just a refinement of Lewis's counterfactual theory, yet Woodward insists the two frameworks come apart in important ways. I'd like a precise explanation of what the "surgical" or "modular" character of interventions is doing that Lewis's similarity-ranking over possible worlds can't capture on its own. In particular, could you unpack how this difference plays out in cases of late preemption, where counterfactual dependence between cause and effect fails even though there's an evident causal relationship—where exactly does the interventionist apparatus gain traction that pure counterfactual semantics lacks?
