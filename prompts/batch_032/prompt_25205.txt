(1) I'm a moral realist — there are mind-independent moral facts, and when we say "torturing cats is wrong" we're asserting something that's true or false independent of anyone's attitudes or cultural conventions.

(2) I think evolutionary debunking arguments basically succeed: our moral intuitions were shaped by selection pressures that had nothing to do with tracking moral truth, so those intuitions are unreliable as guides to the moral facts. The influence runs through fitness, not truth.

(3) Despite (2), I think reflective equilibrium is the right method for moral epistemology. We start with our considered moral judgments, try to systematize them under principles, and revise both judgments and principles until they cohere. I don't see a better alternative on offer.

(4) I think moral knowledge is possible. I'm not a skeptic. The debunking challenge is serious but I'm convinced it's answerable.

(5) I also think the debunking argument is neutral between first-order normative theories. It doesn't give you a reason to be a utilitarian rather than a Kantian, or vice versa — it targets all our intuitions equally, so it can't be wheeled in to favor one side in normative debates.
