A buddy of mine was telling me about this ancient Greek argument that death isn't actually bad for you. His summary went something like this:

"Epicurus had this argument that you shouldn't fear death because when you're alive, death isn't here, and when death arrives, you're not here to experience it. So death can't harm you — harm requires a subject to experience the harm. It's basically the same as before you were born: you didn't exist then and it didn't bother you, so why would not existing after you die be any different? The only thing that can be bad is the pain of dying itself, but once you're dead there's literally no one there to suffer, so being dead is a non-issue. People who say death deprives you of future goods are just confused because you can't be deprived of something when you don't exist."

I'm a software engineer so this isn't my wheelhouse, but something about the "it's just like before you were born" move feels like its glossing over an asymmetry — like, we care about future losses differently than past ones.
