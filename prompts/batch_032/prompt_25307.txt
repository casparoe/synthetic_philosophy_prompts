I'm on my university's IACUC and we're stuck on a protocol thats been kicking around for two meetings now. A comparative cognition lab wants to run a problem-solving study across rats, cleaner wrasses, and octopuses — the animals have to figure out a puzzle box to get food, standard operant stuff. The rat arm is fine, nobody blinked at that. But for the fish and the octopuses the protocol involves a week of food deprivation to motivate them, and then euthanasia at the end for brain histology. And here's where we split right down the middle. Three members say octopuses are clearly sentient — distributed nervous system, play behavior, individual recognition of handlers, the fact that several countries already regulate them like vertebrates for research — so they should get the full rat treatment: anesthesia for anything invasive, enrichment requirements, and maybe we should be asking whether the deprivation is even justifiable regardless of what we do at the end. The other three say this is mission creep and they're not wrong that the logic is slippery: if we start extending vertebrate-level protections based on behavioral markers of sentience, then cleaner wrasses — which are literally in the same protocol — have shown some cognitive capacities in recent work that at least raise the question, and then where do we stop? Bees? Drosophila? One of them muttered "a neuron is a neuron, but a rat is a rat," which I think was meant as a joke but nobody laughed and it kind of hung in the air. The chair is frustrated and wants a decision memo from me — I'm the swing vote — that recommends approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions, with reasoning that actually engages both positions instead of just picking a side and dressing it up. I've got till Thursday.
