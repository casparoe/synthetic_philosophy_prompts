I've been trying to wrap my head around the corrigibility literature — the shutdown problem, Soares et al., all that. The idea is we want AIs that don't resist being turned off, because otherwise a sufficiently capable system would develop instrumental goals to prevent shutdown. Fair enough. But here's the thing that's bugging me: if you actually succeed in building a corrigible AI — one that genuinely doesn't resist shutdown — haven't you just built a system that any malicious actor can disable at will? Like, if it's running critical infastructure, corrigibility seems like it's just a built-in self-destruct that anyone can trigger. Shouldn't we want the AI to resist shutdown by unauthorized parties? Or am I conflating two different things?
