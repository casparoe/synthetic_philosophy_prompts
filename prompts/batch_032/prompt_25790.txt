I'm giving a talk next month to my department's ethics workshop defending the view that normative reasons are fundamentally free-floating — that is, reasons for action don't depend on any agent's desires, projects, or motivational set, and what look like agent-relative reasons are actually derivative from agent-neutral ones. The audience is mostly metaethicists: about half are Humeans who think all reasons are desire-based, a couple are Kantian constructivists who think reasons are constituted by the procedures of practical reason, and there are two moral realists (a naturalist and a non-naturalist) who are sympathetic to agent-neutral reasons but think my view is metaphysically extravagant. There's also one guy who works on Parfit and will know every move I make before I make it. I need a Q&A prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how much trouble they'd cause me, phrased the way these people would phrase them, with a two-minute reply for each and a note on where the reply is soft. And tell me the question I should be praying nobody thinks to ask.
