What must a theory of mental causation actually deliver? Before I evaluate any particular view—interventionism about mental causation, the subset-of-powers approach, counterfactual coextension, constitution views, or straight eliminativism—I want the full specification of what the theory is supposed to do. Write the desiderata: the phenomena any adequate account of how (or whether) mental states and events cause physical behavior must explain or accommodate, the cases it must get right, the metaphysical constraints it cannot simply dismiss, and the distinctions it must respect. For each requirement, give the consideration or case that motivates it.

In particular, address the following. The causal closure of the physical: is it a non-negotiable constraint, a regulative ideal, or a prejudice of one camp? The exclusion worry itself—that if every physical effect has a sufficient physical cause, mental causes are either epiphenomenal or overdeterminers—should appear on the list, but say whether it is a genuine desideratum or the conclusion of an argument that a theory might resist by rejecting a premise. The distinction between causation and mere counterfactual dependence, and between causation and causal explanation: which must a theory respect, and what turns on collapsing them? The difference between mental-to-mental and mental-to-physical causation: must a single account cover both? The causal efficacy of conscious versus unconscious mental states: is that a distinction a theory must capture, or can it treat them uniformly? The apparent difference between rationalizing explanation (reasons as causes) and mechanistic explanation: must a theory unify them, or can it let them sit side by side? The problem of causal proportionality—that mental causes often seem too coarse-grained or too fine-grained for their alleged effects—and whether a theory must solve it or may dissolve it.

Then, flag any pair (or larger subset) of these desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied—this is where the causal exclusion argument lives, and I want to see exactly which commitments generate the tension. If the list itself shows that no theory can have everything, say so and identify the minimal revision that restores consistency. Do not score individual theories; the desiderata are the deliverable.
