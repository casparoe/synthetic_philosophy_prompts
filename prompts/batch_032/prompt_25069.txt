Quine's "Two Dogmas" argues there's no principled way to draw the analytic-synthetic distinction and that our beliefs form a holistic web where any statement could in principle be revised. But if that's right, doesn't it mean we can never really tell whether we're having a factual disagreement or just using words differently? Like, if I say whales are mammals and someone says no, whales are fish — traditionally we'd say one person's wrong about biology and the other's maybe using a weird definition. But if there's no fact of the matter about which parts of our web are definitional vs. empirical, then how do we ever diagnose what kind of dispute we're having? It seems like Quine's view turns all disagreements into a mush where you can't seperate semantic from factual issues, which would make rational debate impossible. What am I missing?
