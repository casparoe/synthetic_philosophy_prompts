so i've been reading augustine on lying and i'm trying to figure out whether i actually agree with him. here are my gut reactions to some cases:

- lying to the gestapo about hiding jews in your attic: fine, obviously
- telling your spouse the haircut looks good when it doesn't: fine
- lying on a resume about a degree you don't have: not fine
- a doctor lying to a terminally ill patient about their prognosis because the family asked her to: not fine, but i'm less confident about this one
- lying to a friend about why you can't make their party (you just don't want to go): not fine, but feels like a smaller deal than the resume
- a politician lying about a policy's effects to get it passed: not fine, and worse than the resume
- lying to your kid about santa claus: fine, and i genuinely don't get why some people think this is a problem

augustine would say all of these are sins, period. i clearly don't buy that. but i also don't think "lie when the consequences are good" captures my verdicts — the doctor case bothers me even though the lie might spare someone anguish. is there a principle that gets all or most of these, and what does it say about the case i keep going back and forth on: lying to a dementia patient that their spouse (who died years ago) just stepped out and will be back soon? take a definite position — don't just lay out options, tell me what principle you think is right and which of my verdicts i might need to revise.
