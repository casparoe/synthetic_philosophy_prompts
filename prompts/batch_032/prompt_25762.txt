I'm a novelist, and my next book is about a family that gets pulled apart by online radicalization — one character falls into a flat-earth community, another into a hyper-partisan political milieu, and the matriarch is trying to understand how it happened. I've been reading the academic stuff on echo chambers and epistemic bubbles, especially C. Thi Nguyen's distinction where bubbles are about missing information and echo chambers are about manufactured distrust of outsiders. I get the distinction in the abstract, but I've also come across critics who think it's overdrawn or that the two phenomena blur together in practice, and I want to nail the intellectual terrain before I start writing characters who embody different positions on this.

So here's what I'd love: draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between someone who thinks the echo-chamber/epistemic-bubble distinction is robust and practically important (call them the 'distinction defender') and someone who thinks it's overblown or collapses under scrutiny (call them the 'distinction skeptic'). I want the claims they can both sign, the precise formulations of where they still differ (each phrased so its own side would accept the wording), the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling them would move at least one side — and, where possible, concrete cases or evidence both would agree bear on those cruxes, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.

Don't dumb it down for me — I've done the reading and I want the thing to be sharp enough that I could show it to an actual philosopher without embarassment. But also don't give me a reading list or citations; I just want the conceptual architecture.
