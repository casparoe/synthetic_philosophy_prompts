I recently joined my employer's benefits policy committee, and we are debating whether to switch our retirement savings plan from opt-in enrollment (employees must actively sign up) to automatic enrollment with an opt-out (employees are enrolled at a default contribution rate and must actively decline). The case for switching seems strong: it increases participation dramatically, it preserves choice because anyone can opt out, and it exploits nothing more than the well-documented human tendency to stick with defaults. But I have noticed something that bothers me. The same committee recently rejected a proposal to automatically schedule employees for annual health screenings unless they opt out, on the grounds that medical decisions require affirmative consent and that defaulting people into health procedures would be manipulative. I cannot see what principle distinguishes the two cases, and I suspect the practice of automatic retirement enrollment carries philosophical commitments about autonomy and legitimate influence that my colleagues have not made explicit — and that, if made explicit, might force us to either abandon the retirement nudge or accept the health-screening nudge.

Please help me think this through. I would like two deliverables.

First, a prose analysis of roughly 500 words that reconstructs the implicit theory of autonomy, welfare, and legitimate paternalism that automatic retirement enrollment presupposes, identifies the most plausible principles that could distinguish it from automatic health-screening enrollment, and assesses whether those principles hold up under scrutiny.

Second, a structured summary as a JSON object with the following fields:

- "presuppositions": an array of strings, each stating a claim about autonomy, welfare, rationality, or legitimate influence that the practice of automatic retirement enrollment cannot do without.
- "candidate_distinguishing_principles": an array of objects, each with fields "principle" (a principle that would license the retirement default but not the health-screening default), "plausibility" (one of "high", "moderate", or "low"), and "strongest_objection" (a string stating the strongest objection to that principle).
- "internal_tensions": an array of objects, each with fields "tension" (a description of an apparent inconsistency within the retirement-enrollment practice itself, independent of the health-screening comparison) and "resolution" (the most plausible way to resolve it, or null if none is available).
- "verdict": a string, one of "the asymmetry is defensible", "the asymmetry is indefensible", or "the asymmetry is defensible but only under a revisionary view of autonomy".
- "practical_implication": a string stating what the committee should do if it wants to be philosophically consistent.
