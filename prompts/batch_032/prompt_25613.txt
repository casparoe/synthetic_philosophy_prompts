I'm a PhD student in aesthetics, and I've been working through the literature on aesthetic testimony for the better part of a semester. The central question is whether testimony can transmit aesthetic knowledge — or, more modestly, justified aesthetic belief — and the field is split between pessimists who deny this and optimists who affirm it. But the more I read, the more I suspect that the two sides are not always answering the same question. Pessimists often seem to be arguing that testimony cannot replace or substitute for first-hand aesthetic experience, which seems right; optimists often seem to be arguing that testimony can provide some genuine epistemic traction — that I can come to know something about a work's aesthetic character from a reliable critic, even if that knowledge is incomplete or inferentially limited — which also seems right. The dispute then looks less like a direct clash and more like a disagreement about what the phenomenon to be explained actually is.

Before I try to stake out a position, I want to reverse the order of operations: instead of starting with a theory and seeing what it can handle, I want to start with the constraints any adequate theory must satisfy and then see which theories survive. So I'm asking for the desiderata. What must a theory of aesthetic testimony get right? I have a preliminary list in mind — explain the apparent asymmetry with ordinary empirical testimony, accommodate the fact that we do in practice rely on aesthetic testimony (reviews, recommendations, criticism), respect the role of first-person experience without simply stipulating that experience is necessary, and clarify whether the barrier is epistemic or merely pragmatic — but I want to know whether I'm missing constraints, whether some of mine are really just parochial intuitions rather than genuine requirements, and, most importantly, whether any subset of the genuine constraints are jointly unsatisfiable. If you can briefly indicate where pessimism, optimism, and the main hybrid views stumble against the list, that would be helpful, but the desiderata themselves are what I need.
