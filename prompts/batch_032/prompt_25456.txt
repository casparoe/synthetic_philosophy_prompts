I've been trying to figure out what I think about karma — not the rebirth stuff, just the core idea that the universe is set up so people eventually get what they deserve. Here are my gut reactions to some cases:

- A cruel boss makes his employees miserable for years, then his own company fails and he ends up working a low-wage job: this feels like karma, like the universe balanced the books.
- Same cruel boss, but instead his company fails because a tornado destroys his factory: this feels different — still bad things happening to a bad person, but it doesn't feel like karma, more like random bad luck that happened to land on him.
- A kind person volunteers at a shelter for years, then a stranger randomly pays for their groceries: feels karmic.
- A kind person volunteers at a shelter for years, then finds a $20 bill on the ground: doesn't feel karmic in the same way, even though it's also a good thing happening to a good person. Something about the connection to their kindness matters.
- Someone does something terrible, feels genuine remorse, and then something bad happens to them: I'm conflicted. Part of me feels the remorse should "count" and the bad thing is now undeserved. Another part feels the books still need balancing.
- Someone does something terrible, never feels remorse, and prospers: this is the one that really bothers me. It feels like a cosmic error.

Is there a coherent theory of karma (as moral desert, not as rebirth) that captures these distinctions? What does it say about the case I can't settle: someone who inherited wealth that was originally obtained through terrible acts, but they themselves are a decent person who uses the money for good?
