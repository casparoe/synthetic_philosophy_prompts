I'm preparing a paper on vagueness and would appreciate an audit of my current commitments before I write myself into a corner. Here is where I stand:

(1) Classical logic is correct for reasoning with vague language. In particular, bivalence holds: for any predicate and any object, either the predicate applies or it does not.

(2) There is a sharp boundary in any sorites series. For a standard sorites on "heap," there is some particular grain whose removal turns a heap into a non-heap. We cannot know where this boundary lies, but it exists.

(3) The sorites paradox is genuinely puzzling, and an adequate account of vagueness must explain why we find the inductive premise so compelling despite its falsity. It is not enough simply to declare one premise false and leave it at that.

(4) Vague predicates admit of borderline cases, and borderline cases are semantically distinctive: they are not merely cases where we do not know whether the predicate applies, but cases where the predicate's meaning fails to settle the question either way.

(5) Higher-order vagueness is real. Just as there is no sharp boundary between the heaps and the non-heaps that we can identify, there is no sharp boundary between the clear heaps and the borderline heaps, nor between the borderline heaps and the clear non-heaps.

(6) The meaning of a vague predicate is determined by our linguistic dispositions—patterns of use, including our dispositions to apply and withhold the predicate, to tolerate small changes, and to defer to others. Nothing beyond these facts fixes what a vague predicate means.

I suspect these do not all sit comfortably together, but I am not sure which pairs are the real problems and which only appear to be. Please go through them and identify the tensions, ordering them by severity. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give to resolve it and what the philosophical cost of that revision would be. Also flag any apparent tensions that dissolve on closer inspection. Finally, please indicate how confident you are in each of your assessments and single out the point you are least sure about.
