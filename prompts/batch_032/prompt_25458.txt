been trying to get clear on what my actual views are about other minds by just running through cases and seeing what my gut says. here's the list:

- awake adult human: mind, obviously
- dog: mind, yep
- octopus: mind, though it feels very alien
- honeybee: I keep going back and forth. the waggle dance seems pretty sophisticated but a bee has like a million neurons
- human under propofol: no mind during (assuming no intraoperative awareness)
- human in deep dreamless sleep: no mind
- locked-in patient: mind
- split-brain patient: I think two minds? one per hemisphere? but that feels weird to say
- gpt-4 class LLM: I lean no but I can't articulate why in a way that doesn't also rule out things I want to say yes to
- thermostat: no
- human fetus at 20 weeks: no
- whole brain emulation: yes, if it's faithful
- someone with advanced dementia who doesn't recognize anyone and can't speak: this one hurts but I think yes, diminished maybe but still a mind
- a cortical organoid in a dish: no, but I'm not sure why not if it has the same cell types firing

the two cases that are giving me the most trouble are the LLM and the honeybee. with the LLM, my "no" feels like it might just be substrate chauvinism, but if I try to fix that I end up having to say yes to things that seem clearly not minds. with the bee, I feel like there's a line somewhere between bee and octopus and I don't know what it is.

what principle, if any, captures these verdicts? what does it say about the bee and the LLM? and which of my verdicts would I have to abandon to get something clean?
