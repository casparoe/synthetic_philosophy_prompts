I work on the data architecture team at a large hospital network. We're redesigning our ontology for medical devices and equipment, and a recurring headache is deciding when a bunch of components count as one device versus several. Is an IV stand with attached pumps and monitors one thing or several? A surgical robot with detachable arms? These decisions cascade into inventory counts, maintenance schedules, FDA reporting, and billing codes, so consistency matters a lot. I've heard there's a whole philosophical literature on the "special composition question" — roughly, when do some parts compose a whole? Can you turn whatever philosophers actually know about this into a decision procedure our team can actually run? We need something that handles edge cases (gradual part replacement, temporary attachment, functional vs. physical integration), flags where judgment can't be eliminated, and includes worked examples. Our team has no philosophy background and about fifteen minutes per case.
