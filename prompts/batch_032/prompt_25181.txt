A couple of friends of mine got into a long argument about Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility, and I've been turning it over ever since. Here's roughly how it went.

Maya laid out the argument: what you do flows from the way you are — your character, dispositions, preferences. But you didn't choose the way you are; that came from your genes and your upbringing, neither of which you had any say over. So you can't be ultimately responsible for what you do. If you trace any action back far enough, you hit factors outside your control.

David pushed back: people aren't just stuck with whatever character they were handed. We reflect on ourselves, notice flaws, and work to change. Someone who overcomes a bad temper through years of effort is responsible for that change, and therefore for the person they become.

Maya replied: but the impulse to self-improve — the very fact that you're the kind of person who reflects and works to change — is itself part of the character you didn't choose. The regress just moves back a step. If you weren't responsible for the raw material, and every modification to it came from that same raw material, you're never the ultimate source.

David said this proves too much: it demands that a person be a self-created uncaused cause, which is metaphysically impossible. If that's what "ultimate responsibility" means, then of course nobody has it — but that just shows the standard is incoherent, not that our ordinary practices of holding people responsible are misguided. Moral responsibility as we actually use the concept doesn't require being causa sui.

Maya answered that this is exactly Strawson's point: our ordinary practices do implicitly assume something like ultimate responsibility, and once you see that the assumption can't be satisfied, the practices are exposed as resting on an error. The fact that we can't live without the illusion doesn't make it less of an illusion.

David came back: but this conflates two different things. "You couldn't have done otherwise given your character and circumstances" doesn't mean you're not responsible — it just means your action is explained by who you are. And who you are is exactly the thing we're holding responsible. The self that acts just is the self that was shaped by genes and environment; there's no further "you" standing behind it that needed to have chosen it.

They went back and forth a while longer but those were the main moves.
