So I'm in this ethics seminar on procreation and population ethics, and last week we read Benatar's *Better Never to Have Been* and a couple response papers. The discussion got pretty heated — there's this one guy in class who kept coming back to the phrase "better never to have been" like it was a conversation-ender. Every time someone raised an objection to the asymmetry argument or pointed out that most people seem glad to exist, he'd just shrug and say "but that's the whole point — better never to have been." The professor tried to get him to unpack what exactly he meant by that sentence, and he sort of mumbled something about "the conclusion of the book" and moved on.

But the more I've thought about it since, the more I realize that little phrase could be doing a bunch of different philosophical jobs, and they're not all equivalent. It could mean: for any particular person, it would have been better *for them* if they'd never existed (a claim about individual welfare). Or it could mean: the world overall would be a better place if no sentient beings ever came into existence (an impersonal claim). Or: coming into existence is always a harm, but maybe a harm that can be outweighed by other considerations? Or: procreation is always morally wrong, full stop. And I'm pretty sure some of these readings are way more defensible than others, and some of them don't even follow from the asymmetry argument even if you grant the asymmetry.

Can you lay out the distinct precise claims that "better never to have been" might be expressing — from the narrowest reading (something about individual welfare comparisons) to the broadest (something about the impermissibility of all procreation, or even a duty to bring about extinction) — and say for each one: (a) whether it's actually supported by the asymmetry argument, (b) whether it's independently plausible, and (c) which reading my classmate was probably leaning on when he used it to shut down discussion? I want to come to next session with a clearer sense of what I'd actually be conceding if I granted the slogan.
