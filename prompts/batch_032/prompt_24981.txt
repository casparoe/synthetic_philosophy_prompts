I'm a PhD student in metaphysics and I've been going in circles on something that I feel like must have a name but I can't seem to find the right search term for it. Here's the basic worry. Take any ordinary fact — like this mug being ceramic. You've got the mug, and you've got the property of being ceramic. The fact is supposed to be these two things standing in some kind of combination. But what actually does the combining? It seems like you need a relation — call it instantiation — that holds between the mug and the property. But now instead of two things you've got three: mug, ceramicness, and the instantiation relation. And these three need to be combined into a unified fact just as much as the original two did. So you posit another relation to bind the mug, ceramicness, and instantiation together. And now you've got four things that need binding, and so on forever.

I know this is in the neighborhood of what people call "the unity of the proposition" problem — how do the parts of a proposition combine into something truth-evaluable rather than just a list? And I've seen it gestured at in discussions of Frege's concept/object distinction, where concepts are supposed to be "unsaturated" and somehow don't need an extra relation to combine with objects. But I'm not sure whether the specific regress I'm describing — the one about facts and instantiation rather than propositions — is treated as the same problem or a distinct one. Does this regress have its own name? And more importantly, what are the main ways people respond to it? I'm not just looking for a label — I want to understand whether this is generally considered a vicious regress or a benign one, and what the standard escape routes are (deny that instantiation is a real relation? say the regress is harmless? something about facts being primitive unities?). If theres a standard taxonomy of positions here I'd love to know what it looks like.
