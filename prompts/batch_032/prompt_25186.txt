I've been working through the "equality of what?" literature — Sen, Dworkin, Arneson, Cohen, Nussbaum, Anderson — and I keep noticing a dialectical structure that recurs across these debates but that I haven't seen given a name of its own. It runs roughly as follows.

Someone proposes equalizing some currency C₁ — say, income. An objector points out that people differ in how much of C₁ they need to achieve the same functioning: a person with a disability requires more income to reach the same level of mobility as an able-bodied person. So equal C₁ does not capture what we actually care about. The proponent concedes and shifts to C₂, which adjusts C₁ for differential needs — perhaps a resource bundle indexed to personal characteristics, or "real income" in some sense. A second objector then points out that people differ in how efficiently they can convert C₂ into well-being: a constitutionally cheerful person gets more welfare from the same resource bundle than a melancholic one. So equal C₂ still misses something important. The proponent shifts to C₃, which is well-being or welfare itself. But now a third objector notes that equalizing welfare would require transferring resources from people with modest tastes to people with expensive ones, which strikes many as unfair. The proponent may retreat to equalizing opportunities for welfare rather than welfare itself (C₄), or add a responsibility-sensitive condition, or loop back to resources with a different rationale — and the cycle threatens to continue.

I am not asking for an explanation of the positions. I understand that Sen, Dworkin, Arneson, Cohen, and others each occupy a point in this space. What I am asking about is the structure of the dialectic itself: the way each proposed currency is vulnerable to a distinctive kind of counterexample that pushes the proposal one step further along a chain, and the way the chain seems to cycle or dead-end rather than converge on a stable endpoint. Is there an established term for this specific iterative structure — not "the equality-of-what debate" as a topic area, but the argumentative regress or cycle itself? If there is no established term, what would you call it, and how would you distinguish it from a garden-variety skeptical regress, a Sorites sequence, or the sort of objection-and-refinement spiral familiar from other areas of philosophy?
