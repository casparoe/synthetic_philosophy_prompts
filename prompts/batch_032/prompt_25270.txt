I'm a novelist, and about two years ago I fell down a rabbit hole reading philosophy of perception — it started because I was trying to write a character whose grief literally changes how she sees the world, and I wanted to know if that's even a thing that happens. That's how I found the cognitive penetration debate: the question of whether beliefs, desires, moods, and so on can actually alter perceptual experience itself, or whether they only affect how we interpret and judge what we perceive.

Here's what's driving me nuts. I've now read a decent chunk of this literature — Fodor, Pylyshyn, Churchland, Siegel, Macpherson, Stokes — and it feels like the debate has been spinning its wheels for thirty years. The modularity people say perception is encapsulated from cognition and point to things like the Müller-Lyer illusion (you know the lines are the same length but you still see them as different, so belief doesn't penetrate perception). The penetration people say that's cherry-picked and point to cases like the banana looking yellower when you think its a banana, or hills looking steeper when you're wearing a heavy backpack. Then the modularity people say those aren't really perceptual changes, they're post-perceptual judgment effects or attention effects. Then the penetration people say the modularity people have defined "perception" so narrowly that penetration is ruled out by definitional fiat. And around we go.

I'm starting to suspect this isn't really an empirical disagreement at all — or at least not primarily. It feels like a disagreement about where to draw the line between perception and cognition, and that line might just be a stipulation. But I'm not a philosopher and I don't trust my diagnosis. So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it a verbal dispute about the word "perception"? Is there a hidden empirical question that would settle it if we could run the right experiment? Are the two sides answering different questions under the same heading? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that would actually count as progress, or should I just go back to writing my novel and stop worrying about whether the science backs up my metaphors?
