So my roommate and his girlfriend got into this argument last weekend that's been living rent-free in my head ever since. They were talking about the Sleeping Beauty problem — you know, the one where Beauty gets put to sleep Sunday, a fair coin is flipped, and if it's heads she's woken up Monday only, but if tails she's woken Monday *and* Tuesday with a memory wipe in between so she never knows what day it is. The question is: when Beauty wakes up, what should her credence be that the coin came up heads?

My roommate (he's a stats grad student) said obviously one-half. The coin was fair, Beauty knew it was fair when she went to sleep, and when she wakes up she hasn't learned anything new — she knew in advance she'd be woken up at least once regardless of the coin. So her credence shouldn't move. His girlfriend (she's in a philosophy PhD) said no, it's clearly one-third. When Beauty wakes up, she knows she's in one of three indistinguishable awakening events — Monday-heads, Monday-tails, or Tuesday-tails — and since she has no way to tell which, she should assign equal probability to each, so P(heads) = 1/3.

He came back with: but those three aren't exclusive in the right way. If the coin is tails, she gets *two* awakenings, not one — so you can't just count awakenings like they're separate possible worlds. She said that's exactly the point: the number of awakenings *is* the evidence. The fact that she's awake right now is something that happens twice as often under tails as under heads, so it's a 2:1 likelihood ratio against heads. He said that's circular — you're assuming that being awake *right now* is a random draw from the set of all awakenings, but that's exactly what's in dispute. She said no, it's just the principle of indifference applied to centered possible worlds. He said the principle of indifference is famously broken and this is just another case where it gives the wrong answer.

At this point I was lost. She then tried a different angle: suppose we run the experiment a hundred times, and Beauty bets a dollar on heads every time she wakes up. If she's a halfer, she thinks the odds are even and the bet is fair. But she'll lose money — because in the long run about one-third of her awakenings will be heads-awakenings. He said that's a different question: you're asking about the frequency of *correct guesses per awakening*, but the original question is about her *credence in the coin*, and those come apart because tails-awakenings are correlated with each other. She said if your credence and your betting behavior systematically diverge, something has gone wrong with your epistemology. He said that's only true if you ignore the fact that she bets *twice on the same coin* in the tails world, which a dutch book argument has to account for.

They went back and forth like this for like forty minutes and neither budged. I genuinely can't tell who has the better of it. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right about which specific point, what each is missing, and who's more right overall?
