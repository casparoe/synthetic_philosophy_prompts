My view is: the paradox isn't just a word trick or a confusion about what "know" means. It's a real problem and the only honest way out is to dump the idea that knowledge is closed under known entailment. Here's what I mean. The paradox goes: I know P, I know that P entails "any evidence against P is misleading," so by closure I know that any evidence against P is misleading, so I can ignore it. But that last step — the closure step — is where the whole thing sneaks in. If I know P, and I know P entails Q, it just doesn't automatically follow that I know Q, not when Q is something weird and self-referential like "any evidence against P is misleading." Closure was never that solid a principle to begin with — there's the lottery paradox, there's the preface paradox, there's all sorts of cases where it breaks. So my argument is that the dogmatism paradox is actually a pretty good argument against epistemic closure, and we should be fine with that.
