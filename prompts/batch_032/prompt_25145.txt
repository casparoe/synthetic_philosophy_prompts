I'm putting together a handout for a political philosophy reading group, and I want to rank the main responses to the leveling-down objection by how much they force you to revise other parts of your view. The idea is: some responses handle the objection cleanly but at the cost of giving up something else you probably cared about (like the intrinsic badness of inequality), while others preserve egalitarianism but at the cost of making it look gerrymandered or implausible.

Rank these: prioritarianism, sufficientarianism, pluralist egalitarianism (equality as one value among others that can be outweighed), telic egalitarianism that bites the bullet on leveling down being good in one respect, deontic egalitarianism (the objection only targets telic views), and the person-affecting response (leveling down doesn't benefit anyone so it isn't better). If you think I'm missing a response that belongs on the list, add it and say why.

For each, give me: what you have to give up to adopt it, how much independent motivation that thing had anyway, and a 1–5 rating of how revisionary the move is (1 = barely changes anything else in your view, 5 = forces a major overhaul).

Output as a JSON array with fields: rank (1 = least revisionary), position_name, what_you_give_up, independent_motivation, revisionary_rating.

Also flag which of these rankings would be genuinly contested by people who know the literature — I don't want to present a contested ranking as if it's the consensus. If the ranking itself depends on substantive commitments that not everyone shares, mark which entries would shift under which assumptions.
