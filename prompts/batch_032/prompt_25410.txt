I just joined the ethics committee at my hospital and honestly I'm drowning. Every meeting we end up in the same fight: someone says withdrawing a ventillator is killing the patient, someone else says no it's letting them die which is totally different, and then a third person says that distinction doesn't even make sense. Meanwhile the family is in the waiting room and we're debating philosophy.

I need a decision procedure I can actually use. Not a treatise — something I can run through in my head during a consult. Here's what I need it to do:

- Tell me whether the doing/allowing distinction actually applies to the case in front of me, or whether it's a red herring.
- When it does apply, tell me what follows — does the distinction make a moral difference here or not?
- Flag the cases where reasonable people can disagree about the classification itself (like, is turning off a pacemaker more like withdrawing a vent or more like an injection?).
- Be honest about where the procedure is just making a value judgment dressed up as analysis.

I want it grounded in the actual philosophical work on this — Rachels, Foot, Quinn, all that — but I don't need citations, I need something that works.

Then please run it on three cases we actually see:
1. Withdrawing a ventilator from a patient who is not brain-dead but has no chance of recovery, at the family's request.
2. A DNR order for a patient with advanced COPD who would likely survive a cardiac arrest but with severely reduced quality of life.
3. Palliative sedation that may depress respiration in a terminal cancer patient — the sedation is intended for pain, but everyone knows it might hasten death.

And tell me where the procedure breaks down or gives an answer I'm not going to like.
