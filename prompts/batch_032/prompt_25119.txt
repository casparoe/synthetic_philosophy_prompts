I'm running a graduate seminar on the Enlightenment, and next week we're staging a mock interview with Rousseau, focused entirely on his Letter to d'Alembert on the Theatre. I need ten interview questions that go beyond having him restate his opposition to a theater in Geneva. I want questions that expose the internal tensions in his argument and force him to take a definite stand rather than retreating to a different strand of his position. For example: he argues both that theater merely reflects existing morals and so cannot improve them, and that theater is dangerous because it can corrupt audiences — but these claims pull in opposite directions. I want a question that makes him confront that tension head-on, plus the follow-up I should ask after his most likely attempt to reconcile them. Similarly: if the actor's profession is corrupting because it involves pretending to be what one is not, doesn't that problem generalize to all social life, which Rousseau himself describes in the Second Discourse as a theater of appearances? And if Geneva's virtue depends on its citizens' active participation in festivals and civic life, what does his argument imply about other forms of passive spectatorship — reading novels, for instance? For each question, draft the most likely reply and then the follow-up that presses past it. The interview should end with a question about what evidence or historical experience could, in principle, have changed his mind.
