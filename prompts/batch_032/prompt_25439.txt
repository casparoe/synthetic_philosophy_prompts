I am preparing a presentation for a philosophy department colloquium on the paradox of deontology. My thesis is that the paradox is genuine and that no extant deontological response fully dissolves it, though I stop short of concluding that deontology must therefore be abandoned — I argue instead that the paradox reveals a structural feature of agent-relative reasons that deontologists should simply own, rather than explain away. The audience will be roughly thirty faculty and graduate students: mostly moral philosophers with a roughly even split between consequentialists and deontologists, plus a handful of epistemologists and one philosopher of physics who wanders into everything. The consequentialists in the room tend to be sophisticated — they know the difference between act- and rule-utilitarianism, they have read Scheffler and Kagan, and they will not be satisfied by replies that merely redescribe the paradox. The deontologists are a mix: some are Kantians who think the paradox rests on a misunderstanding of the Categorical Imperative, some are Ross-style pluralists, and a few are sympathetic to the "self-ownership" or "moral status" tradition (Thomson, Kamm, Quinn). The epistemologists will be listening for structural analogies — Dutch books, accuracy-dominance, that sort of thing. The physicist will ask about rational choice theory.

Please draft my Q&A prep sheet. I want the eight or so objections I am most likely to get from this specific audience, ordered by how damaging they would be if I handle them poorly, not by how likely they are. Phrase each objection the way a member of this audience would actually put it — use their vocabulary, their framing, their dialectical style. For each objection, give me a two-minute reply and a candid note on where the reply is weakest. Include the question I should be hoping nobody asks, and explain why it is the one I should fear. Where relevant, note which objections are likely to come from the consequentialists, which from the deontologists, and which from the epistemologists or the physicist, so I know whom I'm facing when I answer.
