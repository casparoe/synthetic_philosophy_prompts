A colleague in decision theory laid out the following argument at our workshop, and I cannot find the mistake, though the conclusion seems unacceptable.

(1) Jeffrey conditionalization is the correct rule for updating credences when evidence arrives as a shift in your probabilities over some partition {E_i}, rather than as a proposition learned with certainty.

(2) Suppose you undergo an experience that shifts your credence in E from 0.3 to 0.7, without your learning any proposition with certainty. By (1), your new credence in any H should be P_new(H) = P_old(H | E) · 0.7 + P_old(H | ¬E) · 0.3.

(3) But now ask: what justifies your new credence of 0.7 in E? It, too, must be the result of an update on uncertain evidence — otherwise you would have learned something with certainty, contrary to the assumption that all your evidence here is uncertain.

(4) So there must be some finer partition {F_j} such that your shift regarding E is itself the result of a Jeffrey update over the F_j. And that update requires its own rigidity condition: P_old(E | F_j) = P_new(E | F_j) for each j.

(5) Now we face a dilemma. Either this regress of Jeffrey updates bottoms out in an update that is not a Jeffrey update — in which case not all updating is Jeffrey updating, and (1) is false as stated — or the regress is infinite, in which case your posterior credences are never fully determined by any finite procedure.

(6) If the regress is infinite, Jeffrey conditionalization fails to specify a unique posterior, since each step requires a further step to justify the inputs to the previous one. If it bottoms out in standard conditionalization on something learned with certainty, then Jeffrey conditionalization is not a fundamental update rule but a derivative convenience, and the claim that all evidence is fundamentally uncertain collapses.

(7) Therefore, Jeffrey conditionalization cannot serve as a complete and fundamental account of updating on uncertain evidence.

My colleague claims this shows that the very idea of purely uncertain evidence is incoherent. I am not ready to accept that, but I cannot locate the mistake. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
