In my social studies class, we've been discussing climate change and individual responsibility. At a family dinner recently, my uncle made an argument that I haven't been able to stop thinking about. He said that an individual's decision to reduce their carbon footprint — by giving up meat, or avoiding air travel, or biking instead of driving — is like one person deciding to leave a riot. Even if you walk away, the riot continues just the same; your presence or absence made no difference to the outcome. From this, he concluded that personal lifestyle changes are a distraction, and that only systemic change — government regulation, corporate accountability, international treaties — actually matters.

I find this analogy intuitively compelling, but I also sense it might be smuggling something in. I'd like you to audit it thoroughly. What exactly maps onto what between the riot scenario and individual climate action? Where does the analogy break down? Which of those breakdowns matter for my uncle's conclusion, and which are just superficial differences? And once the disanalogies are on the table, does his conclusion — that individual lifestyle changes are pointless — survive, or was the analogy doing more work than it can bear?
