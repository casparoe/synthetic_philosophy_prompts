In my cultural evolution seminar, we have been working through the claim that culture evolves by a genuinely Darwinian process. The core analogy, as presented in the literature, runs as follows: ideas, practices, and artifacts — memes — vary, are selected by human minds and social environments, and are retained through imitation and transmission, just as organisms evolve through variation, selection, and retention of genes. This analogy is used to license a strong conclusion: that we can model cultural change with the same formal tools used in population genetics and evolutionary biology (fitness landscapes, selection coefficients, drift models), and that complex cultural products like legal systems, religions, and technologies are best explained as the cumulative products of blind variation and selective retention rather than intentional design.

I would like you to audit this analogy. First, give me a table of correspondences: what plays the role of the gene, the organism, the phenotype, the environment, the generation, mutation, recombination, and drift? Second, identify where the mapping breaks down. Which disanalogies are merely cosmetic, and which matter for the intended use — namely, the claim that culture is a Darwinian process in a strong enough sense to borrow evolutionary biology's modeling toolkit? Third, tell me whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying more weight than it can bear. If the analogy fails in important respects, is there a better one? Please keep your answer to at most 400 words.
