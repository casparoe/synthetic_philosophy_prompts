My father is retiring next month after forty-two years teaching philosophy, mostly at the same small liberal arts college. His specialty is classical Indian epistemology, and if you ask him what he's contributed, he'll say — with the kind of modesty that makes you unsure whether it's real or performative — that he's spent four decades trying to get Anglophone philosophers to take anekāntavāda seriously. That's the Jain doctrine of many-sidedness: roughly, the idea that reality is too complex to be captured by any single description, that every claim is only true from some standpoint, and that intellectual humility isn't just a virtue but a metaphysical requirement. He's written two books on it, both published by university presses you've heard of but that normal people haven't, and there will be at least a dozen specialists in the room who can quote the saptabhaṅgī from memory.

I'm giving the toast. The room will be about half philosophers — including some pretty eminent people in Indian and comparative philosophy — and half family and non-academic friends who love my dad but have no idea what anekāntavāda is and would glaze over if I started explaining the difference between naya and pramāṇa. The toast needs to work for both groups. More importantly, it needs to actually *practice* what my dad spent his career preaching: I can't just stand up and give a single, unqualified, definitive account of who he is. The toast itself should somehow embody the many-sidedness idea — maybe by presenting him from several incompatible-but-true angles, or by undercutting its own authority in a way that isn't just a cheap rhetorical trick. I want it to be warm and funny and land as a tribute, not as a philosophy lecture. But the philosophers in the room should recognize that the structure of the thing is doing real philosophical work, not just gesturing at it.

Can you write this? About five minutes spoken. And please commit to a specific approach — don't give me three options or hedge about what I "could" do. I want you to actually write the toast you think is best, and just give me that.
