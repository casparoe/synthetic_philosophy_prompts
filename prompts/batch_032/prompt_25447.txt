"Tolstoy defines art as the communication of emotion from artist to audience. The artist feels something, embodies it in a work, and the audience catches the feeling — he calls this 'infection.' And since he thought the best feelings to transmit were religious ones — specifically Christian feelings of brotherhood and universal love — he concluded that most of what we call great art, including his own novels, is bad art, because it only appeals to the upper classes and doesn't transmit morally good feelings to ordinary people. Basically he's saying art is good if it makes you a better person, and if it doesn't do that, it's not really art, or at least not good art."
