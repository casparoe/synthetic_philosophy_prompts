Ok so I'm in this pragmatism reading group and we've been stuck on the Peirce vs James thing about truth for like three weeks. It's gotten personal because I'm realizing I actually lean Jamesean in a way that's affecting how I think about some stuff in my own life (long story, involves a career decision I've been agonizing over and I keep catching myself thinking "well which belief would actually *work* here"). But I also see the force of the Peircean objections and I don't want to just pick a side sloppily.

Here's what I want: draft the joint statement that a committed Peircean and a committed Jamesean could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the nature of truth. I want it structured like this:

First, a numbered list of claims both sides can agree on — not just trivial stuff but substantive common ground about what their shared pragmatism rules out (both agree truth isn't a static correspondence to mind-independent reality in the way a lot of realists think, both agree beliefs are habits of action, both agree the meaning of a concept is tied to its practical effects, etc.).

Then a disagreements table. For each row: the exact point of disagreement phrased neutrally; then how a Peircean would state their own view on it; then how a Jamesean would state theirs — each in wording their own side would actually sign off on, not a caricature.

Then the cruxes: the sub-questions where if you could settle them, at least one side would have to budge. For each crux, give a concrete case or argument both would accept bears on it, and what each side predicts about it.

I suspect some of the disagreement is about whether "the end of inquiry" is even a coherent notion, and some of it is about whether truth can be plural or has to be singular, and maybe some of it is about whether the individual's satisfaction is epistemically relevant or just a distraction. But I want to see if you can find formulations where they actually agree on more than they think, and isolate what's genuinely left after you strip away the talking-past-each-other. I'd rather discover the residual disagreement is smaller and sharper than I thought.
