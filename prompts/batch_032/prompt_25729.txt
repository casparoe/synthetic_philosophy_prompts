I've been running this reading group for like six months now on whether you can separate the art from the artist -- you know, the whole Gauguin / Polanski / Miles Davis problem -- and honestly we've just settled into two camps that talk past each other constantly. One side (call them the autonomists, roughly) holds that moral evaluation and aesthetic evaluation are just different kinds of thing: you can condemn the artist or the work's moral vision and still find it beautiful, formally brilliant, genuinely great as art. The other side (the moralists) thinks that's a cop-out, that moral flaws in a work are internal to it as art -- when a novel asks you to find a rapist charming, or a film makes you root for a sadist, or a song's lyrics ask you to be moved by a sentiment you'd reject in real life, that's not some seperate moral complaint you file alongside the aesthetic one; it's an aesthetic failure of the work itself, because the work is trying to get you to do something and failing.

We're tired of the same moves -- "so you think Lolita is a bad novel?" / "so you'd hang a painting by Hitler in a museum?" -- and we want to try something more constructive. I'd like you to draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between these two camps could produce. It should have: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section, things both sides genuinely accept even if they'd phrase them differently in their own work; (2) a disagreements table where each row is a specific point of dispute, with the autonomist and moralist positions each stated in wording its own side would endorse; (3) for each row, the crux identified -- the sub-question that, if settled, would actually move someone; and (4) for at least the three most important cruxes, a concrete case or kind of evidence both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about what we'd find. Be honest about whether any part of the dispute is just verbal (different uses of "aesthetic" or "good" or "art") and flag that. And if there are cases where both sides would actually make the same judgment about a specific artwork but for different reasons, highlight those -- I suspect those are where the real insight is.
