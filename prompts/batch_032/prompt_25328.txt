I am a PhD student in decision theory, and I have been trying to settle on what I actually think about the following. Consider the standard counterfactual mugging: Omega flips a fair coin. If it lands tails, Omega runs a perfect simulation of you; if the simulation indicates that you would pay $100 upon seeing heads, Omega gives real-you $10,000. If it lands heads, Omega simply presents the situation to you and asks for $100. You see heads. Omega's predictive accuracy is stipulated to be perfect, and you know all of this. I would like your all-things-considered credence that paying the $100 is rationally required. Please do not give me the answer that it depends on one's decision theory — I want a number, and I want to see the decomposition that produces it. In particular, I would like you to assign credences to the correctness of the main candidate decision theories (causal, evidential, functional or updateless, or however you prefer to carve the space), to the claim that rational choice may be sensitive to the payoffs of merely possible but non-actual branches, and to any other sub-claims you take to be load-bearing. For each term, provide a brief justification. Then show how they combine into your overall credence, and identify which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to — that is, which belief, if you were to revise it, would shift your credence about paying the most.
