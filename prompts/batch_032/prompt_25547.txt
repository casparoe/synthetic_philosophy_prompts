Consider Daniel Dennett's three stances — the physical stance, the design stance, and the intentional stance — as strategies for predicting and explaining the behavior of a system. Now consider the following five systems:

(a) A grandmaster-level chess engine (e.g., Stockfish)
(b) A nest-building ant colony
(c) A modern large language model generating text
(d) A thermostat
(e) An adult human engaged in conversation

Please rank these five systems by the extent to which adopting the intentional stance toward them yields predictive or explanatory gains that could not be recovered by adopting only the design stance. In other words, rank them from "the intentional stance adds the least beyond the design stance" to "the intentional stance adds the most beyond the design stance." For each system, provide a brief justification for its placement in the ranking, making reference to Dennett's criteria for when a stance is appropriate — in particular, his claims about the conditions under which one ought to shift upward from a lower stance to a higher one, and about what makes the patterns discerned at each stance real.
