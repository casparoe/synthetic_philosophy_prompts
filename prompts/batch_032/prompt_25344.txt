I'm taking a course on Plato's epistemology, and we've spent the last two weeks on the Meno problem — the puzzle raised at Meno 97a–98a about why knowledge (epistēmē) should be more valuable than true belief (orthē doxa), given that either one will guide action equally well (as Socrates grants with the road-to-Larisa example). Below are six candidate solutions we've discussed in class. Please rank them from most to least convincing, and briefly justify each placement. Lead with your ranking in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.

1. The stability solution: Knowledge is more valuable because, as Socrates' own metaphor suggests, it is tied down by an account of the reason why and is therefore less easily lost, overturned, or forgotten than mere true belief.

2. The credit solution: Knowledge is more valuable because when one knows, one's true belief is creditable to one's own cognitive abilities or intellectual virtues, whereas a merely lucky true belief is not.

3. The counterfactual-robustness solution: Knowledge is more valuable because it supports successful action not just in the actual circumstances but across a wider range of counterfactual scenarios; the knower would still get things right even if the situation were slightly different.

4. The understanding solution: Knowledge is more valuable because it essentially involves grasping the explanation or reason why something is the case, and such understanding is intrinsically valuable over and above getting the right answer.

5. The deflationary solution: The Meno problem is overstated. In many practical contexts, true belief really is just as good as knowledge, and where knowledge does prove more valuable, this is due to pragmatic factors specific to the situation rather than to anything about the nature of knowledge itself.

6. The non-accidentality solution: Knowledge is more valuable because it involves a non-accidental connection to the truth — the belief is safe, sensitive, or otherwise modally connected to the fact believed — and non-accidental success is better than accidental success.
