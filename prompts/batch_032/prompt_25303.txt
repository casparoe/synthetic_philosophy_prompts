I recently finished a graduate seminar on philosophy of mind, and the session on Chalmers's meta-problem of consciousness has unsettled me in a way I didn't anticipate. The hard problem asks why there is something it is like to be us; the meta-problem asks why we think there is a hard problem — why physical systems like us generate the persistent intuition that consciousness resists physical explanation. I find myself caught in a loop: I have the intuition, then I step back and wonder why I have it, and then I wonder whether that stepping-back is itself just more of the same cognitive machinery at work.

Write a villanelle about the meta-problem of consciousness. The two refrains should be, respectively, a line voicing the hard problem (the sense that experience escapes explanation) and a line voicing the meta-problem (the question of why we have that sense). The villanelle form should do philosophical work: the way the refrains alternate, return, and finally meet in the closing couplet should mirror how the two problems circle each other — how the meta-problem neither dissolves the hard problem nor leaves it untouched, and how explaining the intuition might alter what is intuited. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who knows Chalmers's distinction would recognize which line is which and why they end up side by side, and good enough that someone who doesn't would still find it a compelling poem about a mind watching itself think.
