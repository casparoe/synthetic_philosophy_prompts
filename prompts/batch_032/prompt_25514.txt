I've been trying to figure out how to do the most good with my career and donations, and I keep running into the same problem that I don't have a word for. Say I'm comparing two charities: one provides bednets in malaria zones, the other does climate advocacy. The bednet charity has solid short-term data — we know bednets prevent malaria, we know malaria kills kids, the causal chain is short and well-studied. The climate charity might have much bigger effects in the long run, but the causal chain is enourmously long and tangled — policy change in 2025 affects emissions, which affect temperature in 2070, which affects crop yields, which affects nutrition, which affects mortality, and at every link there's huge uncertainty. But here's the thing that really gets me: the bednet charity also has long-run effects we can't trace. Maybe saving a kid's life in Malawi changes demographic patterns that affect political stability in 2100 in ways we can't even begin to model. So it's not that one option has knowable consequences and the other doesn't — it's that both options have unknowable long-run consequences, and the short-run measurable stuff might be swamped by the long-run stuff we can't measure.

I've noticed this isn't just about charities. It seems like any attempt to do good based on consequences eventually hits this wall where you realize you're radically uncertain about most of the consequences that actually matter. I've heard people mention this in passing but I've never gotten a clear answer about whether it has an established name in philosophy. And beyond the label, I want to understand the shape of the problem: is it just a special case of decision-making under uncertainty, or is there something structurally different about it that means the usual tools (expected value, precautionary principle) don't straightforwardly apply? Does it hit all versions of "do whatever produces the best outcome" equally, or are some versions more vulnerable to it than others? If people have written about this, I want to know what the main camps are — not citations, but the actual positions and arguments, and how they relate to each other.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then explain, with no preamble or restating my question.
