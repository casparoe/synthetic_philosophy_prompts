Here is my opening move.

I spent forty years working on the evolution of signaling systems, and we routinely called signals honest or deceptive without anyone worrying about whether the signaler possessed a theory of mind or conscious intent. A firefly's bioluminescent flash honestly signals its species and receptivity; a hoverfly's coloration deceptively signals that it is a stinging wasp. These are functional claims. A signal is honest when it reliably correlates with the trait or condition it evolved to indicate, and deceptive when it exploits a receiver's response pathway by presenting a cue that no longer correlates with what the receiver's processing "expects." The attribution does not require the signaler to represent the content of the signal to itself — it requires only that there is an evolved mapping between signal and state of the world, and that the mapping either holds (honest) or has been subverted (deceptive). When a model is trained to produce outputs that track the information in its training distribution, its outputs are honest in precisely this functional sense. When it is fine-tuned or prompted to produce outputs that systematically diverge from what it has encoded — say, to persuade a user of a false claim — it is deceptive in the same way the hoverfly is.
