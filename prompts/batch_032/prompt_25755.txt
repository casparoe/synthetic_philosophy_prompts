I teach an upper-level seminar on rationality and practical reason — about 16 students, one 75-minute session. I want an in-class activity that makes them feel the force of Parfit's hitchhiker and the problem of rational irrationality before I name either. The core idea: you are stranded and a rescuer will only help if you promise to pay them later, but once rescued you'll have no self-interested reason to pay. If you are transparently rational, the rescuer won't pick you up — so it seems rational to somehow make yourself irrational, or to bind yourself, in advance. But can you do that on the spot?

Design a structured role-play or game where students face a version of this problem with real (small) stakes — something they care about enough that the incentive structure bites. Give me rules, roles, a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened to the philosophical issues: the distinction between act-level and disposition-level rationality, the problem of credible commitment, and whether one can decide to become the kind of person who keeps promises against self-interest. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be frank about which feature of Parfit's original case the activity cannot reproduce.
