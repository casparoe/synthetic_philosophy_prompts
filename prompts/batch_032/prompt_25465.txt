ok so I'm a novelist — literary fiction mostly, but I've done some narrative design work for a mid-size game studio on the side, and I've ended up with this tangle of views about whether fictional characters can be wronged that I can't sort out on my own. I want to know if these hang together or if I'm quietly contradicting myself somewhere. Here's where I've landed:

(1) The characters in my novels don't have moral status. I can make them suffer horribly and I haven't wronged anyone (assuming my readers aren't traumatized etc.). There's no one there to be wronged.

(2) But I can't shake the feeling that there's something genuinely morally objectionable about designing an NPC whose entire raison d'être is to be killed over and over by players for fun — like, the kind of game where you can torture a shopkeeper and they have bespoke pain animations and voice lines. That feels different from just writing a character who dies in chapter three.

(3) The difference can't just be about interactivity, because a choose-your-own-adventure novel where you flip to page 47 to watch the protagonist get mauled doesn't suddenly give the character moral status. So whatever's going on isn't just "the audience has agency."

(4) It also can't be about graphical fidelity or realism — a text-based roguelike where you get detailed prose descriptions of the goblin crying for its children as you stab it would still give me the ick, and a photorealistic cutscene of a death in a linear game might not.

(5) I basically think sentience or consciousness is what grounds moral status, full stop. And video game characters are obviously not sentient. So by my own lights, (2) shouldn't be tracking anything real about the characters themselves.

(6) But I also hold that you don't need the object of your attitudes to be sentient for those attitudes to be morally criticizable. Like, I think it's wrong to take pleasure in hyperrealistic depictions of suffering even if you know for a fact no one actually suffered — the problem is in you, not in some right of the depicted person. So maybe (2) is really about the player's stance, not the NPC's status?

I keep going in circles. I want someone to go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest fix is. Lead with the bottom line in the first sentence — are these consistent or not? — and then walk me through it. No preamble, no restating my own list back at me.
