I am designing a graduate seminar on the cognitive penetration of perception. Before we evaluate the empirical evidence or the arguments for and against it, I want my students to have a clear specification of what a theory in this area is a theory of — what it must explain, what it must be consistent with, and what it must not conflate. Please write exactly five desiderata that any adequate account of cognitive penetration must satisfy. Each desideratum should be stated as a requirement in one or two sentences, followed by a brief explanation of the case, puzzle, or consideration that makes it binding rather than optional. The list should distinguish genuine requirements from the preferences of particular theoretical camps, and it should flag any pair of desiderata that appear jointly difficult to satisfy. Be attentive to the following difficulty: some natural demands one might place on a theory of cognitive penetration — for instance, that it specify which mental states count as cognitive and which as perceptual — may presuppose a sharp perception-cognition distinction that is itself contested in ways that bear on whether penetration is possible at all. Your desiderata should avoid building in such presuppositions without argument, or at minimum flag where they do so. No introduction, no conclusion — just the five numbered items.
