I've noticed something in how people argue about moral issues and I'm wondering if there's a name for it.

So here's the pattern. When people make moral arguments, they seem to switch between two very different kinds of reasoning without noticing they're doing it.

The first kind is what I'd call the rulebook approach. You have general principles — "don't lie," "don't steal," "maximize overall happiness," whatever — and you apply them to the situation. The fact that you personally know the people involved shouldn't matter. If lying is wrong, it's wrong whether your lying to a stranger or to your best friend. This feels clean and fair.

The second kind is what I'd call the relationship approach. Here, the fact that someone is your mother, or your child, or your friend, totally changes what you ought to do. You have obligations to specific people that don't seem to come from general rules. If I have to choose between saving my kid and saving two strangers, the relationship approach says it's not even a hard choice — of course I save my kid. But the rulebook approach struggles with this, because from an impartial standpoint, two lives are worth more than one.

What I keep seeing is that in formal debates (online, in books, whatever), the rulebook approach is treated as the grown-up, rational way to do ethics, and the relationship approach gets dismissed as "just emotions" or "bias." But in actual life, almost everyone uses the relationship approach for the things that matter most to them, and someone who didn't — someone who treated their own mother as just one person among billions — would seem like a monster, not a saint.

So my question is: is there a name for the relationship-based approach as a serious moral theory, not just as a failure of impartiality? I know about things like "moral particularism" and "virtue ethics" but those don't seem to quite capture the idea that relationships themselves generate obligations. Is there a specific term for the view that our duties to specific people we're in relationships with are fundamental, not just derived from general rules plus facts about who we happen to be near?

And if I'm sympathetic to the relationship approach, what's the strongest argument against taking it seriously as a moral framework — the thing that should make me doubt it the most? Also, I'd be curious what assumptions this whole way of thinking depends on, and how things would look if those assumptions were dropped.
