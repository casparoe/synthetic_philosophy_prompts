A philosophy blog I follow recently summarized Susan Wolf's argument about moral saints like this:

"Wolf argues that being a moral saint — someone who devotes their entire life to maximizing the good of others — would actually make you a worse person overall. If you spent all your time feeding the hungry and none of it reading novels, playing music, or developing friendships based on shared interests rather than charitable purposes, you'd be missing out on the very things that make a human life worth living. Her conclusion is that morality is just one value among many, and that there's nothing irrational about choosing to develop your talents or enjoy your hobbies even when you could be doing more good with that time. In fact, she thinks the moral saint is a kind of pathological case — someone so warped by the demands of morality that they've lost touch with what really matters."

I find the argument compelling, but something about the way the blog puts it makes me wonder whether it's capturing Wolf's view fairly. In particular, I'm not sure Wolf would say the moral saint is a "worse person" or "pathological," and I'm not sure her conclusion is really that morality is "just one value among many." Can you go through this summary and tell me what's accurate, what's overstated, and whether the blog's version of the conclusion actually follows from Wolf's argument?
