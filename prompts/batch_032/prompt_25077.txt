I got laid off six months ago — my whole department got replaced by a SaaS product, basically. I've been doing a lot of thinking about what actually makes work meaningful, and I keep going back and forth. I've got a bunch of cases where I have strong-ish gut reactions, and I'm trying to figure out if there's a principle behind them or if I'm just rationalizing.

Here are my verdicts:

1. A carpenter who builds custom furniture by hand, every joint cut and fitted personally. Finds deep meaning in it. (I think: obviously meaningful.)

2. Same carpenter, but now they use a CNC machine for the cutting and joinery. They design the pieces, program the machine, do the assembly and finishing. (I think: still meaningful, maybe 80% as much.)

3. A radiologist whose job now consists almost entirely of reviewing AI-generated diagnoses. The AI flags anomalies and writes draft reports; the radiologist checks them and signs off. (I think: meaning has drained out of this job, even though the work is medically important.)

4. A translator who used to render novels from Japanese into English. Now they mostly do post-editing: a machine translation system produces a draft and they clean it up. (I think: much less meaningful, maybe not meaningful at all.)

5. A chess grandmaster who prepares for tournaments by analyzing positions with a chess engine. The engine does the brute calculation; the human is doing strategic interpretation and selecting which lines to pursue. (I think: still deeply meaningful.)

6. A customer service rep who handles calls the chatbot can't resolve. The chatbot handles 90% of queries; the human gets the weird, angry, complicated ones. (I think: surprisingly meaningful, maybe more than if they handled all the calls, because every call actually needs a human.)

7. A farmer who used to plow with a team of horses, now drives a GPS-guided tractor. (I think: less meaningful than the old way, but not zero — there's still something there.)

8. A software engineer whose job has shifted from writing code to prompting a large language model and debugging its output. (I think: I honestly don't know. It feels different from the translator case but I can't say why.)

Is there a principle that gets all or most of these? And if not, which verdicts should I be most suspicious of? The one I keep going back and forth on is case 8 — I'd especially like to know what your principle says about it.
