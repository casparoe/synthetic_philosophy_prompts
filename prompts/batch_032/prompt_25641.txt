Draft an adversarial-collaboration joint statement between a virtue ethicist and a situationist critic of virtue ethics. The virtue ethicist holds that stable, cross-situationally consistent character traits—honesty, courage, compassion, and the like—are psychologically real and central to moral life. The situationist holds that the empirical record in social psychology (Milgram, Darley and Batson, Isen and Levin, and the broader person-situation literature) shows that behavior is overwhelmingly driven by situational features rather than by robust dispositions of the kind virtue theory requires, and that this undermines the descriptive adequacy of virtue ethics.

The document should include the following.

(1) A numbered list of claims both sides can sign. These should cover what the classic experiments actually show, what they do not show, what virtue ethics does and does not require in the way of behavioral consistency, what each side grants to the other, and any methodological commitments both accept.

(2) A disagreements table. For each remaining point of disagreement, give the precise formulation each side would accept for its own view—not a caricature, but a statement the side's own proponents would endorse after seeing the other side's best case. Disagreements should be substantive, not verbal; where you judge that part of the dispute has been about words (e.g., what counts as a "trait" or what "cross-situational consistency" means), say so explicitly and explain why.

(3) A list of cruxes: sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, identify a kind of empirical finding, argument, or conceptual analysis both sides agree would bear on it, and record what each side predicts that evidence would show.
