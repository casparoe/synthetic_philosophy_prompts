I am a student representative on my university's Committee on Academic Advising, and I am currently enrolled in a seminar on decision theory where we recently discussed L.A. Paul's work on transformative experience. The committee is reviewing a proposal that would require any undergraduate intending to study abroad for a full academic year to have at least one structured, in-person conversation with a student who has returned from the same host program before they can finalize their enrollment. The proposal's author, a philosophy faculty member, argues that a year abroad is an epistemically transformative experience in Paul's sense: it changes what you value and how you see yourself in ways you cannot adequately grasp from the outside, so standard informational materials—course catalogues, housing descriptions, GPA requirements—are insufficient for genuine informed consent. Opponents on the committee counter that the requirement is paternalistic, that students already have access to testimonials and information sessions, and that adding a mandatory step would create an administrative bottleneck during an already pressured advising period.

I need to draft a short written opinion for circulation before next week's vote. It should recommend whether to adopt, reject, or modify the proposal, and it should engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side. Please keep it concise.
