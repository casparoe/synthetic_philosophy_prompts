I'm taking an undergraduate course in philosophy of science, and we've been working through Kuhn. Our professor assigned a chapter from a well-regarded introductory textbook that summarizes Kuhn's account of scientific change in a single paragraph. Here's the summary, as best I can reconstruct it from my notes:

"Kuhn argued that science doesn't progress by steadily accumulating truths. Instead, it alternates between two modes: normal science and revolutionary science. In normal science, researchers work within a paradigm — a shared framework of assumptions, methods, and exemplary problem-solutions that tells them what to work on and how. Normal science is fundamentally conservative: scientists aren't trying to test or challenge the paradigm, they're solving puzzles it sets for them, and results that don't fit are blamed on the scientist, not the theory. Eventually, enough anomalies accumulate to trigger a crisis. A revolutionary period follows, in which a new paradigm competes with the old one. Because paradigms are incommensurable — they carve up the world differently, use different standards of evidence, and even attach different meanings to shared terms — there is no neutral, paradigm-independent way to adjudicate the dispute. Kuhn's controversial conclusion was that theory choice during revolutions is therefore not rationally compelled by the evidence; it's driven by sociological and psychological factors, which is why he's often described as a relativist about scientific knowledge."

I've heard that Kuhn is one of the most frequently misrepresented philosophers, and something about the last couple of sentences feels off to me — it seems to slide from incommensurability to irrationality to relativism very quickly. But the textbook is by a respected scholar and I'm new to this material, so I don't trust my instincts. Could you go through this summary claim by claim and tell me what a careful Kuhnian would accept, what they'd push back on, and where the summary crosses from fair simplification into distortion? In particular, I'd like to know whether Kuhn actually held that theory choice is irrational, and whether "relativist" is a label he accepted or one that was imposed on him. Please keep your answer to around 500 words.
