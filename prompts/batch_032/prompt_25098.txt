I manage a customer support team. Last year we introduced a metric — average resolution time — and tied it to quarterly bonuses. Within two quarters, resolution times dropped sharply, but I began noticing that agents were closing tickets before problems were actually solved, routing difficult cases into procedural dead ends, and subtly discouraging customers from following up. When I raised this, our operations lead argued that the metric is working: it surfaced inefficiencies we can now address with better training. But my instinct is that we have created a new problem rather than solved the original one. I cannot tell whether we picked the wrong metric, whether tying it to compensation is the culprit, or whether any quantified target will eventually be gamed. What is actually going on here, and how should I think about fixing it? Please end with a one-sentence summary of your diagnosis.
