Construct a thought experiment that discriminates among three views of dreaming: (1) the received view that dreams are experiences occurring during sleep; (2) Malcolm's view that dreams are not experiences at all but merely the disposition to tell a story upon waking; and (3) Dennett's cassette theory, on which memory traces are laid down during sleep but the experience of them occurs only upon waking, when the cassette is played back. The case should be one in which the cassette theory handles the subject's dream report more naturally than either of the other two views. Constraints: the scenario must involve only ordinary circumstances — no sleep laboratories, no experimental interventions, no technology beyond what a person has in their bedroom. The discriminating feature must be a structural property of the dream narrative itself (its temporal organization, its coherence, its relationship to waking events, or similar), not a stipulation about underlying neural mechanisms. After presenting the scenario, walk through what each of the three views says about it, explain why the cassette theory gives the best account, and identify which feature of the narrative is doing the discriminating work. Then explain how a defender of the received view would try to accommodate the case and whether that accommodation is ad hoc.
