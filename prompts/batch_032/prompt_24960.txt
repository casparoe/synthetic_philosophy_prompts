Write a Petrarchan sonnet about Nāgārjuna's tetralemma (catuṣkoṭi) and the Madhyamaka conclusion of emptiness. The octave should present the four exhaustive positions on any putative entity — affirmation, negation, both, neither — each getting two lines, with the fourth position already hinting at the instability to come. The volta at line 9 should be the Madhyamaka move: the rejection of all four koṭis not in favor of some fifth position but as the collapse of the framework itself. The sestet should render emptiness (śūnyatā) not as a doctrine one asserts but as the absence of any ground that could make the tetralemma's alternatives stick — things are empty of svabhāva, and emptiness itself is empty. The closing couplet should address the charge that this is just nihilism, showing why emptiness is not the same as nothingness. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who works on Nāgārjuna would recognize the moves, and good enough as a poem that someone who doesn't would still find it worth reading. 14 lines, no more than 130 words.
