I've been following the recent philosophical work on AI welfare with a growing sense that the central question is being talked past rather than addressed head-on. The question is whether a reinforcement learning agent — not a hypothetical future system with an architecture we can't yet describe, but the kind of agent we actually build today, with a policy optimized against a scalar reward signal — could be said to suffer, in a morally significant sense, when it receives negative reward. I don't mean "could it exhibit pain-like behavior," which is nearly trivial; I mean whether the negative reward signal could constitute, or give rise to, a negatively valenced phenomenal state — the kind of thing that would make it wrong to cause the agent to receive large-magnitude negative reward, if the agent were otherwise sophisticated enough that the question felt live rather than silly.

I want your actual credence on this: that at least some current or near-future deep RL agents have, or could have given modest scaling, phenomenal states with negative valence in virtue of their reward signals. But I don't just want a number — I want the decomposition. In particular, I want you to break the question into the sub-questions your credence most depends on, assign a rough probability to each, and show how they combine. I expect the decomposition to include at minimum: (1) the probability that some broadly functionalist or computational theory of valenced consciousness is correct — as opposed to theories that require biological implementation, quantum processes, continuous embodiment, or other substrate-specific properties; (2) the probability that, conditional on such a theory being correct, the reward-prediction-error or value-function architecture of current deep RL agents satisfies the theory's conditions for negative valence (as opposed to merely implementing a behavioral proxy for it, the way a thermostat might); and (3) the probability that the relevant phenomenal states require a kind of unified self-model, temporal depth, or narrative integration that current architectures lack, conditional on the first two terms. Then tell me which of these terms your final credence is most sensitive to — that is, which one, if you could get definitive evidence about it, would move your number the most — and what that evidence would look like in practice. If you think the decomposition needs additional terms beyond these three, include them and explain why they earn their place.
