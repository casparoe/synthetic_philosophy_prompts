Draft an adversarial-collaboration document between a Humean and an anti-Humean about the metaphysics of laws of nature. The Humean holds that the laws are nothing over and above the total distribution of particular matters of fact: on the Best Systems Account, for instance, the laws are the axioms of the deductive system that achieves the best balance of simplicity and informativeness in systematizing the Humean mosaic — the vast catalogue of categorical, non-modal facts about what actually happens where and when. The anti-Humean holds that laws are something more: primitive governing relations, necessitation relations among universals, or an irreducible nomological structure that produces, constrains, or underwrites the regularities, rather than merely summarizing them.

The document should contain the following sections.

First, a numbered list of claims both parties can sign. These should be substantive, not merely verbal agreements or truisms. Aim for claims that each side would accept in its own terms but that jointly narrow the space of dispute — for example, claims about what the empirical success of science does and does not entail, about whether laws must supervene on the particular facts, about what a satisfactory account must explain, about the status of uninstantiated laws, or about the relationship between laws and counterfactuals.

Second, a disagreements table with three columns: the issue, the Humean position phrased so a Humean would endorse it, and the anti-Humean position phrased so an anti-Humean would endorse it. The formulations should be precise enough that neither side can dismiss the other's entry as a straw man. Issues worth tabling might include: whether laws govern or merely describe, whether the distinction between laws and accidents is primitive or derived, whether possible worlds with the same particular facts but different laws are conceivable, whether Humeanism can account for the explanatory and predictive role of laws without circularity, and whether the Best Systems Account's reliance on natural properties concedes too much to the anti-Humean.

Third, a list of cruxes — the sub-questions such that, if they were settled, at least one side would revise its view. For each crux, specify what both sides would accept as a relevant consideration or test case, and record each side's prediction or stance about it. Candidate cruxes include the conceivability of a world of thoroughly lawless but regular successions, the tenability of a principled distinction between the natural properties the Best Systems Account needs and the ones it must earn, and whether the direction of explanation runs from laws to regularities or vice versa.

Fourth, a brief assessment of whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal. If so, identify which disagreements survive translation into each side's preferred idiom and which dissolve once the terms of art are cashed out.

The document should reflect good-faith engagement throughout: neither side caricatures the other, both acknowledge the other's strongest arguments and the costs of their own view, and the residual disagreement is sharpened rather than softened. Do not adjudicate the debate or declare a winner; the task is to produce the document the collaborators would co-author, not to settle the metaphysics.
