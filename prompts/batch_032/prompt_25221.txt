I'm the maid of honor at my best friend's wedding this Saturday and I am completely stuck on the toast. Here's the thing. The bride, Elena, is a total Cliffordian — not that she'd use that word, but she's a data journalist, she fact-checks everything, and she genuinely believes it's morally wrong to hold a belief without adequate evidence. She once broke up with a guy because he forwarded her a chain email about vaccines. The groom, David, is a Catholic who would say that some of the most important truths in his life — about love, about meaning, about God — aren't the kind of thing you arrive at by running a study. He'd be more of a Jamesian if he read James: there are live, forced, momentous options where your passional nature has to decide, and it's not irrational to do so.

They've been together for six years and their relationship works beautifully, but the toast has to somehow honor both of their ways of being in the world without selling either of them short. The room is going to be split: half journalists and data nerds, half David's very religious family. If I frame Elena's worldview as the gold standard and David's as this cute exception she tolerates, his family will be hurt. If I frame David's faith as the deeper truth and Elena as someone who just hasn't gotten there yet, she'll feel betrayed and frankly it wouldnt be true.

I need a toast — maybe three or four minutes — that makes sense of how two people with such different epistemologies can love each other and build a life together, and that lands as celebration, not as a philosophy paper. Can you write it?
