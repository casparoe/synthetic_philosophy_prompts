so my friend diane is a novelist and she has this thing about when characters are being smart vs dumb about following the crowd. ive been keeping a list of her verdicts from arguments weve had over the years:

- tourist who picks the crowded restaurant over the empty one next door: smart, the crowd knows something
- character in a bank run who joins the line without investigating the banks finances: smart
- guy at a party who buys a stock because some stranger there wont shut up about it: not smart, thats just one loud person
- family that evacuates because the neighbors are leaving, without checking the news: smart
- character who buys a house in 2006 because prices only go up and everyones saying it: not smart

now im writing a scene where my protagonist sees that five book reviewers shes never heard of, writing for outlets she doesnt follow, have all given five stars to a debut novel. she buys it on that basis alone — doesnt read the reviews, just sees the star ratings. diane says this is dumb, like the party stock tip. i think its more like the restaurant. but the weird thing is i can think of at least two principles that fit all five of her verdicts perfectly, and they point opposite ways on the book case. what are those principles, which one is diane actually running, and whats a sixth case that would force her to commit?
