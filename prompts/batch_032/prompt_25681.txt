I am trying to get clear on my views about fine-tuning before writing a paper, and I would like a consistency audit. Here is where I currently stand:

1. The fine-tuning of physical constants and initial conditions for life is a genuine fact that calls for explanation — it is not rationally satisfying to treat it as a brute given.

2. The anthropic principle — that we can only observe a life-permitting universe — does not by itself explain fine-tuning. It explains why we do not observe a life-hostile universe, but not why a life-permitting universe exists at all.

3. The multiverse hypothesis (inflationary cosmology plus some mechanism for varying constants across domains) would, if true, provide an adequate explanation of fine-tuning.

4. Theism would, if true, also provide an adequate explanation of fine-tuning.

5. Inference to the best explanation is the right framework for adjudicating between (3) and (4) as candidate explanations of fine-tuning.

6. The prior probability of theism — before taking fine-tuning into account — is very low, low enough that fine-tuning cannot render theism probable all things considered.

7. The prior probability of a multiverse is not so low that fine-tuning cannot render it probable all things considered.

8. The fact that inflationary cosmology has independent motivation (from the horizon and flatness problems) gives the multiverse hypothesis a theoretical advantage over theism that goes beyond fine-tuning itself.

I want to know whether this set of commitments hangs together. In particular, I suspect there may be tensions I am not seeing, and I also worry that some pairs that look like they are in tension might actually be fine on closer inspection. Please go through the commitments and identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the tensions by severity, and for each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be.
