I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about whether compatriots have special claims on each other, and my gut reactions to cases keep pulling me in different directions. Here's where I land:

- A child is drowning in a pond right in front of me; another child is drowning in a pond overseas, and I can press a button to save one but not both. I save the one in front of me. But I don't think nationality explains that — it's proximity. If both were equally far and I could only fund one rescue operation, I'd flip a coin. Nationality doesn't seem to matter there at all.

- But then: my government is deciding between funding a dialysis center here (saves 100 lives/year) or malaria prevention abroad (saves 1,000 lives/year for the same cost). I lean toward the domestic program. I'm not proud of it, but that's my gut.

- An undocumented immigrant who's lived here since childhood, works, pays taxes, and has no connection to their birth country is facing deportation. A citizen who's lived abroad for decades, never contributed, wants to return for retirement healthcare. If we can only take one, I'd take the undocumented immigrant instantly. Legal citizenship status alone feels almost irrelevant to who deserves what.

- My country goes to war. Do I have a special duty to support my country's side? I don't think so. If our side is wrong, I should say so. Patriotism as loyalty-to-a-side-regardless feels like a kind of moral corruption.

- Yet: my country has a history of colonialism and slavery. I think I have some special responsibility to help address the ongoing effects — more than someone from a country with no such history. That responsibility seems to come partly from being a citizen of the state that did it and partly from having benefited.

- A friend asks me to help them move. A stranger from abroad asks for the same help at the same time. I help the friend. But if the stranger will become homeless without my help and my friend just wants to save on movers, I help the stranger. So special obligations to friends are real but defeasible by serious need. Are special obligations to compatriots like that? They don't feel the same way, but I can't articulate why.

Is there a principle that captures most of this, and which of these verdicts should I be most suspicious of? And feel free to tell me if the whole way I'm framing this is confused — maybe I'm smuggling in assumptions that don't hold up under scrutiny.
