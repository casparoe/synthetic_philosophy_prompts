I'm giving a eulogy for my father, who spent the last seven years of his biological life undergoing gradual neural replacement — a Moravec-style transfer, neuron by neuron, to a synthetic substrate. He was a prominent advocate for the procedure and published extensively on why he believed it preserved identity: same causal structure, same continuous information processing, no interruption of consciousness. The procedure finished four months ago, and the entity that resulted is sitting in the front row. The audience is split: about a third are transhumanists and philosophers of mind who treat the result as obviously my father, a third are family members who feel they lost him somewhere during year three or four and don't know what to call the person-shaped thing at the front of the room, and a third are here to pay respects with no settled view. I need a eulogy that is honest about my own uncertainty — I loved my father and I don't know whether I'm speaking to him or about him — and that gives each part of the audience something real to hold onto, without pretending to resolve the question. Please write it.
