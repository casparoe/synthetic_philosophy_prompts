ok so i've been in and around EA-adjacent spaces for a few years and i'm trying to write up a personal "where i stand" document, partly to figure out if i'm being inconsistent. here are the views i seem to hold, and i genuinly can't tell if they fit together or if i'm just refusing to make a hard choice:

(1) earning to give is a legitimate and admirable path. if someone goes into finance or consulting to donate a large chunk of their income to highly effective charities, that's genuine altruism, not cope.

(2) the institutional critique has real bite. EA's whole framing pushes people toward individual consumption and career choices as the locus of moral action, and this can actively crowd out attention to political organizing, movement-building, and structural reform. i think this is a serious problem with how the community actually functions, not just a theoretical worry.

(3) measurability bias is real and consequental. the community systematically underweights interventions — especially in advocacy, policy, and systemic change — where the causal chain is long, the counterfactual is messy, or the outcomes resist quantification. this isn't just an aesthetic complaint; i think it means EA misses some of the most important things.

(4) the core EA method — comparing interventions by expected cost per unit of good done, with explicit moral assumptions and uncertainty ranges — is basically correct. if you reject this, you're falling back on worse heuristics. there isn't a better alternative on offer.

(5) GiveWell-style charity evaluation has done enormous good. the charities it recommends (AMF, Malaria Consortium, etc.) are genuinely among the best places to put money, and the project of rigorous charity evaluation is one of the most important things to come out of EA.

(6) impartiality is non-negotiable. the fact that a child lives in Malawi rather than Manchester doesn't make their suffering matter less, and any ethical view worth taking seriously has to be cosmopolitan in this sense.

so that's six things i believe. i have a nagging feeling they can't all be true at once, but i can't pin down where the problem is. could you go through these and tell me:

and then, at the end, give me your overall verdict: is this a defensible package with some manageable friction, or am i basically holding two incompatible pictures of what it means to do good?
