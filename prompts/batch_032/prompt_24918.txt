ok so I'm a PhD student in philosophy of science, working at the intersection with psychiatry, and my advisor keeps telling me I need to nail down my dissertation topic instead of just "reading around." I've gotten really interested in Ian Hacking's idea of looping effects — you know, the way psychiatric classifications (like ADHD, or DID back when it was MPD) don't just label people from the outside; the people who get classified become aware of the label, and they start to shape themselves around it, which changes what the classification refers to, which feeds back into how people understand themselves, and so on in a loop. Hacking calls these "interactive kinds" as opposed to "indifferent kinds" like quarks, which don't care what we call them.

Here's the thing that's bugging me. A bunch of people in the philosophy of psychiatry lit treat looping effects as basically a defeater for realism about psychiatric categories — the thought being, if the category keeps shifting because the classified people are reacting to it, then it can't be tracking a real, stable kind. But I'm not convinced this is right. I mean, plenty of things we think are perfectly real involve feedback between the label and the labeled — social roles like "teacher" or "defendant," or even biological phenomena where observation changes the thing observed. So maybe the looping effect doesn't show that psychiatric kinds aren't real; maybe it just shows that the wrong model of realism is being assumed, and what we need is a realism that can handle dynamic, interactive kinds without treating them as second-rate.
