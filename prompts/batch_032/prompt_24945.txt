I've been thinking about how to update beliefs when evidence doesn't come in cleanly, and I've settled into a set of views that I'd like stress-tested for consistency. My background is in software engineering, not philosophy, so I may be missing tensions that are obvious to people who work on this. Here's where I've landed:

(1) Bayesianism is the right framework: credences should obey the probability axioms, and belief revision should proceed by conditionalization in some form.

(2) Standard conditionalization — P_new(H) = P_old(H | E) upon learning E with certainty — is almost never applicable in practice. Real evidence is noisy: a sensor reading with error bars, a recollection you're 70% confident in, a claim from a source that's usually but not always reliable. We basically never learn anything with certainty.

(3) Jeffrey conditionalization is the correct generalization for uncertain evidence. When your evidence bears on a partition {E₁, ..., Eₙ} by shifting your credences in the Eᵢ to new values Q(Eᵢ), you update any proposition H via: P_new(H) = Σᵢ P_old(H | Eᵢ) × Q(Eᵢ).

(4) The order in which you acquire evidence shouldn't matter to your final credences. If you learn one thing and then another, you should end up in the same place as if you'd learned them in reverse order. Path-dependence seems like a failure of rationality — it would mean your beliefs depend on an arbitrary feature of your history.

(5) Jeffrey conditionalization genuinely generalizes standard conditionalization: in the limiting case where Q(Eₖ) = 1 for some partition element, the formula reduces to P_new(H) = P_old(H | Eₖ), which is exactly standard conditionalization.

(6) The rigidity condition — that P_old(H | Eᵢ) remains unchanged through the update — is a feature, not a bug. It captures the idea that your evidence bears only on the partition itself, while the conditional relationships between H and the Eᵢ reflect background knowledge that the new evidence doesn't touch.

I'd like an audit: do these six claims form a consistent package? Which pairs are in genuine tension, and which only appear to be? For the real tensions, rank them by severity and say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs. I don't have a favorite among these — I want to know which one has to go, if any.
