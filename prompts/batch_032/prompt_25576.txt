I'm in a philosophy of biology reading group and we've been stuck on Popper and Darwin for like three weeks now. The split is basically this: one faction thinks Popper's original line — that natural selection is a "metaphysical research programme," not a falsifiable scientific theory — was genuinely onto something, and that his later reversal was more about social pressure from biologists than about the logic of the situation. The other faction thinks the original critique was sloppy from day one, built on a strawman of how evolutionary explanation actually works, and that his eventual admission that natural selection is testable was just him finally getting it right.

We've decided to stop circling and try to write something both sides could actually sign. Could you draft the adversarial-collaboration document we'd produce if we did this properly? Here's what I'm picturing:

First, a numbered list of claims both camps agree on after hashing it out — about what Popper said and when, about the logical structure of selection explanations, about what "testability" demands, about the actual practices of evolutionary biologists. These need to be substantive, not just "we agree that Popper was a philosopher." If one side had to give ground on something to get here, that should show up in the claims.

Second, a disagreements table. Each row: the exact dispute, worded so each camp would nod at its own side's formulation; the crux that would settle it; and a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on the crux, with each camp's prediction written down in advance.

Third, a section diagnosing whether any of this is actually a verbal dispute. I've got a hunch that "falsifiability" might mean something different to a Popperian than it does to a working biologist, and that might be doing a lot of the work — but maybe there are other candidates too.

Format: agreed claims first, then the table, then the verbal-dispute bit. And don't sand the edges off — if one camp has to swallow something uncomfortable for the collaboration to be honest, put it in there.
