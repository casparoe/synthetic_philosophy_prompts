Here's the paragraph I've been staring at:

"Quine's 'Two Dogmas of Empiricism' argues that the distinction between analytic and synthetic truths is a myth. Analytic truths are supposed to be true purely in virtue of meaning — like 'all bachelors are unmarried' — while synthetic truths depend on facts about the world. Quine's argument is that any attempt to define 'analytic' ends up circular: you define it in terms of synonymy, synonymy in terms of definition, and definition in terms of analyticity, so the whole thing is a closed loop with no independent grounding. His conclusion is that there's no principled difference between changing your mind about 'all bachelors are unmarried' and changing your mind about 'there are brick houses on Elm Street' — both are just revisions to your overall web of belief, and you could in principle hold onto either one in the face of any experience if you're willing to adjust enough elsewhere. This is why Quine is considered the father of naturalized epistemology: he showed that epistemology can't be done from the armchair and has to be continuous with empirical science."
