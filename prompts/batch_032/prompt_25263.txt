A colleague in my department studies hinge epistemology — the Wittgensteinian view that certain propositions function as "hinges" exempt from doubt, forming the scaffolding that makes inquiry possible. Over several conversations, I've collected her verdicts on which propositions count as hinges:

- "Here is a hand" (said while holding up a hand in normal conditions): hinge.
- "The earth existed before my birth": hinge.
- "Water is H2O": not a hinge.
- "I have a body": hinge.
- "The sun will rise tomorrow": not a hinge.
- "There are other minds": hinge.
- "2 + 2 = 4": hinge.
- "Paris is the capital of France": not a hinge.
- "I have never been to the moon": hinge.
- "The Battle of Hastings was in 1066": not a hinge.
