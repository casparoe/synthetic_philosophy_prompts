I'm giving a talk at an astrobiology conference next month defending the dark forest hypothesis as a serious solution to the Fermi paradox. My argument goes roughly: (1) any civilization that detects another faces a chain-of-suspicion problem — you can't know their intentions, they can't know yours, and the light-speed delay means you can't negotiate in real time; (2) technological explosion means a harmless civilization today could be a threat tomorrow; (3) given finite resources and the survival imperative, the dominant strategy is preemptive strike; (4) therefore rational civilizations stay silent and destroy anyone who doesn't. The audience is mostly astrobiologists and planetary scientists, a few SETI researchers, and maybe two philosophers of science. They're not going to roll their eyes at the sci-fi origins; they're going to pick at the game theory, the biology, and the detection physics. I need a Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get, ordered by how likely and how damaging, phrased the way this audience would phrase them, with a two-minute reply for each and a note on where the reply is weakest. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. And tell me: which premise, if it turned out to be false, would make the whole argument collapse, and where is my reasoning most likely to have gone wrong?
