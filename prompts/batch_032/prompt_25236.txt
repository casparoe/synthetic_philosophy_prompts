What would al-Ghazālī have made of Hume's argument against necessary connection? They both end up denying we can observe causal necessity in nature, but for totally different reasons — al-Ghazālī because God is the only true cause and recreates the world at each instant, Hume because all we ever see is constant conjunction and our minds project necessity onto it. So I'm curious: if you dropped al-Ghazālī into 18th-century Edinburgh and handed him the Enquiry, where would he nod along and where would he think Hume is missing the whole point? And conversely, what would al-Ghazālī's occasionalism look like to Hume — would he see it as a weirdly theological version of his own view, or something fundamentally different?
