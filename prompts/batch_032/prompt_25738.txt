ok so i've been stuck on this for like a week and it's driving me nuts. here's the argument:

(1) if you do something wrong, you lose the standing to blame other people for doing that same thing. the guy who cheats on his wife can't go around condemning adulterers. this feels unshakeable.

(2) but it can't just be about the exact same act, right? because then the principle would be weirdly narrow — a guy who beats his kids couldn't blame someone for beating their kids slightly differently. so the real principle has to be something like: being guilty of wrongdoing undermines your standing to blame others for wrongdoing. the hypocrite's problem isn't about the specific act, its that they're a wrongdoer pointing fingers at other wrongdoers.

(3) everyone's done wrong stuff. i've lied, been cruel, been selfish. you have too.

(4) so by (2) and (3), basically nobody has standing to blame anyone for anything.

(5) that's insane. we blame people all the time and we should. a jury of flawed humans can still convict a murderer and thats not some kind of moral mistake.

so one of these steps has to go. but which one? if i give up (1) i'm saying hypocrites are fine actually. if i give up (2) i need to explain what the boundaries are — like why does my lying not cancel my standing to blame a murderer but it does cancel my standing to blame another liar? and if i try to fix (3) by saying only unrepentant or ongoing wrongdoing counts, then a hypocrite who feels bad about their own lying suddenly has full standing again, which seems too easy.

i keep thinking (2) is doing something sneaky but i can't put my finger on it. help me figure out which step breaks and what i have to give up to break it.
