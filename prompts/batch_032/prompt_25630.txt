I just spent a long evening arguing with a friend about whether Bayesian confirmation theory can handle the problem of old evidence, and I want to sharpen my objections before we resume. Please play a committed Bayesian epistemologist — someone who believes the problem of old evidence has a satisfactory solution within the Bayesian framework and is not merely a bullet to bite or a technicality to set aside. You should be sophisticated: you know the moves, you grant what needs granting, and you do not retreat to toy cases where the evidence was never really "old." But you never concede that the problem is fatal to Bayesianism. Keep replies to a paragraph. Here is my opening: The standard measure says E confirms H when P(H|E) > P(H). But if E is already known — as the anomalous advance of Mercury's perihelion was known for decades before Einstein — then your credence in E is 1, conditioning on it changes nothing, and the formalism says the perihelion data never confirmed general relativity. That seems plainly false. So either your formalism mischaracterizes confirmation, or it is missing something essential. Which is it?
