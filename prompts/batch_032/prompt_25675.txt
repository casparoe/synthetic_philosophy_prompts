ok so I'm a PhD student in epistemology working on testimony and I'm trying to get my views straight before I write my prospectus. here's roughly where I've landed and I want to know if it all hangs together:

(a) I'm a reductionist about testimonial justification in the broadly Humean tradition. A hearer is justified in believing what someone tells them only if they have adequate inductive evidence that the speaker is reliable on the topic. No free passes just because someone said something.

(b) That said, I don't think this requires conscious inference. In ordinary cases we subpersonally track reliability cues—confidence, familiarity, past accuracy, whether the claim fits background knowledge—and we just find ourselves believing. So the phenomenology objection ("I don't feel like I'm running an induction every time someone tells me the time") doesn't bother me.

(c) I think the child-testimony objection to reductionism is overblown. Yeah, kids learn a ton from adults, but they also accumulate massive amounts of data about adult reliability very early, and they're sensitive to cues like familiarity and past accuracy. So I think reductionism can handle the developmental data without going anti-reductionist.

(d) I'm an evidentialist: epistemic justification is entirely about fit between a belief and the subject's total evidence. If your evidence supports P, you're justified in believing P; if it doesn't, you're not.

(e) I think Miranda Fricker is onto something real with testimonial injustice. In central cases, a hearer assigns a speaker less credibility than they deserve because of identity-based prejudice, and the hearer is epistemically blameworthy for this—not just morally blameworthy, but epistemically.

(f) Here's the thing though: in the most structurally troubling cases of testimonial injustice, the hearer's total evidence, shaped by a prejudiced social environment, actually doesn't support the speaker's claim. Like, if you've grown up in a deeply sexist society, your evidence base might genuinely make it unlikely that a woman is reliable about car repair. The injustice is structural—it's in the evidence itself.

So that's my set. I'm worried there's a tension I'm not seeing, or maybe one I am seeing but I'm wrong about how serious it is. Go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. For each tension, say what would have to give, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. And if any of these only look like they're in tension but actually aren't, flag that too—I don't want to abandon a view because of a confusion.
