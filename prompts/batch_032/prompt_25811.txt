I've been trying to wrap my head around moral particularism and I keep getting stuck on the same thing. Particularists say there are no true moral principles—like, you can't say "lying is wrong" as a general rule because in some contexts lying might be fine or even required. Fine, I get that part. But then what do particularists actually appeal to when they make moral judgments? If you can't invoke a principle, aren't you just going with your gut? It seems like particularism either collapses into "I just know it when I see it" intuitionism, or else it's secretly relying on principles but refusing to admit it. What am I missing? Also, what would change your mind about whether particularism is a viable position—like what argument or consideration would make you think it just doesn't work?
