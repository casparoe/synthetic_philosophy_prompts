I recently started working on an AI safety team, and I'm trying to understand what our standard alignment pipeline commits us to, philosophically. The practice I have in mind is this: we train a language model using reinforcement learning from human feedback. A reward model is trained on human preference judgments across a finite set of prompts and completions. The language model is then fine-tuned against that reward model. Once performance looks good on held-out evaluation data, we deploy the model for open-ended use, where it encounters prompts, tasks, and contexts far outside the distribution on which the reward model was trained.

What theory of goal acquisition does this practice presuppose? In particular, does it presuppose that the model internalizes the reward signal as its terminal goal — that it comes to want what the reward model rewards — or is the practice compatible with the model instead learning a proxy goal that happens to correlate with reward on the training distribution but comes apart from it under distribution shift? If the practice is compatible with both, what does it implicitly assume about how severe the distribution shift can be before proxy goals diverge from intended ones?

I would also like to know which assumptions this practice depends on most heavily, and how the training and deployment pipeline would have to differ if we dropped the assumption that models learn the intended goal rather than a proxy. And what empirical finding about how models generalize under distribution shift would most directly undermine the presuppositions of the current pipeline?
