I run a community mediation center — we handle maybe twenty disputes a month, neighbor noise, property lines, landlord-tenant stuff, business partnerships gone sour. The hardest part of my job isn't getting people to agree, it's writing the case summary both sides will sign off on before we even start mediation. Each side tells a completely different story and both seem genuinely convinced they're right.

I've been reading about this Jain idea of anekāntavāda — that truth has many sides and no single perspective captures the whole thing — and the related practice of syādvāda, where you qualify claims with something like "in some respects" rather than stating them absolutely. I'm wondering if there's a practical procedure I can build out of this. Not the metaphysics, just a step-by-step way to take two contradictory accounts and produce a description that honors what's true in each without pretending they're compatable where they aren't.

What I need: a set of questions I ask about each account in order, what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure tells me I can reconcile the accounts and where it tells me they genuinely contradict, and where it throws up its hands and says "this is a judgment call." It has to be something I can run in about an hour per case, and the output has to be a summary I can read back to both parties without them walking out.

Then walk me through it on two cases. First: a tenant says the landlord is harassing them with unannounced visits and the landlord says they're just doing legally required maintenance checks with proper notice — the tenant has a log of seventeen visits in three months, the landlord has work orders for about half of them. Second: two business partners disagree about whether a handshake deal at a bar after three drinks counts as a binding agreement to expand the business — one says they shook on it and that's that, the other says it was just talk and they never discussed dollar amounts.
