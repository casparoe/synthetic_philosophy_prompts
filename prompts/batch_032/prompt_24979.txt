I'm presenting to my local effective altruism group next week on why moral trade deserves more than the dismissive shrug it usually gets. My thesis is that if you take moral uncertainty seriously — even just a little — then refusing to engage in moral trade with someone whose values differ from yours is often a mistake by your own lights, and that the standard objections (it's unverifiable, it's a gimmick, it requires treating values like preferences) don't hold up as well as people think.

The audience is about twenty people, mostly committed utilitarians who have done the introductory EA fellowship and have strong views about cause prioritization. Several think the whole idea of moral trade is a distraction from figuring out what actually does the most good. A couple are longtermists who suspect moral trade is just a way for neartermists to get longtermist money. One or two are sympathetic but unconvinced.
