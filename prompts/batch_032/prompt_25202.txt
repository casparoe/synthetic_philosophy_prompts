I'm giving a department colloquium next month defending origin essentialism — the Kripkean view that you couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg than the ones you actually came from. The audience is a mixed philosophy department: about half metaphysicians (including a couple of diehard Lewisians who think in counterpart theory, one committed haecceitist, and a few agnostics), plus some philosophers of biology, a Kantian ethicist, and a couple of historians of philosophy who'll tune out if the talk gets too insular. My argument is that the standard intuition pumps (could Queen Elizabeth have come from different parents?) are stronger than critics admit, and that the main rival view — that what matters for identity across possible worlds is qualitative similarity rather than origin — collapses once you trace branching possibilities carefully enough.
