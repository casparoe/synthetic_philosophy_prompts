Here's what she's said:

Definately can suffer: cows, chickens, salmon, octopus. She's especially intense about the octopus thing — she'll go on and on about how smart they are.

Definitely can't suffer: shrimp, bees, jellyfish, oysters.

She was unsure about lobsters for a long time, but last time it came up she said she's leaning toward "no," though she wouldn't bet her life on it.

The thing is, I can't figure out what rule she's actually using. It's not just "vertebrates get to suffer and invertebrates don't," because octopuses are invertebrates and she's totally sure about them. And it's not just about intelligence or complex behavior either, because bees do all this incredible stuff — they can recognize faces, they communicate with dances, they solve problems — and she still says they can't suffer. So she's got to be using some principle about what kind of nervous system counts, or what kind of behavior is evidence of suffering, or something like that.

What I want to know is: what do you think she'd say about crabs? She's never mentioned them. They're crustaceans like lobsters and shrimp, so part of me thinks she'd put them in the same bucket. But I've also heard that crabs can learn to avoid painful situations in a way that looks more flexible than just a reflex, so maybe she'd put them closer to the "yes" side.

More than just the crab prediction though, I want to know: what are the different rules that could actually fit all her verdicts so far, what would each of those rules say about crabs, and which one do you think is most likely what she's running?
