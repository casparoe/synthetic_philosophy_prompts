I'm running a reading group on practical reason and we're about to hit the incommensurability / hard choices stuff. Before anyone reads a word of it, I want to hand out a little sorting quiz so people can see where their gut intuitions actually put them — like, are they secretly a Raz-style incomparabilist, a Chang-style parity person, or a boring commensurabilist who thinks all values cash out on one scale.

Please write me a "which position on hard choices do you actually hold?" quiz. Here's what I need:

- Eight to ten questions. Each one should be a concrete choice scenario — career vs. family, saving a stranger vs. a friend, a small-improvement case (like choosing between a plain chocolate bar and a slightly fancier one where adding a tiny perk to the plainer one doesn't flip the choice), that kind of thing. Two or three answer options per question.
- A scoring key that maps answer patterns onto at least these four positions: (1) commensurabilism (all values reduce to a single scale, hard choices are just computational), (2) incomparabilism (some values genuinely can't be compared, so rational choice sometimes goes silent), (3) the parity view (values can be on a par — comparable but not in the better/worse/equal trichotomy), and (4) the epistemic view (hard choices are real but only because we lack information, not because values are metaphysically weird).
- Flag any pair of positions your quiz can't cleanly separate — don't paper over it.

The questions have to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like. So no question should be "do you think values are incommensurable?" or anything like that. Make the cases do the work.

Keep the whole thing under 1000 words — quiz, scoring key, and all.
