I'm a philosophy professor and I sit on the ethics advisory board at our university's fertility clinic. I need to draft our decision memo for a proposal that's coming to a vote next month. The clinic wants to pilot polygenic embryo screening — letting IVF patients rank embryos by polygenic scores for non-disease traits, specifically predicted educational attainment and adult height. This is on top of the standard aneuploidy screening we already do. The medical director's argument is basically: we already let parents select against devastating monogenic conditions; these new scores just give parents more information about which embryo is likely to have the best shot at a good life; refusing to offer them is paternalistic.

The board is genuinely split and the disagreement has gotten specific in ways that I think are philosophically interesting. One camp says this is just reproductive autonomy in action — if we trust parents to make thousands of decisions that shape a child's life after birth, it's inconsistent to block them from making an informed choice at the embryo stage. The other camp says the issue isn't autonomy in the abstract but what kind of parental disposition we're normalizing: selecting embryos by trait scores expresses an attitude toward the future child — that they need to meet a spec — that's corrosive to unconditional parental acceptance. There's also a live disagreement about whether the non-identity problem dissolves the whole worry: if you pick embryo A over embryo B, the child who exists wouldn't have existed otherwise, so who's harmed? But that feels too slick to several of us.

And then there's the practical stuff: the polygenic scores in question were developed on European-ancestry populations and their predictive power drops substantially for other groups, so there's a justice-in-healthcare dimension the director hasnt really addressed.

I need the memo to recommend one of three things: approve the pilot as proposed, reject it outright, or approve with specific conditions (and say what those conditions are). I want the reasoning to do real philosophical work — don't just list pros and cons. Engage the strongest consideration on the losing side and explain why it doesnt carry the day. And give me an actual bottom line the clinic can implement, not a hedge.
