I'm a junior in high school, and I've been noticing something about my own behavior that's started to bother me on a more than practical level. Every Sunday evening I sit down and make a detailed study schedule for the week: I block out two hours each day for SAT prep, I set aside time to work on my history research paper well before the deadline, and I promise myself I'll go to bed by 10:30 so I'm not exhausted in class. The planning itself feels genuinely good — I'm calm, I'm thinking clearly about what matters, and I really do want to follow through. But then Monday comes, and a friend texts me about hanging out, and I tell myself I'll just shift the SAT block to Tuesday. Tuesday I'm tired from staying up late, so I do the bare minimum homework and watch videos instead. By Friday I've broken every commitment I made to myself, and I'm scrambling to start the paper I swore I'd be halfway done with by now.

Here's the part that confuses me. When I'm making the Sunday plan, I'm not lying to myself — I genuinely believe I'll follow it. And when I break it on Monday, I'm not exactly being forced; I could say no to my friend, I could close the laptop, but I don't. It feels like there are two versions of me making decisions, and the Monday version doesn't seem to care about what the Sunday version wanted, even though we're the same person and the Sunday version was, if anything, thinking more clearly. My question isn't really about study tips or productivity hacks — I've read plenty of those. What I want to understand is: what's actually going on when I do this? Is this just a failure of willpower, or is there something structurally irrational about how I'm relating to my own future self? And if the person on Sunday and the person on Monday are both me, why does the Sunday version's judgment feel more authoritative, even though the Monday version is the one who actually decides what happens?
