My reading group has been stuck for weeks arguing about procreative beneficence and whether it inevitably slides into something eugenic. One of us basically defends something like Savulescu's principle — that prospective parents have a moral reason (maybe even an obligation) to select the embryo or fetus that will have the best expected life, all else equal. The other side thinks this whole framework is rotten: that it expresses a disrespectful attitude toward disabled people, that it misunderstands the parent-child relationship, and that once you start ranking possible future people by expected wellbeing you've already stepped onto a slope that ends somewhere ugly. They've been talking past each other.

I want you to draft the adversarial-collaboration document they should have produced. Include:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can sign — about what selection technologies can and can't do, about what the non-identity problem does and doesn't show, about what counts as harm or wronging in this context, and about the historical relationship between eugenics and reproductive choice. These shouldn't be vacuous.

2. A disagreements table. For each disagreement, give the precise formulation of the proponent's view (worded so the proponent would say "yes, that's what I think") and the critic's view (worded so the critic would say "yes, that's what I think").

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions where, if you could settle them, at least one side would update. For each crux, describe a concrete case or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it, and record what each side predicts about that case.

I suspect one crux is whether selection and postnatal enhancement are morally continuous, and another is whether the expressivist argument survives if you grant that disabilities can be both neutral-in-principle and dispreferred-in-practice without contradiction. But dont let me steer you — find the real cruxes.
