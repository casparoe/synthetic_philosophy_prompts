I keep seeing people propose solutions to the Meno problem — why knowledge is more valuable than mere true belief — and each one seems to just pick a feature of knowledge and assert that it's valuable. Before I read another one, I want the full list of requirements. What does a theory of the value of knowledge actually have to deliver? Which cases does it have to get right (the standard ones, but also the weird ones where true belief seems just as good or better)? Which distinctions can't it collapse — instrumental vs. final value, the value of the state vs. the value of getting there, maybe the value of knowledge-for-the-knower vs. knowledge simpliciter? What about the swamping problem: does a theory have to solve it or just explain why it's not fatal? And which single requirement knocks out the most candidate solutions? Flag any pairs that look like they can't be jointly satisfied. Think very carefully and respond in the style of an expert epistemologist.
