I am a novelist developing a time travel story and I want the underlying metaphysics to be internally consistent before I commit to plot mechanics. The grandfather paradox seems to be the central fork in the road: different resolutions come with different costs for narrative stakes, character persistence, and what I owe the reader in exposition. I would like a decision tree that sorts the major philosophical positions on the paradox, built from the logical space rather than from a list of labels.

Please construct the tree around the following diagnostic questions, which are the ones that matter for storytelling:

- Can the past be changed at all, or is the timeline fixed such that any apparent change was always part of history?
- If the past can be changed, does doing so generate logical contradictions, and if so, what mechanism resolves them — branching, overwriting with consistency constraints, or something else?
- Does the time traveler survive changes to their own past, or can they be erased by their own actions (and if so, what does that mean for narrative point of view)?
- Is there a second temporal dimension (hyper-time) in which changes unfold, or does the block universe preclude any sense of "changing" the past?

Each leaf should carry a name for the position and a single sentence on what that position costs a storyteller: what kind of scene it forecloses, what emotional stake it undercuts, or what it forces you to explain to the reader that you might prefer to avoid.

Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate — positions that give the same answers to all diagnostic questions yet remain distinct views. Also flag any logically possible combination of answers that no one defends, with a brief note on why: incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored.

I am aiming for roughly eight to twelve leaves. I do not need a bibliography or reading list; I need the conceptual geography laid out so I can see what I am signing up for with each narrative choice.
