Okay so I'm in this weird interdisciplinary seminar on the ethics and law of space colonization, and we spent last week on the Outer Space Treaty and I cannot stop thinking about this one thing. The OST (1967) says outer space and celestial bodies are "not subject to national appropriation" and that their use shall be "the province of all mankind." Pretty sweeping. But then fast forward to 2015 and the U.S. passes the Commercial Space Launch Competitiveness Act, which basically says: American companies, if you go mine an asteroid, you own whatever you dig up. The Artemis Accords from 2020 double down on this — resource extraction is fine, it doesn't count as national appropriation. Luxembourg passed similar laws. The legal move everyone makes is to distinguish between owning the land (nope) and owning the stuff you extract from the land (yep), analogizing to fishing rights in international waters.

And I get the legal maneuver, but philosophically I'm confused about what's actually being presupposed here. Because "province of all mankind" sure sounds like some kind of collective ownership or common heritage principle — the idea that space resources belong to everyone, not just whoever gets there first with a robot. But the extraction regime seems to presuppose exactly the opposite: a first-come-first-served, labor-based theory of property that's way closer to Locke than to any kind of cosmopolitan distributive justice. And what's weird is that nobody seems to think these two things are in tension. The same treaty that says space is for all mankind is being interpreted to let SpaceX keep all the platinum from an asteroid.

So here's what I want to know: what theory (or theories) of property and justice does the current international space regime actually presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view. Is it coherent, or is it basically a fudge — high-minded language on top and a resource grab underneath? And what would actually have to change if you took the "common heritage" language seriously and replaced the implicit Lockean theory with something more egalitarian? Would you need something like a global tax on space mining revenues, or a licensing regime where companies pay into a fund that gets distributed to all countries? Or is there a way to make the Lockean extraction view and the common heritage view hang together that I'm just not seeing?
