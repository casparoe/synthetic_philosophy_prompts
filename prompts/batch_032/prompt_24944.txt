I've spent enough time with the literature to know the rough shape of the debates: there's the dispute between those who think shared intention reduces to interlocking individual intentions plus common knowledge and those who think joint commitment or "we-mode" attitudes are irreducible; there are questions about whether groups can be agents in their own right or whether that's always metaphorical; there's work on joint attention and its developmental role; and there's a growing empirical literature on the psychology of cooperation. But I'm not an academic philosopher, and I need to decide where to direct my remaining reporting time and which conceptual threads to foreground in the piece.

What I'd like from you is a prioritization of the open questions in collective intentionality and joint action — not a survey, but a defended agenda. Is it that the question is upstream of many others, so understanding it unlocks the rest of the terrain? Is it that rival answers track deep but rarely articulated differences in how people actually experience joint activity? For each question you prioritize, tell me what hangs on it — what else we can't settle until we settle this — and what a satisfying answer would have to look like.
