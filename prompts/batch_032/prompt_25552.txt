A colleague is considering an experimental procedure that would replace each of his neurons, one at a time, with functionally identical synthetic neurons over the course of a year. He dismisses worries about whether he would survive the process by appealing to the Ship of Theseus: just as a ship remains the same ship when its planks are replaced gradually, he would remain the same person. I suspect the analogy is doing more argumentative work than it can bear. Audit the analogy in detail: lay out what corresponds to what, identify where the mapping breaks, flag which features of the ship case the analogy quietly imports that may not hold for persons, and assess whether his conclusion — that gradual replacement preserves personal identity — survives once the disanalogies are on the table. Also, identify the assumptions your analysis depends on most heavily and explain how your assessment would change if those assumptions were dropped.
