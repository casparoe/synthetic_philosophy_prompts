I keep noticing a pattern in how people make decisions when the odds aren't fully spelled out, and I'm trying to figure out whether this thing has a name. It's not just risk aversion. A colleague was picking between two funds. Fund X: "historically 6% annually, SD 4%." Fund Y: "we target 6% but we're too new for historical stats." Same expected return, but he wouldn't touch Fund Y. My sister chose a vacation rental with 40 reviews over one with zero reviews and better photos. I do this too — I'll drive a known route with predictable traffic over a new one my GPS says is faster, because I don't trust the estimate. In each case the avoided option doesn't have worse odds in any calculable sense; the odds are just unknown. I've heard "uncertainty aversion" but I don't know if that's the technical term or if it's even considered distinct from risk aversion. Is there an established name for this, and more importantly, how do you carve the boundary between this and regular risk aversion? And is it considered a bias or a reasonable response to missing information?
