Ive been following the negative utilitarianism vs critics debate for a couple years now and it feels totally stuck. The same loop plays out every time: critics say if you make suffering reduction lexically prior to happiness, logic pushes you to antinatalism or even to ending all sentient life, since life always contains some suffering. Defenders say thats clearly absurd, so there has to be a principled refinement that blocks it—maybe a threshold below which suffering doesnt count, or an asymmetry between not creating new sufferers and failing to eliminate existing ones, or a claim that nonexistence isnt a state you can compare to existence for the person involved. Critics fire back that these moves are ad hoc and, when you push on them, either collapse back into classical utilitarianism or smuggle in deontological machinery the view was supposed to avoid. Everyone agrees on the empirical facts, everyone agrees suffering is really bad. Nobody budges.

What kind of disagreement is this, actually? I have a couple hypotheses. One is that its just a clash of bedrock intuitions—"better never to have been" vs "existence is worth it"—and those arent the kind of thing you can argue someone into or out of. Another is that its a methodological dispute about what counts as a refutation in normative ethics: are counterintuitive implications a reason to abandon a view, or a reason to revise the intuition instead? A third is that its near a verbal dispute, where "suffering reduction has priority" cascades into different concrete commitments depending on what else youre implicitly assuming about population axiology, and nobody bothers to make those background assumptions explicit.

Id love a diagnosis that engages the actual structure of the impasse, not just a rehash of the moves. And if possible, a sense of what would actually count as progress—a distinction both sides could accept, a case that forces movement, or maybe an honest argument that no progress should be expected because the disagreement lives at the level of a fundamental normative orientation.
