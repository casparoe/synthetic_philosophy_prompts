After forty years in experimental physics, I retired last year and have been working through the contemporary metaphysics literature on dispositions. The fink/mask distinction is especially intriguing to me: a fink removes a disposition when its stimulus condition occurs, while a mask leaves the disposition intact but blocks its manifestation. I can follow the standard illustrations, but when I try to map the distinction onto cases from my own scientific experience, I find the boundary surprisingly elusive.

Please provide exactly five examples, drawn from any of the natural sciences, where it is genuinely ambiguous whether a factor is finking a disposition or merely masking it. For each example, state the disposition, describe the interfering factor, and explain in one or two sentences why the classification is unclear. Present these as a bare numbered list — no introduction, no conclusion, just the five items.
