Last week he used it to argue that grief over a death is irrational once the funeral is over, because the person's nonexistence is now just the same kind of nonexistence as their nonexistence before birth — the past isn't real, so what exactly are you grieving? The professor mostly lets it slide, which makes me think I'm missing something, but I can't shake the feeling that the slogan is being asked to carry weight it can't bear. I want to walk into the next session with a clear map of what "only the present is real" could actually mean — the distinct metaphysical claims it might be expressing, from the ones that are nearly tautological to the ones that are radical and revisionary. For each reading, tell me whether it's defensible, whether it actually entails the conclusions he's drawing about ethics and emotion, and what further commitments it brings along that he might not have noticed. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
