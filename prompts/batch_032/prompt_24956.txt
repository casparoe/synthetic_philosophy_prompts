I'm a CS PhD student working on reward modeling and alignment, and I keep running into the same problem in different clothes: you pick a metric, it works fine for a while, then the system (or the users, or the RL agent) finds a way to hit the metric while completely undermining what you actually cared about. Everyone nods and says "Goodhart's law" but honestly it feels like we don't have a real *theory* here — just a pile of case studies and folk wisdom. Before I sink more time into the literature I want to nail down what a theory of specification gaming is even supposed to do. What are the desiderata? What cases does it have to get right, what distinctions can't it collapse (e.g., between gaming the metric and genuinely improving on the target construct), what empirical regularities does it need to explain — like why some metrics resist corruption way longer than others, or why a metric that was fine suddenly breaks? And are there any pairs of desiderata that seem jointly unsatisfiable, like you probably can't have a theory that's both fully general across domains *and* makes sharp predictions about when gaming will occur? I'm less interested in a scorecard of existing approaches and more in the criteria themselves — I want to know what success looks like before I start grading candidates.
