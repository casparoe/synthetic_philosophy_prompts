I have a friend who, over the past few years, has become someone I can only describe as relentlessly good. She volunteers every weekend, donates a large fraction of her income, has gone vegan, reads to the blind, writes letters to prisoners, and seems to have no hobbies that aren't also morally improving in some way. She isn't sanctimonious about it — she's genuinely warm, never lectures anyone, and would be the first to say she's just doing what seems obviously required. And yet being around her makes me feel exhausted and somehow inadequate. I find myself avoiding her, and I feel guilty about it.

A mutual friend told me I'm just uncomfortable because her example exposes my own moral mediocrity, and that the right response is to admire her and try to be more like her. But something about that diagnosis doesn't sit right with me. It isn't just that I feel bad about myself — it's that I'm not sure I would want to be her, even if I could. Her life seems narrow. She doesn't seem to leave room for things that aren't morally serious: she doesn't read novels unless they're edifying, she doesn't follow sports or gossip or go dancing, and she laughs less than she used to. I'm not sure she's happier, and I'm not sure I'd find her life a good human life, however good a moral life it is.

Is there a case to be made that there's something genuinely unappealing — not just threatening to my self-image — about a life devoted single-mindedly to moral goodness? Or am I just rationalizing my own complacency?
