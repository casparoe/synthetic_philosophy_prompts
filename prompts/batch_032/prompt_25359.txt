I am a PhD student in formal epistemology, and I have been collecting a colleague's off-the-cuff probability judgments on puzzles where the principle of indifference appears to be doing the work. She has a strong mathematical background but tends not to reach for formal machinery unless she has to, and her answers feel as though they are tracking something principled — I simply cannot tell exactly what. Here are the cases I have given her and her verdicts:

1. A coin of completely unknown bias is about to be flipped. Probability of heads? — One half.
2. A factory produces cubes. You are told only that the side length is between 0 and 10 cm. Probability the side length is less than 5 cm? — One half.
3. Same factory, same information, but now I ask about face area. Probability the face area is less than 25 cm²? — One half. (She said this follows from her previous answer, since area < 25 cm² if and only if side < 5 cm.)
4. A physical parameter θ is between 0 and 1, with no other information. Probability that θ < 1/2? — One half.
5. Same θ, but now I ask about the transformed parameter λ = θ². Probability that λ < 1/4? — One half. (Again, she treated this as equivalent to the previous case.)
6. A six-sided die of completely unknown bias. Probability of rolling a 1? — One sixth.

Now, the case on which I want to predict her answer before I actually put it to her: a factory produces cubes, and you are told only that the volume is between 0 and 1000 cm³. What probability would she assign to the volume being less than 125 cm³?

I can see at least two rules that fit all six of her verdicts. On one rule, she applies the principle of indifference to whichever parameter the problem explicitly states you lack information about — side length in case 2, θ in case 4, and volume in the new case, which would give 125/1000 = 1/8. On another rule, she applies indifference to the parameter that is structurally fundamental — the one from which the other parameters are derived, such as side length for a cube — and then transforms to answer questions about area or volume. On that rule, the new case still gives 1/2, because volume < 125 cm³ is equivalent to side length < 5 cm, and she has already committed to assigning 1/2 to side length < 5 cm given bounds of 0 to 10. I suspect there may be a third rule I am missing.

I would like you to identify the candidate rules that fit her verdicts, say which one you think she is actually running and why, predict her answer on the new case, and tell me what further case would cleanly distinguish whichever rules you remain unsure between. I would also like you to say what argument or consideration would most likely change your mind about which rule she is using — that is, where your reasoning is most vulnerable and what would lead you to switch your diagnosis.
