I am organizing an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers who have been arguing past each other for years about Sartre's account of radical freedom and bad faith. One defends the orthodox Sartrean position: consciousness is a nothingness that transcends all facticity, so we are always radically free — no situation, however constrained, can determine our choice, and bad faith names our fundamental strategy for fleeing this freedom by pretending we are things. The other argues that this picture is untenable: facticity — embodiment, trauma, material deprivation, social position, internalized oppression — can genuinely foreclose possibilities in ways that make talk of "radical freedom" not just empirically false but ethically pernicious, and the concept of bad faith gets stretched into blaming victims for their own unfreedom.

Please draft the joint statement they would produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want the following structure:

(1) A numbered list of claims both can sign — about the structure of consciousness, about what facticity includes, about what bad faith is and is not, about what Sartre's texts actually say, and about what a satisfactory account of agency must explain.

(2) A disagreements table with three columns: the precise point of disagreement, the Sartrean's formulation of her own view, and the critic's formulation of his own view — each phrased so its own side would accept the wording.

(3) A list of cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, describe a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record what each side predicts about that case or argument.

(4) An honest assessment of whether any part of their dispute is merely verbal. If so, identify it and explain why; if not, explain why the disagreement survives even after terminological clarification.

The goal is a document that makes the residual disagreement exact and that neither party would disown.
