In a course on early modern philosophy, our professor summarized Montaigne's essay "Of Cannibals" roughly as follows: "Montaigne argues that we have no objective standard by which to judge other cultures, so calling the Brazilian Tupinambá 'barbarous' for practicing cannibalism is just European prejudice. He points out that the Tupinambá only eat their enemies after honorable combat and as a ritual of respect, whereas Europeans torture and burn people alive over religious disagreements — which, Montaigne implies, is far worse. The essay is essentially an early statement of cultural relativism: Montaigne's conclusion is that no culture's practices are better or worse than any other's, and that morality is just a matter of local custom."

I've since read the essay myself, and something about this summary feels off — not entirely wrong, but it seems to flatten Montaigne's position in a way that makes it easier to dismiss. I'm not sure I can articulate what the problem is, though. Can you go through this summary and tell me what a careful reader of Montaigne would accept, what they'd push back on, and whether the label "cultural relativism" fits the essay he actually wrote?
