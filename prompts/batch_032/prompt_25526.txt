I'm a PhD student in political science and I sit on my department's graduate admissions committee. We currently decide admissions by having each member read a file and vote "admit" or "reject," with simple majority determining the outcome. Two committee members are now pushing to replace this with a weighted-scoring system: each applicant gets numerical ratings on several dimensions (research promise, writing, fit, etc.), each reviewer weights those dimensions by self-assessed expertise, and a weighted sum produces a final score. They argue that a binary vote discards information and falsely assumes all voters are equally competent. The other side invokes the Condorcet jury theorem: if each of us is more likely than not to judge an applicant correctly, majority rule maximizes the probability of a correct decision, and adding weights could make things worse if we misjudge our own expertise. The chair has asked me to draft a short memo for the committee recommending a concrete course of action — keep majority vote, adopt the weighted system, or propose a third option — with reasons that engage the Condorcet jury theorem seriously, including an honest assessment of whether its assumptions hold in our context and a fair treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side.
