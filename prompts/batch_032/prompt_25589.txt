So I recently got roped into helping moderate my housing co-op's internal forum, and the paradox of tolerance has gone from something I vaguely remembered from undergrad to something I think about literally every day. We've got this one guy who keeps posting stuff that's... not quite hate speech but definitely pushing it, and half the house wants him banned and the other half says that's exactly how the tolerant become intolerant themselves.

But here's what's bugging me: I started reading up on this again, and it seems like the debate about the paradox of tolerance — Popper's idea that if you're unlimitedly tolerant, the intolerant will eventually destroy tolerance itself — has been basically frozen for decades. Everyone knows the moves. One side says you have to suppress intolerance to preserve a tolerant society. The other side says that once you start suppressing speech you disagree with, you've already lost, and besides, who gets to decide what counts as "intolerant"? And then the first side says "come on, we can tell the difference between genuine intolerance and mere disagreement," and the second side says "that's what every censor in history has said," and round and round it goes.

What I want to know is: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it a disagreement about emperical facts — like, would suppressing intolerant speech actually work to preserve tolerance, or does it backfire? Is it a disagreement about values — do the two sides just weigh liberty and security differently? Is it a disagreement about who to trust with power? Or is it something deeper, like a disagreement about what tolerance even means — is tolerance a social contract that the intolerant have voided, or is it a principle you have to apply universally or else it's not really tolerance?

And given your diagnosis: is there anything that could actually move this debate? An empirical study that both sides would accept? A conceptual distinction that cuts the knot? Or is this just one of those things where people's temperaments determine their answer and no amount of argument will change it?

I guess I'm less interested in hearing which side is right and more interested in understanding why smart, well-meaning people who all want a tolerant society can look at the same problem and end up in completely different places, and whether there's any way out of that.
