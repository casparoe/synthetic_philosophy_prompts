I've been reading around in metaphysics lately, specifically about the unity of the proposition — the problem Bradley raised back in the 1890s about how the constituents of a proposition or a fact hang together. The setup is familiar enough: if you've got a particular a and a universal F, the proposition that a is F isn't just the set {a, F-ness} or a bare list; something has to bind them into a unity. But if you posit a relation of instantiation to do the binding, then you need a further relation to bind that relation to a and to F-ness, and off you go into the regress. So the options seem to be: (1) instantiation is a special kind of non-relational tie that doesn't need further binding, (2) facts or states of affairs are primitive unities that don't decompose into constituents needing glue, or (3) there's some kind of ontological dependence or grounding relation that does the work without generating the regress. And there are more variations, some of them quite subtle.

What's driving me a bit crazy is that this debate seems to have been running for over a century — from Bradley through Russell, Armstrong, Vallicella, and plenty of people working on it today — and I can't tell whether anyone has actually made progress. Every proposal seems to get the reply that the regress just reappers in a new guise, and every attempt to stop the regress gets accused of being ad hoc or mysterious in its own way. My hunch is that the disagreement isn't really about the structure of propositions at all, but about what counts as an acceptable explanation in metaphysics: one side thinks you need to analyze unity into further constituents and relations, and the other side thinks that at some point you have to accept a primitive unifier and the only question is where to stop. If that's right, then the impasse is about explanatory standards, not about the world — it's a clash of methodological temperaments dressed up as ontology.

I'd like you to diagnose this. Is my hunch on the right track, or is there a genuine factual or formal disagreement buried in here that I'm missing? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a formal result, a new distinction, a reframing of the question, or should we just accept that this is one of those problems where the arguments are in and nobody's going to be persuaded?
