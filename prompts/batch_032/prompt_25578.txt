I'm a PhD student working on RLHF for language models and there's something that's been nagging at me. The whole setup assumes you can train a reward model that captures human preferences, and then optimize a policy against it. But here's the thing: the human labelers are rating outputs that come from whatever policy we had at the time. As training goes on, the policy changes, so the distribution of outputs the labelers see changes too. The reward model isn't learning some fixed "human values" — it's learning to rate outputs from a particular distribution, and then we use that same reward model to train a policy that produces a *different* distribution. It feels like the agent and the evaluator are tangled up in a way the theory doesn't acknowledge. My advisor says this is just standard distribution shift and it works fine empirically, but I feel like there's a conceptual problem here about whether you can cleanly separate the thing being optimized from the thing doing the evaluating when they're both evolving inside the same loop. Is this a real philosophical issue or just a practical nuisance? And if it is a real issue, what's the right way to think about it? Also — please separate what's actually settled from what people in embedded agency and AI alignment still genuinely disagree about. I don't want to go around thinking something is the consensus when it's actually contested.
