Hey, so I'm a backend engineer at a mid-size startup and somehow ended up on our 'People & Culture' committee (long story, I think I missed a meeting and got volun-told). We have this thing called the 'Living Our Values' award that goes to one employee each quarter — comes with a bonus, public recognition, the whole deal.

Here's the situation. We have this engineer, let's call him Dan. Dan is, by all accounts, a genuinely *good* person. Like, almost unsettlingly good. He reviews every PR within hours even at 11pm, he volunteers for all the on-call shifts nobody wants, he mentors junior devs on weekends, he once spent his entire sprint fixing tech debt someone else created and never complained. He never takes credit for anything. When a project he led shipped, he made sure everyone elses name was on the announcement and left his off. People on other teams come to him with problems because they know he'll drop everything to help.

The committee has to decide whether to give him the award this quarter. And here's where it gets weird: about half the committee is uncomfortable with it. Not because they think Dan is fake — everyone agrees he's sincere. The discomfort is more like... some people feel his example is actually kind of corrosive. One person said 'Dan makes everyone else feel like a selfish asshole just for having boundries.' Another pointed out that his refusal to ever self-promote means his manager has no ammunition to argue for his promotion, so he's actually been passed over twice while less competent but more visible people got promoted. A third person said his constant availability has normalized an unhealthy culture where people feel guilty logging off at 5pm. The phrase 'toxic virtue' got thrown around (and then immediately retracted because it sounded awful).

On the other side, people are saying: the award is literally for living our values, Dan is clearly doing that more than anyone, and if we're seriously considering *not* giving it to him because he's *too* good, then our values are just decorative words on the wall and we should be honest about that.

I need to draft a short recommendation memo for the committee — we vote next Tuesday. The memo should recommend whether to give Dan the award or not (or some third option I haven't thought of), with reasoning that engages seriously with both sides. Also, I get the sense this touches on some deeper philosophical question about whether being maximally morally good is actually desirable or healthy — if there's a real debate about that among people who've thought about it carefully, please flag what's settled vs. what's genuinely contested, so I don't accidentally present one side as obvious consensus. Think very carefully before answering and respond in the style of an expert — I need this to hold up when people push back.
