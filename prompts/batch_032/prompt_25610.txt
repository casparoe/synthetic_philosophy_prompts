For a foundations-of-physics seminar, we want to write down the full specification: what must an interpretation of quantum mechanics actually deliver to count as adequate, given what we now know from Bell's theorem and its experimental tests? I want a list of desiderata — the phenomena an interpretation must recover, the constraints it must respect, the conceptual problems it must dissolve — with the case behind each requirement. Crucially, I want to know which subsets of these requirements are jointly unsatisfiable because of Bell's theorem, the Kochen-Specker theorem, the PBR theorem, or other no-go results. If no interpretation can simultaneously satisfy locality, determinism, and empirical equivalence, say so explicitly and say which theorem forces the tradeoff.

For each requirement, indicate whether it is non-negotiable (any adequate interpretation must meet it), or a theoretical virtue that some interpretations dispense with — and at what cost. Mark any requirement that is contested: that significant physicists or philosophers would deny is a genuine desideratum at all, and give their reason.

Structure it as a numbered list with motivation for each requirement, then a section on joint (un)satisfiability, then a brief scorecard for the major families (Copenhagen, many-worlds/Everett, de Broglie-Bohm/pilot-wave, GRW/objective collapse, QBism, superdeterminism, and any others you think the list illuminates). Keep the weight on the criteria themselves and their motivating cases — the scorecard is there only to show the list doing work.
