Please argue that the all-affected principle — according to which anyone whose interests are affected by a political decision ought to have a say in making it — is untenable as a criterion for constituting the demos, and that the all-subjected principle (which restricts the demos to those subject to the coercive enforcement of a polity's laws) offers a more defensible alternative. Your argument should address the familiar regress objection: determining who is affected by a decision about who gets to decide seems to presuppose an answer to the very boundary question we are trying to resolve. Consider, however, whether the all-subjected principle faces a structurally analogous difficulty, and if so, whether it handles that difficulty any better than the all-affected principle does.
