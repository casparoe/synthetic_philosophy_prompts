Robert Nozick's experience machine thought experiment appears to demonstrate quite decisively that hedonism about well-being is false. After all, almost everyone who encounters the scenario reports that they would not plug into the machine, even when it is stipulated that the simulated experiences would be more pleasurable than their actual lives. This seems like a straightforward empirical refutation of the claim that pleasure is the sole intrinsic good. Why, then, do a number of contemporary philosophers continue to defend hedonistic theories of well-being? Is there some flaw in the thought experiment that I am overlooking, or is this simply a case of philosophers being unwilling to abandon a cherished theory in the face of counterevidence?
