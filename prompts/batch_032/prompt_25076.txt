I've been following the illusionism debate in philosophy of mind for a few years now — the one between illusionists (Frankish, Dennett, Graziano, that crowd) and realists about phenomenal consciousness — and I genuinely can't tell anymore what's actually being fought over. Both sides agree on the cognitive science: the brain does a bunch of processing, we have introspective reports, there's a "what-it's-like" seeming. The illusionist says that seeming is itself a misrepresentation — there's no actual phenomenal property, just the representation of one. The realist says no, the seeming is the thing itself, and you can't explain away the thing by redescribing the mechanism that generates the report of the thing.

What's weird to me is that neither side seems to have moved in like 15 years. The arguments are all meta-arguments now: illusionists say realists are just insisting on a pre-theoretic intuition and refusing to let science touch it; realists say illusionists are trying to define the problem out of existence and then declaring victory. It feels like the disagreement has become about what counts as an explanation of consciousness, not about consciousness itself. Or maybe it's about whether first-person phenomenology is data that can't be revised, or about whether "phenomenal" even picks out a coherent property in the first place.

I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it verbal — are they using "consciousness" and "phenomenal" to mean different things? Is it a clash of bedrock intuitions where neither side has arguments that could convince the other? Is there a hidden empirical question that would settle it if we could answer it? Or is the whole framing of the debate — "is phenomenal consciousness real or an illusion?" — somehow ill-posed?

And given your diagnosis: is there anything that would actually move this debate, or should we expect it to just keep circling forever? If there is something that would move it, be specific — don't just say "more science" or "better arguments," tell me what kind of thing would actually shift someone from one camp to the other.
