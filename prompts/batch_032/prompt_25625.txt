I'm preparing a paper for a philosophy of mind seminar on the epistemology of introspection, and I've been trying to nail down my own position before I start writing. The seminar has covered everything from Descartes's transparency-of-thought doctrine to Schwitzgebel's skeptical challenges, and I find myself pulled in several directions at once. I've written out the five commitments that currently seem right to me, but I'm worried they might not all fit together, and I'd rather discover the tensions now than have them pointed out in the Q&A.

Here's where I stand:

(1) Introspection is our primary and generally reliable source of knowledge about our own conscious experiences — without it, we would have no epistemic grip on the felt quality of our mental lives.

(2) The apparent directness and effortlessness of introspection counts as at least some evidence of its reliability. If introspection were systematically misleading, it is hard to see why it would feel so transparent and automatic rather than laborious or uncertain.

(3) The empirical record — Nisbett and Wilson on our inability to accurately report the causes of our own judgments, change blindness, choice blindness — shows that we are frequently and dramatically wrong about our own mental processes and even about what we are consciously experiencing.

(4) The Cartesian picture of introspection as an infallible inner eye scanning a private theater is dead; introspection is a fallible, constructive, and sometimes confabulatory process.

(5) Despite all this, introspection retains a kind of first-person authority that no third-person method can match. My sincere introspective report that I am in pain right now carries a default warrant that no amount of brain-scanning or behavior-watching by an outsider could override.

I would like you to go through these five commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. Please rank the tensions by severity, and for each genuine tension, identify which commitment would have to be revised or abandoned to resolve it and at what philosophical cost. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to clean up the overall picture.

Please format your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
