I've been chewing on the absent-minded driver problem lately and trying to figure out what's actually worth a serious time investment here. The setup is simple enough — you're driving home, two identical-looking intersections, at each one you have to choose whether to exit or continue, but when you're at an intersection you don't know which one it is — and yet it feels like it punches way above its weight as a toy case for self-locating uncertainty and the whole precommitment-vs.-action tangle.

If someone had, say, two years to go deep on the cluster of issues around this problem, what should they actually work on? I'm thinking about questions like: does the paradox genuinely force an updateless decision theory, or is that reading too much into it? What's the real relationship to Sleeping Beauty — are these two faces of the same thing or is that a superficial analogy? Is the core tension really about planning-stage rationality versus action-stage rationality, and if so, does resolving that tension have payoffs elsewhere in decision theory? Or does the whole thing just dissolve once you're sufficiently careful about the formalism, and people have been chasing a mirage?

I want a defended prioritization, not a survey. Which questions are upstream of others, which are tractable enough that someone could actually crack them in a couple years, and which are dead ends or mostly terminological disputes? And I'd like to know what evidence would reshuffle your ranking — e.g., if someone showed that the absent-minded driver and Sleeping Beauty are isomorphic in some precise sense, does that make some other question more or less urgent, or does it just collapse two entries on the list into one?
