I am a novelist, and I have been struggling with a conceptual problem that keeps surfacing in my fiction. I wonder whether philosophy has a name for it, or at least a well-mapped set of distinctions.

The problem concerns characters who bring about outcomes by not doing something. In my last novel, a father learns that his son is being bullied at school but says nothing to the school, to his wife, or to his son — he simply does not act. The bullying escalates, and the son is seriously harmed. In workshop, half my readers said the father caused the harm through his silence, and the other half said he merely allowed it, and that the bullies alone caused it. I noticed that the disagreement did not track any obvious factual dispute: everyone agreed on what the father knew, what he could have done, and what happened next. They disagreed about whether his silence was a cause.

A similar thing happened with a different manuscript. A woman discovers that her employer is defrauding investors. She resigns quietly, telling no one. The fraud continues for years. Some readers treated her as complicit; others said she had no causal role at all — she simply left. Again, the facts were not in dispute.

I started keeping notes on these cases, and I noticed a pattern. When a character has a socially recognized role — parent, doctor, officer — readers readily treat an omission as causal. When the role is thinner — stranger, former employee — they resist. But the pattern is not stable. If I write the stranger's inner life in enough detail, showing them deliberating about whether to speak and deciding against it, readers begin to treat the omission as a cause even without a role. It is as though rendering the omission psychologically vivid turns it into something more like an act. Conversely, if I give a parent an inner life so chaotic or dissociated that the omission feels like something that simply befell them, readers stop treating it as causal.

I am not asking for advice about how to write these scenes. What I want to know is whether philosophy has isolated and named this phenomenon: the way an omission's status as a cause seems to depend on narrative features — role, deliberateness, the richness of the inner life depicted — rather than on the kind of counterfactual structure that philosophers usually talk about. Is there an established concept for this, and if not, what would you call it, and how exactly does it relate to the nearest philosophical distinctions?
