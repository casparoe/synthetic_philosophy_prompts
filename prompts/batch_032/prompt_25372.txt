Compare the internalist and externalist positions in the debate over normative reasons for action, focusing on the following dialectical asymmetry. Internalists, following Bernard Williams, argue that externalists face an insuperable explanatory burden: they cannot account for how a reason that fails to connect to any element of an agent's subjective motivational set could genuinely be a reason for that agent. Externalists, in turn, argue that internalism cannot accommodate the categorical character of moral reasons — reasons that seem to apply to agents irrespective of their contingent desires or projects. Your task is to assess whether either side has a decisive response to the other's core challenge. In doing so, consider in particular whether the internalist can deploy the notion of a sound deliberative route (from existing motivations, via correct reasoning and full information, to new motivations) to show that moral reasons are internal after all, or whether this move effectively concedes the externalist's point by idealizing the agent's psychology beyond recognition. Conclude by defending a position on which side has the stronger case, and explain your reasoning.
