I recently read a popular neuroscience book that summarized Benjamin Libet's experiments on free will. The author wrote something to this effect:

Libet's experiments in the 1980s showed that when people decide to move their hand, a burst of brain activity called the readiness potential appears about half a second before they become consciously aware of deciding. This means your brain has already made the decision before you know about it. Libet himself acknowledged that people can still veto the movement in the final fraction of a second, but most neuroscientists see this veto as just another unconscious process, so it doesn't rescue free will. The upshot is that conscious free will is an illusion: your feeling of deciding is just a story your brain tells itself after the fact.

I found this striking, but I've since come across people online saying that popular accounts of Libet get important things wrong. I don't have the background to judge. Could you go through this summary and tell me what a neuroscientist or philosopher working on free will would accept, what they'd push back on, and whether the conclusion about free will being an illusion actually follows from the parts that are accurate?
