I'm taking this astrobiology seminar and we've been going in circles about what "life" even means, and it's driving me kind of nuts. The textbook gives the standard NASA-style checklist — metabolism, growth, reproduction, response to stimuli, evolution — but then every single week someone brings up a counterexample that breaks it. Viruses don't metabolize. Mules don't reproduce. A frozen tardigrade does basically nothing on the list but nobody wants to call it dead. And then there's the really weird stuff like prions, transposons, and those synthetic minimal cells the Venter group made that have like 473 genes and can divide but arguably aren't "alive" in any rich sense. Plus the origin-of-life people need a definition that can handle the transition from chemistry to biology, which means it can't presuppose the very thing your trying to define.

So before I read yet another paper proposing a definition, I want to know what I should actually be asking of one. Write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory or definition of life has to deliver. Which cases must it get right (the clear positives, the clear negatives, the borderline ones), which distinctions must it respect (alive vs. dead vs. never-was-alive vs. dormant vs. having-the-capacity-for-life), and which results from biology and biochemistry can't it contradict? I also want to know which of these requirements are non-negotiable and which are more like preferences of particular camps — the astrobiologists want something operational you could run on a Mars rover, the philosophers of biology care more about conceptual coherence, the origin-of-life chemists want it to not presuppose evolution. And here's the thing I'm most curious about: are there pairs of desiderata that can't be jointly satisfied? Like, maybe you can't have a definition that's both operational and handles all the edge cases, or maybe being non-Earth-centric and being grounded in actual terrestrial biochemistry pull in opposite directions. If some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, that's basically a result in itself and I want that flagged. Also, if you had to pick the single requirement that does the most work eliminating candidate definitions, which one is it?
