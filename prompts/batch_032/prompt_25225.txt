ok so i keep seeing people argue about whether X is a disease—addiction, obesity, ADHD, long covid, whatever—and the argument is always just people trading intuitions about edge cases while gesturing vaguely at "dopamine" or "brain scans" or "but people choose to." nobody actually says what they're trying to do with the concept of disease in the first place.

before i read another paper by boorse or wakefield or whoever, i want someone to write me the spec. like: if you were commissioning a theory of health and disease, what does it actually need to deliver? what's the requirements doc?

things i care about:
- what cases does it absolutely have to get right, and why those?
- what distinctions can't it collapse? e.g., the distinction between "this is a disease" and "this is bad but not a disease" seems load-bearing in medicine and law and insurance and ordinary life, so the theory can't just say disease = undesirable bodily state.
- which empirical facts (evolutionary, statistical, physiological) does it have to be consistent with? if the theory says something that contradicts how natural selection actually works, that's a problem.
- does the theory need to unify physical and mental disorders, or is it allowed to treat them as different kinds of things with a family resemblance?
- what about conditions that are statistically typical but clearly dysfunctional in some sense (like dental caries in populations without dentistry, or presbyopia in aging populations)? and the reverse: statistically rare but totally fine (like having an extra spleen or whatever)?
- how much should it care about the fact that disease categories shift historically? like, is "homosexuality used to be a disease and now it's not" a problem the theory has to dissolve, or is that just sociology and the theory can ignore it?

and then the part i actually want: once the list is on the table, which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of the naturalist camp vs. the normativist camp? are any pairs of requirements jointly unsatisfiable? which single requirement does the most work in elimating popular candidates?

basically i want to stop having the object-level argument and first figure out what we're even arguing about.
