I've spent years reading EA and rationalist blogs, and I've internalized a broadly consequentialist approach to ethics — the kind where you're supposed to weigh interests impartially and follow the numbers. But something happened recently that's made me doubt whether I can actually live that way, or even whether I should.

My spouse was diagnosed with a condition requiring urgent and expensive treatment. I had savings that exactly covered it, and I spent them without a second thought. A few weeks later, at an EA meetup, someone asked whether I'd considered directing that money to a charity that could have saved several lives instead. The question caught me off guard, not because I had a counterargument, but because the possibility hadn't even crossed my mind. It wasn't that I considered the tradeoff and chose my spouse — the tradeoff simply didn't appear on my mental horizon.

Here's what's bothering me. The standard consequentialist line would say I made a mistake by failing to deliberate impartially. But when I try to imagine the version of myself who, upon hearing my spouse's diagnosis, calmly weighed her welfare against the welfare of strangers before acting — that person strikes me as having a defective relationship with his own wife. There's something wrong with a husband whose love is compatible with running that calculation at that moment. The very act of impartial deliberation would itself have been a kind of moral failure — a failure to be properly related to one's own deepest commitments.

I'm not looking for permission or reassurance. I want to know whether there's a rigorous philosophical case that my failure to deliberate was not just understandable but actually the correct response — that impartial calculation would itself have been the moral error. And if such a case exists, what are its limits? Surely I can't always just favor my spouse no matter the stakes. Where does the reasoning bottom out?

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
