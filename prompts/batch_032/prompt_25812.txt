I'm a grad student in formal epistemology and I've been tryng to nail down my views on imprecise probabilities for a paper I'm writing. I'd love a consistency check before I commit to this stuff in print — my advisor keeps telling me I'm glossing over tensions and I want to get ahead of it. Here's roughly where I am:

(1) Rational credences are sometimes best modeled by sets of probability functions rather than a single precise function — in particular, when your evidence is sparse, ambiguous, or conflicting, a credal set better reflects your epistemic position than an artificially sharp number.

(2) The right decision rule for imprecise credences is maximality: an act is permissible iff it's not strictly dominated in expected utility by another act according to every probability function in your credal set.

(3) The Principal Principle — roughly, that your credence in A given that the chance of A is x should equal x — should be retained in an imprecise framework. Giving it up is too costly.

(4) Dilation is not a serious objection to imprecise probabilism. The fact that your credal set can widen when you learn something (like the outcome of a coin toss you know nothing about) is a feature, not a bug — it correctly tracks the fact that you've learned something that reveals your prior ignorance.

(5) We should avoid "arbitrary sharpening" — picking one precise probability function from the credal set for the sake of decision-making would defeat the whole point of going imprecise.

(6) In most real-world cases, maximality plus a reasonable credal set yields a manageable set of permissible options, not the paralyzing indecision critics warn about.

I have a nagging feeling that some of these are in tension but I can't quite put my finger on where. (3) and (1) maybe? But there's an imprecise version of the Principal Principle, so maybe that's fine. And I worry that (2) and (6) might be doing more work together than they should — like I'm helping myself to an empirical claim about "most real-world cases" to avoid a structural problem with maximality. Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest fix would be. Rank the tensions by severity.
