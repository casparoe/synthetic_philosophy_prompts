I work at a small software company — about 25 people. I was hired about a year ago, so I'm one of the people with a standard benefits package. Some of the early employees (hired 5-6 years ago) negotiated really good deals — extra vacation days, a much better health plan, that kind of thing — back when the company was tiny and desperate to attract anyone. It's not that us later hires are underpaid; we're at market rate. It's just that a few early people got unusually sweet deals.

Management recently proposed "harmonizing" everyone's benefits. They can't afford to bring everyone up to the level of the best packages. So the plan is to bring the early employees' benefits *down* to match everyone else's. No one gains anything — I'd stay exactly where I am — but the early folks lose their extra vacation days and the better health plan. Management says it's the fair thing to do, that inequality itself is corrosive to workplace culture, and that a more equal workplace is better even if some people end up with less than they had before.

I should be happy about this, right? I'm not losing anything, and the workplace becomes more equal. But something about it bothers me. When someone else pushed back in a meeting, our boss said, "So you're defending a situation where some people get special treatment just because they happened to be hired at the right time?" And the guy didn't have a good response. I don't either. I do think the current inequality isn't great — it's basically luck-based — but making some people worse off when it litterally doesn't help anyone else also seems wrong. I feel like there's some kind of mistake in the reasoning but I can't put my finger on it. Am I just being inconsistent? Is there a coherent way to think about this that explains why both the current inequality *and* the proposed fix seem bad?
