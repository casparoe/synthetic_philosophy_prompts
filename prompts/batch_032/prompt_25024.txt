I'm giving a department colloquium next month defending the claim that Zhuangzi's butterfly dream passage isn't just a quaint precursor to Cartesian dream skepticism — it's doing something genuinely different, and most of the standard analytic-epistemology responses to dream skepticism don't even engage with it. My read is that Zhuangzi isn't asking "how do I know I'm not dreaming right now?" but something more like "what would it even mean for the dream/waking distinction to be a real distinction rather than just another move within one perspective?" — and that this is closer to a kind of meta-skepticism about the framework itself. The department is mostly epistemologists (contextualists, some externalists, a couple of hinge-epistemology people), plus one person who does comparative Chinese/Western philosophy and two philosophers of mind who work on perception and illusion. The epistemologists generally think Cartesian skepticism is a solved or dissolved problem.
