I've been reading a bunch of moral philosophy lately — mostly the stuff about impartiality and whether you should treat your own projects and relationships as just one more input into the universal calculus — and I keep running into this one move that I don't have a good name for, and I'm not even sure it's been carved out as its own thing.

Here's the pattern. Someone is in a situation where a deeply personal commitment pulls one way and an impartial moral requirement pulls another. They do the thing their personal commitment calls for — and it even turns out to be the right thing by impartial standards, or at least not wrong. But the way they got there matters. If they had to stop and deliberate, to run the impartial calculus before acting, something already went wrong. The mere fact that they *could* have stepped back and weighed their spouse or their lifelong project against some stranger's equal claim seems to damage the very thing that made the action valuable in the first place. It's not about getting the wrong answer; it's about what kind of person you'd have to be to treat the question as open in that way. I've seen this come up most vividly in examples where someone saves their partner from a burning building and the partner later finds out they had to *think about it* — that the thought "well, there's another person in there too, and impartially speaking..." even crossed their mind. The partner feels betrayed not by the action but by the deliberation.

I know this is in the neighborhood of alienation worries about impartial morality, and it's got something to do with the idea that some reasons shouldn't even be weighed against others — they're supposed to silence the question, not win the argument. But I'm trying to figure out whether this specific thing — where the problem isn't the outcome or even the decision procedure you settle on, but the very act of treating a particular kind of reason as something that needs to be deliberated about at all — has its own name. If it does, what are its edges? How is it different from plain old partiality, or from the idea that some goods are incommensurable, or from the complaint that consequentialism is too demanding? And if it doesn't have a settled name, what would you call it, and how would you distinguish it from those neighbouring ideas?
