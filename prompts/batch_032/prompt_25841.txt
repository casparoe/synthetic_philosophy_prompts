My friend, a clinical psychologist, has been developing an informal taxonomy of akrasia for a paper she's writing. She's shared her verdicts on several cases with me:

- A dieter who judges that she should skip dessert, sincerely endorses that judgment when the dessert menu arrives, but orders the chocolate cake anyway. Verdict: clear akrasia.
- A heroin addict who judges that using is destroying his life, genuinely wants to stop, but uses again within hours of leaving detox. Verdict: akrasia, though complicated by addiction.
- A person with OCD who judges that washing his hands for the eighth time is unnecessary, but feels an overwhelming urge and does it anyway. Verdict: not akrasia — compulsion.
- Someone who has been awake for 40 hours and makes a rash purchase they later regret, having judged beforehand that they shouldn't spend the money. Verdict: not akrasia — impaired capacity.
- Someone who, after reading Peter Singer, becomes convinced that donating 10% of her income is morally required, feels the force of the argument, but never sets up the recurring donation and eventually stops thinking about it. Verdict: she's unsure — possibly akrasia, possibly something else.

She's now considering a new case and asked for my thoughts: a graduate student with a dissertation deadline in six months. He judges, sincerely and after careful reflection, that starting work now is unambiguously best. He makes a detailed schedule. Each morning he wakes up intending to follow it, but each day he finds a reason to start tomorrow — the topic needs more reading, he works better under pressure, one more day won't matter. The pattern holds for five months, at which point he writes the dissertation in a frantic month, producing work far below what he's capable of. He describes himself as having been akratic the whole time.

I'm less interested in the bare verdict than in understanding the implicit rule or rules my friend seems to be running — what conception of akrasia makes her pattern of verdicts coherent? Are there other rules that fit her verdicts equally well? Which of those rules would classify the graduate student differently, and what further case would best distinguish them? Respond in the style of an expert in philosophy of action. Think very carefully before answering.
