I've been getting into philosophy podcasts lately — I'm a retired chemist, so this is all pretty new to me — and I just listened to one on dispositions, finks, and masks. The host gave this summary:

"Philosophers used to think you could define a disposition with a simple conditional: something is fragile just in case it would break if you dropped it. But then people noticed problems. A 'fink' is when the stimulus itself destroys the disposition before it can manifest — like a sorcerer who would make a fragile glass non-fragile the instant before it hits the ground, so it doesn't break even though it was fragile. A 'mask' is basically the same idea but from the outside: something in the environment blocks the manifestation, like packing a vase in styrofoam. The upshot is that dispositions can't be analyzed in conditional terms at all, which is why most metaphysicians now treat dispositions as primitive and unanalyzable."

Something about the way he lumped finks and masks together as "basically the same idea" bothered me — in chemistry a catalyst that gets consumed versus a reaction inhibitor are different things, and this feels analogous. But I don't know the actual literature. Could you walk me through what's accurate here and what a careful philosopher would push back on?
