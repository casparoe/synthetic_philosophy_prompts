So I just rewatched Arrival (the Villeneuve one) and I've also been working through Dennett's tower of minds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian creatures. And I can't stop thinking about how the heptapods mess with the whole scheme. Dennett's idea is that smarter creatures find ways to let hypotheses die instead of the creature: natural selection does it across generations (Darwinian), then operant conditioning does it within a lifetime (Skinnerian), then internal simulation lets you test ideas in your head (Popperian), and finally culture and language let you borrow other people's tested ideas (Gregorian). Each step is about offloading trial and error more efficiently. But the heptapods in Arrival percieve time all at once — they already know outcomes, so there's no trial and error to offload in the first place. Are they off the tower entirely? Or is there a way to read their kind of mind as a natural extension of the Gregorian stage, where the "cultural tool" is literally a different mode of temporal experience? I'm trying to write a paper on this and I'd love a reading that's faithful to both the film and Dennett — where does the heptapod mind expose a real gap in Dennett's framework, and where does Dennett's framework help us notice something the film takes for granted?
