I serve on the editorial board of a mid-tier philosophy journal. Last night I had a long argument with a friend—a colleague in my department—about whether our journal should adopt a mandatory data-sharing requirement for empirical submissions. We currently only encourage it. My friend argued that this is a textbook case of a well-intentioned reform that backfires: any journal that unilaterally imposes extra requirements loses submissions to competitors that don't, which then pressures the stricter journals to relax their standards, and the field converges on the lowest common denominator—except now the lax venues have captured more of the prestige. I pushed back, but I couldn't offer a clean way around the structural problem. The board meets next week to decide, and I need to draft a short memo recommending a concrete policy with a timeline. The memo should take my friend's collective-action worry seriously rather than dismissing it as cynicism, and it should give the board something they can actually vote on.
