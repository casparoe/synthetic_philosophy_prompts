So I've been working as a backend engineer for about six years and recently got interested in AI alignment — partly because my company is starting to integrate LLMs into our product and partly because I fell into the LessWrong rabbit hole during the pandemic like everyone else. One thing that's been bugging me is that when people talk about "the alignment problem," they seem to mean totally different things depending on who's talking.

Like, sometimes it's framed as: we need to make sure the AI does what we intend, i.e., it's a problem of accurately specifying goals and getting the system to internalize them without weird loopholes (the paperclip maximizer thing). But other times I see it framed more as: we need to make sure that even if the AI is broadly trying to do the right thing, it doesn't disempower humanity in the process of pursuing that goal — more of a coordination or power-sharing problem than a goal-specification problem. And then there's the people who talk about alignment as basically an engineering problem of making current systems not produce harmful outputs, which feels like a totally different thing.

I guess my question is: are the "goal specification" version and the "power-sharing" version actually different problems that need different solutions, or are they just two ways of describing the same underlying issue? If you had to compare them side by side — what assumptions does each framing make about the nature of intelligence or agency, and do those assumptions lead to different research priorities? I'm trying to figure out whether the field is genuinely fragmented or if I'm just not seeing the common thread yet. Also sorry if I'm butchering the terminology, I don't have any formal background in this stuff.
