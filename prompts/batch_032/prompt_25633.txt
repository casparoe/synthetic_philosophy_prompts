So I've been following the philosophical zombies debate for years now — you know, the one where Chalmers says we can conceive of a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a person that has no conscious experience, and that this shows physicalism about consciousness must be false. I've read the back-and-forth in the literature, the LessWrong sequences that touch on it, Scott Aaronson's stuff, the works. And after all this time, I've noticed something weird: the debate doesn't seem to have moved. At all. Both sides have their standard moves — the phenomenal concept strategy, the "conceivability doesn't entail possibility" line, Chalmers' two-dimensional semantics response, Dennett just denying you can really conceive of zombies, etc. — and everyone knows everyone elses moves, and yet nobody budges.

I'm starting to think this isn't really one debate. My working hypothesis is that there are actually (at least) two different questions being asked under the heading "are zombies possible?" — one about what our concepts allow us to imagine without contradiction, and another about what the actual world is like metaphysically — and the two sides are just talking past eachother. But I'm not sure that's right either, because Chalmers explicitly builds a bridge from conceivability to metaphysical possibility, and his opponents reject the bridge, so maybe it IS a single debate about whether that bridge holds.

What I want is a diagnosis. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it a verbal dispute about the word "conceivable"? A substantive disagreement about modal epistemology? A clash of bedrock intuitions where neither side can argue further? Or are physicalists and Chalmersians answering different questions under one label? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — is there an experiment, a formal result, a conceptual distinction that could move things, or should I just accept that smart people will disagree about this forever and go read something else?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give me the reasoning — no preamble, no restating the question.
