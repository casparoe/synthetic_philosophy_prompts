I've been having this recurring argument with a friend about kidney markets, and I'm starting to wonder if I'm missing something. My position is basically: look, if someone wants to sell a kidney and someone else needs one, and both parties benefit, what's the actual problem? My friend keeps saying it's "repugnant" and that some things shouldn't be for sale. But that just seems like squeamishness dressed up as philosophy. Like, some people find gay marriage repugnant too, but we don't let that stop us. Why should "this feels icky to me" carry any weight at all in policy debates about, say, legalizing organ sales or allowing price gouging after hurricanes? If the objection is really just an aesthetic reaction, shouldn't we ignore it the same way we ignore people who find interracial relationships distasteful? I feel like I must be strawmanning the position, so if there's something deeper going on with the whole repugnant markets / commodification literature that I'm not getting, please tell me — and feel free to tell me if my framing is off.
