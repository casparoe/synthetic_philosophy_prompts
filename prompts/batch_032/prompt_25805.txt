I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about aggregating tiny harms across people, and my gut keeps giving me answers that don't fit together. Here are my verdicts:

- Classic dust specks: one person tortured for 50 years vs. 3^^^3 people each getting a dust speck in the eye for a second. I'm with the intuition that the torture is worse. The specks just don't add up.
- One person tortured for a year vs. a billion people each having a really bad migraine for a full day. Here I flip: the billion migraines feel worse to me.
- One person killed vs. a trillion people each stubbing their toe. Death is worse.
- One person killed vs. enough people each losing a finger that we're talking orders of magnitude more lost fingers than there are fingers in the world. The lost fingers are worse — at some point quantity matters.
- One person getting a painful electric shock vs. 10^30 people each experiencing a hundredth of a second of barely noticeable discomfort. The shock is worse. I don't care how many near-zero bads you stack, they don't outweigh a real harm.
- And the one that keeps me up: one person tortured for 50 years vs. enough people each losing a single minute of pleasant experience that the total pleasant-time lost absolutley dwarfs the torture victim's suffering. I find myself thinking the lost minutes are worse. But I can't square that with the dust specks verdict — in both cases I'm aggregating tiny things, but in one I do it and in the other I don't.

Is there a principle that captures all or most of these? What does it say about cases I haven't thought of? And if something has to go, which verdict should I drop and why?
