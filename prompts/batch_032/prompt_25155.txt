I'm a PhD student in political theory and I keep noticing a pattern across totally different domains that I don't have a good name for, and I'm wondering if there's an established concept for it — or if not, what I should call it.

Here's the pattern. You have a bunch of actors (could be people, firms, states, whatever) who are all in some kind of competitive situation. Each actor faces a choice where, given what everyone else is doing, the individually smart move is to take some action X — invest more in something, lower your standards, cut a corner, escalate. But when everyone takes action X, the result is that everyone ends up worse off than if nobody had taken it, and often the thing they were competing over gets degraded or destroyed in the process. What makes this feel different from a standard prisoner's dilemma or tragedy of the commons is that there's often no identifiable moment of "defection" from a cooperative equilibrium — it's more like the logic of the situation itself pushes everyone step by step into a worse state, and even if everyone can see where it's heading, no one can afford to be the first to stop, because the first one to stop just gets eaten by the competition. I've seen versions of this in arms races (obviosuly), but also in things like academic publishing where everyone has to publish more and more just to stay in place and the quality of work suffers across the board, or in social media where platforms compete to be more engaging and end up making the whole information environment worse, or in things like doping in sports or cosmetic surgery norms where standards keep escalating because once enough people do it, not doing it becomes a disadvantage.

I know this is somewhere in the neighborhood of collective action problems and coordination failures, but those categories are so broad they don't really capture the specific dynamic I'm seeing — the way the competition itself has a kind of logic that makes cooperation impossible even when everyone would prefer the cooperative outcome, and the way the deterioration happens gradually through individually rational steps rather than through anyone deciding to cheat on an agreement. Is there a more precise concept for this, and if there are several related ones, how do they relate to each other? I'm less interested in getting a label than in understanding what the core mechanism actually is and where its boundaries are — what counts as an instance of this and what doesn't.
