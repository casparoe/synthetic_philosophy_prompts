so i work as a software engineer — mostly backend, some ML infrastructure lately — and about a year ago i joined this reading group at work where we discuss papers about AI safety and consciousness. it started out fun but now i'm just frustrated. the group has people who've read a ton of philosophy and people who just read blog posts (i'm somewhere in the middle), and whenever we discuss theories of consciousness — like, which computations or processes actually matter for whether something can suffer — the conversation goes absolutely nowhere.

we've gone through the usual suspects: integrated information theory says it's about the amount of integrated information a system has (phi), global workspace theory says it's about a central workspace broadcasting to specialized modules, higher-order theories say you need representations of your own mental states, attention schema theory says it's about having a model of your own attention, and then there's the computational functionalists who say the substrate is irrelevant as long as the causal structure is right. everyone has their go-to examples — the cerebellum has a ton of neurons but nobody thinks it's conscious on its own, split-brain patients seem to have two streams of consciousness, people under anesthesia lose consciousness but their brain is still doing stuff — and every theory handles these cases differently. but here's the thing: nobody in our group has changed their mind about anything in a year, and from what i can tell the academic debate is the same way. the same papers get cited, the same counterexamples get trotted out, and the whole thing just loops.

what i'm trying to figure out is what kind of disagreement this actually is, structurally. is there a hidden empirical question that would settle things if we could run the right experiment? or is it a conceptual dispute about what 'sentience' or 'consciousness' even means, and people are basically arguing past each other? or is it that different people just have different bedrock intuitions — like, some people look at IIT and think 'yeah, integrated information just IS what it feels like to be something' and other people think that's obviously wrong but can't articulate exactly why? i've also started wondering wether the whole approach of trying to reverse-engineer human consciousness and then generalizing to other systems is flawed from the start — like maybe the question 'which computations matter morally' is ill-posed in a way we haven't fully appreciated. anyway, i'd love a diagnosis of the structure of this debate — what kind of disagreement it is, whether it's verbal or substantive or something else — and an honest answer about whether anything could actually move it forward, or if we should just expect it to stay stuck forever.
