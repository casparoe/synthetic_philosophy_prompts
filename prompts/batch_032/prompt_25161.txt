My wife and I have been going back and forth about whether to have a second kid. We have a three-year-old and things are finally starting to feel manageable, but she's been reading some philosophy lately (her book club got really into ethics for some reason) and she dropped this argument on me last week that I can't stop thinking about.

Basically she said: if we have another child and they have a happy life, that's good. But if we have another child and their life turns out miserable — like if they're born with a serious condition, or just end up deeply unhappy — then we've done something genuinely terrible, because we brought suffering into the world when we didn't have to. On the other hand, if we decide not to have another kid, there's no one who exists to feel sad about missing out on a happy life. The absence of that happiness isn't a loss to anyone. So the potential downside of having the kid is huge and real, but the potential upside of not having them is... nothing, really. She said it's not just about probabilities — even if the odds of a happy life were really high, the structure of the decision is lopsided. So the cautious, ethical move is not to roll the dice.

I feel like this argument has to be wrong somehow, because if you took it seriously it seems like no one should ever have kids, which can't be right. But when I tried to push back I couldn't really find the flaw. She's not saying life is mostly suffering or anything — she loves our daughter and is genuinly happy we had her. She just thinks the logic works differently for someone who doesn't exist yet. Is there a good response to this? Or is she actually right and I just don't want to admit it?
