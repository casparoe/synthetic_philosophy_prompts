So I've been going down a rabbithole on discount rates (I work in software but got into EA stuff) and I built this argument that seems airtight but the conclusion is obviously deranged. I cannot find where it breaks and it's honestly keeping me up. Here it is:

(1) If you care less about a harm just because it happens later, you need a reason that distinguishes the later harm from the earlier one.
(2) The only candidate reasons are uncertainty (the later harm might not happen), opportunity cost (resources spent now could grow if invested), and pure time preference (you just care less about the future because it's the future).
(3) Uncertainty and opportunity cost are already handled when we compute expected value — they tell us to multiply by probabilities and growth rates, not to apply an extra discount on top.
(4) So any remaining discounting has to be pure time preference.
(5) Pure time preference says: the exact same suffering matters less the further in the future it is, solely because of its temporal coordinate.
(6) If temporal coordinate matters in this way, then spatial coordinate should matter too — by the exact same logic, suffering far away should matter less than suffering nearby.
(7) But we reject spatial discounting: a kid starving in another country matters just as much as a kid starving in your neighborhood.
(8) So we have to reject temporal discounting too, unless we can point to a relevant difference between time and space.
(9) Therefore the pure rate of time preference should be zero.
(10) With a zero discount rate, the far future dominates any calculation: even a tiny probability of influencing the long-run trajectory of civilization swamps all short-term considerations.
(11) So I should donate basically all my income to x-risk reduction, live on rice and beans, and spend my free time working on alignment.
(12) That conclusion is insane. No reasonable ethical view should demand one person sacrifice literally everything.

I have a gut feeling the symmetry argument in (6) is where it goes wrong — time and space seem different in some deep way. But every time I try to say what the difference is, I either end up letting pure time preference back in through the side door, or I say something that also applies to space (like "future people don't exist yet" — well, distant people don't exist *here* either).

Where exactly does this argument fail? If I have to give up (6), what am I signing up for, and does whatever makes time special actually justify *any* amount of pure time preference, or just zero?
