I've been following my jurisprudence professor's views closely this semester because I need to submit a paper predicting her position on a case she hasn't addressed. Here are the verdicts she's given so far on whether various norms count as law:

1. A municipal bylaw properly enacted, published, and enforced — yes, law.
2. A directive issued by a minister that was never published or made accessible to the public — no, not law.
3. A criminal statute so vaguely worded that no reasonable person could tell what conduct it prohibits — no, not law.
4. A statute enacted without the constitutionally required legislative quorum — no, not law.
5. A long-standing customary rule among merchants that courts have consistently recognized and applied for over a century — yes, law.
6. A statute requiring citizens to perform an act that is physically impossible — no, not law.

The case I need to predict: a statute duly enacted following every constitutional procedure, properly published, clear, prospective, internally consistent, and possible to comply with, that authorizes the expropriation of property from a disfavoured ethnic minority without compensation. Does she say this is law?

I am less interested in the bare yes or no than in identifying the rule or rules that fit her verdicts so far, saying what each candidate rule predicts about the new case, giving your best guess about her verdict with your reasons for preferring one rule over the others, and naming a further case that would settle which rule she is actually running.
