Here's my opening move. You grant that there are cases where conceivability doesn't entail possibility: I can conceive of water not being H₂O, but that doesn't make it possible. So you need a principled reason why the zombie case differs. The standard move appeals to primary versus secondary intensions: 'water' has distinct primary and secondary intensions, so the conceivability of water-not-being-H₂O is only an illusion of possibility about the primary intension. For 'consciousness,' you claim the two intensions coincide — phenomenal concepts are supposedly transparent, picking out the feeling itself with no hidden essence left over. But that's exactly what's in dispute. You're assuming phenomenal concepts are revelatory in a way natural-kind concepts aren't, and that assumption does all the work. Without an independent reason to think phenomenal concepts are special, the zombie case is just another Watergate. What's your independent reason?
