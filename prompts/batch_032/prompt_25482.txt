I've been reading a lot of Nietzsche lately — not just the big stuff like Zarathustra and Genealogy, but also Ecce Homo and his letters, where he goes on and on about diet, digestion, climate, and how all of it affects your thinking. The guy was weirdly obsessed with what he ate: he thought heavy German food made philosophy sluggish, he quit alcohol entirely, he had strong opinions about tea vs. coffee, and he was convinced that his stomach problems were basically the engine of his whole intellectual life.

Anyway, my friend and I got into this long argument about what Nietzsche would make of modern wellness culture if he were alive today. Like, would he be into intermittent fasting? Would he see the whole biohacking / quantified-self movement as some kind of life-affirming project or as a decadent, life-denying obsession with metrics? What about the rise of plant-based diets — he had complicated views on meat and vegetarianism that don't map neatly onto either camp, and I wonder how he'd react to the ethical veganism movement specifically. And then there's the whole anti-diet, intuitive eating, body-positivity thing — that seems like it could go either way with him, given how much he talked about saying yes to the body.

I'm writing a piece for a student philosophy journal and I want to do a speculative section on this. Can you give me a serious but creative run-down of what Nietzsche would likely think about five or six major trends in contemporary food/diet/wellness culture? Try to ground it in his actual writings and temperament, not just "Nietzsche would hate everything."
