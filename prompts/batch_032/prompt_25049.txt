I was recently appointed to my county's Vector Control Advisory Board, and I have discovered that we have no consistent framework for evaluating insecticide proposals. We receive applications ranging from mosquito fogging to prevent West Nile virus, to aerial spraying for agricultural pests, to residents wanting their yards treated for cosmetic reasons. The board members all have strong intuitions — some vote against anything that kills insects on principle, others approve nearly everything citing human interests — but we have never articulated what principles we are actually applying, and our decisions are starting to look arbitrary. I want to change that before our next quarterly meeting.

Please write a decision procedure my board could adopt for evaluating insecticide proposals. The procedure should walk us through the questions we need to ask, in order, and tell us what counts as a sufficient answer to each. It should distinguish cases where the human interest at stake is trivial from those where it is serious, and it should grapple honestly with the moral status of the insects themselves — are we treating them as having no moral weight, or as having some weight that can be outweighed, and if the latter, what kind of weighing is appropriate? I need the procedure to be usable by people without philosophical training, but I do not want it to dodge the hard questions by pretending they are easy. Flag the points where the procedure cannot eliminate judgment and a person has to make a call, and say what kind of judgment it is — a factual question we could settle with evidence, or a value question where reasonable people can disagree. Then apply the procedure to three proposals: (1) fogging a wetland to reduce the mosquito population in a county where West Nile has been detected in local birds, (2) spraying an orchard to prevent codling moth damage to a commercial apple crop, and (3) treating a golf course to eliminate grubs that are causing brown patches on the fairway. I expect the procedure will give an answer I will not like in at least one of these cases — show me where that happens and why, and tell me whether the procedure is right to give that answer or whether the procedure itself needs revision at that point.
