I've been working through an objection to luck egalitarianism that I can't shake, and I'd like help diagnosing where it goes wrong — or whether it doesn't.

(1) Luck egalitarianism says inequalities are unjust when they trace to brute luck (unchosen circumstances) but just when they trace to option luck (the outcomes of freely chosen gambles).

(2) For a gamble's outcome to count as option luck rather than brute luck, the agent's capacity to choose prudently must itself be free of brute luck. Otherwise the distinction merely relocates the luck one step back — from the outcome to the capacity that produced it.

(3) But every agent's capacity for prudent choice — their intelligence, foresight, risk tolerance, and deliberative habits — is shaped by genetics, parenting, education, and formative experiences, all paradigm cases of brute luck.

(4) Therefore no agent's capacity for choice is free of brute luck.

(5) So all apparent option luck is, on closer inspection, a downstream consequence of brute luck.

(6) If all apparent option luck reduces to brute luck, luck egalitarianism cannot justify any inequality as genuinely attributable to responsible choice.

(7) Thus luck egalitarianism collapses into strict outcome egalitarianism, demanding the elimination of all inequalities regardless of their apparent source in choice.

(8) The only way to resist the collapse is to reject (2) — to deny that the preconditions of free choice must themselves be free of brute luck. But rejecting (2) makes the brute luck / option luck distinction look arbitrary: why treat brute luck as grounds for compensation when it shows up in outcomes but not when it shows up in the very capacities that generate those outcomes?

I suspect (2) is the weak point, but I want to know whether there's a principled way to reject it, and what the view looks like afterward. If the luck egalitarian instead accepts the argument through (7), is the collapse genuinely fatal, or is there a version of luck egalitarianism that can live with the conclusion that all inequalities trace back to brute luck while still assigning responsibility a meaningful role? I'd like a map of the options and their costs.
