So I've been in this recurring argument with a friend who trades crypto (I know, I know) and I'm starting to feel like there's a real philosophical puzzle underneath the surface-level disagreement. He has this approach where he finds asymmetric bets — situations where he thinks he has a small edge, like a 55% chance of doubling his money on some altcoin move. The problem is he sizes these bets really aggressively. He'll put 30-40% of his portfolio into a single trade because, in his words, "the expected value is positive and I want to maximize returns, so why would I bet small?"

I've watched him blow up his account twice now. Each time he starts with a few thousand, runs it up to something impressive over a few months, and then one cluster of bad luck wipes him out back to basically zero. When I point this out he says "but each individual trade was +EV, and expectation is linear, so the long-run expectation is just the sum of the expectations. If I keep doing this eventually I'll be rich — the losses are just variance."

I feel in my bones that this is wrong — that there's something about the multiplicative, compounding nature of repeated bets that makes "maximize expected value" the wrong objective when your wealth grows geometrically. But every time I try to explain why, I end up saying something vague about "risk of ruin" and he counters that he's not betting literally 100%, he's leaving money on the table, so he can't go to zero. And technically he's right that his account never hits exactly zero — but going from $12,000 to $400 is ruin in every practical sense.

I half-remember some stuff from the rationalist blogosphere about geometric vs arithmetic expected value, and something about the Kelly criterion and how maximizing log-wealth is different from maximizing expected wealth, but I never really internalized the math and I can't reconstruct the argument in a way that lands. Can someone walk me through what's actually wrong with his reasoning? Like, is there a formal sense in which his strategy is irrational even though every individual bet has positive expected value? And practically, how should he be thinking about bet sizing if the goal is to actually grow wealth over time rather than just maximize the expected value of each trade?
