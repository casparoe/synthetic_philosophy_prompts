trying to figure out if my views on fiction and emotion are consistent, and i keep going back and forth. here's where i've landed:

(1) when i feel fear during a horror movie, it's genuine fear — same physiological profile, same phenomenology. calling it "quasi-fear" is just avoiding the problem.

(2) genuine fear requires a belief that the feared object poses a threat. you can't be afraid of something you're completely certain is harmless and absent.

(3) i don't believe the monster is a threat to me — not even implicitly, not even in some partitioned part of my mind. i know it's pixels on a screen.

(4) however, i do think imagining something can produce genuine emotions. if i vividly imagine winning the lottery, i feel genuine excitement, even though i don't believe i've won.

(5) but in the lottery case i'm imagining a counterfactual about me. in the horror movie, i'm not imagining the monster is threatening me; i'm imagining it's threatening the protagonist. that seems like a disanalogy that matters.

(6) i'm also committed to the idea that the rationality of an emotion depends on the beliefs that cause it. if my fear is caused by a belief i know is false, the fear is irrational. but my fear during a horror movie doesn't seem irrational in that way.

(7) i suspect the solution has something to do with the fact that our emotional systems are phylogenetically old and don't perfectly track the belief/imagination distinction, but i don't want to reduce the phenomenon to a design flaw — that feels like it makes all fiction-emotions irrational by default, which i don't accept.

which of these are in genuine tension, which are only apparently in tension, and what's the least revision that makes the set hang together?
