I'm a theoretical computer scientist with a working knowledge of computability theory, formal logic (Gödel numbering, diagonalization, Löb's theorem), and programming language semantics (type systems, fixed points, self-interpreters). I've been told that the "embedded agency" literature in AI alignment — the problem that real agents are part of the environment they act on, not Cartesian observers standing outside it — has deep structural parallels to the problems I already know from the theory of computation. I want you to restate the core claims of embedded agency in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then give me a residue report.

Specifically, here is what I want you to translate:

1. The Cartesian boundary: the claim that standard decision theory and reinforcement learning assume a separation between agent and environment that breaks when the agent is physically embedded in the same world. Restate this in terms of the distinction between a Turing machine and its tape, or between object language and metalanguage, or between a program and the data it operates on — whichever mapping is most natural.

2. The problem of logical uncertainty and reflective consistency: the claim that an embedded agent cannot treat its own reasoning as a black box whose outputs it merely observes, because its reasoning is part of the environment and must be coherent with the environment's laws. Restate this in terms of self-referential sentences, the halting problem, or Löb's theorem and the difficulty of a system reasoning about its own proofs.

3. The "realistic environments" problem: the claim that in a sufficiently rich environment, the agent's own source code may appear in the environment (e.g., another agent with a copy of its decision procedure), so the agent must have a policy for interacting with copies or variants of itself. Restate this in terms of quines, Kleene's recursion theorem, or the challenges of writing a program that reasons about programs syntactically identical to itself.

4. The subagent problem: the claim that an embedded agent may need to spawn subagents that share its computational resources, and that this creates a tension between the agent's goals and the subagents' goals that is not captured by standard utility-maximization frameworks. Restate this in terms of a parent process forking child processes that inherit, or partially inherit, its code and objectives.

For each of these four translations, I want you to tell me what maps cleanly, what you had to distort or stretch, and what in the embedded agency literature has no counterpart in my computational vocabulary. Then give me the overall residue report: is there something the alignment researchers are worried about that my vocabulary genuinely cannot express — and if so, is that a sign that my vocabulary is missing something important, or a sign that their formulation is less precise than it looks?
