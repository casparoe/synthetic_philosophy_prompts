ok so i'm putting together a syllabus for an upper-division aesthetics course and i realized i need to get clear on my own views about aesthetic testimony before i teach it. here's where i think i am:

(1) pessimism about aesthetic testimony, but the strong kind -- not just that testimony is insufficient for aesthetic *understanding*, but that you can't even get aesthetic *knowledge* from testimony. if i tell you a piece of music is sublime and you've never heard it, you don't thereby know it's sublime, even if i'm reliable and you have no defeaters.

(2) i think aesthetic properties are response-dependent: for something to be beautiful (or sublime, or graceful, etc.) just is for it to be such as to elicit certain responses in suitably situated subjects. not crude subjectivism -- there are correctness conditions -- but the property is constituted by the response, not by some response-independent feature of the object.

(3) i think there are genuine aesthetic experts. some people's aesthetic judgments are more reliable than others', and this isn't just a matter of social authority or better vocabulary; they actually track the aesthetic facts better.

(4) i'm a reductionist about testimony in general. there's nothing sui generis about testimonial knowledge -- it works by the same basic mechanisms as perception and inference. no special "testimonial faculty."

(5) i think you can acquire knowledge through testimony about art-historical facts (who painted something, what school it belongs to, what techniques were used) without any special problem.

now i'm worried these don't all fit together. (1) and (3) look like they're in obvious tension -- if there are experts, why can't i learn from them? but maybe that's too quick. and i'm not sure whether (2) helps or hurts: on the one hand, if aesthetic properties are response-dependent, maybe that explains why you need your own responses; on the other hand, if they're response-dependent, why can't testimony about others' responses do the work?

before i go making a fool of myself in front of grad students, can you go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest fix is? i'd rather not give up (1) but i'm open to weakening it if the cost is lower than revising something else.
