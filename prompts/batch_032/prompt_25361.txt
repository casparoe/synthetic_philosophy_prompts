I'm teaching a unit on infinity in a philosophy of mathematics course, and I'd like a handout that helps students locate their own view in the debate over Thomson's lamp and the possibility of supertasks. Please construct a decision tree or taxonomy that takes a student from their initial stance on whether it's possible to complete infinitely many tasks in a finite time, through the key branching questions, to a named position.

The tree should separate at least the following positions (you may identify more):

- Supertask eliminativism: the lamp scenario is an impossibility proof — no supertask can be completed, and the puzzle about the lamp's final state is the demonstration of this.
- The dissolution view: the description of the lamp simply does not determine its state at t=1, and there is no further fact of the matter; the puzzle arises from assuming the description was complete when it wasn't.
- The non-convergence view: the lamp has no determinate final state because the sequence of on/off does not converge, but this does not rule out supertasks whose state sequences do converge.
- The physical underdetermination view: the abstract mathematical description leaves out physical details that would settle the outcome in any concrete realization, so there is no single answer to the abstract question.

Each branch node should be phrased as a question a student can answer without already knowing the philosophical labels. The questions should turn on distinctions the positions genuinely disagree about — for example, whether the lack of a limit is a problem for the scenario itself or merely for the question "on or off?", whether completing a supertask requires the final state to be a limit of the preceding states, and whether the abstract description is complete or needs physical supplementation.

Please flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate and explain why. Also note any leaf positions that are logically available given the branching questions but apparently undefended in the literature, with a brief diagnosis (incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored).
