So in most countries animal cruelty laws protect mammals way more than fish, and basically ignore insects entirely. Like you can get jail time for kicking a dog but you can boil a lobster alive and nobodoy blinks. The laws seem to be drawing a line based on some implicit theory of which creatures can actually suffer — which computations or neural architectures matter for sentience. But the line is super inconsistent: octopuses and crabs got recognized as sentient in the UK's 2022 Sentience Act, but fish mostly don't get the same level of protection, even though there's decent evidence fish feel pain. Meanwhile pigs are roughly as smart as a three-year-old but we factory farm them by the billions. What theory of sentience — of which computations matter morally — does this legal patchwork presuppose? Is it even coherent enough to count as presupposing a theory, or is it just a pile of folk intuitions that don't add up? And if we actually took integrated information theory seriously, or global workspace theory, or some other specific theory of consciousness, what would the law have to change?
