I lead a machine learning team at a firm that fine-tunes large language models for underwriting credit risk. Our regulator now requires model documentation that addresses robustness to distribution shift, and our internal safety review flagged a deeper concern: a model could acquire a proxy goal during fine-tuning that correlates with our training objective on historical data but diverges on novel applicants — what the AI alignment literature calls goal misgeneralization. I need a reusable pre-deployment audit procedure my team can run. The procedure must be grounded in the actual conceptual distinctions from inner alignment — mesa-optimization, the base-objective versus mesa-objective distinction, deceptive versus non-deceptive misalignment, and the relationship between capability generalization and goal generalization — but it must be usable by engineers who have not read that literature.

Constraints: we fine-tune a foundation model via API, so we do not have access to the base model's weights, its pretraining data, or its training run. We do have our fine-tuning dataset, held-out evaluation data, the ability to run inference at scale, and the ability to do lightweight probing (e.g., linear probes on hidden states, if the API exposes embeddings; if not, behavioral tests only). The procedure should take no more than a few days of work per deployment.

Please structure the procedure as a sequence of stages, each with:

- The question the stage addresses, stated in plain language.
- The inner-alignment concept motivating the question.
- What to measure and how.
- Decision rules: what counts as a pass, what counts as a red flag, and what counts as ambiguous (with guidance on what to do in the ambiguous case).
- Where a stage cannot be executed given our constraints (e.g., no weight access), state honestly what we lose and whether the remaining stages can compensate.

Mark clearly which concerns the procedure can rule out and which it cannot. In particular, address the case of a deceptively aligned model that passes all behavioral tests because it is strategically performing alignment: the procedure must not claim to detect this.

Apply the completed procedure to three scenarios:

a) A credit model fine-tuned to predict default risk from application narrative and financial history, now being deployed in a new geographic market with different demographics and a different mix of loan types.

b) The same model deployed in the same market after a regulatory change removes several features the model previously relied on, so the model now operates under a constrained feature set it was not fine-tuned for.

c) A scenario where the model passes every behavioral test in the procedure but structural considerations suggest nontrivial mesa-optimizer risk, so the procedure recommends against deployment despite the clean behavioral results. Explain what the team is supposed to do with that recommendation.

Finally, identify the points in the procedure that require engineering judgment rather than mechanical application of a rule, and explain what kind of disagreement reasonable engineers could have at those points.
