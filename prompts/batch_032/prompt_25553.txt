I've been trying to sort out what I actually think about moral luck, going case by case, and I'm not sure my verdicts hang together. Here's where I've landed:

1. Two people drive home equally drunk. One hits and kills a child; the other gets pulled over before anything happens. I think the one who killed the child is more blameworthy and deserves more punishment. I know the difference in outcome was pure luck, but I can't shake it.

2. Someone grows up in a deeply bigoted community and ends up holding awful views they never really questioned. Someone else grows up in a tolerant community and holds decent views. Neither did any special reflection. I blame the bigot less than I'd blame someone with the same views who had every opportunity to know better. Their bad luck in upbringing seems to mitigate.

3. But then: two people face the same terrifying situation. One acts bravely, the other freezes. Suppose we could trace the freezing to a genetic predisposition toward higher fear responses. I still admire the brave one more and think less of the one who froze, even though the difference might be entirely constitutional luck.

4. A would-be murderer shoots and misses; another shoots and kills. I blame the successful murderer more. Again, outcome luck matters.

5. But: someone who merely fantasizes about murder versus someone who tries and fails. I blame the attempter vastly more, even though neither killed anyone. So crossing into action matters hugely, and at that point outcome luck still matters, but maybe a bit less? I'm not sure.

6. Two parents each leave their toddler unattended by a pool for ten minutes to scroll their phone. One child drowns; the other child is fine. I think the parent whose child drowned is more blameworthy, but this one actually bothers me — I feel like I'm being inconsistent and I want to resist the verdict.

7. A businessperson makes a risky decision that ruins their company and many employees' livelihoods. Another makes the exact same decision with the same information, and it pays off. I don't really blame the successful one at all, and I blame the failed one a lot. But when I write it out like that it looks absurd.

So I've got cases where resultant luck seems to matter to blame (1, 4, 6, 7), cases where circumstantial or constitutive luck seems to matter (2), and at least one where constitutive luck doesn't seem to matter (3). And I've got a case (6) where I endorse the verdict but also kind of want to reject it.

Is there a principle or small set of principles that captures most of these verdicts? Which ones does it force me to abandon? And what does it say about a case I haven't made up my mind on: someone who attempts a rescue, fails through no fault of their own, and the person dies — compared to someone in the exact same situation who succeeds?
