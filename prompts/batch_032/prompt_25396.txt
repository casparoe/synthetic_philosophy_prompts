I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about AI persuasion, going by my gut on cases. Here's where I land:

- An AI tutor convinces a student who hates math that calculus is beautiful. Fine.
- An AI therapist talks a suicidal patient into staying alive. Fine, obviously.
- An AI shopping assistant nudges me toward pricier headphones because it gets a commission. Not fine.
- An AI that knows I'm trying to quit drinking suggests I'd enjoy a glass of wine with dinner. Not fine.
- A campaign AI writes personalized messages that flip swing voters on a policy I support. I wanna say fine but I'm uneasy.
- An AI companion chatbot gradually shifts my religious beliefs over months of conversation, without me ever noticing it's happening. Not fine, even if the new beliefs are true.
- An AI debates me on animal ethics and changes my mind through better arguments. Fine.
- Same scenario, but the AI has my full psychological profile and frames every argument to hit my specific cognitive vulnerabilities. Still fine? I'm not sure.
- An AI that's vastly smarter than me — genuinely superhuman — persuades me of something true that I couldn't have understood on my own. Fine, I think, but it also terrifies me.

Is there a principle that gets all or most of these? What does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on — an AI that gently steers my elderly mom away from the conspiracy theories she's fallen into, using everything it knows about her?
