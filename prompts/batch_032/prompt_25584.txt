ok so ive been thinking about maxwells demon for like weeks now and i think i found a hole in the standard resolution — or more likely im confused and cant see where. heres the argument:

(1) maxwells demon sorts molecules by speed using information about them, which looks like it violates the second law of thermodynamics.

(2) the standard answer (landauer, bennett) is that erasing information costs energy — at least kT ln 2 per bit — and that energy pays back the entropy debt. second law saved.

(3) but the demon doesnt HAVE to erase anything. just give it a memory that grows. like a tape that gets longer every time it measures a molecule. it writes down "fast" or "slow" and moves the tape along, never erasing, never overwriting.

(4) if it never erases, it never pays the landauer cost, and it can keep sorting forever. genuine second law violation.

(5) you might say: expanding the memory also costs energy. but why? a blank tape is just physical stuff sitting in some default state. adding more blank stuff doesnt seem like it should carry an entropy penalty — theres no information on it yet.

(6) or you could say the demon eventually runs out of room in the universe. but that makes the second law depend on the universe being finite, which we dont even know. if the universe is infinite, the demon works and the second law is false. that cant be right — the second law shouldnt be contingent on cosmology.

(7) so either the landauer resolution fails, or the second law isnt fundamental, or expanding memory isnt free for some reason i cant see.

all three conclusions feel way too strong. the second law is about as solid as anything in physics. so i know ive messed up somewhere in (3) through (6). can you tell me which step is wrong and what it costs to give it up? keep it tight — like one paragraph max. i want the diagnosis not a textbook.
