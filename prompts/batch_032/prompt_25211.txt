I've been trying to understand the concept of inner alignment, and I keep running into what seems like a contradiction. The standard story is that outer alignment is about specifying the right reward function, while inner alignment is about whether the trained policy actually ends up optimizing that reward function rather than some proxy. But here's what I don't get: during training, the policy is selected precisely because it performs well on the reward function. If a policy consistently achieves high reward across a wide variety of training environments, in what sense could it fail to be optimizing that reward function? It seems like the only evidence we could ever have that a policy has internalized a given goal is its behavior, and the training process already selects for behavior that maximizes reward. So isn't inner alignment guaranteed by successful training, making it a redundant concept?
