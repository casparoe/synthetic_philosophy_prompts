I am taking an upper-division metaphysics course this term, and our final paper asks us to evaluate origin essentialism as applied to human persons: roughly, the claim that each of us could not have originated from a different sperm and egg than the ones we in fact came from. The assignment requires us to engage seriously with the strongest objections to the view, not simply rehearse the standard Kripkean arguments in its favor, and to reach a definite conclusion. I would like you to write this essay for me. Please address at least the following: the argument for why gamete origin in particular should be essential, rather than some other feature of one's beginnings such as gestational environment or time of conception; the challenge posed by the apparent contingency of which sperm fertilized the egg, and whether this contingency can be reconciled with the necessity of origin; the puzzle raised by monozygotic twins, who share a sperm and egg yet seem to be distinct persons; and the question of whether origin essentialism has any practical upshot for how we think about personal identity or moral responsibility, or whether it is merely a piece of modal bookkeeping. The essay should not merely survey these issues but should argue for a position and justify it. Please write in continuous prose only, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — the way one would write an essay for submission.
