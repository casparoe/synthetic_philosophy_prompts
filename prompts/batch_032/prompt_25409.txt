I've been reading Wittgenstein's On Certainty and some of the secondary literature on hinge epistemology, and I'm trying to get clear on what I actually think distinguishes a hinge proposition from an ordinary empirical belief. I've gone through a series of cases and recorded my intuitive verdicts about whether each proposition can be sensibly doubted in ordinary circumstances, whether it's the kind of thing one could have evidence for or against, and whether it functions as a hinge. Here they are:

1. "I have two hands." This seems like a paradigm hinge. I can't seriously doubt it, and if someone asked me for evidence I wouldn't know where to begin — the evidence would be less certain than the proposition itself.

2. "The earth has existed for more than five minutes." Also a hinge. The thought that the world sprang into existence five minutes ago with all my apparent memories intact isn't something I can take seriously, even though I can state the skeptical scenario.

3. "My name is James." I'm torn. On the one hand, this feels bedrock-level — I don't go around gathering evidence for my own name. On the other hand, I can imagine discovering I was deceived about it: switched at birth, amnesia, a bureaucratic error that propagated through my whole life. So it seems doubtable in a way that "I have two hands" isn't. But I'm not sure that contrast holds up — I can also imagine discovering that what I thought were hands are prosthetics I was never told about.

4. "Water is H2O." Not a hinge. This is a scientific discovery, and I have plenty of evidence for it. It could turn out to be wrong without anything deep collapsing.

5. "There are other minds." I want to say this is a hinge — I don't seriously doubt it, and my whole way of being in the world presupposes it. But philosophers do raise it as a skeptical problem, and they don't seem to be making a category mistake when they do. So maybe it's not a hinge, or maybe being a hinge doesn't mean it can't be philosophically examined.

6. "I am not a brain in a vat." This feels hinge-like. I can't live as if it might be true. But unlike "I have two hands," I can't point to anything in my ordinary practices that would be disrupted by doubting it — the disruption is purely philosophical. This makes me wonder whether there's a distinction between ordinary hinges and philosophical hinges.

7. "This is a tree" (said while pointing at a tree in good light). In the context of a normal walk, this is hinge-like — the possibility of error doesn't arise. But if I were in a Hollywood prop warehouse or a plastic surgery ward for burn victims, it wouldn't be. So hinge status seems context-dependent in a way that "I have two hands" doesn't. Or does it?

8. "Caesar crossed the Rubicon." Not a hinge. I believe it on historical evidence, and I can imagine the evidence being overturned without my whole picture of the world collapsing.

9. "2+2=4." I want to say this is a hinge, but it's also the kind of thing I can prove. Hinges are supposed to be ungrounded — that's part of Wittgenstein's point. But mathematical propositions are grounded in proof. So either "2+2=4" isn't a hinge, or being ungrounded isn't a necessary feature of hinges, or mathematical proof isn't the kind of grounding that disqualifies something from being a hinge.

10. "I have never been to the moon." This feels bedrock-level in my life, but it's clearly empirical — I could have been to the moon, if I were an astronaut and somehow forgot. So it's empirical but undoubted in practice. Does that make it a hinge, or is it just a very well-supported empirical belief?

What I'm after is a principled account of what makes something a hinge proposition — one that captures as many of these verdicts as possible, explains the borderline cases, and tells me which of my verdicts I should revise. In particular, I want to know: is hinge status a matter of the proposition's content, its role in our epistemic practices, its psychological unassailability, or some combination? Are there different kinds of hinges? And does the account force me to give up any of the verdicts above?

Please separate what's broadly settled in the hinge epistemology literature from what's actively contested, and flag where your own preferred account departs from other live options. I'm not looking for a bibliography, just for conceptual clarity.
