In my philosophy of language seminar, the professor keeps invoking the slogan "one grain doesn't make a heap" to set up the sorites paradox, and then uses the paradox to argue that we have to choose between epistemicism and supervaluationism about vagueness. But every time I try to pin down what the slogan is actually asserting, it seems to shift. Sometimes it sounds like a psychological claim about how we can't detect the difference; sometimes it sounds like a logical claim about tolerance; sometimes it sounds almost like a metaphysical claim about the world lacking sharp boundaries. I want to come to the next class with a clear map of what "one grain doesn't make a heap" could mean — the distinct precise claims it might be expressing, from the obviously true to the obviously false, with an honest assessment of each. And I especially want to know: which reading, if any, actually forces the choice between epistemicism and supervaluationism that my professor says it does?
