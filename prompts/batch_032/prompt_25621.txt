I just spent the better part of an evening arguing with a friend about whether self-deception is even coherent, and I have tried to write down the position I found myself defending by the end of it. I would like a consistency audit before I commit to any of this in print.

Here is where I stand:

(1) Self-deception is a genuine psychological phenomenon. People really do deceive themselves about their relationships, their abilities, their prospects — this is not merely a loose metaphor or a misdescription of something else.

(2) Deception is a unified concept. Self-deception and other-deception are both species of deception proper, not mere homonyms. Whatever the core of deception is, both cases share it.

(3) The core of other-deception is this: the deceiver intentionally brings it about that the deceived forms or maintains a false belief, while the deceived remains unaware both of the truth and of the deceiver's intention to mislead.

(4) In self-deception, the deceiver and the deceived are numerically identical. There is no literal partitioning of the mind into separate agents or subpersonal systems. Talk of mental "parts" is metaphor, and the metaphor should not be asked to carry the philosophical weight.

(5) Self-deception is distinct from wishful thinking or simple confirmation bias. In wishful thinking, the person never really registers the contrary evidence. In self-deception, the person does register the unwelcome truth at some level — that is precisely why we call it self-deception and not self-mistake — and then manages not to attend to it or act on it.

(6) The familiar paradox (how can one person simultaneously intend to deceive and be unaware of that intention?) dissolves once we stop insisting that self-deception must operate by the same psychological mechanism as other-deception. The concept is unified; the mechanisms need not be.

I can feel that (3), (4), and (5) are pulling against each other, and I suspect (6) is doing more work than I am letting on — perhaps it relocates the problem rather than solving it. Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what the least costly revision would be. Rank the tensions by severity, and for each, say exactly what would have to give.
