I hold the view that the debate approach to scalable oversight — in which two AI systems are trained to argue for and against a proposed answer to a question before a human judge, with the judge's verdict providing the reward signal — can in principle allow humans to reliably supervise AI systems that substantially exceed human capabilities, including in domains where the human judge could not independently verify the correct answer. I want to know what would count against this view. I am asking for the arguments, thought experiments, and possible empirical results that should most worry a debate advocate, sorted from those that would be fatal (the approach cannot work even in principle) to those that would merely require a patch or a restriction on scope. I also want an honest assessment of whether the view, as stated, is specified precisely enough to be vulnerable to counterevidence at all, and if it is not, what the most damaging way of making it precise would be. Include the objection that a committed advocate of debate would least want to see developed in a top-tier publication, and say whether it already has been.
