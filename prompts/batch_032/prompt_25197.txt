Liam: That's not what heritability means. A heritability estimate tells you how much of the variation in a trait within a particular population is associated with genetic variation in that population. It doesn't tell you that the trait is genetically determined or that environment doesn't matter.

Maya: But if genes account for most of the variation, then by definition environment accounts for less of it. So changing the environment — like giving kids better schools — will have limited effects on the trait. That's just arithmetic.

Maya: That sounds like a technical loophole. In the real world, environments do vary — that's the whole point. Liam: Consider height. Height is highly heritable — around 0.8 in developed countries. But average height rose substantially across the twentieth century because of improvements in nutrition and public health.
