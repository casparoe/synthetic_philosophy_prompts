Goodman's "grue" paradox has been debated since the 1950s, and the literature still hasn't converged on what it actually shows about induction. On one side, some take grue to expose a deep and genuine constraint: inductive inference requires a principled account of which predicates are projectible, and without one, induction is ungrounded. On the other side, others regard grue as a pseudo-problem — "grue" is gerrymandered in a way that "green" isn't, the paradox only works if you grant a tendentious symmetry between the two predicates, or our actual inductive practices aren't threatened because we have pragmatic or evolutionary reasons to prefer "green" over "grue." After nearly 70 years, neither camp has moved the other, and the debate looks stalled rather than progressive.
