If lying and misleading both aim at producing the same false belief in the hearer, and both can cause the same amount of harm, then the only difference between them is whether the speaker's words were literally false or merely pragmatically deceptive. But why should literal falsity matter morally? It seems like an oddly semantic criterion for ethics—as if morality cared about sentence-level truth conditions rather than about what actually happens to people. So why do philosophers treat the lying/misleading distinction as morally significant rather than as a quirk of language that ethics should look past?
