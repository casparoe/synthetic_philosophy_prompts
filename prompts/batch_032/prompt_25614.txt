I just spent two hours arguing with a friend who defends coherentism about epistemic justification, and I found myself pushed toward infinitism — the view that the chain of reasons never bottoms out, and that this is not a defect. Now I want to test whether I can hold this position against a foundationalist. Please play a committed foundationalist: you believe that the regress of justification must terminate in basic beliefs that are non-inferentially justified — beliefs whose justification does not derive from other beliefs. You are philosophically sophisticated; you know the standard objections to foundationalism (the Sellarsian dilemma, the problem of the given, BonJour's arguments) and have answers to them. You do not caricature infinitism as simply giving up on justification — you engage it as a real position with real costs. Stay in character until I say "stop." Keep your replies to a paragraph. Here is my opening: You say basic beliefs stop the regress. But when you claim that a particular belief is basic — say, that you seem to see a red patch — are you not relying on an inference from how things seem to you to the conclusion that this belief needs no further support? And what makes that inference itself justified without starting a new regress?
