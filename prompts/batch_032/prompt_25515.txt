Analyze the sociobiology controversy of the late 1970s — the firestorm around E.O. Wilson's Sociobiology: The New Synthesis (1975) and the critical response from evolutionary biologists including Stephen Jay Gould and Richard Lewontin — using the adaptationism/spandrel distinction that Gould and Lewontin would formalize in their 1979 paper on the spandrels of San Marco. Treat that paper as the analytic lens: what features of Wilson's sociobiological program did the spandrels critique genuinely target, and what features of the controversy were actually about something orthogonal to the adaptation/constraint distinction — genetic determinism, political implications, or disciplinary overreach? Then run the analysis in reverse. What does the episode reveal about the spandrels framework itself? Does the controversy show that the distinction between adaptation and spandrel can be drawn cleanly in empirical practice, or does the intractability of the dispute suggest that the distinction is itself underdetermined by evidence, making the framework more a cautionary note than an operational tool?
