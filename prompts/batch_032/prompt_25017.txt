I am a novelist at work on a book whose central characters include several non-human intelligences — some animal, some artificial — and I have been studying Daniel Dennett's taxonomy of minds from *Kinds of Minds* to ensure the cognitive architectures I imagine are philosophically coherent. The taxonomy runs from Darwinian creatures (whose only generate-and-test mechanism is natural selection) through Skinnerian creatures (which add operant conditioning) to Popperian creatures (which possess an inner environment where hypotheses can die in their stead) and finally Gregorian creatures (which import mind-tools — words, diagrams, counting systems — from the cultural environment, tools that restructure cognition itself once internalized).

I find the boundary between Popperian and Gregorian creatures especially slippery, and I would like you to compare the two in detail. Specifically: Is the difference one of degree or one of kind? Could a solitary Popperian creature that develops a private, idiosyncratic system of self-generated symbols qualify as Gregorian, or does the public, culturally transmitted origin of the mind-tools matter constitutively? And are there plausible borderline cases — real or hypothetical — that put genuine pressure on the distinction?
