A longform magazine piece I read summarizes Hannah Arendt's view on lying in politics roughly as follows: "Arendt argued that totalitarian movements succeed by replacing factual reality with an entirely fictional world. Once people lose the ability to distinguish truth from falsehood, they become vulnerable to any ideology. Her key insight is that lying destroys the commons — the shared factual ground that makes political deliberation possible — and that without that ground, democratic politics collapses into pure power struggle. This is why she thought the Pentagon Papers were so important: they showed that the U.S. government was lying systematically, and Arendt believed that exposing those lies was the first step toward restoring democratic accountability." I know Arendt wrote about lying in several places — 'Truth and Politics,' 'Lying in Politics' (the Pentagon Papers piece), and The Origins of Totalitarianism — and I suspect this summary runs together claims from different works in a way that obscures important distinctions she drew. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what is accurate, what is a conflation of distinct claims, and what Arendt herself would have pushed back on.
