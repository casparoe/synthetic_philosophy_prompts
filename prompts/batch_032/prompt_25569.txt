I've been reading about the Knobe effect — the finding that people's moral judgments seem to influence whether they classify a side effect as having been brought about intentionally. The classic vignette: a CEO approves a project to boost profits, knowing it will harm the environment as a side effect. People overwhelmingly say the CEO harmed the environment intentionally. But when the side effect is helping the environment, with everything else held constant, people say the CEO did not bring it about intentionally. The asymmetry is striking, and it's been replicated across cultures, ages, and even in people with deficits in moral reasoning.

The interpretation I find most compelling is that this shows moral cognition isn't layered on top of a clean, value-neutral folk psychology. Instead, the very concept of acting intentionally is shot through with normative considerations. We don't first figure out what happened and then evaluate it; the evaluation is part of figuring out what happened. If that's right, it's a problem for any architecture — cognitive or computational — that treats factual attribution and moral evaluation as separate modules with a one-way pipeline between them.

I want to know what would count against this view. I'm not asking for the standard objections I've already seen (e.g., that the effect is just conversational pragmatics, or that it only reveals something about the word "intentionally" rather than the underlying concept). I want a map of the view's exposure: which results, if they came out a certain way, would force a defender of the "moral judgment permeates folk psychology" position to retreat, qualify, or abandon it? I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want to know which ones defenders would themselves concede as counting against them rather than dismiss as missing the point. Are there cross-cultural studies that could break the interpretation one way or the other? Developmental results? Neuroimaging data? Would it matter if the effect disappeared for certain kinds of side effects or certain kinds of moral violations? And I'd like an honest answer to whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if it has, whether that's a problem for a view of this kind, or just the nature of a claim about concept structure.
