A friend of mine was trying to explain epiphenomenalism to me and said this:

"Epiphenomenalism is the view that conscious experiences are caused by brain processes but have no causal powers of their own — they're like the steam whistle on a locomotive, a byproduct that doesn't affect how the train runs. The paradox of phenomenal judgment is the main objection: if epiphenomenalism is true, then when I judge 'I'm in pain,' that judgment is caused entirely by my brain, not by the pain itself. But then my judgment doesn't actually refer to my pain — it's just a physical event — so epiphenomenalism makes it impossible to know our own conscious states. Thats basically why the view is considered dead in philosophy of mind."

I'm not sure how much of this to trust. Can you go through it piece by piece and tell me what's accurate, what's subtly wrong or overstated, and how a defender of epiphenomenalism would respond? Also, please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among philosophers working on this — I don't want to walk away thinking a live debate is closed just because one side talks louder.
