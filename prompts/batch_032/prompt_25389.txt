I recently came across Dennett's idea that the self is a "center of narrative gravity" — not a thing inside us but an abstraction we project onto the stories we tell about our lives, as real as the point where a chair's weight is said to act and no more real than that. The thought has unsettled me in ways I did not expect. If the self is a useful fiction, what follows for how one ought to live? I am not asking for an essay. Write a short story — perhaps 800 to 1,200 words — in which a character comes to internalize this view not through argument but through experience. The story should be concrete and particular: a specific person in a specific situation, with the philosophical idea emerging from narrative rather than being explained inside it. I want something that could stand as literary fiction, not a thought experiment dressed in dialogue.
