I retired from a career in geophysics a few years back and have been getting into Stoic philosophy — Epictetus, Seneca, Marcus Aurelius, the usual suspects. The dichotomy of control has really stuck with me: some things are up to us, others are not. But the more I think about it, the more I wonder if the whole thing kinda collapses under its own weight. Here's my worry. Epictetus says my judgments, desires, and aversions are up to me, but my body, reputation, and property are not. But surely my body is partially up to me — I can exercise, eat well, go to the doctor. And my reputation is influenced by how I act. So the dichotomy seems false as a factual claim about what I can causally affect. And if the Stoic response is "well, you can influence those things but you can't guarantee the outcome," then practically everything ends up in the "not up to us" category except maybe my bare act of trying — which seems like a recipe for learned helplessness dressed up as wisdom. I've seen this line of criticism in a few places online, and I can't quite shake it. Am I missing something about what Epictetus actually meant by "up to us"? Please respond with exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
