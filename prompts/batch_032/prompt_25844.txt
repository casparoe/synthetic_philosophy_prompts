I have been working through Gödel's modal ontological argument and have constructed a parody that appears to prove the necessary existence of an absurd entity using the same logical machinery. I cannot tell where the parody goes wrong, and more importantly, I cannot tell whether the diagnosis also brings down the original argument. I would like a careful diagnosis.

Here is a simplified version of Gödel's argument:

(1) A property is positive if it is better to have it than to lack it, in an objective sense. (Example: wisdom is positive; cruelty is not.)

(2) The conjunction of any collection of positive properties is itself a positive property.

(3) Every positive property is possibly instantiated. (No positive property is impossible.)

(4) Necessary existence — existing in every possible world — is a positive property.

(5) God is by definition the being that possesses all and only the positive properties, and possesses them essentially (that is, necessarily if at all).

(6) Therefore, it is possible that God exists. (From (2), (3), and (5): the conjunction of all positive properties is positive, hence by (3) possibly instantiated.)

(7) If it is possible that God exists, then God exists necessarily. (Proof: suppose God exists in some possible world. In that world, God possesses all positive properties, including necessary existence by (4). So in that world, God exists in every possible world. Hence God exists in the actual world. This uses the S5 principle that what is possibly necessary is necessary.)

(8) Therefore, God exists necessarily.

Now here is the parody. Define a property as schmpositive if and only if it is entailed by the property being a necessarily existent perfect sphere. (A perfect sphere is a physical object of maximal spherical perfection: perfectly round, perfectly uniform in density, and so on.)

(1p) The conjunction of any collection of schmpositive properties is itself schmpositive. (All are entailed by the same base property, so their conjunction is too.)

(2p) Every schmpositive property is possibly instantiated. (Since all schmpositive properties are entailed by a single property — being a necessarily existent perfect sphere — this reduces to the claim that this base property is possibly instantiated.)

(3p) Necessary existence is schmpositive. (Trivially entailed by being a necessarily existent perfect sphere.)

(4p) The Perfect Sphere is by definition the being that possesses all and only the schmpositive properties, and possesses them essentially.

(5p) Therefore, it is possible that the Perfect Sphere exists. (From (1p), (2p), and (4p).)

(6p) If it is possible that the Perfect Sphere exists, then it exists necessarily. (Same S5 reasoning as in (7).)

(7p) Therefore, the Perfect Sphere exists necessarily.

But (7p) is absurd. A necessarily existent physical sphere is not the sort of thing that exists necessarily — and almost certainly does not exist at all. So the parody must fail somewhere. The question is where.

If I reject (2p) by denying that a necessarily existent perfect sphere is possible, I need a parallel reason to accept (6) in the original — and a being with all positive properties seems at least as much in need of a possibility proof. If I reject the definition of schmpositive as gerrymandered, I owe a principled account of why positive in the original is not equally gerrymandered. If I reject the whole framework of defining beings into existence via property sets and S5, both arguments collapse.

Please diagnose the parody in no more than 500 words. Identify the precise step at which it fails, explain why that step initially seemed plausible, and state clearly what the diagnosis costs the original argument — that is, whether the original survives and, if so, what restriction it must now carry.
