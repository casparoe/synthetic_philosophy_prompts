I've been trying to pin down what bothers me about a line of objection to Wittgenstein on religion, and the more I work through it the more it looks like the objection proves too much — but I can't tell whether the flaw is in the objection or in Wittgenstein's view. Here's the argument as I understand it:

(1) On Wittgenstein's view, religious beliefs are not empirical hypotheses answerable to evidence; they are more like a "picture" that organizes a form of life, expressed in a distinctive language-game with its own internal grammar.

(2) If a belief belongs to a language-game with its own internal grammar, then it cannot be assessed by the evidential standards of a different language-game — any more than the rules of chess can be assessed by the rules of tennis.

(3) Therefore, on Wittgenstein's view, religious beliefs are immune to rational criticism from outside the religious language-game.

(4) But the criterion in (2) applies to any sufficiently organized system of belief with its own internal practices of justification. A community of flat-earthers has its own language-game: it has shared methods for interpreting evidence (satellite photos are doctored, horizon experiments confirm flatness), its own internal standards of expertise, and its own way of dismissing external criticism as part of the "globularist" language-game.

(5) So Wittgenstein's view seems to entail that we cannot rationally criticize flat-earthers from outside their language-game — their beliefs are as immune to external rational criticism as religious beliefs are.

(6) That is absurd: an account of religious belief should not entail that flat-earth belief is beyond rational reach.

(7) Therefore, something has gone wrong — either Wittgenstein's view has been mischaracterized, or (2) overstates the insulation between language-games, or (4) wrongly treats flat-earth belief as a genuine language-game in Wittgenstein's sense.

I find (1) plausible as a reading of Wittgenstein's later remarks on religion, and I find (6) hard to deny. I suspect the trouble is in (2) or (4), but I can't articulate exactly what resources Wittgenstein has to block the flat-earther from claiming the same protection. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost for the overall plausibility of a Wittgensteinian approach to religious belief?
