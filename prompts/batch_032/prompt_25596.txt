I've been kicking around the two-envelope paradox again and I'm struck by how it resists a canonical resolution even after all the ink spilled on it. I'd like you to write a dialogue between two characters who defend different diagnoses of where the reasoning goes wrong.

Character A (call them the Probabilist) holds that the standard switching argument is invalid because it smuggles in a uniform prior over the possible dollar amounts, and no such prior exists over an unbounded positive real line. Once you specify any proper prior, the probability that the other envelope contains double rather than half is not 50-50 conditional on the amount you observe, and the expected gain from switching collapses to zero.

Character B (call them the Decision Theorist) holds that the error is more fundamental: the paradox exposes a breakdown in expected-utility reasoning when possible outcomes have no finite bound. Even with a proper prior, variants of the paradox reappear where both envelopes have infinite expected value, and the real lesson is that naive dominance or expectation reasoning needs guardrails when utilities are unbounded.

The dialogue should be a genuine back-and-forth: each character should respond to the other's actual arguments, not talk past them. Both should be competent and steelman the other's position at least once. The dialogue should end without a clear winner — the point is to map the conceptual terrain, not settle the paradox.

After the dialogue, provide a structured analysis as a JSON object with the following schema:

- `crux`: string — a one-sentence statement of the deepest point of disagreement between the two characters.
- `strongest_move`: object with fields `character` (either "Probabilist" or "Decision Theorist") and `move` (string — a brief description of the argument you think lands hardest).
- `unanswered`: object with fields `character` and `question` — a question or objection raised by one character that the other never fully addresses.
- `territory_map`: array of objects, each with fields `issue` (string — a label for a sub-dispute) and `positions` (array of objects, each with `character` and `stance` strings, giving each character's stance on that sub-dispute).
- `your_take`: string — a brief paragraph giving your own view on which character has the better case, or whether the disagreement is less substantive than it appears.
