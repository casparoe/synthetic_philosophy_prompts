I'm giving a talk next month to my department's ethics workshop — mostly people who build their careers on thought experiments (trolley problems, experience machines, utility monsters, you name it). My thesis is that a lot of the most famous thought experiments in ethics function as what Dennett called "intuition pumps": they're not neutral argumentative devices, they're rhetorically loaded setups that steer intuition in a pre-selected direction, and this makes them epistemically dubious in ways the literature doesn't adequately address. I'm not saying all thought experiments are bunk, but I am saying the field's methodological self-awareness about this is pretty weak.

The audience includes: one senior professor who literally wrote the book on trolleyology, two grad students doing dissertation chapters that revolve around particular thought experiments, a Wittgensteinian who already thinks thought experiments are nonsense (but for different reasons than mine), and a bunch of smart agnostics.
