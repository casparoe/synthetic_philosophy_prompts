In debates about whether philosophy makes progress, one of the most common defenses draws on an analogy: philosophy is the mother of the sciences. Physics, psychology, linguistics, economics—each, on this picture, began as a branch of philosophy, gestated there while its foundational questions were being sharpened, and eventually spun off as an independent discipline once a consensus methodology emerged. The appearance that philosophy never settles anything is therefore an accounting illusion: whenever philosophers succeed in making a domain tractable, we relabel the success as "science" and treat the remaining, hardest questions as evidence that philosophy is stagnant.
