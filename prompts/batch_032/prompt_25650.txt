So I'm giving a paper at this history of science conference in a couple months, and the paper is basically arguing that we've been reading Kepler's astrology all wrong. The standard story is that Kepler was a serious astronomer who held his nose and did horoscopes for the money, and maybe he tried to reform astrology a bit but he didn't really believe in it. I think that's wrong. My argument is that Kepler genuinely believed in a reformed, mathematical astrology grounded in his harmonic theory—that for him, the same geometric principles that structured the heavens also structured earthly events, and casting horoscopes was part of the same intellectual project as the Harmonices Mundi. The astrology wasn't a side hustle he was embarrassed about; it was continuous with his cosmology.

The philosophers are going to jump on the epistemological status of astrological claims—like, even if Kepler thought he was doing science, was he?
