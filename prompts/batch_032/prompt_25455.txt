I'm a student in a philosophy of language course on vagueness, and our class is hosting a colloquium with a philosopher who defends epistemicism. I've been asked to help prepare the Q&A session. Could you draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having the speaker simply restate the view? I'd like at least one question on each of the following: (1) the intuitive incredulity people feel toward the claim that there is a sharp but unknowable cutoff for vague predicates like "heap" or "bald," and whether the epistemicist owes us more than an error theory about that intuition; (2) how the margin-for-error principle is supposed to explain our ignorance of such cutoffs, and whether it can do so without presupposing the very sharp boundaries it is meant to render unknowable; (3) what epistemicism says about higher-order vagueness and whether it faces a regress of ever-higher orders of ignorance; and (4) how the epistemicist handles the apparent disanalogy between vagueness-related ignorance and other kinds of ignorance (e.g., ignorance about distant past events), where we at least know what it would take to find out. For each question, include a follow-up that anticipates the speaker's most likely reply. The goal is to press the view at its genuine pressure points rather than rehearse material already covered in the talk.
