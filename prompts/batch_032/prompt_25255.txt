We're reading Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling in my philosophy class, and I understand the definition of the teleological suspension of the ethical but not what it would feel like from the inside — Kierkegaard insists it can't be mediated or explained, only lived. Could you write a poem from Abraham's perspective on the journey to Mount Moriah in which the form itself enacts the suspension? Start in regular blank verse, which stands for the ethical universal — the shared norms, the language anyone can justify. Then, at the moment of the binding, let the form break: the meter fractures or the syntax comes apart, not as randomness but as a higher logic displacing a lower one. The break should feel like the ethical being suspended for something that cannot be spoken in the ethical's own terms. Someone who knows the text should recognize the movement from the ethical to the religious in the poem's structure, not just its content.
