I spent four decades in molecular biology before retirement gave me time to read Plato, and I have become stuck on a problem my lab training did not prepare me for. The Meno problem — why is knowledge more valuable than mere true belief? — strikes me as genuinely urgent, not a parlor game. If you and I both believe truly that a certain drug will cure a disease, and we both administer it successfully, the patient recovers either way. Your having knowledge where I have only a lucky hunch made no difference to the outcome. So what does the extra ingredient buy?

I have been working through whether the additional value of knowledge could be purely instrumental — a matter of stability, say — and I have arrived at an argument that seems to prove too much:

(1) If knowledge is more valuable than true belief, there must be some good G that knowledge provides and mere true belief lacks.
(2) G is either instrumental — it helps produce better downstream results — or final — it is worth having for its own sake.
(3) If G is instrumental, its benefit must show up in some case where mere true belief would have led to a worse outcome than the corresponding piece of knowledge.
(4) But for any instrumental benefit one might name — greater resilience to counterevidence, better integration with action, resistance to epistemic luck — we can imagine a merely true belief that happens to share that feature, or a believer who compensates with a policy of periodic re-checking, without thereby turning the true belief into knowledge.
(5) So G cannot be instrumental.
(6) If G is final, then a subject who has knowledge but mostly false beliefs could be epistemically better-off than a subject with mostly true beliefs that fall short of knowledge. That is absurd, because epistemic value must be tethered to truth.
(7) Therefore G is neither instrumental nor final, so knowledge is not more valuable than true belief.
(8) That conclusion is false; we do and should prize knowledge above mere true belief.
(9) So the argument contains a mistake, and I cannot see where.
