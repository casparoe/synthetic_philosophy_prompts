I'm a novelist and I've been stuck on a scene for weeks. My protagonist is in a fire and he can either pull out his wife or two strangers he doesn't know. He grabs his wife without even thinking about it. Later, another character — the kind of guy who reads a lot of philosophy — presses him hard: "Why was her life worth more than theirs? Give me the justification." And my protagonist just says "she's my wife" and the guy says that's not a reason, that's just restating the fact you're trying to justify.

Here's the thing: I can't tell whether I'm writing my protagonist as philosophically shallow — like he should have a better answer and I need to give him one — or whether the friend's whole demand for a justification is what's broken. I have this gut feeling that having to justify saving your wife in terms of some impartial principle is itself a kind of moral mistake, like it misses what it actually means to love someone. But I can't articulate why. Is the demand for justification here the problem? And if so, what kind of problem is it?
