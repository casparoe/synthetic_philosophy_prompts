I'm a journalist working on an interactive piece for a science & ideas magazine about how people actually make moral judgments — not the labels they pick, but what they say about cases. The hook is the philosophical debate between moral particularists (no true moral principles, it's all case-by-case perception) and generalists (there are at least some moral principles that cover more than one case). I want to build a "which position do you actually hold?" quiz for readers.

Design 8 questions, each a short concrete scenario with 2 or 3 answer options. The questions should test whether someone's judgment relies on exceptionless rules, on principles that can genuinely conflict, or on the particular details of a case in a way that resists codification. No question should just ask "do you think there are moral principles?" — the whole point is to let the answers reveal the pattern. Include at least two cases where a generally plausible principle and a strong intuition about a particular case pull in opposite directions, and at least one case about moral learning or expertise (since that's where particularists and generalists make very different predictions).

Then provide a scoring key that maps patterns of answers onto positions. Include at minimum: strong generalism, Ross-style pluralism with prima facie duties, and Dancy-style particularism. Also flag any logically possible combinations of answers that don't correspond to a well-known position, and say why — is it unexplored, unstable, or just plainly incoherent?

Keep it to 800 words max. That's tight for 8 cases plus a key, so be concise — one or two sentences per case, and the scoring key can be telegraphic.
