I'm taking a graduate seminar on embodied cognition and we've been spending a lot of time on the free energy principle and predictive processing — Friston, Hohwy, Clark, Seth, the whole crew. Next week I have to lead the discussion and I want to come at it from a meta-level: before we even ask whether the FEP is *true*, what would it mean for it to be *adequate*? The literature is a mess on this. Some people treat it as a theory of everything (life, mind, culture), others as a narrow hypothesis about cortical microcircuits. Some say its unfalsifiable, others say it makes concrete predictions about precision-weighting and prediction error minimization. I want to go into class with a clear list of desiderata — what any theory pitched at this level of generality actually has to deliver to count as a genuine explanation rather than just a redescription or a mathematical framing device.

So: write me the desiderata for a predictive processing / free energy principle account of cognition (let's bracket the "theory of life" stuff for now). I want the list of requirements — the phenomena it has to explain, the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the empirical results it has to be consistent with — with the case or consideration behind each. Flag which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps (e.g., the people who want it to say something about consciousness vs. those who are happy if it just covers perception and action). And here's the part I most want: tell me which single desideratum does the most to exclude candidates, and whether any pair of desiderata on the list might be jointly unsatisfiable. If the list itself ends up being a result — like, if you can show that no theory can satisfy all of them — that's exactly the kind of thing I want to bring to class.
