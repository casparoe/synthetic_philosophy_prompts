ok so i've been thinking about Being John Malkovich and Dennett's multiple drafts model. the film literally has a portal into someone's head where you can sit and watch their consciousness play out on a screen -- it's like they built a set of the Cartesian theater and charged admission. but i'm not sure the film is as naive as that makes it sound. for one thing, the people inside Malkovich's head can influence him, and when Malkovich himself goes through the portal he ends up in a world made entirely of Malkoviches saying "Malkovich," which seems to blow up the whole idea of a unified central observer. also Craig ends up trapped inside Emily's daughter's consciousness at the end, watching but unable to act -- is that the film's final word on the Cartesian theater, that its a prison?

i want a reading that takes both Dennett and the film seriously. where does the film actually dramatize something like the multiple drafts model (the way consciousness fragments, the way there's no single author in control), and where does it fall back on a homunculus-in-the-theater picture despite itself? does the film end up vindicating Dennett or does it show something about the phenomenology of being an observer that his model misses? i'm not looking for "the film illustrates Dennett" -- i want to know where it resists the reading too.
