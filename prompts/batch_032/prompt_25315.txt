A friend who's deep into philosophy of mind walked me into this and now I'm stuck.

(1) Searle's distinction: original intentionality is intrinsic aboutness (minds have it), derived intentionality is aboutness that depends on an outside interpreter (words, pictures, computers have it).
(2) When I think a thought in English — like silently saying to myself "I should call my mom" — the vehicles of that thought are English words or something very like them.
(3) English words have only derived intentionality: "mom" means mom because of how English speakers use it, not intrinsically.
(4) If the vehicles of my thought have only derived intentionality, and I'm not somehow adding original intentionality to them just by having them run in my head, then my thought "I should call my mom" has only derived intentionality.
(5) But if that thought has only derived intentionality, then what my thought is about depends on an interpreter — just like what a sentence written on paper is about.
(6) The obvious interpreter would be... me. But that's circular: my thought is about mom because I interpret it as being about mom. And what makes my act of interpretation have original intentionality? If the interpretation is itself another English-ish thought, we're off on a regress.
(7) So either (a) some thoughts have original intentionality non-linguistically (pure mentalese, raw perceptual content, whatever), (b) linguistic thoughts bootstrap original intentionality from something non-linguistic in a way that breaks the regress, or (c) the whole original/derived distinction is bogus and Dennett was right that it's derived all the way down.
(8) (a) seems right but then almost all my actual conscious thinking is merely derived, which feels wrong — like my inner monologue isn't really *about* anything in the way I thought it was. (b) I can't make sense of. (c) I don't want — it makes my thoughts about as "about" things as a thermostat's states are.
(9) My friend says the argument proves that linguistic thought can't be the whole story about original intentionality, but won't tell me what I'm missing. So: diagnose this. Which premise fails? Or if it's valid, which horn do I take and what do I pay?
