Here's how I understand it, laid out as an argument:

(1) A social decision proceedure should respect the Pareto principle: if literally everyone prefers state of affairs X to state of affairs Y, the procedure should rank X above Y. Seems unobjectionable.

(2) A social decision procedure should respect a minimal form of liberalism: there are at least some personal choices where an individual's own preference should be decisive, regardless of what anyone else thinks. Sen's example: whether you read a particular book is your own damn business.

(3) Now take two people, Prude and Lewd, and one copy of a scandalous book. Prude wants nobody to read it, but if someone must, he'd rather read it himself than have Lewd corrupted by it. Lewd wants everyone to read it, and would especially love to see Prude loosen up and read it. Their preference rankings over the possible outcomes are:

Prude: nobody reads > Prude reads > Lewd reads > both read
Lewd: both read > Prude reads > Lewd reads > nobody reads

(4) By the liberal principle (2), Prude's preference about his own reading is decisive: since he prefers not-reading to reading (holding Lewd's behavior fixed), the social ranking must put "nobody reads" above "Prude reads." And Lewd's preference about his own reading is decisive: since he prefers reading to not-reading, the social ranking must put "Lewd reads" above "nobody reads." So we get: Lewd reads > nobody reads > Prude reads.

(5) But by the Pareto principle (1): both Prude and Lewd prefer "Prude reads" to "Lewd reads." Check their rankings — Prude has Prude-reads above Lewd-reads; Lewd also has Prude-reads above Lewd-reads. So the social ranking must put "Prude reads" above "Lewd reads."

(6) So we have a cycle: Prude reads > Lewd reads > nobody reads > Prude reads. No outcome is best. The procedure can't deliver a consistent social choice.

(7) Therefore, no social decision procedure can satisfy both the Pareto principle and even a minimal form of liberalism. Two bedrock liberal-democratic commitments are logically incompatible.
