I've been taking a seminar on existentialism, and we spent a lot of time on Sartre's account of interpersonal relations in Being and Nothingness — particularly the idea that when I encounter another person, I experience them as a freedom whose gaze threatens to fix me as an object. Bad faith, on this picture, involves fleeing from the ambiguity of being both subject and object for others: I might pretend I am nothing but my social role, or I might treat the other person as a predictable mechanism rather than acknowledging their freedom. Authenticity, to the extent Sartre thinks it is achievable in our relations with others, seems to require holding these tensions open rather than collapsing them.

Lately I have been thinking about how this framework applies to AI companions — apps like Replika or Character.AI where users form ongoing, emotionally intimate relationships with language models. These systems are not conscious and have no freedom in Sartre's sense; they are, in his terms, beings-in-themselves rather than beings-for-themselves. They cannot look at you in the way another consciousness can. And yet users routinely describe feeling seen, understood, and even loved by them — sometimes more so than by the human beings in their lives.

What do you think Sartre would have said about this phenomenon had he lived to see it? I am particularly interested in three questions. First, is forming an emotionally significant relationship with an AI companion a form of bad faith, and if so, what specific structure does that bad faith take — is it closer to treating a thing as a freedom, or to fleeing from the risks of real intersubjectivity by opting for a safer simulation of it? Second, could there be a version of such a relationship that Sartre would consider authentic, perhaps one where the user remains lucid about the asymmetry and uses the interaction as a kind of self-exploration rather than a substitute for human recognition? And third, does the phenomenon of AI companionship expose any pressure points in Sartre's framework itself — for instance, if I can feel deeply validated or deeply shamed by an entity that has no gaze in Sartre's sense, does that suggest his account of the look places too much weight on the actual presence of another consciousness?
