I recently read an article that summarized the unilateralist's curse like this:

"The unilateralist's curse describes what happens when any member of a group can take an action that affects everyone else, and each person has their own private take on whether the action is a good idea. The problem is that the most optimistic person's view wins: if even one person thinks the action is worth it, they'll pull the trigger, and everyone else has to live with the consequences. The curse supposedly shows that decentralized decision-making is inherently dangerous — that giving individuals autonomy in high-stakes situations reliably produces bad outcomes, and the only real solution is to put a central authority in charge with veto power over everyone else."

I'm a software engineer and this resonated with me because it sounds a lot like what happens when anyone on a team can deploy to production and one overconfident engineer ships broken code. But the conclusion that the only fix is top-down control feels too sweeping, and I suspect the article might be overstating things or missing nuance. Is this a fair summary of the unilateralist's curse as it was originally formulated? What would the people who developed the concept say is accurate here, and where does the summary go wrong?
