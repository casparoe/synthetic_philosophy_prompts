I've been working on a novel where the protagonist is a cognitive neuroscientist, and in the course of research I kept bumping into this phrase "consciousness as a user illusion." It's such a novelist-friendly idea — the notion that what we experience as our rich inner life is really just a kind of dashboard, a simplified readout that hides the incomprehensible complexity of what our brains are actually doing. I got excited and brought it up at a dinner party, and a friend who did a philosophy minor launched into this summary:

"Right, so the idea is that consciousness is like the desktop on your computer. When you drag a file to the trash, you're not literally reaching into the circuits — you're manipulating icons that hide the machine code. Consciousness works the same way: it's a handy fiction your brain generates so you can get through the day without drowning in neural noise. The really wild part is that experiments show your brain makes decisions before you're consciously aware of them, so consciousness isn't actually in the driver's seat — it just retroactively tells itself a story about being in charge. Dennett and people like him have pretty much proved this. And if conciousness is just a post-hoc narrative, then free will is obviously an illusion too, at least in any sense worth wanting."

Now, as a fiction writer, I find the metaphor electrifying — the idea of a character who gradually realizes their sense of agency is a kind of user interface their brain is running is exactly the sort of thing I want to explore. But as someone who's actually read a bit of Dennett, I remember him being weirdly insistent that he *does* believe in free will, and he seems more careful than "consciousness is just a story." My friend's version feels like it's doing a lot of work sliding from a metaphor to a empirical claim to a metaphysical conclusion, and I can't tell where the slide happens. Could you go through his summary and separate what a defender of the user-illusion view would actually stand behind from what's a mischaracterization or an overreach? I'm particularly curious whether the jump from the desktop metaphor to "free will is an illusion" is something the view actually entails or whether my friend is smuggling in extra premises.
