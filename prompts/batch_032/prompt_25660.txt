I've been defending the following position in my work on the meta-problem of consciousness: that the problem of explaining why we have phenomenal intuitions—why we judge that there is a hard problem of consciousness, why we are inclined to describe our experience in qualitative terms, why we find conceivability arguments and knowledge arguments compelling—is a well-posed scientific and philosophical problem in its own right, one that can in principle be solved without solving the hard problem itself. Moreover, I hold that a successful solution to the meta-problem would likely dissolve the hard problem rather than answer it: once we fully understand the cognitive mechanisms that generate our sense of an explanatory gap, the gap itself would no longer seem to demand a separate ontological solution.
