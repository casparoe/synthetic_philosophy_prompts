In a seminar on cultural evolution, a colleague argued that the meme–gene analogy is more than a metaphor: it licenses a full Darwinian science of culture. On his view, memes are to cultural inheritance exactly what genes are to biological inheritance — discrete units that vary, are differentially retained, and accumulate adaptations over time — and therefore the entire apparatus of population genetics, from replicator dynamics to fitness landscapes to drift, can be imported with only minor adjustments. Another participant objected that the analogy is loose talk that breaks the moment you press on it, and that calling something a "meme" adds no explanatory power beyond what we already have from the social sciences.

I want a systematic audit of the meme–gene analogy. Please provide:

1. A table of correspondences: what plays the role of gene, allele, genotype, phenotype, organism, population, mutation, recombination, fitness, selection pressure, drift, and the environment in the cultural domain. Flag any entries where the mapping is forced or where there is no natural counterpart.

2. The structural disanalogies: identify the features of genetic evolution that have no clear analogue in cultural transmission — the Weismann barrier, the genotype–phenotype distinction, particulate inheritance, the random-variation-then-selection logic — and explain, for each, whether it is a surface-level difference or one that undermines the analogy's intended use.

3. The features cultural transmission has that biological evolution lacks — blending inheritance, Lamarckian effects, intentional design, horizontal transmission at scale, group-level selection without group extinction — and whether these can be accommodated within a Darwinian framework or require a different kind of theory.

4. An assessment of what the analogy can and cannot carry. Specifically: does it support the claim that culture exhibits cumulative adaptation by natural selection in anything like the biological sense? Does it support importing formal methods from population genetics? And does the research program survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or does it collapse into a loose heuristic?

Where the analogy fails for its strongest intended use, suggest what a better analogy would be — or whether the whole project of finding a single master analogy for cultural change is itself misguided.
