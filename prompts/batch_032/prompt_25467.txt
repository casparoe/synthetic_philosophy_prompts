So I've been going down this rabbit hole about genetic selection and designer babies and all that — my partner and I are thinking about having kids and we did one of those carrier screening things, and it got me reading. I've been on forums and read a few articles (not academic stuff really, more like Aeon and Vox and that kind of thing) and I've noticed people have really different gut reactions that they can't always name. Some people think you're basically obligated to pick the embryo with the best shot at a good life, others think there's something creepy about the whole enterprise even if you can't point to a specific harm, others are worried about what it says to disabled people. And I've realized that when I try to sort out my own views, I'm kind of all over the place depending on the case.

I want to make a "what's your actual position on genetic selection?" quiz for my friends — like one of those BuzzFeed-style things but not stupid. The idea is to sort people by what they say about concrete scenarios, not by what label they'd pick for themselves. So no questions like "do you think parents should play God?" or "are you a eugenicist?". I'm imagining maybe eight to ten questions, each a specific case (like: you're doing IVF and have five embryos, one has a genetic marker for a 60% chance of severe depression by age 30, the others don't — do you have a moral reason to pick a different embryo? or: a new prenatal test can tell you your fetus will be born deaf, and you'd terminate — but the Deaf community says this is eugenics; are they right?), with two or three answer options each. The scoring key should map people onto positions like strong procreative beneficence (you're obligated to select for the best expected life), weak procreative beneficence (it's good but not required), liberal neutrality (the state stays out, parents can choose for basically any reason), the expressivist worry (selection against disability sends a harmful message even if no specific person is harmed), and a more "givenness" view (there's value in accepting the child you get rather than treating reproduction like a shopping catalogue). If there are logical positions that nobody really defends or that the quiz cant cleanly separate, just note that honestly rather than forcing everything into neat boxes. I'd rather have gaps than a tidy lie.
