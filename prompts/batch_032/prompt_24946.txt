Exam question: "Explain the skeptical paradox about rule-following that Kripke attributes to Wittgenstein. What role does the claim that meaning is normative play in generating the paradox? Then explain the 'skeptical solution' that Kripke's Wittgenstein proposes, making clear what makes it a skeptical rather than a straight solution."

Student answer:

"The skeptical paradox is that there is no fact about a speaker that determines what they mean by a word. Kripke's Wittgenstein asks us to consider a speaker who has computed sums only for numbers below 57. The skeptic challenges us to show that by 'plus' the speaker meant addition rather than 'quaddition,' where x quus y equals x + y if x and y are both less than 57, and 5 otherwise. Any candidate fact about the speaker's past mental states or behavior seems consistent with both interpretations. For example, the speaker might have had a mental image of a plus sign, but the skeptic can reinterpret that image as representing quaddition. The speaker might appeal to their dispositions, but dispositions are finite and speakers are disposed to make mistakes, so dispositions don't fix what the speaker ought to say.

The normativity of meaning is central to generating the paradox. Meaning is normative because to mean something by a word is to commit yourself to using it in certain ways and not others—it tells you what you should say, not just what you will say. The paradox arises because facts about past behavior and mental states are merely descriptive, while meaning is normative. This gap between descriptive facts and normative facts is what makes the skeptical challenge so powerful: no set of descriptive facts can by themselves entail a normative conclusion about how a word ought to be used.

The skeptical solution accepts the skeptical conclusion that there are no truth conditions for meaning statements—there is no fact of the matter about what a speaker means. But it replaces truth conditions with assertability conditions. The assertability conditions are social: I am entitled to assert that Jones means addition by 'plus' if Jones's use of the term agrees sufficiently with the use of other members of the linguistic community. In effect, what constitutes meaning is not an individual fact but a social fact: meaning is constituted by the community's shared practice and the agreement in judgments that underlies it. This is a skeptical solution because it grants the skeptic's main point—there are no individualistic meaning-constituting facts—and then reconstructs meaning on a social basis. It is not a straight solution because a straight solution would try to identify a fact about the individual that constitutes their meaning, which the skeptic has shown we cannot do."
