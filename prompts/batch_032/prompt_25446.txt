I'm putting together a paper for a metaontology workshop next spring, and I'd love some feedback on the core idea before I sink too much time into it. The paper is about verbal disputes—you know, the idea that a lot of philosophical disagreements are really just people using words differently without realizing it. The standard move in the literature (Chalmers, Hirsch, etc.) is to try to give a criterion for when a dispute is "merely verbal" versus genuinely substantive. But here's the thing I keep getting stuck on: what if the debate over what counts as a verbal dispute is itself a verbal dispute?

Here's the rough argument I'm sketching. Take two metaontologists: one thinks the quantifier is "natural" and carves at the joints (Sider-style), the other thinks there are many equally good meanings for the quantifier and ontology is just choosing a convention (Carnap-style, or Hirsch-style). They disagree about whether the debate between, say, the nihilist and the universalist about composition is verbal or substantive. But notice: they also disagree about what "verbal dispute" means. The Sider-type thinks a dispute is verbal only if the parties are talking past each other using different meanings for some term *and* there's no fact of the matter about which meaning is joint-carving. The Hirsch-type thinks a dispute is verbal whenever the parties could each be speaking truly in their own languages and the choice between those languages is pragmatic. So when they argue about whether the composition debate is verbal, they're partly arguing about what "verbal" means. But that looks like... well, a verbal dispute about verbal disputes.

I want to push this further and argue that this regress isn't just a cute observation—it actually undermines the whole project of trying to give a neutral, theory-independent criterion for distinguishing verbal from substantive disputes. Because any such criterion will embed controversial metaontological commitments. The upshot would be that you can't use the notion of a verbal dispute to deflate ontological debates without already taking sides in those debates. I'm worried this is either (a) totally obvious and already been done to death, or (b) confused in some way I'm not seeing. I'd especially appreciate comments on whether the regress point actually lands, or whether there's a way to charaterize verbal disputes that doesn't beg the question against either side. Also, I'm unsure whether the paper should end on the skeptical note or try to offer a positive proposal—maybe something about how we can still make progress by being more explict about what we're using "exists" to mean in a given context, even if there's no neutral vantage point. Oh, and if you think the whole thing is just a cheap knockoff of something someone's already published, tell me straight—I'd rather find out now than from a referee.
