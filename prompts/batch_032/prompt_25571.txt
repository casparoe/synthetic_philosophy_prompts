I'm putting together a handout for a graduate seminar on consciousness, and I want a decision tree that maps the logical geography of positions on what global workspace theory (GWT) explains, starting from the access/phenomenal consciousness distinction. The tree should sort someone by what they believe about the phenomena, not by which label they like.

The tree should be built from the logical space first: identify the diagnostic questions and the combinations of answers they permit, then locate the actual positions in that space. Each branch node should be a concrete yes/no question phrased so someone can answer it without knowing which position it leads to. No question should just ask "do you agree with Chalmers?" or "are you an illusionist?".

The tree must cover at least these positions as leaves:

- GWT fully explains phenomenal consciousness (P-consciousness just is, or reduces to, global availability)
- GWT explains access consciousness (A-consciousness) only; P-consciousness is a further phenomenon that requires a different kind of explanation
- The A/P distinction itself is misguided or collapses on closer inspection
- GWT explains A-consciousness, and P-consciousness is A-consciousness plus further cognitive functions that cognitive science can in principle explain (no hard problem beyond the "easy" ones)
- GWT explains A-consciousness, and P-consciousness involves a hard problem that no amount of cognitive-scientific explanation will close
- GWT explains A-consciousness, and P-consciousness is real but we have no idea how to explain it (mysterianism about P-consciousness)

For any region of logical space that is unoccupied (the combination of answers is logically possible but nobody defends it), flag it and give a one-sentence diagnosis: is it incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored? Also flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate and explain what additional question would be needed.

Aim for roughly 6–10 branch nodes and 6–8 leaves. The audience is philosophy PhD students who have read Block and at least one presentation of GWT, so you can use terms like "global availability" and "overflow" without defining them, but the diagnostic questions should still test beliefs about cases rather than recognition of jargon.

Please return the result as a JSON object with the following schema:
