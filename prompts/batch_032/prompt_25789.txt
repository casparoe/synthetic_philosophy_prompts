I'm writing up a position paper on teleosemantics and want to check whether my commitments are jointly tenable before I commit to them in print. Here's where I currently stand:

(1) I take teleosemantics to be the most promising approach to naturalizing mental content. On this view, the content of a mental representation is determined by its proper function — what the representation-producing mechanism was evolutionarily selected to indicate — and not by, say, causal-informational relations alone or by interpretivist practices.

(2) I think the disjunction problem is the central challenge for causal-informational theories, and I think teleosemantics solves it: a frog's fly-detector means fly and not fly-or-beebee because the mechanism was selected for indicating flies, even if it occasionally fires for beebees.

(3) I accept the Swampman verdict: if a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me were assembled by sheer cosmic accident — lightning striking a swamp, say — that duplicate would have no mental content whatsoever, because none of its brain states would have an evolutionary history and therefore none would have a proper function. I'm prepared to bite this bullet.

(4) I also accept that Swampman is behaviorally indistinguishable from me. He would write philosophy papers, report having beliefs and desires, pass every cognitive test, and insist — sincerely, from his perspective — that he has mental content.

(5) I hold that mental content plays a genuine causal-explanatory role in the production of behavior. It's not epiphenomenal; when I go to the fridge because I believe there's beer in it, the content of my belief is part of the causal story about why I went to the fridge.

(6) I take teleosemantics to be a theory of representational content only. It says nothing about phenomenal consciousness — what it's like to see red, to feel pain — and I regard questions about consciousness as a separate domain. Nothing in my teleosemantics commits me to a view about qualia one way or the other.

(7) I think that mathematical beliefs — for instance, my belief that 17 is prime — have fully determinate content, and a complete theory of mental content must account for them. I don't yet have a worked-out teleosemantic story for mathematical content, but I'm committed to there being one.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs or subsets are in genuine tension, which are only apparently in tension, and how severe each tension is. I want the tensions ranked by severity, with the most damaging ones first. For each genuine tension, tell me what would have to give to resolve it and what the revision would cost. I'm not signaling which commitment I'm willing to abandon — I want an honest audit.

Finally, please indicate how confident you are in each of your assessments and single out the point you're least sure about. If any part of your analysis leans on a contested reading of the teleosemantics literature, flag that as well.
