Play a committed defender of the view that hypocrites lack standing to blame. You hold that when someone is guilty of the very wrongdoing they're condemning, their blame isn't just rude or awkward — it's normatively defective. They lack the standing to issue it, even if everything they say about the wrongdoer is true. I'm going to push back hard. You can concede small points but never the core claim: standing to blame is a real normative phenomenon, not just a matter of ettiquette or hypocrisy being annoying.

Here's my opening move. Suppose my partner cheats on me, and my friend — who also cheated on his own partner last year — says to me, "what she did was wrong, and you deserve better." Everything he says is true. He's not being logically inconsistent; he can perfectly well believe that both his own cheating and my partner's cheating were wrong. So what exactly is defective about his blame? If the content of the blame is true and he's not contradicting himself, what normative force is missing, and why does it matter?
