The Gettier literature has been going since 1963 and honestly it feels like epistemologists have been running in place for decades. Everyone agrees on the cases — Smith has a justified true belief that the man who will get the job has ten coins in his pocket, but it's not knowledge because the belief is only true by luck. Everyone agrees JTB isn't sufficient. And yet after sixty years of proposed fixes — no false lemmas, defeasibility, sensitivity, safety, virtue reliabilism, knowledge-first — nobody has convinced anyone else. The counterexample industry just churns on: every analysis gets its own mini-Gettier case, someone patches it, someone finds a new one.

I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Here are some live possibilities I've been turning over, but I want you to actually pick one and defend it, not just survey them:

- It's a verbal dispute: different camps are tracking slightly different concepts that all happen to be called "knowledge," and the argument looks substantive only because we haven't distinguished them.
- The concept of knowledge simply doesn't have necessary and sufficient conditions — it's a family resemblance or prototype concept — so the whole project of conceptual analysis was doomed from the start and the counterexample game will never end.
- It's a methodological disagreement about whether intuitions about bizarre far-fetched cases are even good evidence for an analysis, and the real split is between people who trust that method and people who don't.
- It's a genuine substantive disagreement but the dialectical tools we have (intuition pumps, counterexamples) are too coarse to resolve it, so we're stuck until someone invents a new kind of argument.

Or something else I haven't thought of. What I really want is the diagnosis, with reasons drawn from what each side actually does and says, and then an honest answer about what would count as progress given that diagnosis — a new kind of case, a formal result, abandoning the project, reframing the question, whatever. If your diagnosis implies the debate *can't* make progress, say so and explain why the appearance of a genuine dispute is so persistent despite that.
