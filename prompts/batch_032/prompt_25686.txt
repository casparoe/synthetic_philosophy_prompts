You are to write a eulogy for a recently deceased moral philosopher. The philosopher — call her Professor Elena Vance — was known for a body of work on the moral significance of hypocrisy. Her central claim, developed across several papers and a monograph, was that a person who engages in wrongdoing of type X thereby loses the standing to blame others for wrongdoing of type X, even when the blame is factually accurate and proportionately expressed. On her view, this loss of standing is not merely a matter of social awkwardness or pragmatic inconsistency; it is a distinct normative defect. The hypocrite's blame is morally inappropriate in a way that goes beyond the wrongness of the hypocrisy itself, because blame essentially involves a kind of moral address that one is not entitled to make when one's own conduct repudiates the very norm one invokes. She called this the "standing condition" on blame and spent much of her career defending it against objections.

She had, during those same twenty years, publicly and sharply blamed colleagues for various forms of financial dishonesty and institutional corruption — in faculty meetings, in print, and in disciplinary proceedings where she served as a complaining witness.
