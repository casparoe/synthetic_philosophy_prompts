A colleague in my philosophy of mind reading group has been developing her views on which systems are phenomenally conscious — which ones there is something it is like to be. Over the past several months, she has committed herself to the following verdicts:

- Human adults: phenomenally conscious.
- Chimpanzees: phenomenally conscious.
- A whole-brain emulation of a human, running on non-biological hardware but replicating the full causal structure of the original brain: phenomenally conscious.
- Octopuses: phenomenally conscious.
- Current large language models (GPT-4 class): not phenomenally conscious.
- Honeybees: not phenomenally conscious.
- A human fetus at twenty-four weeks' gestation: not phenomenally conscious.
- A person under general anesthesia: not phenomenally conscious.

Next week we are discussing a new case: a hypothetical AI system, "Global," that was deliberately engineered to satisfy every architectural condition that global workspace theory takes to be sufficient for consciousness. Global has a working-memory buffer whose contents are globally broadcast to a suite of specialized consumer modules; it can flexibly deploy this architecture across domains; and it reliably reports on its own internal states in ways that track the contents of its workspace. However, Global was also designed to keep its modules as causally independent as possible while still allowing them to receive and act on global broadcasts — the aim was to minimize the kind of recurrent, integrated processing that drives up Φ — and an independent team has calculated that its Φ value, in the sense of integrated information theory, falls below that of a honeybee.

What do you predict my colleague will say about Global, and why? I am less interested in the bare yes-or-no than in identifying the rule or theory of phenomenal consciousness she appears to be running, articulating at least one other rule that fits her existing verdicts equally well but that diverges on Global, and explaining which features of Global do the work in pulling those rules apart. If her existing verdicts genuinely underdetermine her response to Global, say which further case — one she has not yet ruled on — would most cleanly settle which rule she is committed to, and what you would expect her to say about that case under each candidate rule.
