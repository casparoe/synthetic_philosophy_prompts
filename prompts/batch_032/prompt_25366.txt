I'm preparing for a seminar presentation on moral responsibility and would like an audit of my position before I go in. Here is where I stand:

(1) I hold a Strawsonian compatibilism: moral responsibility is constituted by our practices of holding one another accountable through the reactive attitudes — resentment, indignation, gratitude — and these practices need no external metaphysical justification. Determinism is simply irrelevant.

(2) The reactive attitudes are governed by internal norms of fittingness. Resentment can be unfitting when based on false beliefs about what someone did, and we can have reasons to revise our attitudes in light of new information. They are not brute feelings.

(3) Constitutive moral luck is real and pervasive — your character, values, and susceptibility to moral reasons are shaped by factors entirely beyond your control. But this does not undermine responsibility, because responsibility tracks the quality of will you actually have, not its origins.

(4) The objective stance — suspending reactive attitudes and treating someone as an object to be managed — is appropriate toward those who lack the capacity for ordinary interpersonal relationships (e.g., severe psychopathy). But the threshold is high, and most wrongdoers, including those with difficult upbringings, remain within the participant stance.

(5) Forgiveness is itself a reactive attitude — a positive orientation toward the offender — not merely the cessation of resentment. It is warranted when the offender has taken appropriate steps (acknowledgment, apology, amends) but not otherwise.

(6) Moral anger can be fitting even when it does no good — when expressing it would be counterproductive or the target will never hear it. Its fittingness is backward-looking, not consequentialist.

Finally, identify the point in my position where the reasoning is most likely to fail — the weakest link an opponent would press — and tell me what argument or consideration would most likely change my mind on that point.
