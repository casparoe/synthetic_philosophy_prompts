ok so my two friends got into this huge argument at lunch today and i honestly still dont know who's right. they were talking about that philosophy puzzle where a billionaire says he'll give you a million dollars if, at midnight tonight, you can genuinely *intend* to drink a vial of toxin tomorrow afternoon. the toxin makes you super sick for like a day but no permanent damage. the trick is you get the money the second you form the intention — you don't actually have to drink the stuff. so the question is whether you can form a real intention to do something when you already know you'll have no reason to actually go through with it.

heres how the argument went:

jordan said obviously you cant form the intention. if you know right now that tomorrow you'll already have the million and zero reason to drink the toxin, then you know you won't drink it. and you can't intend to do something you know you won't do. the puzzle just proves intentions have to be about stuff you actually expect to carry out.

maya jumped in and said thats way too fast. people intend to do things all the time that they end up not doing — like intending to go to the gym tomorrow even though deep down they know they'll bail. the intention is real in the moment. so you totally could intend to drink the toxin at midnight, collect the million, and then just... not drink it. the intention was real when it counted.

jordan said thats not the same thing. the gym case is self-deception — you're not fully admitting to yourself that you'll bail. but the toxin puzzle forces you to be completely clear-eyed: you know with certainty that drinking the toxin tomorrow serves no purpose. you can't hide from that fact the way you can hide from knowing you'll skip the gym.

maya said okay but what if you hire someone to threaten your family unless you drink the toxin? then youd have a reason to drink it, so you could form the intention. jordan said that changes the whole scenario — now you have an actual reason to drink it, so of course you can intend it. the puzzle is specifically about whether you can intend it *without* changing the payoffs.

then maya said fine but what about this: what if you just decide to be the kind of person who follows through on intentions? like you make a policy of always doing what you intended even when the reasons change. then you could intend to drink it because you know your own policy will make you follow through. jordan said thats circular — you'd only adopt that policy if you already wanted to be able to form the intention, but the policy only works if you'll actually follow it, and you'll only follow it if the policy is worth the cost of drinking toxin for no reason. which it isnt.

maya's last move was to say look the whole thing is a trick about timing. at midnight you form the intention. between midnight and tomorrow afternoon you have the money. maybe you cant intend something while simultaneously knowing you wont do it, but you can intend it and *then* change your mind. intentions arent permanent. jordan said that just restates the problem — if at the moment of forming the intention you're already planning to change your mind, then it was never a genuine intention to begin with.

they went back and forth for like twenty more minutes and never got anywhere. can you go through their arguments move by move and say who gets the better of each exchange? and is there something neither of them is seeing that might break the stalemate?
