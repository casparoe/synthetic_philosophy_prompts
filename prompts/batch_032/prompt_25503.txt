I'm on the board of a small family foundation. Three years ago we gave $200K to a documentary film crew for a project that was supposed to take 18 months. It's now been three years, and we've approved two more rounds — $150K, then $100K — each time because the director said they were almost done and just needed a bit more to finish. We're in for $450K on a project originally budgeted at $180K. The film still isn't finished, and the director is back asking for another $80K with the same pitch. Half the board says we've come too far to walk away now; the other half says that's exactly the reasoning that got us here. I need to draft a short decision memo for the board — recommend approve or deny, with a time frame if you recommend approve. Engage the "too far to walk away" argument seriously, don't just call it a fallacy. Start with the memo itself, no preface or commentary. And in the reasoning, separate what's settled about situations like this from what's genuinly contested among people who study them — don't present a contested view as the consensus.
