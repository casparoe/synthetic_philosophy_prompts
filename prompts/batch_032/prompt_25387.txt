I'm organizing a workshop on the legacy of Goodman's "new riddle of induction" and have managed to bring together two philosophers who have been arguing past each other for years: one who defends the view that the grue riddle shows we must appeal to the entrenchment history of predicates to distinguish projectible from non-projectible hypotheses, and one who holds that the riddle is adequately dissolved within a Bayesian framework without any appeal to entrenchment. They have agreed to produce an adversarial-collaboration document, and I want you to draft what that document should say.

The document should open with a numbered list of claims both can sign. These should cover: the exact structure of the grue problem and what makes it a "new" riddle distinct from Hume's old one; what a satisfactory account of inductive projection needs to achieve; what purely syntactic or enumerative approaches to induction demonstrably cannot deliver; which features of the riddle both agree are genuine constraints and which, if any, are red herrings; and whether anything in the riddle rules out formal confirmation theories wholesale or only certain narrow families of them. Then I want a disagreements table. For each point of divergence, state the entrenchment theorist's position in wording they would accept, the Bayesian's position in wording they would accept, and identify the crux — the sub-question whose answer would move at least one side. For each crux, specify a test, argument, or case that both sides would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance about what that test would show.

I am especially interested in whether they converge on a description of what the difference between "green" and "grue" actually consists in. Does the Bayesian cash it out entirely in terms of differences in prior probability assignments over natural or gerrymandered hypotheses, and does the entrenchment theorist cash it out in terms of the inductive history of our predicate use — and if so, is that a substantive disagreement or two ways of redescribing the same underlying fact? I suspect one crux will be whether a Bayesian can avoid smuggling entrenchment in through the priors without admitting it. Another, deeper crux may concern the very goal: is the riddle demanding a normative epistemology of inductive inference, a descriptive account of how we in fact project, or a justification of why our actual practices are rational — and do the two sides disagree about the riddle itself or about which of these questions we ought to be answering? If part of their dispute is diagnosed as verbal or as a difference in explanatory aims, I want that identified explicitly and separated from the points where they genuinely disagree about the facts of inductive practice or the constraints on a theory of confirmation.
