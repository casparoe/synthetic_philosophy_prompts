I've been reading Nozick lately — I'm a retired physicist, so the whole style of argument in Anarchy, State, and Utopia feels very familar, like setting up initial conditions and watching a system evolve. The Wilt Chamberlain argument has really stuck with me. You start with whatever patterned distribution you think is just, everyone has their fair share, and then a million people freely choose to pay twenty-five cents to watch Wilt play basketball, and suddenly the pattern is gone even though every single step was voluntary. It's such a clean demonstration that you can't have both liberty and a patterned principle of justice — one of them has to give.

I'd like a villanelle about this. The two refrains should represent the two forces in tension: one for the claim that justice requires maintaining the pattern, the other for the claim that free transfers preserve justice. The form should do the philosophical work — the refrains alternate and never really engage each other until they're forced together in the final couplet, which is exactly the problem Nozick is pointing at. Someone who knows the argument should recognize it without needing Wilt's name in the poem; someone who doesn't know it should still get a good poem. Let the structure carry the idea, not the labels.
