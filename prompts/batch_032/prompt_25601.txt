I'm a novelist—mid-career, mostly literary fiction, the kind of thing that needs a fair bit of research to pull off. Over the last couple years I've found myself leaning on LLMs more and more: summarizing source material for historical settings, sketching character psycologies drawing on concepts I half-remember from undergrad, even talking through the ethical dilemmas my characters are tangled up in. And I've started to notice something that bothers me. When I go back to my own notes from a book I researched the hard way (libraries, interviews, months of reading), the understanding feels deeper, more textured, more mine. The AI-assisted research gets the job done faster but the knowledge feels... thinner? Like I've rented it instead of owning it.

So here's the position I've landed on: epistemic dependence on AI systems is qualitatively different from dependence on traditonal cognitive tools (books, calculators, colleagues). The difference isn't just about reliability or speed. It's that AI short-circuits the process of intellectual struggle—the slow, frustrating, often inefficient work of synthesizing, misunderstanding and correcting, building mental models—that constitutes genuine understanding. Using AI for intellectual tasks doesn't just give you answers; it gradually erodes your capacity to arrive at them yourself, in a way that using a calculator or an encyclopedia doesn't. If this is right, there's a distinctive kind of epistemic harm here, not captured by familiar worries about misinformation or over-reliance on experts.

I want to know what would count against this view. Not just objections I'd shrug off, but the evidence and arguments that should genuinely move me. I want this structured as a JSON object with the following fields:

- "defeaters": an array of objects, each with:
  - "name": a short label for the defeater (string)
  - "description": what the challenge is and why it bites (string)
  - "severity": one of "fatal", "major", "moderate", "minor" (string)
  - "defenders_concede": whether a reasonable defender of my position would grant this as counting against them, one of "yes", "partially", "no" (string)
  - "category": one of "empirical", "conceptual", "normative", "historical" (string)
  - "requires_future_evidence": boolean
- "insulation_assessment": a string evaluating whether the position has been (or could be) qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that's a problem for a view of this kind
- "strongest_remaining_case": a string describing what the position's strongest claim is after accounting for all defeaters

Rank the defeaters from most to least threatening. And be honest—if the view crumbles under scrutiny, I'd rather know than nurse a pet theory that doesn't hold up.
