A colleague and I have been going back and forth on this and I'm starting to think the argument proves too much, but I can't locate the exact misstep. Here's the setup:

(1) In a symmetric one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma between two perfect reasoners who know each other to be perfect reasoners, each can replicate the other's reasoning.
(2) So each knows: if I settle on defection, the other will replicate that reasoning and also settle on defection; if I settle on cooperation, the other will replicate that and also settle on cooperation.
(3) Since (C,C) is better for each than (D,D), each should cooperate.
(4) This is the superrationality argument — it doesn't need causal influence between the choices, just common knowledge of rationality plus symmetry.

Now the extension that bothers me:

(5) The same reasoning seems to apply to any symmetric game between perfect reasoners with common knowledge.
(6) So consider the "Bonus Game": both choose A → each gets $2; both choose B → each gets $1; one chooses A and the other B → the A-chooser gets $100 and the B-chooser gets $0.
(7) By superrational reasoning: if I pick B, my counterpart replicates that and also picks B, giving me $1. If I pick A, my counterpart also picks A, giving me $2. So I should pick A.
(8) But A is strictly dominated: no matter what the other does, B yields a better or equal payoff. (If they play A, I get $100 by playing B vs. $2 by playing A; if they play B, I get $1 by playing B vs. $0 by playing A.)
(9) So superrationality recommends a dominated strategy — which looks like a reductio.
(10) The superrationalist replies: step (8) assumes the other player's choice is independent of mine, which is exactly what's at issue. You can't refute superrationality by assuming causal decision theory.
(11) But now I worry the superrationalist has made their position unfalsifiable: any counterexample gets dismissed as "begging the question" against superrationality.

Where does this go wrong? Is the move from the PD to the Bonus Game legitimate, or is there a principled difference between them? If you reject a premise, which one and what does giving it up cost?
