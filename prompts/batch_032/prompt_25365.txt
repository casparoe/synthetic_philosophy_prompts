So I was arguing with my friend about the Ellsberg paradox till like 2 AM last night — you know, the one with the two urns? Urn A has exactly 50 red and 50 black balls. Urn B has 100 balls but the mix is totally unknown, could be anything from all red to all black. You pick a color, draw from whichever urn you want, and win $100 if your color comes up. Basically everyone prefers to draw from Urn A, and they prefer it for both colors — if they pick red they want Urn A, if they pick black they also want Urn A. Which is weird because if you thought Urn B had more reds you'd pick it when betting on red, and if you thought it had more blacks you'd pick it when betting on black. Preferring Urn A for both colors means you're acting like the urn is simultaneously less than half red and less than half black, which is impossible.

My friend was adamant that this just proves people are irrational — we violate the axioms of expected utility theory and that's that. But I kept pushing back. I said, look, can't you just say that ambiguity itself is a bad thing, and build that into your utility function? Like, winning $100 from a gamble where you know the odds is just a different outcome — a better outcome, experientially — than winning $100 from a gamble where you're in the dark. So you're still maximizing expected utility, you've just defined your outcomes more richly. The probability distribution over Urn B can be whatever you want — say 50/50 by symmetry — and the utility difference handles the rest.

He said I was cheating by redescribing the outcomes and that the whole point of the paradox is that no single probability distribution can make both choices come out as expected-utility-maximizing, even with a fancy utility function. Something about the Sure-Thing Principle. But I don't see why my move is illegitamate — isn't this exactly how we handle any preference? If you hate the feeling of suspense, we don't say you're irrational, we just say suspense is a cost. Why is ambiguity different? We went in circles for hours and I still can't tell if I'm being clever or just refusing to face the music. What's actually wrong with my rescue attempt?
