I teach an upper-level metaphysics seminar and I'm writing the take-home final. I want one essay question on dispositions, finks, and masks. The students have read Martin, Lewis, Bird, and Molnar, so they've got the basic moves down. Here's what I want the question to actually test: the distinction between finks and masks is supposed to matter because they're different kinds of counterexample to the simple conditional analysis — finks remove the disposition when the stimulus condition kicks in, masks leave the disposition intact but block the manifestation. But I'm not sure my students have thought hard about whether this distinction really holds up under pressure, or whether some cases can be legitimately redescribed either way depending on how you individuate dispositions or how you specify the stimulus and manifestation. And more to the point, whether the distinction even matters for what a revised conditional analysis would need to look like — or whether both kinds of case push us toward the same kind of fix.

So please write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain. The question should be unanswerable by just reciting what finks and masks are — it's gotta force them to think about the relationship between the two and what that relationship means for the conditional analysis project. The marking scheme should say what an A answer does that a B answer typically misses, and where the common pitfalls are.
