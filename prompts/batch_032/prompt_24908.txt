ok so i've been noticing this thing and i can't tell if it's just me or if it's an actual Thing that people have written about. it goes like this: you're in a situation where you can see two possible choices, and you can also see that each choice will probably lead to something bad. but here's the weird part — the bad thing isn't the same in both cases. like, if you choose A, bad thing X happens because of A. if you choose B, bad thing Y happens because of B. and you can see all this in advance. but you still have to choose. and then whichever one you pick, you end up thinking "i should have picked the other one" because the bad thing that happened was caused by your choice. but if you had picked the other one, you'd be thinking the exact same thing, just about a different bad outcome.

the example that made me start thinking about this: my friend group had two plans for saturday, a beach trip and a movie night. i wanted to do the beach but i was worried it would rain, so i pushed for the movie. it didn't rain, and everyone was annoyed we missed the beach. but here's the thing — if i had pushed for the beach, it WOULD have rained (i checked the forecast afterwards and the rain was like 50/50 all week, it genuinely could have gone either way). so i would have been the guy who made everyone go to the beach in the rain. either way i look dumb. and the forecast was uncertain enough that i couldn't have known.

a more serious one: my cousin was trying to decide between two colleges. one was close to home, one was far away. she picked the close one because her mom has health issues and she wanted to be nearby. her mom ended up being fine, and now my cousin feels like she gave up the better school for no reason. but if she had gone far away and her mom had gotten sick, she would have felt terrible about that too. and at the time she had to decide, the doctors genuinly didn't know which way it would go.

i feel like this is different from a self-fulfilling prophecy, because in a self-fulfilling prophecy your belief about the outcome causes the outcome. here the outcome isn't caused by your belief — it's more like the universe is set up so that whichever path you take, that path contains a reason to regret taking it. and it's different from just regular bad luck because the regret is built into the structure of the choice itself, you can see it coming and it still doesn't help.

is there a name for this? like in philosophy or decision theory or whatever? and more importantly, is there anything you're actually supposed to DO in a situation like this, or is it just a kind of trap where rational decision-making breaks down? i'm not just looking for a word — i want to understand what exactly this phenomenon is and how it's different from the nearby things people confuse it with.
