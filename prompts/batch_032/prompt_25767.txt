I serve on my university hospital's clinical ethics committee, and we're about to rule on a new proposal. I've spent the last week going back through our precedents to make sure we're consistent, and I'm starting to worry that our past decisions don't settle this one as neatly as my colleagues assume. I'd like an outside read before we vote.

Here are the cases we've ruled on over the past five years, with the decisions we reached:

- Approved: insulin pumps for type 1 diabetes.
- Rejected: erythropoietin for competitive cyclists with normal hematocrit levels.
- Approved: cochlear implants for children born profoundly deaf.
- Rejected: memory-enhancing neural implants for healthy adults with no cognitive deficits.
- Approved: SSRIs for patients with a formal diagnosis of major depressive disorder.
- Rejected: SSRIs requested by healthy adults for "cosmetic" mood elevation.
- Approved with conditions: growth hormone for children below the 1st percentile in height who have no diagnosed growth hormone deficiency (we required psychological screening and ongoing monitoring).
- Rejected: growth hormone for children at the 25th percentile whose parents simply want them to be taller.
- Approved: vaccines for disease prevention.
- Approved with conditions: beta-blockers for professional musicians with debilitating performance anxiety (we required that the anxiety be severe enough to threaten their livelihood and that they also engage in non-pharmacological therapy).

The new proposal is for a gene therapy administered to healthy adults at age 50. It prevents the normal, gradual cognitive slowing that comes with aging — maintaining processing speed, working memory, and executive function at the recipient's age-50 baseline indefinitely. It does not enhance beyond that baseline; it just arrests the decline. The applicants argue that this is continuous with preventive medicine, like vaccines. The critics say it medicalizes normal aging and is enhancement by another name.

If we're trying to be consistent with our own precedents, what should we decide? Which of our past cases is the closest analog, and what principle would we be committing ourselves to with either choice? If our precedents genuinely underdetermine the answer, I'd like to know what the competing principles are and which further case would tell them apart. I'd also appreciate it if you could say how confident you are in your analysis and flag the point you're least sure about — whether that's the interpretation of one of our precedents, the way you've characterized the candidate principles, or something else.
