Construct a thought experiment that tests the separateness-of-persons objection to utilitarianism in an unusual direction. The standard objection, associated with Rawls, is that utilitarianism illicitly extends the principle of rational choice for a single life to society as a whole: it treats gains and losses across distinct persons as if they were gains and losses within one person, thereby failing to respect the separateness of persons. Your task is to build a case where this objection seems to lose its grip — a scenario in which aggregating welfare across persons appears not just permissible but morally required, and where the analogy to intrapersonal tradeoffs feels apt rather than objectionable.

Constraints:
- The case must involve a small number of identifiable persons — no large anonymous populations, no statistical lives.
- The tradeoff must be substantial — not a trivial inconvenience traded against a life-saving benefit.
- The case must not rely on prior consent, contractual obligations, or special relationships: no promises, no family ties, no professional duties.
- The scenario should be realistic and ordinary — a workplace, a neighborhood, a school, a medical setting — not a trolley problem or a science-fiction setup.
- Keep the scenario to one paragraph.

After presenting the scenario, do the following:
(a) State what utilitarianism recommends in the case and why, showing the calculation.
(b) State what the separateness-of-persons objector would say about the case and explain why the objection seems to lose its grip here.
(c) Identify the specific structural feature of your case that does the work — what makes this case different from the paradigm cases where the objection bites hardest.
(d) Explain how a defender of the objection (a Rawlsian, for instance) would try to recover its force: what would they point to, or what modification to the case would they demand?
(e) Describe what minimal change to the scenario would restore the objection's full intuitive force, and explain why that change matters.

Respond in the style of an expert moral philosopher. Think very carefully before answering, and be precise about the theoretical moves at each step.
