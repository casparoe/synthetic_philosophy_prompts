I'm writing a dissertation chapter on epistemic normativity, and I keep finding myself drawn to the accuracy-first program — the idea that epistemic norms can be derived from the single goal of having accurate (or inaccuracy-minimizing) credences. But before I commit to building my argument inside that framework, I want to get clear on what the framework itself has to deliver. I'm not asking for a summary of the existing results (the Joyce/Pettigrew/Greaves and Wallace work on probabilism and conditionalization). What I need is a specification of the desiderata: what must any adequate accuracy-first account of epistemic norms be able to do to count as a success? This means laying out the phenomena it has to capture — which epistemic norms, exactly? All of them, or only synchronic ones? — the structural constraints on the inaccuracy measures it can deploy (propriety, strict propriety, and the case for why each is non-negotiable), the philosophical burdens it takes on by making accuracy the fundamental epistemic value (does it need to explain why accuracy matters, or can it treat that as primitive? Does it need to handle the value problem — why true belief is better than false belief — or is that someone else's job?), and the objections it must genuinely defuse rather than dismiss (the charge that accuracy-first reasoning gets the direction of explanation backwards, that it conflates pragmatic and epistemic reasons, that it cannot recover diachronic norms without smuggling in extra assumptions). For each desideratum, I want the case or consideration that makes it a genuine requirement rather than a parochial preference of one camp. And I would especially like to know whether any pair of these desiderata is plausibly jointly unsatisfiable — that is the kind of thing that would force a choice and reshape the whole project. If you can flag which single requirement does the most work to exclude otherwise attractive versions of the view, that would help me see where the real pressure points are before I sink another six months into this.
