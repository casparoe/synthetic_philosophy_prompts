I'm taking a metaphysics seminar this semester and we spent three weeks on mereology and I think I've broken my brain. The special composition question — when do some things add up to a whole? — sounded straightforward at first but now I can't stop seeing edge cases everywhere. I started keeping a note on my phone of cases and my gut reactions, and the list has gotten kind of long. I'm hoping someone can look at my verdicts and tell me if there's actually a coherent principle behind them or if I need to give some up.

Here they are, roughly in the order I thought of them:

1. The grains of sand on a beach: they compose something (the beach).
2. The molecules that currently make up my body: they compose something (me).
3. Those exact same molecules, if they were scattered evenly across the Milky Way: they do not compose anything.
4. A hammer head and its handle, attached normally: they compose a hammer.
5. That same head and handle, lying unassembled in a drawer six inches apart: they do not compose anything.
6. Two strangers standing next to each other at a bus stop: they do not compose anything.
7. Conjoined twins sharing a torso: I think they compose something, but I can't decide whether it's one thing or two, and this case genuinly bothers me.
8. A cloud: it composes something, even though its boundaries are vague and its water droplets are constantly being replaced.
9. The Eiffel Tower and my left shoe: they do not compose anything.
10. A human body five minutes after death: it composes something, and it seems to be the same object that was there five minutes earlier when the person was alive, though something important has changed.
11. A brand new committee whose members have never met: I don't think they compose anything beyond the individuals. But a committee that's been working together for years, with established procedures and shared norms — that feels like it composes something, maybe a group agent.
12. All the hydrogen atoms in the sun: they compose something (the sun), but I'm not sure whether the sun is an object over and above the particles arranged sun-wise, or just the particles so arranged.

I want you to extract whatever principle or small set of principles best captures these verdicts. For each candidate principle, state it precisely, walk through how it handles each of my twelve cases, and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and why. Then apply each principle to a case I haven't considered: a pair of particles that have become quantum-entangled and are then separated by a light-year. Do they compose something according to each principle?

Give me the whole thing as a JSON object with this schema:

- "candidate_principles": array of objects, each with:
  - "name": string (a short label)

  - "rationale": string (one paragraph explaining the principle's motivation and why it draws the lines where it does)
