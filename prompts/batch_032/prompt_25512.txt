I am a PhD student in philosophy working on the formal structure of value comparisons, and I have been developing a study on how people make pairwise judgments about which of two painful experiences is worse. A colleague in my department — a philosopher who works on population ethics and the metaphysics of value — has agreed to serve as a single-case pilot subject. I presented her with a series of pairwise comparisons involving pains of different intensities and durations, and she gave me her considered verdicts on which of the two was better, where "better" means less bad. We used an ordinal intensity scale we both accept as rough-and-ready: excruciating, severe, moderate, mild, and very mild discomfort. Here are her verdicts.

One minute of severe pain versus two minutes of severe pain: the first is better.
One minute of severe pain versus fifteen minutes of moderate pain: the first is better.
Fifteen minutes of moderate pain versus thirty minutes of moderate pain: the first is better.
Fifteen minutes of moderate pain versus two hours of mild pain: the first is better.
Two hours of mild pain versus four hours of mild pain: the first is better.
Two hours of mild pain versus two days of very mild discomfort: the second is better.
One minute of severe pain versus two days of very mild discomfort: the first is better.
Fifteen minutes of moderate pain versus two days of very mild discomfort: the first is better.

I now want to present her with one further comparison: one minute of severe pain versus two hours of mild pain. Before I do, I would like your help thinking through what her answer is likely to be, given the pattern of her earlier verdicts. In particular, I would like you to do three things. First, identify at least two distinct rules or principles that fit all of her verdicts so far and that yield different predictions for the new comparison. Second, give me your best guess about which rule she is actually running, with your reasons for preferring it, and tell me what she will say about the new case under that rule. Third, propose one further pairwise comparison — not the one I am about to give her — that would most cleanly discriminate between the candidate rules you have identified.

Please write your response in continuous prose, the way you would write an email or a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables. I would also ask that, wherever the philosophical literature on spectrum arguments and the transitivity of "better than" is divided, you flag what is genuinely contested among specialists rather than presenting one side as settled consensus. I am less interested in being told what the right answer is than in understanding the shape of the disagreement and where my colleague's pattern of verdicts places her within it.
