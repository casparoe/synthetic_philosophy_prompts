I'm putting together a research initiative on the welfare implications of habitat destruction for wild animals, with funding for roughly three postdocs over four years — one ecologist, one philosopher, and one with a background in welfare biology or population ethics. The central question is whether anthropogenic habitat destruction tends to increase or decrease net wild animal suffering, and what follows for conservation policy.

I need to decide which sub-questions to investigate first. Please identify what you think are the five or six most important open questions in this area, rank them in order of priority, and defend the ranking. For each question, address: (a) why it is upstream of other questions — what else depends on getting it right; (b) how tractable it is with current methods, and which discipline is best positioned to make progress; (c) what kind of result over the next few years would most change the overall assessment; and (d) whether the question is neglected relative to its importance.

In your ranking, please distinguish between questions that are decision-relevant for conservation policy now and questions that are more foundational but whose answers might not change near-term choices. I am particularly interested in whether there are questions whose resolution would flip the sign of the overall welfare assessment — from "habitat destruction is net bad for wild animals" to "net good" or vice versa — and whether those deserve priority even if they are harder to answer.
