I'm trying to figure out if my views on reproductive ethics hang together and I'd appreciate a consistency check. Here's where I've landed:

(a) Procreative beneficence: if you're going to have a child anyway, and you can select between embryo A and embryo B, you have a moral reason to pick the one whose life can be expected to go better.

(b) But this reason is defeasible—it can be outweighed by other considerations, like parental autonomy or the costs of selection.

(c) I think there's no moral difference between selecting for disease resistance and selecting for traits like height or IQ, provided the expected welfare gain is comparable. The "therapy/enhancement" distinction doesn't track anything morally deep.

(d) I also think the state shouldn't coerce reproductive choices—this is the "liberal" part of liberal eugenics.

(e) But I think the state may subsidize or nudge people toward selecting for traits that are public goods (e.g., lower disease burden), in the same way it nudges people toward vaccination.

(f) I think disability rights critiques of selective reproduction have real force and we should take seriously the expressive harm of a society that systematically selects against certain traits.

(g) Yet I don't think those critiques ultimately defeat procreative beneficence—they just require us to be careful about how we talk about it and to support disabled people who already exist.

Do (e) and (f) pull against each other? And does (c) commit me to anything awkward when combined with (d)—like, if I really think IQ selection is morally on par with disease selection, does my liberalism about state non-coercion become unstable? Please rank any tensions by severity and tell me what the cheapest fix is. Also, separate what's settled in this debate from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic—don't present a contested view as if it were consensus.
