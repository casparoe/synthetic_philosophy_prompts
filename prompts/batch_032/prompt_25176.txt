I'm a novelist working on a book about first contact — not the spaceships-and-lasers kind, more the kind where we detect something (a signal, an artifact, an anomaly) and then the whole story is about what we do with that. I've gotten kind of obsessed with the Fermi paradox while doing background reading, and I've managed to snag a small grant from a foundation that funds "research for artists." It's not huge but it'll buy me six months of reading and thinking time, and they want a short proposal — one page, maybe 800 words max — laying out what I'm going to investigate and why.

Here's the half-formed idea I want turned into a proposal. Most of the Fermi paradox debate takes for granted that we know what "contact" means — a radio signal, a probe, a visit — and then asks why it hasn't happened. But I keep wondering whether the concept of contact itself is doing too much unexamined work. If minds could be radicially different from ours — operating on different temporal scales, in different sensory modalities, with different notions of what counts as an agent or a message — then maybe the question isn't "why haven't they contacted us" but "would we recognize contact if it happened, and would they recognize us as contactable?" I'm not talking about the usual "they're too advanced to bother with us" solution; I'm asking whether the very idea of contact presupposes a shared framework that might not exist, and whether that dissolves or at least seriously reframes the paradox. This feels like it could open up something interesting for the novel too — if contact isn't a binary event but something that has to be achieved or negotiated.

Can you write the proposal? It needs: a precise statement of the question, why existing discussions of the Fermi paradox don't really address it (most of them treat contact as an unproblematic event), what I'd actually do month by month for six months, what a useful deliverable looks like for a novelist rather than a philosopher, and an honest statement of what would count as the project failing. Don't bother with citations — I'll add those myself once I've done the reading. Keep it tight, one page.
