I'm taking a graduate seminar on the metaphysics of science this term, and we've just finished a unit on Humeanism versus anti-Humeanism about laws of nature. As it happens, our department's colloquium series is bringing in a philosopher next month who defends David Lewis's Humean Supervenience thesis about laws and chance, and my professor has asked a few of us seminar students to help draft questions for the Q&A session that follows her talk. I've been reading through her published work to prepare, and I've noticed she has a reliable way of deflecting the standard complaint that Humean laws can't really explain the very regularities they're supposed to be laws of: she likes to say that belonging to the best deductive systematization simply is what explaining a regularity consists in, so there's no further "metaphysical glue" left for critics to demand. I'd like our questions to make that deflection progressively harder for her to fall back on, rather than just handing her room to restate it.

Could you draft eight or nine interview questions along these lines? I'd like at least one question pressing on the fact that the Best System Analysis needs a primitive distinction between perfectly natural properties and gerrymandered ones to keep just any regularity from counting as a law, and asking whether that isn't already a quiet concession of anti-Humean structure into the theory; at least one on quidditism and the "Ramseyan Humility" result, i.e., whether she can really shrug off being permanently ignorant of which intrinsic quality occupies which nomic role, or whether that ignorance ends up infecting our knowledge of the actual world and not just remote possibilia; and at least one on the "undermining futures" problem for chance — Lewis's own "big bad bug" — asking whether patching the Principal Principle into the New Principle actually resolves the tension or just relabels it. For each of these three, please add a natural follow-up question keyed to whatever reply she is most likely to give (for instance, biting the bullet, or arguing that necessitarians face a structurally analogous cost of their own). Please close the set with a question asking what observation or thought experiment, if any, could count as genuine evidence against Humean Supervenience, given that the view is explicitly a thesis about the whole spacetime mosaic, future included.
