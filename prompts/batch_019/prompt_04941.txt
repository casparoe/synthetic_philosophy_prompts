Forty years spent working with quantum statistics left me instinctively wary of assuming that "the same particle" names some further fact over and above a pattern of properties and relations, so when I began reading about David Lewis's counterpart theory I recognized the shape of the worry immediately. I follow Kripke's now-famous objection that the theory seems to change the subject: Humphrey should not care whether some merely resembling counterpart of his wins an election in another possible world, since it isn't him who wins. What I cannot pin down is Lewis's considered reply once he concedes that the counterpart relation itself shifts with context, so that which resemblances count as world-crossing "sameness" depends on what a conversation happens to be tracking. Doesn't that amount to admitting that there is no determinate fact about what is essential to an individual, only facts relative to whichever counterpart relation happens to be salient at the time? I would like a precise account of how a defender of counterpart theory is meant to stop that from collapsing into "essence is just whatever we currently care about," and whether theorists who insist on genuine transworld identity actually avoid the analogous problem, or merely relocate it into an unexplained primitive fact about which individual is which.
