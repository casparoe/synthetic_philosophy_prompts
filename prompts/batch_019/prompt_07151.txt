So my friend Priya is getting married in a few weeks and asked me to give the maid-of-honor speech, which I agreed to before really thinking it through. At her engagement party a couple weekends ago I made some throwaway toast-practice line about the two of them "finally composing one whole," and her fiancé Marcus — who's a few years into a metaphysics PhD and has genuinely become a good friend of mine too — would not let it go. It turned into a real argument, like two hours and several drinks deep, about the special composition question, and at some point he actually cited some book called Material Beings, by this guy van Inwagen, at me. He kept insisting that on van Inwagen's actual answer to the question, composition only happens when some things' activities constitute a life, so two people getting married obviously don't compose anything new — they're not one biological organism — and that I was just being sentimental about mereology. I went home and definately read more about this than I meant to, and now I'm stuck, because it's worse than he even said: on universalism any two things already compose something, wedding or no wedding, so that's not especially romantic either, and on nihilism there isn't strictly even a "Priya" or a "Marcus," let alone a couple, just simples arranged the relevant ways (arranged spousally, I guess, if he wants to be a pain about it).

Here's my actual problem though: there'll be maybe eight metaphysicians at this wedding who will absolutely notice if I fudge this, plus forty relatives who have no idea what mereology even is. Can you write me the real maid-of-honor speech — like 3-4 minutes, meant to be read out loud — that takes the special composition question seriously as its actual backbone instead of as a cute pun? It should be honest that none of the standard answers just hands you the "now they're one" line for free, and it still needs to land somewhere warm and true by the end, not just be a very well-footnoted way of ruining a wedding toast.
