A friend and I spent much of a recent backpacking trip arguing about the following situation, and I still can't decide who had the better of it. I had worked out in my head how many days our remaining food would last, double-checked the arithmetic, and got the same answer both times, which left me confident in it. She objected that the thin air at that altitude is known to impair people's reasoning before they notice anything is wrong, and that this was a live possibility for us that day; she thought I should therefore trust my number less than usual, even though neither of us could point to an actual mistake in my math. I countered that once I had checked my reasoning and found it sound, there was nothing left for a merely general worry about altitude to undermine. Whose position is more defensible, and why?
