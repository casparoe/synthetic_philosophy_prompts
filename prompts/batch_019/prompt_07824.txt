Ok this is a dumb example but it's been bugging me, bear with me. I'm a philosophy PhD student, which mostly means I procrastinate on my dissertation by bingeing TV at night, telling myself "one more episode and then I stop." Problem is, by the second or third time that thought hits me in a night, I'm zoned out enough that every instance feels identical - same couch, same tiredness - so I genuinely can't tell in the moment whether this is the first "one more" thought tonight or the second. Stopping right away with nothing extra is a letdown. Loosing track and watching a bunch more than planned isn't great either, though at least I enjoyed the show, so its better than stopping cold. Catching myself after exactly one more and stopping there is clearly the best possible night. Here's what gets me: deciding in advance, while sober and rested, exactly what I'll do feels like it should settle things, but once I'm actually in one of these identical moments later on, unable to tell which occurrence this even is, whatever reasoning I use right then pulls toward a different answer than the plan I made earlier. Is that just weak will, or is something actually inconsistent about planning ahead for a situation like this?
