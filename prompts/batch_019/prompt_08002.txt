For a problem set in my graduate philosophy seminar on rational choice theory, I would like an example that pushes students beyond the textbook presentation of iterated elimination of dominated strategies, which typically considers domination by pure strategies alone. Please construct a normal-form game in which one player has a pure strategy that is not strictly dominated by any single one of that player's other pure strategies, yet is strictly dominated by some mixed strategy over those other strategies. (You need only specify the payoffs of the player whose strategy is being eliminated.) Explain precisely why the mixed strategy succeeds where no pure strategy alone does, and what this implies about the difference between iterated elimination restricted to pure-strategy dominance and the standard procedure, which also allows domination by mixed strategies.
