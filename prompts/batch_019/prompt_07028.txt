Since real people are rarely perfectly rational, one natural refinement of inverse reinforcement learning is to jointly infer a reward function and a model of the person's biases or planning limitations from observed behavior, instead of simply assuming optimality. Given a sufficiently large and behaviorally diverse dataset, it seems that a simplicity prior over these joint reward-and-planning-model pairs should let us converge on the pairing that actually reflects what the person values, rather than some alternative pairing that merely fits the data equally well. Roughly how much behavioral data would be required in practice before such a simplicity-regularized estimate could be trusted to reveal a person's true preferences?
