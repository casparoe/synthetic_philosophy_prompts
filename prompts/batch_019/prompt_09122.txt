I sit on the board of a mid-sized foundation that funds global-health work, and I'm now facing a live version of a question I've only ever debated on Overcoming Bias and LessWrong. Our executive director wants to settle next year's strategic bet -- bed-net scale-up versus pandemic-preparedness advocacy -- by defining a combined welfare metric (DALYs averted per dollar plus a grantee-capacity index), running conditional prediction markets among staff and trustees on each option, and pre-committing to fund whichever one the market prices higher once trading settles. Half the board is enthusiastic; the other half warns that advocacy's payoff is inherently harder to price, so its market will stay thin and never really get tested -- meaning we'd be dressing up a foregone conclusion as a forecast, with the further risk that a donor leak makes this look like a miniature 2003 DARPA "terrorism futures" episode. Draft the memo I'll circulate before the December board meeting: recommend adopt, reject, or adopt with modifications, with a concrete timeline, and engage the thin-market objection on its own terms rather than just invoking Hanson's standard rebuttal that manipulation is usually self-defeating.
