I sit on the programming committee of a small nonprofit that hosts public discussions of classical texts, including a quarterly "Symposium Night": a dinner with wine, music, and philosophical conversation modeled loosely on Plato's Symposium. At the last one, a member got visibly drunk and upset another guest, and the committee has split between going completely dry from now on and continuing as before, treating the incident as a one-off.

I've been working through Plato's Laws on my own, and unless I'm misreading it, his actual position is neither of these. He doesn't recommend abstinence, which surprised me given his reputation elsewhere for distrusting bodily pleasure, but he also treats a drinking party as something that has to be deliberately engineered, by law and by a sober person in charge, into a genuine test of what he calls a person's sophrosyne, or self-mastery. How much license someone gets, he ties to their age and experience. Neither faction on my committee is working from this.

Could you draft the short memo I'll circulate to the committee before next week's meeting? It should recommend one specific policy for Symposium Night going forward, explain the reasoning in terms Plato would recognize, and seriously engage the strongest objection from whichever side it disappoints, rather than just gesturing at it.
