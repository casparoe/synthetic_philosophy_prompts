I've been working through the exchange between Thomson and Benacerraf on supertasks for a paper on the metaphysics of infinity, and I would like to see the dispute staged as a live argument rather than summarized. Write a dialogue between two philosophers standing before Thomson's lamp at the instant its infinite sequence of on-off switches is supposed to have been completed, disputing what the thought experiment actually establishes. One character should hold, with Thomson, that the lamp exposes a genuine incoherence in the very idea of a completed supertask. The other should hold, following Benacerraf, that Thomson's argument shows only that the lamp's final state is left undetermined by the rules governing the process, and that no impossibility has actually been demonstrated. Neither side should simply restate the canonical position. Have them press each other with harder cases -- for instance, the Ross-Littlewood vase-and-balls paradox, where a supertask lasting one minute, with each stage taking half as long as the last, adds ten balls to a vase and removes one at every stage; depending only on which ball is removed each time, the vase ends up either empty or infinite at the end. Both characters should be equally sharp and argue in good faith, responding to what the other has just said rather than talking past each other, and the dialogue should end with them pinning down exactly where their disagreement really lies.
