I chair my department's ethics colloquium, and this term's invited speaker will defend John Taurek's answer to the question posed in 'Should the Numbers Count?': that the mere fact one group we could rescue outnumbers another gives us no duty to save it rather than the smaller group, when we cannot save both. Draft eight to ten questions for the post-talk discussion that go beyond having her restate the paper.

I want at least one question testing whether her argument from the separateness of persons really licenses full indifference between the two groups -- including the permissibility of an unweighted coin toss -- or whether it only defeats the utilitarian case for preferring the larger one; one that puts to her Parfit's reply, in 'Innumerate Ethics,' that a rescue remains a benefit to the person rescued even though no aggregate entity thereby suffers a further loss; one that presses her on Scanlon's contractualist case for saving the greater number that never combines separate claims into a sum; and one asking whether she would accept a lottery weighted toward the larger group as a principled compromise, or whether she thinks that just reintroduces the numbers by another route. Close with a question that tests her answer against a lopsided case -- a thousand against one, say -- to see whether the verdict there still sounds like an application of principle rather than a concession to intuition. For each of these, include a brief follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give.
