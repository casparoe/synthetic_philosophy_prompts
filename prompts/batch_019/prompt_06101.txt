Next month our department's colloquium series has a decision theorist visiting, and I somehow got roped into running the Q&A afterward. She's spent basically her whole career defending resolute choice as the right answer to Parfit's hitchhiker case -- the one where your dying of thirst in the desert and the only driver willing to take you into town is someone who can somehow tell in advance whether you'll actually pay him once you're safe, so being "rational" about your own self-interest ends up requiring you to become disposed to do the very thing that, once you're safely in town, is no longer in your interest at all. I've been re-teaching the relevant bits of Reasons and Persons in my grad seminar this term, and I'm pretty sure Parfit's own label for this was something like "rational irrationality" -- her whole line, as I understand it, is that any theory of practical rationality which only ever grades individual acts one at a time, rather than policies or standing dispositions, is simply going to fail on cases like this one.

I sat in on her job talk years ago and she's very smooth at deflecting the obvious first-pass objections, so I'd like the Q&A to go further than her stock presentation. Could you draft me 8 to 10 questions that really lean on the position? I want at least one that makes her say something precise about the moment the hitchhiker actually hands over the cash: does she think the *act* of paying is itself rational, or only that becoming the sort of person who'd do it was rational, and if those come apart, which one gets to keep the name "rational"? I'd also like a question tying her view back to Gauthier's constrained maximization, forcing her to say plainly whether resolute choice is just that idea dressed up differently or is doing genuinely new work. And throw in something on the standard causal-decision-theory comeback -- that the driver is basically an unfair predictor and you shouldn't judge a theory of rationality by how it fares in a world custom-built to punish it -- plus, if you can work it in, a question on how her view is even supposed to differ from the "functional decision theory" people, who I gather want to claim the hitchhiker case for themselves too with a totally different apparatus. For each question, add a short follow-up keyed to whatever answer she'd most plausibly give, the kind of pushback you'd actually give her live, not just the next question on a list.
