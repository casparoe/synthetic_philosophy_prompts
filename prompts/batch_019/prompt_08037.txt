So I was reading about this old philosopher G.E. Moore and he's got this thought experiment where you imagine two planets that literally no living thing will ever see or visit, and one is unbelievably gorgeous and the other's just a hideous trash heap. He says most people would still call it better for the beautiful one to exist, and uses that to argue beauty has real value even with zero observers. Then he goes further and says the two best things in life, by far, are enjoying beauty and having close personal relationships, way ahead of stuff like duty or being useful to society in general.

Ive been chewing on this for weeks now. If he's basically right about all this, what are the biggest implications? Like, should i feel actual moral weight about a coral reef getting destroyed even if no diver will ever miss it? And on the friendship side, would two people quietly loving each other on a desert island, doing nothing for anyone else ever, count as basically peak goodness, maybe even better than someone out there curing diseases but who has no one close to them? I cant tell if this is a genuinely profound point about value or just Moore dressing his own preferences up as facts about the universe.
