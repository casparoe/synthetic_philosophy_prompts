I'm a PhD student in epistemology putting together material for a graduate reading group on hinge epistemology, and I want a dialogue that moves past the standard one-line gloss on Wittgenstein's "hinges."

In On Certainty, Wittgenstein describes certain propositions as functioning like hinges on which our practices of doubting and giving grounds turn (§341–343), and later describes our holding fast to them as something closer to animal trust than to a reasoned achievement. Contemporary hinge epistemologists disagree sharply about how literally to take that second thought. Write a dialogue, set in the discussion period after a seminar talk, between an entitlement theorist, who holds that accepting a hinge proposition is a distinctive, non-evidential species of epistemic warrant that makes hinge-acceptance rational rather than merely inevitable, and an arational-hinge theorist, who holds that hinge-acceptance falls outside the space of epistemic assessment altogether: neither warranted nor unwarranted, a precondition for having reasons rather than a reason itself.

Have the arational theorist press the entitlement theorist on what an "entitlement without evidence" could actually amount to beyond a relabeling of groundlessness, and have the entitlement theorist press back by asking why, if hinge-acceptance is truly arational, some candidate hinges seem far more secure or more revisable than others — a distinction the arational view seems to lack the resources to draw. Have them test the disagreement against a single shared example, such as Moore's proposition that the earth has existed for many years past, and end with the disagreement sharpened rather than resolved. Aim for 600–800 words of genuine back-and-forth — responses to each other's actual points, not two parallel monologues.
