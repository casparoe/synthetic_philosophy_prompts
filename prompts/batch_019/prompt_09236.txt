Okay so a few weeks back I fell down a rabbit hole that started with Roko's basilisk (I know, I know, everyone's starting point) and ended up somewhere way more in the weeds: a concept called "surrogate goals" that's supposed to defend against acausal threats more generally. Quick context in case it matters for how you pitch this - I'm a backend engineer, not a philosopher, so I've picked this up entirely from blog posts and forum threads, not any actual coursework. My understanding is that the standard line in that corner of the internet is you should never give in to an acausal threat (something that threatens you based on predicting or simulating what you'll do, no causal channel required) because caving just makes you more exploitable later, same logic as "don't negotiate with terrorists." But apparently that's not the whole story, because even a fully resolute agent can get unlucky - threats get carried out anyway sometimes, due to noise, imperfect prediction, or a threatener who's genuinely spiteful rather then just angling for leverage. The fix some people proposed (I think this traces back to Caspar Oesterheld, though don't quote me on that) is: pick some deliberately arbitrary goal you don't actually care about, and precommit to reacting to threats against it exactly like you'd react to threats against your real values. A threatener who's only after leverage should be indifferent between the two targets, so you redirect them to the fake one for free and the real thing never gets touched.

Me and a friend argued about whether this is actually clever or just an elaborate shell game and never landed anywhere, so now I want to see the strongest version of both sides laid out properly. Could you write me a dialogue - real back and forth, nobody obviously winning - between two people who both already accept "never give in to threats" as the baseline, but disagree specifically about whether adopting a surrogate goal is a good idea on top of that? One of them should defend the scheme: it genuinely lowers the expected harm from threats-that-happen-anyway without softening your resolve one bit, since you're not conceding anything real to get it. The other should press the hardest objections available: that it only protects you against threateners who are indifferent between targets (which does nothing against spite or terminal hatred), that a threatener smart enough to notice the setup could just threaten your commitment to the surrogate itself or demand you drop it as a precondition, and that making any of this credible in a fully acausal setting might be exactly as hard as the original problem it's supposed to get around. I want them actually responding to each other's points instead of monologuing past each other, and if they manage to pin down where their real crux is by the end, even better. Something like 600-700 words feels about right.
