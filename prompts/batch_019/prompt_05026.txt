Okay, this might sound a little unhinged but it's genuinely been messing with my head for weeks, so bear with me. I'm not a physics person at all, but I got sucked into a cosmology podcast a while back where they explained that a bunch of the leading inflation models basically imply the universe is spatially infinite, and if its infinite and matter is spread out roughly the same everywhere on average, then every possible arrangement of particles in an Earth-sized region has to repeat itself infinitely many times somewhere out there. So there are supposedly infinite copies of literally everything, including infinite instances of the worst suffering imaginable and infinite instances of the best flourishing imaginable, all already "existing" right now whether we ever detect them or not.

Ever since hearing that, I can't shake this thought: I've been donating a decent chunk of every paycheck to effective charities for about two years now, partly because I liked knowing I was adding some real, measurable amount of good into the world. But if the total amount of good and suffering that already exists is infinite, then whatever finite difference I personally make doesn't actually change that total at all - infinity plus my donation is still just infinity. Same logic would apply to literally any good or bad thing anyone ever does. My partner just rolls her eyes and says I'm overthinking a random pop-science factoid and should keep doing what I was doing anyway, and my friends mostly just change the subject when I bring it up, but neither reaction actually tells me whether the logic itself is broken somewhere. Is there an actual flaw in this reasoning, or is this a bullet I genuinely have to bite? And if I do have to bite it, how are you even supposed to keep caring about making things better once you've thought it through this far?
