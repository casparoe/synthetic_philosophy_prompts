This argument happened in my chess discord and it's been stuck in my head for days. A stream had the eval bar at 71% for white, and two regulars, Owen and Mei, ended up arguing about what that number even means.

Owen said 71% is meaningless because chess has zero randomness: the position is already either a forced win for white, a forced win for black, or a dead draw with perfect play. The "real" number is 100, 0, or 50 - anything else is just the engine hedging, not a real probability.

Mei said thats true of any forecast about a deterministic system, including tomorrow's weather, and nobody calls rain percentages fake just because the atmosphere isn't "really" random.

Owen shot back that forecasters run tons of slightly-varied simulations and see what fraction come out rainy, so their number tracks something real. Engines don't do that - one board, one game tree - so 71% can't be the same kind of thing.

Mei said it's still not made up: it's the engine's track record - positions scored like that have turned into wins about 71% of the time historically, same as a doctor citing survival rates from similar past patients.

Owen said that just relocates the problem - "similar patients" and "similarly-scored positions" are both judgment calls, and slicing the reference class differently gets a different number, while this one position has exactly one true value that no amount of averaging gets you closer to.
