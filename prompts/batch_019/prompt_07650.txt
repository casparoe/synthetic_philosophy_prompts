I sit on my university's student senate committee, which is revising the rules for who may vote in campus-wide elections. Under current bylaws, only students who pay the mandatory activity fee -- which funds the senate's entire budget and is billed solely to on-campus students -- may vote or hold office, so the roughly 4,000 students enrolled in the university's fully online degree programs are currently ineligible. An online-student caucus is petitioning for enfranchisement, noting that the senate also sets academic-integrity policy binding on every student and speaks for "the student body" when the administration sets tuition. Opponents on the committee counter that a body's electorate should track who actually funds and uses what it controls, and that admitting non-payers would let them help decide how other students' money is spent. Draft the recommendation memo I will bring to the full senate: specify exactly who should be eligible to vote on what, and give the caucus's strongest argument a genuine hearing rather than a procedural dismissal.
