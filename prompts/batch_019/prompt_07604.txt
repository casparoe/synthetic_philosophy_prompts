I just started chairing a three-member subcommittee that recommends tenure cases at my college. Tenure requires meeting the threshold on both "research excellence" and "teaching effectiveness," and our bylaws say the subcommittee decides by majority vote, but they don't specify whether that means voting issue-by-issue or on the bottom-line recommendation. In this year's case, I judged the candidate excellent on research and effective on teaching (grant); one colleague judged her excellent on research but not effective on teaching (deny); the other judged her not excellent on research but effective on teaching (deny). On bottom-line recommendations, it's deny, 2-1. But voting issue-by-issue instead, research gets a 2-1 majority in her favor, and so does teaching -- which, under our conjunctive standard, should mean grant. One member now insists the bottom-line vote is the only real vote and that the other is grasping at straws; another insists that ignoring the issue-by-issue majorities means ignoring what most of us actually believe about her merits. I owe the full faculty committee a recommendation by Friday. Please draft that memo: say how we should resolve this year's case and what procedure we should adopt going forward, and give a fair hearing to the strongest argument for whichever approach you don't recommend.
