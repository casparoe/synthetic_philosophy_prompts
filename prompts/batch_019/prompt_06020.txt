I spent my career as a paleontologist working on the dinosaur-to-bird transition, and now that I'm retired I've gotten into reading philosophy of biology for fun. I recently ran into this whole argument between two seperate camps about what a biological "function" actually is: the etiological view, where a trait's function is whatever it was originally selected for, versus the causal-role view, where the function is just whatever a part currently contributes to some capacity of the organism, history be damned. The case that keeps nagging at me is feathers, which the fossil record says arose for insulation in non-flying dinosaurs long before anything could actually fly with them. Can you properly compare the two accounts using that case, and tell me which one handles a shift like that more naturally, and exactly where the other one starts to strain?
