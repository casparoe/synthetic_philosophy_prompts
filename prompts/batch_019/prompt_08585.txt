I've been part of a rationalist-adjacent reading group for years -- the kind of crowd that cut its teeth on LessWrong and the EA Forum -- and at a recent meetup, right after we'd worked through the standard demandingness objection to consequentialism, two regulars got into a back-and-forth I still haven't been able to adjudicate to my own satisfaction.

(1) Felix opened by arguing that the objection confuses what morality requires as a criterion of rightness with how an agent should actually deliberate day to day: a consequentialist who consciously tried to maximize utility in every decision would, by the theory's own lights, produce worse outcomes than one who simply cultivates good habits and lives a recognizably decent life -- so the exhausting, ever-calculating picture critics paint isn't what consequentialism actually recommends.

(2) Naomi granted the distinction but said it doesn't touch the objection: however an agent gets there psychologically, a well-calibrated consequentialist still has to converge on giving away most income and time above subsistence whenever that produces more welfare, since that's what the theory is tracking. Swapping the decision procedure doesn't shrink the sacrifice; it just hides it from view.

(3) Felix responded that what you owe should instead be fixed relative to full compliance: work out the fair per-person contribution if everyone did their part, and that fixed amount is your obligation, full stop. The sense that consequentialism demands limitless sacrifice from any one person comes from everyone else's non-compliance, not from the theory itself.

(4) Naomi answered that this can't be right, since whether you're obligated to help someone in front of you at low cost has nothing to do with how many other capable people are shirking elsewhere; tying your obligation to a hypothetical fully-compliant world lets you stop helping real people for reasons that have nothing to do with them -- exactly the kind of move consequentialists are supposed to distrust.

Could you go through this move by move -- for each of the four, say whether Felix, Naomi, both, or neither has the stronger position, and justify the call -- and then close with a short paragraph on what you think the two of them are still missing between them?
