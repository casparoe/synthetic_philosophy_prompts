So I started a night class on epistemology this semester (needed the credit for a certificate program, long story) and we spent two whole sessions on the Gettier problem, which has been stuck in my head ever since — mostly because something almost exactly like it happened to me a few weeks ago, and now I can't stop wondering whether I actually "knew" what I thought I knew at the time.

Short version: I was up for a promotion at work, and my manager Denise pulled me aside and told me, very confidently, "it's basically locked in, they already approved the new title and everything." I went around telling people I was getting promoted. Turns out Denise had grabbed the wrong folder off her desk that day and was actually describing a promotion for a different guy on my team whose name sounds kind of like mine (don't even ask). My promotion DID go through about two weeks later, but for a completely unrelated reason — our director pushed it through after a client specifically asked for me to lead their account, something Denise didn't even know about when she talked to me. So: I had a belief that turned out true, I had what felt like rock-solid justification (my actual manager, speaking to me directly, in her professional capacity, about my situation), and yet the reason it was true had nothing to do with the reason I believed it.

I told this story at dinner and two friends of mine, Priya and Sam, argued about whether this is a "real" Gettier case for like twenty minutes straight:
1. Priya: it's a textbook case — justified true belief, but the justification and the truth are connected by pure coincidence, so by definition it isn't knowledge, I just got lucky twice in a row.
2. Sam: disagreed — Denise wasn't actually talking about me, she was talking about Colin, so I was never really justified to begin with, I'd just misheard a fact about someone else's paperwork as being about mine. No genuine justification, no Gettier case, just an honest mix-up that happened to get fixed by unrelated good news.
3. Priya: came back with — Denise WAS talking to me, specifically, using her real professional authority, about what she believed was my situation. The mix-up was completely invisible from my side; my evidence looked and felt exactly like the evidence in an ordinary case that clearly would be knowledge. That invisibility is exactly what makes it Gettier-flavored instead of a plain old mistake.
4. Sam: countered that once there's a fully seperate, independent route to the same true belief (the director's decision), the belief was never actually hostage to Denise being right about anything, which makes this closer to an overdetermined true belief than the classic single-thread bad-luck cases — and if every overdetermined belief counts as Gettiered, that seems to prove way too much.
