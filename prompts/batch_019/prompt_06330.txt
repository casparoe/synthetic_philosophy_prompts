While tuning the position-sizing logic in a small sports-betting model I wrote, I read up on the Kelly criterion and, from there, on the broader idea of "geometric rationality": the claim that because Kelly betting maximizes a bankroll's long-run geometric growth rate rather than its ordinary expected (arithmetic-mean) value, rational decision-making in general should be grounded in geometric expectation rather than the plain expected-value calculations that economics, quantitative finance, and effective altruism all rely on. If the mathematics behind Kelly really does show that arithmetic expected value is the wrong criterion whenever outcomes compound over time, why haven't these fields simply switched over to geometric expectation as their default decision rule? Is there a principled reason plain expected value survives outside of repeated bet-sizing, or is this just a case of established practice lagging behind the better theory?
