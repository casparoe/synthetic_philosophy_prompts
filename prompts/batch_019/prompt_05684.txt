I've been asked to give the wedding toast for two close friends next month. As the resident statistician among their friends, I've been joking for months about opening with the line that a given marriage has, say, a forty percent chance of ending in divorce. The trouble is that I actually think about this kind of thing for a living, and I know that number is a reference class problem waiting to happen: the couple in front of me belongs simultaneously to indefinitely many reference classes -- all marriages, marriages between people who met in college, second marriages, marriages between two people who still can't agree on how to load a dishwasher -- each with its own base rate, and there is no principled fact about which class is the right one for predicting them specifically. Write the toast, something I could actually deliver in about three minutes. It should take the problem seriously enough to explain why the joke doesn't really work, rather than just quietly dropping it, but it still needs to be funny, and it has to land equally well for the couple's data-minded friends and for relatives who have never heard the phrase "reference class" in their lives.
