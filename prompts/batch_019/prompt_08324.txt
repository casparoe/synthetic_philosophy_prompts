I attend a Catholic high school, and in my theology elective we've spent several weeks on the problem of evil, but what has actually been unsettling me lately is a related but distinct puzzle. For over a year I have prayed regularly and, as far as I can tell, sincerely and without any deliberate resistance, for some clear sign that God exists, and I have not received anything back that I could honestly point to as evidence. I recently learned that this experience maps fairly closely onto what J.L. Schellenberg calls "nonresistant nonbelief," which he uses to argue that a perfectly loving God would not permit any capable, nonresistant seeker to remain in nonbelief, so that the mere existence of such nonbelief is itself evidence against theism. Assuming Schellenberg's argument is basically sound, what are the most important implications of it, and of the standard theistic replies to it, such as skeptical theism and appeals to the developmental value of a freely and independently sought faith, for how I should interpret my own experience of unanswered prayer?
