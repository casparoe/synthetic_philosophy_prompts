I'm chairing next week's department colloquium, and our speaker is a metaphysician who defends genuine modal realism in David Lewis's sense: the view that every way a world could be is a way some spatiotemporally and causally isolated concrete world actually is, and that "actual" is just an indexical term picking out whichever world the speaker happens to inhabit. Draft eight to ten questions for the Q&A that go beyond having her restate the position. Include at least one question on why the incredulous stare should count as more than an inarticulate report of surprise; one on whether our knowledge of these causally inert worlds can escape a Benacerraf-style epistemological challenge any better than mathematical Platonism can; one that presses the indexical analysis of actuality by asking why suffering in some other, equally real world shouldn't trouble us as much as equally remote suffering within our own universe does; and one on how counterpart theory is supposed to answer Kripke's objection that a claim like "Humphrey might have won" isn't really about some other, merely similar candidate. For each, add a follow-up question keyed to the reply she's likely to give, and close with a question about what, short of a proof of outright inconsistency, would actually move her toward a cheaper ersatzist alternative.
