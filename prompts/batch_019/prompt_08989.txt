So I've been reporting on crypto staking for a piece and can't shake a nagging feeling about it. A lot of the people I've talked to just lock their coins up in a pool and watch the balance grow — they dont do anything else, the protocol just pays them more coins over time, kind of like interest in a savings account. But other stakers run their own validator node, which takes real computing work and upkeep, and they get paid in the same token for that. Everyone involved calls both of these "yield," but it feels like there should be some meaningful difference between money that grows because you did something and money that grows because you parked it somewhere and walked away. Am I onto a real distinction here, or is that just an old bias against passive income that doesn't actually hold up once you look closely?
