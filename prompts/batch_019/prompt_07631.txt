A friend and I spent most of a long dinner arguing about the Judy Benjamin problem and left without settling anything, so I would like to see the dispute carried out properly by two people who understand the technicalities.

For context: Judy Benjamin is dropped into a region known to be split evenly between Red and Blue territory, with Red itself split evenly between the areas held by Headquarters Company and by Second Company; her prior credence is accordingly 1/2 in Blue and 1/4 in each of the two Red areas. She then picks up a garbled radio transmission telling her that, if she is in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 that she is in Headquarters Company's area rather than Second Company's. Updating by minimizing the relative entropy between her posterior and her prior, subject only to this new constraint, does not leave her credence in Red territory at 1/2: it drops to somewhere around 0.467, even though the message says nothing whatsoever about Blue territory.

Write a dialogue between two formal epistemologists who disagree sharply about what this shows. One holds that minimizing relative entropy is simply the correct way to incorporate the new information, and that the drop in credence, however strange it looks, is exactly what coherence requires once conditional information is taken seriously; on this view, the intuition that Red "shouldn't" be affected is the thing that has to give. The other holds that the result is a reductio of using relative-entropy minimization here at all: it lets evidence entirely about the internal breakdown of Red move the unconditional probability of Red itself, and insists that any adequate treatment of the case must hold P(Red) fixed and redistribute credence only within it. Have the two of them engage with the genuine weaknesses in each other's position rather than simply restating their own, and have at least one of them try to state plainly what general principle about updating on conditionals is really at stake.
