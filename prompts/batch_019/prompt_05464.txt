Every year for the past nine years, the same eight of us have taken a trip together, and choosing the destination has always been a lighthearted formality -- until this past weekend, when the planning meeting turned into a two-hour argument I'm still turning over. We had narrowed things down to four genuinely different options: a beach town, a mountain cabin, a big city, and a lake house. Each of the four has actually won outright in at least one previous year, so nobody was proposing to rule any of them out in advance. Wanting to avoid the crudeness of a simple "whoever gets the most first-choice votes wins" plurality, we asked everyone to privately rank all four from favorite to least favorite and combined the rankings with a simple points system: four points for a first choice, three for a second, and so on down to one. Partway through tallying, it came out that one friend had not ranked sincerely. By everyone's best reconstruction, he had correctly anticipated that his actual favorite would split support with a similar option and lose outright, so he listed his real second choice first instead, and that is exactly what ended up winning.

Another friend was quite upset about this and has spent the days since insisting, at length, that the fix is obvious: we just need a smarter rule next time. He is now lobbying hard for a ranked-choice, instant-runoff-style count instead of our points system, and he's convinced that some sufficiently well-designed method -- so long as it isn't a literal dictatorship where one person is simply handed the decision every year -- can in principle be made fully immune to this kind of tactical misreporting.

I'm skeptical, though in the moment I couldn't defend my skepticism beyond a vague hunch: that this isn't really a flaw specific to our points system, but something close to unavoidable whenever there are more than two real options on the table, people's honest preferences are free to be whatever they actually are, and no single person's word is simply decisive. My friend thinks this is needless defeatism and that I just haven't thought of the right rule yet. Since we couldn't settle it ourselves, I'd like a more rigorous verdict: who is closer to being right? Is there some ranking method -- instant-runoff or otherwise, but short of just letting one fixed person decide -- that our group could adopt for future trips and that would genuinely close off this kind of strategic misreporting for good, or is my friend, however reasonably, chasing a fix that cannot exist? If it's the latter, I would like to understand why, and whether there is anything short of a full fix that would at least help.
