So I fell down a rabbit hole after someone posted this old Simone Weil essay on HN where she argues political parties should basically just be abolished, full stop. Her definition of a "party" comes down to three traits: its a machine for generating collective passion, its built specifically to pressure every member's thinking into line with the group instead of their own judgement, and its real long-term goal, underneath whatever cause it claims to champion, is just to keep growing without limit. She says those three traits alone are enough to make any party totalitarian in tendency, even inside an otherwise healthy democracy.

She obviously died in 1943, so she never saw anything like a modern feed-ranking algorithm. But rereading that list, it maps almost too well onto how Twitter/X's or Facebook's recommendation engine operates on an entire population, way beyond just people who "joined" anything. Do you think Weil would've said an algorithmic feed counts as totalitarian by her own definition and should be abolished the same way she wanted parties abolished? Or is there a part of her argument that only really works for parties specifically, as organizations with actual members who swear some kind of loyalty, that just doesn't carry over to a ranking algorithm nobody consciously signs up to obey?
