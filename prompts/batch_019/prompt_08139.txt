My closest friend from my philosophy PhD program is getting married next month, and she has asked me -- the resident "collective intentionality" person among our friends -- to give the toast. We spent years arguing about shared agency: whether it reduces to interlocking individual intentions plus mutual knowledge, in Bratman's sense, or requires an irreducible joint commitment -- what Margaret Gilbert calls a "plural subject" -- that no party can dissolve unilaterally, whatever their private feelings do in the meantime. She and her fiancé went through a real stretch of long-distance doubt before they got engaged, and I want the toast to use that contrast honestly, to say something true about commitments that outlast wavering feelings, without airing anything private or turning preachy. About half the room are philosophers who will catch every reference; the rest have never heard the word "ontology." Please write the toast: roughly five minutes long, warm rather than clever for its own sake, and genuinely structured around that distinction rather than merely decorated with it.
