Ive had a lot more time for philosophy books since I retired from the lab, and lately I keep getting stuck on Hofstadter's old idea of "superrationality" - that two agents who reason in exactly the same way (twins, copies, whatever) should both cooperate in a one-off prisoner's dilemma even with zero communication and no way to enforce anything, because any argument that convinces one of them to defect would, by symmetry, convince the other too, so the whole case for defecting quietly undoes itself. On paper I can follow it, but it still feels like a sleight of hand to me rather then something I actually believe in my gut. Could you write me a short story where a character actually has to reason their way through a real, high-stakes version of this, so the logic is doing work in the plot rather than just being explained to the reader? I'd rather it wasn't literally set in a prison - that part of the setup was only ever a toy example anyway.
