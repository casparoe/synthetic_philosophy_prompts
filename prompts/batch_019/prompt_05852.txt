I sit on the volunteer standards committee of a regional makers' market, where our only rule for vendors is that goods be "handmade, not mass-produced," with nothing said about what that means. A dispute over the rule has split the committee two against two and left me as the deciding vote: one of our longest-standing vendors, a toymaker, now cuts and shapes every piece on a CNC router from a digital file before sanding, gluing, and painting each one by hand, and I need to tell the committee my decision by Friday, before Saturday's market. I've been reading on my own about how philosophers treat words with no sharp edge, and I can't decide between two views: that "handmade" has an exact cutoff none of us could ever discover, or that the word just gives out here, making any line we draw a decision rather than a discovery. Please write the memo I'll send the committee recommending what to do about her table this Saturday and what standard to apply to similar cases going forward, and take seriously whichever of the two views you don't end up building the recommendation on.
