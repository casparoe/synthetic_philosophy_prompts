Had a whole argument with my friend last night about whether its dumb to say stuff like "the model believes the capital of France is Paris." My take was pretty hardcore Dennettian: on the intentional stance, a belief was never anything more than whatever pattern licenses good predictions of a system's behavior, so if attributing the belief buys you real predictive power, that's all "belief" ever amounted to -- for humans or LLMs, no further fact needed. My friend's comeback was that Dennett's own "real patterns" move requires the pattern you're tracking to actually be robust and compressible, not just noise, and that a lot of what looks like LLM "belief" flunks that test in a way ordinary human inconsistency doesn't: the same factual claim flips depending on how the question's phrased or who you tell the model it's talking to, way more brittle than typical human framing effects. We went back and forth for like an hour on whether thats a difference of degree or of kind and never landed anywhere. Could you write a dialogue between two philosophers of mind really hashing this out -- one running the strict Dennettian line, the other pushing the "not a real pattern" objection -- where they actually engage with each other's strongest version of the point instead of just talking past each other?
