For a research paper in my high school philosophy elective, I've been working through F.H. Bradley's regress: if the fact that Socrates is wise requires a relation to unite Socrates and wisdom, that relation seems to need a further relation tying it to its own terms, and so on forever, so unity never actually gets achieved. Two classic proposed solutions are Frege's doctrine that concepts are 'unsaturated' (an object simply completes the gap in a concept, with no extra relation needed) and Russell's claim that a relation 'actually relating' its terms in a fact is doing something different in kind from that same relation being merely referred to as an entity, which he thought was enough by itself to block the regress. I'd like to see these two solutions argued against each other rather than just listed side by side. Could you write a dialogue between a committed Fregean and a committed Russellian in which the Russellian presses the charge that unsaturation doesn't really explain anything and just relabels the very mystery Bradley identified, while the Fregean has to answer that charge directly rather than simply restate the theory? Please make sure each side scores at least one genuine point against the other, and don't let the disagreement resolve too neatly by the end.
