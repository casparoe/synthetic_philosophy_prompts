Now that I am retired from a long career in vertebrate paleontology, I have taken up reading philosophy in earnest, and I recently came across Aumann's agreement theorem. As I understand it, the theorem demonstrates that two rational people who are aware of each other's opinion on a question cannot go on disagreeing indefinitely. This struck a nerve, because for the better part of three decades a close colleague of mine and I argued over whether the Chicxulub impact or Deccan Traps volcanism bore chief responsibility for the end-Cretaceous extinction, each of us fully aware of where the other stood, and neither of us ever moved an inch, even though I never had reason to doubt her competence or her honesty. Given the theorem, am I obliged to conclude that one of us was simply not reasoning rationally all along, and would it actually have sufficed for the two of us to exchange nothing more than our bottom-line probability estimates every few years, rather than argue over the stratigraphy and geochemistry itself, in order for us eventually to have converged?
