Question (20 points). One family of proposed defenses against acausal blackmail has an agent adopt a "surrogate goal": something it did not previously value, which it publicly and credibly commits to treating as important, precisely so that a rational blackmailer will target the surrogate rather than the agent's real values.

(a) [6 pts] In 2–3 sentences, explain the mechanism by which this is supposed to protect the agent's true values.
(b) [8 pts] An agent could instead just adopt a blanket policy of never paying any blackmailer. Explain why this policy, on its own, does not make the surrogate goal redundant.
(c) [6 pts] Give one substantive difficulty in actually getting the surrogate-goal strategy to work.

Student's answer:

(a) The idea, due to Eliezer Yudkowsky's work on timeless decision theory, is that an agent designates some object or outcome it doesn't actually care about and commits to reacting to threats against it exactly as it would react to threats against something it truly values. A blackmailer who is indifferent about which target extracts payment then has no special reason to threaten the real thing instead of the surrogate, so the agent's actual values end up shielded.

(b) A pure non-payment policy looks like it should already be enough: an agent known to never pay gives a rational blackmailer no incentive to threaten it, since the blackmailer only profits if the target pays up. But this isn't sufficient by itself, because a sufficiently intelligent blackmailer will simply keep escalating the threatened harm until the disvalue of ignoring it finally exceeds the disvalue of paying — refusal just raises the price of the threat rather than removing it, so the agent still needs some way of capping how much can be lost in the worst case, which is what the surrogate goal supplies.

(c) One real difficulty is that the whole scheme depends on the blackmailer being unable to tell the surrogate from the genuine target, or at least not caring which one it hits. A blackmailer who specifically wants to destroy what the agent truly values, rather than merely extract payment, will see through the substitution and threaten the real thing anyway.
