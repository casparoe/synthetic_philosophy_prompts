So I went down a Bayesian rabbit hole this weekend that started from trying to understand Bayesian A/B testing for work, and somehow ended on this thing called the "problem of old evidence," and now its bugging me. Setup: before general relativity existed, physicists already knew about this extra wobble in Mercury's orbit (the perihelion precession, something like 43 arcsec/century) with basically full confidence. Then GR comes along, its equations spit out that exact same number, and everyone treated this as massive confirmation of GR. But the standard Bayesian line is that E confirms T when P(T|E) > P(T) -- and if you already believe E at probability 1, conditioning on it again literally can't budge your credence in T, no matter what T says. So how did old data manage to confirm a brand new theory at all?

Turns out theres an actual split among Bayesians on how to patch this, and I want to understand both sides better by watching them fight it out. One camp says the fix is admitting nobody's logically omniscient -- the physicist didn't actually know "GR implies this number" until the derivation got done, so that discovery counts as genuinely new info you can conditionalize on, even though the raw data itself isn't new. The other camp thinks that's a dodge, and says you should instead measure the confirmation by asking what your credence in GR would've been under some counterfactual prior that never had Mercury's wobble baked into it in the first place, using that gap as the real measure of support.

Can you write me a dialogue, maybe 15-20 exchanges, no summary or resolution tacked on at the end, between one philosopher from each camp hashing this out over the Mercury/GR case. I want actual back and forth, not two monologues sitting next to each other -- specifically have the "logical omniscience" person press on whether that counterfactual prior is even well-defined or just reverse-engineered to give the right answer, and have the counterfactual person press back on whether "discovering an entailment" is just old evidence wearing a disguise. Don't let either one obviously win going in.
