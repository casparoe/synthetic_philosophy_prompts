I host a small philosophy of science podcast, and next week's guest is a philosopher who defends inference to the best explanation (IBE) as the basic form of ampliative reasoning, following Peter Lipton in glossing IBE as inference to the loveliest explanation. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view. I want at least one question on van Fraassen's "bad lot" objection—namely, that ranking the explanations we happen to have thought up says nothing about whether the truth is even among the candidates; one pressing her on why loveliness (unification, simplicity, elegance) should track likely truth rather than merely what we find satisfying; and one on whether defending scientific realism via the no-miracles argument is circular, since that argument is itself an application of IBE and so presupposes the reliability of exactly the inferential practice that divides realists from empiricists like van Fraassen. For each, add a follow-up question keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
