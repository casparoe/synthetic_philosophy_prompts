I'm a junior at my high school and one of five student members on our Student Government Elections Board, which hears complaints about campaign conduct and has the authority to certify, sanction, or void election results; I joined partly because I'm in AP Government this year and wanted to see how rules like this actually get applied rather than just discussed in the abstract. We just finished counting votes for next year's Student Body President, and the runner-up has filed a formal complaint asking us to void the results, which has turned into the most contested case the board has handled in the three years it's existed. Our faculty advisor has asked each of the five of us to submit a short written recommendation before Friday's meeting, which she'll read aloud before we debate and vote, and I'd like mine to hold up on the merits rather than simply restate whichever way I happen to lean personally.

The situation is this. Priya won by 41 votes out of about 590 cast, and Marcus, the runner-up, argues that three of her tactics crossed the line from campaigning into manipulation, even though our bylaws only ban "dishonest or manipulative practices" and separately protect "vigorous persuasive campaigning" without ever saying where one shades into the other. First, in the final week, she and her team sent noticeably different direct-message pitches to different friend groups based on things gathered from those groups' own posts and group chats -- telling club-sport captains she'd fight for a bigger locker-room budget, telling the AP crowd she'd push to cap nightly homework -- so that almost no one outside her inner circle saw one coherent version of her platform, and some of what different groups were promised is in real tension with the rest. Second, our rules bar any new campaign material once voting opens, so she timed a video to go live at the exact moment the 24-hour window began: a montage of real, unedited clips of Marcus taken from a recorded advisory-period discussion on student mental health that the school itself had posted online months earlier for students who'd been absent, resequenced and set to somber music to imply he doesn't take the issue seriously. Nothing in it is fabricated, but because the no-new-material rule had just kicked in, Marcus had no way to respond or add context without breaking the very rule she had technically obeyed. Third, because she filed her candidacy a day earlier, her video appeared above his on the school's official voting portal, and she posted a countdown urging supporters to vote the second polls opened, pulling in a wave of votes before undecided students had even scrolled down to see Marcus's video. It's been the main topic of conversation in the cafeteria all week, with people's opinions splitting almost perfectly along who they voted for, which frankly makes me trust my own instincts on this even less.

Could you draft the memo I'll circulate to the rest of the board? It should run no more than a page, recommend one clear resolution -- certifying the results as they stand, voiding the election and ordering a rerun, or some middle option like certifying the results while formally censuring the campaign -- and defend that recommendation on its merits rather than by appeal to precedent, since we have none. It should also propose two or three concrete criteria we could actually add to the bylaws to separate manipulative tactics from legitimate persuasion going forward, since whatever line we draw this week will become the de facto standard for every election after it. Please engage seriously with the strongest version of Priya's defense -- that no single statement she made was false, and that targeted messaging and favorable timing are just how campaigns work everywhere -- rather than dismissing it, since at least two of the five board members already find it convincing. And if you conclude a rerun is the right call, please suggest a realistic window for it, given that final exams start in three weeks and turnout would likely collapse after that.
