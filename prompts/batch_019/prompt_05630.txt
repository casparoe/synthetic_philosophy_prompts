"Locke's whole philosophy rests on the idea that the mind starts out blank, like white paper, with no ideas or knowledge already written on it — everything we know comes from experience, either through our senses or by reflecting on our own thoughts. That's why he spends so much time in Book I of the Essay arguing against innate ideas. You can see the same idea driving 'Some Thoughts Concerning Education': since kids aren't born with any fixed content in their heads, a tutor has to be really careful what gets put there instead. This is part of why Locke is so against beating children, since he thinks that just teaches fear instead of real virtue, and argues a tutor should use a child's sense of shame and their wish to be well thought of by their parents as the main tools of discipline instead. A good example of the blank slate idea actually working is his point about children being scared of the dark. Since the mind starts totally empty and gets shaped completely by experience and habit, Locke's overall view is that kids are all basically the same raw material at birth, and every difference we see between two kids later — personality, temper, how stubborn or easygoing they are — has to come from differences in upbringing and habit, not from anything they were born with."
