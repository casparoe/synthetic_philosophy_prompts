I spent thirty-odd years doing fieldwork on colour polymorphism in snails (the shell-banding stuff, think Cepaea) before I retired, and only in the last couple of years have I actually sat down and read philosophy of science properly rather than just absorbing Popper-flavoured folk wisdom secondhand from older colleagues. Been working through his stuff on falsifiability and naturally landed on the whole business of what he said about Darwinism, which turns out to be a lot messier than the one-line version everyone repeats ("Popper said evolution isn't science"). Got interested enough that I wrote up some notes for a little discussion group I run with a few other retirees at the library, and I'd like a second pair of eyes on it before I send it round, since half of them will have opinions and I don't want to be caught out on something dumb.

Here's roughly what I've got: Popper, in the mid-70s, called Darwinism "almost tautological" and said it wasn't really a testable theory so much as a metaphysical research programme, the worry being that "fitness" gets defined in terms of survival and reproduction, so "the fittest survive" risks collapsing into "the survivors survive." Then a few years later he apparently walked at least part of this back -- I recall a line along the lines of him being "glad to have the opportunity to make a recantation," which I think was in a New Scientist piece around 1980, though I'm honestly not 100% sure where I first read it, could've been a secondary source paraphrasing him. My own gloss, from the biologist's chair, is that the tautology charge only really bites against natural selection stated as a bare logical schema, and it dissolves once your doing actual science -- melanism in peppered moths, beak depth in Darwin's finches after a drought -- where fitness gets cashed out independently of the very outcome you're trying to explain, so the "recantation" wasn't him finding new evidence so much as him noticing he'd aimed the criticism at the wrong level of description. I also toss in a line wondering whether "metaphysical research programme" was Popper anticipating (or borrowing from) language his colleague/student Lakatos later made famous, though I genuinely don't know who influenced who there, or whether it's even a fair thing to bring up.

Could you go through this and flag anywhere I've got the history wrong, and tell me whether my resolution (bare schema vs. specific hypothesis) is actually the standard move philosophers of biology make here, or whether I'm skating past some deeper problem with it? I'd also like your take on whether the Lakatos aside is worth keeping or just muddies the water, and separately from all the accuracy stuff -- is this actually an interesting angle for a room full of retired engineers and teachers, or too inside-baseball?
