A friend and I got into a long argument about the hole argument in general relativity, and we got stuck on exactly what "sophisticated substantivalism" is supposed to give up and what it is supposed to keep. She maintained that once a substantivalist denies that two diffeomorphic models of the same spacetime — models that are qualitatively identical but differ only in which manifold point plays which role — represent distinct physical possibilities, they have thereby adopted a relational criterion of identity for spacetime points, leaving no principled difference between their view and relationalism. I maintained that giving up haecceitism about spacetime points (an individuation thesis) is logically independent of giving up the claim that points exist as entities not reducible to the matter fields defined on them (an existence thesis), so a sophisticated substantivalist can deny the former while affirming the latter without collapsing into relationalism. Could you lay out precisely what is at stake in this disagreement, and say whether it strikes you as substantive or merely verbal — and if substantive, who has the better argument, and why?
