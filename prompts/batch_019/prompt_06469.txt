So I'm the student rep on my school's Honor Council (it's five students plus a faculty advisor, we review academic integrity cases and send a recommendation to the Dean who makes it official), and we've got a case this week that's splitting the group right down the middle. Every year before finals you have to sign an Academic Integrity Pledge just to be allowed into the exam room, and buried in it is a "non-toleration clause" -- you're literally promising to report any cheating you witness. So there's this junior, let's call her R, who saw a classmate using his phone during the chem final and didn't say anything. The classmate already got caught and punished separately, that part isn't in question. R is being charged under the pledge for not reporting him, not for cheating herself. Her defense is that the "promise" doesn't really count: she had to sign it or she'd get a zero on the final, she signed it something like three weeks before the actual exam when nobody was thinking about specific situations, and honestly nobody even reads that clause, it's boilerplate everyone scrolls past to get to the test. Half the council thinks a promise is a promise no matter why or how you made it, the other half thinks something you're pressured into signing and don't even remember agreeing to isn't the kind of thing that can obligate you to snitch on a friend.

I have to write the recommendation memo we bring to the rest of the council Monday and I honestly keep flip flopping. We just started an ethics unit in my philosophy elective and my teacher mentioned this whole debate about whether a promise binds you just because you went through the "ritual" of making it, like signing the pledge is basically agreeing to play by the rules of a practice that already exists, versus whether it matters if you actually meant it in the moment, or whether it comes down to the other person being entitled to count on you because of what you signed. I don't really get how those views would even settle something like this though, especially since this isn't a normal promise like "I'll pay you back Friday" -- its a promise to report on somebody else, which feels different somehow and I can't quite explain why.

Could you draft the actual memo for me? It needs to land on one clear recommendation -- full violation with the same penalty as if she'd cheated, a lesser seperate penalty just for the non-reporting, or dropping the charge entirely because the pledge doesn't really bind her in this situation -- and lay out the reasoning in a way the council could actually use, not just "rules are rules." If you land on a lesser penalty, say roughly what that should look like too. But whatever you pick, please don't dodge the strongest version of the argument on the other side, because that's exactly where we keep getting stuck.
