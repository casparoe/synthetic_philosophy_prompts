I sit on my state bar's disciplinary board, which investigates complaints that lawyers violated the rules of professional conduct. Attorney A has filed a complaint against Attorney B for billing a client for hours he never worked. During the investigation, it emerged that A herself padded a client's bill in a comparable way five years ago, a matter settled privately and never reported to the bar. B's counsel argues the complaint should be dismissed because A has no standing to accuse B of something she did herself; other board members call this beside the point, since B's conduct either violated the billing rules or it didn't, regardless of who reported it. Our rules say nothing about whether a complainant's own record bears on a complaint's validity. Draft the board's decision memo: recommend whether to proceed against B, dismiss the complaint for lack of standing, open a separate inquiry into A, or some combination, with a timeline — and present the strongest version of the argument you reject before explaining why it fails.
