So this is a weird case of my Democratic Theory seminar colliding with actual real life, and now I have to produce a real document instead of just a response paper. For the last few weeks we've been going over the question of who actually gets to count as "the public" for a given vote -- the all-affected view (roughly: if your interests are impacted by a decision, you should have a say, full stop) versus the narrower all-subjected/coercion view (you only get enfranchised if you'll actually be bound by whatever gets passed), plus that annoying regress problem where you need some group to decide who counts as the deciding group, and that group needs deciding too. Anyway, I'm one of two student reps on a subcommittee our city council and the university jointly convene on town-gown stuff, and this session is specifically about a ballot measure -- since I talked the most in our last meeting, I somehow got stuck drafting our actual recommendation to council.

Here's the situation. The city put a measure on the May ballot that would freeze new multi-unit rental construction within half a mile of campus for five years, framed as protecting "neighborhood character," but everyone knows its going to make student rents brutal for half a decade since its basically a supply cap. Students are something like 40% of the town's population but a tiny fraction of registered voters, because registering here requires being "domiciled" in the city, which legally means intending to live here indefinitely -- and almost no student will sign that in good faith when they know they're graduating in a couple years. The subcommittee (two permanent residents, two students including me, the city clerk chairing) is deadlocked three ways: two members want to leave registration rules untouched and just bolt on extra public-comment sessions aimed at students; two of us want the city to proactively tell students they're eligible to register at their campus address for this and future land-use votes, and make it easy; and the clerk keeps proposing a separate "student advisory ballot" that gets tallied and handed to council alongside the binding result but doesn't count toward it. I have to send council our memo before the meeting in three weeks, and it has to actually recommend ONE option with a timeline, not a pro/con list -- but whatever we recommend, I don't want it to dodge the honestly decent objection that people who mostly won't live here in three years probably shouldn't get to set housing policy the permanent residents are stuck with for decades. Can you help me draft it?
