My friend Helen, a cosmologist who let me hang round her lab back when I was a baby reporter, died last week. For forty years her go-to party trick was paraphrasing Frank Ramsey's essay "Epilogue" at people -- staring at the vastness of the universe never made her feel small, because stars are impressive but they can't think or love, and that's what actually counts, plus something about the heat death of the universe being so far off that its "present value" basically rounds to zero, so don't bother discounting your Tuesday for it. She told her family more than once that whoever did her eulogy was banned from any "watching over us" or "at peace now" stuff, because she reckoned Ramsey's whole point was that you don't need any of that to be okay about dying. Im doing the eulogy now and I'm nervous: half the room is her old physics department, who'll clock it instantly if I just namedrop Ramsey without the actual argument behind it, and the other half is her sister and teenage grandkids, who just need to feel better, not audit my philosophy. Can you write me something around 8 minutes long that actually uses Ramsey's case to comfort both halves of the room, without turning into a lecture?
