A friend and I spent a long evening arguing about whether individuals really persist across possible worlds, and neither of us budged. She invoked Kripke's Humphrey objection: if Lewis's counterpart theory makes "Humphrey might have won the election" true only because some distinct, merely similar counterpart wins in another world, then the sentence isn't really about Humphrey at all. I countered that transworld identity fares no better, since without non-trivial individual essences it collapses into the bare, ungrounded identities of Chisholm's paradox, where a chain of small changes lets Adam and Noah swap identities; and once essences are posited to block this, they look like an ad hoc primitive quietly doing the very job that similarity does openly for the counterpart theorist. Please compare counterpart theory and transworld identity as accounts of modal predication in light of this exchange, focusing on whether individual essences really give the transworld-identity theorist a more principled account of cross-world individuation, or simply relocate the same arbitrariness under a grander name — and defend a verdict on which view fares better overall.
