I'm a senior taking AP Physics, and we spent a week this semester on the second law of thermodynamics. It got me curious enough to keep reading on my own afterward, including part of a popular physics book on entropy and the arrow of time (I think it was by Sean Carroll) and a couple of philosophy-of-physics articles a friend on my school's science-olympiad team sent me. The basic picture I've pieced together is that the fundamental dynamical laws -- Newtonian mechanics, electromagnetism, even the Schrödinger equation -- don't pick out a direction of time at all, and that the only reason "the future" looks different from "the past" is that entropy is overwhelmingly likely to increase rather than decrease. That part I feel pretty solid on.

What confuses me is that every source I've found treats the extremely low entropy of the very early universe as a separate, still-unsolved mystery -- what some of these articles call the "Past Hypothesis" -- rather than as something the second law has already accounted for. If the whole explanation of the arrow of time boils down to "entropy climbs from low to high," then having a low starting point doesn't seem like some extra fact sitting outside that explanation; it seems like the very premise the explanation depends on, so surely it's already covered by the same reasoning that explains why entropy increases in the first place. My teacher insisted this is a genuinely open problem in cosmology and not just the arrow-of-time question restated, but I haven't been able to figure out why treating them as two separate questions isn't just double-counting the same fact. Could you help me see what I'm missing?
