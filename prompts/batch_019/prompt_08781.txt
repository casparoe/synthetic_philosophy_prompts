I've spent years reading LessWrong, Overcoming Bias, and the Astral Codex Ten comments section, so I thought I had Newcomb's Problem settled: one-box, since a policy of one-boxing predictably leaves you richer. The more I examine the argument, though, the more it looks like a restatement of evidential decision theory's own reasoning: you one-box because one-boxing is the best news you could get about what's already sitting in the opaque box. That makes me wonder why Yudkowsky and the MIRI crowd bothered inventing Timeless and then Functional Decision Theory, dressed up in talk of "subjunctive dependence" and "logical" rather than evidential correlation, instead of just admitting that one-boxing is evidential decision theory after all. Is there a substantive difference between one-boxing via EDT and one-boxing via FDT, or is the logical-dependence language just a rebrand to dodge EDT's bad reputation?
