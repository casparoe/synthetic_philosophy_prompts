Over the winter holidays, I got pulled into a long dinner-table argument with my partner's uncle, an enthusiastic amateur astrologer, about whether astrology deserves any intellectual respect. At one point he played what he clearly considered a trump card: "Even Kepler—the man who worked out the actual laws of planetary motion—was a practicing astrologer, so don't tell me it's all nonsense." I didn't have a good response at the time, and the claim has nagged at me ever since, especially once I started reading around the topic and found that the reality is considerably stranger than either "Kepler secretly believed in astrology" or "Kepler only did it for the paycheck."

As far as I can tell, Kepler spent real energy attacking the astrology practiced around him—the zodiac signs, the houses, the planetary dignities and exaltations, the whole apparatus used by the almanac-makers and rival court astrologers—as arbitrary conventions with no basis in nature. And yet in works like De Fundamentis Astrologiae Certioribus and Tertius Interveniens, he just as insistently defended a stripped-down astrology of his own devising, centered on planetary aspects, which he connected to the same harmonic, whole-number ratios underlying the musical consonances he discusses in Harmonice Mundi, and to some kind of sensitive faculty in the earth itself that responds to the geometric angles the planets form. What I haven't been able to work out is whether this was a genuinely principled position—whether the aspects really do follow, by his own lights, from the same commitments that drove his physics and cosmology—or whether it is better described as a face-saving compromise that let a working court astrologer keep his salary while disowning the parts of the trade he found embarrassing.

Could you walk me through, in real detail, exactly where Kepler drew this line, and reconstruct the actual argument—rather than just the biographical fact—by which he thought the astrology he kept survived the skepticism he directed at the astrology he discarded? I would like to be able to explain the substance of his position the next time this comes up, rather than falling back on "it's complicated."
