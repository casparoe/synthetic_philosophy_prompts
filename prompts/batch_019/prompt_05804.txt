I'm taking a seminar this term on Mill's political economy, and we've just worked through the Chapters on Socialism alongside the revised chapters on property and on the future of the labouring classes that he added to later editions of the Principles of Political Economy. I also sit on the governing board of my university's student housing cooperative, which holds its annual general meeting in three weeks. A faction of members wants to scrap the bylaw tying each member's rent discount to hours logged on shared cooking, cleaning, and maintenance duties, and replace it with an "equal shares" rule: the same discount for everyone regardless of hours worked, with labor secured through appeals to solidarity rather than accounting. Advocates call this abolishing our internal wage relation for something closer to communism; opponents predict it will kill the incentive to do unglamorous work, and others separately object to binding every house in the co-op to one scheme by simple majority vote rather than letting houses opt in first. Please draft the decision memo I'll circulate to the board before the AGM. It should use the specific considerations Mill weighs against strict communism and in favor of the more gradualist, Fourierist-style cooperative schemes — what sustains effort in the absence of unequal reward, and his preference for testing contested social arrangements by small voluntary trial rather than blanket imposition — to reach one concrete recommendation, with an implementation timeline, that still engages honestly with the strongest argument for whichever side it doesn't adopt.
