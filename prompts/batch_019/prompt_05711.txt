I recently read about G.E.M. Anscombe's 1956 pamphlet "Mr Truman's Degree," written to oppose Oxford's plan to award Harry Truman an honorary degree. As I understand her argument, what made the atomic bombings murder, on her view, wasn't the scale of death but the intentional structure of the act: civilian deaths were the chosen means of breaking Japan's will to fight, not a foreseen side effect of hitting a military target, and no good consequence — including a shorter war — could redeem an act intentional in that way.

I'm a software engineer, and I keep wondering how that framework holds up against a problem that only really emerged after her death in 2001. In "signature strikes," a strike is authorized against a cluster of people who match a behavioral pattern (e.g., movement between known militant sites), without anyone knowing in advance who, specifically, will die. Does her intention/foresight distinction even apply when what's targeted is a pattern rather than a person? She also wrote, in "Intention," about how the same act can be intentional under one description and not another — given that, would she treat intending to kill whoever matches the pattern as morally equivalent to intending a named person's death, or is there a morally relevant gap there? Please reason from her actual positions rather than a generic deontology gloss, and flag anywhere you're extrapolating beyond views she explicitly held.
