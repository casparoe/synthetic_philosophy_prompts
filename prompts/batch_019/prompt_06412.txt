A close friend of mine, who is finishing a PhD in mathematical logic, and I spent most of dinner last week arguing about what Skolem's paradox actually shows, and several days of follow-up messages later, neither of us has budged. Her position, as best I can reconstruct it, runs like this: the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem guarantees that if ZFC has any model at all, it has a countable one, and that countable model still satisfies every theorem of ZFC, including Cantor's theorem — so, from the inside, it "believes" that its own version of the power set of omega has no bijection with omega. Since this is true no matter which model you inspect, she concludes that "uncountable" can never mean anything more than "this particular model happens to be missing a bijection": there is no model-independent fact about the size of the continuum, and mathematicians who talk as though Cantor proved something about actual infinite collections — rather than merely about the blind spots of whatever axiom system they happen to be working in — are trading on a confusion. It's a confusion, she pointed out with some satisfaction, that even Skolem himself seems to have shared, given how deflationary his own remarks on the "relativity" of set-theoretic notions were.

I objected that this seemed to prove too much: the real lesson looked to me like a fact about the limits of what first-order axioms can pin down, not a discovery that cardinality itself is somehow illusory or purely formal. I also vaguely recall that philosophers ran into a version of exactly this dispute over Putnam's later model-theoretic arguments against realism, and that the inference from Löwenheim–Skolem-style facts to any substantive metaphysical conclusion ran into real technical trouble — but I couldn't reconstruct the details well enough to actually settle the argument. Could you lay out, in some detail, exactly what Skolem's paradox does and doesn't establish, and diagnose where — if anywhere — my friend's move from "countability is model-relative" to "there's no fact of the matter about uncountability" actually breaks down?
