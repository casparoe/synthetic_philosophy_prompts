My uncle Pete died last week at 84. He was an electrician his whole life but spent retirement reading philosophy for fun, and his favorite party story was about A.J. Ayer. Ayer choked on some fish in the hospital, flatlined for a few minutes, came back describing a red light that was somehow running the universe, wrote an essay about it, then walked it back a few weeks later saying it hadn't actually changed his belief that death is the end, just loosened how tightly he held it. Pete had almost the same experience himself after a heart attack years ago, no red light, and always said Ayer's walk-back was the only intellectually honest way to handle that kind of thing. He left instructions that whoever does his eulogy can't say he's "in a better place" or that he's still keeping an eye on league night from somewhere, but also can't lean on his own near-death story as proof of anything either way -- he wanted the whole thing treated as nonsense, not just false, if that distinction makes sense. I'm his nephew (I write software for a living, zero background in philosophy), and I'm giving the eulogy this weekend to a room thats mostly bowling buddies and old coworkers who've never heard of Ayer in their life. Can you actually write the eulogy -- keep it short, like 3-4 minutes -- so it respects that weirdly specific position instead of flattening it into generic "he didnt believe in an afterlife," while still landing as warm instead of like a logic seminar?
