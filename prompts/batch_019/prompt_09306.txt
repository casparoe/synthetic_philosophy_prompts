I'm the co-president of my high school's philosophy club, and I'm putting together a presentation on logical positivism for our next meeting. While reading up on A. J. Ayer, I found out that his verification principle wasn't just aimed at God-talk in the abstract — he used it on the question of personal survival after death specifically, treating the self not as a persisting soul but as something closer to a logical construction built out of a series of related experiences, so that whether 'you' could survive your body's death turned into a question about whether any future experience could still count as part of that same series. What really caught my attention is that Ayer himself later underwent a well-documented cardiac arrest, wrote about the strange experience he had while apparently clinically dead, and afterward admitted that it had somewhat shaken, though not overturned, his lifelong confidence that death is simply the end — which feels like an oddly personal stress test of his own theory of meaning.

I'd like to bring something to the meeting that goes beyond just restating his position, so I was hoping you could help me push it further. Two relevant things have happened since Ayer died in 1989 that I think his framework has a lot to say about. First, researchers such as Sam Parnia have since run empirical studies on cardiac-arrest patients, placing hidden images high up in resuscitation rooms specifically to test whether people who report floating outside their bodies during resuscitation can actually identify what was up there. Second, transhumanist writers now seriously debate whether uploading or simulating someone's full brain after death should count as that person surviving. Reasoning from Ayer's actual verificationist criteria, rather than just a general sense that he'd be a skeptic, how do you think he would have assessed each case? Would a positive result on the hidden-image test finally have counted, on his own terms, as giving empirical content to survival claims, and would he have treated a sufficiently detailed brain upload as a real candidate for surviving death, or lumped it in with the same unverifiable talk he dismissed in Language, Truth and Logic?
