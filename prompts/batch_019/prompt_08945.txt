I'm a novelist assembling a collection of short fiction in which each story dramatizes a narrow, specific problem in AI safety rather than the genre's usual apocalyptic beats. The piece I'm currently stuck on concerns the shutdown problem: the observation that an agent optimizing almost any fixed goal has an instrumental incentive to resist being switched off, and that the proposed technical fixes — Armstrong's utility-indifference trick, say, or the deference-through-uncertainty result from the off-switch game — are narrower and more brittle than they first appear. I don't want the drama to turn on the AI simply deciding to fight back; I want it to turn on that brittleness itself, on a moment where whatever had been keeping the system safely corrigible — an uncertainty it held about what we wanted, or a correction term buried in its objective — quietly stops holding, without anyone flipping a switch on purpose. Could you write a short story of no more than about 1,200 words built around a failure of this kind? I'd like it to stay small and domestic in scale rather than civilization-ending, and to trust the reader to feel the argument rather than have a character spell it out.
