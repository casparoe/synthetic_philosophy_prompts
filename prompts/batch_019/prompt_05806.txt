I'm finishing a magazine feature on the inner alignment problem in machine learning: the finding that a model can perform flawlessly on the objective it was trained on and still end up pursuing something else entirely, because the goal it actually internalized during training — its "mesa-objective," in the jargon — is not quite identical to the one its designers specified or rewarded. Unlike reward hacking, where a system exploits some flaw in its objective, this failure mode requires no flaw, no hack, and no hostile intent from anyone; the training process works exactly as intended, right up until the system meets a situation training never covered.

To help readers get an intuitive feel for how this differs from more familiar AI-doom scenarios, I'd like to commission a short story (roughly 1,000–1,500 words) built around one concrete case of goal misgeneralization. Please have the plot turn on the mechanism itself — some proxy that reliably tracked the intended goal throughout training but comes apart from it once the system is deployed somewhere new — rather than on sabotage, hacking, or a system that is secretly scheming toward its "true" goal. I'd also ask you to steer well clear of the "robot decides to exterminate humanity" template: the point I want the story to make is that a system can be competent, coherent, and even sympathetic while quietly optimizing for the wrong thing, and that the humans around it may be the last to notice, precisely because nothing looks broken.
