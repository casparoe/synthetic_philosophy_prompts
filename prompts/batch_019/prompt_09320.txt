Quick bit of context: at my company we started running a poll to settle which one big tech-debt project our team of about a dozen engineers tackles each quarter, since arguing about it live in meetings kept going nowhere. I threw together a little internal tool for it that does ranked-choice / instant-runoff voting: everyone ranks all six or so candidate projects from favorite to least favorite, and the tool eliminates whoever's got the fewest first-place votes each round and redistributes those ballots to next choices until one project is left standing. I specifically picked ranked-choice because I'd read it's supposed to be way harder to game than just going with whoever gets the most first-place votes, plus some actual cities use it for real elections so it seemed like the legit choice.

Except last quarter one of our senior engineers, who's way more into game theory than the rest of us, told me afterwards that he'd deliberately ranked his actual second-favorite project dead last (below a couple things he genuinely didn't care about) instead of putting it second where it "honestly" belonged. According to him, doing that changed the order in which stuff got eliminated in the early rounds, which snowballed into his real favorite winning instead of loosing to his second choice in the final head-to-head. He wasn't even sneaky about it either, he pulled up the spreadsheet afterward and walked me through exactly why it worked. I mentioned this to a couple friends over drinks and we couldn't agree on whether it's fixable.

Now I'm trying to figure out if this is a "me" problem, like I implemented IRV wrong or just need a smarter tiebreak rule somewhere, or if its something deeper. Would switching to a Condorcet method, or Borda count, or plain approval voting have closed off this loophole, or is there just no way to design a poll like this thats fully immune to someone voting "dishonestly" once they understand how the counting works? Kind of want to actually understand why, one way or the other, before I roll v2 of this out company-wide instead of just cargo-culting whichever method sounds fanciest on Wikipedia.
