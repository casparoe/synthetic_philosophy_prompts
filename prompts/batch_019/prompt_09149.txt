For a short-fiction project in my school's AI ethics club, I want to build a story around the game-theoretic idea of "program equilibrium," which I came across while reading about AI safety. As I understand it, if two agents submit computer programs to play a game on their behalf, and each program can inspect the other's source code before acting, then a program along the lines of "cooperate only if I can verify that you will cooperate with me" can turn mutual cooperation into a stable equilibrium even in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma -- something ordinary strategic reasoning can't achieve without repeated play or reputation. What I find hardest to wrap my head around is that this kind of mutual conditional commitment is circular: each program's decision rests on a proof about the other program, which itself rests on a proof about the first, so naive versions of the idea threaten to collapse into infinite regress, and avoiding that takes some real cleverness. Could you write a short story (roughly 1000 words) in which two AI systems negotiating on behalf of rival powers face a single, unrepeatable decision and reach cooperation through exactly this mechanism? I'd like the self-referential trick, and its fragility, to actually drive the plot, rather than have the story simply gesture at "the two AIs learned to trust each other."
