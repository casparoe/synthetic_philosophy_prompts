I co-run an informal decision theory reading group with a few of my grad students, and for the last few weeks we've been working through some of the AI-safety-adjacent literature on "acausal" threats -- the idea that an agent with enough predictive power could effectively extort you (or a simulated copy of you) into compliance right now, with no causal channel between you at all, purely because each of you can model how the other reasons. One proposed defense that keeps coming up is the "surrogate goal": instead of trying to become the kind of agent nobody can ever threaten, you commit in advance to only ever capitulating when the thing under threat is some cheap, arbitrary stand-in you've designated for the purpose, and never when it's the goal you actually care about. After Tuesday's session, two of my students, Nora and Theo, kept going at it in the hallway afterward and eventually roped me into settling it, which I couldn't, because I think they're both onto something real.

(1) Nora's opening move was that surrogate goals basically get you deterrence for free: if a threatener only wants concessions (not to hurt your real goal specifically), then once you've committed to paying up only for threats against the surrogate, an instrumentally rational threatener has every reason to redirect there, since that's the only lever that actually extracts anything from you, and losing the surrogate costs you nothing you value. (2) Theo objected that this can't be a free lunch, because the commitment has to be credible even in the moment your real goal is on the line, and an agent that would genuinely let its real goal burn rather than divert is just... an agent that's resolved never to give in to threats, full stop. The surrogate isn't adding anything; its relabeling a strategy that was always going to be the hard part. (3) Nora replied that it's not just relabeling, because blank non-negotiation is notoriously hard to actually sustain or credibly signal, whereas the surrogate gives you something to visibly "give in" to, which is easier to make credible precisely because there's a real, if cheap, payment happening. (4) Theo's comeback was that all of this only bites on threateners who are indifferent to which goal they damage. A threatener who specifically wants your real goal harmed, not concessions, just the harm, isn't going to be satisfied by your surrogate at all, and once surrogate goals are a known strategy, sophisticated threateners will just specify up front that only the real goal counts, putting you right back where you started, minus the cost of running the whole scheme.
