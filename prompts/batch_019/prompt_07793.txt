I am assembling case studies on classical Chinese philosophy of language for a graduate seminar. Write a dialogue that stages, at full philosophical strength, a fresh encounter between Gongsun Long and Kong Chuan at Lord Pingyuan's residence. Gongsun Long should defend 白馬非馬 ('a white horse is not a horse') using the actual moves of the White Horse Dialogue -- that 'horse' names a shape and 'white' names a color, and that wanting a horse is satisfied by a yellow or black one while wanting a white horse is not -- rather than resting on the slogan alone. Kong Chuan, arguing as a defender of the rectification of names, should not simply insist that white horses are obviously horses; instead, he should locate the exact point at which Gongsun Long's use of 非 ('is not') slides from denying that the concepts 'horse' and 'white horse' are identical to denying that white horses belong to the category of horses, and press him on why the former sense should be taken to settle the latter question. Should Gongsun Long fall back on the parry attributed to him in the Kongcongzi about the King of Chu's lost bow, Kong Chuan should meet it more squarely than either recorded version of that anecdote manages. Neither character should concede more than the dialectic actually forces.
