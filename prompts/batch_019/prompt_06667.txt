I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for a long time, and one relic of that habit is a slogan I picked up years ago while working through Eliezer Yudkowsky's quantum mechanics sequence on Less Wrong: that Bell's theorem presents a choice between locality and "realism," and that the tasteful resolution is to give up realism (or, on some versions, give up the assumption that a measurement has one single, definite outcome) rather than accept literal action at a distance. I had more or less filed this away as settled until I recently read further into the philosophy-of-physics literature on the subject — Maudlin, Norsen, and others who argue that Bell-inequality violations demonstrate genuine nonlocality — and ran into a serious counter-argument: that "realism" was never doing independent work in Bell's own derivation, once you notice how the EPR argument extracts it from the perfectly correlated outcomes of distant measurements under an assumption of locality, or once you use Bell's later, explicitly stochastic formulation of local causality, which never presupposes determinism or hidden variables to begin with. On that reading, discarding "realism" does not actually buy locality back, and the theorem is best read as a direct proof that nature itself is nonlocal. I would rather not relitigate whether Many-Worlds counts as local, since that feels like its own separate mess; I would rather watch the cleanest version of the underlying dispute fought out directly, by two people who each hold their position for serious reasons.

Could you write a dialogue between a committed Bohmian-mechanics physicist and a committed QBist physicist that stages exactly this disagreement? I like the idea of setting it at a science museum that has just installed a working tabletop Bell test — a down-conversion crystal producing entangled photon pairs, polarizers, coincidence counters — where both have been invited to record a short explainer video; once the event wraps up, they find themselves alone in front of the apparatus, unable to agree on even one sentence describing what it demonstrates, while the exhibit's director waits by the door for a script.

I would like the Bohmian to argue for nonlocality on the merits rather than simply assert it, including some version of the point above about where "realism" actually comes from in the EPR/Bell argument, and I would like the QBist to state the real content of the QBist dissolution of the puzzle — personalist, agent-relative probability assignments, no observer-independent outcome sitting out there to be influenced — rather than gesture vaguely at Copenhagen. Each of them should engage the other's strongest move rather than talk past it, and neither should come off as the weaker thinker. They do not need to reach agreement, but I would like the exchange to end with the crux stated precisely enough that a careful reader could say exactly what further fact or argument would have to give for either one of them to change their mind.
