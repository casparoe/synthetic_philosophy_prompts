In my seminar on practical reason we just spent two weeks on Ruth Chang's work on hard choices: her rejection of the "trichotomy thesis" — that any two options must be better, worse, or equal — in favor of a fourth relation she calls "parity," where alternatives are comparable but ranked by none of the three. My sister is marrying her fiancé, also an economist, in three weeks, and as the resident philosophy student in the family I've been asked to give the toast. Half the room will be people from their PhD program who treat the completeness axiom as obvious; the other half is family who will glaze over at any hint of jargon. The real story is that she left a genuinely good relationship to be with him, and relatives keep asking, half-joking, what finally tipped the scales — a question I'd like the toast to quietly dismantle. Please write it: about three minutes long, warm and funny rather than lecture-like, using Chang's notions of parity and of choice as an exercise of agency to show why that question rests on a mistake, without ever saying "parity," "incommensurable," or "completeness axiom" out loud, and without collapsing into "you can't help who you love."
