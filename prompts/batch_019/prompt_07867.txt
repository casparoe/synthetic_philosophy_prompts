I was covering an effective altruism conference last month and ended up at the bar afterward with two attendees arguing about whether it's even rational to donate to a "safe bet" charity like a malaria bednet program. Nadia's point was that we have no real idea whether saving a life nets out positive long-term -- that kid could grow up and do anything, and the downstream effects on population and resources are totally unknowable, so confidently ranking bednets above other options is unjustified. Marcus said that cuts both ways: every option, including doing nothing, carries the same unknowable tail, so if it's a wash everywhere you just fall back on what you can foresee, which still favors bednets. Nadia said it isn't a wash here, because theres a known-ish effect where cutting child mortality in high-fertility regions can boost population growth for a while before fertility catches up -- a real asymmetric unknown, not fog that cancels out. Marcus said fine, but thats just another factor you slap a rough probability on and add to the sum -- not being able to pin a number down exactly isn't the same as the calculation being impossible. Nadia said that's the sleight of hand: you can't even roughly quantify it because there are a dozen further downstream effects and no idea which way any of them point, and "just estimate it" is doing all the work while pretending it isn't.

Go through this turn by turn and tell me who's got the better of it at each point, not just who wins overall, and flag anything you think they're both missing.
