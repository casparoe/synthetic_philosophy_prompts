So this happened at a grad-student happy hour a few weeks back and it's been rattling around in my head since. I'm doing a PhD in phil of language, so I wasn't one of the people actually arguing, but two friends from the metaphysics side of the department (I'll call them Renata and Owen) got into it about whether Owen's lab is "the same lab" it was when he joined six years ago. Some background: every single person who was around when Owen started has since left, the lab moved buildings two years ago, and the PI switched the whole group's model organism after a grant fell through, so even the actual research questions are unrecognizable now. The trigger for the argument was dumb — a first-year in the lab refused to take part in a decade-old prank war with the lab next door, on the grounds that "we're not even the lab that started it anymore."

Renata's first move was that obviously it isn't the same lab in any sense worth caring about — same name and PI, sure, but that's just a label, and once you've swapped out every person, the building, and the research question, you've swapped out everything that made it that lab rather then a label attached to nothing in particular. Owen said she was ignoring real continuity: the weekly lab meeting has run every single week for fifteen years, techniques and lab notebooks get handed down person to person before anyone leaves, and the PI has been the throughline the entire time — that's a genuine causal chain, not just a sticker. Renata then tried to dissolve the whole thing: she said they're probably just using "lab" differently — she means something like "the community of people and their shared ongoing project," he means something like "the standing unit with continuous leadership and funding" — and once you seperate those out there's nothing left to actually fight about, they're each just right about their own sense. Owen wouldn't take the out, though: he said even under Renata's own "community and shared project" reading, the apprenticeship-style handoffs and unbroken meetings are enough to satisfy it, so they still disagree even holding her sense fixed. Renata's last move was that this just shows "lab" is a vague predicate with no sharp turnover cutoff, so there's no fact of the matter left to disagree about — which, she said, amounts to her being right anyway by default. Owen shot back that this was a dodge: this isn't some borderline case sitting near a cutoff, it's about as extreme an example of total turnover as you could construct, so if vagueness were doing real work here it should show up near the margins, not in a case this lopsided.

I've got some metaontology from a seminar last year (Hirsch on quantifier variance, Chalmers on verbal disputes, all that) but not enough to actually referee this myself, and it's bugging me more than it should. Could you go through their exchange move by move and say who's got the better of it at each stage, rather than just picking an overall winner? I'm especially interested in the last two moves — is Renata right that the dispute ultimately collapses into either "merely verbal" or "no fact of the matter," or is Owen right that something substantive survives both of those attempted dissolves? And is there anything about the case you think they're both missing?
