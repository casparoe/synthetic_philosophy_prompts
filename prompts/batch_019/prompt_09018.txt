I've been chewing on this idea from L.A. Paul's work on "transformative experience" — might be fuzzy on some of the details since I got it from a philosophy podcast a while back, but the gist is: some decisions can't be handled by ordinary expected-value reasoning, because the thing you'd need to know to weigh the options — what the outcome would actually be like, from the inside — is only accessible after you've already gone through with it, and by then the experience itself has rewired what you value, so your "after" self isn't even a neutral judge of whether it was worth it. She uses becoming a vampire as the toy example (undead, but apparently a wonderful sort of undead, and only vampires can tell you what it's like), and something like having a first kid as the real-world version. What's supposed to make it different from ordinary uncertainty (like not knowing if you'll enjoy a new restaurant) is that there's no way to close the gap in advance even in principle — its not that you happen to lack info, its that the relevant kind of access doesn't exist yet for you.

Could you write me a short story that puts a character inside that exact bind, someone clear-headed enough to see the trap for what it is rather than just feeling generic "big scary decision" dread? Doesn't have to involve literal vampires — a hive mind, a one-way colony ship, a personality-altering treatment, a religious order, whatever setup you like is fine, as long as it keeps the actual structure: the character has no way to get evidence about their post-transformation values from where they're standing, and every attempt to reason it through runs straight into that wall. I'd rather you didn't have them land on some tidy resolution at the end, since I think part of what makes this puzzle interesting is that there isn't an obviously correct way out of it — but wherever they do end up, I want it to feel like it's actually grappling with that specific problem and not just standing in for indecision in general.
