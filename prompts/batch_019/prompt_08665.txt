I've been going through Bentham's unpublished essay on "paederasty" (his 18th century term, basically consensual sex between men, not what the word implies now) for a dissertation chapter on offense and the harm principle. He builds the whole case for decriminalizing it on hedonic calculus grounds, but then has to deal with the objection that tons of people feel genuine disgust just from knowing such acts happen somewhere, and that disgust is real pain that could tip the scales. His way out is, more or less, to say antipathy-pain doesn't count, or counts way less, treating it like inherited prejudice rather than a genuinely felt harm - but his calculus (intensity, duration, certainty, propinquity, etc) is supposed to be totally neutral about where a pain comes from or how "reasonable" it is. Can you argue the strongest version of the claim that Bentham has no principled way, within his own hedonistic system, to discount that antipathy-pain, i.e. that this move is a quiet departure from strict utilitarianism rather than an application of it? Not looking for a both sides survey, i want the best possible case for the inconsistency specifically.
