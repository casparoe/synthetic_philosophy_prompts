I am moderating a graduate colloquium Q&A whose guest is a contemporary philosopher who has spent her career defending and refining P. F. Strawson's naturalistic, reactive-attitudes account of moral responsibility from "Freedom and Resentment." Draft seven or eight questions for the session that go beyond having her restate the view; for each, add a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. I want at least one question on: (1) whether Strawson's claim that the participant stance is too deeply woven into ordinary human life for us to give up actually answers the skeptic's demand that the practice of holding people responsible be justified, or merely explains why we cannot stop engaging in it regardless of whether it is justified; (2) how a Strawsonian should handle Pereboom-style manipulation cases, in which an agent who satisfies every fairness or reasons-responsiveness condition on offer was covertly engineered to reason and act exactly as she did, and whether excluding such agents by adding a historical, non-reactive-attitude condition abandons the naturalistic spirit of Strawson's original proposal; and (3) why Strawson resists letting the objective attitude spread, case by case, into a global replacement for the participant stance, given that he already grants its local appropriateness toward children and the severely mentally ill. Close with a question asking what kind of consideration -- as opposed to a particular exempting fact about a single agent -- could in principle show that the whole participant framework, rather than just one of its applications, is unjustified.
