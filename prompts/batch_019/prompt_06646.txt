So a friend of mine whose doing a cosmology PhD was telling me about the Boltzmann brain problem the other day and it's kind of been stuck in my head since. The basic idea: in a universe with eternal accelerated expansion (a de Sitter-ish vacuum that never fully decays), random thermal/quantum fluctuations will, given enough time, spontaneously assemble isolated conscious brains complete with a full set of false memories - and they'll do this vastly more often than the universe produces "ordinary" observers like us through the normal route of a low-entropy Big Bang plus billions of years of structure formation. Cosmologists apparently take this seriously as an actual constraint on theories: if a model implies that a randomly selected observer is overwhelmingly more likely to be one of these freak fluctuation-brains than a real one, that's treated as a strike against the model, something you're supposed to fix (by requiring the vacuum to eventually decay, or by favoring certain "measures" for regulating the infinities eternal inflation generates, etc.).

I want you to push back on that whole practice. Argue that a cosmological model predicting "Boltzmann brain domination" is not actually disconfirmed by the fact that we're having ordered, coherent experience right now, and that physicists who treat BB-domination as a real problem to be avoided are making an illegitimate inference. I think the crux involves some self-sampling-style anthropic assumption doing all the work here (something like "I should reason as if randomly drawn from everyone who has my exact memories"), plus the fact that the ratio of ordinary observers to Boltzmann brains is itself measure-dependent - it changes depending on which regularization scheme you pick for all those infinites - which seems to make "avoids BB domination" a weirdly circular thing to use when choosing between measures in the first place. Anyway, don't just tell me anthropic reasoning is confusing and leave it at that, actually make the case, get technical if you need to.
