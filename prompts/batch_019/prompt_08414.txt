My younger brother drowned two weeks ago pulling a little girl out of the river near our parents' place — he got her to the bank but the current dragged him back under before anyone could reach him in time. Ever since college he'd been stuck on that Peter Singer pond thing, the one where if a kid's drowning right in front of you, you have to wade in and save it even if it ruins your clothes, and he used to argue that means we're all basically failing kids just as bad by not giving more to save the ones starving somewhere we can't see. He never really made peace with that, it kind of ate at him honestly. I'm giving his eulogy Saturday, theres gonna be like 200 people there, half of them firefighters and guys from his rowing club who just see him as a straightforward hero, plus a few friends from his old philosophy reading group who know exactly what he was wrestling with. I don't want to turn my brothers funeral into an ethics lecture, but I also don't want to flatten him into a simple hero story when the whole reason he didn't hesitate is tangled up in an idea he could never actually resolve. Can you write the eulogy so it actually deals with that honestly instead of dodging it?
