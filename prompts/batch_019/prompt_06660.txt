Okay this is going to sound oddly specific but I need a sanity check because I've fallen down a total rabbit hole. Short version: my grandma had a fall in her apartment last month and was stuck on the floor for something like 14 hours before my aunt found her. I'm normally the one who calls her every day around 6, without fail, but that week I had a brutal work deadline and skipped three days in a row. She's recovering okay-ish now but there's some kidney damage from being down that long, and ever since, my uncle has decided my not calling is basically THE reason she was down there so long - his exact words were "if you'd called like you always do, none of this happens," like that just settles it that I caused the extra hours on the floor.

This sent me into a very unproductive 2am spiral of googling, and it turns out there's an entire metaphysics literature on exactly this kind of case, under the heading "causation by omission." From what I can piece together, David Lewis basically bites the bullet and says omissions can be genuine causes as long as the right counterfactual dependence is there - she wouldn't have been down there that long if I'd called, so my not-calling caused the extra time, full stop. But Helen Beebee and Phil Dowe seem to push back hard on this. I think Dowe's view is that real causation requires some actual physical process or transfer between cause and effect, and since "my not calling" doesn't transfer anything, it can't be a genuine cause the way an actual event is - at best some kind of "quasi-causation." And I vaguely remember Beebee arguing that which omissions we're even willing to single out as "the cause," out of the literally infinite list of things that also didn't happen (the neighbor didn't call either, the mailman didn't check in, I didn't fly out and personally install a fall sensor), is driven by norms about who was expected to act, not by anything that tracks a real causal structure in the world.

So here's my actual ask: could you build out the Beebee/Dowe-style case as strongly as you can - i.e. argue that my not calling was not a genuine cause of her being on the floor for 14 hours, that the pull to call it a "cause" is really just tracking the normative fact that I was the expected/salient intervener, and that this holds even though the counterfactual dependence obviously does too. I'm not trying to weasel out of feeling bad about it, for the record, I just want to know if my uncle actually has the metaphysics on his side or if hes confusing causal responsibility with "the person who normally would've prevented this."
