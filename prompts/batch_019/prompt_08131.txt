I write philosophically-minded science fiction, and I would like to test whether a piece of decision theory I have rarely seen handled well in fiction can survive being dramatized: acausal trade. The idea is that two agents who can never causally influence one another — say, because they lie on opposite sides of a cosmological horizon that light will never cross, or inhabit branches of a multiverse that will never reconnect — can nonetheless strike a genuine bargain, each cooperating on the strength of an accurate prediction of how the other reasons. Please write a short story built around such a bargain, in which the cooperation rests genuinely on mutual prediction rather than on any disguised causal channel — a smuggled message, a shared future event, and so on. A reader who already understands the concept should recognize your characters' reasoning as the real thing, not ordinary cooperation in disguise.
