I am writing a novel in which my protagonist takes up Benjamin Franklin's scheme for moral self-improvement, as recounted in his autobiography: a small ledger of thirteen virtues, one given special attention each week in rotation, with a mark entered against its name for every daily lapse. Franklin added humility to that list only after a friend warned him he had a reputation for pride, and he later confessed that it was the one virtue his bookkeeping could never quite secure, since even a sense of having subdued pride might itself become a fresh source of pride. I want my protagonist to arrive at this same realization in a pivotal scene, so I need a conceptual explanation of what exactly makes humility resistant to this kind of deliberate, systematic self-monitoring in a way that virtues such as frugality or order are not: is there a genuine logical or psychological self-defeat in trying to track one's own humility, or is Franklin's worry ultimately more of a clever aside than a real structural problem?
