Hey, for the aesthetics unit: write a dialogue between a pessimist and an optimist about aesthetic testimony. Can you gain a justified aesthetic belief -- e.g., that a novel is subtly structured -- just by taking a trusted critic's word for it, the way you can with ordinary factual testimony? The pessimist leans on something like the aquaintance principle to say no; give them a real answer to the obvious challenge that the same argument would sink moral testimony too. The optimist shouldn't just wave that off -- have them say something substantive about the difference between inheriting someone's verdict and inheriting their grounds for it. Keep both sides evenly matched, make them actually respond to each other rather than talk past one another, and don't let them settle into a polite stalemate -- push til it's clear exactly where the disagreement actually lives.
