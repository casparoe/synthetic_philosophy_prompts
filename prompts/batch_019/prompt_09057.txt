Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument is normally pitched as a problem for mental causation specifically, but there's a nasty follow-up: the same argument form seems to apply to any higher-level property that supervenes on a lower-level base, not just mental ones. Why should a rock's shape, a gene, or a hurricane get to cause anything, once the microphysics is already doing all the causal work? Kim's own fix is to say most higher-level properties can be functionally reduced (so they're not extra causes competing with the physical stuff, just identical to/realized by it), and that its only qualia which resist this move and end up epiphenomenal.

Write an essay on whether Kim's containment strategy actually works: is singling out qualia as the sole casualty principled, or does this "generalization problem" show that the exclusion argument either proves way too much (all higher-level/special-science causation becomes illusory) or has to be abandoned outright (e.g., by dropping the no-overdetermination premise)? Weigh the strongest moves on both sides, but land on an actual verdict rather than a wishy-washy "both sides have a point."
