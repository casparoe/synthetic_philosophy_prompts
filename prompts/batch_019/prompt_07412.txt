Two volunteers at the shelter where I work spent all of Saturday comforting a distressed new arrival. Afterward, one told me she stayed because that is simply how she believes one ought to respond to someone in distress, whatever her personal feelings toward the woman happened to be; the other said she stayed only because the woman's distress moved her, and offered no further reason. My supervisor later praised the first volunteer's conduct as showing real moral seriousness, while describing the second's as "just being kind." That distinction unsettles me, though I can't quite say why. Is there a good reason to think one volunteer's help was more admirable than the other's, or is my supervisor's distinction confused?
