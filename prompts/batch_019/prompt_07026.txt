QUESTION: Explain Thomson's lamp thought experiment and what Thomson takes it to establish. Then explain how Benacerraf replies to Thomson, and say whether you think the reply succeeds.

STUDENT ANSWER:
Thomson's lamp is a thought experiment about a lamp with a toggle switch, starting off at t=0. After 1/2 a minute it's switched on, after another 1/4 minute (so at the 3/4 mark) it's switched off, after another 1/8 minute (at 7/8) switched on again, and so on forever, each switch happening in half the remaining time before the 1-minute mark. Since 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... converges to 1, this means infinitely many switchings get completed inside a single minute -- what Thomson calls a "super-task." The question he poses is: once the minute is up, is the lamp on or off?

Thomson argues it can't be either. It can't be on, because every time it was switched on it was later switched off; it can't be off, because every time it was switched off it was later switched on again. So at t=1 the lamp would have to be both on and off, which is a flat contradiction. He uses this to argue that super-tasks -- completing an infinite series of distinct actions in a finite stretch of time -- are conceptually impossible, since the very idea generates a logical contradiction.

Benacerraf pushes back on this. He points out that if you actually work out the state of the lamp at t=1, the "on" switches and the "off" switches in the infinite sequence are equal in number, so they cancel out and the lamp ends up back in its original state, off. That means there's no contradiction after all -- the lamp does have a determinate state at t=1, Thomson just didn't calculate it correctly.

I think Benacerraf's reply works, because it shows the scenario is perfectly coherent and actually answers Thomson's question, so Thomson's claim that super-tasks are impossible doesn't hold up.
