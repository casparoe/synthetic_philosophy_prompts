A friend and I spent much of last night arguing about a bet he wanted to make. He works in finance and is convinced that a stock we have both followed for months will underperform the market this year, and he offered to bet me even money on it. My instinct was to decline, since his willingness to offer the bet felt like evidence in its own right—but then it struck me that if he expects me to reason that way, he should start doubting his own edge the moment I hesitate, which ought to make him want to withdraw the offer too. In practice, neither of us budged: he kept offering, I kept declining, and we never converged on a shared view. He insists this is a philosopher's trick with no bearing on real trading, since people obviously buy and sell against each other's judgment on public markets all the time. Was I right to hesitate, and if my reasoning holds up, what does it actually tell us about why real-world markets see so much trading at all?
