For my philosophy of language seminar, our professor has invited a guest speaker who defends semantic externalism in the Putnam-Burge tradition, and each of us has to submit questions in advance for the Q&A following her talk. She tends to meet the self-knowledge worry with a standard compatibilist line — that privileged access is a fact about how we know our own thoughts, not about what makes them the thoughts they are, so there is no real tension with anti-individualism. Could you draft seven or eight questions that go beyond having her restate Twin Earth or Burge's arthritis case? I'd like at least one on whether natural-kind externalism and Burge's deference-based social externalism really individuate content the same way or only appear to; one on what the slow-switching cases are supposed to show about whether a switched subject can tell, from the inside, that her thought contents have changed; and one that presses her stock reply to the McKinsey-style worry by raising the 'transmission failure' response as a further move she may need. For each, add a brief follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, and end with a question about what result or argument would actually make her abandon the compatibilist line.
