Been down a rabbit hole for a piece on psychiatric advance directives -- turns out in the bioethics literature these are sometimes literally called "Ulysses contracts," after Odysseus getting his crew to tie him to the mast so his future, siren-addled self couldn't act on what he wanted in the moment. Once I noticed that pattern I started seeing it everywhere: casino self-exclusion lists, constitutions with clauses that can't be ammended even by a huge majority (Germany's "eternity clause" protecting human dignity is the textbook example), advance directives where someone with early-stage dementia specifies they should be refused care later even if their future, no-longer-lucid self seems content and wants otherwise, and of course Odysseus and the mast itself. They're all cases of an earlier "trustworthy" self overriding a later "compromised" one, but my gut says they sit on a spectrum from genuinely respecting someone's own autonomy to just paternalism with better branding.
