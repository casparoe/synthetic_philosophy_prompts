As a science journalist who has covered the replication crisis for several years, I keep encountering the claim that the crisis is, at bottom, a frequentist pathology: had psychology and biomedicine relied on Bayes factors rather than p-values from the outset, so the argument goes, most of the findings that later failed to replicate would never have cleared the bar for publication. Please argue against this claim, addressing in particular why the "garden of forking paths" — the researcher degrees of freedom involved in selecting a model, exclusions, and priors — threatens the evidential value of a Bayes factor in much the same way it threatens a p-value.
