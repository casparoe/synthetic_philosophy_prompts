I got roped into joining my newsroom's editorial standards committee last month, right after two incidents that happened almost back to back and that everyone's now arguing about without realizing it's actually the same underlying question. First, we ran a whole piece on a state senator's fundraising hedged six ways from Sunday ("sources allege," "according to documents obtained by our reporter"), and a rival outlet scooped our follow-up by just stating flatly that he'd broken campaign finance law — turns out they were right, and we looked gutless. Then two weeks later a reporter on the schools beat wrote flatly that a superintendent had "ordered records destroyed," based on one very confident source, and it turned out the source had the timeline wrong, so we had to run a mortifying correction. Now the committee has to write an actual house rule for when a reporter can state something as flat fact ("X did Y") instead of hedging it, and we've split into three camps: one editor wants the bar to be something like "you could defend this under oath," basically requiring the reporter to really know it; another says that's an impossible standard on deadline and all that should matter in hindsight is whether the claim turns out true, since punishing a reporter for a claim that held up seems crazy; a third camp (mine, tentatively) wants it to turn on whether the reporter was reasonable and responsible given what they had at the time, accepting we'll occasionally be wrong anyway. My housemate, who has a philosophy MA, told me this is an actual live debate in epistemology about what "norm" governs assertion — knowledge, truth, or justified belief — and that all three camps have genuine arguements and genuine embarrassing counterexamples on their side, which did not help my stress levels.

Could you draft the memo I'm bringing to Tuesday's meeting? I need an actual recommendation for the house standard, not just a survey of the options — ideally with example language for at least two hedge tiers below full flat assertion, so reporters have something concrete to reach for — plus some kind of trial period with a built-in review point rather than treating this as settled forever. Please also take the strongest argument for whichever camp(s) you're not recommending seriously, since I'll get pushback in the room and "the philosophers who disagree with me are wrong" isn't going to land with this committee.
