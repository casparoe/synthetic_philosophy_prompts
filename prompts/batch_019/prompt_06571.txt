So I'm teaching a graduate seminar this term on Hume's political and economic essays, and last week we spent two long sessions on "Of the Balance of Trade," "Of the Jealousy of Trade," and "Of Public Credit" from the Political Discourses. My students got pretty into the specie-flow argument - the idea that if gold and silver flow out of a country because of a trade deficit, domestic prices fall, which makes your goods cheaper abroad and imports pricier at home, so the money just flows back in on its own and all the mercantilist panic about "the balance of trade" is basically confused. One student asked a question that's been nagging at me since: that whole mechanism assumes something like a shared metallic currency actually moving between countries, and we obviously don't have that anymore. Exchange rates float, nobody settles trade imbalances in gold coin, and most of the "money" involved these days isnt even physical.

Given that, can you work out, as carefully as you can from Hume's actual stated premises rather than just the vibe of "Hume liked free trade," what he'd probably say about something like the persistent US trade deficit with China and the recurring political calls to slap tariffs on it to fix the "imbalance"? I guess what I'm really asking is: would he think the self-correcting logic still basically holds, just running through exchange rates and price levels instead of specie shipments, or is the mechanism specific enough to metallic money that it doesn't transfer, so that any Humean case against tariffs would have to lean on different arguments entirely (like the mutual-gains-from-prosperity stuff in the jealousy of trade essay, rather than the automatic-correction stuff in the balance of trade one)? And if you've got space for it, I'd love you to fold in his fairly alarmed views on public debt from "Of Public Credit" too, since a lot of the current anxiety about the trade deficit is bundled up with the fact that a good chunk of it gets financed by foreign holdings of US government debt - including, awkwardly, the same trading partners running the surplus. That's a wrinkle Hume obviously never dealt with directly, but it feels like exactly the kind of case his premises should still have something to say about. Mostly I just want something with enough teeth in it that I can throw back at my students on Thursday.
