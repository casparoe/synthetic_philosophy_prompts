so i've been going down a rabbit hole about the whole "scalable oversight" problem -- basically, how do you keep any real check on an AI once its smarter than the humans supervising it. one proposal that keeps coming up is "AI safety via debate": you get two copies of the AI to argue opposite sides of a question infront of a human judge, and since its way easier to spot a flaw once someone points it out to you than to find it from scratch, the honest debater should usually win, so the judge ends up believing true stuff even about questions thats way over their head.

the bit i can't get past is what people call the "obfuscated argument" problem. what if the dishonest AI's case rests on some huge messy computation where the mistake is smeared out over a ton of tiny steps, so theres no single short spot the honest AI can point to and go "there, thats the lie"? the honest AI might genuinely know somethings off without being able to make that cheap for the judge to check. and if that can happen, it seems like debate hasn't actually bought you cheap oversight of superhuman AI, its just moved the hard problem somewhere else.
