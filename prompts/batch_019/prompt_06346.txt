Working on a piece about wild animal suffering and something in the r/K selection stuff doesn't add up for me. Most wild animals (insects, frogs, fish, etc) are r-selected, meaning they crank out huge numbers of offspring and the vast majority die within days or weeks of being born. Since these animals barely live long enough to suffer much, doesnt that mean the total amount of suffering in the wild is actually pretty small? Kind of unclear to me why effective altruism people treat this as such a massive moral priority if the lives involved are that short.
