So this is a bit of a strange one -- half real, half something I'm trying to work into the novel I'm drafting at the moment, so bear with me. My great-uncle is stupid rich (sold some patent back in the 90s) and has this decades-long reputation in the family for being freakishly good at telling when someone's bluffing -- nobody in three generations has ever managed to fool him, it's basically become family lore at this point. At my cousins engagement party last month he cornered me with an offer: if, by midnight tonight, he becomes personally convinced that I have truly and sincerely made up my mind to spend this coming New Years Eve completely alone, phone off, in his unheated boathouse, he'll wire $40,000 into my account first thing tomorrow. He was very clear that the money's for the deciding, not the doing -- once he's convinced and the transfer's gone through, whether I actually show up at the boathouse in December is entirely my business and won't change anything after the fact.

Here's the bit that's been chewing on me since. Say he does wire the money tomorrow because he's convinced tonight. By the time New Years Eve actually rolls around, the $40k will already be sitting in my account, spent or not, and freezing alone in a boathouse for a night gets me literally nothing at that point -- there's no reason left for me to actually go through with it. And since I can already see that, right now, tonight, it's hard to figure out how I could honestly talk myself into deciding to do something I already know I'll have zero reason to do once it actually matters. But it also feels wrong to say I can't sincerely decide anything about the future unless the reasons for doing it survive all the way to the moment of action -- because that would describe basically none of the promises people actually keep. I'm not really asking whether I should take the deal (I'd take it in a heartbeat). I'm stuck on the puzzle underneath it: is there something actually incoherent about resolving, tonight, to do a thing I can already tell I won't want to do later, or is that just a totally ordinary kind of decision that people make all the time and I'm overthinking it? Curious what you make of it -- feel free to get properly into the weeds, since whatever's most interesting is probably going to end up in the book anyway.
