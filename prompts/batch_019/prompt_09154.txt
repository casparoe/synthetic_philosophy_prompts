I'm taking a graduate seminar on the epistemology of testimony this term, and this week our professor has asked us to hand in a dialogue instead of the usual response paper. Please write a dialogue between a Humean reductionist and a Reidian anti-reductionist debating whether testimony is a basic source of epistemic warrant or must instead be underwritten by independent, non-testimonial grounds. At some point, have the anti-reductionist press the reductionist with a case due to C.A.J. Coady: a young child who acquires an enormous stock of justified beliefs -- indeed language itself -- through testimony long before she could possibly have assembled the kind of inductive track record reductionism seems to demand. Rather than conceding, the reductionist should retreat to something like Elizabeth Fricker's "local" reductionism, monitoring each speaker case by case instead of vouching for testimony wholesale; the anti-reductionist should then press on whether this retreat still deserves the name "reductionism" or has quietly become a version of the opposing view. Keep both speakers equally sharp, have them engage each other's specific points rather than talk past one another, and let the dialogue end at a genuine impasse rather than a tidy resolution.
