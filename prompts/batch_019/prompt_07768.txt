After our philosophy of language reading group last night, a friend and I spent much of the evening arguing about Kripke's reading of Wittgenstein on rule-following, and neither of us walked away satisfied. The sticking point was the so-called communitarian solution: the idea that what makes it the case that I mean addition by "+", rather than some deviant "quus" function of the sort Kripke uses to dramatize the problem, is not any fact about my own past dispositions, but my agreement with the wider community's practice of using the sign. My friend's objection was that this move is basically a shell game: shifting the explanatory burden from an individual's finite, error-prone dispositions onto a community's finite, error-prone dispositions doesn't remove Kripke's original worries about finitude and normativity, it just relocates them one level up, so that "checking one's usage against the community" can't be doing the philosophical work of grounding correctness that it is supposed to do. I tried to push back in the moment but kept ending up restating the same intuition in different words rather than actually meeting the challenge.

I would like you to make the strongest case you can that this "it just relocates the problem" objection is mistaken: that grounding rule-following in agreement with a community's practice is a substantive philosophical move, not merely a restatement of the individual-level puzzle at a larger scale. Please engage directly with the finitude and normativity worries — communal dispositions are, after all, just as finite as an individual's, and the whole community could in principle be collectively mistaken — rather than sidestepping them, and try to say something about whether there is a difference in kind, and not merely in scale, between checking one's usage against one's own past reactions and checking it against a community's reactions. It would help if you addressed how the community view is supposed to bear on the distinction between a rule merely seeming right to someone and a rule's actually being followed correctly, since that distinction is presumably where any real solution has to earn its keep.
