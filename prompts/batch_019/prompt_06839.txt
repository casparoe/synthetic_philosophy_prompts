I host a philosophy podcast built around one contested argument per episode, and next week's episode covers Kripke's rule-following paradox. My guest has built her career defending the communitarian "skeptical solution": the claim that no fact about my past behavior or dispositions could ever have settled whether, by the symbol "+", I meant addition rather than some deviant "quus" function, and that meaning-talk is nonetheless vindicated by an individual's agreement with her community's practice rather than by any truth-conditional account. I want the conversation to get well past a restatement of the view from her book, so please draft eight to ten questions. Include one that presses her on why the finiteness and fallibility of anyone's dispositions already undermine the dispositionalist's answer before the skeptical solution is even needed. Include one that asks her to defend calling the move from "there is no fact about what I mean" to "meaning-talk remains in good order" a genuine solution, rather than, as Boghossian has argued, an unstable halfway house that collapses into either eliminativism or a covert reinstatement of the very facts the paradox was meant to rule out. Include one on the solitary rule-follower: could a Crusoe with no community ever mean anything at all by his signs, and doesn't grounding correctness in communal agreement just relocate the paradox one level up, given that a whole community's actual responses are exactly as finite as any individual's? And include one that raises Baker and Hacker's charge that this entire package -- the position now nicknamed "Kripkenstein" -- isn't Wittgenstein's own view at all, asking whether its philosophical interest can survive that charge. For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
