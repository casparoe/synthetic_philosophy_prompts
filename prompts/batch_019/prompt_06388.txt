I've been reading pop-science accounts of split-brain patients -- people whose corpus callosum, the roughly two-hundred-million-fiber cable connecting the brain's two hemispheres, was severed as a last-resort epilepsy treatment -- and one detail from the old lab tests has stuck with me. Researchers could show an image to only one side of a patient's visual field so that just a single hemisphere registered it; because speech mostly runs through the left hemisphere, the patient's mouth sometimes had no idea what their left hand, controlled by the right hemisphere, had just pointed at, and would calmly invent a confident, wrong reason for it, apparently unaware it was making the reason up. That's not the 'evil twin' premise people usually reach for with this material: it's the same person, still walking and talking and holding a conversation, explaining an action in words while the part of the brain doing the explaining had no access to the real reason for it. I'd like a short story, a few pages long, built around a moment like that -- it needn't be set in a hospital or a lab, and I'd actually find it more interesting if the disconnection surfaced somewhere ordinary, but the plot should turn on the real mechanics of what each hemisphere can perceive, name, or explain, and what it can't, rather than a vague 'two souls in one skull' metaphor. I'm less interested in a tidy resolution than in a story that makes me feel why it's genuinely unclear whether someone like that is one mind having a strange day, or something closer to two minds sharing a body.
