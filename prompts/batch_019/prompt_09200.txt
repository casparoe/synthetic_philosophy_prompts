I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and next month I'm interviewing a philosopher of psychiatry whose recent work extends Ian Hacking's account of looping effects to autism. She argues that the DSM-5's 2013 folding of Asperger's, PDD-NOS, and autistic disorder into a single 'autism spectrum disorder' is a textbook case of what Hacking called an interactive kind reshaping itself through the very people it sorts, as against an indifferent kind that couldn't care less how it's classified. Draft seven or eight interview questions that push past a restatement of the framework. Include at least one question that pins down what a looping effect adds to the self-fulfilling-label idea that sociologists like Merton and Becker described decades earlier; one asking whether the neurodiversity movement's own feedback into diagnostic criteria commits her to denying that autism has any stable reality apart from that feedback; and one asking what observation would convince her that a given psychiatric category is not interactive, so the claim has some empirical bite. For each, add a short follow-up keyed to the deflection she's most likely to reach for, and close with a question on whether coverage of 'rising autism rates' makes journalism itself part of the loop she studies.
