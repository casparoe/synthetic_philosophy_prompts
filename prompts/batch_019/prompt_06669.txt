And here's the answer I have to grade:

"Realists think the success of our best theories would be a miracle unless those theories are at least approximately true, but antirealists like Laudan point out that plenty of once-successful theories (phlogiston, caloric, the elastic ether) turned out to be totally false in their central claims, so approximate truth can't be what's tracking predictive success. Worrall's structural realism is supposed to split the difference: what survives theory change isn't the ontology but the mathematical structure. His example is Fresnel's equations for the reflection and refraction of light, derived on the assumption that light is a vibration in a mechanical ether. When Maxwell replaced the ether with the electromagnetic field the ontology was overturned completely, but Fresnel's equations carried over into the new theory almost unchanged, just reinterpreted as relations between field amplitudes instead of mechanical displacements. So Worrall says we should be committed to the equations and agnostic about the underlying nature - on his view there aren't really hidden objects with a nature we're ignorant of, there's just the web of relations itself, and that's exactly what lets structural realism dodge the pessimistic meta-induction, since it was never committed to ether/field/whatever in the first place.

The Newman objection is the standard reply to this: Newman's 1928 critique of Worrall's paper points out that for any set of objects with the right cardinality, you can always define relations on them that realize whatever abstract structure you like, so claiming that the world "has" a certain structure is close to empty - it reduces to a claim about how many objects there are, not a substantive claim about the world. Ladyman's response is to push toward ontic structural realism, where structure is tied to modal and nomic features like symmetries and conservation laws picked out by the theory, rather than to bare set-theoretic relations, which is supposed to block the trivialization since not just any old relation counts, only the ones with the right modal role. I think this mostly works, though I can see why some people think you'd still need some notion of a "thing" to individuate the relations in the first place, so the worry might just resurface one level up."
