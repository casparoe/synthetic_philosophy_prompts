For a philosophy of mind reading group, we've been working through the Lucas–Penrose argument that Gödel's incompleteness theorems rule out human mathematical understanding being just the operation of some fixed, consistent formal system (equivalently, some algorithm). The introductory version of the argument is straightforward enough: for any consistent formal system F that is strong enough to encode arithmetic, there is a Gödel sentence G_F that F itself cannot prove, and yet a mathematician who understands how F and G_F are constructed can, the argument goes, simply see that G_F is true; since no such F can do this for itself, the mathematician's mind cannot be equivalent to that F.

What I haven't been able to get a clean handle on is the standard objection to this, which turns on the fact that the mathematician's "insight" really only licenses the conditional claim "if F is consistent, then G_F is true" -- and, as I understand it, this conditional is itself a theorem of F (it is essentially the content that gets used inside the proof of Gödel's second incompleteness theorem). So the interesting, mind-distinguishing step has to be the unconditional claim that F is consistent, which is exactly what the second incompleteness theorem says F cannot establish about itself, leaving it unclear how the mathematician is supposed to know this about their own reasoning without either begging the question or quietly relying on a form of "insight" whose reliability is precisely what's in dispute. I know that Penrose tries to head off some version of this worry in Shadows of the Mind by reformulating the argument around a formal system that would have to be "knowably sound" rather than merely consistent, but I've never seen a clear walkthrough of how that reformulation is supposed to block the circularity rather than just push it back a step. Could you explain, in some technical detail, how Penrose's knowably-sound version of the argument is meant to work, and then give your own assessment of whether it genuinely escapes the consistency-circularity objection or merely relocates it?
