So my uncle died last week and I've somehow ended up giving the eulogy this Saturday, which is awkward, because for almost twenty years his actual profession was forging Old Master drawings, good enough that a couple of auction houses fell for them, until one didn't and he did a bit of time for it. Half the family treats him like a lovable rogue, the other half can't, especially my cousin Dana, who'll be sitting in the second row and who spent real money on a 'newly discovered' drawing a few years back that turned out to be his and nobody else's. He use to love telling us about the Dutch forger who beat a Nazi collaboration charge after the war by proving in open court that the 'lost Vermeer' he'd sold was actually his own fake, like that settled something about art instead of just about his sentence. I write novels for a living so people assume I can just make this sound nice, but I don't want to smooth him into either a con man or a misunderstood genius, because neither is quite true and Dana would know it. Can you write the eulogy? It has to be something Dana can sit through without feeling insulted, it can't pretend his fakes were worth what the real things are worth, and it still needs to leave people with something true and warm about a man who spent his whole life getting other people's genius exactly right.
