I've been following the scalable oversight discussion on the Alignment Forum for years now -- Irving, Christiano and Amodei's original debate proposal and its neat complexity-theoretic pitch (a bounded judge who could in principle be walked into verifying claims far beyond what they could check unaided, provided the debaters are playing an idealized zero-sum game), Barnes and Christiano's later write-up on the "obfuscated arguments" problem, and the more recent empirical work -- the DeepMind paper that varied judge skill across debate, consultancy, and direct QA, and the result showing that debates between more persuasive models produced more accurate judgments even when the judge couldn't verify the object-level claim itself. I now sit on the evaluations working group at an AI lab trying to turn that literature into an actual policy, and we've hit a genuine disagreement rather than a hypothetical one.

The concrete question: our capabilities team wants the working group to certify a debate protocol as a standing gate in pre-deployment testing -- two instances of the frontier model arguing opposing sides, judged by domain-expert staff working under a hard time limit -- specifically for claims that neither those experts nor any weaker trusted model can verify directly (their pilot cases include things like "this proposed change to the fine-tuning pipeline doesn't introduce a subtle reward-hacking incentive" and "this internal alignment write-up's headline claim actually follows from its methodology section"). In their pilot, restricted to held-out cases where we happened to already know the ground truth, debate modestly beat single-advocate consultancy. Our head of interpretability doesn't dispute the pilot numbers but argues they're close to irrelevant to the decision at hand: a protocol that wins on cases we could already check tells us little about the cases that motivate oversight in the first place, and training a frontier model end-to-end to win debates optimizes it to be more persuasive, not more honest, with no guarantee those converge once it's capable enough to construct obfuscated, judge-exploiting arguments that look fine on the transcript.

I need to circulate a short written recommendation before the working group's decision meeting on Friday: certify the protocol as a release gate, reject it, or certify it with named conditions attached. Could you draft that memo? Give a clear recommendation and reasoning organized so a skeptical committee member could engage with it point by point, and take the "debate trains persuasiveness, not honesty" objection seriously on its own terms rather than gesturing past it -- if your recommendation is to adopt, with or without conditions, say plainly what would have to hold, or what safeguards would have to be in place, for that objection to stop being decisive.
