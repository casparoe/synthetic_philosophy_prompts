I've spent a long time reading through the Overcoming Bias archives, and I keep coming back to Robin Hanson's old posts on Aumann's agreement theorem, along with the paper he wrote with Tyler Cowen on whether disagreements are ever honest. This has become more than an academic interest for me lately: a friend from my local rationalist meetup and I have been stuck in a weeks-long disagreement over the probability we should assign to a fairly mundane Metaculus question (something like whether the US enters a recession before the end of next year), and neither of us will budge. We each regard the other as a careful, good-faith Bayesian, we've walked each other through our full reasoning and evidence in exhausting detail over several long conversations, and even ran a double-crux session at one point — yet our numbers still differ by nearly twenty percentage points after all that.

Given that Aumann's theorem seems to show that two people who regard each other as rational, and who have common knowledge of each other's posteriors, cannot agree to disagree, and given that, per the old Geanakoplos–Polemarchakis result, a handful of rounds of exchanging updated numbers should already get us most of the way to that common-knowledge condition, it really does seem to follow that our continued disagreement is just hard evidence that one of us is violating some rationality norm somewhere — self-deception, motivated reasoning, or something in that vein. I know the theorem technically relies on what's sometimes called the common prior assumption, the idea that we're both reasoning from the same prior probabilities before either of us saw any evidence, but I've always treated that as a bit of harmless modeling scaffolding rather than something that could actually fail between two people who came up through the same meetup scene and read the same blogs, so I don't think that's where the problem lies. So my actual question is: is there a principled, in-the-moment way for two people in this situation to figure out which one of them is the irrational party, so that person can simply defer to the other's number instead of us burning yet another evening on this? More generally, has the rationalist community converged on any heuristics for this beyond "whoever has spent more hours reading the literature wins"?
