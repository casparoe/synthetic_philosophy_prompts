This term's seminar on Bertrand Russell's ethics has me working closely through Marriage and Morals, and my cousin, who is getting married next month, has asked me to give the toast as "the one who reads Russell." Half the guests will be my philosophy friends and the other half will never have heard of him, and I'd like to draw on what he actually argues there -- that jealousy is more a matter of custom than of nature, his qualified sympathy for companionate marriage, and his distinction between the passionate part of a marriage and the deeper, life-entwining part -- rather than reach for a decorative quotation. The complication is that Russell also thought strict fidelity wasn't essential to a good marriage, that this book helped cost him a professorship at the City College of New York in 1940, and that his own four marriages hardly modeled the stability he described, so the toast shouldn't come across as a wink toward infidelity or as a lecture at someone else's wedding. Could you write a three-minute toast that draws honestly on Russell's actual view of what makes a marriage worth having, works for both audiences, and stays on the right side of that line?
