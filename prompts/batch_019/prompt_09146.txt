Two people in my rationalist-adjacent reading group got into it after our last meetup (we'd just done a chapter on prediction markets) about whether futarchy -- Hanson's "vote on values, bet on beliefs" scheme -- actually survives the manipulation objection, and I keep replaying the exchange because I can't tell who won.

(1) Owen opened with the standard worry: someone with money to burn and a stake in the outcome could just dump a huge bet against a policy to tank its price and get it killed, no need to be right about anything, just rich enough to move the market.

(2) Naomi said that gets the finance backwards -- a manipulator pushing the price away from the informed consensus is basically leaving free money on the table, so manipulation attempts tend to get arbed away by traders correcting the price back, and can even leave the market MORE accurate than if the manipulator had never shown up, since they're subsidizing everyone else's research.

(3) Owen said sure, if there's alot of informed trading volume waiting to pounce, but for the low-stakes, nobody-cares, municipal-variance-type decisions futarchy is supposed to handle day to day, there might be like twelve people who even know the market exists, and none of them are doing real research to correct a $500 manipulative bet.

(4) Naomi granted the thin-market point but said that's an implementation detail, not a flaw in futarchy itself -- you subsidize liquidity, e.g. with an automated market maker willing to eat some loses, same as real prediction platforms already do, and now there's enough action for correction to kick in.

(5) Owen said once somebody has to decide how much to subsidize a market (and who eats the losses when it doesn't pay off), you've smuggled a human institutional judgment call right back into the middle of the process -- which is exactly the kind of thing futarchy was supposed to get us away from, so the manipulation objection doesn't actually go away, it just relocates one level up.

Neither of them budged. Could you go through this move by move and say who's got the better of it at each step -- not just an overall winner -- and point out anything substantive you think both of them are missing?
