I'm taking a seminar on twentieth-century political thought, where we recently read Simone Weil's short wartime essay, usually translated as 'On the Abolition of All Political Parties,' which argues that a party's real purpose always collapses into securing its own unlimited growth at the cost of its members' independent judgment, and that the only honest remedy is to abolish parties outright rather than reform them.

I also sit on my university's Student Senate Rules Committee, which meets Thursday to decide what to do about 'slates': coordinated tickets whose candidates campaign and vote as a bloc. Slate-backed candidates have won every contested race for three years; unaffiliated senators say amendments go nowhere unless a slate adopts them first, while slate organizers counter that slates are how new senators learn to build coalitions and give voters some cue as to how a candidate will actually govern.

Could you draft the short memo I'll circulate before the meeting? It should recommend one concrete option -- ban slates, keep the current rules, or allow them under specified disclosure conditions -- defend that choice using Weil's actual reasoning rather than generic pro- or anti-party talking points, and take the strongest argument against its own recommendation seriously.
