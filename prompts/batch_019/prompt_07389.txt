I'm TAing a graduate seminar on decision theory this term, and I'd like to try out a way of teaching Gibbard and Harper's Death in Damascus case that goes beyond the usual one-box/two-box rehearsal. Please write a dialogue between a causal decision theorist and an evidential decision theorist working through the case together, where Death is a highly reliable predictor and the choice between staying in Damascus and fleeing to Aleppo is fully symmetric. Have them argue specifically about ratifiability: whether either act comes out optimal once the agent supposes they will actually perform it, whether evidential decision theory really escapes this instability or just reproduces it under another description, and whether randomizing between the two acts is a genuine fix or simply changes the question being asked. Each speaker should engage with the specific point the other has just made rather than restate a prepared position, and the dialogue shouldn't manufacture a resolution that the arguments don't actually support.
