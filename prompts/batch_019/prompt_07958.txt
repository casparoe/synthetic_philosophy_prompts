Earman and Norton's hole argument confronts manifold substantivalism with an uncomfortable result: given a spacetime in general relativity, a diffeomorphism that acts as the identity outside a matter-free region (the "hole") but non-trivially inside it yields a distinct model, agreeing with the original everywhere outside the hole and disagreeing somewhere inside it. If spacetime points have identities independent of the metric field, these two models seem to represent genuinely distinct possibilities compatible with exactly the same initial data -- an indeterminism generated by the metaphysics of substantivalism, not by physics. Sophisticated substantivalists block this by denying that diffeomorphic models represent distinct possibilities at all, individuating spacetime points by the qualitative role they occupy in the metric field rather than by any further, primitive identity.

Write a dialogue, set as two philosophers of physics work through the argument together at a blackboard, between a sophisticated substantivalist and a relationist. The relationist should press the charge that this maneuver, taken seriously, leaves substantivalism asserting nothing that relationism does not already assert; the substantivalist should resist this, and press back, in turn, on how a relational ontology is supposed to represent matter-free but geometrically nontrivial spacetimes -- such as one containing nothing but a passing gravitational wave -- given that there is no matter there for spatiotemporal relations to hold among. Each should engage the other's specific objections as they come, and neither should be left with an easy, unanswered last word.
