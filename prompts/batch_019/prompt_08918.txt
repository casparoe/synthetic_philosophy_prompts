I spent about thirty-five years as a health physicist doing radiation dose reconstruction work, and now that I'm retired I've fallen into a bit of a philosophy habit — podcasts on my walks, a couple of intro books, that sort of thing. Last week I finally got around to Yudkowsky's old "torture vs dust specks" thought experiment: one person tortured horribly for fifty years versus some absurd, unnameable number of people (3^^^3, he writes it, though God knows what that number actually is) who each get a single dust speck in their eye, and which is actually worse if you just add up the harm. It stopped me cold, because it's the exact arguement my friend and colleague Warren and I had at every single conference for twenty-some years, except our version was called "collective dose."

For anyone who hasn't run into that term: regulators like to take some radiation dose so tiny that no individual could ever be shown to be harmed by it, multiply it by a huge population and a standard risk coefficient, and out comes a number of "statistical" cancer deaths that can never be pinned to any one actual person. Warren thought this move was close to a con — you dont get to shut up and multiply your way from a trillion trivial doses into a body count. I always argued that if you accept the linear no-threshold model at all, which is what the low-dose evidence mostly supports, you don't get to cherry-pick when the arithmetic applies to you. Twenty-some years and we never once settled it, and I don't think Yudkowsky settles it either, which I gather is rather the point. (Someone in my book club mentioned there's an older, more academic version of this too, in Parfit, with multiple torturers and an imperceptible dial — I haven't tracked that one down yet, so don't assume I've got it right.)

Warren's retiring in three weeks and I'm giving the toast at his dinner. Roughly half the room will be old radiation-protection and health-physics people who've had this exact fight themselves and will catch the dust-specks parallel immediately; the other half is his kids, grandkids, and neighbors, who've never heard of collective dose or Yudkowsky and will glaze over if I explain either one properly. Could you write me the toast — four or five minutes spoken — using the dust-specks/collective-dose parallel as the actual spine of it and not just a name-drop, with at least one line that gets a real laugh or groan out of the specialists? Two things I'd ask you to be careful about: don't flatten Warren's actual position into "small risks don't matter" — his real point was that below some threshold you can't meaningfully call it a risk at all, which is a subtler claim than it sounds like; and please leave our old disagreement unresolved in the speech, the same way it's unresolved in real life, rather than writing it like one of us finally won.
