I serve on the scientific advisory panel of a national research agency that funds several centuries-scale "civilizational continuity" programs, including a global seed vault and a deep-space settlement feasibility study. A commissioned report recommends defunding the longest-horizon programs, invoking the Doomsday Argument in its Carter-Leslie form: our position in the sequence of all humans who will ever be born should be treated as a random draw, so we should update sharply against very long human futures. It proposes an extra tenfold discount on any benefit accruing more than two centuries out. The panel is split: some think the report leans on an inference that the reference-class problem and the Self-Indication Assumption already undercut; others think those responses are no better off, given the Self-Indication Assumption's own "presumptuous philosopher" problem, and that rejecting a serious Bayesian argument merely because its conclusion is unwelcome isn't intellectually honest. Please draft the panel's short recommendation memo: state clearly whether, and with what parameters, the extra discount should be adopted, note when the panel should revisit the question, and take seriously the strongest version of whichever side you don't recommend.
