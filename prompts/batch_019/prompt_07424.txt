I host a small philosophy podcast whose listeners mostly found their way to the show through the rationalist and EA blogospheres, which means most of them can already recite the Wilt Chamberlain example from memory after seeing it rehashed in a dozen LessWrong and ACX threads about redistribution. Next week's guest is a political philosopher who has just published a book defending Nozick's entitlement theory against the standard egalitarian rebuttals, and who has a habit, whenever a host presses her on inequality, of retreating to some version of the line "you're objecting to a pattern, and I only ever promised you a history" -- which sounds decisive in the moment but mostly just relabels the question rather than answering it.

I don't want an hour of her restating the entitlement theory for an audience that has already read the book, and I don't want the standard "what drew you to libertarianism" softball either. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond restatement, with at least one question, plus a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, on each of: (1) whether the Wilt Chamberlain scenario really shows liberty and patterned distributions to be incompatible, given Cohen's reply that a pattern can be restored through periodic lump-sum redistribution without ever touching what anyone does with their holdings between redistributions; (2) how much constraint the Lockean proviso is actually imposing on original acquisition, if "no one ends up worse off than under non-appropriation" is satisfiable by almost any historical land grab once you factor in the general productivity gains of a property system; (3) the gap between granting someone full self-ownership and granting that whoever first appropriates an external, previously unowned resource thereby owns it outright, given that Cohen's rival starting premise -- that the external world begins as jointly owned -- seems at least as natural a baseline; and (4) whether the principle of rectification, once you take seriously how few real-world holdings have a clean causal history, ends up smuggling back in exactly the kind of patterned, end-state target the entitlement theory was supposed to make unnecessary. Sequence the questions so that her "pattern versus history" line gets harder to deploy each time, and close with one question she can't dispatch just by repeating that line once more.
