In my school's philosophy elective, we covered Frankfurt's argument that moral responsibility doesn't require being able to do otherwise, and it sparked an argument between two classmates that our teacher never fully resolved.

Marcus said that since Jones decides entirely on his own to shoot Smith, Black never has to intervene at all, so Jones ends up exactly as responsible as he'd be in a world without Black -- even though Jones couldn't have done otherwise, since Black was standing by the whole time. That, Marcus said, shows the principle that responsibility requires alternate possibilities is false.

Leah answered that Jones did have an alternative, just a thin one: he could have shown whatever prior sign would have triggered Black's intervention, even though the outcome down the line would have been identical either way. Since that flicker of a different path was open to him, she said, the principle survives -- it just needs a much smaller alternative than we assumed.

Marcus replied that a flicker like that can't be what grounds responsibility, since Jones has no control over it and isn't even aware it exists -- it's a mere physical precursor, not a choice, so it can't do the work the principle actually needs done.

Leah said that's fair as far as it goes, but it just shows Marcus is now quietly requiring alternatives to be "robust" before they count, and that requirement isn't part of the original principle -- so he's really redefining it to fit Frankfurt's conclusion rather than refuting it as originally stated.

Could you go through this move by move, say who has the stronger position at each stage, and point out anything important that both of them are missing?
