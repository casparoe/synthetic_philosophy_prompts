So I'm noodling on a piece about all these "AI is finally letting us talk to whales/dolphins" stories that keep doing the rounds, and it sent me down a bit of a Donald Davidson rabbit hole. His radical interpretation stuff basically says you can't work out what someone believes and what their words mean as two seperate problems - you solve for both at once, and the only real lever you get to pull is assuming the speaker is mostly rational and mostly right about the world (the "principle of charity"). The part that stuck with me is that even leaning on that lever as hard as it goes, Davidson thought you could still end up with two totally different, equally coherent, equally "charitable" readings of the exact same behavior, with nothing left over anywhere that says which one's actually true. Could you write me a short story, maybe 800-1000 words, where that specific bind is the actual engine of the plot and not just set dressing? I don't want a generic decode-the-signal story that ends in triumphant mutual understanding - I want two interpreters (of a whale, an AI, whatever setting earns it) building genuinely defensible but diverging charity-based readings of the same material, with real stakes riding on which reading gets acted on, and the story actually sitting with the fact that there's no further fact of the matter to appeal to, instead of quietly smuggling one in by the end.
