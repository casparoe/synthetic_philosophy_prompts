I'm putting together a problem set for my graduate seminar on moral responsibility, and I'd like a minimal pair of cases that tests recent "equal standing" accounts of why hypocrisy undermines the standing to blame (in the spirit of R. J. Wallace's work). On these accounts, what disqualifies a hypocritical blamer is not her plain inconsistency but a further, implicit claim—to an exemption from, or superiority with respect to, the very standard she is enforcing—that is false. Construct two cases. In the first, an agent is exactly as guilty of some offense as the person she blames for it, and her blame is intuitively hypocritical and standing-undermining. In the second, minimally different case, the agent is equally guilty and equally inconsistent in the bare sense, yet her blame intuitively retains full standing, because the implicit claim is absent. Please say precisely what feature of the second case removes it.
