I don't have any background in philosophy, but I've been turning something over in my head for the past year or so, and I can't quite argue my way out of it, so I'd like an outside take.

Until my mid-thirties I had a genuinely bad temper — it cost me two relationships and nearly a third. About five years ago, after one particularly ugly argument, I decided enough was enough, went through a long stretch of therapy, and worked hard at becoming someone calmer. People who've known me a long time now say I'm a different person, and most days I'm quietly proud of that. But ever since I picked up a couple of popular books on habits and self-control, a different question has been nagging at me: what actually made me capable of changing right at that point, after failing to at every earlier one? Whatever I answer — I was finally scared enough, or tired enough, or just ready — seems to describe something that happened to the person I already was, not something that earlier self chose in order to become someone new. And if I picture that earlier self setting out to deliberately engineer its own transformation, I hit the same wall one step further back, because it would already have needed the values and willpower to do that, and it didn't choose those either. However far back I push it, I can never quite find a point where "I" was just standing there, before any of it, freely picking what kind of person to become — and supposing that luck or chance filled in some of the gaps doesn't seem to help either, since a lucky break isn't something I did. What makes this more than an idle puzzle is that the same logic runs backward just as easily: if I don't really get to take credit for calming down, it's hard to see how I was ever fully to blame for the temper in the first place.

When I've raised this with people close to me, they mostly think I'm turning a good outcome into a headache and should just accept the credit gracefully. Maybe they're right. But I would genuinely like to know whether I've stumbled onto a real problem with the whole idea of deserving credit or blame for how we turn out, or whether it's just a knot in the language that comes undone once you look at it properly — and if it is a real problem, whether there's still some honest, everyday sense in which a person can be said to have earned their own character.
