My friend Mara is getting married in three weeks and asked me to give the toast, and I've been stuck on the draft for days now. Some background: Mara and her fiance Theo are the couple from our old ACX-meetup friend group who are the most insufferably well-read about this stuff, and Theo especially will not shut up at parties about Bertrand Russell's Marriage and Morals -- his argument that jealousy isn't some hardwired instinct we're stuck with but mostly a learned, possessive thing tangled up with old patriarchal anxieties about whose kid is whose, and that a marriage's actual center of gravity is the household and the children rather than sexual exclusivity, so two honest, decent people shouldn't automatically wreck what they have just because they're also involved with other people. Theo and Mara have pretty much built their own marriage around this idea -- they've been openly non-monogamous since grad school. About a third of the guest list is our rationalist-adjacent friends, who will 100% clock a Russell reference and be a little let down if I don't make one. The other two-thirds is Theo's extremely Catholic extended family, who do not need a seminar on open marriage delivered to them over the chicken dinner.

Heres my actual problem though, and its the reason I keep deleting drafts: I went down a bit of a Wikipedia hole this week and remembered that Russell basically ran this exact experiment on himself. He and his second wife Dora agreed, openly, to something very like this arrangement -- and by most biographical accounts, when Dora had two kids with another man, Russell was privately wrecked with jealousy anyway (this despite having been perfectly happy having affairs of his own on the side), and the whole thing ended in a genuinely bitter divorce a few years later. So the one time the guy who wrote the book got to run the experiment on his own life, it didn't hold. I don't want to pretend I don't know that -- some of the meetup crowd definitely knows it too, and will notice if I conveniently leave it out -- but I also can't get up at my friend's wedding and structure a toast around "here's a theory that failed its one real trial."

So here's what I'm asking: can you write me the toast, something you could actually read aloud in about 4-5 minutes, that uses the Russell material honestly, awkward bit included, without turning into either a philosophy lecture for half the room or an accidental bad omen -- and that still comes out the other side as a genuinely warm, funny blessing on this specific marriage rather than a hedge?
