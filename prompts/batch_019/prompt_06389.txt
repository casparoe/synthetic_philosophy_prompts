I spent about thirty-five years running a Drosophila genetics lab before I retired, and for the last couple of years I've been getting seriously into philosophy of science -- partly thanks to a reading group at the community college, and partly because my daughter, who's finishing a philosophy PhD, keeps sending me articles "to debunk." Popper has taken up an embarrassing amount of my brain lately, which turns out to be relevant, because I still sit on the science advisory committee for our local natural history museum, and we're in the final stretch of putting together a new permanent evolution hall. One of the trustees, a retired patent attorney who's also been reading Popper on his own time, is pushing hard for a didactic panel that says something like "the philosopher Karl Popper argued that natural selection is not a falsifiable scientific theory," which he's framing as basic intellectual honesty rather than as a wedge for creationism. Most of the rest of the committee wants to just kill the panel and file the whole thing under creationism-in-a-labcoat, but that feels too easy to me, because as best as I can tell Popper really did say more or less that, at least for a while -- the trustee isnt inventing anything, he's just stopping the story halfway through.

Could you draft the memo I'll circulate to the committee before next Tuesday's vote? I need it to land on an actual recommendation about the panel -- kill it, approve it as written, or approve some specific rewrite, your call -- but be concrete, and say who does what and by when. And I need it to actually engage with the Popper point instead of waving it off with a one-liner about creationists misquoting dead philosophers: I want my colleagues to come away understanding what Popper's unfalsifiability argument against natural selection actually was, what happened to that view later in his career, and whether any of that has real bearing on how a museum should talk about natural selection to a room full of twelve-year-olds. Something I could read aloud in under five minutes would be ideal.
