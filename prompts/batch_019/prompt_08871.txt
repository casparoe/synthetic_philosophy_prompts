Quick one for my social ontology seminar next week. Compare David Lewis's account of convention (from his book Convention - an arbitrary regularity kept in place by a coordination equilibrium plus common knowledge of everyone's expectations) with Brian Skyrms's evolutionary retelling of the same phenomenon, where the exact same kind of signaling convention Lewis talks about evolves via replicator dynamics or plain reinforcement learning, no common knowledge or even much rationality required. Heres what I can't settle for myself: when Skyrms gets a signaling convention to evolve that way, has he actually explained the thing Lewis cared about - a regularity sustained by mutual expectations, which is what gives convention its normative flavor - or has he swapped in something thinner, a merely stable behavioral pattern, and kept the word convention around out of habit? And is Lewis's common knowledge apparatus doing indispensible work here, or is it just baggage now that we've got the evolutionary story? Don't describe both sides evenhandedly - pick a winner and defend it.
