I am taking a seminar on the semantics of vagueness this term, and I also sit on my college's Honor Council, which is deciding a 'patchwriting' case: an essay that mirrors a source's structure and sequence of claims almost sentence by sentence — the wording changed throughout — with no citation attached to those paragraphs, though the source is cited elsewhere in the essay. Our code bars presenting 'the language or ideas of another as one's own without adequate attribution,' but says nothing about how much reworking defeats that description, and the case genuinely sits on the line. Half the Council wants to rule on its best guess at the underlying fact; the other half thinks there is no underlying fact to guess at, only a decision to make, which is essentially the disagreement between epistemicism and supervaluationism from seminar. Please draft the memo I will circulate before the vote: recommend whether the student is responsible and, if so, what sanction; propose a standing rule for future borderline patchwriting cases; and be honest about what the Council would be giving up, whichever of the two views of vagueness it ends up leaning on.
