My younger sister is a behavioral ecologist, and in a few weeks I'm giving the toast at her wedding, which is a more intimidating assignment than it sounds. Something like half the guests will be her old PhD cohort along with her fiancé's advisor and labmates, and her fiancé's own doctoral research happened to be on costly signaling -- specifically on why animals like gazelles engage in "stotting," that stiff-legged jump they do in full view of a predator instead of just running. I have no scientific background myself, but over the last few years I've done a fair amount of reading on my own just to keep up with what she does, including Zahavi's book on the handicap principle and, on a bit of a tangent, Geoffrey Miller's book applying the same logic to human courtship -- the idea that a signal can only be trusted when it is costly enough that a low-quality signaler couldn't afford to fake it.

That idea is what's actually been troubling me while I draft this, rather than just supplying material for it. Everything a wedding toast normally consists of -- "they're perfect for each other," "I've never seen him happier" -- is exactly the kind of cheap talk that, on her fiancé's own theory, nobody in that room has any principled reason to believe, and I suspect several of his old labmates will be thinking precisely that while being too polite to say it out loud. So instead of just asserting how devoted the two of them are, I'd like the toast to earn that conclusion the way the theory says it has to be earned: by pointing to something that actually cost him something. Three years ago, when it looked like the long-distance stretch of their relationship wasn't going to survive another year of fieldwork on separate continents, he turned down a considerably better postdoc so that he could take a weaker one in her city. I want that story to function as the toast's real evidence, with anything sentimental built on top of it rather than standing on its own.

If it's possible, I would also like a brief, non-lecturing nod to why this isn't quite the same thing as Fisherian runaway, where the trait doesn't have to be honest at all, just correlated with a preference -- mostly so the biologists in the room don't spend the toast quietly filing an objection -- but it still has to work as an ordinary, warm wedding toast for my grandmother, who won't know either term and shouldn't have to. Could you write me that toast? It should run about four minutes.
