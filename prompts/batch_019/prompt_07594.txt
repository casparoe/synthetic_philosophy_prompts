Ok, this is going to sound like a dumb workplace thing but bear with me. My company's in the middle of redoing how we approve big architecture RFCs (I mean the "we're switching our primary datastore" or "we're adopting a service mesh" type decisions, not just any old PR). Right now it needs unanimous sign-off from our 3 principal engineers, and everyone agrees that's too slow and too concentrated in three people's judgment. So one of our staff engineers, Priya, wrote a doc proposing we scrap that and instead let any senior-or-above engineer opt into a vote on each RFC -- simple majority, quorum of at least 11 -- her pitch basically being "more independent reviewers means better odds of catching the right call, it's the wisdom of crowds." She even cited Condorcet's jury theorem by name in the doc (I had to go look it up -- something about how if each voter is even a little better than a coinflip and they all decide independently, a big enough group nails the right answer with near certainty). The principals pushed back with two things: (a) plenty of the eligible 11+ wouldn't have any business judging a given RFC, like a frontend engineer weighing in on a storage engine redesign, so "better than a coinflip" is generous for a chunk of that pool, and (b) nobody actually votes independently in practice anyway, people skim the thread and pile onto whichever confident-sounding comment got posted first, so we wouldn't even get the independence the theorem is leaning on.

Anyway, I'm one of about 6 people on the working group leadership set up to make an actual recommendation here, and I'd rather show up to that meeting with something more rigorous than just my gut feeling. Can you write the memo I'd circulate before next Tuesday's working group meeting? I want it to land on an actual recommendation -- keep the 3-principal model, go with Priya's big-committee version, or something else entirely -- with a real rollout timeline, and I want it to seriously engage with whatever objection ends up on the loosing side instead of just hand-waving past it. Extra credit if there's some structural fix (how votes get collected, who's even eligible per RFC, whatever) that a person who actually understood the math here would land on, instead of a mushy compromise designed just to keep both camps happy.
