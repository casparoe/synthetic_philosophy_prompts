As a longtime reader of the rationalist blogosphere, I've recently begun delegating a good deal of my email triage and calendar scheduling to an AI agent, and it hardly ever seems to make the kind of side-effect blunders that Dennett's old thought experiments about robots and bombs were meant to dramatize: it just quietly reschedules conflicting meetings and updates the relevant downstream details without my having to spell out everything that should or shouldn't change. Doesn't this suggest that the frame problem, at least in the sense that matters for building genuinely useful systems, has essentially been solved by modern machine learning, and that whatever failures remain are just ordinary software bugs rather than instances of a distinct philosophical problem?
