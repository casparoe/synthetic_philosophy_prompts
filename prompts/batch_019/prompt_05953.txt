Cohen and Vogel's bootstrapping problem shows that reliabilism is guilty of circular reasoning: the view lets an agent use a belief source's outputs to establish that the very same source is reliable, as when one infers that a fuel gauge is accurate purely from a long track record of its own readings, without ever checking the tank by any independent means. Since circular arguments are illegitimate no matter what theory of justification one holds, isn't this so-called "problem of easy knowledge" really just a special case of question-begging, rather than a distinctive difficulty for reliabilism?
