I'm writing my IB Extended Essay on Henry Sidgwick. While working through The Methods of Ethics, I learned that he also co-founded the Society for Psychical Research and served as its first president, spending years investigating mediums, telepathy, and possible evidence for life after death. This seemed like a strange use of time for such a rigorous, skeptical philosopher, until I read that some historians tie it directly to the "dualism of practical reason" he identifies in the book's concluding chapter -- the idea that rational egoism and rational benevolence can conflict with no further principle available to decide between them. Please explain how his psychical research plausibly connects to this problem, and why he seems to have thought empirical evidence about an afterlife might bear on what looks like a purely abstract question about the rational authority of morality.
