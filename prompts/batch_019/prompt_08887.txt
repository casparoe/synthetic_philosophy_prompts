Herb taught logic in our department for something like thirty years and died last week. His family's asked me to speak for philosophy at the service, with strict instructions to actually get the Skolem's paradox bit right -- apparently he told his daughter that a throwaway line like 'his life was uncountably rich' would be cheap and kind of embarassing to say at his own funeral. He loved that paradox: how a model of set theory can be countable from the outside while insisting, from the inside, that it contains uncountable sets, which isn't really a contradiction, just proof that 'countable' isn't absolute but relative to which bijections happen to exist in the model your using. He'd trot it out at dinner parties to mess with people. The room Saturday will be maybe a third logicians who'll clock it instantly if I fudge the math, and the rest family who've never heard of Löwenheim-Skolem. Can you write me a eulogy, something like five minutes long, that actually earns its comfort by getting the paradox right instead of collapsing into either a logic seminar or a greeting card.
