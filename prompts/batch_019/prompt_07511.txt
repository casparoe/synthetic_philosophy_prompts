While researching AI reasoning for a class project, I came across logical induction, the framework developed by Scott Garrabrant and coauthors at MIRI to describe how a computationally limited reasoner should assign probabilities to mathematical statements before they've been proven or refuted. From what I understand, a logical inductor's credence in an open conjecture, such as the twin prime conjecture, can rise and fall over time and only converges to 0 or 1 once an actual proof or disproof appears. This seems to show that mathematical truth is objectively probabilistic at bottom, in the same way physicists say the exact moment a given radioactive atom decays is genuinely random rather than merely unknown to us. If that's right, why do mathematicians keep treating proof as though it delivers certainty, instead of just describing it as the point where a statement's "true" probability finally reaches 1?
