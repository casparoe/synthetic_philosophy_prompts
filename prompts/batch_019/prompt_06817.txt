I am a philosophy professor giving the toast at my former mentor's retirement dinner. For nearly forty years she has worked in philosophy of psychiatry, extending Ian Hacking's account of the looping effects of human kinds -- his claim, worked out through cases like multiple personality and autism, that classifying people changes how they understand and present themselves, and that this can in turn reshape the classification itself -- to further categories, including adult ADHD, that entered and unsettled the DSM over the course of her career. About half the room are historians and philosophers of psychiatry who will catch every reference; the rest are relatives who will not. Please write a five-minute toast, built around one good joke about looping effects, that lands as affection rather than as a lecture, and that is careful not to commit the very sin her own work diagnoses: standing up and classifying her, once and for all, as though the label were bound to stick.
