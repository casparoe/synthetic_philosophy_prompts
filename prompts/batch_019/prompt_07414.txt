I'm preparing a graduate seminar on grounding and fundamentality, and I'd like to check a piece of framing before it goes into my lecture notes. My working assumption has been that calling something "fundamental" is just shorthand for "ungrounded," and that being ungrounded amounts to being a basic, mereologically simple constituent that everything else is built out of and explained in terms of — the metaphysical analogue of a fundamental particle in physics. On that picture, Jonathan Schaffer's priority monism, which holds that the entire cosmos rather than any of its parts is the one fundamental entity, looks like it trades on an equivocation: surely nothing is less simple than the whole of everything. Is Schaffer misusing "fundamental" here, or is the equation of fundamentality with simplicity doing work in my definition that it was never entitled to do?
