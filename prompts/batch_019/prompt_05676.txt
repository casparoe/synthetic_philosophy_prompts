Every semester at least one student's paper on Mary's Room just retells the thought experiment as a scene: black-and-white room, she walks out, sees a red rose, gasps, the end, physicalism apparently refuted. I want something with more teeth for once. Write me a short story built around the phenomenal concept strategy instead of the obvious "she learns something new" reading -- the idea that Mary doesn't discover any new property or fact in the world beyond what was already fixed by the complete physical description she had; what's new is only the *concept* she now has for grasping that same old fact, a recognitional concept that nonetheless feels like a total revelation from the inside. Make the story actually earn that distinction dramatically rather then having someone just state it in dialogue, and if you can, leave a reader who doesn't know this literature genuinely unsure whether something metaphysically new showed up or not. A couple pages is plenty.
