So me and my friend got into a whole argument last night about Parfit's teleporter thing (the machine on earth scans and destroys you, then builds an exact copy on Mars). We agreed the copy is fully psychologically continuous with you - same memories, personality, plans, all of it - but then spent like an hour arguing about whether that actually settles anything about survival. I was on the "identity doesn't matter" side: what matters is just that continuity relation itself (Parfit calls it relation R), and the fission version proves it - if the machine glitches and builds two copies on Mars, your not literally identical to both, but you'd still care about each of them exactly like ordinary survival, so identity was never doing the real work. My friend wouldn't drop it though, he'd read some reply (David Lewis i think?) where the move is basically: no, identity really is what matters, you just have to admit that before the glitch there were secretly two people the whole time, overlapping and sharing every pre-fission stage, who only become distinguishable once they diverge - so each Mars-person really is identical to one of those two, not just "continuous" with some single earlier person. We never actually got anywhere. Can you write a dialogue between a Parfitian and a Lewis-style identity theorist hashing this out specifically over the fission case?
