So I've been reading up on the classic inverse reinforcement learning underdetermination problem -- how the exact same behavior (say, a busy academic who keeps skipping the gym) is technically consistent with a ton of different reward functions: lazy person who doesn't care about fitness, vs. hardworking person who really wants to be fit but keeps getting overridden by work. Every time this comes up on LW or the alignment forum though, someone eventually says "just put a simplicity prior over the space of reward functions and the sane human-like explanation wins out over the gerrymandered ones, so its basically a non-issue in practice." That seems right to me -- sure, there's technically infinite reward functions consistent with any dataset, but the vast majority are absurd, and no reasonable prior would put weight on them. Is "IRL is fundamentally unidentifiable" actually still a real obstacle people in alignment worry about, or is it more of a theoretical curiosity that a decent complexity prior just makes go away?
