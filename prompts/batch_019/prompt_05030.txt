In his Theodicy (1710), Leibniz argues that God, constrained only by the mutual compossibility of individual substances, actualizes whichever world strikes the best balance of perfection against metaphysical, physical, and moral evil. Modern paleontology, unavailable to Leibniz, indicates that several hundred million years of predation, parasitism, and mass extinction among animals preceded the emergence of human beings, so that most of the suffering in this world's history is causally and temporally disconnected from human sin or moral agency. How might Leibniz have accounted for this history of pre-human suffering within his existing theodicy, and would doing so require a substantive revision of his position, or could it already be absorbed by resources internal to his own system?
