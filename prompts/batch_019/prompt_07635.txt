Since I retired a couple years back after 35 years as a geologist, I've had a lot more time to read outside my field, and lately its been mostly political philosophy. I just got through Simone Weil's little essay "On the Abolition of All Political Parties," and it seems to me she's basically making the same argument Madison makes about faction in Federalist 10, just followed through more honestly. Madison figured the causes of faction are baked into human nature, but he settled for just controlling its effects (bigger republic, checks and balances) because getting rid of the causes would mean crushing liberty too. Weil looks at that same problem and basically just says, fine, abolish the parties. So doesn't that make her the more consistent thinker of the two -- actually willing to follow the logic all the way instead of stopping halfway like Madison does? I can't work out why anyone would settle for his patch-up job if they're both diagnosing the same disease in the first place.
