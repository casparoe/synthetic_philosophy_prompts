So everyone's favorite bit of weirdness, Moore's paradox: "it's raining, but I don't believe it." Its absurd to assert, even though nothing about the content is actually contradictory -- both halves could easily be true together. Here's the task: construct a sentence with the exact same form (p, and I don't believe that p) that ISN'T absurd, where the only thing you're allowed to change to get there is tense and/or grammatical person -- nothing else can differ. Then use your example to actually defend a specific diagnosis of what's going wrong in the original: is it a semantic problem, a pragmatic norm on assertion, something about the transparency of first-person present-tense belief (how you answer "do I believe it's raining?" by looking out the window rather than introspecting), or something else entirely? Don't just gesture at an answer -- head off the obvious objection that your "fix" secretly smuggled in some other difference besides tense/person.
