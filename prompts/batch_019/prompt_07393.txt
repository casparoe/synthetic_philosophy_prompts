Since retiring after forty years as a bench chemist, I have taken up philosophy in earnest, and it came as something of a surprise to find "water is H2O" doing serious work in the metaphysics of mind. I have grasped, more or less, the primary/secondary intension apparatus of two-dimensional semantics, and why it lets that identity be necessary yet knowable only a posteriori. What I have not settled for myself is whether the same apparatus entitles Chalmers to run his zombie-conceivability argument against materialism: his claim that phenomenal concepts, unlike "water", admit no appearance/reality gap, leaving nothing for primary and secondary intension to pull apart -- so that, in this instance, conceivability really does track possibility. Stalnaker, as I understand him, denies this. On his more deflationary, metasemantic reading of the same framework, primary intensions reflect contingent facts about how reference gets fixed, not contents that any ideal reasoner could recover by a priori reflection alone, so the inference from zombie-conceivability to the falsity of materialism never gets off the ground.

Please write a dialogue between an epistemic two-dimensionalist in Chalmers's mold and a metasemantically minded critic in Stalnaker's, in which the two fight this out point by point, each answering the other's strongest objections rather than simply restating their own position.
