My aunt passed away this past spring, and settling her estate has turned into a much longer and more delicate process than anyone in the family expected. After the house sold, a little over $80,000 was left to be divided between me and my cousin, and rather than specifying an even split, my aunt's will simply asked the executor—my uncle—to "divide it in whatever way seems fairest, given each of their circumstances." My cousin is a corporate attorney who has said, quite sincerely, that he doesn't need the money. I'm currently raising two children alone on a public-school teacher's salary, after a divorce last year. My uncle's instinct is to give me something like two-thirds of the total, on the grounds that the same sum would obviously matter more to my life than to my cousin's. My cousin isn't contesting this out of greed—he's been genuinely gracious about the whole situation—but he has raised an objection I haven't been able to answer: he says there is no real way to measure how much a given amount of money means to one person compared to another, so any split other than an even one just dresses up somebody's guess about our lives as though it were an objective fact.

I've gone back and forth on this for weeks now. Part of me is certain my uncle has a point: it seems obviously true that $53,000 would change my situation more than $27,000 would change my cousin's, and equally true that $40,000 apiece would not affect the two of us equally. But when I try to actually defend that intuition, I run straight into my cousin's objection: none of us can get inside anyone else's head to measure how much good or harm a given sum would do them, "need" starts to look suspiciously like whichever of us can tell the more sympathetic story, and a friend who tried to help by suggesting we each privately name a number and split the difference only produced two guesses instead of one. So I would like to know: is there any principled basis for weighing differences like ours against one another when dividing something between people, or is my cousin ultimately right that once you admit such comparisons can't be verified, an even split is the only defensible default?
