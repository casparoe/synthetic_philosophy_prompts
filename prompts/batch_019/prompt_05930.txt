Quick one — I've been down a rabbit hole on 'money pump' arguments for a piece on decision theory and AI. The classic move: if your preferences are cyclic or incomplete, someone can walk you through a sequence of trades that leaves you strictly poorer, which is supposed to prove rationality requires transitive, complete preferences. But that argument only really bites over a sequence of choices spread across time — its fundamentally diachronic — so couldn't an agent keep genuinely cyclic or incomplete preferences and just dodge the whole trap with something like McClennen's old 'resolute choice,' where you commit to a full plan up front and refuse to be re-talked into each step along the way? Can you make the strongest case that money-pump arguments establish, at most, a constraint on how you carry out sequences of choices over time, and don't actually show anything about whether your preferences are irrational at any single moment?
