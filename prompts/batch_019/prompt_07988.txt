So I've been down a bit of a Kierkegaard rabbit hole for a new short story collection I'm noodling on (each story is loosly hung on a different philosopher's pet idea), and I got kind of obsessed with the crankier, later Kierkegaard -- not the leap-of-faith, fear-and-trembling guy everyone already knows, but the one who got absolutely mauled by a satirical Copenhagen paper (The Corsair, I think it was run by a guy called Goldschmidt) after more or less daring them to attack him, and then spent years afterward writing about "the public" and "leveling." His basic idea, as I understand it, is that the press conjures up this faceless, bodiless mass that can crush an individual's reputation while nobody in particular is ever responsible for it, because everyone doing the judging hides inside the abstraction -- a "public" isn't a real community or a generation with a shared history, its more like a phantom made up of people who, taken one at a time, might not even believe what "everyone" supposedly believes, but who feel entitled to anonymous, consequence-free verdicts precisely because they're never speaking as themselves.

I want to write a story that actually earns that idea instead of being another generic "the internet is a mob" piece -- I want the mechanics of it to feel like his specific diagnosis: the anonymity, the way "the public" only exists parasitically through gossip and print and screens and dissolves the moment you try to pin it on an actual accountable person, and the leveling of anyone who tries to stand out as a distinct individual instead of dissolving into the crowd's chatter. I'm torn between setting it in something like 1840s Copenhagen, maybe from the point of view of the editor doing the mocking rather than the victim (feels like the more interesting angle, and less on-the-nose), or transposing the whole setup onto a present-day pile-on -- genuinely not sure which serves the idea better, so use your judgement on that, or even do something in between. Could you write me the short story -- a few pages is fine -- and try not to have anyone stop and explain the theory out loud, I want it dramatized rather than lectured.
