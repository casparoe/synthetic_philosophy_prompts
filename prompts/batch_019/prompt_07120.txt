So standard epistemic logic makes knowledge/belief closed under logical consequence, which is basically the logical omniscience problem - know the axioms and apparently you "know" every theorem too. The classic fix is awareness logic (Fagin & Halpern style): you only count as believing phi if its true in all your accessible worlds AND you're "aware" of phi. But that doesn't really solve things when the agent is already aware of the conclusion and just hasn't drawn the inference yet - the awareness filter does nothing in that case. Can you give me a concrete example (actual sentences, not schematic P/Q stuff) of a premise, a conditional, and a conclusion, where the agent is aware of and believes the premise and conditional, is also aware of the conclusion, but obviously hasn't figured it out yet - so awareness logic alone would still (wrongly) count them as knowing it?
