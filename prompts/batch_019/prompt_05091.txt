Quick one that came up while I'm working on a piece about the mini-revival of "causal powers" talk among philosophers of science — the idea that things like fragility or solubility involve some real disposition sitting in the object, not just a pattern of behaviour we've noticed. Everyone in this literature eventually gets dragged back to Hume, so I went and reread the relevant bit of the Enquiry (Section VII, "Of the Idea of Necessary Connexion" — the 1748 one, not the Treatise) and got stuck on this passage:

"We have sought in vain for an idea of power or necessary connexion in all the sources from which we could suppose it to be derived. It appears that, in single instances of the operation of bodies, we never can, by our utmost scrutiny, discover any thing but one event following another, without being able to comprehend any force or power by which the cause operates, or any connexion between it and its supposed effect."

Around the same time I fell into a totally seperate rabbit hole on "finks" and "masks," the counterexamples philosophers like C.B. Martin came up with to show a disposition can't just be a stand-in for a conditional, because the two can come apart. Roughly: a dead wire hooked to a gadget that switches it live the instant you touch it, so it conducts without ever really being "live" beforehand; or a poison dosed together with an antidote, so it's genuinely poisonous even though the "if swallowed, it would kill" conditional comes out false. What I can't work out on my own is how these two things relate. Is the fink/mask stuff basically a present-day, dressed-up version of exactly the gap Hume is pointing at in that passage — that no single instance ever reveals a power, only one event following another? Or does it actually cut the other way, showing that even Hume's next move in the essay, appealing to a pattern across many repeated instances rather than any one instance, doesn't rescue things either, since a real disposition and the "matching" pattern of outcomes can apparently be pulled apart by the right bit of interference? I'd like to actually get this right for the piece instead of hand-waving it, so could you walk through both readings rather than just picking one — I've got one undergrad metaphysics class and no more, so pitch it for a smart general reader rather than a specialist.
