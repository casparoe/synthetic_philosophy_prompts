I recently went down a rabbit hole reading a popular introduction to Marx (I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, so please forgive any imprecision in how I describe his ideas), and I keep returning to a question about how his concepts would map onto something he obviously never encountered: open-source software. From what I understand, "exploitation" in Marx's technical sense isn't just a synonym for treating someone unfairly — it names a specific mechanism tied to wage labor, in which a capitalist buys labor-power at its value and then extracts more value over the working day than that wage represents, pocketing the difference as surplus value. That whole mechanism presupposes an employment relationship. But an enormous share of the software the modern economy depends on — think of the log4j library, whose vulnerability caused a global scramble a few years back, or the thousands of small npm and PyPI packages quietly sitting in everyone's dependency tree — is written for free by volunteers who are nobody's employees and never sell their labor-power to the companies profiting from it. There's no wage, and often no single identifiable capitalist on the other end of the relationship, yet corporations plainly extract enormous value from this unpaid work, and maintainers periodically burn out or revolt over it (there's a well-known case from a few years back of a maintainer deliberately sabotaging his own widely-used packages after venting publicly about exactly this kind of uncompensated extraction).

What I'd like from you is a careful, conceptually rigorous reconstruction of how Marx himself, reasoning strictly from his own apparatus rather than generic "capitalism is bad" sentiment, would analyze this situation. First: does his specific notion of exploitation actually apply here, given that there's no wage relation at all, or would he need to reach for a different part of his own toolkit — something closer to primitive accumulation, enclosure of a commons, or the unpaid appropriation of a use-value produced entirely outside any wage contract — to describe what's going on? Second, I'd like you to work through the alienation side on its own terms: open-source labor seems to satisfy several of the conditions Marx associates with unalienated work (freely chosen, creative, directed by the producer rather than a boss, carried out in voluntary association with others), which makes it an odd fit for his usual picture of estranged factory labor. Does that mean this kind of work actually escapes most of his four-fold alienation, and if so, would Marx have seen capital's capture of already-unalienated collective labor as a strange new twist on exploitation, as a phenomenon his framework simply isn't built to describe, or as something else entirely?
