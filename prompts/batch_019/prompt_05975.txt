Her signature move is defending the all-subjected (or "all-coerced") principle over the more popular all-affected-interests view: on her account, whoever is subject to a state's coercively enforced laws thereby has a claim to a say in making them, and she's used this to argue that unilateral border and immigration control is illegitimate toward the very people it's excluding.

Can you draft me 9 or 10 interview questions that don't just let her restate that pitch?
