This has been bugging me for a while and I want an actual compare/contrast, not just vibes. Good's theorem says a Bayesian about to act should never refuse free evidence - expected value of information is always >=0, hitting exactly 0 only when the evidence wouldn't change what you'd do anyway. But in info econ there's the Hirshleifer effect: public information can make an entire economy worse off, since it destroys risk-sharing/insurance deals that only worked because nobody knew yet who was gonna draw the short straw. Rationalist blogs cite "VoI is always non-negative" like its basically a law of rationality, but nobody ever reconciles that with Hirshleifer. So compare the two properly: are they actually in tension, or does Hirshleifer sneak in a different notion of "value" (social vs individual, pre- vs post-contract) such that Good's theorem was never making a claim Hirshleifer could contradict? Pin down exactly where "more info, weakly better" stops holding once other agents and existing contracts enter the picture, and tell me straight whether that fully dissolves the paradox or leaves something genuinely surprising behind.
