My dad passed away a couple months ago, and last weekend my mom finally let me go through his workshop and take what I wanted. I ended up with his old Guild acoustic, which he'd had since 1979 and which, it turns out, has had almost every part replaced at some point over the decades: a new top after a humidity crack in the 90s, refretted twice, a replacement bridge, new tuning pegs, and eventually a whole new neck after the original one warped so bad it stayed visibly bowed for like a year before he got around to fixing it. Holding it gave me this weird feeling I wasn't expecting, like it both was and wasn't "his guitar" anymore, and I brought that up at a small dinner with two of my closest friends, Jonah and Reyna, who ended up in a real argument about it that outlasted two rounds of drinks after everyone else had wondered off to bed.

Here's roughly how it went, move by move:

1. Jonah said the guitar obviously persisted the whole time, because objects extend through time the same way they extend through space: just as the neck and the body are both parts of the guitar right now, the 1979-guitar and the 2023-guitar are both "temporal parts" of one longer four-dimensional object, and dad's actual guitar is that whole thing -- we just only ever see one slice of it at a time.
2. Reyna said that's a weird trade, because on that view dad never actually played "the guitar" itself at any single moment, only some razor-thin slice of a much bigger thing most of which never coexists with any given strum -- and worse, nothing stops you from gluing half of this guitar's slices to half of some totally different guitar's slices into an equally well-defined four-dimensional object, so Jonah owes a story for why the natural-seeming guitar-worm is special and not just one gerrymandered bundle among trillions of equally real ones.
3. Jonah admitted that composition worry isn't unique to time-slices (spatial parts have the exact same puzzle), but said he still had to explain the neck: the very same guitar was, at one point, visibly bowed, and later, dead straight, and if it's one undivided thing wholly present at both times, then the very same thing has two incompatible shapes at once -- which is only not a contradiction if you either say "being bowed" is somehow relative to a time (which he thinks is a dodge) or admit it's really two different temporal parts wearing two different shapes, no contradiction needed.
4. Reyna said the "dodge" charge is unfair: the guitar is also narrow at the headstock and wide at the lower bout, and nobody thinks that needs explaining away by slicing the guitar into a stack of "width-havers" -- we just say it has different widths at different places -- so persisting through time might likewise just be a basic further fact about sameness and history that doesn't require chopping anything into instants.
5. Jonah said pure continuity-of-history can't be the whole story either, because in principle you could rebuild a second guitar entirely out of the discarded original parts once everything on dad's had cycled through at least one replacement, and that reassembled guitar would have a continuity story back to 1979 every bit as good as the continuously-repaired one -- and they can't both be it, so something besides "same history, full stop" has to break the tie.
6. Reyna said maybe nothing does break that tie, and maybe that just shows the question of which one is "really" dad's guitar gets settled by us, by what we care about (sentimental value, insurance, whatever), rather than by either of their theories -- and that Jonah's four-dimensional worm doesn't dodge that, it just relocates the same tie-breaking problem to "which bundle of slices gets to count as the guitar."

I've replayed this in my head about ten times since and keep flipping on who's actually winning each point. Could you go through moves 1 through 6 and give me a verdict on each as a JSON object like {"1": <score>, "2": <score>, "3": <score>, "4": <score>, "5": <score>, "6": <score>}, where the score runs from -2 (clearly wrong) to 2 (clearly right), then explain each score in a couple sentences, and finish by telling me what you think both of them are still missing about the guitar, or about persistence in general, that never came up that night.
