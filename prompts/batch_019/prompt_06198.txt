Unofficially, everyone in the department suspects the philosophy is standing in for something neither of them will name. For the chapter I'm drafting now, I need to seat them next to each other, against all odds, at the rain-soaked memorial reception for the advisor who supervised them both, and have the old argument reignite within minutes, as though the decade apart had been a single long pause in the same sentence.

Could you write this scene as a dialogue of roughly 700 to 900 words, one in which the philosophical content does real argumentative work rather than functioning as mere decoration? I don't need the textbook-level statement of the dispute, where epistemicism says vague predicates have sharp, unknowable cutoffs and supervaluationism says borderline claims are neither true nor false because they vary across admissible precisifications; I'm already familiar with that much. Please have them genuinely engage each other's strongest points rather than talk past one another, don't let either of them come out the obvious loser, and if a flicker of their old friendship shows through the argument by the end, so much the better.
