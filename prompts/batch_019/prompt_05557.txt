This has been bugging me since our phil of science reading group last week. Everyone name-drops the Newman objection as the reason Worrall's epistemic structural realism (ESR) doesn't really work -- roughly, that "we only know the world's structure" turns out almost empty once you notice any domain of the right cardinality can be made to satisfy pretty much any Ramsey-sentence structure via some gerrymandered relation. The standard line is that ontic structural realism (OSR; Ladyman/French, relations without relata, structure "all the way down") dodges this. But I've never actually seen anyone walk through why swapping the metaphysics blocks Newman's triviality result instead of just relocating it -- doesn't OSR still need some relations to count as the "natural"/physically real ones rather than the gerrymandered alternatives, which sounds like exactly the extra non-structural fact the view wasnt supposed to need in the first place? Can you compare ESR and OSR specifically on this point, say which one (if either) actually has a principled answer to Newman, and give an example of a case where the difference would actually show up?
