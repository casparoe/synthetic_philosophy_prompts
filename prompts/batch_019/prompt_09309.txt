I recently tried a ninety-minute session in a sensory-deprivation float tank while reporting a story on the wellness industry's enthusiasm for them: body-temperature salt water, total darkness, complete silence. About twenty minutes in, I lost any clear sense of where my skin ended and the water began, and I could no longer feel my arms or legs unless I deliberately moved them, yet at no point did I stop being certain that I existed and was having the experience. If anything, that certainty felt sharper once the ordinary bodily sensations dropped away. Several float-tank regulars I interviewed for the piece take this sort of experience as evidence that the "self" is something separate from the body, merely obscured by it in everyday life; my instinct is that this is too quick, but I can't quite pin down what, if anything, is wrong with the inference. How should I think about what my experience in the tank does, and doesn't, actually show?
