I'm prepping a lecture on Nietzsche and can't shake a worry about something. In Ecce Homo he goes on at length about diet, climate, and recreation, insisting these 'little things' matter way more than the big questions philosophy usually obsesses over, and hes incredibly fussy about his own regimen -- what to drink, how much sun, when to rest. But so much of the Genealogy is him attacking ascetic self-denial and hostility to the instincts as symptoms of decadence and reactive life-denial. Please argue that Nietzsche's own dietary self-management is really just a more refined version of the ascetic self-mastery he diagnoses as sick everywhere else in his work, so theres a genuine 'Nietzsche contra Nietzsche' problem here, not just a cheap gotcha.
