I'm working on a piece about scalable oversight for a newsletter I write on AI alignment, and I keep getting stuck on the debate approach -- the 2018 OpenAI paper (Irving, Christiano, and I want to say Amodei was on it too) where you get two copies of a powerful model to argue opposite sides of a question in front of a human judge who can't verify the answer themselves, the bet being that its easier to expose a lie under cross-examination than to tell one convincingly. One of the alignment researchers I interviewed for the piece kept describing this as basically Milton's "truth and falsehood grappling" idea grown up and put into code, which actually got me thinking less about Milton and more about Mill, since he's the one who actually tried to build a real argument for why open contest favors truth, in On Liberty -- the bit about how a silenced opinion might turn out to be the true one, or, if it's false, might still hold a fragment of truth the reigning view is missing, or how even a wholly correct opinion calcifies into "dead dogma" once nobody's left to seriously contest it.

So here's the thing I can't work out on my own, and where I'd love your help: if you sat Mill down in front of an actual debate transcript -- say, two AI systems arguing over whether a chunk of code has a hidden vunerability, with a judge who has to rule despite being unable to check the code directly -- would he recognize this as his own argument at work, or would he think something essential got lost along the way? Two specific snags I keep bumping into: first, his case for free discussion is built around a whole society re-litigating a question across generations, not one fallible arbiter ruling once, under a deadline; second, there's a known failure mode debate researchers call the "obfuscated arguments" problem, where a dishonest debater can bury a flaw so deep, or split it across enough steps, that no amount of cross-examination lets a bounded judge actually locate it. Would that second one worry Mill, given how much weight his optimism puts on error eventually getting exposed through contest -- or does his framework already have an answer to it built in? Walk me through what you think his actual position would be, and where it'd be weakest.
