Ok, I've been chewing on the absent-minded driver problem again (the classic Piccione-Rubinstein one: two intersections that look identical from the inside, exit-early pays 0, continue-then-exit pays 4, blow through both pays 1) and there's a thing about it that bugs me, and I never see it addressed head-on. Everyone agrees the optimal policy is to continue with probability 2/3. And the standard move is to say this also pins down what you should *believe* once you're actually standing at one of the intersections not knowing which -- something like 3/5 credence you're at the first one, 2/5 you're at the second. But that credence is computed FROM the 2/3 policy, you need p already fixed before you can derive it. So is there really an independent, prior fact about what a rational driver should believe about their location that then justifies the 2/3 policy, or does the "belief" just fall out of whichever policy happens to be optimal, with no independent life of its own? Kind of has the feel of a dispute that might be entirely verbal, since both sides can agree on what to actually do at the intersection.

Can you write me a dialogue between two decision theorists hashing this out while literally standing at the unmarked intersection together. One thinks self-locating credence is a real, well-defined thing thats fixed by the situation prior to any choice of policy, and that 2/3 is only correct because its what a rational agent with the "right" credence would do. The other is more the updateless/policy-first type (very lesswrong-brained) who thinks the credence talk is dispensable and you should just directly rank whole policies by their ex-ante payoff, no self-location required. Steelman both sides, don't want either of them to be a pushover. Have them actually respond to each other's specific points, push on the circularity worry, and maybe bring up Sleeping Beauty at some point as the obvious cousin case. By the end I'd like them to at least try to figure out whether they're actually disagreeing about something or just prefer different vocabularies for the same behavior. Doesn't need a tidy resolution, just keep it to a page or so.
