So in my Theory of Knowledge class we just finished the miracles section of Hume's Enquiry (the "Of Miracles" part) and I feel like I actually get his argument now. Like his whole point is that a "wise man proportions his belief to the evidence," and since a law of nature is basically defined by every single past observation without exception, the evidence against a miracle happening is about as strong as evidence can ever get. So for someone's testimony to be enough to make you believe a miracle occured, it would have to be even LESS likely that the testimony is false than that an actual law of nature got broken, which pretty much never happens in real life. That seemed like a genuinely airtight argument to me.
