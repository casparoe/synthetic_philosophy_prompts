Ok so I've been chewing on something about coherence arguments for EU maximization and want a sanity check. The standard money-pump for cyclic preferences (A over B over C over A) seems to work no matter what policy the agent adopts, since by construction every single trade in the cycle is a strict improvement. But when I try to build an analogous pump for an agent with merely incomplete preferences (say A and B just aren't comparable), the agent can dodge it by adopting a rule like "only trade if its a strict improvement over my current holding" -- and that rule costs nothing against a cycle-free incomplete ordering. So it looks like transitivity and completeness aren't actually resting on symmetric arguments, even though people (Yudkowsky's "coherent decisions imply consistent utilities" post is probably the worst offender) tend to lump them together like any violation of either axiom is equally exploitable. Am I missing something, or is completeness actually the much weaker link in the standard "rationality requires expected utility maximization" chain? Curious how you'd explain the asymmetry, and whether the usual escape hatches for incompleteness (resolute choice etc) actually hold up against a sufficiently clever bookie.
