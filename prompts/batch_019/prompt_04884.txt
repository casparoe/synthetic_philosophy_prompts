So this happened at my cousin's birthday dinner and it's been stuck in my head since. Her husband Marco and her friend Priya got into an actual argument over whether evolution counts as "real science."

Marco's opening move: natural selection is basically a tautology -- "survival of the fittest" just means "the ones that survived, survived" -- and this isnt some fringe take, since Karl Popper, the guy who invented the falsifiability test for science, once wrote that Darwinism "is not a testable scientific theory, but a metaphysical research programme."

Priya said that's not how fitness actually works in biology -- scientists predict ahead of time which traits should do better in a given environment (like beak depth in a drought) and then check, and sometimes they're wrong, so it's testable, not circular.

Marco said fine, but Popper wasn't just some guy with an opinion -- he was THE authority on what falsifiability even means, so his verdict should count for more than one counterexample.

Priya came back with two things: Popper himself, in that same talk, admits he's "not a scientist" and "simply an amateur" on Darwin, so being an authority on falsifiability in general isn't the same as being right about this specific case. And anyway, he later changed his mind and admitted the theory could be tested after all -- so his "final word" actually backs her.

Marco (googling by now) said that walk-back is weaker than it looks, because in that same passage where he recants, Popper still calls natural selection basically the same "metaphysical research programme" as before, just gentler -- so it sounds like he only dropped the word "untestable" and quietly kept the tautology worry alive.

Priya said dropping "untestable" basically IS dropping the tautology worry, since that was the whole charge to begin with, and changing your mind once real counterexamples show up is good epistemics, not a dodge.

Nobody budged and dessert got weird. Can you referee this blow by blow and say who's actually got it right at each step, not just who you'd side with overall? I'm especially stuck on the last exchange -- did Popper really keep the substance of his original worry while only dropping the label, like Marco says, or is there no real daylight between the two, like Priya says?
