I'm a software engineer by trade with no formal training in philosophy, but a few of us at work run an informal reading group, and we just finished a general relativity primer whose historical introduction leaned heavily on the Newton's bucket experiment to motivate the whole substantivalism/relationism debate. As I understood the chapter: Newton observed that the water's surface goes concave once the bucket is spinning fast enough, even during the interval when the water is not yet moving relative to the bucket's walls, and took this as evidence that the water's rotation had to be relative to space itself rather than to any surrounding body — hence "absolute space." Leibniz objected on broadly logical grounds, and Mach later sharpened this into an empirical proposal: what actually explains the water's climbing up the sides is its rotation relative to all the other matter in the universe (the distant stars), not some featureless container called space. The chapter then treats Mach's principle as a direct inspiration for Einstein, and presents general relativity's general covariance — the fact that its field equations take the same form in any coordinate system, with no preferred background frame — as the result that finally vindicated Mach and Leibniz over Newton, retiring absolute space as a piece of pre-relativistic metaphysics that a correct physical theory simply doesn't need.

Assuming that potted history is basically right, I'm trying to figure out what's actually left for the substantivalist side of this debate today. If general relativity already builds the relativity of motion into the field equations themselves, is contemporary philosophy of physics mostly just cleaning up historical loose ends and refining the verdict against Newton, rather than treating substantivalism as a serious live option? I want to understand this well enough to explain it to my reading group without misrepresenting the current state of the debate, so I'd rather not walk in next week citing a zombie position as though it were still genuinely contested.
