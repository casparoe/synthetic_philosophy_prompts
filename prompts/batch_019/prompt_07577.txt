Without any formal training in philosophy, I have spent a good deal of time working through the literature on the repugnant conclusion on my own, and I have come to think that the whole difficulty is really just an artifact of total utilitarianism's insistence on maximizing aggregate welfare. If we simply adopted something like average utilitarianism, or a person-affecting view that cares only about making people happy rather than making happy people, the repugnant conclusion would no longer arise. Given this, why do philosophers continue to treat population ethics as an unresolved problem rather than simply adopting one of these alternative axiologies and considering the matter settled?
