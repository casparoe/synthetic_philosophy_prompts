I've been down a rabbit hole reading about the prisoner's dilemma lately, partly because of all the talk about AI agents negotiating with each other, and I keep running into this "superrationality" idea from Hofstadter - that two copies of the same perfectly rational agent will both cooperate because they know they reason exactly alike. But isn't this just regular game theory? I get that defecting is supposed to be the "dominant" move, but once its common knowledge that both sides are perfect reasoners, shouldnt that dominance logic just break down, so ordinary Nash equilibrium reasoning already lands them on cooperate with no extra assumptions needed? I don't get why superrationality gets treated as this whole separate, kinda controversial idea instead of just being what plain old rational choice theory gives you once both sides know the other one's rational too.
