I'm a junior in high school, and something that happened over winter break has been bothering me ever since, so I wanted to get an outside opinion on it. A friend of mine lost close to four hundred dollars to an online scam that promised her a spot in a selective summer research program if she paid an upfront "application processing fee." About a week before that happened, someone had posted a warning about that exact scam in a group chat that includes her, me, and roughly fifteen other people from our grade. I saw the post and even reacted to it, but I never thought to send it to her directly, and I'd honestly forgotten about it completely until she told everyone what had happened to her. She has since told me, more than once, that if I had just forwarded her that one message, she never would have paid the fee, and that she holds me at least partly responsible for losing the money, since the two of us talk almost every day and she assumes that I would have mentioned anything to her that seemed important.

Obviously, the person who actually caused her to lose the money was the scammer, not me, but she doesn't see it as an either-or situation, and honestly I'm not sure she's wrong to feel that way. At the same time, something about her reasoning bothers me. Those same fifteen or so people, including some of her other close friends, saw the exact same warning and also never sent it to her, and none of them are being blamed the way I am. If failing to send her that message is really part of what caused her to get scammed, it seems like that should apply equally to everyone in the chat who stayed silent, and if it doesn't count as causing anything at all, then I don't understand why I still feel as guilty as I do. I brought it up with my older brother, hoping he'd tell me I was overthinking it, but he just said "it's complicated" and changed the subject. Is my friend right to hold me responsible in a way she doesn't hold anyone else in that chat, or is there something off about how she — and maybe I — are thinking about it?
