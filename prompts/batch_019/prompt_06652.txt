So this has been bugging me for weeks now and I keep dragging it up at my writers group, probably to everyone's annoyance at this point. I plot pretty much everything on this huge corkboard covered in index cards, physically moving them around, grouping scenes, yanking a card out the second a subplot stops working, and honestly, if you took the board away from me I don't think I could tell you the actual shape of the book I'm working on right now. I know isolated bits, sure, but the way everything connects, the structure, that only really seems to exist once its laid out in front of me and I've had a chance to shuffle it around for a while. One older guy in the group, who does everything in his head and doesn't write a word until the whole thing is basically finished up there, said something that's stuck with me since: he reckons that if I can't hold the whole plot without the board, I don't actually know my own story yet, I've just built a workaround that mimics knowing it. He compared it to a chess player who has to physically move the pieces to calculate three moves ahead versus a master who just sees the position.

Part of me wants to push back but I don't think I have solid footing to do it from, beyond "well, it works, and the books get finished." Is he onto something real, that the understanding isn't fully mine unless I could in principle pull it off without the crutch, or is that a weirdly arbitrary standard to hold something like understanding to? I'd love an actual argued take on this rather than a reassuring "different people work differently," because I have a hunch there's a real distinction buried in his objection and I genuinely can't tell which side of it my corkboard habit falls on.
