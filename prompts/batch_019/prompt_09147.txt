I'm on my department's graduate fellowship committee this year, and we've got exactly one endowed fellowship to hand out to an incoming PhD student -- no splitting it, and the guidelines just say to rank finalists by "overall scholarly promise." We're down to two files and stuck, and it's become a running argument in the department lounge. Candidate A's writing sample is a small, airtight proof-theoretic argument about presupposition failure in counterfactuals -- every reader agrees it's flawless, but it's also just narrow. Candidate B's sample is a much bigger swing: an attempt at a non-ideal theory of intergenerational justice that actually engages with real climate modeling, genuinely exciting but with a couple of moves none of us are fully sold on. Two of my colleagues keep insisting this is really just a case of uncertainty -- that if we knew more about how B's project would pan out, we could rank them straightforwardly -- but I don't buy it. It feels less like we're ignorant of some fact and more like we're being asked to compare a flawless short story to an ambitious unfinished novel, where "the better scholar" doesn't obviously name a fact to be ignorant of in the first place. I've read enough Ruth Chang on hard choices and parity, plus the older Berlin and Raz stuff on value pluralism and incommensurability, to suspect that's whats actually going on, but that literature is much better at diagnosing the problem then telling a committee what to actually do with it by a Friday deadline.

Could you draft the memo I'll circulate to the committee before we meet? I want an actual recommendation -- who should get the fellowship, or, if you think the honest answer is some kind of tiebreak procedure rather than a real ranking, what that procedure should be and why it isn't just a fig leaf for arbitrariness -- along with a concrete timeline for getting this settled. But please really engage with the "we just need more information" camp rather than just asserting incommensurability and moving on, since half the committee will read a memo that skips that step as having begged the question.
