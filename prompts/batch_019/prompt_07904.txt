So I've been re-reading van Fraassen's "Belief and the Will" and it's given me an idea for an essay prompt: his Reflection Principle. Quick refresher -- if you're currently certain your credence in some proposition E will be r at a later time, your current credence in E, conditional on that certainty, should also equal r; van Fraassen defends this with a diachronic Dutch book argument, same style as the synchronic one for probabilism. Problem is, there are plenty of cases where I'm pretty sure what my future credence will be -- I'm about to get drunk, or hypnotized, or I just know future-me rationalizes whatever he already wants to believe -- and deferring to that credence right now seems nuts. Van Fraassen's fix is to restrict Reflection to future selves you have no reason to distrust, but that move can look like its just smuggling in the very rationality requirement it was supposed to earn, which threatens to make the whole principle trivial or circular. Write an essay that actually stakes out a position: is there a principled, non-circular way to restrict Reflection so it survives cases like these, or should we just drop the principle (Dutch book defense and all)? Weigh both sides, but don't hedge -- tell me where you land and why.
