I've been reading rationalist-adjacent blogs for something like a decade now (started with the Sequences back in the day, still lurk on the forums), so I thought I had a decent enough handle on basic decision theory. But something that happened at a local meetup a couple weekends ago is still bugging me and I can't fully untangle it. A friend who trades for a living offered a bunch of us a repeatable bet: flip a fair coin, heads you get $140, tails you pay $100. Clearly good expected value, so I said sure, I'd play a few rounds for fun. But when he offered to just let it run as long as we wanted, like twenty or fifty flips in a row, I said no thanks, I'd rather stop after a handful. He jumped on that right away and said my position didn't hold together: either the bet's good enough to take, in which case you should want MORE of it, since the law of large numbers means your actual winnings converge toward the expected value and the odds of ending up behind basically evaporate the longer you play, or it isn't good enough and you shouldn't take it at all. Drawing some arbitrary line at "a few times but not a ton," he said, isnt a principled stance, its just nerves dressed up as caution.

I didn't have a great comeback in the moment, but it still doesn't sit right with me, and I don't think it's just wounded pride talking. It's not that a bad run would actually hurt me financially — we're talking about amounts that don't come anywhere near bending my finances, so this isn't a "money means less to me once I have more of it" thing, at least not one that should kick in at these stakes. I just don't want that much accumulated exposure even at good odds, and that feels to me like a real preference rather than a bias I ought to train myself out of. So, is he right that there's no coherent place to draw a line between "sure, a couple times" and "sure, forever," which would make my instinct here basically confused? Or is there a legitimate way of thinking about risk that can make sense of stopping partway through, even while granting that every individual flip is a great deal? Curious how you'd untangle this, since my usual mental toolkit isn't cutting it here.
