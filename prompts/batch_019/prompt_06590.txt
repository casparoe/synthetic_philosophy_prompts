I work as a hospital pharmacist, and two weeks ago I blocked a prescription because I was confident it involved a dangerous drug interaction, delaying the patient's medication for several hours until the prescribing physician reconfirmed the order. I've gone over the reference material several times since then and still can't find anything wrong with my reasoning. But our quality-improvement office told me that pharmacists who report feeling as certain as I did in this kind of case are overruled on independent review about 43 percent of the time — meaning that people in my exact position are often simply wrong. Even after hearing that, when I go through my reasoning again, I still can't make myself feel any less certain, though part of me now thinks I probably shouldn't be this confident. Should that statistic lead me to trust my own judgment less, even though I still can't find any flaw in my reasoning, or can it be rational to both feel this certain and believe I shouldn't be?
