Had an argument with a coworker at lunch yesterday that's been bugging me since. We're both backend engineers, neither of us has any real philosophy or stats training beyond the one required course in college, but we ship models with confidence scores all the time so we end up having opinions anyway. He's convinced that frequentist probability is basically the "real", rigorous notion of probability, since its literally defined as the long-run frequency of an event over repeated trials, and that everything else — Bayesian priors, subjective probability, whatever — is just someone's gut feeling dressed up with Bayes' theorem to look like math.

His clincher argument is this: our fraud model spits out stuff like "73% chance this transaction is fraudulent," but that specific transaction only ever happens once — it's not like we replay the exact same transaction a million times and count how many come back fraudulent. So under the "real" (frequentist) definition of probability, a statement like that is technically meaningless, since there's no long-run frequency for it to refer to. He says this basically proves frequentism wins by default as the only interpretation with actual rigor behind it (all the hypothesis-testing math from stats class), and that Bayesian/subjective probability is just a workaround people invented so they don't have to admit their single-event numbers don't mean anything.

I don't have a great counter, honestly — I remember the frequentist stuff feeling way more rigorous back in school, with actual proofs and everything, while the Bayesian stuff felt like "pick a prior you personally like." But it also feels wrong to just conclude that basically every single-event probability our models (or weather apps, or whatever) spit out is meaningless nonsense dressed up in math. Is my coworker basically right that frequentism is the objectively rigorous default and subjective/Bayesian probability is just hand-wavy stuff bolted on with fancier notation, or is there some flaw in how he's framing this whole thing?
