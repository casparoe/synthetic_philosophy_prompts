So I'm a few drafts into a novel based on a hospital that really did close near where I grew up, and my editor says it doesn't work because there's no villian -- she wants a board member skimming money or an insurance exec who guts the place on purpose, someone the reader can be mad at. What I want to write is messier: the board makes a defensible call, the best doctors leave for better pay, the state won't expand the subsidy that could've saved it -- each choice reasonable on its own, and the hospital dies anyway without anybody deciding to kill it. She says thats not a story, just a weather report, since nothing's explained till it comes down to one person's choice you can point to. Is she right that nothing really counts as an explanation until it bottoms out in somebody's decision, or can 'the system did it' be true in its own right and not just a cop-out?
