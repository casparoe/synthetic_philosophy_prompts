Now retired after a career in evolutionary biology, I have recently been working through Maimonides' Guide of the Perplexed, and his account of the sacrificial cult in III:32 and III:46 struck me as an exaptation story: animal sacrifice, inherited from the idolatrous culture the Israelites absorbed in Egypt, is redirected toward the true God rather than abolished outright, because a sudden reversal of ingrained practice was, in Maimonides' view, more than human nature could bear. Nahmanides objects that Abel's offering and Noah's post-diluvian sacrifice were both accepted -- indeed prized -- by God long before any idolatrous cult existed for later worship to be weaned from, so accommodation cannot be doing the explanatory work Maimonides assigns it. Please make the strongest case available that Maimonides' theory survives this objection. Engage directly with the Abel and Noah cases, rather than simply restating the general weaning thesis, and explain how sacrifice can be a mere concession to idolatry while God nonetheless approved of it before any idolatry existed to concede to.
