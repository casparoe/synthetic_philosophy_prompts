I got into rereading Moritz Schlick's old essay on the meaning of life this week. He argues meaning can't come from means-end, purposive activity chained onto further purposes forever -- that just regresses -- so it has to bottom out in something with the character of play, which is basically why kids have it easy and most adults lose the knack except maybe through art or work that keeps some playfulness in it. Kept thinking about how hard this cuts against a lot of rationalist/EA-ish self-optimization culture: optimizing your morning routine, treating rest as just recovery so you can be productive later, picking a career by expected future impact, everything instrumental to some further goal, indefinitely deferred. If you plopped Schlick down at a present-day EA meetup and walked him through all that, what do you think he'd actually say about it? Is it just the exact thing he was warning about, dressed up with spreadsheets, or could he find a version of it thats compatible with his idea of what makes a moment meaningful? Would appreciate an actual answer rather than a 'he'd probably have mixed feelings' cop-out if you can manage it :)
