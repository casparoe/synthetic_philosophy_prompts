I'm a retired geologist who fell down the Godel rabbit hole in retirement, which is apparently how I ended up on the board of a small institute that funds long-shot math and physics research. This year's stack has an application from a logician wanting a semester's support to try his own version of what Ed Nelson attempted years back: an actual proof that the Peano axioms are inconsistent, or, failing that, that ZFC is. He knows Nelson's own attempt collapsed once Terence Tao found the gap in it, but argues thats a reason for more care, not for giving up. Two board members want to decline outright -- every working mathematician treats consistency as settled in practice, and they dont want our name on a hunt for a unicorn -- while two others say refusing to fund a serious, credentialed look at our own basic assumptions is exactly the overconfidence Godel's second theorem should've cured us of, especially since foundational systems have turned out inconsistent before (ask Frege). I need a memo before we vote Tuesday recommending fund, decline, or fund-with-conditions -- can you draft it, actually taking a side, with reasoning the applicant and both skeptics could engage with, not just a summary of the positions?
