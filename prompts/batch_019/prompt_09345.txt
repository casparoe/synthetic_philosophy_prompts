My grandmother was an Episcopal priest with a doctorate in philosophy of religion who taught a seminar on the Euthyphro dilemma every year for three decades, and she had no patience for either horn of it, holding that "the pious is loved by the gods because it is pious" subordinates God to a standard outside himself, and that "it is pious because it is loved by the gods" makes goodness an arbitrary decree. Her actual, distinctly Thomist view was that this is a false choice: goodness is convertible with being as such, and since God's very essence is to exist, God simply is the good rather than inventing or consulting it -- his commands express that nature rather than constituting or merely tracking it. She marked seminar papers down for collapsing the distinction into either horn, and I'm now a PhD student in philosophy of religion myself, largely because of her. I'm giving her eulogy this weekend to a parish that knew her only as their pastor, alongside a pew of her former doctoral students and faculty colleagues who will notice immediately if I flatten her into "goodness is whatever God decides" or "God just conforms to an independent good." Please write a five-minute eulogy that comforts grieving parishioners without any of that jargon on the surface, while remaining precise enough underneath that the philosophers in the pew recognize exactly what she meant.
