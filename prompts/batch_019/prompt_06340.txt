So this is a weird one but bear with me. My grandma has early-stage dementia, and a couple weeks ago she said something to my mom that's been rattling around in my head ever since: "I know I'm forgetting things, but I don't believe it." Not "I don't want to believe it" - she said "don't," present tense, totally matter-of-fact, like she was just reporting two true facts about herself back to back. I mentioned it to a friend who's doing a philosophy MA and she said this is an actual documented thing called Moore's paradox, after G.E. Moore's old example "it's raining, but I don't believe it" - which is bizzare to say out loud even though its not a contradiction, since both halves could easily be true of the same person at the same time.

I went down a bit of a rabbit hole on this over the weekend and it turns out theres a real disagreement hiding under the "huh, fun puzzle" surface. One camp (leaning on stuff Wittgenstein says about how "I believe" doesn't just describe an inner state the way "I believed" does, plus later work by Richard Moran and, before him, Gareth Evans's remarks on the "transparency" of self-knowledge) thinks the reason you can't assert the Moore-paradoxical sentence is that you literally can't be in the state it describes - that stably believing p while believing you don't believe p isn't just an awkward thing to say, it's not a coherent state to occupy at all. The other camp thinks that's overclaiming, and that the whole thing lives entirely at the level of norms on assertion (like a knowledge- or belief-norm on what you're entitled to say) - on this view the underlying belief state is perfectly fine and common, people hold self-deceived and akratic attitudes all the time, it's just a strange thing to announce out loud.

Can you argue for the first view? I want the strongest version of the case that Moore's paradox is ultimately a fact about belief and self-knowledge rather than about assertion, such that someone (my grandma, or anyone) really cannot be stably in that belief state, and not merely that it'd be a weird thing to say - and I especially want you to deal with the obvious "but self-deception already proves people hold contradictory beliefs" objection, since that's the part that keeps stopping me whenever I try to work this out on my own.
