I'm a junior at my high school and I sit on our Honor Council, the student-run board that hears academic integrity cases and recommends sanctions to the Dean of Students. We have a case coming up this Thursday that's messier than anything I've dealt with before, and I'd like help drafting the recommendation memo I'm supposed to circulate to the other council members beforehand.

Here's the situation. A sophomore, Maya, used ChatGPT to help outline and rephrase parts of a graded history essay on the causes of World War I. She admits this but says she didn't realize it counted as "unauthorized assistance" under our honor code, partly because her teacher had demonstrated ChatGPT in class two weeks earlier as a research tool without ever clarifying where the line was for actual assignments. This is her first offense, and she's on a renewable need-based scholarship; a transcript notation is exactly the kind of thing that could jeopardize its renewal, meaning the "standard" sanction would land on her much harder than it would on most other students here. Our honor code says academic dishonesty "ordinarily results in a recommended two-week suspension and a permanent transcript notation," but allows the Council to recommend something else "if extenuating circumstances warrant." To complicate things further, this is our fifth AI-related case since winter break, and several council members think we need to come down hard on someone soon or the policy will lose all deterrent force — even though the four earlier cases all involved students who submitted essays ChatGPT had generated wholesale, which seems like a much clearer violation than what Maya did.

Could you draft the memo I'll bring to Thursday's meeting? I'd like it to take a clear position — the standard sanction, something reduced, or an alternative sanction entirely — and to genuinely grapple with the idea that Maya deserves to be treated in proportion to what she actually did and knew, rather than being sanctioned mainly to send a message to the rest of the school, while still taking seriously my classmates' worry that going easy on her undermines the whole point of having a policy in the first place. I've read a little about retributivism and consequentialism in an ethics elective and I suspect that tension is basically what's splitting the council, but I might be misapplying the labels, so feel free to correct me if that framing doesn't quite fit. For what it's worth, I originally joined the council because it looked good for college applications, but cases like this are what have actually gotten me interested in the ethics underneath it.
