I'm giving the toast at my father's retirement party. He's spent thirty-eight years as a theoretical physicist working on string theory and its landscape of possible universes—decades of work that has never produced a single testable prediction, and that some of his own colleagues have spent just as long publicly calling unfalsifiable, 'not even wrong.' I never went to college myself, but I've spent years reading popular physics and philosophy of science on my own, largely so I could follow what he and his colleagues were actually arguing about at family gatherings. Half the room will be physicists split down the middle on whether his life's work even counts as science, and the other half will be relatives who've heard some version of that argument every holiday for twenty years without ever quite following it. Please write the toast, built around one good joke about falsifiability that both halves of the room will genuinely laugh at, and make sure it reads as love for a man who gave his career to something he could never prove, not as a verdict on whether he was right to spend it that way.
