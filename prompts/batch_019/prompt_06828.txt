So I'm about two-thirds through drafting a novel set in Weimar between roughly 1805 and 1815, and one of my POV characters is a minor court official who's a little obsessed with the "real" Goethe -- not the Werther-and-Faust Goethe everybody gets taught in school, but Privy Councillor Goethe, the guy quietly running the theatre and the university budget and the roads commission for Duke Carl August while the rest of Europe is on fire. The more research I do for this book (Boyle's big biography, some old Lukács essays I found in a secondhand shop, Goethe's own "Campaign in France" memoir), the more I keep running into one specific disagreement among the people who write about him that I cant settle for myself, and I sort of need to before I can figure out this character's inner life.

Here's the disagreement, as best I can lay it out. One reading -- Lukács is the bluntest version I've found -- says Goethe's famous gradualism (refusing to cheer the French Revolution, sitting out the patriotic fever of the Wars of Liberation while practically every other German writer was cranking out anti-Napoleon verse, openly admiring Napoleon the man even as the wars wrecked the country) was never a real politics at all, just a comfortable court poet protecting his own dependence on aristocratic patronage, with the philosophy bolted on afterward to make it look principled. The other reading -- mostly from Boyle, plus some stuff on Goethe's plant morphology and his fight with Newton over the theory of color that I went down a rabbit hole on -- wants to say the gradualism is continuous with something real and load-bearing in his thought: the conviction, straight out of his own science, that development happens through continuous unfolding and never through leaps, and that this is exactly as true of societies as it is of plants.

Could you write me an essay that actually adjudicates between these two readings rather then sitting on the fence? Weigh the self-interested-courtier account against the coherent-organicist-worldview account and tell me which one the evidence really favors -- or some third option, if "a bit of both" is secretly the honest answer, but if so I need you to specify exactly how they combine rather than just gesturing at complexity. I want something I can actually use to decide how self-aware, or how self-deceived, this character should be about his own hero-worship.
