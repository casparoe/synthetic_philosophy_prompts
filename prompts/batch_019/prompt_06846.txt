This semester I'm taking a seminar on formal epistemology, and we've just finished a unit on self-locating belief and observation selection effects: Bostrom's self-sampling assumption (SSA) versus the self-indication assumption (SIA), and the two thought experiments usually taken to embarrass each view in turn — the Doomsday Argument against SSA, and the Presumptuous Philosopher against SIA. For our short response paper (around 1,500 words, due Friday), I've drafted an argument that these two embarrassments are not actually symmetric, and I would like detailed comments on it before I submit.

The core move in my draft is this: the Presumptuous Philosopher only tells against SIA if there can really be pairs of theories that are, first, equally well confirmed by all the ordinary, non-anthropic evidence, and second, associated with enormously different numbers of observers. I argue that this antecedent is much harder to satisfy in practice than the Doomsday Argument's premises, namely that we don't know the eventual size of the human population and that our own birth rank counts as genuine self-locating evidence. So SSA saddles us with an uncomfortable conclusion about a real, open empirical question, while SIA only saddles us with an uncomfortable conclusion about a scenario that essentially never arises. I conclude that SIA is the more rational principle to hold, even though the Presumptuous Philosopher makes for the flashier objection on paper.

There are three things I would like your comments on. First, whether the empirical premise doing the real work here — that "equally confirmed, wildly different population" theory-pairs are practically negligible — actually survives contact with real cosmology; I have a vague sense that debates around eternal inflation and the so-called measure problem might already instantiate exactly this structure, in which case my whole asymmetry collapses, but I only know that literature secondhand. Second, whether this asymmetry move is already a standard reply in the anthropic-reasoning literature that I am unknowingly reinventing. And third, assuming the argument is basically sound, what you take to be the single strongest objection to it that I should pre-empt before revising.
