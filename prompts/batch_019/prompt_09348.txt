She did research on logical uncertainty and worked adjacent to the group that put out the 2016 "Logical Induction" paper out of MIRI. I spent most of this week rereading the actual paper plus a couple of the old LW explainer posts to prep, and I really don't want the episode to just be the 30-second version everyone gives -- "its basically a prediction market that bets on math problems" -- because that's all that ever seems to surface in interviews with people in this space and its starting to drive me a little nuts.

Could you draft me 8-10 interview questions that push past that? I want at least one question, each with a sharp follow-up keyed to whatever answer she's most likely to give first, on the following: (1) the fact that the actual construction in the paper is computable but nowhere near efficient to run, so in what sense this counts as a "solution" for any real bounded reasoner rather than an idealized existence proof, sort of the way Solomonoff induction is for empirical uncertainty rather than something you could actually run; (2) what philosophical work the "no efficiently-computable trader can exploit the market" clause is really doing here -- is it a principled rationality constraint in the Dutch-book tradition, or more of a technical patch needed to make the fixed-point construction go through at all; (3) the fact that (as I understood the paper, anyway) there's no general computable bound on how fast the market's price on a given sentence converges, so at any finite time you don't actually know how good your current credence is, which to me sounds like it just relocates the logical omniscience problem rather than dissolving it; and (4) the non-uniqueness issue, since apparently tons of different trader populations satisfy the logical induction criterion, so it doesn't pin down a single "correct" credence for something like the Riemann Hypothesis -- which seems like a worse kind of indeterminacy than ordinary Bayesian prior-dependence, not a fix for it. If you can work it in naturally I'd also love one question connecting this back to the self-trust / Löb's-theorem stuff that got MIRI interested in the whole area to begin with, since I've never been totally clear on whether logical induction is meant to actually resolve that obstacle or is just a separate result that happens to share a family tree with it.
