so I've been going down a philosophy-of-mind rabbit hole and hit this idea called "teleosemantics" - basically the claim that a mental state gets its content (what its actually about) from the function it was selected for by evolution. Classic example: a frog's neuron is "about flies" because ancestor frogs whose neurons fired like that in response to small dark moving blobs caught more food and survived, even though the frog will happily snap at a bb pellet shot past it too.

This got me thinking about neural nets. Training via gradient descent is also a selection process of sorts - weight patterns that cut the loss get reinforced over the run, ones that don't get suppressed, over and over, thousands of times. So by that same logic, shouldn't a trained model's internal representations geniunely be "about" whatever they got selected to track, in the same naturalistic sense as the frog's fly-detector, and not just a metaphorical one?

Can you actually argue for that, as hard as you can, instead of hedging? And please deal directly with the obvious objection that gradient descent isn't real selection since there's no population of reproducing individuals - just one network's weights getting nudged around inside a single run. Is that disanalogy actually fatal to the whole comparison, or kind of beside the point?
