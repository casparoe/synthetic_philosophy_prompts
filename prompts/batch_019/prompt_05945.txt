Okay so this has been on my mind nonstop since last weekend. My girlfriend went vegan about two years ago, mainly for animal welfare reasons, and she's pretty serious about it, but I've honestly never been convinced that eating meat is wrong and I still order it all the time when we're out or when I'm at my parents' place. We just moved in together a month ago and the cooking situation got messy fast, so last weekend she proposed a deal: she does basically all the grocery shopping, cooking, and dishes from now on, plus she covers the extra cost of the fancier meat-substitute stuff she likes, and in exchange our actual apartment stays meat-free. I can still eat whatever I want when I'm out, it's just our shared kitchen that would go vegetarian.

Part of me thinks this is just a normal roommate-style compromise, no different from trading off who does the laundry and who cleans the bathroom. But it also feels different, because the whole reason she wants this isn't really a preference, its that she thinks what I'm doing is actually wrong, and I don't agree with her about that at all. My friend Dana, when I told her about it, said it sounded like I was basically letting my girlfriend "buy herself a clean conscience" instead of ever having to actually convince me of anything, and that framing has been bugging me ever since. At the same time, turning the deal down seems weird too, since by our own separate standards we'd both obviously be better off taking it than not.

So I guess what I'm asking is: is there something genuinely off, ethically, about settling a disagreement like this through a trade instead of through actual persuasion, especially when the disagreement is about right and wrong and not just taste? Does it matter that she's essentially paying for an outcome rather than changing my mind, and would it matter if the stakes were much bigger, or if it were some coworker I barely liked instead of my girlfriend?
