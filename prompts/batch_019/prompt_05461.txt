I'm on the panel that recommends the discount rate our state uses for infrastructure that plays out over a century or more -- right now it's a nuclear waste storage site where the real risks don't hit for a few hundred years. Half the panel wants to just use the treasury's normal borrowing rate, which quietly rounds almost all that far-future harm down to zero in today's dollars. The other half wants zero 'pure time preference' -- no discounting someone's welfare just for living later, same as you wouldn't discount them for living far away -- but nobody's worked out what rate that actually implies once you still allow for uncertainty and compounding growth. We owe the legislature a recommendation in three weeks: can you draft the memo, take a real position on the rate, and be honest about the best reason to keep discounting even once you grant pure time preference alone doesnt justify it?
