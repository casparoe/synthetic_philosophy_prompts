So a while back I fell down a rabbit hole after seeing the "a white horse is not a horse" thing mentioned as some ancient Chinese logic puzzle on a random blog. Everyone just quotes that one line like it's a cute gotcha, but when I actually tracked down a translation of the Gongsun Long text itself instead of just the summary, I found out theres a whole separate dialogue by the same guy, the "hard and white" one, about a stone thats both hard and white. His argument there is that the hardness and the whiteness are actually two separate things rather than one stone with two properties sitting on it, and the reason he gives is basically that you only ever find out about the hardness by touching and the whiteness by looking, and since those are two different senses, the properties themselves must be separate too. That felt like a really different kind of move to me than the horse thing, which seems to be more about messing with categories and what counts as a "member" of the group "horse," not about which sense organ you happened to use.

The one friend I have who studied philosophy in college just said "oh that's just his whole universals thing" and moved on, and I've seen a couple of write-ups online make the same move, treating both dialogues as basically one trick (universals exist on their own, apart from the physical things they show up in) run twice on two different examples. But the more I sit with it the less convinced I am that the two arguments really get you to "separateness" the same way, and I don't know enough logic or philosophy to tell if I'm onto something real here or just being dense about it.
