Compare two applications of the "Ulysses contract" idea: (1) a psychiatric advance directive in which a person, while competent, authorizes involuntary treatment for a future crisis during which they are expected to refuse it, and (2) a constitution in which a founding generation entrenches certain rights or procedures against revision by ordinary future majorities. Both are routinely described as instances of rational self-binding on the model of Ulysses and the Sirens, but in which of the two cases is that description most apt, and in which does it break down? Justify your answer with reference to who is doing the binding, who is bound, and how easily each arrangement can be revised by the party it constrains.
