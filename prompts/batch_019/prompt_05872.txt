My writing group's resident math nerd said this basically collapses "perfectly rational" and "already knows the answer to every decidable question" into the same property, which honestly bugs me more than it seems to bug most smart-AI fiction.

What I actually want from you isn't a general explanation (I think I've got the gist) but one specific, carefully worked-out example of the problem actually biting: some small, explicitly stated set of premises where, according to the standard closure-under-entailment picture, a perfectly rational believer of those premises would already have to "know" some further, non-obvious fact — even though working that fact out took genuine cleverness, or was historically hard-won, or would take a real computer a long time to churn through. I don't want "she'd know all of math," I want one concrete case I can point to and say "see, this is the gap, and its a little disturbing."
