I've been down a rabbit hole this week for a science mag piece on falsifiability, and I keep getting stuck on this one episode that barely anyone outside philosophy-of-biology circles seems to know about. Everyone remembers Popper for the "falsifiability" soundbite, but almost nobody brings up that he spent years insisting natural selection was basically a tautology in a lab coat -- his "metaphysical research programme" line from Unended Quest -- and then more or less recanted at his 1978 Darwin lecture, though even that recantation reads to me like he's still holding something back, like he never fully let go of the worry that "survival of the fittest" just means "survival of the survivors." I mentioned this to a biologist friend at a dinner party and she got weirdly heated about it, which is exactly the kind of reaction I want to bottle for readers.

So could you write me a dialogue, maybe 700ish words, between two contemporary philosophers of biology hashing this out directly -- not a historical re-enactment of Popper arguing with himself, but two smart people picking up the argument where he left it. One should think Popper's original instinct was right and the "recantation" gave away the store: that fitness can't be specified independently of actual survival and reproduction without smuggling in the very thing you're supposed to be testing, so most claimed "tests" of natural selection are really just redescriptions after the fact. The other should think that's a slight-of-hand that ignores how selection is actually tested in practice -- population genetics, experimental evolution, predictions that were genuinely risky and could have come out wrong. I don't want either one to just restate their opening position and wait their turn -- I want the tautology defender to actually have to deal with a specific predictive success the other side raises, and I want the other side to concede at least one real point before pushing back further. Both of them should clearly know this literature cold, not just be mouthpieces for a Wikipedia summary of the guy.
