My friend Theo and I got into a whole thing last night after his book club, about immigration ethics of all things, and somehow it ended up being about Michael Dummett — the Oxford logician, the Frege guy. Apparently later in life he wrote this book, On Immigration and Refugees, where his main line is that people basically have an unlimited right to emigrate, and asylum seekers have close to an unconditional right to be let in — except for one carve-out he calls "submersion." A country — he sometimes says "the indigenous population" — has a right not to be swamped, but according to him that only kicks in if immigration comes in so fast and so large that the existing society can't do its normal thing of gradually absorbing newcomers and being changed by them in turn. Theo's whole point was that this is just the "I'm not racist, but" move dressed up in careful philosopher-speak, and that it's extra rich coming from a guy who apparently used to drive to airports himself to intervene for detained immigrants and co-founded an immigrants rights group. I actually think there might be a real, non-panicky version of the worry in there somewhere — that sheer pace and scale can overwhelm schools and services in a way that's bad for everyone, newcomers included — but I couldn't construct a version of it on the spot that Theo couldn't immediately relabel as submersion talk.

This isn't just a bar argument, unfortunately, because I'm on my town's refugee resettlement advisory board (small town, maybe 4,200 people), and next month we owe the county an actual recommendation on a case that's basically our argument with the numbers filled in. A resettlement agency wants to place around 900 refugees here over the next 14 months, since a reopened plant needs workers and there's a big block of housing sitting empty. About half the board wants to just say yes, full stop, on the strength of the refugees' situation; the other half wants to cap it or spread it over several years, pointing out that's definately a 20%+ jump in population for a town with one understaffed school district and basically zero ESL infrastructure. To make it messier, a couple of the older members keep bringing up that the town itself nearly doubled in about three years back in the 60s, when the original plant first opened and pulled in workers from way outside the county — some people treat that as proof it always works out fine given enough time, others remember that whole decade as genuinely rough on everybody.

Could you write the actual memo I'd circulate to the board before that meeting? I need it to land on one concrete recommendation — yes, no, or some specific third option — with a real timeframe attached, not just "more study needed." And I need it to take the pace/scale worry seriously as possibly a distinct consideration from ordinary xenophobia, rather than just dismissing it outright, while also being honest with the board about how much of it actually survives once you try to state it as a general principle, given that even Dummett himself apparently could only defend it as real but very narrow.
