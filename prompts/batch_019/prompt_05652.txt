Last night a friend and I got into a long, unresolved argument about the Judy Benjamin problem. In van Fraassen's setup, Judy is lost somewhere in a region split into Red and Blue territory, each of which is further divided into a Headquarters Company area and a Second Company area, and she starts out with a uniform credence over the four resulting cells. A garbled radio message tells her only that, conditional on her being in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 that she is in the Headquarters Company area; nothing in the message compares Red to Blue directly. My friend insisted that any reasonable way of folding this into her prior has to leave her credence in Red versus Blue exactly where it started, at even odds, since the message is silent about Blue. I argued that this is not what happens once you update by minimizing relative entropy (Kullback-Leibler divergence) subject to that conditional holding, and that the resulting shift in her Red-versus-Blue credence isn't just some artifact of the technique we happened to pick. Neither of us budged, so, without walking me through the Lagrangian optimization itself, could you give me a gut-level explanation of why satisfying a constraint on a conditional probability by minimizing relative entropy ends up moving credence on a proposition that the evidence seems, on its face, not to touch at all, and why ordinary Jeffrey conditionalization can't just be applied here to sidestep the whole issue?
