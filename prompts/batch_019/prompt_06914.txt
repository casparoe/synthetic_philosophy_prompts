I'm the student representative on my university's Honor Council, and this term I'm taking a seminar on epistemology where we just discussed the dogmatism paradox that Gilbert Harman poses in his book Thought: if you know that P, you already know that any future evidence against P must be misleading, so you seem entitled to discount such evidence in advance rather than weigh it on its merits. Two months ago the Council voted unanimously, on the strength of a signed confession, to find a student responsible for fabricating data in a lab report and suspended her for a semester; she has since retracted the confession as coerced and submitted a corroborating sworn statement from her lab partner. Two Council members argue that since we already know she's guilty, we're entitled to treat this new material as unreliable without close examination, and that reopening cases whenever sanctioned students produce new "evidence" would make our findings worthless. Please draft the Council's internal memo, due before next Tuesday's session, recommending whether to grant a full rehearing: it should reach a concrete decision and take the finality worry seriously rather than simply insisting that we must always follow the evidence.
