For my AP European History class, we're closing out the Scientific Revolution unit with a "living history" Q&A panel, where each of us researches a figure and then has to field questions from the rest of the class while staying in character. I've been assigned to interview whoever ends up playing Johannes Kepler, and since I already know almost everyone else in my group is going to ask him about the elliptical orbits or the fight over heliocentrism, I'd rather spend my turn on something I find more interesting and much less discussed: the fact that Kepler made part of his living casting horoscopes and clearly took some reformed version of astrology seriously, even though he mocked most of what professional astrologers of his day actually did, tried to stake out a middle position in a book called Tertius Interveniens, and is reportedly on record calling astrology something like the foolish daughter who nonetheless keeps her wise mother, astronomy, fed.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions that get past the "so you don't actually believe in astrology, right?" surface level and force him to defend the specifics of his own position? I'd like at least one question on why he threw out the zodiac signs and houses as essentially arbitrary human conventions while keeping, and even expanding, the "aspects," on the grounds — laid out in Harmonices Mundi — that only certain angles count as harmonic; one on how the soul is supposed to actually register those angles, and whether that story still holds together once you're a committed heliocentrist working with angles that were originally justified from an Earth-centered picture of the sky; one on the tension between the "foolish daughter" line and his continuing to sell detailed horoscopes, including the extended prognostication he prepared for Wallenstein that supposedly flagged serious danger right around the time Wallenstein was actually assassinated; and one closing question about what, if anything, he thinks could count as evidence against his own reformed astrology, as opposed to evidence against just the "vulgar" kind he was so eager to distance himself from. For each question, please add a short note on the answer he would most plausibly give and a sharper follow-up that keeps him from resting on it.
