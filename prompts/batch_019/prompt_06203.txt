I'm taking a seminar on formal epistemology this term, and I'm also the student representative on my university's Academic Integrity Board, which is drafting a policy for cases where the only evidence is a score from a well-calibrated AI-writing detector: of every hundred essays it flags at "98% confidence," about ninety-eight really are AI-written. Several faculty argue that this alone justifies finding each flagged student responsible for a violation; others want the score barred from ever being sufficient by itself, since treating it as sufficient means we already know, before hearing a single case, that some students found responsible under the rule are actually innocent. Even our Chair concedes this is almost certainly true of the Board's record over the years, though she stands behind every verdict she's personally signed. Draft the short memo I'll circulate before our next meeting: recommend an evidentiary standard the Board can actually apply, and engage seriously with the "calibration is enough" position rather than dismissing it.
