Question (10 points total, allocated (a) 3, (b) 3, (c) 2, (d) 2): Using the apparatus of two-dimensional semantics, explain (a) why "Water is H2O" is standardly classified as necessary yet a posteriori, and (b) why Kaplan's sentence "I am here now" is classified as contingent yet a priori, making explicit use of the primary/epistemic and secondary/subjunctive intension distinction throughout. Then (c) say how this distinction relates to Kaplan's own character/content framework, and (d) briefly note how it bears on Kripke's discussion of the necessary a posteriori in Naming and Necessity.

Student's answer:

Two-dimensionalism assigns each sentence two intensions, evaluated relative to two different kinds of index. The secondary (or subjunctive) intension is a function from possible worlds, considered as counterfactual, to extensions; it is what ordinary modal logic tracks and is exactly what delivers Kripke's rigid designation. The primary (or epistemic) intension is a function from worlds considered as actual to extensions; its value at a world can be worked out just by supposing that world to be actual, without further empirical investigation, which is why it tracks a priori status. This is, in effect, Kaplan's notion of character carried over from the theory of indexicals to modal semantics generally: character is likewise a function from ways a context might actually be to a content, so "character" and "primary intension" name the same construction, only Kaplan introduced it for demonstratives specifically and later two-dimensionalists generalized it to every expression.

On this picture, "water is H2O" is necessary but a posteriori because its two intensions diverge. The secondary intension is necessary: once we fix that the watery stuff around us is H2O, "water" rigidly picks out H2O at every counterfactual world, so there is no possible world where water fails to be H2O. The primary intension, by contrast, is contingent: prior to empirical investigation, the actual world might have turned out to be a Twin-Earth-style scenario in which the clear, potable liquid is XYZ rather than H2O, so the primary intension is true at some epistemically possible scenarios and false at others. It is a posteriori because settling which scenario is actual takes empirical work. This is precisely the strategy Kripke uses in Naming and Necessity to reconcile the necessity of true identity statements flanked by rigid designators with the fact that such statements are knowable only a posteriori, as with "Hesperus is Phosphorus": the two intensions let us say that the modal and epistemic dimensions of such statements are tracking two different things.

"I am here now" exhibits the mirror-image profile. Its secondary intension is contingent, since, holding fixed who is speaking, where, and when, that very individual could easily have been elsewhere at another time; the proposition expressed is not true at every counterfactual circumstance. Its primary intension, however, is necessary: for every scenario that might turn out to be actual, whoever is speaking will, trivially, be there and then when they speak, so the sentence is true no matter which scenario is actual. That is why it is knowable a priori despite expressing, in any given context, a merely contingent proposition.
