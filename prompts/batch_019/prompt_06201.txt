Aquinas's discussion of tyranny in his commentary on the Sentences seems to me refreshingly unambiguous: since a tyrant's rule is a corruption of true dominion rather than an instance of it, whoever kills him cannot be guilty of sedition, and nothing in this reasoning restricts that license to any particular class of persons. I have always read De Regimine Principum as simply restating the same doctrine in more explicitly political language, so that, taken together, the two texts establish tyrannicide as a standing right of any subject whatsoever. For a lecture on medieval resistance theory, which passages would you point to as the clearest support for this reading, and how would you situate it relative to Locke's right of revolution?
