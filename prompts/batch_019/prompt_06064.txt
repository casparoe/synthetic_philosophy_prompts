Write a dialogue between two philosophers arguing about whether Spinoza's politics actually supports democracy or secretly undercuts it. One thinks Spinoza's naturalism -- right just IS power, nothing more -- gives democracy its strongest possible foundation, since you don't need any fictions like natural rights or a social contract, just an honest look at how power is actually distributed among people. The other thinks thats exactly the problem, and brings up the unfinished Political Treatise, where Spinoza uses that same "right = power" reasoning to argue women should be excluded from citizenship -- not as some random lapse, they say, but as proof that naturalism can't ground any principled commitment to democratic inclusion. Have them really engage with each other's strongest move instead of just repeating themselves, and let the fact that Spinoza died before finishing that chapter hang over the conversation a bit. Don't make either one the obvious winner.
