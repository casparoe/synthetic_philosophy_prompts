Got into it with my friend Dana last night over moral worth -- specifically the Huckleberry Finn case, where Huck helps Jim escape even though he sincerely believes, given his upbringing, that he's committing a sin and is going to hell for it. I said that's actually a purer case of moral worth than acting from an explicit principle would be, since Huck's responding directly to Jim's humanity rather than to any rule. Dana said no: without the thought "this is what duty requires" actually doing the motivating work, its just moral luck that he did right, and it shouldn't fully count to his credit. We argued for like an hour about the fetishism-of-duty objection and whether the standard Kantian comebacks to it secretly give up the game, and neither of us budged.
