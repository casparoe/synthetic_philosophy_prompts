So I've been down a rabbit hole of old MIRI "agent foundations" stuff on the Löbian obstacle for a formal epistemology reading group, and something's bugging me about how its usually framed. People treat it as a narrow problem for proof-based AI agents trying to trust a successor running the same proof system, but I don't see why it doesn't generalize way further than that. Can you make the strongest case you can that *any* reasoner sophisticated enough to model its own reasoning faces some version of this same obstacle to fully trusting its own future judgments, and that we don't notice it in ourselves only because our self-model is inconsistent or incomplete, rather than because we've somehow dodged Löb's theorem? I want the actual mechanics of why the naive move — "I trust my future self because I'd only act on judgments I can justify, and future-me reasons the same way" — breaks down, not just the AI-safety headline version of the story.
