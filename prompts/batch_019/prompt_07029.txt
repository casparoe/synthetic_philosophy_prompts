So for a piece I'm working on about what general relativity actually tells us about the reality of space and time, I've been going down the rabbit hole (pun intended) of Einstein's old "hole argument" and the substantivalism/relationalism fight it kicked off in philosophy of physics. I think I've got the basic mechanics down: take a solution to the field equations, apply a diffeomorphism that's the identity everywhere except inside some little "hole" region, drag the metric along with it, and you get a second solution that agrees with the first everywhere outside the hole but disagrees inside it. If spacetime points are genuine individuals with their own identity, that looks like a case of totally unmotivated indeterminism. What's been messing with my head is the standard substantivalist comeback: give up "haecceitism," i.e. deny that points have any identity over and above the qualitative role they play, and suddenly the two solutions just describe the same possible world, so the indeterminism evaporates. A physicist I interviewed shrugged this off as basically relationalism with extra steps; a philosopher friend insists there's still something the substantivalist keeps that the relationalist doesn't, she just couldn't quite tell me what over coffee.

Could you write me a dialogue between a "sophisticated" (anti-haecceitist) substantivalist and a relationalist about exactly that question: whether, once the substantivalist drops haecceitism to dodge the hole argument, there's any real metaphysical daylight left between the two positions, or whether it's a merely verbal dispute at that point. Maybe frame it as the two of them arguing in front of a diagram of the hole region, each pushing the other on concrete cases (e.g. does it matter for what counts as "the same possible world," or for how many distinct possibilities there are, or for what explains what) rather then just trading slogans. I want them evenly matched -- no easy knock-out, each should have to actually answer the other's best objection instead of talking past it.
