I'm my high school's valedictorian, and I want to build my graduation speech around Alfred North Whitehead's writing on education: his idea of "inert ideas" -- facts you can produce on a test but never actually use or connect to anything -- and what he called the rhythm of education, the cycle from Romance to Precision to Generalization that he thought real learning has to move through. My English teacher assigned us an excerpt of his a couple of years ago, and it named something about my own schooling that I'd never had words for. The audience will include a few teachers who would recognize Whitehead by name and a lot of relatives who have never heard of him and would be lost by jargon. I don't want a speech that just complains about standardized testing, and I especially don't want it to be the kind of string of inert, forgettable lines that graduation speeches are famous for. Could you write about five minutes of remarks that genuinely uses his framework to say something specific about what our education was and wasn't, and that lands somewhere hopeful rather than bitter?
