For a dissertation chapter on Nietzsche's genealogical method, I need an essay addressing a specific interpretive tension in the Second Essay of *On the Genealogy of Morality*. At GM II 12–13, Nietzsche insists that the origin (Entstehung) of a practice such as punishment is strictly independent of its later purpose or use (Zweck), since any custom, once established, is continually seized upon and repurposed by new configurations of will to power that have nothing to do with why it first arose; he goes so far as to claim that only what has no history can be defined. Sympathetic readers invoke this origin/function distinction to shield the genealogical method from the charge that it commits a version of the genetic fallacy: exposing "good and evil" as originating in priestly ressentiment supposedly tells us nothing about whether that valuation is justified today, since Nietzsche himself denies that origin fixes function. Yet his polemic throughout the First and Second Essays seems to depend on tracing valuations to disreputable origins precisely in order to discredit them, while noble/master valuations are treated as though their equally contingent origins were comparatively innocuous. Write an essay weighing whether the origin/function distinction can be squared with Nietzsche's actual genealogical practice, or whether it is instead an unstable methodological device that his own rhetoric routinely violates. Reach and defend a specific verdict rather than concluding that it depends on which passages one emphasizes.
