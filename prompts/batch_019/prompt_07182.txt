I spent much of last evening in a long argument with a friend about self-driving cars and autonomous weapons, and in particular about whether the standard proposals for closing the "responsibility gap" actually solve the problem, or merely substitute an easier one in its place. He maintained that the gap is closed as soon as some party can be made to pay for the harm; I argued that compensation, public accountability, and genuine culpability are distinct things, and that most proposals only secure the first two.

I would like your considered judgment on this. Please rank the following proposed responses to the autonomous-systems responsibility gap according to how well each actually closes the culpability gap specifically: that is, how well it identifies an agent who is fairly liable to blame or punishment for the harm, as opposed to merely compensating victims, diffusing accountability across a network, or managing public perception.

- Meaningful human control requirements imposed on system design and deployment
- Strict, no-fault liability modeled on product liability law
- Electronic legal personhood granted to the system itself
- Military-style command responsibility imposed on operators
- Insurance or compensation pools funded by manufacturers
- Tracing culpability back to negligence at the design or testing stage
- Treating the gap as genuinely unbridgeable and revising retributive practice accordingly
- Distributing responsibility across the whole human-machine system, understood as a kind of group agent
