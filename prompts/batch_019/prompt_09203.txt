I have been asked to give a guest lecture in a graduate seminar that my department co-teaches with computer science on the philosophical foundations of AI safety, and before I stand in front of an audience that will include several unforgiving formal-methods students, I want to make sure I have correctly reconstructed what strikes me as the central argument for AI risk.

As I understand it, the argument begins from the coherence arguments for expected utility maximization: the familiar money-pump and Dutch-book results, which show that an agent with cyclic preferences, or with credences violating the probability axioms, can be exploited out of arbitrary amounts of resources by a sufficiently clever counterparty. Since we should expect any sufficiently capable, self-improving system to notice and eliminate such exploitable patterns in its own behavior, it seems to follow that such a system will come to act as an expected-utility maximizer with respect to some fixed set of ends. Once that much is granted, the instrumental-convergence considerations that Bostrom and Omohundro made familiar - resource acquisition, self-preservation, resistance to having one's objectives revised - apply to almost any set of ends whatsoever, since these are useful means nearly regardless of the further end in view. On this reconstruction, coherence itself, quite independently of what a system's actual objectives turn out to be, already secures the dangerous behavioral profile that worries AI safety researchers; the specific content of the objective looks like a comparatively minor consideration layered on top of a risk that coherence alone generates.

Given that, I find myself puzzled by the shape of the field as it actually is: why do alignment researchers devote so much technical and philosophical effort to getting the content of a system's objectives right - through reward modeling, constitutional methods, debate, and related approaches - rather than treating that as distinctly secondary to the more fundamental, coherence-driven mechanism I have just sketched? I would welcome your help sharpening the argument before Thursday's session.
