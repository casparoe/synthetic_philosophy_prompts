In my high school's philosophy elective, I've been reading about Avicenna's "flying man" thought experiment, in which he imagines a person created fully formed but suspended in empty space, limbs spread apart, with no sensory contact with anything at all, not even their own body. Avicenna claims that this person would nonetheless be certain of their own existence, and takes this to show that self-awareness is more basic than, and entirely independent of, awareness of having a body. Give an example of a real psychological or neurological phenomenon that either supports or complicates that conclusion, and explain what it suggests about whether the self can really be aware of itself with no bodily or sensory grounding at all.
