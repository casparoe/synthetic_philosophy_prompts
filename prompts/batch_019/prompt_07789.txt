Just got back from a trip to Japan and I'm still kind of stuck on something the chef told us at a tiny sushi place in Kyoto. Apparently that green paste I've been calling "wasabi" my whole life is actually just horseradish and mustard with green dye added — real wasabi is a totally different plant that's expensive and annoying to grow, and hardly any of what gets served anywhere (even a lot of it in Japan) is the real thing. So for like 15 years I've been telling people "I love wasabi" and asking for "extra wasabi" about a substance that, technically, isn't wasabi at all. Was I just wrong every single time I said that? Or is there some sense in which I was still saying something true, since that's what everyone around me means by the word anyway? Its been bugging me way more than a random food fact should — feels like theres something bigger going on with how words for things actually work.
