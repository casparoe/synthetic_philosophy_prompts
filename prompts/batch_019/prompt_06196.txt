For a philosophy of biology reading group, I have been working through Stephen Jay Gould and Richard Lewontin's 1979 paper on the "adaptationist programme," along with some of the secondary literature on their spandrels-of-San-Marco analogy. As I understand their central argument, evolutionary biologists have a habit of decomposing an organism into a list of discrete traits and then inventing a plausible-sounding selective story for each one in isolation, without seriously testing that story against alternatives such as developmental constraint, pleiotropy, or drift, and without asking whether the trait might simply be a structural byproduct, or "spandrel," of selection acting on something else entirely, regardless of how useful the trait might look today. Since these adaptationist narratives are so easy to construct after the fact and so rarely subjected to real falsification, it seems to me that Gould and Lewontin's critique licenses a fairly strong methodological rule: for any given trait, especially in a field like evolutionary psychology, the responsible default assumption should be that the trait is a non-adaptive byproduct, with the burden of proof falling entirely on whoever wants to claim it is a genuine adaptation shaped by natural selection for its current use.

When I proposed this as a general heuristic to my reading group, for sorting credible adaptationist claims from mere "just-so stories," one member objected that this simply inverts the Panglossian error Gould and Lewontin were diagnosing, trading one unfalsifiable default for another. That objection strikes me as too quick, since the whole point of their critique was that biologists had been helping themselves to selection as an explanation without evidence, and correcting that bias by shifting the default toward non-adaptation seems like the natural remedy rather than a symmetrical mistake of its own. Could you help settle this dispute for us, and, assuming my heuristic holds up, help me sharpen it into a rule I could actually apply when I encounter adaptationist claims in the evolutionary psychology literature?
