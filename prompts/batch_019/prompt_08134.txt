I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, but I've gotten interested in philosophy-of-religion debates in my spare time. Lately I keep running into the Hartle-Hawking "no-boundary" proposal, where time near the Big Bang behaves like a curved surface that tapers off smoothly instead of starting at a sharp edge, meaning there may never have been a genuine first moment. Since the cosmological argument for God's existence seems to rest entirely on the premise that the universe began to exist, it seems like a model like this would knock the argument out completely: no beginning, no need for a first cause. Yet several philosophers who specialize in this argument don't treat a beginningless universe as a serious problem for it, which confuses me. Am I missing something about how the argument is actually supposed to work?
