ok so I've been prepping for my metaethics comps and I can't shake this worry about Gibbard's solution to Frege-Geach. His move in Wise Choices, Apt Feelings is to fatten up the objects of normative attitudes into hyperplans -- maximally specific plans for living -- so that accepting or rejecting a normative sentence can be modeled with basically the same possible-worlds-style apparatus we use for ordinary descriptive content, which is supposed to let embedding and validity "come along for free." But doesn't that just walk straight into Dreier's creeping minimalism problem? If hyperplan-content behaves exactly like truth-conditional content, in what sense is Gibbard's view still a geniunely non-cognitivist theory, rather than a notational variant of some deflationary cognitivism that happens to talk about plans instead of facts? Can you argue FOR the claim that Gibbard's hyperplan machinery, precisely because it solves the logical half of Frege-Geach, thereby looses whatever theoretical advantage expressivism was supposed to have over cognitivism in the first place? Feel free to bring in how Blackburn's tree-structured attitudes or Schroeder's "being for" proposal try to dodge this same trap, if it helps sharpen the case. I want an actual sustained argument for the thesis, not a survey of both sides.
