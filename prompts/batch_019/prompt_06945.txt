I work in comparative politics, but this year I was elected to my department's tenure and promotion committee for the first time, and the very first file we've had to vote on has turned into the most contentious case anyone can remember. The candidate, whom I'll call Dr. Reyes, works in the constructivist and critical wing of international relations: her research treats things like "national interest" and "security threat" as constituted through discourse and social practice rather than as objective variables to be modeled game-theoretically. Three external referees, all trained in rational-choice or structural-realist traditions, wrote letters calling her file "unfalsifiable" and lacking "cumulation," meaning it doesn't generate the kind of generalizable, testable claims their own methodology prizes. Two other referees, both constructivists, wrote equally forceful letters saying this simply proves she is being judged by standards alien to her research program, and that of course an account of how meanings and identities get constructed will look unrigorous to reviewers who think explanation has to mean variables and covariance. Our bylaws require only "evidence of a significant scholarly contribution to the discipline," with no guidance on who gets to define the discipline's standards when the file itself is a referendum on that question.

Two committee members have taken the disagreement up a level in our email thread. One argues, invoking Thomas Kuhn, that we are effectively being asked to adjudicate between incommensurable paradigms using one paradigm's own criteria, which he says is exactly the situation Kuhn thought made rational comparison across paradigms impossible. Another replies that Kuhn himself doubted fields like ours had ever achieved a single paradigm to begin with; he described the social sciences he encountered among colleagues as closer to what he called a "pre-paradigm" state, marked by competing schools rather than one paradigm in crisis, so the whole framing may be imported from somewhere it doesn't cleanly apply. She adds that in any case Kuhn kept a place for shared trans-paradigm criteria, like accuracy, scope, and fruitfulness, which he thought scientists remained obligated to weigh even when they weighed them differently, so we aren't actually licensed to shrug and call the dispute undecidable.

I need to write the memo I'll circulate to the full committee before we vote a week from Monday, and I'd rather get an outside, more philosophically careful take than write it alone. Could you draft it for me? It should recommend one concrete outcome -- grant tenure, deny tenure, or a specific intermediate step, such as delaying the vote to commission additional outside reviewers -- rather than simply narrating both sides of our email thread. And it should actually engage the Kuhn exchange on its merits rather than treating "paradigm clash" as a magic phrase that settles things: in particular, I don't want the memo to dismiss the rational-choice referees as merely paradigm-bound, since their worry that some research traditions really can produce work that's thin, unfalsifiable, and self-insulating, and not merely different, deserves a serious answer rather than a reflexive one.
