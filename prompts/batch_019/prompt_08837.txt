Eliezer Yudkowsky's informal case that this dynamic should push sufficiently capable agents toward expected-utility-maximizing, and hence more single-mindedly goal-directed, behavior drew substantial pushback from people like Rohin Shah, who argued that coherence alone gets you nothing resembling a simple or legible goal. More recently, philosophers such as Johan Gustafsson have tightened the money-pump argument so that it targets mere incompleteness rather than requiring outright cyclic preferences, while others working on proposals like the "shutdown problem" for AI agents have tried to show how an agent might retain some deliberately incomplete preferences without thereby handing an exploiter a free lunch.
