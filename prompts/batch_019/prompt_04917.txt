I've spent weeks stuck on whether to commit to my school's robotics team or its music program next year, and "just make a pro/con list" hasn't helped, since the two options don't sit on any single scale yet neither one obviously beats the other. I've since read about Ruth Chang's argument that choices like this are hard not because we're missing information or because the options are simply incomparable, but because two options can stand in a fourth relation besides "better than," "worse than," and "equally good": being "on a par." Since my own case is basically her lawyer-versus-clarinetist example with the names changed, could you invent a genuinely different hard-choice scenario and use something like her small-improvement argument -- showing that a small improvement to one option, one that doesn't change the basic kind of value it offers, still fails to make it determinately better than the other -- to demonstrate that the two options in your example are truly on a par rather than merely roughly equal?
