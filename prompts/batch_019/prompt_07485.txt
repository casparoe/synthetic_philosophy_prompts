I've been reading LessWrong and the decision-theory corners of the rationalist blogosphere since roughly the Sequences era, and a couple of years ago that turned into a small interview podcast I co-host with a friend from my old grad-school reading group. The audience is small but demanding: people who already know what a Newcomb problem is and will notice immediately if a guest is allowed to coast on a stock answer.

Next week's guest is a decision theorist who has spent most of her career defending updateless decision theory against just about every objection that's come out of the LessWrong and Alignment Forum literature on Wei Dai's original proposal. She has a habit, whenever someone raises the standard complaint about counterfactual mugging — that paying up in the branch where you get nothing back can't be rational — of saying some version of "that's just your updated self trying to renegotiate a deal it already struck" and moving on to the next question. I'd rather she not get to use that line again without being pushed on it.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions that don't let her simply restate the view? I want at least one question that presses directly on that stock reply rather than letting it stand as a settled answer, one on where the prior an updateless agent is supposed to be updateless "with respect to" is actually supposed to come from, given that no embedded agent ever occupies a genuine pre-evidential vantage point, one on whether treating one's own source code as subject to counterfactuals is coherent at all or just a useful fiction, and one on the so-called commitment-races worry — that agents who reason this way have an incentive to lock in policies before they know enough to choose them well. For each question, add a short follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, the way a good interviewer would have it prepared in advance rather than improvising on the spot.
