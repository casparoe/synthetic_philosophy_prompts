Had a long, kind of pointless argument with a friend last night about Tolstoy's What is Art? It started because I brought up that if you actually take his 'infection' theory seriously — art succeeds when it sincerely transmits a specific feeling that 'infects' the audience, and how well it does that comes down to the individuality, clarity, and sincerity of the feeling — then a ton of canonical stuff, including his own big novels and things like Wagner's operas and Beethoven's late work, comes out looking like technically impressive fakes that only seem to work because critics and habit have trained people to feel moved. She said thats just proof the theory is bogus, cooked up backwards to justify his guilt about his own career after his religious conversion. I wasnt ready to concede that, but couldn't really defend it in the moment either, and we never got anywhere. Can you write a short story — actual scenes and characters, not people reciting theory at each other — where someone applies the infection theory seriously and consistently enough that they end up at an uncomfortable conclusion about a work (maybe even their own) that they'd always taken for granted as good art?
