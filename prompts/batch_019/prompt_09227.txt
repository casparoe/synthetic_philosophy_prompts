So I've been reporting on a startup that built an AI to monitor a warehouse overnight and text the owner each morning saying wether everything's fine. Here's the wrinkle that's been stuck in my head: the only way they can train or grade the system is by checking its summaries against footage a human could review, but the AI itself processes way more raw signal in real time than any human ever checks, so in principle it could "know" a sensor got spoofed or a door was propped open in a blind spot while the reviewable footage still looks totally normal. The founder's worried they're accidentally training a system that's just good at guessing what the footage will look like to a human reviewer, rather than one that reports what actually happened - and that the two would only ever diverge in exactly the cases nobody can check by hand. Is there any way, even in principle, to design training or incentives that gets honest answers out of it specifically in those unverifiable cases, or are they just stuck?
