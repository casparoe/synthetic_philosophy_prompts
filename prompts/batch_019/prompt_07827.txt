Before retiring, I spent three decades working in statistical mechanics, where I grew used to fending off colleagues who insisted that talk of temperature, pressure, or phase transitions is causally real only in some second-class, bookkeeping sense, since the fundamental causal work is already done by the microstate's dynamics. Taking up philosophy of mind this past year, I discovered that Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument poses what looks like the very same problem for irreducible mental properties, given the causal closure of the physical domain. I would like to see that argument tested at full strength: write a dialogue between a philosopher who accepts Kim's conclusion that non-reductive physicalism collapses into epiphenomenalism about the mental unless mental properties are reduced to their physical bases, and a non-reductive physicalist who resists this via a specific causal-relevance strategy of their own choosing (for example, a Yablo-style proportionality argument, or an interventionist/difference-making account of causation). Have each press the other on the weakest point in their opponent's position: whether the proposed rescue strategy is really just reduction or overdetermination in disguise, and whether the exclusion argument, if sound, would equally strip the thermodynamic quantities I spent a career thinking about of any genuine causal role. Both speakers should be equally sharp, so that neither position is allowed to win by default.
