The part that clicked for me is the semantic externalism move: if you'd literally been a brain in a vat since birth, your word "vat" would only ever have been causally hooked up to the simulated vat-image or whatever electrical impulses are feeding you, never to an actual vat, so when you assert "I am a brain in a vat" the sentence comes out false (or fails to even express the worry you meant it to) pretty much no matter what. My professor's whole point is that this is supposed to be a way more interesting response to skepticism than "well, you can't rule it out," because the supposition undercuts itself the moment you try to state it.
