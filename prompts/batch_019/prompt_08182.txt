Okay so I had this whole argument with my friend Jake last night that kinda spiraled and neither of us backed down, so now I want an actual outside verdict instead of just agreeing to disagree over beers. It started with the classic peacock's-tail/handicap-principle example. Jake's position is that the tail HAS to be a genuine, ongoing burden on the peacock wearing it right now -- it has to really make him more visible to predators and worse at getting away -- because otherwise a low-quality male could just grow one too and the whole signal falls apart. That's basically the textbook Zahavi story and honestly it sounded right to me too at first. But then I half-remembered something from an evolutionary bio class (or maybe it was a paper building on Grafen's game-theory models, I can't find it again) that seemed to say the actual math doesn't require the tail to be a severe, crippling burden on the honest signaler at all. What has to be true is just that the SAME tail would be relatively more costly for a low-quality male to grow than for a high-quality one -- it's the cost gradient across quality levels that does the honesty-enforcing work, not the absolute size of whatever cost the honest peacock actually eats. So the "it has to really hurt to be credible" framing might just be a popular oversimplification of something that's actually more about relative cost than absolute cost.

Jake says I'm being pedantic and that "a cost is a cost, who cares how you slice it," but I think it's actually a bigger deal than that, because pretty much every pop-science retelling of costly signaling (and a ton of the "costly signaling" arguments people make about human stuff -- big weddings, needlessly expensive degrees, status goods) leans entirely on the "it must genuinely hurt the signaler" framing. There's also this other wrinkle we got into where some perfectly honest signals -- like a red deer's roar, which tracks body size because of actual throat and vocal-tract anatomy -- don't seem to need any strategic cost at all, they're just hard to fake for boring physical reasons. Which made me wonder how much "cost," in the handicap-principle sense, is really doing the explanatory work people think it's doing, versus being just one mechanism among several for honest signaling.
