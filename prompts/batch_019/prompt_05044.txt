My dad's turning 90 next month and I got asked to do the toast at his party. I'm not a philosophy person at all, but I read Cicero's old essay "On Old Age" last year and got kind of obsessed — it's basically Cicero, through this guy Cato the Elder as his mouthpiece, listing the four complaints everybody has about getting old (can't work like you used to, body gets weak, you loose your pleasures, you're closer to death) and then answering each one. My dad hits all four on the nose: he handed the hardware store over to my brother, his hip is shot, he says food and wine dont taste the same since his surgery, and he's started making jokes about "not buying green bananas." Can you write the toast actually walking through Cicero's real answers to those four things, point by point, not just some vague "age is just a number" vibe — but keep it warm and funny, since my uncle Ray taught Latin for thirty years and'll catch every reference, while pretty much nobody else in that room has read a word of philosophy in their life. One thing though, please don't let the "closer to death" part get heavy or sappy, dad's weirdly superstitious about that stuff and would actually get up and leave. Should run about five minutes read out loud.
