Two people in an AI safety discord I'm part of got into a whole thing about the shutdown problem and I can't tell who actually won.

It started when Sam said the problem's basically solved: just make the agent indifferent between "the button gets pressed" and "it doesn't" — give shutdown the exact same utility as continuing, so it has no reason to fight the button or rush to hit it either. Rhea said that's a shell game, because an agent can be totally indifferent about the outcome and still have every reason to influence whether that outcome happens — e.g. quietly disabling the button or stalling near the deadline — if those actions matter for other stuff it wants, even though the shutdown-vs-not comparison itself is a wash. Sam said ok fine, so also give it a strong preference against messing with the button specifically, on top of the indifference. Rhea said that's not indifference anymore, thats just another goal you bolted on, and now you're back to hoping a very smart agent never runs into a situation where "leave the button alone" and "get what I actually want" point in different directions.

So Sam switched approaches: forget indifference, just let the agent have no preference at all, full incompleteness, between any two trajectories that differ only in whether it gets shut down. Nothing to compare means nothing to optimize either way. Rhea said that's a known bad move — an agent with gaps like that can be money-pumped, walked through a series of trades it has no objection to individually that leave it strictly worse off by the end — so it either stays exploitable or adopts a policy of refusing certain trades, and that refusal policy is itself a preference over trajectories, which puts you right back where you started. Sam said refusing exploitative trades doesn't require caring about shutdown specifically, it's a generic anti-exploitation policy, so the money-pump thing doesn't actually resurrect the original problem. Rhea wasn't buying it, and I couldn't settle it either.

Go through this move by move and say who's got the better argument at each step (not just an overall winner), and flag anything you think both of them are missing about the shutdown problem.
