I spent my career as a geneticist, and since retiring I've fallen into a bit of a philosophy rabbit hole, currently Galileo. I keep rereading his letter to the Grand Duchess Christina, where he lays out actual rules for when scripture and science seem to clash: it's written in accomodated, everyday language for ordinary folks, so you don't override the literal sense unless the science side has a genuine necessary demonstration, not just a good guess (he's even got that famous line, borrowed from Cardinal Baronius, about scripture teaching us how to get to heaven, not how the heavens go). That got me thinking about my own old field: population genetics makes a pretty strong case that human genetic diversity just can't come from one founding couple a few thousand years back, theres no bottleneck like that anywhere in the data. So by Galileo's own rules, not just "he liked science" vibes, what would he actually say about the Adam and Eve question today, and would he even think that bit of Genesis was meant to be read literally to begin with?
