Over dinner last week, a friend of mine — who has spent far more time with formal epistemology than I have — and I ended up in a long, unresolved argument about Bayesian updating. It started from an offhand comment about how forecasters revise their models as noisy, imperfect polling data comes in, and it grew into a broader dispute about whether the order in which uncertain evidence arrives can ever affect where one ends up. Her position was that a properly Bayesian agent reaches the same credences regardless of the order in which evidence is absorbed, provided the total evidence is eventually the same. I agreed that this holds for ordinary conditionalization on propositions one comes to accept outright, but objected that it need not hold for Richard Jeffrey's rule for updating on uncertain evidence — "probability kinematics" — where one shifts one's credence in some proposition to a new value without ever becoming certain of it or its negation. My recollection is that Jeffrey conditionalization is not, in general, order-independent: updating first on one piece of uncertain evidence and then on a second can leave you with a different final credence in some proposition of interest than updating in the reverse order, even though the two new credal values being moved to are identical either way. She wasn't convinced by my say-so and asked, fairly enough, for an actual demonstration.

I'd like your help settling this properly. Could you construct a small, fully explicit toy example — a handful of possible worlds with a stated prior probability distribution, two propositions E and F that each get updated on Jeffrey-style (that is, by specifying a new credence short of certainty for each), and some further proposition H whose probability we can track throughout — such that Jeffrey-conditionalizing on E and then on F yields a different final value for P(H) than conditionalizing on F first and then E? Please keep the numbers simple enough to check by hand, and add a short explanation of exactly what it is about Jeffrey's updating rule, as opposed to ordinary conditionalization on evidence one becomes certain of, that makes this order-sensitivity possible in the first place.
