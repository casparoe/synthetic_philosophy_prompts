Since Aristotle's sea-battle argument in De Interpretatione 9 shows that classical bivalence — the principle that "There will be a sea battle tomorrow" is already true or false today — commits us to full-blown fatalism about the future, and the only logically consistent way to block this result is to follow Łukasiewicz in denying determinate truth-values to future contingents, I've long been puzzled why so many contemporary tense logicians and philosophers of time still work with classical two-valued semantics for future-tensed statements rather than adopting a three-valued logic. Am I missing some further move that lets them keep bivalence without embracing fatalism, or is this simply an unacknowledged inconsistency in how the field has developed?
