I'm working on a novel where Nietzsche wakes up in present day LA and gets dragged along to one of those Bryan Johnson-style longevity clinics -- blood plasma swaps, rapamycin, obsessive calorie and biomarker tracking, the whole biohacker routine. I dont want his reaction to just be the 'ubermensch, number go up' joke everyone would write. He was genuinely obsessive about his own diet and digestion (see the nutrition passages in Ecce Homo, plus his jab at Cornaro in Twilight of the Idols for mixing up cause and effect on the meager-diet-causes-longevity claim), and he also had a lot to say about the ascetic ideal and self-denial as a symptom of decadence. So which is it, in his own terms -- would he see these guys as kindred self-experimenters, or as decadents in disguise? Work it out from his actual arguments, not the t-shirt-slogan version of him.
