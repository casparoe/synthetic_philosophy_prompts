For my philosophy of AI seminar this week we read Turing's 1950 paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," and there's a part in the objections section that our professor spent basically a whole class on, since most people apparently skim right past it: Turing takes the ESP argument dead serious. He actually walks through telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition and psycho-kinesis one by one, says the statistical evidence for telepathy specifically is "overwhelming," and then revises the imitation game itself so the interrogator can throw in telepathy-style guessing questions, with everyone stuck in a "telepathy-proof room" so nobody can cheat. Its such a strange moment in the paper because he's not being ironic or hedging at all, he genuinely thinks this could wreck the whole thought experiment if it isn't handled properly. Me and a couple friends got into a whole argument after class about how seriously to take this.

Here's what's been bugging me: the evidence he's almost certainly gesturing at (Soal's card-guessing experiments with Basil Shackleton, probably the single most famous ESP result in Britain at the time) got shown decades later, when Betty Markwick dug into the "random" number tables he used, to have been at least partly cooked. And parapsychology as a field has basically never produced a result that survives serious replication since then either - even later "rigorous" attempts like Bem's 2011 precognition study just ended up fueling the replication crisis instead of vindicating anything. So, if you had to actually predict it: does Turing quietly drop the ESP objection once he learns his key evidence was fabricated, or does his own stated epistemic policy in the paper (basically, "follow the statistics wherever they lead, no matter how uncomfortable") commit him to something more awkward, like having to take a Bem-style result seriously too? I'm less interested in a cheap "ha, he was wrong" answer than in whether this whole episode points to an actual weak spot in the operationalist, evidence-first attitude that drives the rest of his argument. Please actually argue for your prediction rather than just asserting how you think he'd react.
