So this happened with my own paper and I cant stop turning it over in my head. My subfield's top conference runs double-blind review, and the policy says authors can't advertise or promote their submission on social media during the review period, to protect anonymity. About eight months ago, well before this review period opened, I posted a twitter thread about early results from this same project. I never posted anything new once reviews started, but that old thread is still up, and anyone mildly curious could match it to my submission in about ten seconds. A friend of mine who's reviewing for the conference this year flagged it as a possible violation. My advisor says I'm fine - the rule restricts actions taken during the review window, and I didn't do anything during that window. My friend says thats a cop-out, since the whole point of the rule is that reviewers shouldn't be able to identify you, and a live, googleable thread defeats that no matter when it was posted, so it should still count against me. Both arguments seem genuinely reasonable, not just people being stubborn. Who actually has the better argument here, or is there a smarter way to frame the disagreement than just picking a side?
