So a few weeks back I got into it with my brother-in-law about climate change. He said something like "yeah I get that emissions are gonna screw over people alive in like 2150, but I just don't care about those people, so why would I have any actual reason to change what I do?" That kinda stumped me - I obviously think he's wrong, but I couldn't explain WHY without it just boiling down to "well you should care," which felt like cheating somehow. So I went down a google rabbit hole and it turns out theres a whole real philosophy debate about basically exactly this, called "internal vs external reasons." Theres this guy Bernard Williams who wrote a famous paper arguing that a reason to do something only counts as a reason for YOU if it connects up, through some chain of reasoning, to stuff you already want or care about (he's got this term for it, "subjective motivational set"). So on his view if my brother-in-law truly doesn't care about future people at all, then, strictly speaking, he really doesn't have a reason to act for their sake - full stop, no matter how much the rest of us wish he did. But then apparently other philosophers, McDowell being the big one, pushed back hard and said no, there totally can be reasons that apply to you whether or not you happen to care, and that Williams' idea of what counts as "sound" reasoning your way to a new motivation is way too narrow/rigged in his own favor.

I tried to read an explainer on the actual back-and-forth between them and got lost around the part where Williams talks about "sound deliberation" and whatever alternative McDowell is proposing instead. Could you write me a dialogue between a Williams-style internalist and a McDowell-style externalist actually hashing this out, using my brother-in-law's climate thing as the example they keep circling back to? I don't want one of them to obviously be the strawman here - both should be genuinely sharp and each should respond to the other's specific last point instead of just restating their own view again. Like, if the internalist explains away an easy counterexample using that "sound deliberation" move, I want the externalist to have an actual comeback aimed at that exact move, then the internalist responding to THAT, and so on for a few rounds, so by the end I come away actually understanding where the real disagreement is instead of just agreeing with whoever talked last.
