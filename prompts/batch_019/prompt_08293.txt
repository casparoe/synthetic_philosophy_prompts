I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have spent a good deal of time on my own puzzling over something about hypocrisy and blame, and I would like help thinking it through more carefully than I can manage by myself. I understand that many philosophers hold that a hypocrite can lack the "standing" to blame someone for something, even when the accusation is true and the hypocrite sincerely believes it: the disqualification is supposed to attach to the blamer, not to the content of the charge. Two explanations for this seem to be on offer: (1) it is a matter of unfairness, since the hypocrite exempts themselves from a standard they are enforcing on someone else; (2) blaming carries an implicit claim to a kind of moral authority over the other person, and the hypocrite's claim to that authority is false.

I cannot see how to tell these two explanations apart, so here is a case I keep coming back to. Suppose a hypocrite blames someone while explicitly disclaiming any superiority: "I do this too, I'm no better than you, but it's still wrong, and you should stop." If explanation (1) is correct, saying so out loud should not change anything, since the underlying unfairness is untouched. If explanation (2) is correct, this should restore their standing, since they are no longer claiming an authority they lack. Which of these views is more defensible, and does my case actually succeed in separating them?
