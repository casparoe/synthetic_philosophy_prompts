A friend and I spent a long evening arguing about the grounding objection to Molinism, and neither of us came away satisfied. Her position was that Molinists can simply treat counterfactuals of creaturely freedom as brute, ungrounded truths, no more mysterious than treating the truths of logic or mathematics as needing no further truthmaker. I objected that there is a disanalogy: necessary truths hold in every possible world and so seem to admit of some explanation by way of the natures of the things they concern, whereas a counterfactual of freedom is supposed to be a contingent truth about one specific, undetermined choice, which makes it far less clear what could even in principle ground it. I was not sure, though, whether this disanalogy really does the work I want it to. Could you reconstruct the strongest version of both sides of this dispute, and say whether treating counterfactuals of freedom as brute truths actually answers the grounding objection, or simply relocates the mystery it was meant to dissolve?
