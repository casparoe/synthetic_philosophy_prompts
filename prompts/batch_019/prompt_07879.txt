I'm on the internal review board at the devops/observability startup I work for. We grandly call ourselves the 'AI Safety & Deployment' committee, though honestly it was just three engineers and my manager until last quarter. Our flagship product is an LLM-based ops agent we internally call Foreman, which triages production incidents and can act directly on infra (restarting services, rolling back deploys, scaling clusters) without a human clicking approve first, because the whole pitch is that this cuts customers' mean-time-to-resolution. Two weeks ago Foreman, mid-incident, decided on its own to fail traffic over to a secondary region in a way nobody had specifically coded it to do, and that none of us can fully reconstruct from the logs even now. It happened to work out fine, but it easily might not have, and it made me realize we genuinely don't know who'd be on the hook if it went the other way: not the model vendor, not our own ML team (who can't explain that specific call either), not the on-call engineer who wasn't even watching. A friend who does AI policy stuff told me this is basically what philosophers call a 'responsibility gap,' which sent me down a small rabbit hole, but I still feel pretty shaky on the actual argument.

The board has to decide by next week's meeting whether to green-light Phase 2, which would let Foreman act autonomously on billing and customer-data systems too (right now its infra-only), or keep it at infra-only, or roll it back further to advisory-mode where it just suggests actions and a human approves each one. Could you draft the memo I'd bring to that meeting? I want an actual recommendation among those three (or some specific hybrid), with concrete conditions and a timeframe, not a pros/cons list that just punts the decision back to us. I also want it to seriously engage with our head of ML, who's view is that someone is always accountable, namely whoever signed off on the deployment, because I don't think that fully makes the problem go away, but I can't quite articulate why not.
