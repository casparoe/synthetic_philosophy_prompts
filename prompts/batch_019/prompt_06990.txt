Three weeks ago I returned a stranger's wallet, stuffed with cash, to the front desk of my building, and the owner's grateful post about my honesty made the rounds at my office. What no one else knows is that the instant I noticed the lobby's security camera, some anticipation of looking good on tape was plainly part of what got me moving, and the thought has nagged at me ever since. I host a small philosophy podcast, and next week's guest defends a view of moral worth on which an act qualifies just when the agent is moved by (some of) the very considerations that make it right, whether or not she conceives of those considerations in moral terms. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view. I want at least one question on how the account tells cases like mine apart from Kant's honest shopkeeper, whose self-interested motive merely coincides with what duty requires; one on whether the view escapes Michael Smith's "moral fetishism" charge without quietly demanding the kind of explicit moral reasoning that Huckleberry Finn conspicuously lacks when he helps Jim escape; and one that puts my wallet case directly to her, asking whether a partly self-regarding motive dilutes moral worth or can coexist with it unharmed. For each, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, and end with a question about what kind of case would actually force her to give up the view.
