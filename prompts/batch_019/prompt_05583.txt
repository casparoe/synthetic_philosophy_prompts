I'm putting together a collection of short stories that each turn on a real math or logic paradox, and I'm stuck on the one for Skolem's paradox, so can you take a crack at it? The paradox, in short, is that a countable model of set theory can still contain a set the model itself proves is uncountable, just because the bijection that would list it against the natural numbers isn't one of the model's own elements. I want a character — a person, an AI, a whole civilization, whatever fits — who has an airtight, internally valid proof that some part of their world can't be enumerated, is genuinely too big to list, while the reader slowly realizes that from outside, their entire reality could fit on one numbered list. Dont have anyone actually lecture the math out loud though, I want the dread to come from the story itself, more Borges or Ted Chiang than a classroom scene. Keep it around 1000 words, and I'd love it if the 'outside enumerator' stays ambiguous — a god, a simulator, a bigger mathematician, whatever — so long as the ending makes clear that being 'uncountable' was only ever true from the inside.
