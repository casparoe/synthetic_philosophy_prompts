I spent a long evening arguing with a friend about the Judy Benjamin problem, and we never reached agreement. Her position is that the puzzle is ultimately an artifact of using minimum cross-entropy to extend Jeffrey conditionalization to conditional constraints: adopt a different, equally well-motivated extension, and the awkward shift in the marginal probability of Blue territory (from one half down to roughly 0.47, despite the message saying nothing about Blue) simply disappears. I am skeptical of this deflationary reading, since it seems to me that this kind of "leakage" should arise under most reasonable updating rules whenever the two crossed partitions are merely probabilistically independent under the prior, without being independent in whatever stronger sense would be needed to actually block it. Rather than keep arguing in the abstract, I would like you to construct a new example, drawn from a domain unrelated to soldiers and territory, that preserves the essential structure of the original problem (two independent binary partitions crossed into four cells, with a conditional statement constraining the ratio within only one branch) and brings out its central puzzle as sharply as the original does. Please work through, using your own example, exactly why updating redistributes probability into the untouched branch, and tell me whether that redistribution strikes you as a quirk of minimum cross-entropy specifically or a more general feature of updating on conditionals.
