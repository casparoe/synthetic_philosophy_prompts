I've been reading about Émilie du Châtelet on my own, mostly out of curiosity about her Newton translation, which eventually led me to her "Discourse on Happiness." She argues there that illusions — about love, about the importance of our own pursuits — are things we genuinely need for happiness, not defects to be corrected. Yet in the same essay she is coolly realistic about the fact that romantic passion fades with age regardless of what we do, and that anyone who has not cultivated some other durable passion, such as study, to take its place is setting themselves up for real unhappiness. I keep wondering what she would make of modern cosmetic culture — filters, anti-aging treatments, and the like — which function as an industrial-scale way of manufacturing exactly the illusion of undiminished youth she says we require. Would she treat that as a legitimate use of illusion, or as precisely the trap she warned against? I would like your best reconstruction of how she would actually reason through that tension, rather than a neutral list of considerations on each side.
