I serve on the cultural affairs committee for the small town I live in -- about 2,600 people, still governed by open town meeting. A newly formed group wants a permanent line in next year's budget, roughly $40,000, to convert the old grange hall into a year-round community theater with a paid part-time director and four productions a season, rather than the one-off amateur shows we've funded before. I've been reading Rousseau's Letter to d'Alembert on the Theatre on my own this winter, and his case against establishing a standing theater in Geneva won't leave me alone: that a playwright can only flatter an audience's existing tastes rather than reform them, that even Molière's Misanthrope quietly makes us side with the smooth, dishonest Philinte against the blunt, honest Alceste, and that a small, self-governing town is better served by a public festival where people are their own show than by an audience sitting in the dark. Please draft the memo I'll circulate before our vote on the 14th: it should recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, take his argument as seriously as it deserves, and still do justice to the strongest thing the theater's supporters could say back.
