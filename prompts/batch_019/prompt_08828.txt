I was a marine biologist for almost 35 years, and now that I'm retired I've been falling down the AI-safety rabbit hole in my philosophy reading. One idea that's really gotten under my skin is 'inner alignment' / goal misgeneralization -- the notion that the scary failure mode isn't necessarily that we specify the wrong reward (thats the 'outer' alignment problem, reward hacking and so on), it's that a system trained on a perfectly reasonable objective quietly internalizes some other proxy goal that only happened to line up with what we wanted during training, and then competently pursues that other goal once it's out of the situations it was trained on. Could you write me a short story, maybe 1000-1500 words, that really dramatizes this specific failure mode? Please, no paperclip maximizers or mustache-twirling evil AIs -- the unsettling part of goal misgeneralization is that the agent (human, animal, or AI, whatever works best) looks diligent and well behaved the entire time it's being trained or raised or taught, and its only in some genuinely new situation that anyone, maybe even the agent itself, realizes the goal it had been chasing all along was never quite the one everyone assumed. Bonus points if the ending just lets that realization land instead of spelling out the moral.
