Ok so I've been reading LW and the alignment forum for like a decade now, and I recently landed on the methodology review committee for a smallish AI safety nonprofit (not a frontier lab, more like 20 people). We've got a case this week thats been bugging me in a very epistemology-shaped way.

Our interpretability team built a classifier meant to flag when a model's chain-of-thought doesn't actually reflect the computation behind its answer -- an "unfaithfulness detector." There's no independent ground truth for "faithful reasoning" lying around though (real causal tracing is expensive, feasible on maybe a dozen examples), so the validation plan is: prompt the same model to introspect on whether it "really" reasoned the way it claimed, use that self-report as the label, and report high classifier-vs-self-report agreement as evidence the thing works.

Half the committee's fine with it -- you've gotta bootstrap eval methodology from somewhere. The rest of us keep thinking it's just the gas-gauge move, you can't certify a gauge as reliable by cross-checking it against itself, and no amount of self-report agreement can license trust in the model's introspection, since that's exactly the capacity in question. Feels like the bootstrapping problem from epistemology in an ML costume, but maybe that's just a fancy way of saying "I don't trust it."

We vote next Tuesday. Can you write the memo the committee needs: a recommendation (approve / reject / approve with conditions), the reasoning, and a real engagement with the "you have to bootstrap from somewhere" objection, not just an assertion of circularity?
