ok so I've been down a Galileo rabbit hole for a piece I'm writing, and I keep running into this lazy "Church bad, science good" framing that kind of falls apart once you read the actual primary docs. Galileo's 1615 letter to the Grand Duchess Christina is a genuinely careful piece of biblical hermeneutics -- leaning hard on Augustine, arguing scripture accomodates itself to ordinary speech and isn't in the business of teaching astronomy (hence that Baronius line he quotes about scripture teaching how to go to heaven, not how the heavens go). But then Bellarmine's letter to Foscarini that same year reads as almost more careful, epistemically: fine, he says, get me an actual demonstration that the earth moves and we'll revisit the exegesis, but right now its a hypothesis -- and he's not wrong that there was no real proof on the table yet. No stellar parallax had been detected, Tycho's system still accounted for the telescopic evidence (phases of Venus etc) just as well as full heliocentrism did, and the closest thing to a "proof" Galileo ever offered, the tides argument, came years later and turned out to be wrong anyway.

So here's what I want: an essay that actually weighs Galileo's hermeneutic case against Bellarmine's demonstration standard on their own terms, using only what was known/provable in 1615, not four hundred years of hindsight. Skip the standard martyr-for-science retelling. I want an actual verdict on who had the stronger argument, not a "both sides had a point" shrug.
