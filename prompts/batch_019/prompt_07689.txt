So my friend Nora's brother Alex has pretty serious ADHD (diagnosed, medicated, the real deal) and she made him best man for her wedding. He went dark on the bachelor party planning group chat for two weeks, showed up 40 minutes late to the rehearsal dinner, and ended up loosing the notecards for his own toast. Nora was livid and said she's never trusting him with anything important again. Her husband Ben told her to go easier on him -- 'that's literally just how his brain works, he's not doing it to hurt you.' Nora shot back that this is actually kind of insulting: it treats Alex like he isn't a full person who can be held to normal standards, and it's also a heads-I-win-tails-you-lose deal, since nobody's handing him bonus credit on the days his ADHD doesn't get in the way. Ben said intentions still count for something -- Alex feels genuinely terrible about it, and getting mad at him here is a bit like getting mad at someone over their eyesight. Nora said fine, maybe you can't blame him in the moment for any one slip-up, but he knew his own track record when he said yes to being best man in the first place, so if anyone's on the hook it's the guy who made that call, not some separate 'ADHD Alex' who gets to absorb all the blame-dodging. They went back and forth like this the entire car ride home and neither budged. Who's actually right here, and at which specific move? Go through it step by step instead of just declaring an overall winner, and say what -- if anything -- you think both of them are missing.
