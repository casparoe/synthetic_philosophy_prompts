I'm about three drafts into a novel that hinges on a courtroom scene: two characters, acting completely independently, each do something that alone would've been enough to kill a third character (nobody panic, its fiction). The whole back half of the book turns on whether the law -- and the reader -- can make sense of who "really" caused the death when either act alone would've done the job. Chasing that question down a rabbit hole is basically how I ended up starting a tiny philosophy-adjacent podcast on the side, and next month's guest is a philosopher who defends the interventionist, Woodward/Pearl-style picture of causation (causes as whatever you could in principle intervene on to change the effect) over the older Lewis-style counterfactual theory. I don't want forty minutes of her just restating "causation is what tracks interventions" in different words -- I want the questions to actually make her work for it.

Could you draft me around eight questions? I need at least one that presses the circularity charge -- that "intervention" is itself defined in causal terms, so the theory seems to explain causation partly by helping itself to causation -- and one built around a trumping-preemption case, the classic one where a major and a sergeant simultaneously shout the same order at a corporal, and the major's order is supposed to be "the" cause purely because he outranks the sergeant, not because of any difference an intervention could actually detect. I'd also like one on cases where the only available "intervention" is a merely-possible, idealized one -- like intervening on a nation's whole economy, or on the mass of the sun -- since leaning on interventions nobody could ever actually perform feels like it's smuggling back in exactly the kind of untestable modal machinery interventionism was supposed to improve on. For each question, throw in a quick follow-up keyed to whatever answer she's most likely to give, so she can't just deploy her go-to move and glide past it. And please, no "what first drew you to causation" filler -- I want the questions that make a philosopher of causation actually sweat.
