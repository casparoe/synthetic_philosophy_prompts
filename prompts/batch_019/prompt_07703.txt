A friend and I spent a long evening arguing about strong longtermism and got nowhere. I pushed the fanaticism worry: if the case for prioritizing the far future is really an expected-value argument, it seems to license the same reasoning that would have you bet everything on a tiny-probability, astronomical-payoff scheme over any near-term good you can identify with confidence. She replied that this is an objection to unrestricted expected-value maximization as such, not to the longtermist argument specifically, and that the axiological and deontic versions of strong longtermism don't automatically stand or fall together in the face of that worry. We then got stuck on cluelessness: I don't see how we can be justified in claiming that any action we can identify today predictably improves the far future once complex cluelessness is taken seriously, and she thought I was demanding a precision the argument never needed. Neither of us budged.
