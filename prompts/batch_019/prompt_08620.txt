I'm a retired chemist, and these days I sit in on a philosophy discussion group at the local university, mostly just for fun. Last week one of the younger members, who's clearly read way more moral philosophy than I have, told me flat out that its wrong to keep eating meat, no hedging at all, and I honestly couldn't poke a hole in her argument on the spot. Driving home I realised I'd basically just adopted her conclusion wholesale, the same way I'd accept a colleague's read on an NMR spectrum I wasn't trained to interpret myself. But taking her word for the meat thing feels shakier somehow, even though structurally it seems like the exact same move: trusting someone who's thought about it more than me. Is there an actual difference between deferring to someone on a moral question and deferring to them on a factual one, or is that just some bias I've picked up along the way?
