Ok so I'm putting together a collection of speculative short fiction and I want one piece to hinge on real metaphysics instead of just vague multiverse vibes. Premise I keep coming back to: a woman's husband -- a modal logician, of all things -- dies, and not long after she finds a way to glimpse a neighboring possible world where a version of him is still alive. Turns out he'd left behind notes where he'd already staked out a position on exactly this situation before it happened to him: Lewis's counterpart theory vs. the transworld identity view, i.e. whether that other-world guy really is him (one continuous individual who just happens to also be in another world) or merely a similar stranger who happens to share his memories and face up until the point their histories forked. Can you write that story for me? I want it to actually engage the real philosphy instead of hand-waving it -- Kripke's old jab that the real Humphrey couldn't care less what happens to a mere 'counterpart' of his no matter how similar, and whatever reasoning got Lewis to insist the same individual can't just have flatly contradictory properties across worlds (part of why he needs counterparts instead of one continuous person in the first place). Something like 1500-2000 words is fine, doesn't need a tidy moral, but the ending should actually take a side instead of shrugging.
