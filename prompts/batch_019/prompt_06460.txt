Ok this has been bugging me for a while. Theres that Yudkowsky thing about beliefs needing to 'pay rent' in anticipated experience -- if a belief doesn't change what you expect to see or feel, you don't actually hold it, your just cheering for a team. Then I actually went and read Wittgenstein's 1938 lectures on religious belief, where he's staring at basically the same phenomenon (someone who says he believes in the Last Judgement but wouldn't bet on it, and wouldn't treat contrary evidence as counting against it) and lands somewhere totally different: not 'so he doesn't really believe it' but 'this was never the kind of claim that's supposed to predict anything, so stop grading it like a weather forecast.'

Write me a dialogue between a rationalist blogger from the 'beliefs should pay rent' school and a philosopher working in Wittgenstein's tradition, fighting this out over that exact case. Make the rationalist really press on whether 'it's a picture, not a hypothesis' is just a phrase that lets any belief dodge ever being wrong while still getting to count as a belief -- and give the Wittgensteinian an actual comeback, not just 'form of life' repeated as a thought-terminating cliche. Neither one needs to win, but by the end they should've pinned down exactly what they still disagree about instead of just talking past each other.
