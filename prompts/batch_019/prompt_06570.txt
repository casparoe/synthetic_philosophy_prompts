The axiom of choice is a good example: I understand the informal statement well enough -- given any collection of nonempty sets, you can select one element from each, even when there's no explicit rule for doing the selecting -- and I know it's independent of the rest of ZFC. Could you lay out the most important implications of this whole axiom-of-choice controversy for someone in my position -- that is, for how I should think about the decision between working constructively and working classically when writing machine-checked proofs and formally verified code?
