So we have this philosophy elective at my school (basically the only class this semester I don't zone out in), and this week we covered the "dogmatism paradox." Our teacher said it traces back to some unpublished Kripke lecture from the 60s that a guy named Gilbert Harman later wrote up. The basic move is: if you actually know that p is true, then you already know that any evidence against p that might ever show up would have to be misleading, since evidence against something true can't actually be pointing toward the truth — so knowing p seems to license you to decide, right now, to disregard in advance any future evidence against it, no matter how convincing that evidence looks when it actually shows up. Basically everyone in class agreed that sounds bad, but nobody could agree on why.

He says that's just skepticism wearing a trenchcoat, and that it proves way too much since almost nothing would count as knowledge anymore.
