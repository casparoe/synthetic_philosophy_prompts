Write a short story that turns on teleosemantics - the attempt to naturalize mental content (Millikan, Dretske, etc.) by saying what makes a mental state actually about something is the selection history of whatever produced it, not its current structure or behavior. The stock problem case is Swampman: a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a person, assembled by pure freak accident with zero evolutionary or causal history, whose internal states technically mean nothing on this view even though he acts completely normal. I want the story to use that exact gap - content vs behavior - as the actual engine of the plot, not just another clone-is-it-conscious story, which is a different question. Something like: someone gets an exact reconstruction of a dead parent, and the parent had literally been a teleosemanticist who explained all this to them growing up, so when reconstructed-dad says "I love you" they cant help running it through his own theory and wondering if its a sentence with nobody behind it yet. Doesn't have to be that exact setup, but it should hinge on meaning/content specifically, not consciousness or personal identity in general. Keep it an actual story though, not a dialogue that just lays out the thought experiment.
