So a friend of mine who's way too into the rationalist blogosphere was arguing with me last night about something political, and after like two hours of going back and forth he pulled out Aumann's agreement theorem on me. His claim was that the theorem proves two honest, rational people who both know what the other one believes literally cannot end up disagreeing -- their beliefs have to converge. So the fact that we were still disagreeing after hours of arguing was, in his telling, itself evidence that one of us (definitely me, according to him) wasnt reasoning straight, or was just motivated by bias rather than actually trying to get at the truth.

I went and read a summary of the theorem afterward and it does seem to say something like that on its face. Is he basically right that two sufficiently rational, honest people who talk for long enough are logically forced to converge, so that persistent disagreement between reasonable-seeming people is basically always a tell that someone's fooling themselves? Feels like a really big conclusion to hang on one theorem, and I've got a nagging sense he's skipping over something, but I don't know enough game theory to say what.
