So in my non-classical logics seminar we've been going through Priest's stuff on dialetheism (we're using his *Introduction to Non-Classical Logic* plus some photocopied bits of *In Contradiction*), and this week was his Principle of Uniform Solution -- the "same kind of paradox, same kind of solution" idea, where the Liar, Russell's, Curry's, Berry's etc. all supposedly instantiate the Inclosure Schema and so all deserve a dialetheic fix, which is meant to be dialetheism's big advantage over patchwork approaches like Tarski's hierarchy bolted onto a separately-restricted comprehension axiom. My professor kind of waved his hand at Curry's paradox when a classmate asked about it, but the more I think about it the less I buy that its actually covered by the same move as the Liar. Curry doesn't even need negation to go through, so accepting a glut isn't obviously doing anything -- what actually stops the triviality is restricting contraction (or detachment) on the conditional, which seems like a totally different kind of fix. And LP's own material conditional isn't even properly detachable once gluts are around, so it looks like you need a stronger conditional to reason with at all, and the second that conditional validates modus ponens plus contraction, Curry comes back with a vengeance.

I want you to actually argue for the position that this is a real, substantive problem for dialetheism and not just some solvable technical wrinkle: that Curry's paradox shows dialetheism can't deliver the uniform solution Priest advertises, because whatever fixes Curry (dropping contraction/detachment) is not the same *kind* of solution as what fixes the Liar (accepting a glut), so dialetheism ends up guilty, by its own Principle of Uniform Solution standard, of exactly the ad hoc patchwork it accuses classical logicians of. Please don't just present it neutrally as "some say X, others say Y" -- build the strongest version of this specific case, and then take on the most obvious dialetheist comeback (e.g. that the contraction-restriction is itself independently/dialetheically motivated, or that the weak material conditional was sufficient all along) and explain why it doesn't actually rescue the uniform-solution claim.
