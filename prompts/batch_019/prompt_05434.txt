Two students in my epistemology seminar got into a running dispute over Timothy Williamson's anti-luminosity argument that spilled into a long email thread after class. They've asked me to settle it, but I want an independent read first.

(1) Elena reconstructed the argument: take a subject whose bodily state shifts so gradually, from clearly feeling cold at t0 to clearly not feeling cold at tn, that any two consecutive moments are introspectively indiscriminable. If "feels cold" is luminous -- if feeling cold always puts you in a position to know you feel cold -- then a margin for error principle on knowledge (roughly: a belief isn't knowledge if an indiscriminable case would have made it false) lets you run an induction showing she feels cold at every ti, including tn, contradicting the setup. So "feels cold" isn't luminous. Run the same schema on "I know that p," using a case of inexact perceptual knowledge, and you refute KK instead: you can know p without being in a position to know that you know it.

(2) Daniel objected that the induction never starts: luminosity only claims she's in a position to know she's cold at each ti, not that she actually believes it there, and the margin principle is stated in terms of what a knower's belief is safe from.

(3) Elena answered that it's a reductio: granting luminosity for the sake of argument, she is by hypothesis in a position to know at every ti, so if she exploited that position there she would come to know she's cold -- which is all the margin principle needs at that step.

(4) Daniel switched targets and denied the margin principle itself for this case: judging a tree's height from a distance needs a margin because the estimate could easily miss a mind-independent fact, but introspecting your own present sensation isn't like that, since there's no further fact about how cold you feel beyond how it currently seems to you.

(5) Elena replied that this can't be right across the board, since introspective reports about current sensations are sometimes mistaken -- blurred together, mislabeled, overwritten by expectation -- so Daniel owes an account of why introspection of present sensation is exempt when introspection generally plainly isn't.

Please adjudicate moves (1)-(5): say who has the better of it at each step, not just overall, and flag anything philosophically important that neither of them notices, including whether Elena's KK-corollary in (1) is as well supported as her main luminosity argument. Give your verdicts as a JSON object of the form {"1": <score>, ..., "5": <score>}, each score an integer from -2 (clearly mistaken) to 2 (clearly right), then justify each score in a sentence or two.
