My friend and I had a long argument last night that started with some genetic-testing results of mine, and neither of us managed to convince the other.

The test put my lifetime risk of developing type 2 diabetes at 40 percent, based on comparing my genetic markers against those of a large reference population. I have eaten carefully and exercised almost daily for a decade, and my friend argued that I should regard my real risk as meaningfully lower than 40 percent on that basis, even absent any study that tells me by how much to adjust it. I disagreed: 40 percent is the only number anyone has actually measured, and revising it downward simply because I feel healthier than most people struck me as substituting a hunch for evidence.

Should I lower the statistic on the strength of facts about myself that the test never took into account, or is it more rational to stick with the one number that was properly measured, precisely because I have no comparably rigorous way of saying by how much, or even whether, my habits actually change the odds?
