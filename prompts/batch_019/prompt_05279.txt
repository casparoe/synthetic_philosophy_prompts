I have recently been reading about Kripke's rule-following paradox -- the argument, sometimes personified as "Kripkenstein" since it is arguably more Kripke's construction than the historical Wittgenstein's -- that no fact about my past behavior or mental states can determine whether I have been meaning addition by "+" rather than some deviant function that agrees with addition on every sum I have actually computed. As someone who works with code, I can't help noticing the resemblance to the way a finite battery of unit tests never uniquely pins down the function being tested. I would like to see two people who take the paradox seriously argue through the two responses that strike me as most serious, rather than the ones usually dismissed within the first five minutes of an introductory lecture.

Write a dialogue between two philosophers of language who disagree sharply: a dispositionalist and a communitarian.

- The dispositionalist defends the idealized version of the view: what makes it true that I mean addition is a fact about how I would respond to any query under suitably idealized conditions (unlimited memory and time, no fatigue). She must answer the standard charge that appealing to "idealized conditions" either fails to say anything determinate or covertly presupposes the very notion of correctness it is meant to ground.
- The communitarian defends Kripke's own skeptical solution: there is no such fact, and "meaning addition" is instead constituted by whether my answers are licensed by a community's practice of endorsing and correcting one another. He must answer the charge that this simply relocates the indeterminacy one level up: what fixes that the community itself is disposed toward addition rather than some deviant function it has never yet been collectively tripped up by?

Both characters should be equally sharp, respond to the specific point the other has just made rather than talking past each other, and neither should concede prematurely. Let the exchange end in genuine disagreement rather than a tidy resolution.
