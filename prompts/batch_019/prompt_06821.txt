A former student of mine, now running a small manufacturing firm, recently asked for my advice on a dispute she is caught in, and I have not been able to give her a fully satisfying answer.

Her firm and a former supplier are locked in litigation over a breached contract worth roughly $50,000; eighteen months on, each side has already spent upwards of $200,000 in legal fees. Both principals are, by her account, shrewd and level-headed businesspeople who each privately admit that, had they foreseen these costs, they would never have pursued the claim — yet whenever settlement is raised, both refuse, since dropping the case now would mean the money already spent bought them nothing, while pressing on still holds out some chance of recovering it. Is it rational for her to keep fighting, given how much money has already been poured into it, or is she merely compounding an earlier mistake — and is there a principled way of telling the two apart, given that both sides seem to be reasoning carefully at every step?
