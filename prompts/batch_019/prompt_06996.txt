So I went down a rabbit hole a while back about this game theory thing called the centipede game (nothing to do with actual centipedes lol). Quick version: two players take turns, and theres a pot of money that keeps growing each round. On your turn you can either grab the pot (you get the bigger cut, the other player gets whatever's left) or pass and let it keep growing. If both players keep passing all the way to the end, they both end up way richer than if someone grabs early. But the "rational" answer, according to something called backward induction, is that player 1 should just grab the pot immediately on turn one. The logic: reason backwards from the last round — the last player should obviously grab there since the game's ending anyway, so the second-to-last player should grab one round earlier to avoid getting screwed, and that logic unravels all the way back to move 1.

Here's the part that's been bugging me though. That whole chain of reasoning only works if each player is confident the other one is rational and is gonna stay rational for the rest of the game. But say your 5 rounds in and the other guy hasn't grabbed yet, even though the theory said he should've grabbed ages ago — doesn't that basically prove the assumption wrong, right there in the middle of the game? And if the assumption propping up "grab immediately" has already been falsified, why should the conclusion still hold? It kind of feels like the argument eats itself once you actually get more than one move into it.

Anyway, can you write me an essay that actually takes a stance on this instead of doing the "there are many perspectives" cop-out? Lay out the strongest case that backward induction is still the correct way to reason through the centipede game, then the strongest case that it's self-undermining (for the reason above, or some other reason), and then tell me straight what a rational player should actually do. Conditional answers like "grab immediately if X, otherwise don't" are fine — I just want an actual verdict at the end, not a shrug.
