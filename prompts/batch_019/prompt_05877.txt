I'm chairing the mock Committee on Elections at my state's Youth and Government conference, a YMCA program where high schoolers debate and vote on bills modeled on our state legislature. Our committee votes Saturday on a bill that would lower the voting age to 16 for municipal elections only, as a few Maryland towns — Takoma Park among them — have already done. Half the delegates want to settle this by arguing over whether sixteen-year-olds are "mature enough," but that skips what political theorists call the boundary problem: the two standard tests for belonging to a political community's demos — the all-affected-interests principle, and the rival view that what matters is being subject to the community's coercive laws — seem to apply about as well to twelve-year-olds as to sixteen-year-olds or full adults, so an age cutoff needs some further principle behind it, not just an assertion. I need a memo to circulate before the vote that actually reckons with this, weighs "Demeny voting" (giving parents a proxy vote for each minor child) as the amendment some delegates prefer to direct enfranchisement, and ends with a clear recommendation: report the bill favorably, unfavorably, or favorably with that amendment. Please draft it, and make sure it gives real weight to the strongest case for keeping the line exactly where it is, rather than treating 18 as a number that nobody actually has to defend.
