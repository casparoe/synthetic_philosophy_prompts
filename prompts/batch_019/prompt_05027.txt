My old writing mentor Howard died last month after a genuinely brutal eight-month decline (pancreatic cancer) -- total literary guy on the surface, but secretly he'd been chipping away at an unpublished manuscript defending "best of all possible worlds" against Voltaire since grad school, and he used to scrawl "not compossible!" in the margins of my drafts whenever a plot point felt unearned. I'm giving the eulogy at his memorial next week (I've got about seven minutes), and half the room is former writing students who just knew him as the guy who ran Tuesday workshop, the other half is his old philosphy department crowd who will absolutely clock it if I go full Pangloss and say something like "everything happens for a reason" -- which is exactly the flattened version of his own favorite idea that used to make him furious. He explained the antecedent-will-versus-consequent-will thing to me at every holiday dinner for like a decade and I still don't fully get it, but I know it matters for whether this eulogy ends up honest or just comforting. Can you write something that's actually faithful to what he believed -- the compossibility stuff, that will distinction, "best on the whole" versus "best in every part" -- but still lands as real comfort for a room of sad people who do not want a metaphysics lecture at a funeral?
