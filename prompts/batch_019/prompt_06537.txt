I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole comparing Kierkegaard's rant against "the public" in Two Ages (the part that usually gets excerpted on its own as "The Present Age") with Toqueville's chapter on newspapers and associations in Democracy in America, and it struck me that these two guys are staring at basically the exact same 19th-century phenomenon -- a mass reading public getting stitched together by newspapers -- and walking away with totally opposite verdicts. Tocqueville thinks the newspaper is more or less the only thing that can glue together isolated, formally equal democratic individuals once you've lost the guilds and estates and aristocratic ties that used to do that job automatically. Kierkegaard thinks "the public" is a leveling abstraction, a kind of nobody made out of everybody, that lets people acquire opinions and even a sort of collective power while ducking any actual individual responsibility for them -- and this wasn't just an abstract worry for him, given his own very personal experience with the Corsair affair.

So, can you write me a dialogue between the two of them? Imagine they somehow ended up seated next to each other at a dinner sometime in the 1840s and the conversation turns to newspapers. I don't want two monologues stapled together -- I want them actually responding to each other's specific points, not just restating their own books louder. Have Tocqueville press Kierkegaard on what the alternative even is: once you're in a big, mobile, equal society with no more organic communities left, doesn't the lone individual just end up powerless rather than more responsible? And have Kierkegaard press back on whether Tocqueville's "associations" aren't just another way for people to melt into a crowd and borrow their opinions instead of forming their own, and on whether a newspaper can really do the binding-together work Tocqueville wants from it without also doing the leveling Kierkegaard is worried about. Neither one should cave or suddenly concede the point -- I want to see them actually find the pressure points in each other's positions. Something like 700-900 words is fine, script format (just names and lines, no stage directions) works great.
