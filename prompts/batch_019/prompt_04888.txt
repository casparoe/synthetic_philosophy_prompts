A friend of mine who follows space science closely was delighted last week by a paper reporting possible biosignature gases in the atmosphere of a nearby exoplanet. Her reasoning was straightforward: if the finding holds up, it shows that life isn't some vanishingly improbable fluke, which she takes, by extension, as good news for humanity's own prospects. My own reaction was closer to unease than delight, though I couldn't justify that to her on the spot, and when I tried, it came out sounding like reflexive contrarianism, which is how she's since chosen to read it. I suspect there's a more principled worry lurking here about what a finding like this would and wouldn't actually tell us, but I can't fully reconstruct the argument myself. Is there a real case for treating this kind of news as unsettling rather than reassuring, or is she right that I'm just souring good news out of temperament?
