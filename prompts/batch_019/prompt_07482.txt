For a dissertation chapter on French political thought under the Occupation, I have been rereading Simone Weil's wartime essay calling for the abolition of political parties. As I understand her central charge, competition among parties breeds factionalism and crowds out any coherent, disinterested judgment about the common good. If that is the crux of her objection, it would seem to follow that a one-party state, having done away with inter-party rivalry entirely, satisfies her own criteria better than the parliamentary systems she attacks. Is that a fair extension of her argument, or is there something in her actual position that blocks the inference to single-party rule? I would rather address this directly in a footnote than let a reader draw the implication unchallenged.
