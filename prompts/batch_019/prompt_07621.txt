Quick one for a piece I'm working on about how outlets (mine included) report those scary relative-risk headlines, like "eating bacon every day raises your bowel cancer risk by 18%." A philosopher of science I interviewed for background kept coming back to the reference class problem - the idea that theres no principled way to decide which population you should be compared against to get "your" individual risk number, and slicing by age vs. diet vs. genetics vs. zip code each hands you a different answer. She even name-dropped a paper with a title something like "the reference class problem is your problem too," which I loved but didn't have room for. Can you walk me through the most important implications of this for how individual risk stats should actually be reported - does it mean these single-number risks are basically meaningless for any one person, or is that overstating it?
