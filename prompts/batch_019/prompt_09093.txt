So I've been down a bit of a Simone Weil rabbit hole lately (started from an offhand mention in an old SSC post, then just kept going) and I wrote up some rough notes trying to connect her essay on abolishing political parties to stuff I see online these days. Not polished, but curious what you think before I try to turn it into somehting postable.

Her whole argument rests on defining a "party" structurally instead of by platform. She says every party, regardless of ideology, has three features: (1) it's basically a machine for manufacturing collective passion, (2) it's built to pressure every member into falling in line on more or less any question the party has staked out a position on, and (3) its own growth quietly becomes the actual terminal goal, dressed up the whole time as a means to some public good. On her view this makes parties structurally bad almost by definition, totally seperate from whether the founding cause was noble, and she thinks a functioning democracy would honestly be better off with none of them, just individuals reasoning toward truth and the common good directly.

My extension: that three-part structure fits online political fandoms/coalitions even better than it fit the parties she was looking at, since social media is basically purpose-built for manufacturing collective passion and enforcing conformity at scale, and "growth" (followers, engagement, vibes) is even more nakedly the terminal goal than it was for a party that at least needed actual votes to survive.

Weak point I can't get past: if you actually abolished formal parties, wouldn't the exact same three dynamics just reappear as informal factions, minus even the modicum of accountability a real party has? Feels like she's proposing a structural fix for what might be a coordination/virtue problem all the way down, a very Moloch-shaped hole in the plan.

Can you check whether I'm being fair to her actual argument (I only know it secondhand through summaries), tell me whether the social media analogy actually holds up or if I'm just forcing it, and weigh in on whether that weak point is fatal to her whole proposal or whether there's a good response available to her?
