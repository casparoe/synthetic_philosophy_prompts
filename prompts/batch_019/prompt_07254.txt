In my formal epistemology seminar we recently covered van Fraassen's Reflection Principle, as well as the diachronic Dutch book argument often used to defend it, and I've been trying to work out what the principle actually demands of me outside the seminar room. Suppose I'm currently about 50/50 on whether to accept a PhD offer, but I strongly suspect that a few years into the program, sunk costs and disciplinary socialization will have pushed my credence that "this was the right choice" well above 0.5, regardless of whether my reasons for feeling that way actually track the truth. Taken at face value, Reflection seems to tell me to shift my credence now toward whatever I predict my future self will believe. Could you lay out the most important implications of the Reflection Principle for a case like this: does it really commit me to that kind of deference, how have defenders of the principle tried to exclude cases of anticipated bias without draining it of its force, and where do those responses seem weakest to you?
