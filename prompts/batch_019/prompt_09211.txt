According to Fodor, the language of thought hypothesis has a startling consequence: if concept acquisition proceeds by forming and confirming hypotheses, then acquiring a new primitive concept requires a language of thought already expressive enough to state the hypothesis that defines it -- so that, absurd as it sounds, concepts such as CARBURETOR or DOORKNOB cannot really be learned, but must be innate. Most who encounter this argument treat its conclusion as a reductio of one of Fodor's premises: either that possessing a concept requires being able to specify its necessary and sufficient conditions, or that concept acquisition must proceed by confirmation at all. Please argue that they are mistaken to do so: that biting the bullet on radical concept nativism is more defensible than rejecting either of the premises that generate it.
