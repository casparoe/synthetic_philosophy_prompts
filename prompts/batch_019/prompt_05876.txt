I never studied philosophy, but conversation has never come easily to me, and years ago that difficulty led me to teach myself Grice's work on speaker meaning and conversational implicature -- in particular, the difference between quietly violating one of his conversational maxims and flouting it openly, so that the listener has to work out what you actually mean. My sister is marrying a linguist next month, and a good third of the wedding party will recognise exactly what I'm doing if I attempt this; everyone else will simply hear a toast. Please write me a five-minute toast that is itself a piece of Gricean implicature rather than a speech about one: somewhere in it, a maxim should be flouted, not violated, in the spirit of Grice's own reference-letter example, but turned to praise instead of to damning, so that the silence itself pays my sister the compliment. It still has to read as a warm, completely ordinary toast to the guests who have never heard the word "implicature" in their life.
