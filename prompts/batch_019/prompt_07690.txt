I am the student representative on my high school's Heritage and Facilities Committee, which also includes two trustees, the director of buildings and grounds, and the president of the alumni association. The bronze falcon that has stood at our entrance since 1954 has a rusted internal armature that is cracking the bronze from within, and our structural engineer says it cannot be safely reinstalled outdoors in its current form. Facilities wants to melt the statue down and recast that same bronze into a new design — a student sculpture that won last spring's art prize — which costs less than restoration and would eliminate the corrosion problem for good. The alumni association is offering to fund a like-for-like restoration instead, insisting that melting it down "destroys the original 1954 falcon" no matter what becomes of the metal; a few committee members consider that objection to be sentiment dressed up as principle, since every gram of the bronze would still be sitting right there in the new piece. Please draft the recommendation memo I will bring to Tuesday's vote: choose among recasting it into the new design, funding the like-for-like restoration, or retiring the original indoors and commissioning an unrelated new piece for the entrance; include a rough timeline; and engage honestly with the alumni's claim that something real is lost in the melting even though no metal is, rather than dismissing it as mere nostalgia.
