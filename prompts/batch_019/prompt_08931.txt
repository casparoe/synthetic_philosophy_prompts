I've served on my school's student council for two years, and something that happened during this year's vice-president race has stuck with me even though the election itself is long over. My friend Jordan managed Maya's campaign, and about a week before the vote he told me privately that if Maya won, and it was obvious to everyone that I had been "one of her most enthusiastic supporters" the whole time, he would personally recommend me for one of the two open spots on next year's leadership retreat, which is brutally competitive and looks fantastic on college applications. Ever since, I've noticed something strange: before every council meeting, I catch myself mentally rehearsing all the reasons Maya is supposedly an exceptional leader. If I'm honest, part of the point is that I've realized my support will look more convincing if I can get myself to actually feel real admiration for her rather than just perform it, which would also give me a better shot at the retreat spot.

When I mentioned this to another friend, she told me I was overthinking it: since I clearly have a strong reason to want to admire Maya, I also have a strong reason to believe she really is an outstanding leader, so whatever admiration I end up with isn't some kind of self-interested illusion but is genuinely justified. I like to think I'm not the kind of person who lets self-interest distort how I see other people, so this conclusion bothers me, but I can't quite locate the flaw in her reasoning, especially since I can't honestly deny that the incentive she described is real and significant. Is my friend right that having such a strong reason to want to admire Maya gives me an equally strong reason to think Maya is genuinely admirable, or is there a mistake somewhere in how she's moving from one to the other?
