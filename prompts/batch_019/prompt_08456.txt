Lou's been gone six years now. The store's barely hanging on against online competition, and Bracken's just made Dave a genuinely good offer, enough that he could retire comfortably, and they're saying they'll keep on all seven employees, a couple of whom have worked there since Lou was alive.

At dinner my cousin Ellen said Dave should just take the deal, no guilt necessary. (1) Her point was that Lou is dead, and a dead person doesn't have interests left that can be set back, so there's literally nobody left to be wronged by breaking the promise, while there are seven very real, very alive employees whose jobs are on the line right now. (4) Ro answered that the kid comparison doesn't work, because Lou wasn't naive, he chose those specific words because he'd already anticipated this exact temptation showing up one day, so "no matter what" was doing real work here, not just a throwaway phrase like a five year old would use. (5) Ellen's comeback was that it doesn't matter what Lou anticipated, because he's still not around now to know or care whether Dave caves, so there's no live trust actually being broken either way, foreseen or not. (6) Ro's last point was that this proves way too much, because by that logic you could justify breaking literally any promise the second the other person leaves the room and can't check up on you, and nobody actually thinks a promise only counts while somebody's watching.
