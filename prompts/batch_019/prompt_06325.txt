Since retiring from a career in nonequilibrium statistical mechanics, I have taken up philosophy of mind and philosophy of science as a serious pastime, and Karl Friston's free energy principle is one topic I keep returning to. I am already well acquainted with the standard "dark room" objection, so there is no need to rehearse it. Instead, I would like you to argue for the following, more structural claim: that the free energy principle's derivation trades on an equivocation between (a) the comparatively modest mathematical fact that any system with a Markov blanket and a nonequilibrium steady-state density can be redescribed as if its internal states minimize a variational free energy functional, and (b) the much stronger, substantive claim that perception, action, and biological self-organization are best explained as literal approximate Bayesian inference. In constructing the argument, engage specifically with what the Markov blanket formalism and the assumptions of ergodicity and a nonequilibrium steady state actually license, as opposed to what is merely read into them by the surrounding interpretive gloss.
