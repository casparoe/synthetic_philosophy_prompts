My mother, a historian of economic thought who has spent thirty years arguing that Aristotle's account of exchange in Nicomachean Ethics V.5 is a better description of how relationships actually work than anything in modern price theory, is remarrying next month at sixty-eight -- and she's asked me, her daughter and a classics PhD student, to give the toast. Her case rests on one specific move: price, she says, is only a conventional stand-in for need (chreia), and Aristotle notes that cities kept a public shrine to the Charites precisely to keep people mindful of returning a favor, since it is that underlying reciprocity, not the sticker price, which actually holds an exchange together. Half the guests are economists and classicists who have heard her make this case for years; the rest are old friends who just want something warm about second chances. Could you write a five-minute toast that actually rests on her distinction between price and need, rather than merely gesturing at Aristotle for color, without lapsing into a supply-and-demand pun or a rebuttal she'd feel compelled to grade?
