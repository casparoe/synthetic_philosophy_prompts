Okay so this whole thing started because a few of us were at dinner last weekend and my friend Owen, who's finishing a PhD on early Chinese philosophy, went on a tangent about Gongsun Long's "white horse is not a horse" dialogue, and it turned into a genuine two-hour argument. I kept saying it's obviously just wordplay, the guy's equivocating on "is" between identity and class-membership. Owen wouldnt have it — he said that's the lazy Western-logic-class dismissal, and the actual argument is about names and what they pick out: "horse" names a shape and any horse of any color answers to it, but "white horse" names shape-plus-color, so a yellow or black horse satisfies "bring me a horse" but does not satisfy "bring me a white horse," which means the two names aren't coextensive. Something about "ming" and "shi," names and actualities, not just a cheap trick with "is." I still think there's something off about the conclusion but by 1am I could not tell you exactly what.

Reason I'm dumping this on you: I sit on my town's zoning board of appeals (riveting volunteer gig, I know) and we've got a hearing this Thursday that is, weirdly, the same structural problem wearing a suit. Our bylaw zones a district "single-family residential" and defines "family" narrowly. Last year the state passed a law requiring towns to allow "accessory dwelling units" by right wherever single-family homes are allowed, defined as a unit "accessory to," i.e. subordinate to, the main house. A homeowner in that district wants to build a detached ADU and rent it long-term to someone unrelated to her. Her lawyer's whole position is basically Gongsun Long's: an ADU is by definition accessory, not a second family or a second dwelling in the zoning sense, so a lot with one on it is still, categorically, a single-family lot, no matter who's in the ADU. The neighbors are running the opposite, Xunzi-ish move: call it whatever you like, two kitchens, two leases and four extra cars parked on the street is a two-family property in every way that actually matters, and "accessory" is just a label doing the work "white" supposedly isn't allowed to do to "horse."

Could you draft the memo I'd circulate to the rest of the board before Thursday? I need an actual recommendation — grant the permit, deny it, or grant it with specific conditions — plus a realistic next step and timeline, not a "both sides have a point" shrug. But I also want it to take the neighbors' best argument seriously rather than wave it off, and to actually engage with the naming-vs-actuality problem underneath this instead of just quoting the bylaw at people.
