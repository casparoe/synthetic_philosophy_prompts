For a project in my philosophy elective, I've been working through the section of Nozick's Anarchy, State, and Utopia where he floats a two-tier moral scheme: "utilitarianism for animals, Kantianism for people." Human beings receive strict side-constraints that can never be outweighed by aggregate benefit, while animals' suffering and happiness can simply be weighed and traded off for the greater good. He tries to justify drawing the line there not by appealing to species membership directly, but by appealing instead to a creature's capacity to form and steer its life according to some "overall conception" it has chosen to accept. Could you construct a specific, concrete case that shows why anchoring the human/animal distinction in that capacity, rather than in species membership as such, creates serious problems for his scheme?
