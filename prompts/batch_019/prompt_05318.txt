I'm the only engineer on my fintech company's newly formed Model Risk & Fairness Committee, which has to sign off before our loan-underwriting model ships. Our data science team has shown us, mathematically, that we can't have it both ways. Option A uses a single risk threshold for everyone, so the model stays well-calibrated (predicted default risk matches realized default rates) within each of the two protected applicant groups we track for fair-lending compliance -- but because the groups have different true default rates, it also denies credit to proportionally more creditworthy applicants in Group B than in Group A. Option B uses a separate threshold for each group so that this false-positive rate is equalized instead, but then two applicants with identical risk scores can get opposite decisions based only on group membership, and calibration breaks down. Legal calls Option B a textbook disparate-treatment problem; two committee members counter that Option A just launders the same disparity through a neutral-looking score. We brief the board Tuesday and need a written recommendation beforehand. Draft that memo: choose Option A, Option B, or propose a third path, sketch a rollout timeline with monitoring milestones, and directly engage the strongest argument for whichever option you don't pick.
