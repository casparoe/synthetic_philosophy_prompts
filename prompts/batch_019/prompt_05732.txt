I am a novelist, and a decision from early in my career still unsettles me. Three months before my first book was due, the manuscript was collapsing and my publisher was ready to cancel the contract. A classmate from my MFA program, a far sharper writer than I have ever been, quietly rewrote half the book with me during that stretch, but only after I promised her, in a late-night phone call, a genuine share of whatever the book earned if it succeeded. Nothing was ever put in writing, and there was no way she could have forced me to honor it; she has since told me she believed my promise only because she was certain I was not the sort of person who would renege once I was safely past the deadline. The book succeeded far beyond what either of us expected, and no one in publishing knows how much of it is hers. A friend I confided in about all this says I am free and clear: the promise was made under pressure, it is not enforceable, and honoring it now would not change anything, so a generous gift is more than enough. That reasoning sounds airtight, but it does not sit right with me. Do I actually still owe my classmate the share I promised, or is my friend correct that a generous gift settles it?
