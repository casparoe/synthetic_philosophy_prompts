Before I record a grade, I'd like a more experienced second opinion on one particular answer, since I don't yet trust my own calibration.

Here is the question (worth 20 points):

"The 'disjunction problem' asks how a naturalistic theory of mental content can distinguish a state's misrepresenting its target from that state's simply coming to represent a disjunction of whatever triggers it. Using the case of a frog whose tongue-snapping mechanism fires both at flies and at BB pellets shot past it, explain how a teleosemantic theory of content, such as Millikan's, is supposed to solve this problem, and then briefly evaluate whether the solution succeeds."

And here is the student's answer:

"Teleosemantic theories solve the disjunction problem by fixing content historically instead of by looking at everything that can cause a representation to occur. On this view, what a state represents is whatever it is the biological function of the producing mechanism to detect, where a mechanism's function is whatever effect explains why it was retained by natural selection. In the frog's case, the tongue-snapping mechanism was selected for because triggering at flies led to the frog catching and eating them, and that nutritional payoff is what explains why frogs with the mechanism outreproduced frogs without it. So the mechanism's proper function is to detect flies, which is why Millikan can say the content of the frog's state is FLY rather than 'small dark moving thing' or 'fly-or-BB-pellet.' When a BB pellet sets the mechanism off, the state still means FLY; it has simply misfired. Since flies are the statistically normal trigger for the mechanism and BB pellets are a rare, abnormal one, this is exactly the situation Millikan has in mind when she calls fly-triggering the mechanism's 'Normal' condition of operation. She makes the resulting notion of content precise with the idea of asymmetric dependence: the mechanism's disposition to fire at BB pellets depends on its disposition to fire at flies, but not the other way around, so fly-detection is explanatorily prior and BB-pellet-triggering simply piggybacks on it. This is what lets teleosemantics succeed where a purely causal or informational theory could not, since the latter has no principled way to privilege one member of the disjunction over the others. I think the account is broadly successful, though it still owes us an explanation of why the content is FLY specifically and not something more general like AMBIENT NUTRITIOUS OBJECT, since flies matter to natural selection only because of the nutrition they provide."
