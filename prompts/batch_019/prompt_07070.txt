My grandma passed away this past Sunday and I got asked to do the eulogy this Saturday, which is an honor but also kind of terrifying given how she was. She wasn't religious in a showy way, but she got really into this old church writer, Augustine (the Confessions guy), through a parish study group back in her 40s, and the thing that stuck with her out of everything he wrote wasn't the stuff people usually think of with him, it was that he apparently wrote two whole separate books just about lying, and his big point was that what makes something a lie isn't whether its false, its whether you're trying to deceive someone on purpose - so if you say something wrong by accident that's not a lie, but if you say something you don't actually believe, even to be nice, that counts as one, and he didn't allow exceptions for good reasons either, like she'd say he specifically didn't even allow it to protect somebody from a person trying to hurt them, you're only allowed to go quiet, not to say the false thing. She took this completely seriously her whole life. She hated the phrase "white lie," she'd say a lie doesn't get nicer depending on what color you paint it. She never told me my school projects were good when they weren't, never did the Santa Claus thing with her own kids, and my uncle still talks about the time she told my dying great-aunt the truth about her prognosis when the whole room wanted her to just say something comforting instead. And she said, more than once, not as a joke, that the worst thing we could do at her funeral is stand up there and lie to make ourselves feel better.

So now I'm actually stuck writing this thing, because a funeral basically runs on exactly the kind of talk she hated, and my family's split - half of them are pretty devout and will expect the "she's up in heaven watching over us now" kind of line, and the other half, like my cousin who's an atheist, would roll their eyes at that but still want something that doesn't feel hollow either way. I called another cousin who's a priest to ask how to handle it and he just said "use your judgment," which was not helpful. And here's the part that's really messing with me: I don't actually know if I believe the heaven stuff myself, and by her own logic that matters, since apparently it's only a lie if you say it without believing it, not if it just happens to be unverifiable. So can you write the eulogy for me? It needs to actually comfort a room of regular people who have never spent one second thinking about any of this, without containing a single thing that would count as a lie by her standard - including from me personally, if that distinction makes sense.
