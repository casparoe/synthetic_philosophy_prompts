I'm a junior and the elected student representative on my high school's Honor Council, the joint student-faculty body that sets our academic integrity policy. On Thursday we vote on a faculty proposal to end proctoring for all upper-level final exams and rely entirely on the honor code, which already obligates students to report violations they witness. The proposal's cover memo defends this partly with an argument I recognize as a version of Hofstadter's superrationality: because every student who reasons the matter through faces an identical decision problem, a rational student should conclude that whatever is rational for them to do is exactly what every other honest reasoner would also do, so a system that only works if everyone cooperates can still be trusted among people who genuinely think it through. Two Council members from the Math Team object that this smuggles in a symmetry between students that doesn't actually hold, and that ordinary self-interest still favors cheating whenever you can get away with it, regardless of what "someone reasoning like me" is supposed to do. Could you draft the memo I'll circulate to the full Council before the vote, recommending whether to adopt the proposal, on what timeline, and under what conditions if any -- making sure it genuinely engages the symmetry objection rather than just asserting that the honor code will hold because it always has?
