A friend and I spent an evening arguing about population ethics without resolving anything. She maintains that critical-level utilitarianism is a genuine improvement on total utilitarianism: by subtracting a fixed positive constant from each person's welfare before summing, it blocks Parfit's Repugnant Conclusion, since a sufficiently large population of lives barely worth living can no longer come out ahead of a smaller, flourishing one. I countered that this move is not progress but relocation, since critical-level views thereby inherit Arrhenius's Sadistic Conclusion: that adding people with bad, negative-welfare lives can be better than adding people with good but sub-critical lives, other things equal. She remained unconvinced, and I'd like a sharper version of my objection. Please argue that critical-level utilitarianism is not a genuine improvement over total utilitarianism, on the grounds that whatever intuitions make the Repugnant Conclusion unacceptable make the Sadistic Conclusion equally unacceptable, and then address the strongest reply available on my friend's behalf.
