I'm writing a novel about a falling-out between two agricultural scientists: Elena, who defends her institute's animal-testing and breeding programs, and Marcus, who resigned over them. In the scene I'm drafting, Elena justifies her work with an idea she attributes to Robert Nozick: persons are shielded by inviolable, Kantian side-constraints against being sacrificed for the aggregate good, while animals fall under a purely utilitarian ledger, so their suffering can, in principle, be outweighed by human benefit. Marcus calls this — "utilitarianism for animals, Kantianism for people," a line Nozick tossed off in Anarchy, State, and Utopia and seemed to regret by the time he returned to ethics in The Examined Life — not a principled position but a line drawn to protect her conscience, since the argument from marginal cases sinks it on both flanks: some humans lack the rational agency the constraints are meant to track, while some animals plainly have more of it than those humans do. I haven't decided who should win this scene. Write an essay that weighs the strongest considerations on both sides of whether Nozick's asymmetry is a stable, principled position or an ad hoc compromise, and defend a definite verdict I could put in a character's mouth — not a balanced non-answer.
