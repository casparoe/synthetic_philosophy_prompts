I've been falling down a game theory rabbit hole for a piece on prediction markets and keep running into this "no-trade theorem" idea. As I understand it, two rational people who both know the other is rational can never agree to a bet based on one of them having secret info, because the mere fact that you're willing to make the trade tips the other person off that you know something, so they just walk away. Doesnt that basically mean insider trading should be self-correcting in real markets too -- like, the second an exec tries to quietly dump shares before bad news breaks, whoever's on the other side of that trade should smell a rat and refuse it? If the theorem's right, I'm honestly struggling to see why the SEC spends so much time and money chasing insider trading cases instead of just trusting the market to freeze those people out on its own.
