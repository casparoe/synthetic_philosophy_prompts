Ok this is a bit of a rabbit hole but bear with me. I was reading about split-brain patients (people who had the two halves of their brain surgically disconnected, back in the 60s/70s, to treat severe epilepsy) and the part that stuck with me wasn't the usual "two people living in one skull" thing everyone brings up -- its this other phenomenon, I think it was Gazzaniga's group that documented it, called cross-cuing, where even though the hemispheres can't talk to each other directly anymore they apparently find sneaky indirect ways to pass info back and forth anyway, like one side pulling a face or humming a certain way and the other side picking up on it as a clue. As a programmer that sounds a lot like two processes with no shared memory improvising a covert channel to leak bits to each other, which is a wild thing for a brain to be doing to itself.

Write me a short story that runs with that idea: two parts of a mind (or a system, or whatever you want) that supposedly cant communicate, finding a backchannel to coordinate anyway, and use that to actually dig into whether it means there's secretly one self under there or genuinely two that just learned to cooperate. Please don't do the cliché "guy literally argues with his own hand" story, and don't wrap it up with a tidy answer at the end -- I want to still be unsure which one it is when I'm done reading.
