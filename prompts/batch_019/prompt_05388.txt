I've been working through the philosophy-of-physics literature on Bell's theorem for a dissertation chapter, and I keep getting stuck on a distinction that popular treatments tend to elide entirely. The factorizability condition used to derive the Bell/CHSH inequalities can be split into two logically independent sub-conditions, generally called parameter independence and outcome independence (in the tradition of Jarrett and Shimony). Quantum mechanics, on the standard formalism, violates one of these while strictly respecting the other, which is exactly why Bell-inequality violations don't permit superluminal signaling. Could you explain, at an intuitive rather than formal level, what each sub-condition actually asserts, which one quantum correlations violate, and why a violation of the other would have been the more genuinely alarming result for relativistic causality -- rather than just telling me that "quantum mechanics is nonlocal"?
