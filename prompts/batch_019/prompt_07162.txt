At my company, a data scientist on our team argues that we should A/B test every significant product change before shipping it, on the grounds that testing can never leave us worse off than skipping it: in the worst case, the result is inconclusive or beside the point, and we just go ahead with whatever we would have done anyway. I find this hard to argue against directly, but it still doesn't sit right with me, since I've seen tests add weeks of delay to changes that were obviously good ideas, and I've seen a single noisy result talk a team out of a decision that was almost certainly correct. Is she right that gathering more data before deciding is essentially a free option, and if she isn't, exactly where does her argument break down?
