I cover science and tech for a mid-size paper, and mostly because of that beat I somehow got roped into being the "public member" on my state's forensic science oversight commission — the one seat that isn't a working scientist, cop, or prosecutor. We just got back an internal audit of the crime lab's latent print unit and its not pretty: in a blind re-test, several examiners reversed their own earlier "definite match" calls once they were told (falsely, as part of the test) that the suspect had already confessed, or had been cleared by other evidence. Lab leadership's position is that this only shows up in how examiners phrase their confidence for court, and that having a second examiner "confirm" the match afterward without seeing the case file is a perfectly adequate fix. A couple of commissioners who've been reading up on cognitive penetration of perception — the idea, I think from Itiel Dror's work or people building on it, that what you expect to see can shape the perceptual experience itself and not just the conclusions you draw from it afterward — think that fix is way too weak: if expectation shapes what the examiner perceptually registers as a matching ridge detail on the very first look, a blind check done later, on a judgment already formed, doesn't undo the damage.

We vote in three weeks on whether to mandate that examiners' very first look at a print happen with the case file fully masked (no info on suspects, confessions, or which side requested the comparison), instead of the lab's preferred lighter-touch fix of blind confirmation after the fact. Could you draft the short memo I'll circulate to the other commissioners beforehand? I need it to land on an actual recommendation, not "more research is needed," with a realistic phase-in timeline given the lab is already badly backlogged, and I want it to take seriously the lab director's best objection — that nobody actually knows whether this bias operates at the level of perception itself or only at the level of after-the-fact judgment and reporting, and that mandating full masking on a guess about which one it is could grind casework to a halt for a benefit that's still unproven. Don't hedge into a split-the-difference position just to be diplomatic — give me something the commission can actually vote up or down.
