We had an outage a few weeks back, and in the postmortem it came out that two things were going on at the same time: I'd pushed a caching config change that spiked load on one path, and completely separately, a connection-pool leak that had been building for days was on track to hit its ceiling on its own about six minutes later anyway. Derek (who pushed the config change) and Maya (who was on-call) ended up in this argument about whether his rollout actually caused the outage, and it derailed the whole meeting. I can't tell who's right, and now I can't stop thinking about it.

Roughly how it went: (1) Derek said it's simple — would the outage still have happened if his rollout hadn't happened? Yes, because the leak gets you there anyway, so by definition his change isn't a cause and shouldn't go in the root-cause field. (2) Maya said that test is broken because it can't distinguish a backup cause from the real one, and the better question is: if you could reach in and flip only the rollout, leaving the leak exactly as it was progressing, does anything about what actually happened change? She said yes — the timing changes, the error signature changes, and the fix that actually worked (rolling back his flag) wouldn't have touched the leak at all — so it's a cause. (3) Derek shot back that once "changes the timing or manner" counts as causing something, literally everything upstream is now a cause of the outage, including "we deployed that week at all," which makes root-causing meaningless since you can't list fifty things. (4) Maya said no, there's a principled line: you only count stuff that's an actual node doing actual work in the dependency graph of the system, not just anything temporally before it, and the rollout is clearly on that graph. (5) Derek said the graph she's pointing to is exactly the thing they disagree about — whether the leak and the rollout were on the same path or independent ones — so she's baking in the answer by how she draws it, and this is basically the exact problem his friend's Judea Pearl book warned him about with "interventionist" causation stuff. Neither of them budged and then we had to move on to the next incident.

Can you go through moves (1) through (5) and say, for each one, who's actually got the stronger point and why (please just number your answers 1-5 to match)?
