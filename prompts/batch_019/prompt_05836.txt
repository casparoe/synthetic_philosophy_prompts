My older brother is marrying a fellow economist next month. The two of them met in a social-choice-theory seminar, so a good third of the guests at the reception will be decision theorists who will catch every reference, while the rest will be family who have never heard of a von Neumann–Morgenstern utility function. As the one relative currently doing a PhD in economics, I have been asked to give the toast, and I want to build it around Harsanyi's aggregation theorem: if both partners' preferences over uncertain outcomes satisfy the expected-utility axioms, and their joint preference respects Pareto indifference, then household welfare must be some weighted sum of their two individual utility functions — except the theorem is notoriously silent on what those weights actually are, since each person's utility function is only ever defined up to its own positive affine transformation. Please write the toast, about three minutes read aloud, with one moment where that indeterminacy actually lands as a joke for anyone who knows the theorem, while still reading as a real compliment to everyone who doesn't, and without quietly promising the family more mathematical certainty about the marriage than Harsanyi's theorem could ever deliver.
