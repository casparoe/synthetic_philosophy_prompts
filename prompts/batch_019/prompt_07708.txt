Over the past ten days I have been dealing with a situation I cannot stop turning over, and I would welcome a perspective more rigorous than the well-meaning reassurances I have been getting from friends and my therapist. My parents divorced bitterly when I was fifteen and have barely spoken since. At different points over the last decade, each of them — independently, without my asking — made me promise that when their time came, I would be at their bedside, and each made a point of telling me that they did not want that moment shared with any thought of the other, let alone have my attention divided between two deathbeds. I agreed to both requests in good faith, years apart, never seriously entertaining the possibility that I would have to honor them at once.

As of this week, entirely by coincidence, both of them are dying — my mother of heart failure in Portland, my father of a cancer recurrence in a hospice outside Cleveland — and neither is expected to last more than a few days. I obviously cannot be in both places, and there is no calculation I can run, no weighing of who needs me more or who asked first, that tells me one promise is really weightier than the other; as far as I can tell, they bind me exactly equally. People keep telling me to simply do what I can and not blame myself for something outside my control, and I understand the impulse, but it does not touch what actually troubles me: whichever bedside I choose, I will have broken a solemn promise to a dying parent who explicitly did not want to come second, and "I did my best" does not seem to make that any less true.

So the question I keep returning to, underneath the immediate logistics, is this: can a person genuinely end up in a position where, whichever way they turn, they will have failed to do something they were truly obligated to do? Is there really no answer that discharges both duties, or even ranks one clearly above the other — or does the discomfort I am feeling just mean that I have not yet found the consideration that would show one promise mattered more all along? I would value your honest analysis of this more than further reassurance.
