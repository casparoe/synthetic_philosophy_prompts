A friend and I stayed up far too late arguing about whether 'disease' names a purely biological condition or is inherently evaluative, and it turns out to bear directly on a vote my employer's health-plan committee is about to take. She defended something close to Boorse's biostatistical theory: health as nothing more than statistically normal functioning for one's reference class. I kept insisting that no purely descriptive account can do the work, since calling something a disease already involves a judgment about harm. Here's the case: our plan ties fertility-treatment coverage to the traditional clinical marker of infertility (twelve months of unprotected heterosexual intercourse without conception), so single members and same-sex couples can only qualify by first self-funding several failed cycles of donor insemination, even if they already have a diagnosed condition like PCOS. Some committee members want eligibility tied purely to documented internal dysfunction, partner status aside; others think that abandons any real concept of disease and just ends up funding unmet reproductive desire. I sit on the committee and need to circulate a memo before next week's vote. Please draft it: recommend a specific eligibility definition, and engage honestly with the strongest version of whichever side you reject.
