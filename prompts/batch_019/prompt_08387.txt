So I've been chewing on this for like a week now and cant shake it. It started because my daughter made me watch that old black and white movie where the guy finds out his whole town would've basically fallen apart if he'd never been born, and how a bunch of tiny things he did without even thinking about them turned out to matter hugely down the line. Got me thinking though - doesn't that cut both ways? If some small good thing I do today (letting someone merge in traffic, picking up a wallet someone dropped, whatever) can snowball into something great thirty years from now, it could just as easily snowball into something terrible, and there's no way for me to ever know which, not because I haven't gathered enough info yet but because the effects just keep spreading out and mixing with everyone else's choices forever, in ways nobody could ever untangle. I mentioned this to a friend who actually reads philosophy for fun and she said theres apparently a real term for this in ethics - something about how theories that judge actions by their consequences run into trouble because you can never actually add up all the consequences, so you're not just uncertain whether you did right, you're "clueless" in some deeper way, and she wasn't even totally sure she had it right either. Kind of freaked me out honestly, because if you take it seriously it sort of means you can never know, even in principle, whether trying to be a good person actually makes things better.

What I'd love is a short story that actually sits with how unsettling that is, instead of doing the cute It's-a-Wonderful-Life thing where you find out at the end it all worked out for the best. Maybe center it on one small, totally ordinary decision - nothing trolley-problem dramatic - and then follow two or three different threads of how that decision ripples outward over years or decades, with at least one thread clearly good and at least one clearly bad or seriously ambiguous, far enough out that neither the character nor the reader could ever trace it back with any real confidence. No need to namedrop any philosophy in the actual story, I just want it to leave the reader with that specific "i will genuinely never know if what I did was right" feeling instead of a tidy moral at the end. Doesn't need to be super long, 800-1000 words is probably plenty, and I'll leave the setting and characters totally up to you.
