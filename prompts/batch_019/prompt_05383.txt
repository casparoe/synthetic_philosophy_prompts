My closest friend is getting married next month, and I have been asked to give the best man speech. He wrote his dissertation on the philosophy of promising and remains persuaded by a broadly Humean view of the subject: that the obligation to keep a promise is not created by some special mental act of self-binding at the altar, but is a matter of convention, sustained by the expectations a promise deliberately raises in the person to whom it is made and by the usefulness of the practice in making trust and cooperation possible at all. On his view, talk of vows forging some further bond that exists independently of that trust is simply confused, and he has asked, half-jokingly, that the speeches not lean on it. About half the room will be his philosophy department, who will notice immediately if I fudge the point; the other half are relatives who have never heard of Hume on promises and just want a good toast. Write the speech: five minutes long, warm and genuinely funny, faithful to his view of why promises bind, and moving enough that everyone in the room, philosophers included, has real reason to be glad they came.
