I work as a backend engineer, and lately I've been reading around the edges of the AI safety via debate literature. The core problem it addresses -- getting a less capable verifier to correctly adjudicate the output of a much more capable, opaque process -- reminds me of designing protocols for mutually distrustful parties, which is closer to my day job than most alignment research usually gets. I keep running into the same cluster of objections to debate as a scalable oversight mechanism, and I'd like help thinking through which of them are actually fatal to the approach versus which look more like engineering problems that better protocol design could plausibly fix.

Please rank the following five objections from most to least fundamental, where "most fundamental" means the objection would still undermine debate as a route to scalable oversight even after substantial further refinement of the protocol, and "least fundamental" means you expect it to be largely addressed by refinements such as more debate rounds, better decomposition of claims, or improved cross-examination:

1. Obfuscated arguments: a dishonest debater can spread a subtle flaw across many sub-claims, or simply exhaust the allotted rounds, so that no single exchange the judge inspects is clearly false.
2. Persuasion/correctness gap: a debater optimized to win the judge's favor may become more persuasive rather than more honest whenever the two diverge.
3. Shared blind spots: debate's incentive to expose error relies on the judge's mistakes being asymmetrically exploitable; a bias neither debater can exploit any better than the other gives neither side a reason to correct it.
4. Decomposition assumption: debate's theoretical guarantees presuppose that a disputed claim can be broken into a bounded tree of independently checkable sub-claims, which may not hold for many empirical, ethical, or aesthetic questions.
5. Consistency-check evasion: cross-examination assumes a dishonest debater will eventually contradict itself, but a sufficiently capable one might maintain a coherent false position indefinitely.
