In my epistemology seminar this term we've been working through pragmatic encroachment and contextualism as rival explanations for why knowledge attributions shift with stakes. After class, two classmates, Nora and Owen, argued about a case from the problem set, and neither landed a clean win.

The case: a teaching assistant, Elena, checks a derivation once against a formula she half-remembers from lecture, and on that basis is happy to say "I know this is right" when she's only prepping for her own weekend review. With exactly the same evidence, she instead says "I don't know -- I should check the solutions manual" once that same answer key is about to go out to three hundred students before a final.

Nora said this is a clean case of pragmatic encroachment: Elena's evidence never changes, and there's no second speaker or context-shift anywhere in the story -- it's one person, effectively talking to herself -- so whatever is doing the work has to be Elena's own stakes, meaning knowledge itself, not just the word "know," is sensitive to practical factors.

Owen said this doesn't get past contextualism, because whoever narrates or evaluates the story -- including the two of them, arguing about it right now -- supplies a context of their own, and that context's standards rise the moment three hundred students and a final exam enter the description, regardless of what is or isn't happening inside Elena's head.

Nora replied that this proves too much: if merely narrating a high-stakes scenario always raises the narrator's own standards, no case could ever distinguish contextualism from pragmatic encroachment, since every case has to be narrated by someone.

Owen answered that this is a real cost of his view but not a fatal one -- at most it shows the two theories are hard to pull apart extensionally, which is a known limitation of the debate, not a reason to prefer Nora's theory over his.

Could you go through their exchange move by move and say who has the better of the argument at each stage? I'd also like to know what, if anything, both of them are missing, and what a genuinely decisive case would have to look like.
