So I've fallen down a bit of a rabbit hole because of work. I do dashboard/data-vis design for a health-data startup, and my boss asked me to "make it more visual, less spreadsheet-y," which somehow led me into the history of pictogram design and from there to Otto Neurath and the whole Isotype project from the 1920s-30s. I knew infographics predate Excel obviously, but I didn't realize how strict and honestly kind of philosophical Neurath was about it. It wasn't just "pictures instead of words" -- he had actual rules, like: you never show a bigger quantity by making one icon bigger, you show it by repeating the same-size icon more times, because resizing messes with how people perceive area/volume vs. actual value and is basically a form of visual lying to him. And all of this was tangled up with his Vienna Circle stuff -- unified science, physicalism, wanting a standardized "picture language" that ordinary people could read without needing a translator or even literacy.

Now I can't unsee how almost every modern infographic breaks his rule -- bubble-size COVID maps, growing/shrinking icons in news graphics, all of it. And I keep thinking about emoji too, which feel like the closest thing we actually got to his dream of an international picture language, except nobody designed them with anything like his rigor -- they're mushy, emotional, mean different things in different contexts, and look different on every platform depending who renders them.

So here's my actual question: if you could sit Neurath down in front of a smartphone today, what do you think his honest reaction would be? Would he see emoji as some kind of fulfillment (even a degraded one) of the picture-language project he spent his life on, or would he say they fail to be a "language" in his sense at all? And the resized-icon thing in data journalism -- minor technical foul in his eyes, or exactly the kind of visual propoganda he was trying to build a whole discipline to prevent? I'd like this grounded in what he actually believed and wrote, not just "he liked pictures so he'd probably like emoji."
