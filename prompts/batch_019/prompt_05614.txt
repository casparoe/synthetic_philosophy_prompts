My first writing teacher is retiring after forty years running the same fiction workshop, and I have been asked to give the toast at her farewell dinner. She has always judged fiction—student drafts and, later, my own published novels—by Tolstoy's 'infection' test from *What Is Art?*: not craft, only whether a piece carries one sincere, particular feeling that any reader can actually catch. She has never let me forget that, by that standard, my own novels belong with the 'bad art' Tolstoy eventually decided even his own *War and Peace* was. Half the room will be former students who know his opera-rehearsal chapter cold; the other half will be family who have never read a word of it. Write the toast—five minutes, anchored in that rehearsal scene—honest about exactly where her standard leaves my work, yet still unmistakably a gift, not a verdict.
