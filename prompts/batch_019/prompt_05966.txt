We just finished All Quiet on the Western Front in English class, and my teacher mentioned that the questions it raises about ordinary soldiers connect to a live debate in just war theory over the "moral equality of combatants." The traditional view, associated with Michael Walzer, holds that a soldier who fights within the rules of war bears no personal guilt for the justice of his cause; revisionists like Jeff McMahan argue that fighting for an unjust cause is itself a wrong that no amount of "clean" fighting cancels out. I haven't been able to stop thinking about it since, so could you write me a short story of around 1,000 words, centered on a low-ranking soldier on the unjust side who kills an enemy combatant in a lawful, fair, one-on-one engagement, and let the story sit with whether he has done nothing wrong, something seriously wrong, or something in between, without settling the question for the reader?
