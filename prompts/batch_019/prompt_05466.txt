I'm a PhD student working in philosophy of physics, and I keep getting stuck on a gap that the standard textbook contrast between epistemic and ontic structural realism doesn't really resolve. The epistemic version holds that we can know only the relational structure of unobservable reality, not the intrinsic nature of the relata, but this looks vulnerable to the Newman objection: any domain of sufficient cardinality trivially instantiates whatever abstract structure you like, so the epistemic claim threatens to collapse into something nearly empty. Ontic structural realists try to get around this by denying that there are relata with any nature prior to or independent of the relations at all, often motivating the move with the individuality puzzles raised by indistinguishable particles in quantum statistics. What I can't work out is whether dropping the relata like this genuinely dissolves the Newman-style triviality worry, or whether it just relocates the same problem into the metaphysics of structure itself. Could you compare the two positions specifically on this point, and tell me, with reasons, which one actually comes out ahead?
