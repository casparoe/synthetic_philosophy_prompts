I'm the philosopher on my university's Faculty Conduct Committee, and I've been asked to draft the memo we'll circulate before next week's vote. A tenured colleague has had three documented outbursts this year toward staff and junior faculty -- shouting twice, and once throwing a folder across a conference room -- since returning from medical leave after a frontal-lobe stroke. A neuropsychological evaluation attributes the outbursts to reduced impulse control; his memory, reasoning, and teaching evaluations are all reportedly unaffected. He acknowledges the incidents, says he can no longer always "catch it in time," and is asking that we handle this as a medical accommodation rather than discipline. Our faculty code routes cases either to a disciplinary track, which can end in dismissal for cause, or to a fitness-for-duty track that suspends discipline pending treatment, but it says nothing about which applies when a diagnosed condition is plausibly implicated in the misconduct itself. The committee is also split over a further wrinkle: every incident targeted someone junior to him, never the chair, a dean, or anyone positioned to retaliate. Some take this as evidence he retains more control than he claims; others point out that this kind of selective expression is well documented in genuine impulse-control conditions and so proves little either way. Please draft the memo: it should recommend a track (or a specific combination of both) with concrete next steps and a timeframe, take the case for medical accommodation as seriously as the case for continued discipline, and address what, if anything, is owed to the colleagues and staff he's targeted, independent of how we classify his condition.
