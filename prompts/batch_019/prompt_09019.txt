Two nights ago, at the rehearsal dinner, my friend Priya and I got into an argument that ran for the better part of an hour and nearly derailed the toasts. Priya, who clerks for a committed textualist judge, found a typo in the vows she and Daniel had drafted together and wanted to fix it by invoking the scrivener's-error exception that even hardline textualists allow; I spent the rest of dinner needling her that reaching for any purpose-based escape hatch, however narrow, was really a concession that purposivism wins in the end. She never conceded the larger point. I'm giving the toast at their wedding tomorrow, to a room split about evenly between fellow law clerks who will notice if I get the doctrine wrong and relatives who have never heard the word "purposivism." Please write me a five-minute toast that makes our argument its running joke, built around that scrivener's-error moment, without letting the joke flatten either textualism or purposivism into a strawman - both Priya and Daniel will be listening closely enough to catch it - and that still lands, first and foremost, as a love letter to the two of them rather than a lecture on statutory interpretation.
