My sister is getting married in a few weeks and has asked me — her older brother and the family's resident philosophy nerd — to give the toast. She and her fiancé met in the chorus of a regional production of Così fan tutte, so I've been re-reading Bernard Williams's On Opera, where he resists treating the piece as just a cynical farce about interchangeable lovers. That's awkward material for a wedding: the plot is essentially an experiment to test whether the sisters' fidelity survives the wrong circumstances, and it doesn't. It strikes me that this sits close to Williams's better-known idea of moral luck — the thought that character and constancy are more hostage to circumstance than we'd like to believe. I'd like a five-minute toast that is honest about what the opera actually stages, uses that idea to say something true and warm about lifelong commitment rather than a comforting lie, and still works for a room split between opera people who know the piece cold and relatives who've never heard of it, without turning into a philosophy lecture.
