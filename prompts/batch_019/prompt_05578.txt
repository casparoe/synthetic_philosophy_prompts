Piccione and Rubinstein's "absent-minded driver" problem rarely receives the attention it deserves outside of technical decision theory, yet it raises, in an unusually pure form, a question that should trouble anyone who assumes Bayesian updating is simply mandatory for rational agents facing uncertainty about their own situation. A driver must travel along a highway with two consecutive, visually identical exits. Taking the first exit yields a payoff of 0; missing both exits yields a payoff of 1; taking the second exit yields the best payoff, 4. Because the driver cannot tell, at either exit, whether it is the first or the second, she must commit in advance to using one and the same probability, p, of continuing at both. Maximizing expected payoff over choices of p gives p = 2/3.

Now suppose the driver actually arrives at an exit and must decide what to do. What should she believe, at that moment, about her location? One answer holds that, since the two exits are subjectively indistinguishable, she should split her credence 50/50 between them. A rival calculation weights each exit by how often it is actually reached under strategy p, and assigns her instead a credence of 1/(1+p) of being at the first exit. These are not merely two descriptions of the same fact: fed into ordinary expected-utility reasoning at the moment of choice, the two credences recommend different actions, and only one of them can be squared with p = 2/3 being the right strategy after all.

Write an essay addressing this controversy. Explain what, if anything, is wrong with each of the two credences described above; engage with what Piccione and Rubinstein themselves call the distinction between "planning optimality" and "action optimality" (or with some other resolution you find more compelling); and defend a definite answer to the question of whether the absent-minded driver case exposes a genuine breakdown of Bayesian reasoning about one's own location, or merely a confusion that dissolves once the relevant notions are properly sorted out. Do not settle for a non-committal survey of the options.
