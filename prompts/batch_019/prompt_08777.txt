So this happened during a postmortem at work a few weeks back and it's been bugging me ever since. We had this gnarly intermittent bug in prod, like 1 in a couple thousand requests would just hang forever. Two theories came out of the investigation. My skip-level's theory was "it's just network flakiness under load" - you can say the whole thing in one breath, sounds nice and simple. But to actually make it match what we were seeing, you had to bolt on a bunch of extra assumptions: a specific concurrency threshold, a particular retry/backoff interaction, some load balancer setting that only ever seemed to bite on Tuesdays for reasons nobody could explain. My theory was a single off-by-one bug in how we evict entries from a shared cache. It sounds way more "technical" and complicated when you actually say it out loud, but it's a one-line fix, and it accounts for literally every single failed request in the logs, no exceptions, no asterisks.

My skip-level ended the meeting with "let's not overthink this, the simpler explanation is usually the right one," and we shipped his fix. It didn't work - mine did, a week later, after we'd wasted a week chasing his. What's been eating at me isn't really being right, it's that I can't tell if his reasoning was even good in principle or just sounded good. By one way of measuring "simple" - how short the explanation is, how few new ideas you need to introduce to say it to a room of people - his theory genuinely was simpler then mine. But by another way of measuring it - how many separate extra assumptions you have to add on to make it actually fit the data - mine was way simpler, and it also happened to be true. So is "go with the simpler explanation" even a coherent rule, if it can point in totally opposite directions depending on which of those two things you mean by "simple"? Or is one of those two actually the "real" notion of simplicity the rule is supposed to be about, and my skip-level was just using it wrong? I don't have any philosophy training, this is just a thing engineers say to each other constantly like it's obviously true, and now I'm not sure any of us could actually defend it if pushed.
