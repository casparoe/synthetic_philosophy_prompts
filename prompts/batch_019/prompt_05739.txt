I've been down a rabbit hole reading about Quine's 'gavagai' thing (indeterminacy of translation) after it came up in a philosophy of language subreddit, and it seems to me like its basically the same deal as underdetermined parameters in ML — like when two completely different models fit your training data perfectly but disagree on new inputs. If thats a fair comparison, isn't indeterminacy of translation just an old-school version of that problem, and therefore not actually that deep? Couldn't you in principle settle which translation manual is "correct" just by collecting more behavioral data (more sentences, more contexts, whatever), the same way you'd reduce underdetermination by adding more training examples? Everyone in these threads talks about gavagai like its some huge unsolved mystery about meaning and reference, but from where I'm sitting it just looks like a data sparsity problem that Quine didn't have the tools to get around yet. Am I missing something, or is this really just overfitting with extra philosophy vocab?
