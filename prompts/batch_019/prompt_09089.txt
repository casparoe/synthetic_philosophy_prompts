So my friend Owen is getting married in about six weeks and asked me to give the best man speech, and I am mildly panicking. Backstory: Owen wrote his senior thesis on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility and has never really recovered -- he's the guy at parties who will gently ruin "you should be so proud of yourself" by explaining that you can't be truly responsible for the way you are, since being responsible for that would require having chosen to be that way, which just pushes the question back to why you made that choice, forever, so nobodys ever really the ultimate author of themselves. (He's very fun at parties.) I'm actually in the free will and moral responsibility seminar this semester where we're covering the Basic Argument properly, and now that I understand it better I've realized my speech has a real problem: basically every stock wedding-toast line -- "you two deserve each other," "look at the person you've become," "you built this life through who you really are" -- quietly assumes exactly the kind of ultimate self-authorship he thinks is impossible, and he will notice, even at his own wedding, even a little drunk.

I don't want to just dodge the philosophy and write something generic, because half the guests are people from our undergrad philosophy cohort who will absolutely clock it if I chicken out, and the other half are family and coworkers who have never heard of Galen Strawson and shouldn't have to. Can you write me a warm, genuinely funny 4-5 minute best man speech that takes the Basic Argument seriously -- including the bit where it doesn't even need determinism to be true, so "it's all just quantum randomness anyway" isn't an escape hatch for him either -- without turning into a philosophy lecture, and without secretly smuggling in the "you earned this" language he'd wince at? Extra credit if you can work in something true and fun about how even his intellectual hero's own dad, P.F. Strawson, spent his career defending basically the opposite view.
