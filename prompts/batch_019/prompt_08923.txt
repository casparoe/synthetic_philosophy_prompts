So this came up at a family dinner last week and I haven't been able to let it go. My nephew is a few years into a number theory PhD, and he and his housemate have this running bet — loser buys the beer — over whether some enormous number they cooked up (150-odd digits, no special form, nothing convenient like a Mersenne number) is prime. Neither of them will just run a primality test on it, becuase apparently chipping away at it by hand with clever tricks over the next few months is half the fun of the bet. Being me, I couldn't resist asking what odds each of them would actually take, expecting a throwaway answer, and instead I started a real argument. My nephew's position was that the whole question is confused: the number is, right now, already either prime or it isn't, so the "true" probability is 0 or 1 and we just don't happen to know which — meaning any betting odds are basically just vibes, not the kind of thing that can be more or less rational. His housemate thought this was ridiculous, and pointed out that you can obviously use stuff like the density of primes near numbers of that size to defend some odds over others, even knowing nothing else about this particular number.

I teach epistemology, so I figured there had to be a clean answer to this, but I honestly got stuck. It seems clearly wrong to say all odds are equally defensible here — and yet my nephew's deeper point, that ordinary probability theory quietly assumes you already know all the logical consequences of everything you know (and so has nothing to say about degrees of belief in a specific, not-yet-settled claim like "this number is prime"), also feels like it's onto something real, not just a debating trick. I ran it by a colleague in the math department the next day, and even she couldn't quite cash out the disagreement precisely, which only made me want a proper answer more.

Could you help me actually adjudicate this? I want to know whose intuition is closer to right, and, more importantly, whether there's a principled reason — not just a psychological or pragmatic one — for treating some betting odds on "this specific number is prime" as more rational than others, given that the matter is already settled one way or the other as a plain mathematical fact.
