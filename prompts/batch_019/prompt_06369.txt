So this weekend I got into it with my friend Jake about something going on with my parents' lake community and I honestly can't let it go. My parents live on a smallish lake with an HOA that handles stuff like dock permits, no-wake zones, and noise curfews. Only the ~40 households that are dues-paying members actually get to vote on any of this. But there's also a bunch of long-term renters living in HOA houses who are on the lake just as much as the owners (more, actually, since half the owners only show up in summer), plus 6 or 7 houses right on the water that were built before the HOA existed and never joined, so they get zero protections and zero vote, and theres a little marina renting out kayaks and pontoons whose entire business depends on how the lake gets used, and they obviously don't get a vote either since they're not "residents."

It all came to a head because the HOA is about to vote on restricting wake boats (apparently they tear up the shoreline and everyone's dock), and my mom, who's on the board, was venting that it's become a whole mess because every one of these other groups wants input, but "the whole point of an HOA is that members decide, that's what membership means." Jake basically took her side: once you say "anyone affected gets a say," where does it stop? Technically the marina, the renters, the neighbors, even some rando who launches a boat off the public ramp once a summer are all affected to some degree, so you have to draw a line somewhere, and paid-up membership is at least a real, non-arbitrary line. I said that feels like it proves too much - by that logic pretty much any group could justify excluding anyone, as long as they've officially defined themselves as a members-only club. Then I pointed out that the old neighboring houses can technically apply to join the HOA, which means the current members are also the only ones who get to vote on whether to let them in, so the people who benefit from staying small are literally the ones deciding whether to grow. Jake just said that's true of every club, team, or country that's ever existed, so it can't really be a problem, or nothing would ever get to make rules for itself. We went back and forth on this for like an hour and neither of us budged.

Who's actually right here, and about which part? Is Jake correct that you basically can't avoid an arbitrary-ish line, and that the "who decides who gets to join" circularity is a non-issue since every self-governing group runs into it? Or is there a more principled way to figure out who should get a say in a situation like this - and if there is, does it actually change anything for the renters, the old lake houses, or the marina, or does it just collapse back into "dues-paying members" no matter what?
