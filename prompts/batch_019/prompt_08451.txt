My parents are getting older and have started talking about how they eventually want to split their savings between me and my two siblings, and it's turned into this slow-burn argument that's been bugging me for weeks, ever since it first came up at Christmas dinner. My sister was in a bad car crash a few years back and now needs a wheelchair, home modifications, ongoing physio, the whole deal, just to have anything like a normal life, so everyone in the family agrees pretty easily that she should get a bigger cut to cover all that. Nobody's really fighting about her part.

The part I can't wrap my head around is my brother. He doesn't have any disability, he's just always been the type to develop genuinely expensive tastes - he can't relax on a holiday unless its a nice resort, gets weirdly low for days if he has to fly economy, that sort of thing, and it doesn't seem like an act, he actually feels it. His argument is that if the whole reason for giving my sister more is "give people what they need so everyone ends up equally well off," then he should logically get extra too, since he needs more money than the rest of us just to be content, so its the same principle underneath, just a different flavor of need.

Something about this feels wrong to me, but every time my family asks me to explain why, I end up just repeating myself or sounding like I'm calling my brother shallow, which isn't really what I mean. It's not that I think he's faking being unhappy with less. I just can't shake the feeling that what my sister needs and what my brother wants aren't the same kind of thing, even though from the outside they both look like "needs more resources to reach the same happiness level." My partner says the whole family is overthinking a basic money split, but I keep thinking there's an actual distinction buried in here about what a "fair share" even means when people's needs come from such different places. So who's actually right, me or my brother, or is it more complicated than either of us is making it out to be?
