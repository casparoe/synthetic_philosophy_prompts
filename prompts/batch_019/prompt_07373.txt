So this happened at a dinner party and it's been bugging me since. A friend of a friend spent months torn between two job offers — a well-paid consulting job and a much lower-paid one at a tiny design studio he actually loved — and eventually just took the design job because it "felt right." Two people at the table would not let this go.

One guy said the two jobs are basically incommensurable, no common scale to weigh them on, and if neither one was actually better than the other then there was no real reason to pick either one — so "going with his gut" was just a coin flip wearing a nicer outfit.

The woman next to him said thats too fast: if the jobs were simply equal, sweetening the design job with even a small raise should obviously have tipped it, and it clearly wouldn't have — so "equal" isn't the right word for whatever's going on between them either.

He said fine, not equal then, but not better or worse either — which only proves his point harder, since now there's no fact at all about which one he had more reason to take, so its still arbitrary no matter what fancy name you put on the relationship.

She said arbitrary would mean it shouldn't matter who's choosing or how they go about it, but this is exactly the kind of decision where committing yourself to one option is what makes it the right call for you afterward, and that's not the same thing as randomness.

He shot back that this sounds very convenient for her theory, since it means a hard choice can basically never turn out to have been a mistake — apparently committing to literally anything makes it retroactively fine.

They both turned to me like I was supposed to break the tie and I had nothing. Can you go through this move by move and say who's got a point and who doesn't at each step (not just who "wins" overall), and flag anything important you think both of them are missing?
