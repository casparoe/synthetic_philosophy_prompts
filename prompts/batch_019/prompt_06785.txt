My uncle runs a small hardware store, and the stockroom has had a mouse problem for months. He uses cheap glue traps, which can take a mouse hours or even days to die, rather than quicker alternatives that cost only a little more. When I objected, he said the mice can't actually be wronged by how they die, since they have no grasp of fairness and can't hold any claim against him; in his view, the only reason to prefer the quicker method is that getting comfortable with causing suffering might make him a harsher, less careful person in general, including toward people. He added that if he could somehow be certain this habit wouldn't affect his character at all, switching traps would make no moral difference. That last part bothers me, but I can't quite explain why, especially since I can't simply appeal to the mice having rights when he's already dismissed that idea. Is his reasoning actually consistent, and if not, exactly where does it break down?
