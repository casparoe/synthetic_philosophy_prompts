I spent thirty-five years as an evolutionary biologist studying reciprocal altruism in primates, and since retiring I've been teaching myself metaethics, where I keep returning to evolutionary debunking arguments. The sharpest version I've come across is Sharon Street's dilemma: either natural selection shaped our evaluative attitudes by tracking independent moral truths -- which strikes me, on biological grounds, as an extravagant hypothesis -- or it shaped them for fitness alone, in which case it would be a remarkable coincidence if those attitudes also happened to be true. I would like a short story built around a character who is forced to confront that dilemma through some concrete moral conviction of their own, ideally someone whose training or work gives them a personal stake in taking the evolutionary story seriously. Within the story itself, please give real weight to at least one serious realist reply -- a third-factor explanation, say, or Parfit's point that necessary normative truths require no more of a causal backstory than mathematical ones do -- so that the debunking worry is not allowed to win, or lose, too easily by the end.
