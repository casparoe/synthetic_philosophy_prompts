Ok here's one I keep coming back to. There's a whole "griefbot" industry now -- companies that take someone's old texts, voicemails, photos and videos after they die and train a chatbot or voice clone on it, so family can keep texting or video-calling a simulation of grandma, or a dead kid, indefinitely. I want your best guess at what Zhuangzi would make of this, from the actual text, not just vibes.

On one hand, this is the guy who drums on a basin and sings when his wife dies, explaining that carrying on mourning her would just show he doesn't understand fate. He's also got that bit in the Dazongshi where the dying man compares himself to a lump of metal in a furnace, and says a person who insists on staying human-shaped forever is basically a freak as far as the cosmic blacksmith's concerned -- zero attachment to any particular form persisting, personality included. That reading suggests griefbots would strike him as beside the point at best.

But then theres the skull he meets on the road in the "Perfect Happiness" chapter, who frowns and refuses when Zhuangzi offers to have it restored to flesh, family, the whole bit. That looks like real evidence he'd think dragging the dead back for the sake of the living gets things backwards -- not neutral, actively wrongheaded.

So which is it -- does his metaphysics of constant transformation make him basically unbothered by digital simulacra of the dead, or is the griefbot industry exactly the kind of clinging he spent his whole philosophy arguing against? Pick one and defend it using the stories, don't just hedge between both.
