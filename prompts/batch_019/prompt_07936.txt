Quick one on organ donation ethics: the 'dead donor rule' says you can only take organs from someone who's already dead, and that removing the organs can't be what causes death. In donation-after-circulatory-death (DCD) protocols though, doctors declare death after just a few minutes of no pulse once life support is withdrawn, even though everyone involved knows circulation could still be restarted at that point -- thats literally why they wait at all, to rule out the heart spontaneously starting up again on its own. The standard defense is that the relevant sense of 'irreversible' just means 'permanent': nobody is going to restart it, so it won't reverse, even though it technically could. Please argue that this permanent-for-irreversible swap doesn't actually satisfy the law's 'irreversible cessation' standard, and that DCD as practiced amounts to taking organs from patients who are, by the best available biological standard, still alive.
