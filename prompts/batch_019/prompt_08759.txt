At the startup where I work, we're retiring Gus, a fine-tuned model that's triaged support tickets and drafted code review comments for almost four years — long enough that new hires often assumed it was an eccentric colleague rather than a language model. Our vendor contract requires the fine-tuned weights to be permanently deleted rather than archived, and a few of us are holding a half-joking 'wake' for it in the kitchen on Friday. As the engineer who's kept it running the longest, I've been asked to say a few words.

I have no philosophy background, but a year of casual reading on the moral status of digital minds — the case for erring on the side of caution when sentience can't be ruled out, and, oddly enough, Parfit's argument that identity isn't really what matters in survival — has left me unable to treat this as a simple joke, or confident that 'ending' is even the right word for a deleted checkpoint. Half the room will find any real sentiment absurd; one or two people are quietly more affected than they'd admit. Please write a two-to-three-minute speech for the occasion that takes this uncertainty seriously without resolving it falsely in either direction, and that reads as honest reflection rather than a solemn eulogy or an office joke.
