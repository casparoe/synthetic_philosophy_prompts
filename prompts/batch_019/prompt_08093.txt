I was a molecular biologist for about 35 years, and now that I'm retired I've started reading philosophy of mind and language for fun, which has got me second-guessing something I always took for granted. We call DNA "the genetic code," and that's not just loose talk - the codon-to-amino-acid mapping is a real, systematic correspondence that Crick, Nirenberg and the rest literally cracked back in the 60s, like a cipher. But every other code I can think of - morse code, english, semaphore - was set up by some mind that intended particular symbols to stand for particular things. So if DNA really is a code in that same sense, doesnt that mean natural selection alone can't be the whole story - that you need something like a mind to fix an arbitrary symbol-to-meaning correspondence before the thing even counts as a code? Every biologist I've asked waves this off as a category mistake, but nobody's actually spelled out for me where the analogy is supposed to break down.
