So this came up at a dinner party a couple weeks ago, in one of those conversational spirals that starts with "have you seen Oppenheimer" and ends forty minutes later in moral philosophy -- somebody brought up that old Anscombe pamphlet from the 50s where she goes after Oxford for giving Truman an honorary degree, basically calling him a murderer over the bomb. Two friends of mine, Nadia and Chris, got into a proper back-and-forth about whether her argument actually holds up, and honestly niether of us at the table could settle it.

1. Nadia said Anscombe's right: the whole point of hitting a city instead of a military target was to kill huge numbers of civilians and shock Japan into surrendering, so the deaths were the means, not a side effect, and killing innocents as a means is murder no matter how many lives it supposedly saved later.
2. Chris said you could just as easily describe the intention as destroying Japan's capacity and will to keep fighting, with mass civilian death as a foreseen but not strictly intended cost -- the same double-effect move used to justify a ton of "ordinary" bombing campaigns.
3. Nadia said that redescription doesn't work here, because the strategy specifically depended on mass civilian death doing the coercive work -- you don't get to launder your way out of intending the very thing your plan requires.
4. Chris said fine, but then her standard convicts basically all of strategic bombing, sieges, and sanctions regimes too, since those also "work" by inflicting mass civilian suffering to change a government's behavior -- and if the theory ends up calling most of modern war murder, that's a reason to doubt the theory.
5. Nadia said that's not a bug, that's basically her point: most of that probably is murder by her lights, and everyone doing it doesn't make it not-murder -- there's still a real, if fuzzy, line between aiming at a population and merely foreseeing deaths from a legitimate target.

Could you adjudicate this move by move, using Anscombe's actual argument (not just vibes about the bomb) as the reference point? Give me a JSON object scoring each move from -2 (clearly wrong) to 2 (clearly right), keyed "1" through "5", then a couple sentences justifying each score, and flag anything you think both of them are missing.
