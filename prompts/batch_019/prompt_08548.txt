So this happened in my own newsroom a couple weeks ago and I honestly can't stop turning it over in my head. We ran a story with a stat that turned out to be slightly off (an old percentage from a report that had since been revised), and once we fixed it, someone in our Slack asked whether that meant we also had to go back and check the two follow-up pieces that cited the same report, plus the automated "related coverage" widget that pulls from our tagging system, and honestly it kind of spiraled from there. Half the room wanted to just fix the one line and move on, the other half wanted to audit basically everything even loosely downstream "just to be safe," and our managing editor sort of drew a line based on what felt right to her, which she couldn't really defend when somebody pushed her on why that particular set of articles and not some other set.

I cover science and ideas for a living, so naturally my brain went somewhere more general: how does anyone, or anything - a person, a newsroom, a machine - actually decide what counts as "affected" by a change versus what's safe to just assume stays exactly as it was? You obviously can't re-verify every fact in an archive every time one small thing changes, thats insane, but a hard rule like "only flag stuff that repeats the exact same sentence" clearly misses real cases too, since sometimes something three steps removed really is compromised, and something that looks related isn't touched at all. My editor's honest answer amounted to "you just build up a feel for it," which might be true but feels like a dodge when you say it out loud.

I've got a nagging suspicion this is an actual, recognized problem that people in philosophy or AI or cognitive science have wrestled with seriously, and not just a fact of newsroom life, and that "you build up a feel for it" might even be a real, defensible answer for reasons someone's already worked out. Is there a principled account of how a mind or a machine is supposed to bound the ripple effects of some change, without either checking literally everything or leaning on totally unexplainable instinct? Or is "you develop a feel for it" genuinely the best anyone's got - and if so, what does that actually mean about how minds (or machines) work? Would love a steer, partly for my own sanity and partly because I think there's a piece in here somewhere.
