I'm working on a piece about the symbol grounding problem -- that old puzzle from cognitive science about whether a system that only shuffles symbols around based on their shape and their relations to each other can ever actually latch onto meaning, rather than just onto more symbols. Stevan Harnad's classic illustration is trying to learn Chinese from a Chinese-only dictionary: every definition just sends you in circles to more symbols you don't already understand. Some researchers I've talked to think multimodal LLMs have basically solved this, since their symbols are now tied to pixels and audio, not just scraped text. Others say thats not grounding at all, just a fancier dictionary -- pixels correlated with words are still symbols correlated with other symbols, and without a body that acts on the world and gets pushback from it, there's no real connection to meaning. Could you write me a dialogue between two cognitive scientists hashing this out at a whiteboard after a conference talk, really engaging with each other's strongest points instead of talking past each other? Neither one should be the obvious pushover.
