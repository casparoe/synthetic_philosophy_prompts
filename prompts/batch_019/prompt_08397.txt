My best friend Priya's getting married in three weeks and asked me to do the speech. Never went to uni, but a few years back I found a battered copy of G.E. Moore's Principia Ethica in a charity shop and it kind of rewired how I think about this stuff, specially his 'organic unities' idea -- that a whole can be worth way more than just adding up its parts. He uses that to explain what personal affection actually is: not just nice feelings (too cheap on its own), not just noticing someone has good qualities from a distance (too cold on its own), but really seeing someone's real qualities and loving them for it, which -- if I'm remembering the book right -- he reckons is one of maybe two best things going, the other being appreciating beauty. I want the toast actually built on that structure, not the greeting-card version of 'love is more than the sum of its parts,' and it can't just come out sounding like they're good for each others happiness either, cause Moore was pretty clear thats not the whole story. Problem is half the room is Priya's nan and cousins who'll switch off if it sounds like a lecture, and the groom Sam's a physicist who'll cringe at anything soulmate-y. Can you write me a 4-5 minute toast that actually uses his argument, not just the vibe of it, and still comes out warm and a bit funny for both crowds?
