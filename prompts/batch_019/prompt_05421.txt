Ok so I fell down a rabbit hole this week rereading old Overcoming Bias and LessWrong posts on anthropic reasoning, and there's a connection between two of the "big" anthropic arguments that I feel like should get discussed constantly but somehow almost never is: the doomsday argument and Bostrom's simulation argument both run on basically the same move (you infer something about the wider world from the "weird" fact that you happen to be a particular observer sitting in a particular spot among a bunch of possible observers), but everyone treats them as totally seperate debates.

Here's the specific thing that's bugging me. The doomsday argument is basically the flagship use case for the self-sampling assumption (SSA), and the self-indication assumption (SIA) is the standard "get out of doomsday free" card, since SIA's built-in bias toward big-population hypotheses exactly cancels the pessimistic update you get from your birth rank. Fine, I buy that part. But then when I actually go back and look at how Bostrom sets up the third horn of the simulation trilemma (the "you're probably a sim" horn), he seems to run it through SSA too, just with the reference class narrowed down to observers having experiences like ours, not through SIA at all. And yet basically every informal explanation of the argument I've ever read or given myself describes the reasoning as "there are way more simulated minds than non-simulated ones, so you should expect to be one of the many" -- which sure sounds like an SIA move wearing an SSA costume, not genuine SSA.

So, three things I want from you: (1) is the third horn actually doing SSA-work, or is the reference-class restriction basically smuggling in SIA's conclusion by other means -- and is that even a real distinction or just a verbal one? (2) If someone adopts SIA specifically to defuse the doomsday argument, are they thereby on the hook for something like Bostrom's own presumptuous-philosopher problem showing up again to support the simulation hypothesis, or is there a principled way to invoke SIA-ish reasoning for one and stay agnostic on the other? And (3), your actual verdict: once you push on the reference class problem for both, which argument's anthropic machinery is more fragile -- doomsday's or the simulation argument's? Don't just list considerations on both sides, I want you to actually commit to an answer on (3) and defend it.
