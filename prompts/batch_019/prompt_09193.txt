I sit on the pediatric ethics committee at the university hospital (they let philosophers on those, apparently), and I've got a case for Tuesday I can't get a clean read on. An 11-year-old boy has no growth hormone deficiency and nothing wrong on any lab panel -- he's just tracking in the bottom percentile or so for height, projected adult height around 5 foot 1. Insurance won't cover growth hormone shots since there's no diagnosis to code for, but the endocrinologist -- her note calls it 'idiopathic short stature' -- will prescribe off-label if the family self-pays, and wants our sign-off since its ethically contested. The parents say he's already getting bullied over his height and don't want to just tell him to toughen up. Half the committee treats this as restoring normal function, same as any hormone deficiency; the other half says there's no dysfunction to restore, just a kid at the low end of a bell curve, and that approving it leaves us no principled way to refuse the next parents who want their average-height son a few inches taller for basketball scouts. Draft the short memo I'll circulate beforehand: recommend approve, deny, or approve with conditions (if conditional, say for how long -- this is a years-long treatment, not a one-off), give reasoning the parents could actually respond to, and take the strongest argument on the side you reject seriously rather than just wave it off.
