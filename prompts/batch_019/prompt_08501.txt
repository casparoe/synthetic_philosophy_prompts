Ok so this got weirdly relevant to my actual life this month. I'm on a two-week course of high-dose prednisone for an autoimmune flare, and my doctor flagged that it can make people mood-swingy and overconfident, basically "don't sign anything big right now." Except the whole time I've felt unusually clear that I should quit my job and go freelance, clearer then I've felt about it in the two years I've been waffling on it. I know that's exactly the kind of situation where I'm supposed to distrust my own confidence, but when I actually re-run the reasons to quit, they still check out even now, sober-headed as I can tell.

So I've fallen down a rabbit hole reading about "epistemic akrasia" and higher-order evidence. There's this whole fight between people who say you're rationally required to soften a first-order belief once you get solid evidence your reasoning is compromised (the "enkratic principle" crowd) and people who defend "level-splitting" - keeping the first-order belief while still fully granting that the higher-order evidence is real and troubling. Can you make the strongest possible case for the level-splitting side here, and actually deal with the objection that this just turns rationality into something nobody could ever use to catch their own errors, instead of just talking around it?
