I'm drafting a piece on the private metaphysical commitments of well-known scientists, and the strangest case I've come across is the argument for an afterlife that Kurt Gödel worked out for his mother in their correspondence: in outline, that a genuinely rational world could not permit a mind's development to be arbitrarily cut off just as it approaches maturity. For the piece, I'd like a short dialogue between two contemporary philosophers assessing the argument: one who thinks it can be defended by drawing on Gödel's mathematical Platonism, and one who thinks it quietly equivocates between there being a sufficient reason for something and there being a good reason for it. Have the skeptic press the defender on whether this cosmic optimism sits comfortably alongside Gödel's incompleteness theorems, and have the defender give a substantive answer rather than sidestep the question. The two should be evenly matched and genuinely responsive to each other's objections, rather than each simply restating a prepared position.
