A friend and I recently argued for over an hour about underdetermination of theory by evidence, before realizing we had been talking past each other the whole time: she meant the Duhem-Quine point that auxiliary hypotheses can always be adjusted to save a theory from any recalcitrant observation, while I meant Kyle Stanford's argument that scientists have recurrently failed to conceive of alternatives to well-confirmed theories that later turned out to be just as well supported by the evidence available at the time. We never did agree on which of these, if either, actually threatens scientific realism.

Write an essay that distinguishes at least three arguments that travel under the single label "underdetermination" -- the Duhem-Quine holist argument, the claim (developed most explicitly by André Kukla) that any theory has algorithmically generated empirically equivalent rivals, and Stanford's argument from unconceived alternatives -- and assess which of them, if any, gives realists genuine reason to worry. Defend a definite verdict rather than settling for "it depends."
