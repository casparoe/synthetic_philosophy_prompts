For a piece on whether war serves any positive function in political life, I want to move past the flattened "Hegel loved war, Kant wanted peace" version of this debate. Please write a dialogue between a committed Hegelian and a committed Kantian in which each engages the actual substance of the other's position: the Hegelian arguing, in the spirit of the Philosophy of Right, that war performs a genuine ethical function by checking civil society's tendency to harden into private, particular interests and reminding citizens that their finite existence and property are not the highest things; the Kantian countering, in the spirit of "Perpetual Peace," that this mistakes an educative side-effect for a justification, and that reason instead requires states to leave the lawless condition they occupy relative to one another — just as individuals must leave the state of nature — through a federation of free states that renounces war as a means of settling disputes among its members. Neither side should strawman the other, and the exchange should not resolve into an easy concession on either side.
