My younger brother, Marcus, is shorter than almost everyone in his grade, and when our pediatrician ran hormone tests, the results came back completely normal: he is simply on the short end of the height range, the same way some kids end up unusually tall. He has been miserable enough about the teasing he gets at school that his doctor is recommending growth hormone shots anyway, but our insurance denied the claim as "not medically necessary" since nothing is technically wrong with his body. My best friend, Diego, is about the same height as Marcus, yet his hormone tests came back low, so his treatment was approved without any argument. My parents think it shouldn't matter why the two boys ended up equally short, since the shots would help them by the exact same amount, but the insurance company evidently believes there's a real difference between correcting something that has gone wrong and simply making an already-normal kid taller. I can't work out what that difference is supposed to be, or whether it can really bear the weight being placed on it — is there a principled way to draw this line, or is it ultimately arbitrary?
