Okay, this has been rattling around in my head since a LW-adjacent discord I'm in spent a whole evening arguing about mind-splitting and everyone just talked past each other on the actual metaphysics. I want a short story that engages with the endurantism vs. perdurantism debate properly, not another "am I still me after the teleporter" mood piece — I feel like I've read that exact story a hundred times and it never explains why the four-dimensionalists think they've dissolved the puzzle rather than just answered it differently.

Setting, roughly: near future, duplication/teleportation booths are common enough (scan-and-rebuild, don't care about the hard-SF mechanism) that there's a whole body of "duplication law" and tribunals that rule on disputes when a scan goes sideways — two bodies get printed off one scan, or the original doesn't get destroyed like it's supposed to. Make the protagonist someone who dropped out of a metaphysics PhD (persistence through time was literally her subfield) and now makes rent as an expert witness in exactly these tribunals, arguing under oath whether the two post-split people count as "the same person" for the purposes of a will, a marriage, a criminal sentence — whatever stake you want to hang the plot on.

Here's the part I actually want dramatized instead of just gestured at: endurantism says a person is a three-dimensional thing wholly present at every moment it exists, so persistence is strict numerical identity, and since identity is one-to-one and transitive, at most one of the two post-split continuers (if either) can really BE the pre-split person — somebody looses, maybe both do. Perdurantism, especially the "stage theory" flavor some four-dimensionalists (Sider, roughly) go for, treats ordinary persons more like momentary stages linked to past and future stages by a counterpart relation rather than strict identity, so one earlier stage having two equally-good later counterparts just isn't a contradiction — "same person over time" was never doing the heavy metaphysical lifting people assume it was. I want the verdict to hinge on which picture the tribunal (or the story itself) ends up leaning on, with at least one character running the transitivity-of-identity argument for real rather than hand-wavily, and bonus points if you can work in a smaller, personal version of Lewis's "problem of temporary intrinsics" for the protagonist too, like how she's bent-over-exhausted one hour and upright-fine the next and is still supposedly one and the same enduring thing. Keep it an actual story and not an essay wearing a story's clothes, a few pages, dialogue and scenes doing the work — i'd honestly rather the metaphysics stayed a little unresolved even if the legal case gets a clean verdict, and don't have anyone info-dump the jargon, just have people who've clearly thought about this way too much talk like it.
