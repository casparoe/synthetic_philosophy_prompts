So in retirement I've fallen into reading philosophy of causation, and I can't quite settle an argument I keep having with myself between the counterfactual camp and the interventionist camp. As an old bench scientist, my gut says Woodward and Pearl basically have it right -- causation is roughly "if you could intervene on X, Y would change" -- but then you read Lewis's older idea that causation is just counterfactual dependence between particular events, no intervention required, and its hard to shake the feeling he's onto something too. I'd love to watch the two views actually fight it out over a case I ran across that's apparently a headache for both sides: a sergeant and a major shout "Charge!" at the exact same instant, and the troops are trained to always obey whichever order comes from the higher rank, so they charge because of the major -- except if the major hadn't said a word, they'd have charged anyway on the sergeant's order alone. (I think this "trumping preemption" case is originally from Jonathan Schaffer, though don't hold me to that.) Could you write me a dialogue between a committed Lewisian and a committed interventionist hashing out who actually caused the charge -- the sergeant, the major, both, neither -- where they really engage with each other's actual arguments instead of just repeating there own positions, and neither one comes off as the weaker side.
