Ok so I've been reporting on x-risk and longtermism stuff and fell down a rabbit hole on the doomsday argument. I get the basic Bayesian logic – reason as if your birth rank is a random draw from everyone who'll ever live, and it pushes you toward expecting extinction sooner. But there's apparently a rival principle, the self-indication assumption (SIA), that's supposed to cancel that update out completely – except SIA comes with its own weird bill, the thing Bostrom calls the "presumptuous philosopher," where you can supposedly settle open empirical questions about how populous the universe is just by reasoning from an armchair, since theories implying more observers get weighted up more. So which bullet is actually worse to bite? Argue for sticking with the plain doomsday-favoring conclusion (SSA, reference-class mess and all) over dodging it with SIA – dont just lay out both sides, make the case for that horn specifically.
