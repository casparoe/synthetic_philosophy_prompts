So this happened in my early modern seminar last week and it's still bugging me. We'd done the standard two sessions on the Principles and Three Dialogues (esse est percipi, the whole deal), and then just for variety I assigned some excerpts from the Querist. Partway through discussion one of my sharper students puts her hand up and says: wait, if Berkeley's whole point is that matter doesn't exist independent of being perceived, isnt it a little rich that he then spent years writing about money and the Irish economy? Shouldn't an immaterialist think wealth and specie are just as illusory as tables and chairs, so that pursuing money is basically a category mistake - like, mistaking a mere "idea" for something substantial worth caring about? A couple other students nodded like this was obviously a real tension in his thought.

I mumbled something about compartmentalization and moved the discussion along, but I didn't actually have a good answer and it's nagged at me since. "He just wasn't being consistent" feels too cheap of an answer, and my hunch is that there's actually a pretty tight connection between the metaphysics and the economics here, just not the one my student was gesturing at (i.e., not "money is illusory so an immaterialist shouldn't care about it"). Could you help me figure out exactly where her inference goes wrong, and also what Berkeley's actually up to with the "ticket or counter" idea of money and all the anti-hoarding, pro-industry stuff in the Querist? I'd love something sharp enough to bring back to class on Thursday - bonus points if there turns out to be a real point of tension between the two projects, just a different one than the one she invented.
