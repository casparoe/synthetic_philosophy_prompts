I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, but I recently spent a weekend reading about the endurantism/perdurantism debate — the disagreement over whether persisting objects are "wholly present" at every moment they exist (endurantism), or are extended through time the way they are extended through space, so that what exists at any one moment is only a temporal part of a larger four-dimensional whole (perdurantism). The debate reminded me so strongly of a pattern I use professionally that I wrote up the comparison below, and I'd like your feedback on whether it actually holds together, since I suspect I might be smuggling in some confusions.

The specific puzzle that got me interested is the standard one concerning temporary intrinsic properties: a metal rod is straight at 2pm and bent at 3pm, after being heated and hammered. Naively, "the rod is straight" and "the rod is bent" both seem to be plain facts about one and the same object, which looks contradictory unless the shape is relativized to a time somehow. Perdurantists deny there's any real contradiction, because it isn't literally one self-identical, momentary thing that is both straight and bent: the 2pm temporal part is straight, the 3pm temporal part is bent, and "the rod" is just the sum of all such parts, the way a road is the sum of its segments. Endurantists want to keep the common-sense claim that the whole rod is present at both times, so instead they say the rod has its shape only relative to a time.

This maps neatly onto event sourcing, an architectural pattern I use at work. In an event-sourced system you never overwrite an entity's state directly. Instead, you append immutable events to a log — "AccountOpened," "Deposited $50," "Withdrew $20" — and the "current balance" is obtained by replaying (folding over) the whole log, or by consulting a disposable cached projection of that fold. Engineers routinely call the event log the "source of truth" and treat the current-state snapshot as a derived, secondary artifact that could be discarded and rebuilt at any time.

That framing looks a lot like perdurantism to me: the fully temporally-extended log is the "real" object, and the balance-right-now is just the temporal cross-section nearest our own present, dignified with the name "current state" for convenience only. And the fact that this architecture is so useful in practice — auditability, replay, time-travel debugging — struck me as mild evidence that four-dimensionalism is the more natural picture of persistence in general, and that endurantism's "wholly present at each moment" story is a folk simplification, in the way "the sun goes around the earth" is a folk simplification of orbital mechanics.

One thing nags at me, though. Every event in the log carries a constant streamID (or aggregate ID), which is not itself part of the payload of any event; it is simply the label by which we gather a set of events into "one account's" history in the first place. That ID does not change or accrue properties — it just persists, numerically identical, across the whole log. That looks suspiciously like exactly the kind of primitive, all-at-once persisting identity that endurantism posits and that perdurantism is supposed to explain away. So maybe I have only relocated the endurantist object rather than eliminated it.

I'd appreciate your comments on: (1) whether I have stated the endurantism/perdurantism distinction and its motivating puzzle accurately; (2) whether the event-sourcing analogy tracks the real metaphysical distinction, or quietly conflates it with something merely epistemic, such as what is convenient to store versus what is metaphysically fundamental; (3) whether my worry about the streamID is a genuine problem for the analogy, or something perdurantists already have a standard answer to; and (4) whether, on reflection, this analogy gives me any real reason to favor perdurantism, or whether I should drop it as cute but confused.
