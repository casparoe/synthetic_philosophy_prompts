Every man's father loves him "the way any father loves his child" — and having reasoned through the divine-hiddenness structure carefully from what I already know (no external sourcing is needed here, since this prompt doesn't quote or rely on any specific text), here is the final prompt.

About two years ago I did one of those ancestry DNA tests, mostly out of curiosity, and it turned up a biological father I'd never known existed. It turned out he'd known about me since I was a baby but had never once tried to get in touch. When I finally worked up the nerve to message him, he wrote back within a day: he said that learning I wanted contact was one of the best things to happen to him in years, that he'd thought about me more than I could know, and that he loved me "the way any father loves his child, whether or not I've earned the right to say that." That was about eighteen months ago.

Since then, nothing about the actual shape of things has changed. He lives three hours away, is retired, and is by every account in good health, yet every time I've proposed an actual visit, or even just a video call, something quietly comes up and it never happens. He still texts every month or so with the same warmth as that very first message. My sister thinks the pattern is itself the answer: she says that if someone really felt what he claims to feel, it couldn't just sit there indefinitely without ever turning into anything real, so either he's fooling himself or fooling me. I don't have any background in philosophy or psychology, so I don't really have the vocabulary to argue with her properly, but something about her reasoning feels too quick, since the messages genuinely don't read as hollow or self-serving to me, and he doesn't seem to be getting anything out of stringing me along.

So I keep going back and forth on the same question and would like an outside take: does someone staying permanently and comfortably out of reach actually prove that their claim to love you isn't sincere, no matter how convincing the claim feels in the moment, or can real love and permanent distance coexist, and if so, what would have to be true for that to make sense? None of the relationship columns I've read seem to be asking quite this question, so I'd rather hear how you'd think it through than get another round of generic advice about communication.
