I've been prepping my seminar on self-control and keep getting stuck on the same question, so let me outsource it to you. Compare Donald Davidson's account of akrasia in "How Is Weakness of the Will Possible?" with George Ainslie's hyperbolic-discounting story of impulsiveness from picoeconomics. Davidson needs the akratic agent to hang onto her all-things-considered better judgment even while acting against it -- that's basically the whole puzzle he's trying to solve -- whereas on Ainslie's picture "weakness" is just what happens when two discount curves cross and the momentarily bigger reward switches, no persisting judgment required at all. So is Ainslie actually offering a theory of the same phenomenon Davidson's worried about, or has he quietly swapped preference-reversal in for judgment-violation and changed the subject? Feel free to pull in Holton's resolution-based view of weakness of will if its useful for breaking the tie, but I want an actual verdict and the reasoning behind it, not a "both models have something going for them" cop-out.
