I'm taking a seminar on formal epistemology this semester, and we recently finished a unit on Richard Jeffrey's generalization of conditionalization for cases where evidence is not learned with certainty. I follow the basic setup well enough: in the standard illustration, someone examines a piece of cloth by candlelight and comes away with a redistributed confidence across the hypotheses that it is green, that it is blue, and so on, without ever becoming fully certain of any one color; this shift is then propagated to the rest of her opinions via the probability-kinematics formula, using her old conditional probabilities on each color as fixed weights -- what is usually called the rigidity condition.

What I haven't managed to get an intuitive grip on is a complication that came up only in passing during discussion section, and that a couple of us argued about afterward without reaching agreement: unlike ordinary strict conditionalization, Jeffrey conditionalization generally fails to commute. If an agent first redistributes her confidence across one partition of possibilities and then across a second, different partition, she can end up with a different final credence in some further proposition than if she had updated in the reverse order -- whereas conditionalizing on two propositions each learned with certainty always yields the same result regardless of the order in which one conditions on them. Could you explain, mechanically and ideally with a small worked example, exactly what it is about rigidity, or about how the weights in the kinematics formula get fixed, that makes the order of updating matter once the evidence is uncertain but not when it is certain?

I would also like to understand what to make of this philosophically. Is the failure of commutativity a genuine embarrassment for Jeffrey's rule, since it seems to leave a rational agent's final opinions hostage to the more or less arbitrary order in which she happens to process simultaneous or near-simultaneous uncertain impressions? Or is there a principled reason, having to do with rigidity always being anchored to a particular moment and evidential history, why this order-sensitivity is not actually a mark against the rule? I would rather come away with a real feel for the underlying mechanics and the stakes of this disagreement than with the bare slogan that Jeffrey conditionalization need not commute.
