Okay this has been rattling around in my head for weeks. There's the St Petersburg paradox - that coin flip game where the payout doubles every round and the expected value is literally infinite, yet nobody would pay more than like $20 to actually play it. The classic fix, from Bernoulli like 300 years ago, is that money has diminishing returns, so you should maximize expected utility (log of your wealth) instead of expected cash, and that tames the infinity. But then I ran into this "ergodicity economics" thing from Ole Peters, where he argues the utility function story is basically unneccessary sleight of hand - the real issue is that we're averaging over a bunch of parallel copies of you instead of averaging over time for the one actual you, and once you fix that the infinity disappears without ever mentioning risk-aversion or psychology at all. So are these genuinely two different solutions to the paradox, or is Peters secretly just doing log-utility again under a new name (isnt maximizing long-run growth rate basically the same math as maximizing log wealth)? Which one, if either, actually explains why nobody wants to pay much for that coin flip?
