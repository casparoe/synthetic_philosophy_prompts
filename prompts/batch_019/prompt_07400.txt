I am preparing the session on higher-order evidence for a graduate seminar on rational belief that I am co-teaching this term, and before I commit anything to my slides, I would like a sanity check on how I am planning to motivate the material. My working gloss on "epistemic akrasia" has been that it is a fairly direct transposition of Aristotelian practical akrasia into the doxastic case: just as the practically akratic agent judges, all things considered, that she ought to do A, yet does B instead because appetite overpowers her better judgment, the epistemically akratic thinker judges that her evidence does not support P, and yet goes on believing P anyway, out of something like intellectual laziness, stubbornness, or wishful thinking — a failure to let her own considered, all-things-considered assessment actually govern what she believes.

On this reading, when the literature constructs cases in which a reasoner learns that she may have taken a drug that impairs logical inference, or that a normally reliable colleague has reached the opposite verdict on the very same problem, the point of the example is to show that she is tempted toward a kind of doxastic self-indulgence if she brushes the higher-order information aside and simply keeps believing whatever her first-order reasoning told her. So the "enkratic," or "no akrasia," requirement that writers such as Sophie Horowitz and David Christensen take seriously strikes me, on reflection, as basically a demand for more epistemic self-discipline: thinkers should not let stubbornness or motivated reasoning stop them from following through on what their all-things-considered judgment recommends.

My co-instructor thinks this "weakness of will, but for beliefs" framing is basically right and would like to present it to the class this way. Before we do, I would value your view: is this an accurate rendering of what is actually doing the philosophical work in these cases and in the requirement itself, or is something importantly different going on that we would end up misrepresenting to our students?
