I went down a Kurt Gödel rabbit hole recently (not the incompleteness theorem stuff, the biographical stuff), and it turns out that seperately from his famous logical proof of God, he also had an actual argument for why there has to be an afterlife -- not just "I have faith," but something he apparently thought was forced on you if you're being rational about it. The gist as I understand it: he thought the world is fundamentally "rational" in some strong sense, and that it would be irrational for a mind to spend a whole life developing itself and then just get switched off with nothing carried forward, so there has to be some further "world" or stage where that development keeps going. I've also seen it described as a kind of dilemma: if the world IS rational then the afterlife follows from that, and if it's NOT rational then you can't use rationality to rule an afterlife out either -- so either way you end up stuck with it. This sounds kind of clever but also maybe like a shell game to me. Can you actually spell out, premise by premise, what this argument needs in order to work, and tell me honestly which step looks the shakiest?
