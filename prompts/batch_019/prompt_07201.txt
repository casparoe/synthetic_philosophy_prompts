I keep seeing this exact move play out in arguments online, and it actually happened with a coworker of mine too a while back: someone calls another person out for doing something shady, and the person being called out just goes "oh that's rich coming from you, you did the exact same thing last year," and everyone sort of nods like that settles it. I looked into it a bit and it turns out that's literally a named logical fallacy, tu quoque ("you too"), and the whole point of calling something a fallacy is that its bad reasoning that doesn't actually prove the original claim false. Like if I say "cutting corners on safety is wrong" and you point out that I've cut corners myself before, that doesn't retroactively make cutting corners okay, the claim "cutting corners is wrong" is exactly as true as it was before you brought up my history.

So as far as I can tell, hypocrisy is basically a non-issue philosophically speaking: people just weaponize "you're a hypocrite" to dodge the actual point instead of engaging with it, and since that's fallacious reasoning, a hypocrite should have just as much right to call out bad behavior as anyone else, since their own hypocrisy has zero bearing on whether the criticism lands or not. My friend, who took one philosophy course years ago, keeps insisting there's a real ongoing debate among philosophers about whether hypocrites lose their "standing" to blame people, even granting that the tu quoque thing is a fallacy, but that sounds like a flat contradiction to me. Either the reasoning is fallacious and hypocrisy doesn't matter, or it isn't fallacious and hypocrisy does matter to blame - it can't be both, right? Would appreciate someone actually walking me through what's going on here, because right now it just sounds like philosophers found a fancy way to dress up what's basically a schoolyard "no u" comeback.
