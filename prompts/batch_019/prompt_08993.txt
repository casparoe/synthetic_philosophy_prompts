So about two years ago my ex and I split up, and ever since I've had this habit of imagining the version of me in some other possible world where we never broke up, or never even met, and honestly getting kind of jealous of that guy, like he's out there living an actual better life somewhere. My coworker on the night shift said I sound like I'm describing the multiverse from the movies, and that comment sent me down a few weeks of reading about possible-worlds metaphysics on wikipedia and random philosophy blogs, which is not how I expected to be spending my lunch breaks at the warehouse.

What I can't get past now is this disagreement between David Lewis's "counterpart theory" and what people call transworld identity (which I think is more the Kripke side of things, though I get the feeling he was more interested in shooting down the need for a theory than building his own). From what I can tell, Lewis says that guy in the other world isnt me, not even a little - he's just some other person who resembles me closely enough on whatever similarity scale counts, to be my "counterpart," and this counterpart relation isn't even one-to-one, apparently I could have two counterparts in some world or zero in another. The transworld identity side seems to say no, it can genuinely be the same numerical me, doing something different, in that other world, no resemblance required, just stipulated to be me by fiat. There's apparently a famous complaint (I think from Kripke) that if a guy named Humphrey loses an election, and we say "Humphrey could have won," Lewis's theory means we're not talking about anything that could have happened to Humphrey at all, only to some other guy standing in for him, and the real Humphrey presumably couldn't care less about that.

So here's my actual question: does any of this technical stuff make a difference to whether my jealousy of "other me" is even a coherent thing to feel, or is it just a category error dressed up as a feeling? Can you walk me through the most important implications of the counterpart theory vs. transworld identity debate for that specific kind of parallel-world regret/envy thinking - like, does it actually matter which theory is true for how much weight this stuff should be allowed to carry, and please don't just be nice about it if the honest answer is that I've built an emotional habit around a metaphysical picture that doesn't hold up.
