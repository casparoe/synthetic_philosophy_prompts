ok so aumann's agreement theorem gets cited on rationalist blogs constantly as basically "if you're both bayesians and you both know you still disagree, someone's being irrational," but i feel like people just wave the theorem around and never actually stress-test it in an actual conversation. can you write me a dialogue between two old friends, both sharp rationalist-adjacent types, who've just realized they have a real disagreement (pick whatever topic, doesn't matter) -- one of them is a hardcore hanson-style modesty person who thinks that if honest full disclosure between epistemic peers doesn't converge you to the same number, somebody's definately fooling themselves and you should update hard toward the outside view. the other thinks the theorem is kind of a trap for real life, because the actual assumptions -- literally shared priors, common knowledge that the other person is both rational and honest, unlimited processing -- basically never hold irl, so two genuinely rational people can keep disagreeing forever. i want them to get into the actual weeds: can differing priors ever be legit rather than just a stand-in for private info somebody's not fully sharing, and what should we make of the fact that you don't even need literal common priors for convergence results to kick in, just repeated honest exchange of your actual number. neither one should be a pushover, i want to genuinely not know who's winning by the end.
