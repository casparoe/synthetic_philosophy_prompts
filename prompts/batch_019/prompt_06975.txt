I first ran into the Sleeping Beauty problem a couple of years ago in a probability-puzzles book a friend lent me, and although I never studied philosophy or statistics formally, I've spent a lot of evenings since then working through the halfer/thirder debate on my own. I now run a small philosophy podcast out of my spare room, mostly interviewing people willing to talk to an amateur, and next month's guest has built her reputation defending the thirder position.

What makes her interesting to me is that she doesn't just rely on Adam Elga's original indifference argument for the 1/3 answer; she grounds her thirdism in the more general Self-Indication Assumption -- the principle that, in reasoning about our own existence, we should favor hypotheses on which a greater number of observers like us exist. That stronger commitment seems to expose her to sharper objections than an ordinary thirder would face, and I don't want to spend the interview just letting her restate that the answer is 1/3.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions for me that push past the standard explainer version of this debate? I'd like at least one question on the classic halfer complaint that Beauty gains no new evidence upon waking -- she already knew with certainty on Sunday night that she'd wake up regardless of the coin, so what exactly licenses moving away from 1/2 if not evidence in the ordinary Bayesian sense; one on the Dutch-book/betting argument for thirding, and whether it really tells us anything about her credence rather than just about how the bets happen to be settled differently across the two tails-awakenings; and one on the Presumptuous Philosopher problem, since grounding thirding in the full Self-Indication Assumption seems to commit her to absurdly confident conclusions about how many observers must populate the universe -- which is, as I understand it, exactly why some philosophers who agree with her about the number 1/3 still refuse to accept her route to it and reach instead for some kind of hybrid view. For each of these three, please add a natural follow-up question tailored to whatever answer she's most likely to give, so we don't just stop at the first response. If there's room, I'd also like a question connecting her position to the Doomsday Argument, since that argument seems to rely on a similar kind of self-sampling reasoning, and I'd like to know whether accepting one actually commits her to the other.
