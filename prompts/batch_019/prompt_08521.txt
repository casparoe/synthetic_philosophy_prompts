Last week, a friend of mine who is finishing a doctorate in philosophy of physics and I sat through a seminar on Bell's theorem together, and ended up arguing over dinner afterward about what the theorem actually forces us to give up. We kept at it until the restaurant closed, with neither of us having conceded a thing. She holds something like the view associated with Bell himself and with Maudlin: the experimental violations of the Bell inequalities show that nature is nonlocal in a full-blooded sense, and that Bohmian mechanics simply has the honesty to admit this, since its guiding equation makes a particle's trajectory depend instantaneously on the positions of distant particles. I found myself defending the Everettian line instead: once you drop the assumption of a single, definite outcome and let superpositions simply branch, the statistics can be reproduced without any superluminal causal influence, so that quantum mechanics comes out local after all, in roughly the sense Deutsch and Hayden had in mind when they argued that the information locally accessible in an entangled system never outruns the speed of light. We got as far as Jarrett's decomposition of the standard locality condition into parameter independence and outcome independence, argued about which horn each interpretation actually violates, and disputed what the 2015 loophole-free experiments leave open, before giving up for the night.

I'd like you to reconstruct the argument properly, because I don't think either of us was arguing at our best. Please write a dialogue between a committed Bohmian and a committed Everettian who have this same disagreement immediately after the seminar, lingering by the whiteboard before the raw disagreement has had time to soften into dinner-table politeness. Each should argue in good faith and at full strength for their own diagnosis of what Bell's theorem does and doesn't show about locality, and each should be made to answer the genuinely awkward question the other side raises rather than sidestep it: the Bohmian should have to say something about why a guiding equation that outruns any light cone, and appears to need a preferred foliation of spacetime just to be stated, is preferable to an interpretation that claims to need no superluminal influence at all; the Everettian should have to say something about whether the locality bought at the level of signalling is worth much if the underlying wavefunction remains an irreducibly holistic object on configuration space. It's fine, and probably realistic, if the two end up no closer to agreement, but I'd like the exchange to leave the actual point of disagreement sharper than my friend and I left it.
