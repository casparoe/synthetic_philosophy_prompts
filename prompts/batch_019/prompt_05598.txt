So I'm about two thirds through a novel with a side character who's basically Descartes — I haven't decided yet if he literally IS Descartes via a time-slip thing or just a stand-in who thinks exactly like him — and there's a scene where a modern character walks him through David Chalmers' philosophical zombie thought experiment: the idea that you could have something physically and behaviorally identical to a person, down to the last atom, who nonetheless has nobody "in there," no inner experience at all. Every draft of his reaction I write comes out as "oh, that just proves dualism, i called it centuries ago," and it feels too easy, like im using the cardboard-cutout version of the guy instead of actually thinking through what he'd say.

Because the more I dig into it the messier it gets. Descartes already believed something zombie-ish about animals — the whole beast-machine doctrine, where animals are just unconscious automata that behave in complicated ways with nobody home. And in the Discourse he gives pretty specific reasons for why humans can't be reduced to that same thing: the test about language (a machine could never use words flexibly to respond to whatever's said to it, the way even a dull person can) and the one about how a machine would inevitably fail at some tasks because it acts from the particular disposition of its parts rather than universal reason. So it's not obvious he'd think a full human zombie is even coherent — as opposed to just another beast-machine that's somehow missing whatever ingredient he thought mere matter couldn't supply on its own. On top of that, his own conceivability argument in the Sixth Meditation runs the opposite direction from Chalmers' — he's imagining a mind existing without a body to prove they're distinct substances, not a fully human-behaving body without a mind — and his license to move from "I can conceive it" to "it's really possible" depends on clear and distinct perception guaranteed by a non-deceiving God, nothing like Chalmers' two-dimensional semantics.

Could you actually work through, in some real depth, what a well-informed and internally consistent Descartes would say if all this were explained to him properly? Would he take the zombie argument as basically his own point in new clothes, reject the human case as incoherent given what he thought matter alone could and couldn't do, or land somewhere else entirely? I want something with enough texture that I can actually mine it for dialogue, not just "he's a dualist, of course he'd nod along."
