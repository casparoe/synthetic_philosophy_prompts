Spent 35 years running a genetics lab before I retired, and I've always been obsessed with how you tell a real signal from an artifact of your setup. Turns out ethics has the exact same problem and nobody seems to agree on the answer. Take the trolley/footbridge pair: Greene's line, from all his fMRI work, is basically that our reluctance to push the guy off the footbridge is just an evolved alarm bell for up-close violence going off -- a bias -- while the flip-the-switch case shows the more trustworthy judgement underneath. I've also read philosophers calling that move a dressed-up version of the genetic fallacy, insisting the footbridge intuition might be tracking something real, like it being wrong to use someone as a mere means. Could you write me a dialogue between two equally sharp philosophers on opposite sides of this, who actually engage each others best moves instead of just restating their own, and see if they land on any principled way to seperate a genuine moral preference from a mere bias -- or end up having to admit it can't be done.
