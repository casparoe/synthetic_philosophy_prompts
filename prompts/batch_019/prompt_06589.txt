Three weeks ago, at my qualifying exam in evolutionary biology, one committee member tried to dismiss my entire research program — modeling how niche construction reshapes the selective pressures organisms face across generations — by invoking Karl Popper's old charge that natural selection collapses into a tautology, apparently unaware that Popper himself recanted that very charge in print in the late 1970s, saying outright that he had changed his mind about the theory's testability. I passed the exam, but the exchange has stayed with me, and I want more than the easy rebuttal. Popper died in 1994, a decade before niche construction theory was fully formulated and well before it fed into the extended-evolutionary-synthesis debates over whether standard evolutionary theory needs updating; yet he was already drawn to picturing organisms as active, propensity-driven agents rather than passive targets of selection, and he took evolutionary epistemology seriously as a research program in its own right. Given the full arc of his actual position, rather than the one line still being quoted against people like me, what do you think Popper would have made of niche construction theory and the extended-synthesis debate it fed into: a vindication of instincts he already held, a testable extension of orthodox Darwinism, or simply another "metaphysical research programme" in modern dress? I would like your best-supported prediction, not a rehearsal of the tautology story that everyone already knows.
