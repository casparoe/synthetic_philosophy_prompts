A friend and I are both backend engineers, and something that happened to her has been nagging at me ever since. Late on a Friday, with everyone else already gone for the weekend, I once pushed a small hotfix straight to production without waiting on a second reviewer, judging the change to be obviously safe. Nothing came of it, and I never mentioned it to anyone. My friend, at a different company, did the exact same thing under the same kind of pressure and with the same reasoning -- except her fix contained a subtle bug that leaked a slice of customer data before anyone caught it, and she was formally reprimanded for it. I know enough about outcome bias to have a tidy explanation on hand -- that we're letting the result color our judgment of two decisions that were equally (ir)responsible going in -- but naming the bias hasn't made the underlying intuition budge: I still feel that she was more at fault than I was. Is there a principled reason our blame should track outcomes in a case like this, or am I just refusing to update on a bias I can already name?
