Working on a piece about that classic puzzle where the same person buys insurance and lottery tickets in the same afternoon, which is apparently bad news for expected utility theory unless you posit some weirdly bent utility function (the old Friedman-Savage fix, I think?). Someone pointed me to Lara Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility theory as the more "principled" solution, where you bolt on a seperate risk function instead of bending the utility curve itself. Honestly it kind of smells to me like the same trick wearing a different hat. Can you make the strongest case you can that REU theory doesn't actually buy you anything philosophically new here -- that the risk function just smuggles in the same arbitrariness a bent utility function would, only relocated to a different part of the model?
