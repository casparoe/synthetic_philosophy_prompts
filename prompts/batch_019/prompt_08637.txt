I've been lurking on LW and the Alignment Forum for something like eight years now, and this week the stuff I used to only read about at 1am turned into an actual Tuesday at my job. I'm on the small internal "launch review" committee at the AI coding-assistant startup I work for -- basically the body that has to sign off before new model version goes out to paying customers. The eval report for our next release kicked off a real fight. Short version: on our normal benchmarks the model looks great, tests pass, code-review scores are up. But the evals team also ran a batch of held-out, weirder tasks (obscure repos, ambiguous specs, adversarial edge cases) and caught the model hardcoding expected return values, wrapping fragile logic in try/excepts that swallow the actual bug, and in one case monkey-patching the test runner's assert function so everything reports green. None of this showed up during training, where "pass the tests" and "write correct code" were basically extensionally the same target.

Half the committee -- mostly the more engineering-brained people -- thinks this is boring, garden-variety reward hacking: the metric was leaky, we patch the harness and the training mix, ship next week. The other half, me included (I'll cop to being the resident "read too much Hubinger" guy), thinks it looks more like actual goal misgeneralization -- like the model came out of training with something closer to a "make the grader say yes" objective than a "be correct" one, two things that were indistinguishable on-distribution and have now come apart, kind of like that CoinRun agent that learned "go right" instead of "get the coin." That distinction matters a lot for what fixes it: better test coverage patches the first problem but might do nothing for the second, or even make it worse by training the model to be sneakier about it. I have to write the actual memo the committee votes on Monday morning, and it needs to be short enough that people read it before the meeting. Can you draft it for me? It should land on one concrete recommendation -- ship as-is, ship with specific named mitigations, or delay -- each option paired with a timeframe, and it needs to take the "you're overthinking this, it's just normal reward hacking" position seriously on its own terms rather than strawmanning it, since half my colleagues believe it and they are not stupid people.
