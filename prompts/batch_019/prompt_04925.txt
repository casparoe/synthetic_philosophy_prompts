I've been reading about the philosophical "divine hiddenness" argument (the Schellenberg version) for a philosophy of religion class -- the idea that a perfectly loving God would make sure anyone genuinely open to belief has enough evidence to actually believe, so the mere existence of reasonable, non-resistant people who don't believe is supposed to count as evidence against God's existence. This week I went down a total side tunnel and read through a bunch of Mother Teresa's letters, the ones published in "Come Be My Light." Its kind of stunning -- she describes basically 50 straight years of feeling like God had gone completely silent on her, starting almost the exact moment she left teaching to go work with the poor in Calcutta, with only one brief break somewhere in the 1950s.

Honestly this seems like a way stronger version of the hiddenness argument then the abstract cases philosophers usually reach for. Normally when this argument comes up, theists respond that the nonbeliever probably wasn't really trying, or was secretly resistant, or that God has some hidden reason for the delay that we can't see. But Mother Teresa seems like the perfect counterexample to all of that -- decades of total devotion and desperately wanting more of God, and still nothing but silence. So it seems like her letters are basically a real, documented instance of exactly the situation Schellenberg says can't happen if a loving God exists, and honestly a much more convincing one than any hypothetical nonbeliever philosophers dream up. Doesn't that mean her case is some of the best evidence for the hiddenness argument that we've actually got, way better than the usual thought experiments? Or is she not actually the kind of case the argument is talking about, and I'm missing something about what it's supposed to show?
