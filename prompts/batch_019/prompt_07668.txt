A ride-hailing company's self-driving car swerved to avoid a car door that had just been flung open into its lane and, in doing so, clipped a cyclist riding alongside it. The car's driving policy isn't hand-coded; it was trained on millions of miles of driving footage, and the manufacturer's own engineers say no one specifically designed, tested, or foresaw this particular maneuver. Two coworkers of mine, Ines and Marcus, spent a lunch break arguing about who, if anyone, is responsible.

(1) Ines argued that the company is fully responsible: they chose to deploy a system whose exact behavior even they can't predict or explain, and accepting that opacity is itself the culpable act, regardless of whether this specific swerve was foreseen.

(2) Marcus objected that this proves too much. Nobody can fully predict how a newly licensed teenager will react in a split-second emergency either, and we don't hold the driving instructor fully responsible for whatever the teenager later does; unpredictability is just a feature of any sufficiently capable driver, human or artificial, and can't by itself be a mark of negligence.

(3) Ines replied that the analogy fails at the crucial point: once licensed, the teenager becomes an independent bearer of responsibility in her own right, whereas the car never can be -- you can't fine it, revoke its license, or expect it to feel remorse. So whatever responsibility would normally settle on a driver has nowhere left to land except back on whoever released the system.

(4) Marcus granted the disanalogy but said "nowhere else for it to go" doesn't make the company culpable; it just means we badly want someone to be. Perhaps the honest conclusion is that nobody did anything blameworthy here, even though the company should obviously still cover the damage.

(5) Ines answered that this is exactly the problem: if the worst that ever happens to the company is a payout, they have effectively purchased the right to run unpredictable machinery among cyclists at a fixed price, and that isn't the same as anyone being held responsible.

(6) Marcus shot back: so what would satisfy her -- someone at the company being punished for a maneuver nobody wrote, reviewed, or could have reviewed? That risks being a worse injustice than the gap she's complaining about.
