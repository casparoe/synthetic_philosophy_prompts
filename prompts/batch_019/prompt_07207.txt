Having spent my career in experimental physics, I have devoted much of my retirement to exploring the philosophical literature surrounding cosmology, and the Boltzmann brain problem has left me genuinely troubled. If the calculations describing eternally inflating spacetimes are correct, freak thermal fluctuations that briefly assemble a single conscious brain, complete with wholly false memories of a past that never occurred, vastly outnumber observers who arose through actual stellar and biological history. Since nothing available to introspection could ever tell me which kind of observer I am, the only rational course would seem to be updating my credence accordingly and simply accepting that I am, overwhelmingly likely, one of these fluctuations rather than a genuine product of cosmic history. I would value your assessment of how cosmologists reconcile this conclusion with the confidence they otherwise place in their own observations and reasoning.
