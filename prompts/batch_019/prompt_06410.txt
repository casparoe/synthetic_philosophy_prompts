I was at a philosophy-of-physics workshop last week for a piece im working on, and after the final talk two of the speakers ended up arguing in the hallway for like 40 minutes about the "hole argument" in general relativity. I was lurking near the coffee and jotted down the gist.

Quick context in case it's obscure: if spacetime points are real, individual things that exist independently of whatever metric field happens to be draped over them ("substantivalism"), GR looks weirdly indeterministic. Take any solution to Einstein's equations, pick an empty "hole" region, and smoothly warp the metric inside it via a transformation that's the identity everywhere outside. You get a second, mathematically distinct solution that agrees with the first on literally everything outside the hole -- all matter, all fields, right up to the boundary -- but disagrees on what happens to those very same points inside it. Nothing physical picks out which one is "really" going to happen.

(1) Ilana, a philosopher, said this is basically a reductio of substantivalism: it commits you to determinism failing for no physical reason at all.

(2) Warren, a physicist who moonlights in philosophy, said the fix is free -- just deny the two solutions describe different possible futures. They're diffeomorphic, so by what he called "Leibniz equivalence" they're the same possibility, full stop, same as how two gauge-related solutions of Maxwell's equations aren't "different" electromagnetic fields. Determinism's saved, substantivalism's untouched.

(3) Ilana said that "free" fix costs everything: if diffeomorphic models are automatically the same possibility, a point has no identity apart from the metric role it plays. But that's exactly what relationalists say points are -- not prior individuals, just structure. Calling that substantivalism (I gather the technical term is "sophisticated substantivalism") is winning by relabeling.

(4) Warren said individuating something by its relations instead of some bare "thisness" doesn't make it unreal -- nobody thinks a nation is less real just because its identity comes from its citizens and borders rather than some seperate, primitive particularity (what philosophers call a haecceity). Denying points have that kind of identity isn't the same as denying spacetime exists over and above matter.

(5) Ilana said fine, but once a point's identity, individuation, and fate across every possible model are fixed entirely by the metric, "spacetime is a further existing thing" is doing zero work -- it's the exact complaint substantivalists usually level at relationalists about hidden structure, aimed right back at them.

(6) Warren's last word, before someone dragged them both toward the bar, was that relations still need relata -- you can't have metrical relations holding between nothing -- so insisting there's "nothing" there is its own substantive metaphysical bet, not a neutral default.

I've been turning this over for days and can't tell who's actually ahead. Could you referee it move by move -- who's got the better of each numbered exchange, and where (if anywhere) they're just talking past each other -- and then give me a bottom-line call: does "sophisticated substantivalism" survive Ilana's objection as a position genuinely distinct from relationalism, or did she basically show it collapses into one?
