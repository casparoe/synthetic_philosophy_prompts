I write novels for a living, and for the past year I have been quietly taking notes on my father's recovery from a corpus callosotomy — surgery undertaken to control severe, drug-resistant seizures — on the theory that there might be a book in it eventually. Most of the time he is entirely himself, but when he is very tired, his left hand will occasionally undo what his right hand has just done — unbuttoning a shirt, or pushing away a glass he asked for moments earlier — and he insists, quite sincerely, that he did not do it. My sister calls this a meaningless reflex, no different from a hiccup, and thinks I am reading far too much into it; I am not convinced, and since I also hold his medical power of attorney, the question is not purely academic for me. I keep wondering, uncomfortably, whose wishes I am actually honoring in the moments when his hand and his voice disagree — is my sister right that nothing is really at stake here beyond an odd reflex, or is there a genuine question underneath what I am feeling?
