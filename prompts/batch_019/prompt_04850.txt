I'm the only philosopher on the board of a mid-sized humanitarian NGO that runs relief and development programs across several low-income countries, and I've been asked to draft the memo on a compensation dispute the rest of the board can't resolve on its own. We are reviewing a policy under which staff recruited from our donor-country headquarters are paid two to three times what local staff earn for comparable work, a gap that management defends as necessary to recruit and retain internationally mobile talent. One board member argues that the policy fails a veil-of-ignorance test and amounts to a plain injustice our human-rights mission cannot condone; another replies that Rawls's own account of justice between peoples deliberately declines to extend the difference principle this far, and that the first member is pressing his framework well past where he was willing to take it himself. Draft the short memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting: it should recommend whether to retain, phase out, or restructure the dual pay scale and on what timeline, and it should take whichever side it does not adopt as seriously as the side it does.
