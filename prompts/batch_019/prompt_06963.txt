The simple conditional analysis of dispositions holds, roughly, that an object is disposed to give response r to stimulus s just in case it would give r if it underwent s. C. B. Martin's "electro-fink" is the classic counterexample: a wire that is not live is wired to a device that senses an approaching conductor and instantly makes the wire live, so that the wire satisfies the conditional "if touched, it would conduct" without actually having the disposition beforehand, since the disposition is switched on only by the very stimulus that was supposed to test for it. David Lewis's Reformed Conditional Analysis tries to save something like the original biconditional by relativizing it to the object's retaining one and the same intrinsic causal basis throughout, on the thought that what a fink does is covertly change the basis at the moment of the stimulus. Alexander Bird's antidotes were designed to show that this repair does not work either: a poison's chemical basis for killing is left completely untouched by an antidote that merely intercepts its effect further downstream, so the basis is retained right through the stimulus and Lewis's analysis still delivers the intuitively wrong verdict. Dispositional essentialists such as Bird himself and Stephen Mumford take the moral of both cases together to be that no amount of further tinkering with the conditional analysis will succeed, and that dispositions, or powers, must instead be treated as metaphysically primitive.

A reading group I am part of got stuck on a case that does not sit comfortably as an instance of either textbook phenomenon, and I would like to see it argued over properly rather than filed under a label by fiat. A manufacturer sells two outwardly identical music boxes. In the standard model, turning the crank winds a spring which, on release, drives a barrel of pins directly against a tuned steel comb, producing the tune. In the "export" model, built to keep the product out of a tariff category for musical toys, the release mechanism first engages a small cam, fused into the barrel itself at the factory rather than bolted on as a separate part, which lifts the comb a fraction of a millimeter clear of the pins at the exact instant of release, so that the pins pass beneath the comb without ever striking it. Spring tension, pin layout, and comb tuning are identical between the two models, and an export-model box with the cam ground away plays the tune perfectly. Is an unsold, un-cranked export-model box, sitting in its packaging in a warehouse, disposed to play its tune when the crank is turned?

Please write a dialogue of somewhere around 700 to 900 words between a philosopher defending a broadly Lewisian Reformed Conditional Analysis and a philosopher defending dispositional essentialism, working through the export-model case together. Both should be pushed by the specifics of the example, rather than by prepared talking points, to say and defend whether the cam is a fink, a mask, or neither, and each should respond directly to what the other has just argued rather than simply restating a general view; neither should concede outright, and neither should come across as the obviously weaker thinker. Before they finish, have them reach, and take a real stand on, the further question of whether their disagreement about how to classify the case is a substantive metaphysical disagreement or merely a verbal dispute over where to draw a line that was never meant to be perfectly sharp.
