Moral trade occurs when two people with different, non-overlapping moral commitments each do something that costs them little by their own values but matters a great deal by the other's, in exchange for the other doing likewise — so that both end up judging the world improved by their own lights, without either having been argued into revising a single view. The idea is usually illustrated with an ethical vegetarian who is indifferent to carbon emissions trading favors with an environmentalist who is indifferent to animal welfare. Construct a different, realistic example of a moral trade, one that does not involve diet, animal welfare, or environmental offsetting. Specify each party's relevant moral commitment, what each side gives up and gains, and explain why the arrangement counts as a genuine moral trade rather than an ordinary self-interested bargain.
