I've spent years reading rationalist blogs and forums, so when I finally got serious about finishing a writing project, I did what anyone steeped in that literature would do: I set up a commitment device with an accountability partner. The arrangement is that if I haven't opened my laptop and started working by 8:30 p.m. each day, she comes over and sits with me until that day's page quota is met — no exceptions, and no renegotiating once 8:30 arrives. Ten weeks in, that deadline has never once caught me short: I've sat down and started on my own every single day, usually well before dinner, and I'd begun to feel genuinely proud of the streak, as though it reflected real improvement in my self-discipline. My partner, who likes to play devil's advocate, says that pride is unearned: given the standing arrangement, there was never a day when I was actually going to end up not working, so calling what happened a "choice" I made, rather than just the arrangement functioning as designed, is a kind of self-flattery. I suspect she's missing something but can't quite locate the flaw. Is she right that the credit I'm taking isn't really mine, or is there a problem with her argument?
