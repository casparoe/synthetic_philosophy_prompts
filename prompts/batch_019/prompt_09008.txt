Ok so this happened at dinner with an old friend from undergrad (she's in marketing now, not a philosopher) and I have not been able to let it go since. She's been with her partner for something like eight years and is about as confident as a person can be that he would never cheat on her — long track record, no red flags, the works. At some point she said something like: "Honestly, at this point I just know he wouldn't cheat. And because I know that, if I ever did stumble across something that looked like evidence he was cheating — a weird text, someone claiming they saw him somewhere he shouldnt be, whatever — I'd already know in advance that it has to have some innocent explanation. So theres no real reason for me to even take that stuff seriously if it ever comes up, I can basically write it off before I've even seen it."

I've taught epistemology for years and my first reaction was that this is just garden-variety confirmation bias, but the more I sat with it the less sure I was that that's all that's going on. If she really does know he's faithful, and if knowing something entails knowing that any apparent evidence against it must be misleading (it literally can't be good evidence against something true), then it looks like she's entitled, right now, to pre-emptively write off any future evidence against it, sight unseen. But that seems insane, because the same move would let anyone dismiss in advance any evidence against literally anything they currently feel sure they know, no matter how confident they are for bad reasons. And yet I can't immediately spot which step in her reasoning is the culprit — each one seems to follow pretty naturally from the one before it.

So here's my question for you: is she actually reasoning validly here, and if not, where exactly does it break down? I'd love an answer that actually engages with the logical structure rather than something reassuring about staying open-minded — I've got a room full of undergrads who are going to hit me with basically this exact case in a few weeks, and "don't be so sure of yourself" isn't going to cut it as an answer.
