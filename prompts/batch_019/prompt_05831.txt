It started with the classic "blue-eyed islanders" puzzle, where a fact every single islander already privately knows only starts to matter once it becomes common knowledge — once everyone knows it, knows that everyone else knows it, and so on without end — after being announced out loud in front of the whole group. Once that finally clicked for me, I got hooked on the idea that common knowledge is something qualitatively different from everyone just happening to know the same thing, or even from everyone knowing that everyone knows it up to some fixed, finite level. What confuses me is that different solution concepts don't all seem to lean on this assumption in the same way, or to the same degree. From what I've pieced together, iterative deletion of dominated strategies, and rationalizability more generally, are often said to follow from common knowledge of rationality more or less on their own. Nash equilibrium seems to additionally need some form of common knowledge of the players' actual beliefs or conjectures, not just of their rationality, and correlated equilibrium, at least in the way Aumann originally set it up, seems to further rely on everyone sharing the very same prior over the underlying state space.
