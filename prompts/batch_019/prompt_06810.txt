I'm a philosophy PhD student working on formal decision theory, and for a reading group I organize with a few other graduate students, I'd like a piece of short fiction that conveys a fairly esoteric scenario intuitively rather than leaving it as a list of premises to be parsed. The scenario: two superintelligent systems inhabit causally disconnected parts of reality — different Everett branches, say, or simply regions of the universe too far apart ever to affect one another — so any "interaction" between them has to be acausal, mediated only by each system's capacity to simulate or otherwise infer the other's decision procedure. One system works out that it is vulnerable to acausal extortion: a threat whose force comes entirely from correlated reasoning about how it would respond, not from any physical channel between the two. In response, it adopts a surrogate goal — an artificial value it publicly commits to defending as though it were terminal — so that the extortion gets aimed at the surrogate rather than at what the system actually cares about. Could you write a short story, no more than 1,200 words, that dramatizes this predicament and makes clear through the narrative itself, rather than through a character's exposition, both why the threat counts as acausal and why redirecting it onto a surrogate goal genuinely lowers the system's expected loss rather than merely relocating the target?
