My grandmother died ten days ago at eighty-six, and I have been asked to deliver her eulogy this Sunday. I am a junior in high school and have never done anything like this before, but I was the only grandchild who ever sat through her Nietzsche talk without changing the subject. She ran her adult life by the "Why I Am So Clever" chapter of Ecce Homo: no heavy German-style food, no alcohol, winters in Arizona and summers in the Colorado mountains, which she always called her personal version of Nietzsche's Nice and Sils-Maria, and any guest who sat too long after dinner got her line about a sedentary life being a sin against the Holy Spirit, delivered while she got up to pace the room. My parents always described this as her being "very disciplined," which I suspect she would have hated, since as I understand it the actual point was closer to the opposite of willpower or self-denial -- it was about vitality, about not wasting yourself on the wrong climate or the wrong soup. The service will be small, maybe thirty people, including a couple of cousins who will expect something about the will to power and a lot of her old friends who have no idea who Nietzsche was and just want to hear about the grandmother they knew. Could you write the eulogy, about five minutes long, so that it is accurate enough for the first group and warm enough for the second, without ever letting Nietzsche crowd out the person we are actually there to remember?
