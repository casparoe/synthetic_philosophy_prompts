I sit on my company's Experimentation Review Board, the internal group that must sign off before an A/B test result can be used to justify a permanent product change. The growth team stopped their onboarding-flow test on day 9 of a planned three-week run, the moment the frequentist p-value for the conversion-rate difference crossed 0.05, and now want to ship on that basis; our statistician objects on optional-stopping grounds, but the growth PM counters that a colleague re-ran the same nine days of data through a Bayesian beta-binomial model, got a 97% posterior probability that the new flow is better, and argues that number is untouched by the objection, since Bayesian updating doesn't care why or when you decided to look at the data. The board is split on whether that reply actually defeats the statistician's objection or just relocates it. Please draft the board's written decision memo: rule on whether the rollout can proceed, with a concrete timeline for whatever you recommend, and set a monitoring policy for future tests -- and make sure whichever side you rule against gets a fair hearing rather than a one-line dismissal.
