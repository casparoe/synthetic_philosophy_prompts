I'm a software engineer currently trying to decide whether to leave a comfortable staff-engineer position at a large tech company to become a founding engineer at an early-stage startup, and a friend who studied philosophy told me my agonizing over this is a textbook case of what L.A. Paul calls a "transformative experience" in her 2014 book of the same name. I've only encountered the idea secondhand, through a couple of blog posts and podcast summaries rather than the book itself, so please correct me if I've gotten the details wrong.

As I understand it, Paul's point is narrower and more interesting than just "big decisions are hard to predict." She distinguishes experiences that are epistemically transformative — ones where you cannot know in advance what they will actually be like, however much testimony or imagination you bring to it, because the relevant knowledge is only available by actually undergoing the experience — from experiences that are personally transformative, where living through them reshapes your core values and preferences, so that the person evaluating the outcome afterward is no longer quite the same person who made the original choice. Her famous illustration is the decision of whether to become a vampire, chosen precisely because nobody starting out human has any real basis for imagining vampiric psychology, unlike, say, having a child, where everyone assumes they already have some idea what it involves. Apparently the punchline is that when both features are present at once, ordinary expected-utility reasoning breaks down, since you can't assign an expected value to an outcome you cannot picture, using preferences you won't even hold anymore.

What I can't tell from secondhand summaries is how special this category really is, as opposed to just being an academic label for "life-changing event" in general. I'd like your help pressure-testing it against a range of concrete decisions. - having a first biological child

- trying a strong psychedelic for the first time
- permanently emigrating to a country with a very different language and culture
- moving from an individual-contributor engineering role into full-time people management
