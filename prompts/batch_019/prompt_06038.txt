I spent thirty-some years as a condensed-matter physicist before retiring, and for most of that time I taught the second law of thermodynamics to undergrads the lazy way: wave hands at coarse-graining, mutter something about "overwhelmingly likely" macrostates, and move on before anyone asks why time-reversible microphysics gives you an irreversible, Loschmidt-defying arrow at the macro level. Retirement gave me time to actually sit with that discomfort, so lately I've been working through some philosophy of physics -- David Albert's Time and Chance and Huw Price's Time's Arrow and Archimedes' Point, back to back, which I wouldnt recommend to anyone looking for a peaceful week. Albert seems pretty convinced that a low-entropy "Past Hypothesis" plus a uniform probability measure over the compatible microstates does real explanatory work -- not just for the second law but for memory, causal order, the whole psychological arrow. Price keeps insisting that this picture smuggles in an unjustified asymmetry: reasoned consistently, statistical mechanics alone would predict high entropy in both directions away from now, so the Past Hypothesis isn't so much discovered as stipulated to patch the hole.

What I'd like is an essay that doesn't just referee this politely and leave me hanging. Walk through whether positing a low-entropy boundary condition near the big bang genuinely explains the direction of the thermodynamic arrow (and, through it, the psychological and causal arrows), or whether Price is basically right that the move just relocates the very asymmetry we set out to explain into an ad hoc cosmological fiat. Please also take on the Boltzmann brain business somewhere in there -- it strikes me as more than a cute thought experiment, since if fluctuation-produced observers vastly outnumber ones that arose the "honest" thermodynamic way, that seems to threaten to undercut the whole framework thats supposed to be doing the explaining. Weigh it seriously, but actually land on a side at the end instead of concluding that smart people disagree -- I get enough of that already from the philosophy discussion group I've joined down at the library.
