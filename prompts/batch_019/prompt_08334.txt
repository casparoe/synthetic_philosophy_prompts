I'm a retired physicist who spent most of my career in statistical mechanics, and I've been filling retirement with philosophy books instead of golf. Something about David Lewis's 'Principal Principle' has been bugging me: as I understand it, the whole principle just says your credence should match the objective chance whenever you know it, but isnt that exactly the equal-a-priori-probabilities postulate we used constantly in stat mech? You assume every microstate consistent with the macrostate is equally likely and build the rest of thermodynamics on top, no deep metaphysics needed. Nobody in physics thought that assumption needed its own fancy name, we just treated it as Laplace's old principle of indifference applied to phase space. So why do philosophers act like Lewis discovered something profound about chance, instead of just re-labeling indifference reasoning with better branding? Genuinely curious what I'm missing, not trying to be a crank about it.
