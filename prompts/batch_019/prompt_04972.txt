I'm outlining a novel in which two rival powers no longer negotiate directly but delegate their dealings to AI systems, each fed intelligence the other side will never see. I want the plot's central standoff to turn on something sharper than the usual hand-wave about machines "learning to trust" one another, so I've been reading about a concept from game-theoretic AI-safety research called a safe Pareto improvement: a jointly known change to both delegates' strategies, relative to some tense status quo, that each side can verify — using only its own private information — will never leave it worse off, and may leave both sides better off, without either side disclosing what it's protecting. Construct a concrete example of such a standoff, specifying the status quo, the private information each side holds, and the inefficiency this produces, in which a genuine safe Pareto improvement exists, and then show, within that same example, why a more obvious fix — such as full disclosure or a simple mutual pledge to cooperate — would fail to be safe in the same sense.
