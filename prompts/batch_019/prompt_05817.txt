This semester my graduate seminar on philosophy of language has spent several weeks on Quine's indeterminacy of translation and Davidson's account of radical interpretation, in particular Davidson's suggestion — in essays on the connections between decision theory and interpretation — that the interpreter's task of simultaneously assigning an agent beliefs and desires might be modeled on Ramsey- and Savage-style representation theorems, where preference-plus-choice data are jointly decomposed into a utility function and a probability function, with a principle of charity doing the work of constraining the decomposition. A student who splits her time between the philosophy department and the machine learning lab pointed out over coffee that this is structurally very close to the identifiability problem that has dogged inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) almost since its inception: given only an agent's observed policy in some environment, one cannot uniquely recover the reward function the agent is (putatively) optimizing, because the same behavior is compatible with indefinitely many pairings of a reward function with a model of the agent's (possibly imperfect) planning process — including thoroughly deflationary pairings, such as a null or arbitrary reward function combined with a "planner" stipulated simply to output whatever behavior was observed.

This underdetermination result is sometimes discussed in that literature under a "no free lunch" heading, and various fixes have been proposed to break the symmetry — simplicity priors over the reward/planner pair, or independent psychological assumptions about the agent's biases and bounded rationality (analogous to how behavioral economists build in things like hyperbolic discounting) — somewhat as charity is meant to break the symmetry between belief and desire for Davidson.

I'd like you to work out this comparison in some detail and then render a verdict rather than just a survey. Is the underdetermination Davidson (following Quine) locates in radical interpretation genuinely the same problem as IRL's reward/planner non-identifiability, just recast in decision-theoretic versus mentalistic vocabulary — so that, say, standard objections to charity as a solution should carry over as objections to simplicity priors, and vice versa? Or is there a principled disanalogy: in what exactly is being jointly fixed (meaning-and-belief versus reward-and-rationality-model), in the resources available for resolving the indeterminacy, or in whether the indeterminacy is merely practical — in principle resolvable given enough data or the right prior — as opposed to constitutive, which is how Quine and (on at least some readings) Davidson treat the indeterminacy of meaning? Please argue for a specific answer rather than simply laying out considerations on both sides.
