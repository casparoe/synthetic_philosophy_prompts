Ok this has been bugging me and I feel like I'm missing some Bayesian trick here. Every January I do that Scott Alexander thing where you write down like 50 predictions for the year with confidence levels attached, then grade yourself against reality in December. This year I'm genuinely 90%+ confident in pretty much every individual prediction on my list, but I'm also close to 100% confident that by December at least a couple of them will turn out false, since thats just what happens every single year. A friend who's way more into formal epistemology than me says this is straight up incoherent -- you can't rationally be highly confident in every claim on a list while also being confident the list as a whole contains a falsehood. Part of me thinks hes right, but it also feels like the obviously sane way to think about a long list of predictions. So which of us actually has this right, and is there a real resolution, or does rationality just kind of run out here?
