Three months ago I was asked to chair the newly formed liturgy subcommittee of my congregation's ritual committee. We are an independent, post-denominational minyan that has finally outgrown the photocopied, cobbled-together High Holiday booklet we have used for a decade, and we are producing our own machzor for the first time. I assumed this would take two meetings; we are now on our fifth and are entirely stalled on the Avodah service and the Musaf passages dealing with the Temple sacrifices, where the traditional liturgy asks God to restore the sacrificial service and once more accept Israel's "fire-offerings."

One faction wants to do what several Conservative prayer books did decades ago and quietly shift these passages from future tense into a more descriptive, past-oriented framing, so that we would be commemorating the sacrificial cult rather than actively petitioning for its return. A second faction regards this as exactly the sort of editorial squeamishness that turns a machzor into a mirror of its editors' own theology instead of an honest transmission of the received text. A third member, who has been working through the Guide for the Perplexed, has complicated matters further: Maimonides' own account of sacrifice, she points out, is not obviously univocal. In the Guide (III:32), he treats the sacrificial cult as a concession to a people habituated to idolatrous worship, permitted and redirected toward God only because weaning Israel off it all at once was psychologically impossible -- which makes sacrifice sound like a mode of worship the tradition should be relieved to be leaving behind. But the Mishneh Torah's Hilkhot Melakhim describes the messianic king rebuilding the Temple and resuming its service in perfectly unqualified terms, with no hint that this is a lesser or provisional form of worship, and Nachmanides had already objected, against the Guide's account, that it cannot explain why Noah, Abel, and the patriarchs offered sacrifices, apparently to God's pleasure, long before there was any idolatrous cult for anyone to be weaned from.

I have volunteered to circulate a written recommendation before our meeting in three weeks, so the committee can vote rather than relitigate this every time we gather. Could you draft that memo? I need it to recommend one specific way of handling the sacrificial passages in the new machzor -- leaving them as they stand, revising their tense or framing, replacing them, annotating them, or some other option -- and to defend that choice on its merits. Because our most textually careful member is also the one most committed to keeping the traditional wording intact, the memo has to take the Mishneh Torah's apparently literal, unqualified commitment to restored sacrifice seriously as a reading of Maimonides in its own right, rather than simply assuming that the Guide's philosophical account is the "real" Maimonides and the legal code merely exoteric cover for the unsophisticated.
