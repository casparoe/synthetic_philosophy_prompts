For my IB extended essay on decision-making under risk, I've been reading about the Kelly criterion and keep running into two justifications for it that get treated as interchangeable, though I'm not convinced they are. The classical justification observes that betting to maximize expected log wealth almost surely outgrows any other fixed-fraction strategy over a long enough horizon, and then treats this as showing that Kelly betting is rational for any investor — an inference Paul Samuelson mocked in his one-syllable-word paper on the subject, since almost-sure long-run dominance does not imply expected-utility optimality unless one's utility happens to be logarithmic. The "ergodicity economics" justification associated with Ole Peters instead argues that because multiplicative wealth dynamics are non-ergodic, it is the time-average growth rate, rather than the expectation, that should be optimized, and insists that this is not a claim about risk preferences at all. Please compare these two justifications: is the ergodicity argument genuinely distinct from simply assuming logarithmic utility, or does it smuggle in the same assumption under a different name? And if they are genuinely distinct, is there a realistic situation in which they would actually recommend different bets?
