In covering the effective-altruism debate over longtermism, I keep running into writers who credit Derek Parfit with having resolved the central puzzle of population ethics in Reasons and Persons by replacing total utilitarianism with the "average view" of welfare—ranking outcomes by mean rather than summed well-being, which avoids the Repugnant Conclusion. Given that this fix has apparently been on the table since 1984, could you help me understand why philosophers today still write as if finding an adequate population axiology were an open, maybe intractable, problem?
