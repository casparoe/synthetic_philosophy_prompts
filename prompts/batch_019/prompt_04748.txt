I spent thirty-five years as an experimental particle physicist, much of it on collaborations measuring CP violation in neutral meson decays, and only since retiring have I started reading seriously in philosophy of physics. Working through the literature on the thermodynamic arrow of time, I keep encountering the same move: the fundamental dynamical laws are treated as time-reversal symmetric, so that the observed asymmetry — entropy reliably increasing rather than decreasing — has to be pinned entirely on a special low-entropy initial condition, the Past Hypothesis, as in David Albert's version of the Boltzmannian story, rather than on anything in the laws themselves. That premise has been strictly false since 1964: CP violation in the neutral kaon system entails, via the CPT theorem, that the weak interaction is not time-reversal invariant, a conclusion later confirmed directly, independent of CPT, in kaon and B-meson oscillation experiments. Please argue for the position that this fact is nevertheless philosophically idle: that it lends no support to anyone hoping to ground the thermodynamic arrow in the dynamics rather than in boundary conditions, and that the Past Hypothesis strategy survives this wrinkle unscathed. I am not looking for the easy version of that argument, which merely notes that the effect is numerically tiny; what I want is the principled reason why even a much larger amount of microscopic time-reversal violation would still fail to explain why entropy increases.
