I am assembling a collection of short fiction dramatizing strange results from decision theory, and I would like a complete draft for the entry on acausal threats. Imagine two advanced intelligences that can never make causal contact with one another—separated across branches, epochs, or regions of the universe that will never physically touch—yet each can model the other with enough fidelity that a threat from one lands on the other purely through that mutual prediction, with no signal ever sent. I want the protagonist to have already committed, openly and in advance, to a surrogate goal: some value it cares about only slightly, which it will treat as the sole stake whenever its true values are threatened, so that a rational threatener stands to gain nothing by targeting what it actually cares about. Please write this as a self-contained short story of roughly 1,000 to 1,500 words that makes vivid both why such a threat can feel urgent despite the total absence of causal contact, and why the surrogate-goal commitment is what drains the threat of its force from the outset, rather than merely cushioning the protagonist after the fact.
