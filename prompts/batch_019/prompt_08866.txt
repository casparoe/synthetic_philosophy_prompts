My uncle, who has become increasingly drawn to intelligent design over the past year, recently sent me a Karl Popper quotation he had come across online: Popper's description of Darwinism as "not a testable scientific theory, but a metaphysical research programme," alongside his separate remark dismissing "survival of the fittest" as "almost tautological." I understand that Popper is supposed to have walked this back later on, but when I looked into it, I found real disagreement about how substantial that retraction actually was, rather than a tidy story of straightforward recantation. I would like to understand the issue well enough to respond to him properly, rather than simply reaching for a counter-quotation of my own. Could you explain what exactly Popper's original objection to the scientific status of natural selection was, as laid out in Unended Quest; what he actually says in the 1978 Dialectica paper ("Natural Selection and the Emergence of Mind") usually cited as his recantation; and whether the tension between the two dissolves once one distinguishes the theory of natural selection from evolution understood as a historical claim -- or whether something of the original worry survives even after the "recantation"?
