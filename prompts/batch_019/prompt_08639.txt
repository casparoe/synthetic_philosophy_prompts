Ok so I fell down a rabbit hole this week reading old LessWrong / AI safety posts (a coworker sent me one link and then I just kept clicking related stuff instead of doing my actual sprint work, oops) and I got stuck on this idea called "surrogate goals," which is apparently a proposed defense against "acausal threats." Best I can explain it: the premise is that two agents don't need any real causal channel between them to threaten each other -- if each one can accurately predict what the other would do, that's supposedly enough for one to credibly threaten to hurt something the other cares about unless they comply, purely through mutual modeling, no messages or physical interaction required. The defense that really got me is that an agent can precommit, way in advance, to act as if some totally arbitrary thing is one of its core values (like, I dunno, the number of odd-numbered rocks on Mars) purely so any would-be blackmailer targets THAT fake value instead of a real one. It's basically a honeypot for extortion, except the honeypot has to be maintained so convincingly, forever, that its functionally indistinguishable from a genuine value from the outside -- and maybe from the inside too.

Can you write me a short story built around this? It doesn't have to literally be two AIs negotiating, though I think that's the most natural setting since that's where I keep seeing the idea discussed, but I'd like at least one character to be some kind of artificial mind that has actually adopted a surrogate goal like this, years ago, as policy. What I'm most interested in seeing explored is the moment the strategy stops feeling like strategy: if you have to pour real resources, or even something like suffering, into defending a goal you know is fake, at what point do you stop being sure it's fake? I don't want the ending to neatly resolve that -- I'd rather it land on genuine ambiguity about whether the character protected what it actually cared about, or just slowly turned into the mask. Something like 800-1500 words feels right, and honestly it's fine if it ends up a little unsettling.
