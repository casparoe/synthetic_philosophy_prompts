I spent thirty-odd years doing acoustics and noise-control work, so the Weber-Fechner law has been rattling around in my head since grad school -- every decibel scale you've ever squinted at is basically Fechner in disguise. What I never knew until this year, when I started reading philosophy of mind books in retirement, is that the same Fechner was a died-in-the-wool panpsychist: he thought plants have some kind of inner life, and in one book (Zend-Avesta, IIRC) he argues the Earth itself is a conscious being, with the biosphere acting almost like it's nervous system, and that the stars have their own kind of consciousness too. Now that I've fallen down the panpsychism rabbit hole, I keep running into "the combination problem" as basically THE objection to the view these days: how do you get one unified subject of experience (me, sitting here typing) out of a swarm of tiny little subjects -- particles, neurons, or whatever the fundamental bearers of proto-experience are supposed to be.

Here's what's been bugging me: Fechner spent a huge amount of effort on exactly this kind of combination question himself, just running it in the other direction -- from our single momentary consciousness already binding together countless separate nerve-signals, on up to a planetary and even stellar mind that would include ours the way ours includes the doings of our organs. So he clearly wasn't skittish about combination; his whole system depends on it working somehow. Given that, I'd love your best reconstruction of what he'd actually say if a modern panpsychist cornered him with the "subject-summation" version of the problem, or the "palette" worry about how a limited stock of simple micro-qualities could ever add up to the rich, structured qualities we experience. Would his own picture genuinely dissolve those worries, or is he just running into the same wall everyone's hit since James's "mind-dust" critique, dressed up in nicer metaphors? Please actually argue for one answer instead of hedging -- and be honest about where, if anywhere, his framework would still leave him stuck.
