For my political philosophy class we've been talking about why constitutions deliberately make themselves hard to amend — like needing something like two-thirds of Congress plus three-fourths of the states just to change a single line — basically so a temporary majority can't rewrite the rules just because everyone's mad about something this particular week. My professor described it as protecting the document from the passions of the present, and it got me thinking about this whole situation with my friend thats been bugging me for weeks.

So, after my breakup a while back, I kept almost-texting my ex whenever I was drunk or having a bad night. To stop this, my friend and I made a deal: I'm not allowed to unblock his number unless she unblocks it for me, and — this is the important part — she is NOT allowed to do it just because I say I have a good reason, since obviously that's exactly what I'd say if I were rationalizing. I told her explicitly, more than once, to ignore future-me no matter what future-me says, no exceptions, no negotiating, no "just this once."

Then two weeks ago I asked her to unblock him because I genuinely needed to coordinate picking up some stuff I'd left at his apartment, which felt like an actual boring, non-emotional reason and not me caving. She still said no — her exact words were "that's exactly what you'd say either way" — and made me send a mutual friend to grab my stuff instead. I was so annoyed at her in the moment, even though I was literally the one who set the rule up like that on purpose. Now I can't decide if she did the right thing by refusing to ever make exceptions, or if a rule like that is actually kind of a bad way to run your life since it can never respond to a real exception when one actually comes up. Is this basically the same kind of problem as the constitution thing my professor was talking about, or are they not really comparable? And either way, was my friend right to hold the line no matter what?
