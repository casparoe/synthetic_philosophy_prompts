i've been chewing on the Condorcet jury theorem for a while now - the idea that if every voter has better than 50/50 odds of getting the right answer and they vote independantly, then as the group gets bigger the majority vote is basically guaranteed to land on the correct choice. people bring this up all the time as a mathematical case for democracy, more voters = wiser crowd. but the more i think about it the more it seems like the theorem actually cuts the other way. if the whole result hinges on each person's individual competence being above half, and bigger numbers only help once you've already got that, why isn't the natural conclusion something like "give the decision to a small panel of genuinely well-informed people" instead of "let everyone vote"? i don't mean the usual complaint that real voters aren't independent, i mean taking the theorem completely at face value. can you argue as hard as you can that the Condorcet jury theorem, honestly applied, actually supports narrowing who gets to decide rather than widening it?
