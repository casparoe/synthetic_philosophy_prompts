So I've been slowly working through Lara Buchak's *Risk and Rationality* and there's one thing that keeps bugging me. Her whole pitch for risk-weighted expected utility (REU) theory is that you can drop the independence axiom to accomodate Allais-type preferences while staying fully rational, and unlike cruder probability-weighting theories it still respects stochastic dominance. Fine so far. But the moment you put an REU-maximizer into a multi-stage decision tree (spin one wheel, then a second one, same final odds either way), the usual dynamic-consistency/money-pump problem that hits basically every theory that drops independence seems to come roaring back. I recall Buchak leaning on something like resolute choice, plus denying that a compound lottery just reduces to its single-stage equivalent, to dodge this -- but maybe i'm misremembering the details. Anyway, can you argue that REU theory can't actually escape the dynamic-consistency bind except by either (a) quietly collapsing into ordinary EU maximization over a richer, history-indexed outcome space, or (b) accepting a bullet at least as costly as the Allais violations REU was invented to accommodate in the first place?
