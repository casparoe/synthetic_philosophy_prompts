Okay so I'm finishing a term paper for my philosophy of language seminar on Putnam's model-theoretic argument against realism (its due Friday), and I went down a total rabbit hole trying to actually understand the bit of set theory it leans on -- Skolem's paradox -- instead of just citing it secondhand like I suspect half the class is going to. I think I've landed on a stronger version of the thesis than what's actually in the reading, and my roommate, who's doing a math PhD, says something about it feels off to her but couldn't say what exactly, so I wanted to run it by someone before I commit to it in writing.

Here's the reasoning: Löwenheim-Skolem says that if ZFC has a model at all, it has a countable one. But ZFC also proves Cantor's theorem, so that same countable model still contains some set it insists is uncountable -- it just doesn't contain any bijection between that set and the naturals, even though we, sitting outside the model, can plainly biject the whole model with N, since it's countable from our vantage point. So it looks like "uncountable" was never a property intrinsic to a set, it's relative to which model you happen to be standing inside when you ask the question.

That reminds me a lot of how whether two events are simultaneous isn't a fact about the events themselves in special relativity, it depends on your reference frame, and no frame is privileged over any other. My thesis is that Skolem's paradox is doing the exact same move for cardinality: it shows that "uncountable" is frame-relative in just the way simultaneity is, which means Cantor's proof that the reals outnumber the naturals never actually pinned down an observer-independent fact about the size of the continuum, and honestly that seems like a way bigger problem for realism about mathematical objects than the independence of the continuum hypothesis is, since with CH you can at least still believe there's a fact of the matter we just haven't proven. Is that basically right, or is the "it's just like relativity" move smuggling in something illegitimate that I'd regret putting in an actual paper?
