I'm a freelance software engineer weighing two ways to bill a client for a large project. Option A is a fixed fee, spelled out precisely in the contract. Option B is a cut of the revenue the client's product earns after launch, and by the client's own projections, its expected payout is actually a bit higher than the fixed fee. Even so, I find myself strongly preferring Option A, mainly because I have no good way to judge how much to trust those revenue projections, whereas the fixed fee is simply certain. A friend who works in statistics tells me this preference doesn't hold up: if I trust my own expected-value calculation, I should at worst be indifferent between the two options, so my hesitation is really just ordinary risk aversion in disguise. Is she right, or is there a genuine difference between a gamble whose odds I know and one whose odds I can't even pin down in the first place?
