Aristotle is fairly explicit about a few paradigm cases along this spectrum: natural barter conducted to meet household needs sits at one end; retail trade (kapelike) undertaken purely for monetary gain counts as a milder corruption of it; and interest-bearing loans (tokos) are singled out as the most unnatural mode of acquisition of all, since money is supposed to function only as a measure of demand (chreia) in exchange, not as something that breeds more of itself. What the text doesn't do is tell us where most modern financial practices would fall, and one of my cohort-mates kept insisting that the whole apparatus collapses into a crude "producing value" versus "extracting value" distinction as soon as you try to extend it to anything Aristotle didn't discuss directly. That struck several of us as too quick, since it erases exactly the kind of gradation the text seems to be after.
