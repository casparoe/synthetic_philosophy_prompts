Foundationalism stops the regress of justification at basic beliefs that require no further support. Coherentism and Peter Klein's infinitism both reject any such stopping point, but they replace it with structures of very different kinds: infinitism posits an endless linear chain of reasons, while coherentism replaces the chain altogether with a holistic web of mutual support. Compare the two views with respect to Klein's own distinction between a reason's merely being "available" to a believer upon request and its being consciously entertained by that believer: does this distinction genuinely allow infinitism to escape the objection that no finite mind could traverse infinitely many reasons, in a way that is not equally available to a coherentist confronting the parallel charge that a web of mutual support is simply circularity under another name?
