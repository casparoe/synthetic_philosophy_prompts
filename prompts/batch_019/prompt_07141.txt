I sit on the internal ethics review board for a meditation app. Our usage data show that users who sustain a daily streak for a month have substantially better outcomes than those who lapse early, and most early quitters later say they wish they'd kept going. The product team wants loss-framed push notifications ("Don't lose your streak!"), a streak counter, and a paid option to repair a broken streak -- none of which tells users anything they don't already know; it works by making losing progress feel worse. Half the board calls this ordinary, benevolent design; the other half calls it manipulation dressed up as care, and is uneasy that the company also profits from the anxiety it creates. The product lead notes this isn't obviously different from a default retirement-savings enrollment, which nobody on the board objects to. Draft the board's decision memo -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions -- that gives the team a principle for telling the two cases apart, if they truly can be, plus a genuine steelman of whichever side the memo doesn't take.
