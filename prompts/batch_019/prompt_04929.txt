One of my closest friends, Priya, spends nearly all of her free time volunteering: she tutors elementary schoolers at the public library twice a week, organizes a coat and blanket drive every winter, and last year she skipped her own birthday dinner because she had already committed to covering an overnight shift at a homeless shelter. Everyone in our friend group admires her, but lately that admiration has started to shift into something more like guilt. A couple of people in our group chat have begun suggesting that the rest of us are basically slacking, or even a little selfish, since we only complete the minimum service hours our school requires for graduation and nothing more. One friend has already quit her part-time job so she can free up more hours to volunteer, mostly, I think, because she feels like she has to keep pace with Priya.

I don't want to dismiss what my friends are saying, because Priya really is doing something admirable, and it feels wrong to act as though her choices don't matter or say nothing about the rest of us. At the same time, something about the comparison strikes me as unfair, and I haven't been able to work out exactly why. Is there an actual moral difference between an action being good to do and an action being required? And if so, does one person choosing to do far more than what's expected genuinely change what the rest of us owe, morally speaking — or does it just generate social pressure to match her without actually shifting anyone's real obligations? I'd like to have worked out a clearer answer before this comes up again in the group chat, since right now all I have is a vague intuition rather than an actual argument.
