So I'm on the peace and social concerns committee at my meeting (small Quaker congregation, nothing academic about any of us, we just take turns) and every so often we get asked to write a letter of support for someone applying for conscientious objector status when they're called up. We've got one this week that's split the committee right down the middle, and I have to bring some kind of recommendation to discuss this Thursday, so I'm hoping you can help me actually think this through properly.

The young man, Theo, isn't a pacifist in the usual sense - he's told us plainly that if the country were invaded tomorrow he'd defend it without hesitating. What he won't do is answer the call-up for the war we're currently sending troops to fight overseas, because after alot of reading on his own he's become convinced that particular war just isn't just. About half the committee say that doesn't qualify as genuine conscientious objection, since being recognized is supposed to be about opposing war "as such," not a private citizen picking and choosing which wars pass his own muster - if every soldier got to opt out whenever they personally disagreed with a war, you wouldn't have much of an army left. The other half say that's backwards: the whole reason we even use words like "just" and "unjust" for wars is that some genuinely are unjust, so of course someone of conscience should be able to object to one specific unjust war without giving up on defending his own country altogether, and that demanding blanket pacifism as the only legitimate kind of objection kind of dodges the real question.

I borrowed an old paperback on just war theory from our library sale to try and get my head straight before Thursday, and honestly came out with more questions than I went in with - like whether it even makes sense to tell a soldier "your job is to fight, not to judge whether the cause is just," when everybody on our committee - people on both sides of this - already agrees some causes genuinely aren't just. Could you write the actual recommendation memo I'd bring to the meeting? I don't need it to sound official, I just need it to land on a real position - support the letter, decline, or support it with some condition attached - and to take the strongest version of whichever side it doesn't land on seriously, rather than just brushing it off.
