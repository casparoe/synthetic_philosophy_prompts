I mostly write about science and ideas for a living, so people are always dropping their weird little dilemmas on me, and this one from a friend has been stuck in my head for days. She runs a small nonprofit and had exactly one fully-funded spot in a leadership program to give away. Two staffers were, by her own account, totally tied on performance, output, and enthusiasm -- nothing to separate them. Her deputy said just give it to whoever's been at the org longer, since you have to break ties somehow and thats as reasonable a rule as any. My friend couldn't bring herself to do that and flipped a coin in front of both of them instead. The deputy thought that was pointless theater: "neither of them is better or worse off on average whichever way you decide, so why does the method matter?" But my friend insists the coin flip really was the more honest and fair option, and that this isn't about which staffer ends up happier -- the two methods just aren't the same choice, even though the numbers come out identical. Is she onto something real here, or is she just uncomfortable being the one who picks?
