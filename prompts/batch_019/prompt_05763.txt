So I recently read Borges' short story "Pierre Menard, Author of the Quixote" and it's kind of stuck in my head. Menard doesn't copy Don Quixote -- he re-derives a chunk of it from scratch, out of his own totally different life and reading, and ends up with text that's word for word identical to Cervantes'. The narrator then argues the two identical texts actually mean different things, and that one is the greater achievement, purely because of who produced them and how. As a dev this messes with me a bit, because in my world identity is just content -- git literally hashes a file's contents to decide what it is, so if two people independently write byte-identical code it IS the same object as far as any tool is concerned, full stop, there's no concept of "arrived at independently but not really the same thing." Can you write me a short story that takes this problem somewhere new (not a retelling of Menard) -- where a character produces an exact, uncopied duplicate of something that already exists (a painting, a song, a piece of code, whatever) and the story actually has to reckon with whether the duplicate is the same thing as the original, and what that even hinges on if it's not the physical or textual properties. Bonus if it touches on why this seems to matter way more for some art forms than others -- I gather theres a real term from aesthetics for this, something like paintings being "autographic" and music or novels being "allographic," but feel free to just have a character stumble into that idea rather than explaining it outright.
