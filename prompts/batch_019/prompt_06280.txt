Okay, this has been bugging me for ages: why do I actually tear up during a sad movie when I know for a fact that none of it's real and nobody actually died? Turns out theres an actual philosophy puzzle about this, people call it the paradox of fiction. From poking around, there seem to be two camps: one side (I think this is Kendall Walton's crowd) says what we feel watching fiction isn't real fear or sadness at all, just a make-believe version of it that we work up by playing along with the story - supposedly that's why we don't bolt from the theater when the monster shows up. The other camp says that's basically a cop-out, and that the emotions are completely real, we just don't need to believe something is actually happening in order to genuinely feel something about it - vividly imagining it is apparently enough. Can you write me a dialogue between one person from each camp really hashing this out, maybe right as they're leaving a cinema together after both got a bit choked up? I don't want either of them to come off as the obvious loser though - i want them actually engaging with each other's best points instead of just repeating their own side louder.
