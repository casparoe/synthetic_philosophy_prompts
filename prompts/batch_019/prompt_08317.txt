I'm taking a seminar on analytical Marxism this term, and we've just worked through the Wood–Husami exchange on whether Marx's condemnation of capitalist exploitation amounts to a charge of injustice. On one side, Marx insists that the exchange between capitalist and worker is fair by capitalism's own juridical standards, since workers are paid the full value of the labour-power they sell, and in the Critique of the Gotha Programme he dismisses talk of fair distribution as unscientific moralising — all of which fits his general historical-materialist view that conceptions of right are internal to, and generated by, a given mode of production. On the other side, he just as persistently describes the capitalist's appropriation of surplus labour in the language of theft and robbery. Please write a dialogue between two equally capable Marx scholars: one who argues, following Wood, that Marx's own premises rule out condemning capitalism as unjust, so that the real Marxian indictment must instead be that capitalism alienates workers from their productive activity, its products, and one another; and one who argues, following Husami, that Marx does think capitalism unjust — judged by the standard of the higher mode of production whose emergence he anticipates — and that this is fully compatible with historical materialism. Have the two engage directly with each other's textual evidence rather than simply restating their own reading.
