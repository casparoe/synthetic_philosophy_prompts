(a) [8 pts] Explain Molina's three-fold division of divine knowledge into natural, middle, and free knowledge, making clear what "logical" as opposed to temporal priority is doing in that scheme, and why middle knowledge counts as "middle."
(b) [8 pts] State the grounding objection to middle knowledge and explain why it's specifically a problem for middle knowledge and not for natural knowledge.
(c) [4 pts] Plantinga's Free Will Defense relies on transworld depravity. Is he thereby committed to the truth of Molinism? Defend your answer briefly.

And the student's answer in full:

(a) Molina splits God's knowledge into three logical (not temporal) moments. Natural knowledge covers every necessary truth and everything that's merely possible - it's the same across all possible worlds and doesn't depend on anything God decides. Middle knowledge is God's knowledge of counterfactuals of freedom - conditionals like "if Peter were in circumstances C, he'd freely deny Christ" - and it's called "middle" because it comes between natural and free knowledge in the order of explanation: God doesn't reason through these moments in time, it's a logical rather than a chronological ordering, which is how Molinists avoid saying God "learns" anything. Because these counterfactuals are true independently of what God wills - otherwise God would be responsible for Peter's sin - they have to be necessary truths in the same way logical and mathematical truths are, which is exactly why they're available for God to consult before He decides what to create. Free knowledge, the third moment, is God's knowledge of the actual world, fixed only once God freely decides which possibility to actualize.

(b) The grounding objection, which Plantinga presses against Molina's picture, is that nothing makes these counterfactuals true. It can't be the creature's actual choice, since in most cases the circumstances in question never actually obtain - most possible creatures are never even created. And it can't be God's will, since Molinists need these truths to be independent of God to protect libertarian freedom. So there's no truthmaker: nothing makes it true, prior to creation, that Peter would deny Christ in C rather than affirm him. This is specifically a problem for middle knowledge because, unlike the truths of natural knowledge, these are contingent - they could've gone the other way - so they can't be grounded the way necessary truths usually are, e.g. in God's own nature.

(c) Not quite. The Free Will Defense is a defense, not a theodicy, so Plantinga only needs it to be possible that every creaturely essence suffers transworld depravity - which just requires that counterfactuals of freedom have truth values, not that God actually has and uses middle knowledge in deciding what to create. So you could accept the argument's logic without buying the whole Molinist package.
