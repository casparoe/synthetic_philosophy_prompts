My close friend from college is getting married in a few weeks and asked me to give a speech at the reception. Here's the wrinkle: she did her whole PhD in philosophy on the ethics of promising - basically the question of why a promise actually obligates you (not in the "the marriage license is a legal contract" sense, the actual words-exchanged-out-loud sense), rather than it just being a nice thing people say to each other - and a decent chunk of the guest list is people from that world. The rest is just our families and old college friends who have no idea any of this is a Whole Academic Field. I really dont want to give the generic "and they promised to love each other forever, aw" toast, but I'm also nervous about botching the substance in front of people who've spent years on it.

Here's the one bit I actually remember clearly, from a half-drunk conversation with her at a conference dinner once: she said a promise seems to still bind you even in a case where literally nobody, not even the person you promised, will ever find out whether you kept it or broke it - so whatever's doing the binding can't just be about them relying on you or being let down, it has to be something else, and apparently most of the obvious candidates for what that "something else" is don't fully hold up either. I don't need the speech to solve her dissertation for her, but I'd love it to actually commit to one real answer instead of hedging across the going theories, and use that answer to say something genuine about what she and her fiance are about to do up there. Bonus points if there's at least one moment where her old cohort nudges each other while everyone else just experiences it as a normal, lovely wedding speech. Can you draft something like a 4-5 minute spoken toast?
