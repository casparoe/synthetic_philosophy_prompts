I'm teaching a graduate seminar on formal epistemology this term, and we just spent two sessions on the anthropic reasoning literature that left half the room -- me included, if I'm honest -- going back and forth without ever landing anywhere. Every writeup my students hand in just lists both sides and shrugs, so I want to see what an essay looks like that actually commits. Quick recap in case it helps calibrate what I'm after: Bostrom's self-sampling assumption (SSA) says you should reason as if you were a random draw from the set of all actual observers, past, present, and future, who share your "reference class" -- and that's exactly the premise behind the Carter-Leslie doomsday argument, on which your own birth rank among all humans who will ever live is mild evidence that the total human population (and so our remaining future) is smaller than you'd otherwise think.

The self-indication assumption (SIA) was cooked up more or less specifically to kill the doomsday argument: it says you should reason as if you were a random draw from all possible observers, so any hypothesis on which more observers exist gets boosted in proportion -- which does cancel the doomsday shift, but only by comitting you to what Bostrom calls the presumptuous philosopher, who can apparently out-reason a room full of physicists running a genuinely decisive experiment just by pointing out that whichever cosmology implies vastly more observers is thereby vastly more probable. Feel free to lean on the reference class problem as a strike against SSA, and, if you think it's relevant, the sleeping beauty case, which plenty of people suspect is secretly the same dispute wearing a different coat.

Anyway -- write me an essay that actually picks a side rather than cataloguing the two views and their respective monsters. Weigh the doomsday argument against the presumptuous philosopher, decide which bullet is less costly to bite (or whether there's some principled way to dodge both), and defend a real verdict -- hedged if it needs to be ("SIA, except when..." is a fine answer) -- but not a shrug.
