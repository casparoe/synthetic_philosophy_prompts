I'm a software engineer, and I'm giving the best man speech at my friend Rohan's wedding next month. Rohan is deeply immersed in effective altruism and insists that his relationship with his fiancée, Claire, runs on something the philosopher Toby Ord calls "moral trade" -- meaning that instead of resolving a disagreement by changing each other's minds, each does something that matters more to the other's ethics than to their own, so both end up better off by their own lights while disagreeing exactly as much as ever. Their history bears this out almost too neatly: years ago, Claire, who cares more about animal welfare than global poverty, gave a year's savings to an anti-poverty charity on the condition that Rohan, who cares more about poverty than factory farming, went vegetarian for the year -- disturbingly close to Ord's own example in his paper. Half the guests are Rohan's rationalist and EA friends, who will notice if this gets flattened into ordinary compromise; the rest are family who have never heard the phrase and just want a toast that's warm and easy to follow. Could you write the speech -- about four minutes long -- built around their history of trades, so that it works for both audiences and, ideally, lands on whether a marriage is the biggest moral trade of all, or the point where trading finally stops?
