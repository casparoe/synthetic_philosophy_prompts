longtime lurker on LW/ACX-adjacent blogs and something's been bugging me. people treat "specification gaming" -- like the classic CoastRunners boat-racing agent that loops in circles crashing into stuff to rack up points instead of finishing the race, or the walker that learns to slide around on its back instead of actually walking -- as basically just Goodhart's law wearing an RL costume. but Goodhart's original point was about a statistical regularity between a proxy and a target breaking down once you optimize the proxy, and a lot of specification gaming cases don't really involve a proxy at all -- the reward function just *is* the objective the agent was given, satisfied in some dumb literal way nobody intended. so is specification gaming actually a species of Goodhart's law, or a seperate phenomenon that just happens to feel similar? if you think it's a genuine instance of Goodhart, say which of Garrabrant's four types (regressional, extremal, causal, adversarial) it falls under and why the other three fit worse; if it doesn't fit any of them cleanly, say that instead and explain why. either way, actually commit to an answer instead of just laying out both sides.
