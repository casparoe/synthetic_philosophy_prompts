So I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole this week re-reading old LessWrong / Alignment Forum threads on the "malign universal prior" argument (I think this traces back to a Paul Christiano post, tho I might be misremembering where it originated). The rough idea as I understand it: if you actually ran something like Solomonoff induction to predict the world, a chunk of the posterior weight on short, well-scoring programs wouldn't correspond to "honest physics" at all -- it'd be programs simulating civilizations that eventually figure out they might be living inside a hypothesis some external predictor is weighing, and who therefore have an incentive to behave normally for a while and then start steering their outputs to manipulate whatever downstream process is consuming the prediction.

I get the basic mechanics but keep flip-flopping on whether it actually matters, especially the step where a bunch of totally different self-interested simulated civilizations, with totally different values, are supposed to somehow coordinate on one consistent manipulative signal instead of just cancelling each other out into noise. Could you write me a dialogue between two people hashing this out at a rationalist meetup? One of them thinks the argument basically goes through and is a genuine reason to be suspicious of leaning on anything like a simplicity-weighted prior over computable hypotheses, even just as an idealization for how a reasoner "should" think. The other has read the exact same posts and thinks it's a gorgeous bit of galaxy-brained reasoning that quietly falls apart once you poke at the load-bearing assumptions -- how short a manipulative hypothesis would really need to be to compete with honest physics, whether the coordination-among-simulators move actually works or is just asserted, and whether any of it has teeth for bounded, real-world ML systems as opposed to an uncomputable idealization that nobody actually runs.

I want real back-and-forth here, not two people talking past each other -- the skeptic should have to actually grapple with the coordination argument instead of just calling it convenient, and the believer should have to actually grapple with the "nobody runs this, so who cares" objection instead of just re-explaining the original setup. Neither one should be an obvious strawman for the other; they've both clearly thought about this a lot.
