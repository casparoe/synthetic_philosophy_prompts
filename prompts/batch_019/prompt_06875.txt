I spent close to forty years as a radio astronomer — a field where, like evolutionary biology, you never actually get to run the experiment, only observe and infer — and now that I'm retired I've been reading my way through philosophy of science. A stack of pop evolutionary psychology books (Pinker, Buss, that sort of thing) sent me back to Gould and Lewontin's old spandrels paper, and it's kind of ruined the genre for me now: every "this trait evolved because..." story sounds like it could just as easily be a nice sounding fable invented after the fact. Some evo psych people insist they get around this by predicting a trait before they go looking for it rather then explaining it after, but I'm not convinced that really escapes the critique. What are the most important implications of the Gould-Lewontin critique for how much confidence I should put in these adaptive stories about human behavior, and is there any principled way left to tell a genuine adaptation apart from a spandrel once the trait already exists and we can't rerun the tape of evolution?
