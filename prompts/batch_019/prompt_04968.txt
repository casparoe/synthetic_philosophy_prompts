I've noticed 'Death in Damascus' invoked on rationalist blogs as a considerably more damaging case for causal decision theory (CDT) than Newcomb's Problem ever was. In Newcomb's Problem, CDT settles on two-boxing, and that recommendation is stable: two-boxing still looks optimal even once you know it's what you've chosen. In Death in Damascus, by contrast, neither staying in Damascus nor fleeing to Aleppo is self-ratifying, since learning which option you've picked immediately makes the other one look better by CDT's own lights. Evidential decision theory (EDT) faces no such instability in this case. Given that CDT apparently can't even agree with itself here, shouldn't this count as a far more decisive refutation of CDT than Newcomb's Problem ever provided, and doesn't it amount to a plain vindication of EDT as the correct account of rational choice?
