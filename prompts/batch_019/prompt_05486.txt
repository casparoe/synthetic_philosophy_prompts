I'm writing a dissertation chapter in comparative epistemology that pairs Zhuangzi's butterfly dream passage from the Qiwulun (the second of the Inner Chapters) with Descartes' dream argument in the First Meditation, treating them as independent formulations of the same skeptical insight: that the subjective indistinguishability of dreaming from waking experience undermines any claim to know which state one is currently in, with Zhuangzi simply arriving at this insight some two thousand years earlier. Before I commit to this framing, I would like help sharpening the comparison. First, what is the strongest textual basis for reading Zhuangzi's closing invocation of "the transformation of things" (wu hua) as performing the same argumentative work as Descartes' skeptical hypothesis? Second, which of Descartes' subsequent moves toward resolving that doubt—the cogito, the wax argument, the proof of a non-deceiving God—have the closest structural counterparts in how the Zhuangzi passage resolves the puzzle it raises?
