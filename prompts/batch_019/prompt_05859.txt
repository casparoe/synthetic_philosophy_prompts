I'm on the methods-reform committee for a psychology journal, and we're finally rewriting our stats policy after years of replication-crisis hand-wringing. Half the committee wants to stay frequentist but tighten the threshold to p < .005 and mandate preregistration; the other half wants to ditch null-hypothesis significance testing entirely for Bayes factors and credible intervals, arguing NHST is rotten at the root. One member keeps pointing out that Bayesian updating isn't supposed to care about optional stopping or how many tests you ran, so preregistration is basically a patch for a disease Bayesians don't even catch -- but nobody's checked whether that holds up once you factor in how much wiggle room there is in picking priors and models. I need to send our decision out by friday. Draft the memo: recommend an actual policy rather then splitting the difference, and take the strongest objection to whatever we land on seriously instead of hand-waving past it.
