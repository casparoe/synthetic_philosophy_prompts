I spent thirty-five years as an experimental physicist, a good part of it working on precision timekeeping with atomic clocks, and only since retiring three years ago have I had the leisure to read philosophy seriously. A small group of retirees I meet with monthly at the local library has spent this term on the metaphysics of time, and something about our discussion has left me unsettled enough that I would like an outside opinion.

As I understand it, special relativity already decides the old dispute between presentism, on which only the present moment is real, and eternalism, on which past, present, and future all exist equally within a single four-dimensional block. Since simultaneity is only ever defined relative to an inertial frame, there is no frame-independent fact about which distant events are occurring "right now" - and without such a fact, presentism seems to lose the very distinction it needs between the real present and the not-yet-real future. I keep returning to the old thought experiment, popularized by Roger Penrose, of two pedestrians passing each other on the street, one walking toward the Andromeda galaxy and one walking away from it, whose respective planes of simultaneity out at that distance disagree about whether a civilization there has already launched a space fleet or is still deliberating. If there is no observer-independent answer to whether that launch has occurred, it is difficult to see how the launch, and by extension the whole future, can be anything other than already fixed and real, exactly as eternalism claims. When I raised this at our meeting, a friend in the group who taught philosophy before she retired insisted that presentism remains a live, respectable position among specialists who understand the physics perfectly well. That surprised me a great deal. Is there an actual gap in the argument I have just laid out, or is this simply a case of professional philosophers being unwilling to accept a conclusion that the physics has, in effect, already forced on everyone else?
