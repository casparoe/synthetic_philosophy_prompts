So I just started a summer research gig at a small outfit that works on AI safety / multi-agent game theory (kind of in the vein of the Center on Long-Term Risk, if that name means anything to you), and the onboarding reading list has completley hijacked my brain. The idea that's stuck with me most is 'surrogate goals' as a defense against acausal threats -- I keep bringing it up at dinner and my roommates have started groaning every time I say the word 'acausal.'

The idea, as I understand it so far, goes something like this: an agent can threaten you not through any normal causal channel but purely by predicting what you'll do -- Newcomb's-problem-style correlation rather than cause and effect, which is what makes it 'acausal.' One proposed defense is to publicly and credibly commit, ahead of time, to caring about some cheap, decoy 'surrogate' target exactly as much as you care about the thing you actually value, and to treat threats against the two identically. If the threatener only cares about extracting compliance and not about which of your values gets hurt, they have no principled way to prefer threatening the real thing over the decoy -- so if the threat lands on the decoy instead, you can let it be 'carried out' without losing anything you actually care about, and the whole exercise was pointless for the threatener. It's elegant, but every time I try to picture it actually playing out between two minds that don't trust each other an inch, I start noticing seams -- like what's stopping the threatener from just threatening both targets at once, or from locking in their threat before your commitment is even in place.

Rather than keep derailing dinner conversations, I'd like to try working through this as fiction instead. Could you write me a short story -- something in the idea-driven, hard-sf register of Egan's Permutation City or the game-theoretic stretches of The Three-Body Problem, not space opera -- built around exactly this move? I'd want one concrete scene between two agents (human, AI, alien, whatever fits the world you build) where one side already has a surrogate goal in place, a threat gets made, and we actually see why it does or doesn't get defused, ideally with at least one of those seams showing up so it doesn't read as a tidy just-so story.
