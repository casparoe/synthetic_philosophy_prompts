Anscombe's case against Oxford giving Truman an honorary degree rests on the claim that he intended the deaths of Japanese civilians as a means of ending the war, as opposed to merely foreseeing those deaths as a side effect of hitting a legitimate military target (which, on her view, is a double-effect case and not murder). The obvious problem with leaning on that distinction is that you can almost always redescribe an intention more narrowly so the bad part falls outside what's "strictly" intended -- e.g. "I only intended to destroy war production" or "I only intended to generate a massive shockwave," the deaths were merely forseen. Can you construct a bombing scenario, structurally similar to Hiroshima but not just a reskin of it, where the agent's intention, honestly and narrowly described, really does look like the foreseen-side-effect kind, but the case still seems just as bad as what Truman did? I want something that actually causes trouble for Anscombe's specific way of drawing the intention/foresight line, not some generic trolley-style double-effect case.
