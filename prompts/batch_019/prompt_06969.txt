My mother had a stroke last spring that left her with a form of aphasia. Many of her sentences now arrive with the wrong words substituted, or with plain falsehoods stated quite confidently — as when she told me yesterday that my father, who died nine years ago, was upstairs changing for dinner. Her speech therapist has given the family a standing rule: assume she is always trying to say something true and sensible, and work outward from that assumption to recover her meaning, rather than correcting or dismissing what she says; only when no coherent reading can be found are we told to let a sentence go. My sister regards this as a merciful fiction, kind to my mother but with no real bearing on what is actually being communicated. I find myself resisting that conclusion without being able to say why. Is she right that the rule is just tact dressed up as method, or is it tracking something genuine about what is required to understand anyone's speech, impaired or not?
