Random thing that's been bugging me lately. I cover the replication crisis a lot, and I keep running into the same move from researchers: "I know this effect is real, I've run the study a dozen times myself" — so when someone's replication fails, they just wave it off as a botched attempt without ever digging into it. Somethings off about that logic but I can't quite put my finger on it. If you genuinely do know an effect is real from all that experience, isn't tossing out a shaky failed replication actually the rational move? Or is that exactly the kind of reasoning that lets bad results survive forever?
