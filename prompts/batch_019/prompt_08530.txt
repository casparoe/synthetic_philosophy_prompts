Consider two principals who will each delegate the playing of some game to an agent or representative -- an AI system, a diplomat, a lawyer -- and suppose each agent may come to learn private information that its principal never sees. A "safe Pareto improvement" is an ex-ante agreement between the principals about how to instruct their agents so that, whatever the private information turns out to be, both end up at least as well off as under the equilibrium their agents would otherwise default to -- without either principal needing to disclose, or simply trust, the other's private information. Explain why "safe" is doing real work here rather than being redundant with "Pareto improvement": walk through a concrete way a naively negotiated improvement over the default could backfire, and state the general condition that rules this out. I'd also like your gut sense of what's given up, in terms of achievable gains, by insisting on safety rather than settling for an improvement that is merely better in expectation.
