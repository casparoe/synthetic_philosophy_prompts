I spent about thirty-five years as an experimental particle physicist before retiring a few years back, always chasing what's "really" fundamental in the sense of the smallest constituents of matter, and now that I've got time on my hands I've started reading philosophy for fun. A few weeks ago I fell down a Stanford Encyclopedia rabbit hole on "grounding" that kept citing Jonathan Schaffer's paper on priority monism, so I went and read the actual thing. I think I followed the main argument okay: Schaffer sets up a "tiling constraint" (whatever's fundamental has to jointly cover all of reality, no gaps, no overlaps) plus foundationalism (there has to be a bottom level somewhere, no infinite regress), and then runs the gunk argument -- if matter turns out to be "atomless gunk," where every part has a further proper part forever, the atomist picture (fundamental = the smallest bits) simply has no candidates left to be fundamental, so it can't guarantee satisfying foundationalism. Monism supposedly dodges this since the whole cosmos can do the fundamental job whether or not it's innards are gunky. As a physics guy I actually found this a pretty elegant move, like an existence proof.

Here's what's bugging me though, and it might just be me missing something: doesn't a "junky" world -- where every object is a proper part of some bigger object, so there's no maximal whole either -- make exactly the same trouble for the monist that gunk makes for the atomist? If Schaffer has to worry about gunk undermining "there's a bottom," why doesn't he have to worry just as much about junk undermining "there's a top"? I brought this up with my old book club (a bunch of retired engineers who've basically turned into an amateur philosophy society) and we went around in circles over coffee for an hour. I have a vague half-memory of something about classical mereology's unrestricted composition automatically guarenteeing a fusion of absolutely everything, which would rule out junk "for free" in a way that nothing rules out gunk, but I don't trust that memory and can't reconstruct the argument myself. Could you walk me through how the gunk and junk cases are actually treated in the fundamentality literature -- is there a real asymmetry there, or is Schaffer just helping himself to foundationalism whenever it's convenient? And does the whole gunk-and-junk mess ultimately push people toward giving up on there needing to be a fundamental level at all, rather than patching monism or atomism to survive it? (I've also got opinions about his entanglement argument for monism, being an actual physicist, but that's a rant for another day.)
