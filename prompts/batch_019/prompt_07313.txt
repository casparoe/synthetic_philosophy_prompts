This term in my Kierkegaard seminar we've been working through Two Ages: A Literary Review alongside the background of the Corsair affair, where he was lampooned in print and then jeered at by strangers in the street who recognized him only from caricature. In that text Kierkegaard is careful to distinguish "the public" — an abstraction with no concrete existence, answerable to no one, which he says the press essentially conjures into being — from things like a people, a generation, or a genuine assembly, all of which retain some real, accountable substance even when they act collectively. Given that distinction, how do you think he would assess a small, stable online forum where the same pseudonymous members have argued with and answered to one another for years, as against a single post that goes viral and draws in a crowd of strangers for a day? Would the forum count, on his terms, as something closer to a real association than to "the public," or does its press-like, disembodied character put it on the leveling side of the ledger regardless of how stable the membership is? I'd like your best reconstruction of his actual reasoning here, not a generic "Kierkegaard predicted the internet" answer.
