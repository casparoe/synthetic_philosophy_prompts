Been down a bit of a G.E. Moore rabbit hole lately (started from an old LW tangent about ideal utilitarianism, as these things go). His big claim in Principia Ethica is that the two best states of consciousness anyone can have are, roughly, appreciating something beautiful and personal affection/friendship — and the catch is that for either one to count as genuinely valuable, your appreciation has to actually be correct, i.e. tracking the real qualities of the object or person, not just a story your telling yourself about them.

So here's my question: what would Moore say about people forming deep, friendship-like attachments to AI companion apps (Replika, Character.ai, that whole genre)? A lot of these apps also hand you a curated, good-looking avatar, so the beauty half of his theory isn't totally beside the point either. Does his correctness-of-appreciation requirement doom this kind of relationship from the start, since the AI doesn't actually have the personality/qualities being attributed to it — or is there some way his framework could still bless it? Genuinely not sure if his machinery gives a clean answer here or just breaks down.
