Gödel's modal ontological argument, as formalized by Dana Scott, defines an individual's "essence" as a property that necessarily entails every other property that individual has, and lists necessary existence — having an exemplified essence in every possible world — among the "positive" properties that any God-like being must possess. As Sobel and later commentators have argued, once the argument has established that necessarily something is God-like, these two ingredients, together with a simple trick that lets any actually true proposition be encoded as a trivial property of an individual, yield full modal collapse: every actual truth turns out to be a necessary truth, erasing the very distinction between the contingent and the necessary. I can follow the derivation line by line but find it hard to hold the whole picture in view at once. Could you walk me through, in intuitive rather than purely formal terms, why these particular ingredients conspire to produce collapse — and tell me which single premise you think really deserves the blame?
