I'm drafting a section of a paper in comparative epistemology that pairs Zhuangzi's butterfly dream, from the end of the Qiwulun chapter, with Descartes' dream argument in the First Meditation, on the assumption that both raise the same doubt about appearance and reality: Zhuang Zhou wakes unable to say whether he is a man who dreamed he was a butterfly, or a butterfly now dreaming he is a man. What I can't find much on is how, if at all, the Zhuangzi tradition answers that doubt once it's raised: is there some analogue to the cogito, or to Descartes' later appeal to a non-deceiving God, that lets Zhuang Zhou close the gap the dream opens up? This puzzles me because the passage doesn't actually trail off into suspended doubt - it goes on to insist that some distinction between Zhou and the butterfly holds, then folds the whole episode into the notion of "the transformation of things" (wu hua), which I'd have thought counts as some kind of resolution, though none of the secondary sources I've read treat it that way. Am I missing an existing debate about how this passage is meant to be resolved, or is "resolution" simply the wrong category to bring to it in the first place?
