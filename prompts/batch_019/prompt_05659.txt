I've spent the last several years writing about the friction between cosmology and scripture, which is presumably why the organizers of an upcoming workshop pairing astrophysicists with biblical scholars have asked me to give the opening remarks. Half the room will be scientists who think any theological framing of their work is a category error; the other half will be scripture scholars tired of having their field treated as a relic whenever a telescope comes up. I don't want to open with a truce — I want to open with Galileo's actual argument in his 1615 letter to the Grand Duchess Christina: that scripture and nature are both true because they share one author, that apparent conflicts between them are failures of interpretation rather than a real contest of authority, and that this licenses overriding a literal reading of scripture only where there is genuine demonstrated proof, not wherever science currently finds it convenient. Please write those opening remarks, about five minutes read aloud, using that specific argument rather than the flattened 'non-overlapping magisteria' version people reach for by default, so that both halves of the room leave with real stakes in the disagreement, not a polite exemption from it.
