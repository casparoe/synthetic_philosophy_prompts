I'm doing the toast at her leaving drinks next friday, and since building a decent arc out of real material is literally my day job (I write novels), I dont want to hand her some limp "follow your passion" speech. I want it built around what Keynes ACTUALLY argued in that grandchildren essay of his — the bit about the love of money being basically a pathology, and his worry that most people, once they actually got the leisure, wouldn't have the first clue how to use it well — not the fridge-magnet version people trot out whenever they namedrop him. Problem is half the room is her old banking colleagues who'll smell a lecture a mile off, and the other half are the artist friends she's been neglecting for two decades who think finance people are lost causes. Can you write me a five-minute toast that uses his actual argument to say something true and specific about what Priya's doing, and still feels like a toast and not a seminar?
