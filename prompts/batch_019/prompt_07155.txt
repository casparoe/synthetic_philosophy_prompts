I have recently been accepted into a clinical program offering psilocybin-assisted therapy for the existential distress that has come with my terminal diagnosis, and in preparing for it I came across L.A. Paul's work on transformative experience. Her central distinction — between experiences that are epistemically transformative, teaching you something about what it is like that no amount of testimony could have conveyed, and experiences that are personally transformative, reshaping the very preferences you would use to judge them afterward — describes my situation almost too precisely. Write a short story, told in the first person by someone in my position on the eve of their first session, that dramatizes both of these dimensions at once, without resolving the bind by having the experience turn out simply 'good' or 'bad' in retrospect. The philosophical difficulty should be felt in the texture of the narration rather than stated outright.
