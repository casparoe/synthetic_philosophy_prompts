My uncle Denny died two weeks ago and I'm giving his eulogy Saturday, which is terrifying because Denny got me into philosophy of mind in the first place -- neither of us ever went to college, we just spent like fifteen years arguing about consciousness on his porch, working through library books and Keith Frankish videos. Near the end he was fully convinced of illusionism about phenomenal consciousness (I'm maybe 80% there myself): not that he didn't really feel things, but that the vivid "what it's like" glow experience seems to have is itself a trick introspection plays on us, not some extra ingredient floating in there. He used to joke "don't you dare tell people I'm watching over them, and don't you dare tell them I didn't really love them either -- those are wrong for the same reason." A couple guys from our old porch-debate group will clock it if I flub this into eliminativism or a smuggled-in soul, but most of the room is just his bowling league and cousins who want to feel better about loosing him. Can you write it -- true to what he actually believed, comforting for people who've never heard the word "qualia," maybe five minutes tops, and not a lecture?
