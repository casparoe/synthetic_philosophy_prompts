I'm a PhD student working on the formal foundations of inductive inference, and I keep encountering the claim -- in textbooks and informal seminar discussion alike -- that Solomonoff induction is essentially Occam's razor made mathematically precise, with Kolmogorov complexity standing in for the informal notion of simplicity. This strikes me as too quick: Occam's razor, on its usual reading, tells us to discard every hypothesis but the simplest one consistent with the evidence, whereas the universal prior underlying Solomonoff induction assigns a nonzero weight to every computable hypothesis and merely lets more complex ones decay in influence, which sounds closer to what is sometimes called Epicurus's principle of keeping every explanation the evidence hasn't ruled out. Please compare Occam's razor, taken in its eliminative sense, with what Solomonoff induction actually does with complexity weightings, and tell me in which respects the label "Occam's razor, formalized" is earned, and in which respects it obscures the fact that Solomonoff induction is better described as a reconciliation of Occam and Epicurus than as a formalization of Occam alone. I would also value your judgment on whether the minimum description length principle -- which does select a single hypothesis rather than averaging over all of them -- has a stronger claim to that label than full Solomonoff induction does.
