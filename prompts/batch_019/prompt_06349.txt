Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have spent much of my free time reading philosophy of mind, and I keep returning to the combination problem for panpsychism. I understand the basic Jamesian worry about how a multitude of micro-subjects could ever add up to one macro-subject like ourselves, but I have had more trouble seeing how the two leading strategies for handling that worry actually confront each other. Could you write me a dialogue between a constitutive micropsychist, who thinks combination can be achieved bottom-up via a sui generis phenomenal bonding relation among micro-subjects, and a priority cosmopsychist, who thinks this "subject-summing" problem is principled rather than merely difficult, and so instead derives finite subjects like us top-down from one fundamental cosmic subject. Each should press the other on the specific weak point of the other's strategy — whether deriving bounded individual minds from an antecedently unified cosmic consciousness is really more tractable than building unified minds up from many tiny ones, and whether "phenomenal bonding" names a real relation or just relabels the mystery — and should answer in kind rather than simply restate an opening position. Keep it to roughly a dozen turns, with no tidy resolution imposed at the end.
