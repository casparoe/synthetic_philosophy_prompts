I cover science and tech for a mid-size outlet, and every spring I'm one of five judges on a panel that picks the winner of a regional science journalism award. We vote live on a call: each judge names their pick out loud, always in the same order (alphabetical by last name, don't ask why), before we open it up to discussion. Lately whoever goes first basically decides it -- the rest of us "converge" even though I happen to know a couple of judges walked in favoring something else. One judge keeps calling this an information cascade and wants us to switch to blind, simultaneous ballots; our chair says that'll kill the discussion that actually catches real mistakes, like the year we almost gave the award to a piece who's central quote turned out to be fabricated. I gotta write the memo the panel votes on before next year's cycle -- recommend a specific procedure (keep it live and sequential, go blind and simultaneous, or some hybrid) and give reasons that actually engage the chair's worry instead of just insisting that cascades are bad.
