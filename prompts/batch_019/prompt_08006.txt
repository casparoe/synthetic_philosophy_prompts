I've been teaching myself Bayesian reasoning by working through examples on my own, and I've gotten stuck on a specific worry about Jeffrey conditionalization: the rule for updating your beliefs when a piece of evidence makes you more confident in something without ever making you certain of it. When I chain two such updates together, for two pieces of evidence that both bear on the same underlying possibilities, the order in which I take them into account seems to change where my beliefs end up. Ordinary conditionalization on evidence you become certain of doesn't have this problem: you land in the same place no matter which fact you consider first. Please argue that this sensitivity to order shows that Jeffrey conditionalization cannot be a fully rational procedure for updating on uncertain evidence, and then present the strongest reply you can construct on Jeffrey's behalf.
