A student of mine recently described a dilemma that I have not been able to resolve to my own satisfaction, and I would value your perspective on it. After her father died, she folded his final letter to her into an origami crane, which has stood on her desk ever since. She has now decided to have the letter properly preserved—flattened and framed—but finds herself strangely reluctant, insisting that something would be destroyed in the unfolding, even though every word on the page would survive untouched. Her partner thinks this reluctance is simply confused: it is one and the same sheet of paper whether folded into a crane or lying flat, so nothing is created when it becomes a crane and nothing is destroyed when it is unfolded again. I am not convinced her partner has the better argument, but I cannot say exactly where it goes wrong. Could you help me work out whether her reluctance is tracking something real, and, if so, what precisely would be lost?
