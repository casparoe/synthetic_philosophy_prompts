My grandpa passed away this past Tuesday at 74, and I'm giving his eulogy Saturday. He got drafted into Vietnam at 19, and for fifty years the family line never changed: he did his job, he came home, don't make it more complicated then that. Except two summers ago, out of nowhere, he told me late one night that he used to lie awake over there wondering if the guy shooting back at him from the treeline had just as much right to be doing it as he did. I'm halfway through a just war theory seminar right now where we just spent two weeks on McMahan's argument against the moral equality of combatants, and I honestly cannot hear my grandfathers confession as anything other than him working out that exact problem alone, decades early, with no vocabulary for it. His old army buddies are gonna be sitting in the front pews expecting the usual 'he served with honor' stuff -- I don't want to get up there and turn it into a philosophy lecture, or imply he was some kind of war criminal, but I can't just bury what he told me either. Can you help me write it?
