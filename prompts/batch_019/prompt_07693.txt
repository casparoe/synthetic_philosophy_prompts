Write a dialogue between an updateless decision theorist and a causal decision theorist who are standing together when Omega — a perfect predictor — appears and announces the result of a counterfactual mugging: it flipped a fair coin, the coin came up tails, and it is therefore asking them for $100. Omega adds that had the coin come up heads instead, it would have paid them $10,000, but only because it predicted they would hand over the $100 in the tails case. Neither of them made, or ever had any opportunity to make, a binding commitment beforehand; they must simply reason, on the spot, about whether to pay. Have each try to talk the other out of their position, engaging with the specific reasons the other gives rather than restating their own view, so that the exchange brings out their underlying disagreement about whether the rational act is the one dictated by the best overall policy or the one that is causally efficacious given how things now stand.
