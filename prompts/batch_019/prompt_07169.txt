My aunt Miriam taught philosophy of mind for forty years, and her one big idea, the thing she brought up at every family dinner, was that you can perfectly well imagine a creature that's physically identical to a person -- same brain, same behavior, yells "ow" when it stubs its toe -- with nobody home inside, and that this proves consciousness can't just be brain stuff. She died Tuesday and I'm giving the eulogy Saturday to a room that's basically all cousins, her book club, and my very Catholic grandmother, plus one former PhD student of hers who's flying in and will absolutely clock it if I get her actual view wrong. Problem is, Miriam was NOT a soul-and-heaven person -- she used to get annoyed when people heard "zombie argument" and assumed she believed in ghosts, and she'd insist that consciousness being "extra," not reducible to physics, is a totally different claim from consciousness surviving once the brain stops. So I can't do the easy "she's watching over us" line without kind of lying about what she thought, but I also can't do the flat "she's gone, it was just atoms" line either, since that's exactly the view she spent her career arguing against. I write novels for a living so the writing itself doesn't scare me -- I just don't know how to thread this for people who've never heard the word "qualia" in thier life. Can you draft me something like 5 minutes long that actually gets this right?
