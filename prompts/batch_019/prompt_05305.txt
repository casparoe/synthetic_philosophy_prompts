Two friends of mine got into a disagreement at dinner about a third friend, Ray, who had snapped angrily at his partner in front of a bunch of us the week before. Elena said we shouldn't really hold Ray responsible for it in the deepest sense: he acted exactly as his temperament, upbringing, and that particular rough week disposed him to act, and since he never chose any of the things that made him that way, he isn't the ultimate source of the outburst. She added that there's no way around this either, since any attempt on his part to reshape his own character would itself just be another act performed by the very character in question. Marcus objected that this proves too much: by the same reasoning, Ray deserves no real credit for the patient, generous things he does either, since those flow from a character he equally didn't choose. He asked Elena whether she would actually say that to Ray's face. Elena said yes, strictly speaking she would; we just find it psychologically very hard to stop praising and blaming people once we're used to it. Marcus then pointed out that even if part of Ray's outburst had been sheer undetermined randomness in his brain rather than anything traceable to his history, Elena's argument wouldn't budge an inch, which suggests her real complaint isn't with determinism at all but with an impossible demand: that a person somehow create themselves from nothing, prior to having any character at all. He called that a strange thing to hold anyone to, and said our everyday practices of praise and blame were never built on any such demand in the first place. Elena replied that showing a demand can't be met doesn't make it the wrong demand: if genuine responsibility requires a kind of self-authorship that nothing could ever have, then nothing is genuinely responsible, and it being inconvenient for our habits doesn't make it false.
