I have followed the units-of-selection debate on and off for years through the rationalist blogosphere, where it is something of a rite of passage -- Eliezer Yudkowsky's old essay on the "tragedy of group selectionism" still gets cited as a parable about how seductive-but-wrong reasoning can capture an entire field. I have now secured a guest for my podcast who argues the other side of that parable: an evolutionary biologist who defends multilevel selection theory in the lineage of Sober and D. S. Wilson, and who has publicly sided with the Nowak-Tarnita-Wilson challenge to the generality of inclusive fitness theory. When pressed on the charge that any group-selection model can be rewritten term-for-term as a kin-selection model with identical predictions, she tends to answer that the two formalisms are "algebraically interchangeable but not explanatorily interchangeable."

Please draft eight or nine interview questions that go beyond having her restate the position, sequenced so that line gets progressively harder to lean on. I want at least one question that presses this equivalence charge directly, in the spirit of West, Griffin, and Gardner's "social semantics" papers, and asks what observation -- even in principle -- could favor one "equivalent" formalism's causal story over the other's; one on Maynard Smith's haystack model and how much assortment and how little migration a real population would need before between-group selection could plausibly matter; one on how she squares her position with the roughly 140-signature rebuttal that the 2010 Nowak-Tarnita-Wilson eusociality paper provoked from the rest of the field; and one on whether "superorganism" talk about ant or termite colonies survives contact with a formal adaptation test like Gardner and Grafen's, or borrows more credibility from the metaphor than the underlying math actually supports. Close with a question asking what specific empirical finding would be enough to make her abandon multilevel selection and concede that gene-level selection was doing all the causal work. For each question, add a short follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give.
