Quick one that came up in my phil of mind seminar this week. We were going through Turing's 1950 Mind paper and got stuck on the ESP section — he basically says the statistical evidence for telepathy is overwhelming, and then reworks the imitation game with a 'telepathy-proof room' to route around it. One of my students argued that since Turing was apparently happy to treat parapsychology as solid science, we shouldn't put much stock in his intuitions about machines and thinking either — that the whole paper's kind of compromised by that one section. I was inclined to agree and told them to just skip past it as an embarassing blind spot on his part. Is that the right way to think about it, or is there a better way to read what he's doing there?
