I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and for the past three years I've also served on the judging panel of a national science-writing prize. Each year nine of us rank a shortlist of five finalists, and the winner is determined by Borda count: points are assigned by rank and summed across judges. This cycle we learned that a bloc of judges affiliated with a rival outlet, anxious that their own nominee would lose, ranked the presumptive front-runner near the bottom of their ballots even though they privately rated her work second-best overall -- a textbook "burying" maneuver that handed the prize to someone else. The panel is now split: one faction wants to replace Borda count with a Condorcet method or approval voting to make this harder to pull off, while another insists that's wasted effort, since the Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem already guarantees that any ranking-based rule we could adopt is manipulable by someone, so we might as well keep the system we know. I need to circulate a short memo before next month's meeting that actually recommends what we should do, with a timeline for implementing it, and that takes the second faction's objection seriously rather than brushing past it. Could you draft that memo?
