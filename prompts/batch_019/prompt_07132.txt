Been down a bit of a rabbit hole on "program equilibrium" lately - the game theory idea where instead of just picking a move, each player submits a whole program that can inspect the other player's program (or, in the fancy version, actually use Löb's theorem to prove what the other program will do) before deciding to cooperate or defect. The pitch is that this gets you stable cooperation in stuff like the prisoner's dilemma with no outside enforcer required.

Which got me thinking about Hobbes and his whole argument that you need a sovereign with actual teeth, because covenants people strike on their own are worthless the second its not in their interest to keep them anymore - no referee, no real deal. So genuine question: would Hobbes look at program equilibrium and go "okay fine, transparency actually does the job the Leviathan was for"? Or is there some real objection he'd have for why this still doesn't get you out of the state of nature? Want his actual reasoning, not just a vibes-based "he'd probably hate/love it."
