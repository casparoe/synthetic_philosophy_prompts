I am at work on a novel whose central figure, a celebrated literary critic, is eventually unmasked as a fraud: for her entire career she has reviewed novels she never read, basing each verdict on a trusted assistant's plot summaries and handpicked quotations. I want her exposure to land as more than a case of professional dishonesty. As I understand it, the acquaintance principle in aesthetics already settles the deeper question here: since a genuine judgment of a work's beauty or literary merit must be grounded in firsthand experience of that work, no amount of secondhand description could have given her anything more than unjustified opinion, however accidentally accurate it turned out to be. Given that her fraud is therefore a matter of settled epistemology rather than mere deception, how might I dramatize that impossibility clearly enough for readers to feel it, and not merely conclude that she behaved unethically?
