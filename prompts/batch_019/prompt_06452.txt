Ok, this is going to sound like a weirdly specific mashup, but it's been stuck in my head all week. I'm taking an ancient philosophy seminar that just covered Stoic fatalism, specifically the old "Idle Argument": if its fated that you'll recover from your illness, you'll recover whether or not you call a doctor, and if its fated you won't, you won't either way, so why bother calling the doctor. Chrysippus's famous reply is that recovery isn't simply fated but "co-fated" with calling the doctor - your action gets baked into the very causal chain that produces the fated outcome, so deliberating isn't actually pointless. Completely separately, I'm in a decision theory reading group that just did "Death in Damascus," the Gibbard/Harper case where Death has already committed to showing up wherever you're actually going to be tomorrow, so fleeing to Aleppo doesn't causally help you, even though the moment you condition on having decided to flee, it looks like Death will obviously be waiting in Aleppo too.

I mentioned to a friend from the reading group how similar the underlying structure of these two puzzles felt, and now we've got a genuine disagreement going. She thinks Chrysippus's co-fated move basically anticipates the causal decision theorist's response and lets him escape the Damascus case pretty cleanly. I think he'd walk straight into the same ratifiability mess that trips up modern causal decision theorists once Death is stipulated to be a truly perfect predictor. Could you actually work through, using only the conceptual tools Chrysippus himself had available (no smuggling in modern decision theory), how he would respond if someone put the Death in Damascus scenario to him directly? Does "co-fated" really give him a way out, or does it just relocate the same problem one level down? And once you translate his eventual answer into modern terms, does it land closer to the causal or the evidential decision theorist's camp? Would love an actual adjudication rather than a "both readings are defensible" shrug - this has genuinely become a whole thing between us.
