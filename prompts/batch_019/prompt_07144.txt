In my philosophy of law seminar, we recently read David Lewis's essay "Do We Believe in Penal Substitution?" Lewis argues there that the Christian doctrine of penal substitution is puzzling because punishment, unlike a debt, does not seem to be the kind of thing that can simply be transferred from a guilty party onto a willing, innocent substitute. This puzzle strikes me as overstated, though, since our own legal system already recognizes vicarious liability: an employer can be held liable for an employee's tort, and a firm can be fined for a manager's misconduct. Doesn't this show that responsibility for wrongdoing can already be legitimately shifted onto someone else? If so, why does Lewis treat the transferability of punishment as such a distinctive philosophical problem, rather than as something the law has already resolved?
