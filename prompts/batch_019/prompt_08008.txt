So a few weeks back someone in the ACX comments (or maybe it was a LW shortform, I can't remember) mentioned that Anscombe basically coined "consequentialism" as a term of abuse, which sent me down a rabbit hole into her old pamphlet "Mr Truman's Degree," written to stop Oxford from giving Truman an honorary doctorate. What stuck with me is that her argument isn't the generic "don't kill civilians" line — her specific claim is that Truman didn't just foresee civilian deaths as a side effect of hitting military targets, he had the cities themselves targeted, with killing and terrorizing the population being the actual mechanism for ending the war, and that this makes the usual double-effect defense unavailable and the "but it shortened the war and saved more lives overall" argument simply beside the point when it comes to whether the act was murder.

Anyway, this turned out to be less idle than I expected, because I sit on the honorary-degrees subcommitee at my university, and we've got a live nomination that rhymes with this uncomfortably closely: a retired four-star general, Marion Voss, who ran the closing strategic-bombing campaign of a war a few decades back, where the acknowledged targeting doctrine was to hit civilian housing and morale directly — not railyards and factories, where civilian deaths would just be a side effect — on the theory that collapsing the population's will to fight would end things faster than a land invasion would. Historians mostly agree it worked, in the narrow sense of ending the war sooner and probably reducing total deaths on both sides. About half the committee treats that as basically dispositive; the other half thinks the intentional-targeting-of-civilians part makes the body count irrelevant, full stop, and is visibly uneasy that "the math worked out" is doing any real work in the argument at all (it's basically our own local, higher-stakes version of the "do the ends ever justify the means" fight that this whole corner of the internet has every few months).

I need to write the recommendation memo we'll circulate before the vote next Thursday, and I'd rather not just hide behind "reasonable people disagree" — the committee wants an actual recommendation. Could you draft it for me? I want it to land on something concrete (approve, decline, or table pending some specific further step), and I want the reasoning to actually engage with Anscombe's intention-vs-foresight argument rather than gesturing vaguely at just-war theory, while still giving the "it shortened the war" camp the strongest version of their case before explaining whether or why it moves the needle.
