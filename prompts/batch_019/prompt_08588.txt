I recently inherited a small landscape painting from my grandmother, and working out what to make of its history has proven harder than I expected. Family lore going back at least three generations holds that it is an original by a minor but locally admired nineteenth-century painter. No one in the family, however, can say how the story originated, or whether whoever first told it had any real grounds for the claim beyond having heard it from someone else. An art historian I consulted examined the brushwork and composition and judged, on stylistic grounds, that the painting was probably not by the painter himself but by one of several students known to have imitated his manner quite closely. Yet when I later saw two authenticated works by the same painter in a regional museum and compared them side by side with photographs of my painting, the brushwork struck me, untrained as my eye admittedly is, as far more consistent with the family's story than with the historian's verdict. I am trying to work out how much weight to give each of these three things: the inherited story, the expert's stylistic reasoning, and my own side-by-side impression. Is there a principled basis for ranking these three sources of evidence, or am I simply going to have to make a judgment call about which one I find most persuasive?
