In my philosophy of religion seminar, we recently worked through Dana Scott's reconstruction of Gödel's modal ontological argument, deriving the conclusion that a God-like being necessarily exists from axioms about positive properties, essence, and necessary existence. Our professor mentioned that the argument, despite being valid in S5, is widely thought to entail "modal collapse" -- the result that every true proposition turns out to be necessarily true -- but we didn't get to unpack why. I can follow the symbolic derivation well enough, but I don't yet have a real feel for what's actually driving the collapse. Please walk me through, in intuitive terms, why the definition of "essence" forces this result, why it generalizes to any contingent truth rather than staying confined to claims about God, and whether a defender could revise that definition -- as I gather has been attempted -- without giving up the argument's basic structure.
