At my company we maintain a payment-processing service with a known timing bug: a specific combination of network latency and retry logic causes it to submit a duplicate charge to a customer's card, which happens a handful of times a month. No customer has ever actually been double-charged, though, because a reconciliation job scans transactions every few seconds and refunds any duplicate before it could appear on a statement. For our upcoming compliance audit, our compliance lead wants the documentation to state that the system "does not have a double-charging defect," since a customer being double-charged is now structurally impossible. Something about that feels wrong to me, even though the logic seems hard to fault: the bad outcome genuinely cannot happen anymore. Is our compliance lead right that there's nothing to disclose, or is there a meaningful sense in which the system still has the defect, just permanently hidden from view?
