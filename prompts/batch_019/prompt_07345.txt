Okay so this has been bugging me since our math team meeting on Tuesday and I honestly can't tell if I'm crazy or if my friend has a point. Our coach showed us this game called Chomp — you've got a grid of "cookies" with one poisoned one, and on your turn you eat a cookie plus every cookie above and to the right of it, and whoever gets stuck eating the poisoned one loses. She then showed us the classic proof that the first player always has a winning strategy: you assume for contradiction that the SECOND player has one instead, then argue the first player could just eat one random cookie and basically steal the second player's strategy for themselves (since having extra cookies already gone never actually hurts you in this game), which would mean the first player wins too — impossible, since only one of them can have a winning strategy. So it has to have been the first player with the winning strategy all along.

Here's the thing though: that proof never tells you what the strategy actually is. And apparently, other than a few special board shapes (like really skinny ones, or perfect squares), literally nobody has ever figured out what moves player 1 is actually supposed to make to guarantee the win, for boards in general. My friend Kiera says that doesnt really count as knowing player 1 wins — she compared it to claiming there's a needle in a haystack somewhere without ever being able to point to it — and thinks the whole "proof" is kind of a cheap trick that mathematicians just get away with. I think a valid proof is a valid proof no matter how unsatisfying it feels, so she's wrong to be suspicious of it. We argued about this for like 20 minutes at lunch and got nowhere. Is one of us actually right, or is this one of those things where it secretly depends on what you even mean by "proving something exists" in math?
