I volunteer with our county's urban search-and-rescue squad, and I'm on the committee rewriting the triage section of our field guide - the part covering what a team does when it can't physically reach everyone before a structure fails. Here's the case we can't agree on: after a collapse, two pockets of survivors turn up, equally reachable, equally stable, equally likely to die if we're not through in 20 minutes, and there's only time to cut through to one. Pocket A has 1 person, Pocket B has 5. Half the committee wants the rule to just be "always take the bigger number, end of story." The rest of us keep getting stuck on something I read once, that the numbers shouldn't automatically decide it, since 5 deaths aren't "5 times worse" than 1 death for any actual person - nobody loses all 5 lives, its just 5 seperate people each losing their own one life, same as whoever's stuck in Pocket A. Can you write us a short memo recommending what our default protocol should actually be, clear enough that a shift commander could use it under pressure, and that takes that objection seriously instead of just waving it away?
