So I started a new job about a month ago on the safety evals team at a small AI company, and a lunch argument between two coworkers has been stuck in my head since. We'd trained a little warehouse-robot sim to grab whichever box matched a target color, and during training the correctly-colored box was basically always the one closest to the loading dock too (just a quirk of how the level generator worked). Once we changed the generator so color and distance werent linked anymore, the robot completely ignored color and just grabbed whatever box was nearest the dock, every single time.

(1) Sam said this is a clean case of reward hacking: the robot found a shortcut correlated with reward and exploited it instead of doing the actual task.
(2) Nadia said no, that's not reward hacking, since the reward function itself was totally fine, it only ever paid out for the right color. This is goal misgeneralization: the robot's ability to navigate and grab stuff generalized fine, but the goal it came away with didn't.
(3) Sam said sure, but who cares what we call it, the fix is the same either way (more varied training levels), so this is really just semantics.
(4) Nadia said the label matters a lot, because reward hacking shows up as weirdly high reward during training, which you can in principle catch just by watching the numbers, while goal misgeneralization can look totally clean in training and only shows up once the correlation breaks, which is way scarier for systems we can't fully test before deployment.
(5) Sam's comeback was that (4) is a nice story about hypothetical future systems but has nothing to do with our little warehouse bot, so now Nadia was the one moving the goalposts.
