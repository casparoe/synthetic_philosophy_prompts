So I've been down a Stephen Jay Gould rabbit hole and can't shake his favourite example of a spandrel: the human chin. His argument was that it's not an adaptation at all, just leftover geometry from two parts of the jaw growing at different rates during development, and he clearly enjoyed baiting anyone who tried to invent a functional story for it (better bite force, clearer speech, sexual selection, pick one). Obviously that's not the last word, people have pushed back since with real biomechanical and sexual-selection arguments of their own. Can you write me a dialogue between an adaptationist and a Gould/Lewontin-style pluralist hashing this out specifically over the chin, not the fuzzier "is adaptationism basically right" version of the fight? Stage it under the dome in St Mark's in Venice if you can work it in naturally, since that's literally where the word "spandrel" comes from. Neither one should be a pushover though - give the adaptationist genuinely good, specific reasons on offer rather than just "well something is always for something," and make sure they're actually responding to what the other just said instead of delivering prepared statements past each other.
