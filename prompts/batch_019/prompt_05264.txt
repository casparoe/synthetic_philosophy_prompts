I spent my career as a field biologist, and since retiring I've gotten pretty into reading philosophy, which has made me rethink some old stuff. Decades ago, a grad student of mine announced, completely flat-out with no hedging, that a beetle population we'd found on a remote island was a genuinely new species rather than just an odd-colored variant of a known one -- and this was based on really just one slightly-off wing pattern and a hunch. I told her she had no buisness stating that so confidently in her thesis. Years later, DNA testing that didn't even exist yet at the time proved her completely right, and now she teases me that I was wrong to push back since she turned out to be correct. But something still feels off to me about just letting the outcome settle whether her claim was okay to make in the first place. Am I wrong to think that?
