I'm a backend engineer, and about a year ago I started a small interview podcast on the side, aimed mostly at other people in tech who enjoy watching a tight argument get pulled apart. The itch started after a Slack argument at work over whether it's fair to blame whoever's on call for a bug that traces back through four years of decisions none of us made, which turned out to be roughly the shape of a philosophical debate I've since fallen into: Galen Strawson's "Basic Argument" against moral responsibility, the claim that no one can ever be truly, ultimately responsible for anything they do, because that would require being responsible for the way they are, and you can't be responsible for the way you are without having chosen it, and any such choice would itself only reflect a way you already were before you made it. My guest next month has built her career defending that argument in print, and from clips of past interviews I gather her go-to move, whenever a host presses her on the real-world stakes, is some version of "nothing changes at the checkout counter" -- meaning our everyday habits of praising, blaming, and punishing can carry on exactly as before, since they never actually needed the kind of desert her argument is denying.

Could you draft nine or ten interview questions that don't just have her re-walk the regress for the audience, but push on the places where the argument is genuinely contested? I want at least one question that makes her defend the claim -- which I gather is what's supposed to separate her from a garden-variety hard determinist -- that the argument goes through whether or not determinism is actually true, paired with a follow-up on why smuggling a bit of quantum randomness into the causal chain wouldn't rescue responsibility either, rather than just making the outcome a matter of luck. I'd like one question built around Nietzsche's jab that wanting to be your own causa sui is a kind of self-contradiction, pushed far enough to ask whether even a God who supposedly created His own nature from nothing would escape the same regress. I'd also like a quick one asking her to distinguish her sense of "ultimate responsibility" from Robert Kane's very different use of that identical phrase for his own indeterminist account, since I keep seeing the term used to mean two incompatible things. Since I only recently learned that Galen Strawson's father, P. F. Strawson, wrote one of the classic compatibilist defenses of our reactive attitudes, I'd like a question on whether the son's argument actually undermines the father's, or just answers a different question. Finally, I'd like the last two or three questions to keep circling back to her "nothing changes at the checkout counter" line, each one making it a little harder to lean on, ending with a question about what -- if anything -- she thinks could count as evidence against the Basic Argument itself. For each question, please add a short note on the reply she's most likely to give, so I can walk in with a real follow-up instead of just nodding along.
