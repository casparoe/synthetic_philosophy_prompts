In the Politics (Book I), Aristotle distinguishes natural wealth-getting, which aims at provisioning the household and has a limit set by need, from the unnatural pursuit of unlimited monetary accumulation for its own sake; within the latter he singles out usury (tokos, literally "offspring") as the most unnatural mode of acquisition, since it makes money itself "breed" more money rather than serving its proper function. That proper function, laid out in the Nicomachean Ethics (Book V), is to act as a conventional measure that makes goods commensurable for exchange — indeed money (nomisma) takes its very name from nomos, "law" or "convention," since its value rests on agreement rather than on nature.

Reasoning from that specific argument, rather than from Aristotle's general reputation as hostile to commerce, how do you think he would judge contemporary "staking" and "yield farming" in decentralized finance? There, participants lock up cryptocurrency in autonomous smart contracts and receive newly minted tokens purely as a return on the locked capital, often with no laborer, merchant, or exchange of tangible goods involved at any stage; moreover, the token supply itself is governed not by any lawgiver or civic authority but by decentralized software consensus. Would this count as an intensified, purer instance of the vice he attributed to usury, a wholly new category his scheme never anticipated, or something that fails even to qualify as nomisma in his sense to begin with? Please work through real complications for his framework — such as the computational or capital resources some of these protocols do consume — rather than settling for whichever reading makes for the easiest answer.
