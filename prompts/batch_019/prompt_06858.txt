Random one from my quals prep, but figured I'd throw it at you. I'm deep in the dispositions literature (Ryle, Goodman, Martin, Johnston, Lewis, that whole cluster) and the fink/mask distinction, which everyone treats as basic, is not sitting right with me -- my advisor's "it'll click eventually" pep talk hasnt helped. For context: the simple conditional analysis says "x is fragile" comes down to "x would break if struck." The classic finks are things like Martin's electro-fink, a dead wire rigged so that the instant something touches it, it goes live -- so "if touched, it would shock you" is true even though the wire isn't actually live beforehand -- plus the mirror-image case (a live wire that gets killed a split second before contact, which I think is Lewis's variant but don't quote me) where the disposition's present but the conditional's false. Masks/antidotes are supposedly a totally different animal: Johnston and Lewis's go-to is a poison dosed alongside an antidote, so the stuff is still, chemically, just as poisonous -- the causal basis never changes -- but the antidote derails things further down the causal chain, so "if ingested, it would kill" is false anyway.

Here's my actual hangup. Lewis patches the conditional analysis in "Finkish Dispositions" by requiring the causal basis to persist through the stimulus, and the standard line is that this move kills finks dead but does nothing against masks. I can repeat that sentence, but I can't yet explain to myself, at the level of what's actually happening causally, why persistence-of-basis is fatal to finking and totally toothless against masking -- isn't the antidote case just as much a story about some relevant intrinsic feature failing to stick around? So here's what would actually help: invent a brand-new example of a disposition ascription going wrong -- nothing exotic, a boring household object is fine, just not the electro-fink, the reverse-fink wire, or poison-and-antidote -- and use it to work out whether it's a fink, a mask, or genuinely undecidable between the two, and whether Lewis's reformed analysis gets the right verdict on it. If the distinction survives contact with a fresh case I think I'll finally beleive it's principled.
