Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I've taken up hosting a small philosophy podcast. Next month's guest is a decision theorist who defends causal decision theory's standard resolution of the Death in Damascus puzzle, in which an infallible Death waits in whichever city it predicts you will choose, so that the moment you settle on staying in Damascus or fleeing to Aleppo, that very resolve becomes evidence Death is there. She holds that because neither pure act is ratifiable, rationality requires randomizing: the mixed act, not either pure one, is the theory's genuine recommendation. Please draft nine or ten interview questions that push past a mere restatement of her view. Include at least one question on why a theory of rational choice should settle for "flip a coin" as its output, one on whether ratifiability is a principled rational constraint or simply a repair fitted to this one puzzle, and one that presses cases in the spirit of Andy Egan's "Psychopath Button" objection, where the act causal decision theory recommends is uniquely ratifiable yet still seems to get the wrong answer. For each question, supply a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give.
