I'm a tech lead at a mid-sized startup, and we recently tried to settle a technical-direction dispute democratically. Nine of us — senior engineers, the PM, and design — privately ranked three options: a full rewrite in Rust, an incremental refactor of the existing codebase, or adopting a third-party vendor SDK. Head-to-head, the rewrite beat the refactor 6-3, the refactor beat the SDK option 7-2, and the SDK option beat the rewrite 5-4. So each option loses to one and beats another, and it turns out that whichever option we happen to leave out of the first comparison ends up winning overall — meaning whoever sets the meeting agenda effectively controls the result. As a tiebreaker we also tried a simple point count (3 points for a first-choice vote, 2 for second, 1 for third), which produced a dead tie between the rewrite and the refactor, with the SDK option trailing — a result that doesn't match any of the three head-to-head orderings either. My co-founder is convinced we simply haven't found the right tallying method yet and wants me to spend a weekend building a more rigorous internal voting tool for calls like this going forward. I have a nagging feeling that no tallying method will actually fix this, that we've run into some kind of wall rather than a bug, but I can't articulate why. Is there a genuinely fair way to combine everyone's rankings here, or is my co-founder chasing something that can't exist — and if it can't, what should we actually do instead?
