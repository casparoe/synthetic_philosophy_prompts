In my seminar on Wittgenstein's On Certainty, we've been working through the split in the secondary literature between the "belief model" and the "non-belief model" of hinge commitments. Please write a dialogue between a proponent of each view. One, arguing roughly in the spirit of Crispin Wright's entitlement account, should hold that a hinge like "The earth has existed for many years before my birth" is groundless yet still a genuine, truth-apt belief that we are rationally entitled to accept without evidence. The other, arguing roughly in the spirit of Danièle Moyal-Sharrock, should insist that calling such hinges "beliefs" smuggles back exactly the possibility of justification and doubt that Wittgenstein was trying to foreclose, and that they are better understood as manifest in practice rather than held as propositions. Have them press each other on concrete objections and try to reach a verdict on whether their disagreement is substantive or merely verbal.
