I have spent enough time reading rationalist blogs and forums to think of David Lewis mainly as the philosopher behind "carving reality at its joints": the thesis that some properties are objectively more natural than others, independently of our interests, and that our theories should be stated in terms of these rather than gerrymandered alternatives. It turns out Lewis puts this same apparatus to work in his Best System Analysis of laws of nature, on which a regularity counts as a law just in case it is a theorem of whichever true deductive system best combines simplicity and strength — where simplicity can only be assessed once the competing systems are stated in a language whose predicates are restricted to perfectly natural properties, since otherwise any regularity could be dressed up as maximally simple. Given that Lewis's Humean supervenience is supposed to rule out any primitive structure to the world over and above the pattern of what is instantiated where, please argue that leaning on a further, unanalyzed ranking of properties by naturalness imports exactly the kind of brute nomic structure that Armstrong's necessitation relation between universals was invoked to supply, so that the Best System Analysis earns its advantage over necessitarian rivals only by quietly giving up on being Humean.
