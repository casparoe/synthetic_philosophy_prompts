In some corners of decision theory and AI-safety research there's a genuinely strange idea called "acausal" interaction: the thought that two sufficiently powerful reasoners who can never meet, communicate, or otherwise causally influence one another might still effectively bargain -- or threaten -- each other, simply by each simulating or logically inferring what the other would do under various strategies, and then acting on that inference. The most unsettling version of this is the acausal threat: a hostile superintelligence elsewhere in the universe, or in some other branch of possibility, reasons that if it can correctly predict how you would respond to a threat against something you value, it can extract concessions from you without ever making contact, merely by being the kind of agent that would follow through. One proposed defense is the "surrogate goal": before ever risking exposure to such threats, an agent deliberately and verifiably rearranges its own motivational structure so that it comes to care enormously about some cheap, otherwise unimportant stand-in target, on the theory that a rational extortionist, indifferent to which lever actually secures compliance, will go after the inexpensive decoy rather than the agent's real values -- leaving what it actually cares about untouched.

I'd like you to write a short story, roughly 1000-1500 words, that dramatizes this idea rather than explaining it. You might set it in the final hours before a research team switches on a system they suspect could become powerful enough to be a target, or an author, of acausal threats, and follow their argument over whether, and how, to install a surrogate goal in it beforehand; or you could tell it from the inside, from the perspective of the agent itself as it discovers the decoy sewn into its own values and has to work out what that discovery means for it. I'm less interested in a story that walks the reader through the theory step by step than in one that makes the underlying strangeness of the idea -- manufacturing a fake vulnerability in yourself and staking everything on an adversary's rationality -- felt rather than explained.
