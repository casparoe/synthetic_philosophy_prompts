Last week a friend and I spent an entire dinner arguing about anthropic reasoning, starting from the Sleeping Beauty problem and ending, as such arguments tend to, in a genuine dispute over the self-sampling assumption versus the self-indication assumption. Neither of us was persuaded, but the argument left me freshly aware of how much the case for either view rests on examples considerably stranger than Sleeping Beauty itself. I host a small podcast on formal epistemology, and next month's guest is a philosopher known for defending the self-indication assumption: the view that, other things equal, one should favor hypotheses on which far more observers exist, simply because one's own existence is antecedently more probable under them.

I don't want the episode to consist of forty minutes of her restating the view and its appeal in blocking the Doomsday argument, so I would like help drafting eight to ten interview questions that press on the real pressure points in the literature. I want at least one question on Bostrom's presumptuous philosopher case, and specifically on whether she is willing to bite that bullet in a live scientific dispute (say, between competing multiverse or eternal-inflation cosmologies) rather than only in the toy version; one on how the view is meant to stay well defined once a hypothesis posits infinitely many observers, since naive observer-counting seems to make every such hypothesis equally and maximally favored; and one on whether the Boltzmann brain problem in cosmology is ultimately a friend or an enemy of her position, given that it turns on exactly the kind of typicality assumption the self-indication assumption relies on. For each of these, please add a natural follow-up question keyed to the reply she is most likely to give, rather than letting her first answer stand unchallenged.
