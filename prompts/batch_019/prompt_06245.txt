so i've spent enough time on the EA forum and old LW threads that 'shut up and multiply, obviously you save the 5 over the 1' just feels like background common sense at this point. then i actually read Taurek's og 'Should the Numbers Count?' paper and his argument is way less dumb than the strawman people usually argue against — the point that there's no single perspective from which 5 deaths add up to something worse than 1 death, since nobody actually feels the 'sum,' so flipping a coin between the 1 guy (David) and the group of 5 might honestly be defensible.

can you compare Taurek's argument here to Parfit's direct reply to him in 'Innumerate Ethics'? Parfit's supposed to rescue numbers-counting without smuggling the aggregation back in, using something like: each of the 5 has their own separate complaint that's just as valid as David's. does that move actually work or is it just the aggregative intuition wearing an individualist costume? whose position is actually more consistent with taking the seperateness of persons seriously, and why?
