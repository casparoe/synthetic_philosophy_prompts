I'm preparing a lecture on metaethics and want to make sure I present the history accurately. In the Euthyphro, Socrates leads Euthyphro to concede that the pious cannot be identical with what is loved by the gods, since a thing's being loved by them is merely a consequence of its being pious rather than what constitutes its piety in the first place (10a–11b). Since this is exactly the logical structure later formalized as the "Euthyphro dilemma" against Divine Command Theory, it seems to me that Plato had, in effect, already refuted Divine Command Theory some twenty-four centuries before Robert Adams and other contemporary philosophers of religion tried to rehabilitate it. Given that the refutation has been sitting in the corpus since antiquity, why do philosophers of religion still treat command-based metaethics as a live research program rather than as a position Plato closed off at the outset?
