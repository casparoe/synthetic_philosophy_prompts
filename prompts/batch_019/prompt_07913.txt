I sit on my company's incident-review board, where the same disagreement keeps recurring whenever we write up a major outage. Every postmortem turns up a triggering bug, but it also turns up omissions further upstream -- no alert existed for that failure mode, or the on-call engineer didn't escalate after the first page. Half the board wants these listed as root causes in their own right, so that fixing them becomes a tracked action item; the other half calls this a category error, since absences can't do causal work, and treating every missed safeguard as a cause would make nearly everyone who ever touched the system a cause of every outage that follows. Our blameless-postmortem commitment is also on the line, since naming one engineer's omission as 'the' cause reads as blame no matter how we phrase it. I need a short memo before we lock next quarter's postmortem template: settle how it should treat omissions, give the board a rule it can apply consistently, and offer a fair account of the strongest objection to whichever rule you choose.
