Ok, this is a dumb story but its been bugging me all week. A coworker showed me one of those penny-auction apps during lunch as a joke — I think it was called DealDash or something like that, one of those sites where every single bid costs you a small non-refundable fee (something like 60 cents) no matter what happens, each bid only bumps the actual price up by a cent, and whoever's bid is standing when the clock hits zero gets the item at that tiny price. I told myself I'd just watch, then told myself I'd do one bid for fun, and somehow ended up in like an hour-long back and forth with one other username over a $130 espresso machine. At literally every point where it was my turn again, backing out meant for sure eating the like $60-70 in fees I'd already burned for nothing, while one more bid still had a shot at winning the machine and making all of it "worth it" — so obviously I kept bidding. I walked away about $85 deep in fees alone, and the other person won the machine anyway, so I got literally nothing out of it.

Everyone I've told this story to just goes "lol that's the sunk cost fallacy, you idiot," and sure, fine, that's obviously part of it, but it doesn't feel like the whole story to me. It's not like I was clinging to money out of some vague emotional attachment — at each individual point, continuing genuinely felt like the more sensible move given that the other bidder clearly wasn't backing down either, so quitting right then always felt worse than quitting one round earlier would've. Something about having an actual other person on the other end, presumably going through the exact same reasoning I was, made this feel different from a normal "I overspent because of sunk costs" story, but I can't quite put my finger on what. Which bugs me more than it should, honestly, partly because I've got a slow-motion version of the same standoff going on right now with a neighbor over who pays for a shared driveway repair, and I'd rather not blow that one the same way too. So: is my read on this even fair, or am I just rationalizing being bad at a dumb app? And is there an actual smarter way to handle situations shaped like this once you're already a few rounds in, or is "never take the first step" basically the whole lesson?
