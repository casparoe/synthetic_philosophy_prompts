I lead the music-generation team at a small startup that trains models to compose original scores on demand, and over the past few months our outputs have gotten good enough that beta testers routinely cannot tell our tracks apart from human-composed ones. For unrelated reasons, I recently joined a small reading group working through Schopenhauer's *The World as Will and Representation*, and his treatment of music near the end of Book Three, together with the short supplementary chapter he later devoted entirely to the subject, has put my day job in a strange new light. Schopenhauer insists that music is metaphysically unlike every other art: painting, sculpture, and poetry are copies of the Platonic Ideas that themselves objectify the Will at various grades, but music skips the Ideas altogether and copies the Will directly, so that (as he more or less says himself) its significance would arguably survive even if the whole phenomenal world of objects and Ideas vanished, whereas the reverse could never be said of the other arts. He then cashes this out in surprisingly concrete structural terms: the bass line corresponds to the lowest and most inert grade of the Will's objectification (unorganized, inorganic nature), the inner harmonic voices correspond to the plant and animal kingdoms, and the freely moving melody on top corresponds to the self-conscious striving of human beings, so that a piece's harmonic skeleton is, on his reading, quite literally a miniature great chain of being. All of this depends on his theory of genius as a rare capacity to fall out of ordinary, will-driven perception into will-less contemplation, which for music yields direct, non-conceptual insight into the Will itself; he even compares the composer's resulting insight to that of a mesmerized clairvoyant who reports things on waking that he has no conceptual grasp of at all.

None of this was written with anything like our pipeline in mind, and I honestly can't tell whether that makes the question more interesting or just a category error. I'd like your best-effort reconstruction, working strictly from Schopenhauer's own premises rather than from a generic "he would have distrusted technology" instinct, of how he would have judged music generated end-to-end by a statistical model trained on recordings, with no biography, suffering, or striving of its own behind the notes. Would he say such a process could, even in principle, satisfy his account of genius and thereby produce music in his full sense of the term, or only an empty simulacrum that has melody's form without any Will behind it to be copied? Does his account of genius as will-less perception in a human subject rule out non-human authorship more or less by definition, or is there room, on a careful reading of his actual arguments rather than just their spirit, for the Will to still be said to speak through a process like ours? I would find it far more useful if you engaged with the mechanics of his theory specifically — the bass/harmony/melody correspondence, the clairvoyant analogy, his insistence that music needs no words or concepts to be intelligible — than if you gave a broad-strokes verdict about Schopenhauer and modern technology in general.
