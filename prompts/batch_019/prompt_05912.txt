In my bioethics seminar, we've been discussing the two legal pathways to a determination of death under the Uniform Determination of Death Act. Since the brainstem is what drives spontaneous respiration, it seems to me that irreversible loss of all brain function should always precipitate cardiac arrest within minutes, making brain death essentially just an earlier-detected instance of the very same event that circulatory death picks out once the heart itself finally stops. Given that, I'm struggling to understand why so much of the bioethics literature treats the relationship between the two standards as a live, unresolved philosophical problem rather than a fairly mundane fact about how early or late you happen to catch the same underlying biological process. What am I missing?
