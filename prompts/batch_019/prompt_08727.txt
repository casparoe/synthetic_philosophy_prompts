My dad's retiring next month after 34 years as chair of our county's party committee, and I got roped into giving the toast at his dinner. Here's my problem: I just read Simone Weil's essay on abolishing political parties for a night class and I cant unread it. She wrote it in the last year of her life, 1943, basically as a memo on what French democracy should look like after the war, and her whole argument is that a party's real structural job is to manufacture collective opinion in its members and press everyone toward conformity, with keeping the party growing as basically the only goal that survives contact with reality — truth and justice be damned. I don't think my dad's a bad guy, honestly one of the most decent people I know, but I can't get up there and toast the committee itself like it was some noble truth-seeking project for three decades, not with a straight face. Half the room's gonna be party lifers who want the usual rah-rah toast, the other half is just aunts and cousins who dont care about any of this and will tune out the second it turns into a lecture. Can you write me something warm and honest that doesn't pretend the Weil problem away, but also doesn't turn into a philosophy seminar or an insult to a guy who gave this thing 34 years of his life?
