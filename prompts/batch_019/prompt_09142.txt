Been down a bit of a Newton rabbit hole lately — not the gravity/calculus stuff everyone learns in school, but the theology he kept hidden his whole life. The guy was secretly anti-trinitarian, sank decades into Biblical chronology and decoding prophecy (Daniel, Revelation, dating the apocalypse, all of it), and left behind way more alchemy notes than physics notes. He kept nearly all of it quiet becuase openly denying the Trinity could've ended his career (or worse) in 17th century England.

What I can't figure out is how seriously to take this stuff relative to the "real" science. Others treat the theology and alchemy as a walled-off private obsession — interesting biographically, sure, but not something that actually shaped the physics in the Principia and Opticks.

Write me an essay that picks a side here instead of dodging it. Go through the strongest evidence and arguments on both ends, then commit to an actual verdict — "integrated in his head but separable in the physics" counts as a real answer, "who knows, it's complicated" doesn't.
