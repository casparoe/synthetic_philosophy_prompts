A friend of mine, Theo, has worn the same pair of argyle socks to every job interview he's had over the past three years, and he's gotten an offer all five times. By his own admission he was also simply more confident than usual on each of those occasions, so nothing in the actual record distinguishes "the socks did it" from "the confidence did it" — both explanations fit equally well. He's rattled about a final-round interview next week because the socks have gone missing in the laundry. His roommate, Naomi, told him this was silly: five data points prove nothing, and there's no mechanism linking socks to hiring decisions. Theo shot back that Naomi believes plenty of things, such as that the sun will rise tomorrow, on nothing stronger than "it's happened every time so far" — so if that counts as a reason, his socks should too. Naomi said the sunrise case is different because it's backed by physics and fits into a huge web of other confirmed regularities, whereas "socks cause job offers" is a coincidence standing entirely on its own. Theo replied that calling one idea "well-connected" and the other "isolated" just describes which beliefs Naomi already happens to hold central: if their social circle had a going folklore about lucky objects the way it has one about confidence and body language, the sock story would sound just as natural and connected. Naomi answered that this couldn't be right, since it would make any pattern as credible as any other, and some regularities really do have to count as more trustworthy than others or prediction becomes impossible. Theo said fine, granted — but she still hadn't said what actually makes "confidence causes success" the trustworthy story and "socks cause success" the untrustworthy one, beyond simply insisting on it.

Could you go through their exchange move by move — Naomi's opening dismissal, Theo's sunrise comparison, Naomi's "web of regularities" reply, Theo's point about naturalness being relative to what you already believe, Naomi's "prediction becomes impossible" worry, and Theo's closing challenge — and say who has the better of it at each step? Then say what, if anything, both of them are still missing.
