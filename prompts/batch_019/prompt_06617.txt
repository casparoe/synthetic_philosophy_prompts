I'm a second-year philosophy PhD student, and this came up while I was preparing notes for a reading group on radical skepticism. I think I've finally worked out what "hinge epistemology" adds to Wittgenstein's On Certainty, and it seems to me that it's really just foundationalism wearing a new costume. Wittgenstein's hinges — propositions like "I have two hands" or "the earth has existed for a long time," exempt from doubt and held fast so that our ordinary practices of giving and demanding reasons can get going at all (this is the point of the hinge-and-door passage, §§341–343) — look to me exactly like Moorean or Cartesian basic beliefs: an unjustified, terminal bedrock layer that stops the regress of justification and licenses everything built on top of it. I know Wittgenstein also says the boundary between hinge and ordinary empirical hypothesis can shift over historical time (the river-bed passages, §§96–99), but that just sounds like saying the foundations occasionally get swapped out, not that there's no foundational structure in the first place.

Given that, I'm trying to work out how hinge theorists such as Pritchard think they've avoided the objections that sank classical foundationalism. If hinges are just relabeled basic beliefs, then the regress of justification hasn't actually been stopped in any principled way — it's been stopped by fiat, at whichever propositions we happen to find psychologically indubitable, which is exactly the arbitrariness charge BonJour pressed against foundationalists (why should stopping there count as a solution rather than simply refusing to keep asking the question?). And doesn't the Sellarsian worry apply too: that something which isn't itself justified by anything — a bare "given" — can't coherently be doing justificatory work for other beliefs, so that calling hinges a "terminus" just helps itself to the very thing that needed explaining?

Could you help me see whether hinge epistemology actually has a good answer to this, or whether talk of hinges just repackages foundationalism's old problems in more fashionable Wittgensteinian language? I'd like to understand this precisely enough to address it directly in my notes, rather than hoping nobody in the reading group raises it.
