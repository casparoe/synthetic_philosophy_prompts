Write a dialogue between a defender of Nozick's entitlement theory and a defender of patterned or end-state principles of distributive justice, set in the aftermath of the Wilt Chamberlain scenario from Anarchy, State, and Utopia: Chamberlain has just finished a season in which, under his contract, twenty-five cents from the price of every ticket to his home games went directly to him, a million fans gladly paid to watch him play, and he ended up far richer than anyone else in what had, by hypothesis, been a perfectly just distribution. The entitlement theorist argues that reversing this outcome would be tantamount to having banned the fans' payments in the first place; the patterned theorist insists that taxing Chamberlain's resulting income afterward is a genuinely different, and more defensible, kind of interference than prohibiting the transfers themselves. Have the two press each other on whether that distinction between taxing an outcome and banning the transaction that produced it can really bear the weight the patterned theorist puts on it, with neither side settling for the other's weakest argument.
