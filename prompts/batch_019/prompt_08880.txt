Write a dialogue between two expressivists in metaethics who both agree that moral claims express attitudes rather than describe facts, but who disagree about how to actually solve the Frege-Geach problem. One character thinks a purely expressivist fix works -- something in the spirit of Blackburn's logic of attitudes or Gibbard's hyperplans, where validity gets explained via consistency among attitudes/plans instead of truth-preservation. The other thinks that project quietly fails and that you need a hybrid view (like Ridge's "ecumenical expressivism") where moral sentences get genuine truth-conditional content plus an attitude riding along, so ordinary logic just applies to them. Make them actually argue about a concrete case -- like why "lying is wrong, and if lying is wrong then getting your kid brother to lie is wrong too, so getting your kid brother to lie is wrong" comes out valid -- instead of staying at the level of slogans, and have the pure expressivist accuse the hybrid theorist of basically smuggling in cognitivism to dodge the hard part of the problem. Keep it a real back-and-forth, not two monologues, and dont let either side win too easily.
