I've been working through classical Chinese philosophy on my own, and the butterfly dream passage in the Zhuangzi -- where he wakes from dreaming he was a butterfly and can no longer tell whether he is a man who dreamed of being a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming he is a man -- seems to me like the same move Descartes makes almost two thousand years later in the First Meditation, using the impossibility of distinguishing waking from dreaming to show that the senses cannot be trusted as a source of certain knowledge. Given that Zhuangzi got there first, I'm curious why that insight never pushed early Chinese philosophy toward the same tradition of skeptical, certainty-seeking epistemology that took over European philosophy after Descartes.
