From what I've pieced together on my own, Bell's theorem shows that we have to give up either locality or "realism" (the assumption that a particle has definite properties before it is measured), and since the theorem treats these two assumptions symmetrically, choosing which one to sacrifice is really just a matter of taste rather than something the physics itself decides. What puzzles me, then, is why whole research programs, from quantum foundations to device-independent cryptography, are organized around the term "nonlocality," as though the experiments had specifically singled out locality as the assumption that fails. Shouldn't careful researchers remain agnostic about which of the two assumptions is actually false, rather than treating nonlocality as the default conclusion?
