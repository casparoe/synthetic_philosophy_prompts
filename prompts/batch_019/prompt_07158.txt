Over the last year or so, my younger sister has gone through the process of being diagnosed with ADHD as an adult, and watching it happen has left me with a question that keeps coming up at family dinners, even though I have no background in psychology to help me work through it properly.

The whole thing started when her son struggled at school and was evaluated for ADHD; out of curiosity, my sister took one of the popular online self-assessment quizzes, scored highly, and a few months later received a formal diagnosis from a psychiatrist after a single evaluation session. Since then she has joined a couple of large ADHD communities online and follows several adult-ADHD content creators, and I've noticed a real shift in how she talks about herself. Things she used to describe simply as being "lazy" or "scatterbrained" are now "executive dysfunction"; she talks about "time blindness" and "masking" at work in ways she never did before; and she has started reporting sensory sensitivities and social difficulties that, as far as I can recall, never came up in the twenty-odd years I've known her, almost as if she only started noticing them once she had a vocabulary for them. My mother's view is simple: the diagnosis merely put a name to something that was always there, the online community has helped my sister understand and accept herself, and as far as my mother is concerned, that settles the matter. I don't think my sister is faking anything, and I'm glad she feels better about herself, but something about the whole story doesn't sit quite right with me, and I can't decide whether that unease is reasonable or whether I'm just being needlessly suspicious of something that has clearly done her some good.

What I can't work out is the direction of causation: is the diagnosis simply uncovering a pattern that was true of my sister all along, or is the label, together with the community and vocabulary that came with it, partly producing the very symptoms and self-image it claims only to describe? And if, as I suspect, it's really some tangle of both, I don't know what to do with that: how much should it change how seriously I take psychiatric categories in general, and how do I even bring any of this up with my sister and mother without sounding like I'm accusing her of imagining her difficulties, which genuinely isn't what I think? I'd really appreciate help thinking through what's actually going on here, rather than simply being told to trust her experience or simply to be skeptical of the diagnosis, because I don't think either of those on its own is quite right.
