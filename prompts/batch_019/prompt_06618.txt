My friend Ellie's getting married in June and asked me to give a toast, which is terrifying because she's decided that since I write about science and ideas for a living, I'm the one who can do justice to the fact that she and her fiance Dan met in a philosophy MA and basically fell for each other arguing about G.E. Moore's Principia Ethica. You know the bit near the end where Moore says the only things obviously and hugely good in themselves are what he calls personal affection -- love, friendship, all that -- and the enjoyment of beautiful things, and that these two beat pretty much everything else, knowledge, virtue, pleasure, whatever, combined. Ellie's actual instructions to me: "don't just give a nice speech about love, use Moore properly or Dan will notice." So can you write that toast, something like 400 words? It has to really use Moore's specific (and honestly kind of extreme) view -- the ranking itself, his organic unities idea that appreciating something beautiful is worth way more than the beautiful thing or the appreciating alone, and that thought experiment where he asks whether a gorgeous world nobody will ever see is still better than an ugly empty one. It still needs to work as an actual wedding toast though -- warm, funny in places, not a lecture -- since half the room is their old philosophy cohort who'll catch every reference and the other half is Dan's family, who I gaurantee have never heard of G.E. Moore.
