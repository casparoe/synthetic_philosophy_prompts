Compare David Lewis's Principal Principle with Bas van Fraassen's Reflection Principle. Both instruct you to defer your present credence to another privileged quantity that plays the role of an "expert" -- objective chance in Lewis's case, your own anticipated future credence in van Fraassen's -- and, in their unrestricted forms, both run into well-known counterexamples: Lewis's principle runs into chances that can be "undermined" by their own possible future outcomes, while van Fraassen's runs into cases where you can rationally anticipate becoming irrational -- through manipulation, forgetting, or the like -- before the later time in question. Explain, for each principle, exactly what feature of the problematic evidence causes the trouble, and how the standard fix is supposed to work. Then say whether the two fixes amount, at bottom, to the same philosophical move, or whether the parallel breaks down under closer scrutiny.
