Me and my friend Priya got into it for like two hours last night about Davidson's radical interpretation stuff and neither of us really backed down. Her whole point was that the "principle of charity" is basically cheating: you can't figure out what someone means by assuming they mostly believe true things and reason the way we do, because "true" and "reasonable" are supposedly the very things you're trying to work out from scratch when you interpret someone. I kept saying no, that's the actual insight — meaning and belief aren't seperate things you can pin down one at a time, they get fixed together in one move, like solving two equations at once, and charity is just the constraint that makes the system solvable at all, not an answer smuggled in early. We also argued about that later essay of his with the great title, "A Nice Derangement of Epitaphs," where he goes even further and says two people talking are really just improvising, converging their "prior theory" and "passing theory" on the fly, and that there isn't really a shared, fixed "language" sitting underneath ordinary conversation the way we assume there is — which Priya took as him basically admitting the whole radical interpretation project never actually ends, even between people who've known each other for decades.

Anyway, rather than keep going in circles about it, I want to see the idea worked through as fiction instead of argued about in the abstract. Write me a short story about a man whose wife wakes up from a long illness (pick whatever's medically plausible — a coma, severe amnesia, whatever) with no memory of him, their marriage, or any of the private references and shorthand they built up over the years, even though she can still speak and understand ordinary sentences just fine. Follow him across several conversations with her in the days after, as he has to work out what she means and what she believes at the same time, with no shared history to fall back on and no way to just look up "what she meant by that." Don't have anyone in the story namedrop Davidson or explain the theory out loud — I want the actual mechanics of interpreting-under-uncertainty to be doing the work of the plot itself, not sitting in the background as flavor. And please don't wrap it up neatly at the end: I'd like it left genuinely open whether he ends the story understanding her again or has just gotten very good at predicting her, since that's exactly the point Priya and I couldn't settle either.
