My dad's turning 80 next month and I somehow got voted into giving the toast at the dinner - it's kind of a combined birthday/retirement thing since he finally sold the hardware store last year after running it something like 45 years, even though he still shows up twice a week to "make sure they're not screwing it up." A while back I picked up a beat-up copy of Cicero's "On Old Age" at a used bookstore, honestly just because the title made me laugh, and ended up reading the thing three times, which is not normally what I do with 2000 year old books. It's set up as this old guy Cato talking to two younger guys, basically talking them out of dreading getting old, and I got kind of into it.

What I remember is that Cato lays out four things people usually complain about with old age - can't stay active, body's falling apart, can't enjoy stuff anymore, and you're just closer to dying - and actually answers each one instead of just saying "cheer up." There's a bit where some old blind guy gets carried into the senate to argue against making peace with Pyrrhus because people still wanted his opinion, theres a part about an old farmer planting trees he knows he'll never live to see grown, and then a whole ending section on why death specifically isn't something to be scared of that's an actual argument (something like: either you stop existing completely, in which case it can't hurt you, or you go somewhere better, so either way there's nothing to dread) not just "have faith and it'll be fine."

Here's my problem though. My dad cannot stand it when people do the "80 is the new 60" bit, he thinks it's a nice-sounding lie people tell so they don't have to actually think about aging, and hell call it out mid speech if I try it. But Cicero was writing this about, and basically for, a rich and powerful Roman senator who owned farms and slaves and was still totally plugged into politics at eighty, which is a pretty different life than a guy who did payroll and fixed lawnmowers for four decades - so I don't want to just lift Cato's lines like they map onto my dad perfectly. On top of that the room is gonna be like 30 people, half his old army buddies and half my cousins who'll be on their phones by minute two. Could you write me the toast, maybe 4-5 minutes out loud, that actually uses Cato's real structure and a couple of his specific examples, is honest about where they don't quite fit my dad's actual life, and still lands as something true rather then just nice?
