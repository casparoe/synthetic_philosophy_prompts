I'm a PhD student working in metaphysics and philosophy of language, and a puzzle about Bradley's regress has been nagging at me since I taught it in a graduate seminar on truthmakers and the metaphysics of propositions a couple of weeks ago. The regress starts innocently enough: if the fact that a is F, or equivalently the proposition that a is F, is supposed to be something more than the mere list or aggregate {a, F} — which is exactly the "unity of the proposition" problem Russell posed when he asked what distinguishes a genuine proposition from a bare collection of its constituents — then a and F must be tied together by some relation of instantiation or exemplification. But then it looks as though that relation itself needs a further relation to tie it to a and F, and that further relation needs another, and so on without end, which is what led Bradley to conclude that relations, and any view of reality as a plurality of externally related items, are ultimately incoherent.

A colleague in my reading group and I disagree about the two responses that keep getting offered as if they were genuinely distinct solutions. Frege blocks the regress by insisting that concepts are inherently "unsaturated": a concept just is the sort of entity that combines with a saturated object without any further relational glue, so there is no gap left for a regress to open up in. Armstrong blocks it by insisting that states of affairs are ontologically prior to their constituents, composed by a sui generis, non-mereological mode of composition rather than assembled after the fact out of independently existing particulars and universals. My colleague thinks these are two different solutions drawing on different resources; I think they are the same move wearing different jargon, since both simply invent a technical label for "the kind of thing that doesn't need unifying" and then declare the regress solved once the label has been introduced, without ever explaining why that labeled item achieves unity where an ordinary relation supposedly cannot.

Please argue for my side of this as rigorously as you can: make the strongest possible case that Fregean unsaturatedness and Armstrongian non-mereological composition are structurally the same unexplanatory maneuver, and that genuinely stopping Bradley's regress requires more than stipulating a category of entity that is, by definition, immune to it. I'd like you to engage with the strongest versions of both views rather than caricaturing them, since I need to know whether this objection actually survives contact with the literature before I commit to it in writing.
