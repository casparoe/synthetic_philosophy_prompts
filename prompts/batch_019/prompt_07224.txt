A friend and I had a long, unresolved argument about how to read my sister's career choices. She has stayed in the same mid-level job for fifteen years despite being clearly capable of more, and has never applied for a promotion even when her manager has explicitly encouraged her to. I've always taken this as fairly conclusive evidence that she doesn't actually want the added responsibility or income: if she wanted it, she would go after it. My friend argued that this inference is much weaker than it looks, since the very same pattern of behavior is just as consistent with her wanting the promotion but doubting she would get it, or believing that a visible attempt and failure would cost her more than a quiet non-attempt ever could. Neither of us could explain why one reading should be preferred over the other, or whether her actions by themselves are even the kind of evidence that could settle it. Who has the better argument here, and can watching what someone does, on its own, ever really tell you what they want?
