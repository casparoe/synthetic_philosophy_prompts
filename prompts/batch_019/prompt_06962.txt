I sit on my hospital's Root Cause Analysis Committee as part of a bioethics PhD practicum, and the group is stuck on a sentinel-event review. A post-surgical patient died of septic shock after three nursing checks across two shifts, each of which recorded vitals meeting our sepsis-escalation trigger, yet none of the three nurses on duty escalated; a rapid response was finally called only once the patient had already decompensated. The unit was also short-staffed that night because of a last-minute callout. Our Just Culture policy requires the committee to agree on which actions were genuine causes of the death before it can even ask whether any individual should be held accountable for it. Some members want to name all three nurses as causes, since escalation at any single check would probably have saved the patient; others point out that the same reasoning would indict the attending who never reviewed the chart that day, or the visiting relative who noticed at dawn that the patient "didn't look right" and said nothing to staff. Please draft the causal-responsibility section of our report: recommend which of the omissions, if any, should be named as causes of death, state a principled rule for where you draw that line, and give a fair hearing to the strongest reason for drawing it differently.
