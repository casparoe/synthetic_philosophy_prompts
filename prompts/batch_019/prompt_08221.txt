Q: IIT holds that a system is conscious to the degree that it has integrated information, Φ, and that the quality of its experience corresponds to the shape of its "maximally irreducible conceptual structure."
(a) What job does the exclusion postulate do in IIT, and why isn't the integration postulate enough on its own?
(b) Explain why IIT implies that a purely feedforward network has Φ = 0, no matter how complicated the function it computes.
(c) Critics have pointed out that certain small, densely-interconnected systems (e.g. grids of simple logic gates) can end up with enormous Φ despite doing something computationally trivial. Is this the same worry as in (b), or a different one? Explain.

Here's what the student wrote:

(a) The exclusion postulate says that out of all the possible ways of carving up a system into subsets, only the one subset with the maximum Φ actually counts as a conscious "complex" — everything else is excluded. It's needed because without it, every larger system containing a conscious complex (like your whole brain, or you-plus-the-chair-you're-sitting-in) would also come out conscious, giving an infinite pile-up of overlapping consciousnesses at every scale. Exclusion is what picks out one definite complex as the actual locus of experience.

(b) Because a feedforward network only sends information one way, you can always find a partition — basically cutting between two layers — that doesn't destroy any information the system carries about its own past or future states. Cutting a real feedback connection would lose something, but there are no feedback connections to cut, so the "minimum information partition" always ends up being a partition that loses nothing. Since Φ measures the loss caused by the best partition, that means Φ is zero.

(c) This is really the same problem as (b), just showing up from the other direction: both cases come down to the fact that Φ only cares about a system's wiring, not what it's actually computing. The logic-gate grid is basically a large feedforward circuit, so it ends up with a huge Φ for the same structural reason a feedforward net gets zero — IIT is scoring the wiring diagram, not the computation, and once the network is big enough that same feedforward loophole flips from giving Φ = 0 to giving Φ close to its theoretical max.
