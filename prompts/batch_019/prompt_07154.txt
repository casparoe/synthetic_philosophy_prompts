I spent about thirty-five years as a comparative anatomist before I hung up my lab coat, and now that I'm retired I've been filling the time reading philosophy of biology instead of doing sudoku. Lately I've gotten stuck on the old etiological vs. causal-role fight over what "function" actually means, and it's bugging me more then a puzzle has any right to. The etiological crowd (Wright originally, then Neander and Millikan patching things up with "selected effects" so malfunction makes sense) say a traits function is whatever effect explains why it was selected for and stuck around in the population. The causal-role crowd, following Cummins, just say the function is whatever a part contributes to some capacity of the containing system you happen to be analyzing right now, no history required at all.

What's really got me chewing on this is a case from my own old field: the malleus and incus, the two tiny bones in the mammalian middle ear, are homologous to bones that used to form the jaw joint in the reptile-like ancestors of mammals. So for a couple hundred million years those bones had one job (helping the jaw articulate) and then, after mammals split off, that very same lineage of bones got recruited for a completely different job (transmitting sound to the inner ear). I mentioned this to my nephew, who's doing a philosophy PhD, and even he admitted it's a nasty case for both camps, though naturally he thinks his side (etiological, if you're wondering) comes out ahead in the end.

Could you write me a dialogue between a committed etiological theorist and a committed causal-role theorist who sit down to hash out the ear-bone case together? I don't want a polite exchange of position statements, I want real back and forth where each one lands genuine blows on the other: the causal-role theorist should have to answer for the malfunction problem and for how promiscuous their notion of function gets once you're free to pick any capacity of any containing system to analyze, and the etiological theorist should have to answer for exactly how far back in a lineage you're allowed to reach for "the" selection episode that fixes a trait's function, especially once that function has quietly changed underneath an unchanged bit of anatomy. Neither one should fold or get a strawman treatment, and they don't need to land on a tidy resolution at the end - I mainly just want to see where the real pressure points are.
