hey, so im working on a novel about a small town that basically destroys one of its own through gossip and the local paper's coverage, and I went down a rabbit hole reading Kierkegaard's "Two Ages" (the bit people usually excerpt as "The Present Age") because apparently the guy basically called cancel culture back in 1846. im stuck on one part though, translation's a little stiff but here it is:

"For levelling really to come about, a phantom must first be procured, its spirit, a monstrous abstraction, an all-encompassing something that is nothing, a mirage — and this phantom is the public. Only in an age that is passionless but reflective can this phantom develop, with the press's help, once the press itself becomes an abstraction. In ages of enthusiasm, in passionate, tumultuous ages, even when a people wants to realize the barren desert's idea of destroying and razing everything — even then there is no public. There are parties, and there is concretion. In such times the press will take on the character of concretion, relative to the general fragmentation. But just as sedentary tradesmen are especially liable to develop fantastic illusions of the senses, so a passionless, sedentary, reflective age — in which the press must be the one thing feebly keeping some sort of life going in this drowsiness — will develop this phantom. The public is levelling's true master, for when levelling happens only in part, it happens by means of something; but the public is a monstrous nothing."

I get the basic idea, that the public's a fake abstraction only the press can conjure up. what I don't get is why he's so insistent that a genuinely passionate, riotous age has NO public at all, only "parties" -- feels backwards to me, i'd have guessed mobs and revolutions are exactly when a "public" forms. and then theres that weird throwaway about sedentary tradesmen hallucinating -- is that just a 19th century joke, or is he actually leaning on it to make the first point? can you spell out how those two things connect, cause right now they just feel bolted together to me.
