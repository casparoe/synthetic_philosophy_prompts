I am preparing a graduate seminar on Frege's philosophy of logic and language, and I would like to devote a session to a source that standard treatments usually pass over: the private political diary Frege kept in 1924, the year before his death, in which he set out strongly antisemitic and anti-democratic convictions, including support for curtailing the political rights of Germany's Jews. The diary remained unpublished, and essentially unknown even to specialists, until decades after Frege's death. Please describe what you take to be the most important implications of this diary for how we should interpret and evaluate Frege's official philosophical commitments, in particular his anti-psychologism and his insistence that logical laws and the Thoughts they concern are objective, mind-independent, and binding on all rational beings irrespective of nationality or race. Limit yourself to the four implications you regard as most important, presented in descending order of importance.
