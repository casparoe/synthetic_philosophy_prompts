I am at work on a historical novel set in the mid-1950s and need help with a scene I have not managed to write to my satisfaction: an invented private conversation between John von Neumann and Norbert Wiener -- real, if not especially close, acquaintances -- shortly after von Neumann's 1955 Fortune essay "Can We Survive Technology?" begins circulating among their colleagues. Please write this dialogue so that von Neumann argues his actual position: that no prohibition or one-time safeguard against a given technology can be enforced across a divided world, and that survival depends on the patience and political skill to keep making adequate decisions as crises recur, not on any final, once-and-for-all fix. Wiener should press the objection latent in his own postwar writing -- most concretely, his refusal, on ethical grounds, to send a researcher his wartime work on guided-missile control systems -- that this realism is really a way of letting individual scientists off the hook for what their work makes possible, and that certain collaborations should be refused on principle before any question of "adapting" to their consequences ever arises. Each man should be pressed by the other's strongest point to concede something he would not have volunteered on his own, and the scene should end with the disagreement still standing.
