So I went to this effective altruism meetup last month (background research for a piece on the longtermism crowd) and ended up listening to two people go at it in the pub afterward over something called "cluelessness" -- apparently an actual term philosophers use, not just a vibe. Roughly how it went, near as I can reconstruct:

1. Nadia said the whole cause-prioritization project is kind of a joke, because we're clueless about the real consequences of pretty much anything -- donate to a malaria charity and you've changed who gets born, which changes everything downstream in ways nobody can trace, so ranking charities by "expected impact" is really just ranking our guesses.

2. Owen said that cuts both ways: if we truly have no idea whether those untraceable downstream effects skew good or bad, they should cancel out in expectation, so we're licensed to just act on what we CAN foresee (kids not dying of malaria) and set the untraceable stuff aside, since worrying about it is a wash either way.

3. Nadia said thats fine for genuinely random stuff, but some downstream effects aren't symmetric at all -- saving lives predictably means more people, which predictably means more emissions and more factory farming, so there's a directional consideration you can't just average away, and no principled exchange rate between that and the lives saved.

4. Owen said fine, that's a real problem, but it's a problem for precision, not for acting at all -- declining to donate has those same untraceable systematic effects too, so cluelessness cant be a reason to prefer inaction, and Nadia's move basically collapses into "be humble about the rankings," not "the rankings are baseless."

I went back and forth all night and honestly couldn't settle it. Can you referee round by round instead of just picking an overall winner? For each numbered move, say who came out ahead -- Nadia, Owen, both, or neither -- as a JSON object like {"1": "Nadia", "2": "Owen", "3": "both", "4": "neither"} (those values are just placeholders), then give a sentence or two justifying each call, and close with one line on whether "cluelessness" actually threatens EA-style cause prioritization or is more of a speed bump.
