For the final essay in my graduate seminar on Frege, I want a question that resists the easy "separate the art from the artist" move, and that doesn't just reduce to a rehearsal of the Heidegger-and-Nazism debate with the names changed. In 1924, the last full year of his life, Frege kept a diary -- unpublished until decades after his death -- recording vehemently antisemitic views and hostility to the Weimar Republic's parliamentary democracy, material that unsettled even Michael Dummett, the philosopher most responsible for Frege's centrality in twentieth-century analytic philosophy. Frege's own logical theory rests on a strenuous anti-psychologism: the laws of logic, and the thoughts they concern, are objective denizens of what he called a "third realm," binding on every rational being as such and indifferent to any thinker's psychology, culture, or nation. Write an essay on whether the diary bears on the philosophical assessment of Frege's work in logic and philosophy of language. Be explicit about which sense of "bears on" is at stake -- the validity of his formal results, which plainly do not depend on his character, or something broader about how we should read the ambitions of his philosophical project -- and address directly whether the universalism built into his anti-psychologism is in tension with, merely orthogonal to, or quietly complicit with the particularism of the diary. Weigh the strongest considerations on each side, but close with a definite verdict rather than a balanced survey.
