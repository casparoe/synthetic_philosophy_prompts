For my modal metaphysics seminar we've been going through the clash between Lewis's counterpart theory and the more Kripke/Plantinga-style transworld identity view, and this week's readings had the bit from Naming and Necessity where Kripke raises his "Humphrey objection" - basically that when we say Humphrey could have won the election, we mean HIM, not some other guy in a different world who merely resembles him and happens to win instead, and the real Humphrey couldnt care less about that other guy's electoral fortunes. We also went through the chunk of On the Plurality of Worlds where Lewis bites the bullet on this and ties it back to his accidental-intrinsics style argument, i.e. that treating you as literally, numerically the same individual across worlds with incompatible properties (different shapes, different number of fingers, whatever) is almost as weird as one thing being bent and straight at once, which is supposed to be a point in favor of counterparts over strict identity.

I get both views fine in isolation, but I'm having trouble seeing how an actual back and forth between them would go past both sides just restating their opening move. Like, does the transworld identity theorist running the Plantinga-style individual essences/haecceities line have anything better to say once Lewis just says "that's what de re modal claims mean, deal with it" - or does the counterpart theorist have a real comeback when they get pushed on the fact that the counterpart relation isn't even required to be transitive or symmetric (you can have multiple equally-good counterparts, or none at all), so calling it "identity preserving" in any deep sense seems off? Could you write me a dialogue between a committed counterpart theorist and a committed transworld identity theorist that really digs into this instead of just talking past each other - I want both sides to be genuinely strong, actually respond to the specific objections the other one raises (including the Humphrey objection and the accidental intrinsics point above), and ideally not resolve too neatly, since i dont think this one actually does.
