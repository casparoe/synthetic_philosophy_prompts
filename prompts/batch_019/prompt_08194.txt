Apparently his big move in there is to argue that money was never wealth to begin with, just a kind of ticket or counter we use to keep track of who's owed what, and that the real wealth is the industry/labor the ticket lets us mobilize — so on his view even a gold coin is basically just a token, valuable because people trust it and keep it circulating, not because of the metal in it. This came up partly because Ireland at the time apparently had a chronic shortage of actual coin, and Berkeley was arguing (I think partly for a national bank on credit rather than specie) that this didn't matter as much as everyone assumed, since it was industry and circulation doing the real work, not the metal.
