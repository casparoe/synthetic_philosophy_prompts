I've spent the last few weeks of a graduate seminar on causation working through the disagreement between David Lewis's counterfactual theory and the interventionist framework developed by James Woodward and Judea Pearl, and one case keeps generating disagreement among us: trumping preemption, a phenomenon first introduced into the literature by Jonathan Schaffer. Here is a version of it: a general and a colonel simultaneously order a soldier to advance, and the soldier, trained always to obey the highest-ranking officer present, advances. Had the general given no order, the soldier would still have advanced, obeying the colonel instead -- so the advance does not counterfactually depend on the general's order. Yet intuitively it is the general's order, and not the colonel's, that actually causes the advance, since the colonel's order is trumped whenever a superior's order is also present.

Write a dialogue of roughly 600 to 900 words between a committed Lewisian counterfactual theorist and a committed interventionist (working within the structural-equations/causal-graph tradition of Woodward and Pearl) who are arguing about what their respective frameworks should say about this case. Each side should offer the best version of how their theory can be refined to deliver the intuitive verdict -- for instance, an appeal to a context-sensitive similarity ordering on counterfactuals for the Lewisian, or to a specific choice of structural equations and admissible interventions (or contingencies) for the interventionist -- and each should press a real objection against the other's fix rather than a strawman. For example, the interventionist might challenge how principled the Lewisian's similarity metric still is once it has been tuned to handle trumping, while the Lewisian might challenge whether specifying the "right" causal graph in the first place already presupposes the very causal facts the interventionist analysis is supposed to explain. The two characters should be equally sharp, should genuinely respond to what the other has just said rather than talk past each other, and the dialogue should end with the disagreement still live, since this remains a genuinely open dispute in the literature rather than one with a settled resolution.
