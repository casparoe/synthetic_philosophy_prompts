Write a dialogue between a dialetheist and a classical logician about whether Curry's paradox is actually worse news for dialetheism than the liar is. The classical logician's challenge: the Curry derivation never uses negation, so just being willing to accept true contradictions (gluts) to handle the liar doesn't obviously do anything for Curry -- so what, concretely, does the dialetheist give up to block it, and isn't that a totally seperate, ad hoc concession from the one that handles the liar? Have the dialetheist actually work through a real answer instead of just repeating the case for gluts, and have the classical logician keep pressing on whatever move gets made. Both sides should be equally sharp -- don't strawman either one.
