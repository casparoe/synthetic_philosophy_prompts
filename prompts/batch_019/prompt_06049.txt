I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and next week I'm interviewing a cognitive scientist known for defending a strongly Fodorian language-of-thought hypothesis: the view that thinking consists in manipulating mental representations with genuine syntactic structure and combinatorial semantics, a real "Mentalese" rather than just activation patterns that look structured. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view. I want at least one question each on why the productivity and systematicity of thought are supposed to require combinatorial syntax rather than merely appearing to have it; on the concept-learning paradox that pushes Fodor's own position toward radical concept nativism -- press her on how she avoids the conclusion that concepts like DOORKNOB or CARBURETOR are innate; and on whether large language models, which generate systematic-seeming sentences from distributed, non-symbolic representations, count as a live counterexample to the classic argument against connectionism, or whether she would say they covertly implement a language of thought after all. For each, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give.
