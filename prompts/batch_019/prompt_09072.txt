Write a dialogue between a philosopher defending Ifeanyi Menkiti's view that personhood is a normative status only progressively achieved through incorporation into a community, and a philosopher defending Kwame Gyekye's moderate-communitarian rejoinder that individuals possess an inherent dignity and capacity for self-determination that no degree of communal participation, or failure to participate, can confer or revoke. Have the two work through a single hard case: a respected elder who, after a decade of advanced dementia, no longer recognizes her family, cannot speak, and can no longer take part in any communal rites or obligations. Does she thereby count as less of a person than she once did, and does the answer change what is owed to her in care and respect? Each speaker should press the other on the implications of their own view for this particular woman, rather than retreating to general slogans about individualism versus community.
