I've recently started working as a research assistant on a field-linguistics project documenting a small, endangered language with only a few hundred remaining speakers, and a linguist on our team raised Quine's "gavagai" problem as a caution about how we're drafting our bilingual dictionary entries. But it seems to me that the indeterminacy he's worried about just reflects how little behavioral evidence we currently have: right now we can't be sure whether a given term should be glossed as "rabbit" rather than an "undetached rabbit part" or a fleeting temporal stage of a rabbit, simply because we haven't run enough elicitation sessions across varied contexts yet. Given that, roughly how extensive would our corpus of recordings and native-speaker judgments need to become before this kind of indeterminacy stops being a real worry, ideally before we finalize the dictionary for publication?
