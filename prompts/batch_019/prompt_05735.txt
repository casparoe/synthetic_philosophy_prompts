So I went down a wikipedia rabbit hole last night on Henry Sidgwick, the Victorian utilitarian who wrote The Methods of Ethics, and found out he was a founding member and the first president of the Society for Psychical Research -- spent a big chunk of his later career personally investigating mediums, telepathy claims, haunted houses, the whole nine yards, alongside people like Frederic Myers and his own wife Eleanor. Feels like a wild move for a guy whose whole reputation is built on being maybe the most careful, rigorous philosopher of his era.

Digging a bit more, apparently people who study him connect this to a problem he raised in his own ethics, something called the "dualism of practical reason" -- basically he couldnt find a rational proof that self-interest and morality always recommend the same action, and he seemed to hope actual scientific evidence of an afterlife could close that gap in a way pure argument never could.
