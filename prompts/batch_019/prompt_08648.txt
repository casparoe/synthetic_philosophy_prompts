I'm a software engineer with zero formal philosophy training, but I've been down an effective altruism rabbit hole and keep running into this claim that 'interpersonal utility comparisons' are basically impossible -- like there's no valid way to say person A wants or enjoys something more than person B does. Feels like a solved problem to me, though. At work we score feature requests by having each teammate rate how bad they want something 1-10, then min-max normalize everyones scores to their own 0-1 range before averaging, so the harsh graders dont skew results. Why don't economists and philosophers just use that same normalization trick on happiness or preference data instead of treating interpersonal comparison like some deep unsolved mystery?
