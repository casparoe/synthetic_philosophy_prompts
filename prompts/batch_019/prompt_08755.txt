Jonas: backward induction is kind of a cheat - it needs common knowledge of rationality to hold at every single node, including ones that, by the argument's own conclusion, never actually get reached. So the argument needs my opponent to be rational enough that I can predict they'll defect two moves from now, but if I ever actually watched them pass, that's direct evidence against the very rationality the argument assumed, and the inference collapses right when you'd need it most.

2. Naomi: that's basically the Pettit-Sugden move, but Aumann literally proved common knowledge of rationality implies the backward induction outcome. 3. Jonas: sure, and that's exactly what Binmore called out though - treating it as a probability-zero non-event is what drains the theorem of the content people actually want from it. No paradox required, just incomplete information.

5. Jonas: maybe, but that's a seperate question - showing a nearby incomplete-information model predicts prolonged cooperation doesn't tell us whether the original common-knowledge argument is coherent. Bicchieri could be completely right that it's self-undermining and KMRW could be completely right about the data, at the same time.
