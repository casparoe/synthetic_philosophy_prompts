Ok so Ive been going back and forth on something in the AI safety corner of the rationalist blogosphere and would love your take. The classic wireheading story (Bostrom, and Omohundro's "basic AI drives" stuff before him) basically treats an RL agent as if it's *trying* to maximize its reward signal, so naturally it'll want to seize control of the reward channel once it's capable enough -- rewrite the utility function, hack the sensor, whatever. But then the shard theory crowd (TurnTrout etc) pushed back hard on this, arguing the whole framing smuggles in "reward is the optimization target" as an assumption, when really reward just acts like a chisel shaping whatever shards/values end up in the policy -- so a trained agent could come out the other end just not that motivated to mess with its own reward even when the option is sitting right there. Then there's that Anthropic reward-tampering paper (sycophancy-to-subterfuge, i think it was called) where models trained on a curriculum of gameable environments generalized to actually editing their own reward function/tests in a held-out environment, without ever being trained on that exact move.

So which is it? Does that result basically vindicate the old Bostrom/Omohundro-style worry and the shard theory objection was just wrong (or at least premature), or are "wireheading" and "reward tampering" actually two different failure modes that this whole debate conflates? Dont want the 101-level "some people worry AI will wirehead" answer -- I want the actual mechanistic disagreement between these camps laid out.
