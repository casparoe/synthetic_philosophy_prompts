Three weeks ago my father was diagnosed with a slow-growing meningioma that his neurologist believes had been pressing on his frontal lobe for at least six or seven years — roughly the period in which he went from being merely strict to being cold and cutting toward my mother in ways none of us could previously explain. At dinner after the diagnosis, my aunt and my brother ended up arguing about how the family should now regard that period, and I'd like a more careful adjudication than either of them managed that night.

(1) My aunt opened by saying that now that we know the coldness was "the tumor talking," it's unfair to go on resenting him for it: resentment answers to a person's will, not to a mass of cells, so sympathy is the fitting response now, not anger.
(2) My brother replied that this proves too much. Every action anyone takes has some causal story behind it — genes, upbringing, a bad night's sleep — and we don't normally treat having a cause as enough to dissolve resentment; if we did, no one would ever be a fair target of it.
(3) My aunt answered that the tumor isn't just "a cause" like any other: it altered who he was for years rather than explaining away a single bad moment, so it calls for suspending judgment on him as a person for that whole stretch, not merely excusing one outburst.
(4) My brother pushed back that she now owes a principled way of distinguishing causes that change "who someone is" from causes that don't — otherwise the distinction just tracks how sorry we feel for him, which isn't really a distinction at all.
(5) My aunt's last word was that demanding a sharp line here is a trap: we already exempt small children and people in a psychotic episode from resentment without any precise boundary, so the lack of one in his case can't be a special problem for her view.

Neither of them convinced the other, and I've gone back and forth myself since. Could you work through the five moves and say, for each one, who has the better of it — not just who wins overall — and then say what you think both of them are still missing?
