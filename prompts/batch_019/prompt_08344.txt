For my philosophy elective, I've been working through Kavka's toxin puzzle: an eccentric billionaire offers to pay you a large sum tonight if you sincerely intend to drink a toxin tomorrow that will make you painfully ill for a day but cause no lasting harm, and you collect the money regardless of whether you actually go through with it. It seems to me that the real difficulty here is just the same reason people fail their New Year's resolutions: it is hard to make yourself follow through on something unpleasant once you know you will not feel like doing it. So I am having trouble seeing why philosophers treat this as a deep puzzle about the nature of intention and rationality, rather than as just another case of weak willpower. Could you help me understand what I might be missing?
