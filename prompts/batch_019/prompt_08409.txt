so i went down a rabbit hole a while back on moritz schlick, the guy who ran the vienna circle (logical positivism, verification principle, all that). kind of funny that a hardcore positivist - the type who'd usually say "meaning of life" is a meaningless pseudo-question - actually wrote a sincere essay trying to answer it anyway. his big point was that a life built around "purpose" is a trap, because every purpose just points to another purpose down the line (you work to get paid, to pay rent, to keep working, etc), so it never actually lands anywhere and can't be where the meaning is. he says real meaning comes from stuff you do just for itself with no further goal, like a kid playing, and that losing that playful, purposeless attitude is basically what happens to us as adults.

been thinking about how that holds up against all the gamified apps everyone uses now - duolingo streaks, fitness apps with xp and levels, habit trackers etc. it kind of LOOKS like play, theres points and little rewards, but your still doing it for an outcome (learning spanish, losing weight) not for its own sake. so by his own logic maybe it doesnt actually count as real play at all, its just purpose wearing a costume.

what do you reckon schlick would actually say about this stuff if he saw it today? would he count gamification as smuggling some real playful meaning back into modern life, or would he say its still just the same old means-end trap dressed up to look fun? walk me through how he'd actually reason it out, not just your verdict.
