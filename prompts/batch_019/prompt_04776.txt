I'm a retired physicist (condensed matter, of all things) using retirement to finally read the philosophy I never had time for, and a few weeks ago I fell into the quantum suicide/quantum immortality literature and got a bit obsessed. Wrote up some notes trying to sort out my own thinking on it, would appreciate your comments, especially on point 3, which is the bit I'm least confident I've got right.

1. The standard argument: if many-worlds is true, someone who runs a device that kills them in some branches with fixed quantum odds, over and over, should become subjectively convinced of many-worlds after enough rounds, since they'll only ever find themselves in a branch where they survived to notice the result.

2. Most objections I've read attack the slide from "the Born rule gives the correct betting odds beforehand" to "conditional on still existing, every surviving branch is an equally good candidate for what I should expect to experience next" - which smells a lot like smuggling in something like the self-indication assumption from the Sleeping Beauty debates (I hope Im using that term right), just applied to one continuously-forking person instead of a population of guessers.

3. Here's the bit that's been keeping me up. Suppose we grant the whole picture anyway, for the sake of argument. Almost no real killing mechanism is a clean binary between "totally fine" and "dead" - there's a whole continuum of ways to half-survive. So as the device (or the risky behaviour) gets more lethal, shouldn't the surviving branches increasingly be drawn from the long tail of freak partial failures, rather than clustering near "unharmed"? There are just more ways to survive maimed than to survive perfectly intact, and that gap should widen as the lethality goes up. If that's right, a consistent quantum-suicide believer shouldn't expect immortality so much as a ratchet towards ever-worse survival - what I've seen called "quantum torment" somewhere, thought I can't remember where.

Am I reasoning about the measure concentration correctly here, or is there a standard reply that heads this off? And more generally, does "what I should expect to experience next" even mean anything once you take the branching seriously, or does the whole argument fall apart before we even get to my point 3?
