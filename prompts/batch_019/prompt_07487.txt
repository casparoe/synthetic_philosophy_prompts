So I was at a friend's place last week and we ended up staying up until like 2am arguing about propositions of all things (she's finishing a phil of language PhD, I took one grad seminar on this stuff two years ago and apparently that makes me think I know things now). It started because she was going on about the old "unity of the proposition" problem - you know, Bradley's regress, the thing where if a fact like "Kate loves Mark" were just a list {Kate, loving, Mark}, you couldn't distinguish it from the list for "Mark loves Kate," or even from a case where those three things just sit there without forming any fact at all - so you need SOMETHING to actually glue them into one unified fact, except then that glue needs its own glue to attach it to the rest, forever. Her claim was that this is basically solved now by the "act-based" theories of propositions from people like Soames and Hanks (and King's version too, even though his is a bit different with the interpretive relations). Her pitch: once a proposition just IS an agent's act of predicating a property of an object, the regress stops, because an act of predicating isn't the kind of thing built out of separate parts that need a connector - the unity is just built into what an act is.

I pushed back that this is basically Russell's old multiple relation theory of judgement in a new coat, and that theory died for a specific reason - Wittgenstein's objection that it can't explain why judging that Othello believes Desdemona loves Cassio is a different judgement from Othello believing Cassio loves Desdemona, given that its the exact same relata hooked up by the exact same multigrade relation either way. My worry is the act-based crowd just relocated Bradley's regress one level up, from "what unifies the constituents of the proposition" to "what unifies the constituents of the act," without actually earning the right to call acts regress-proof, the way Frege's unsaturated concepts or Armstrong's brute non-relational tie at least try to earn it. She says I'm being uncharitable and that acts really are a different kind of thing than lists of objects and properties, full stop. We never landed the argument and it's been bugging me all week.

Could you actually argue my side of this properly? Not a wikipedia-style "here are both views" rundown - I want the strongest possible case that act-based/predicational theories of propositions (Soames/Hanks, and King's too insofar as it applies) fail to genuinely dissolve Bradley's regress, and are vulnerable to basically the same direction/discrimination problem that sank Russell's multiple relation theory - as opposed to moves like Frege's unsaturatedness or Armstrong's non-relational tie, which at least attempt a principled story for why the regress halts where it does. Steelman her view for a bit if it helps your case, but I want an actual verdict in my favor by the end, not a shrug.
