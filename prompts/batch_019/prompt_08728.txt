I'm a software engineer on the working group that sets accommodation policy for technical interviews at my company. A candidate for a senior engineering role has documented ADHD affecting her working memory, and already qualifies for our standard accommodation of extra time plus a one-page personal reference sheet during the live coding round. She now wants to swap the sheet for her own AI coding assistant, pre-loaded only with her personal notes, arguing -- via what she calls "the extended mind thesis" and "Otto's notebook" -- that the assistant does exactly what the sheet already does, just more reliably, and that since we already expect engineers to lean on docs and AI tools rather than unaided memory on the job, unaided recall isn't really the skill this round is meant to certify. Two of my colleagues on the group disagree: they say a generative assistant differs in kind from a static sheet because it can hand her solutions she never actually worked out or reviewed beforehand, and that granting this would make the round untestable while every other candidate is barred from live AI use. Her interview is next week, so we need to decide. Please draft the working group's short decision memo -- approve, deny, or approve with specific conditions -- framed as a policy we could apply consistently going forward rather than a one-off ruling, with reasoning she could actually respond to, a timeline for implementation, and an honest account of the strongest objection to whatever you recommend.
