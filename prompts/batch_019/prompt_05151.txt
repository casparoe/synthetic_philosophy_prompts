I like to open the session of my graduate seminar on decision theory where we discuss Hofstadter's notion of superrationality with a short joke, and I could use a new one this year. Please write a joke whose punchline depends on the actual logic of superrationality in the one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma: the thought that two perfectly rational, perfectly similar agents should each infer that the other will reach the very same verdict they do, and so ought to treat their choice as one between mutual cooperation and mutual defection rather than as a matter of best-responding to a fixed, independent opponent. The joke should reward genuine understanding of why superrationality is thought to license cooperation, and of why this appears to conflict with the orthodox game-theoretic verdict that defection is dominant -- it should not be a generic riff on game theory, prisoners, or nerdy stereotypes more generally. Feel free to exploit edge cases, such as what happens when the presumed symmetry between the two agents turns out not to hold.
