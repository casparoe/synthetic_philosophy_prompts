The setup is basically: a soldier gets dropped somewhere in a region split into Red territory and Blue territory, and each territory is further split into a "Headquarters area" and a "Second Company area," so there's 4 equally likely spots to start with (1/4 each). Then she gets a garbled radio message that doesn't say where she actually is, just something like "if you're in Red territory, it's 3 times more likely you're in the Second Company area than the Headquarters area." Apparently if you update your beliefs using this "minimize relative information" rule that a lot of people treat as the natural generalization of Bayes' rule for this kind of situation, your beliefs about the Blue side end up shifting too, even though the message said literally nothing about Blue. That seems super weird to me, and apparently a bunch of philosophers have argued for decades about what this means for how we're even supposed to update on "iffy"/conditional info in general, as opposed to just learning a plain fact.
