I did my career in animal behaviour - lots of discrimination learning experiments with pigeons - and now that I'm retired I've been reading old philosophy of mind papers for fun. Just got through Harnad's 1990 paper on the symbol grounding problem, where his fix is to hook symbols up to "categorical representations": invariant features picked out by a learned feature-detector, sitting between raw "iconic" sensory input and the symbols proper, so that meaning is supposed to flow up from the bottom instead of the symbols just floating free.

But my old feature-detector nets sorted stimuli into categories through reinforcement all the time, and nobody thought THAT meant anything - it was just reliable differential responding, no different in kind from a thermostat tracking temperture. So it looks to me like Harnad hasn't solved the grounding problem, he's just relocated it: now the question is how a "categorical representation" gets hooked to the world, and mere reliable covariation doesn't seem to answer that any better then it answered the original question about symbols.

Could you argue FOR that objection as strongly as you can - that Harnad's hybrid solution doesn't actually close the gap between meaningless token-shuffling and real meaning, it just pushes the unexplained step down a level into the categorization mechanism? Please engage with the best reply a defender of Harnad would give, and show why it still doesn't work, rather than just listing the objection and moving on.
