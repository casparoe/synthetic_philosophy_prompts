Two friends of mine, Lena and Marcus, co-run a small design studio and have always paid a referral bonus of "5% of the invoice" to freelancers who bring them a client, a rule they've applied without incident to dozens of invoices, all comfortably under $10,000. Their first six-figure referral just came in: a $200,000 contract, which at 5% works out to a $10,000 bonus. (1) Marcus said the payout is obviously $10,000 -- that's just what 5% means, and it's what they've always meant by the rule. (2) Lena said it isn't obvious at all: every past payout is equally consistent with a rule that charges 5% up to some cap and then flattens out, since no invoice large enough to tell the two rules apart has ever come up before now, so nothing in their history actually settles which one they were following. (3) Marcus said he can settle it by introspection: he's always privately had "5%, no matter how large the invoice" in mind, and that fixes the answer. (4) Lena said that doesn't help, since whatever he had "in mind" is just as open to two readings as the rule itself was, and if the number had been outlandish enough he might well have balked or miscalculated, so his say-so doesn't obviously track a fact that already existed before this case came up. (5) Marcus, frustrated, said that by this logic no agreement between two people could ever be shown to mean one determinate thing rather than another, which is absurd. Lena had no good answer to that, and neither did I. Please go through the exchange move by move, including move 5, saying who has the better of it and why, and note anything important you think both of them are missing.
