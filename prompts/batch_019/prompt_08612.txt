(1) Nora said there's no extra mystery to explain here, no special faculty called "weakness of will" -- it's just hyperbolic discounting. (2) Devin said that's too quick. If Theo's preferences had genuinely flipped so couch-tonight beat gym-tonight, fine, no puzzle. But ask him on the couch at 7pm and he doesn't say "I've reconsidered, chips are better" -- he says "ugh, I know I should be at the gym." That persisting judgment that the gym is better, held at the very moment he's not acting on it, is the whole phenomenon -- and a model that only outputs a utility number, with no separate slot for a belief about what's best, can't even represent the split.

(3) Nora said the "I know I should" talk is cheap and mostly post-hoc face-saving -- of course he says that, it's the expected thing to say. What matters is what the curves imply: at that moment the near reward numerically wins, so in-the-moment Theo's revealed preference just is for the couch. The lingering "should" is an echo of 6am-Theo's now-outvoted preference, not a live, simultaneous judgment.

People reliably report effort, and sometimes win against the impulse, which only makes sense if two things are actively competing at the moment of choice, not one curve quietly beating another with nobody home to notice.

(5) Nora said effort is just what it feels like from the inside when two similarly-sized quantities are close together, no extra faculty required -- close votes feel effortful, lopsided ones don't, and that's true even in setups with obviously no "higher judgment" anywhere in sight.
