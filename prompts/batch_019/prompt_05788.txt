So I had this long, kind of ridiculous argument with my friend Leah last night about Wittgenstein's On Certainty (we're both stuck doing a reading group on it, send help). It started because I said hinge propositions are basically bedrock that can't budge, and she pulled up this bit and said it actually proves the opposite -- that for Wittgenstein nothing is permanently off-limits to doubt, some stuff just hasn't gotten around to shifting yet. Here's the bit we got stuck on (it's sections 96 through 99):

"It might be imagined that some propositions, of the form of empirical propositions, were hardened and functioned as channels for such empirical propositions as were not hardened but fluid; and that this relation altered with time, in that fluid propositions hardened, and hard ones became fluid. The mythology may change back into a state of flux, the river-bed of thoughts may shift. But I distinguish between the movement of the waters on the river-bed and the shift of the bed itself; though there is not a sharp division of the one from the other. But if someone were to say 'So logic too is an empirical science' he would be wrong. Yet this is right: the same proposition may get treated at one time as something to test by experience, at another as a rule of testing. And the bank of that river consists partly of hard rock, subject to no alteration or only to an imperceptible one, partly of sand, which now in one place now in another gets washed away, or deposited."

Leah's read: there's no principled line between a "hinge" and an ordinary empirical belief here, it's purely a matter of degree and elapsed time, so anything Wittgenstein calls certain is just contingently undoubted for now, no different in kind from something I could revise next week. My read: he explicitly says the bank is partly hard rock and partly sand, and that's not just decoration, he's saying some things genuinely don't shift the way ordinary beliefs do, even if the boundry between the two is fuzzy. We went back and forth for like an hour and neither of us budged.

Can you actually unpack what's going on in this passage -- what work the hard-rock/sand image is doing, whether the hinges are supposed to differ in kind from ordinary beliefs or just be more entrenched at the moment, and whether the bit about logic possibly being "an empirical science" changes that? And if you had to say, whose reading is closer to how this passage usually gets used in the hinge epistemology literature?
