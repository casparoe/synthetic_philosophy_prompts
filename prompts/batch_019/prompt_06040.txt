Over the past few weeks I've been sitting in on a philosophy of physics seminar that several of us physics PhD students cross-registered for, mainly because my dissertation work involves numerical simulations of vacuum spacetimes and I realized I had never carefully thought through what the points of the manifold in my equations are supposed to be. Two sessions ago the instructor walked us through Earman and Norton's hole argument: take a solution of the Einstein field equations, apply a diffeomorphism that is the identity everywhere outside some small "hole" region but smoothly deviates from it inside, and drag the metric field along by that map. The result is a second, mathematically distinct field on the same manifold that agrees with the original on every Cauchy surface prior to the hole and everywhere outside it, yet differs inside the hole itself, even though both count as equally good solutions of the same field equations. If spacetime points are individuals whose identity doesn't depend on the metric, this seems to show that general relativity is radically indeterministic; if they aren't, the argument is supposed to lose its force. What made the whole thing feel less like an academic curiosity was learning that Einstein himself ran into essentially this worry around 1913 and used it to talk himself out of generally covariant field equations for a couple of years before changing course in late 1915. I followed the construction well enough, but I came away unsure whether either side of the substantivalism/relationalism dispute can really hold its ground once you push on it, and I'd like to see the positions argued out rather than just summarized.

Could you write a dialogue between a sophisticated substantivalist, who keeps spacetime points as genuine entities but denies that they carry any primitive, metric-independent identity, so that the two diffeomorphic models don't count as representing distinct possibilities, and a relationalist, who denies that spacetime points are anything over and above the relations holding among events and fields? I'd like the relationalist to press the charge that "sophistication" is just relationalism wearing substantivalist clothing, and the substantivalist to give a substantive answer rather than simply reasserting anti-haecceitism; I'd also like the substantivalist to push back by asking whether a relational theory can even be formulated without covertly quantifying over point-like individuals to do the work the manifold does for the substantivalist. Both should be arguing as the strongest versions of their view, responding directly to whatever the other has just said, and neither should be left holding an unanswered objection by the end.
