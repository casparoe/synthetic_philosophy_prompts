At a family barbecue this past summer, my brother-in-law—who likes to tease me for accepting evolution too uncritically—told me that even Karl Popper, a serious philosopher of science, had admitted that natural selection isn't really a testable theory at all, just a tautology dressed up as biology. I didn't have a good answer for him, which bothered me enough that afterward I tracked down some of Popper's own writing rather than simply trusting either side of the argument. What I found was messier than the dinner-table version. Popper did, at one stage in his career, describe the theory of natural selection as something like a "metaphysical research programme" rather than a genuinely falsifiable scientific theory, and he came close to granting the much older complaint that "survival of the fittest" just means "survival of the survivors." But later in his life—in a lecture I believe was connected to some Darwin commemoration, though I can no longer pin down the exact reference—he said he wanted to recant that earlier judgment, apparently having decided the theory does generate testable predictions after all.

What I still haven't been able to work out on my own is how many distinct claims are actually in play here. Is Popper's "metaphysical research programme" charge simply a more technical restatement of the plain old tautology objection, or is it a logically separate criticism, rooted in his own particular views on falsifiability and demarcation, that merely resembles the tautology complaint from a distance? And when he recanted, did he take back both objections in one stroke, or could someone consistently go on holding the tautology worry while still accepting his later claim that natural-selection theory, properly formulated alongside genetics, does make risky, testable predictions? I would like you to set the two objections out side by side, be precise about exactly where they overlap and where they diverge, and state plainly what the recantation did and did not undo. If you are willing to go one step further, I would also value your own judgment as to which side of Popper's reversal comes closer to being right, given how biologists actually seem to define and measure fitness in practice, rather than in the shorthand version that comes up at family barbecues.
