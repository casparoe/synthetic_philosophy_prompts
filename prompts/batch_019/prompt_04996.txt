Over dinner last night, I told a close friend about a decision I made several months ago, and what followed was a long, rather heated argument that lasted well into the night, with neither of us convincing the other. I would like a more disinterested take on the matter than either of us could manage.

Some months ago, I was offered an excellent position at my company's overseas office. After a great deal of soul-searching, I turned it down, entirely because I did not want to impose the upheaval of relocating on my relationship with my partner, whose career and family ties here would have made moving very difficult for her. At the time, and ever since, I regarded this as one of the more meaningful sacrifices I have made for our relationship. A few weeks ago, though, I learned by chance, through a former colleague, that the overseas office had in fact been under a quiet hiring freeze that would have led to my offer being withdrawn within days regardless of what I decided. In other words, unbeknownst to me at the time, there was never actually any possibility of my ending up with that job.

When I told my friend this, he argued that the discovery retroactively empties my decision of any significance: since I could not have taken the job no matter what I chose, I never really gave anything up, and so I have no grounds for regarding it as a sacrifice or taking any pride in it. My instinct is that this gets things backwards: my reasoning and motives at the time were exactly what they would have been had the hiring freeze never existed, and none of that seems undone by a fact I did not even know about and that had no bearing on my thinking. But I could not fully explain why his argument fails, if it does, and we eventually agreed to disagree. So, who is right: does the vanished alternative mean that I really have nothing to feel proud of, or does my decision still count as a genuine sacrifice - and if so, why?
