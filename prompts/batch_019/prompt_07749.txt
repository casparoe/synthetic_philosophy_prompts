I'm the newest member of my small housing co-op's three-person board, which also serves as the panel that hears rule-violation cases and decides whether to fine residents. Under our bylaws, a resident can be fined only if a violation occurred and a valid written warning was already on file for it. In our last case, one of us doubted the violation had actually happened, another doubted the warning was valid, and I accepted both, so a straight yes-or-no vote on the fine lost two to one. Yet a separate majority of us believed the violation happened, and a separate majority believed the warning was valid -- meaning that, taken one question at a time, the board had actually supported a fine. Our chair now wants every future case decided question by question rather than by a direct vote; the other member objects that this just lets whoever frames the questions control the outcome, and that only a direct vote reflects what the board actually decided. We need a standing rule before our next hearing. Please write the short memo I would circulate to my two colleagues, recommending which procedure the board should adopt going forward -- or a better alternative, if you think both are flawed -- with reasoning that fairly answers whichever side you do not take.
