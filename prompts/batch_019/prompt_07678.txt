For my school's Ethics Bowl team, I've been reading about why promises are morally binding, and I keep getting stuck between two theories that flatly contradict each other. The first, which draws on Hume and was developed further by Rawls, treats promising as a social practice: saying "I promise" binds you only because we maintain a useful convention that gives those words their force, the way the rules of a game create obligations that don't exist outside the game. The second denies that any such convention is necessary at all, holding instead that sincerely assuring someone of something is inherently obligation-generating — so that even the very first promise ever made, before any convention existed for either party to invoke, would already have bound its maker.

Could you write a dialogue between a proponent of each view, focused specifically on that "first promise" case? Each side should directly engage with the other's strongest objections rather than just restating their own position, and neither should come out as the obvious winner.
