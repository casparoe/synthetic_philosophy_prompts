So a few of us from the LW/ACX-meetup crowd have a running paper club, and last month we worked through one of those old "acausal trade" / multiverse-wide superrationality writeups. Afterward two people kept arguing about it in the group chat for like three days and never landed anywhere, and I'm reporting it here because it's still bugging me.

The scenario that kicked it off: a future friendly AI refrains from harming simulated copies of an unfriendly AI's creators in branches where the unfriendly AI wins, on the theory that the unfriendly AI will acausally reciprocate and spare us in branches where we win — even though neither side ever gets a causal signal from the other, ever.

(1) Mira said this is a perfectly coherent trade: if both AIs can model each other accurately and each conditions its action on a prediction of the other's, then choosing to cooperate is evidence the other side cooperates too. Same logic as Hofstadter's superrational twins in a prisoner's dilemma, just scaled up to strangers.

(2) Dev said calling it "trade" smuggles in something the logic doesn't actually license: ordinary trade works because reneging is costly (you get caught, sued, whatever). Here nothing stops either side from just not paying up once its part is "delivered," since by construction neither one can ever check up on the other afterward.

(3) Mira replied that the shared decision algorithm just *is* the enforcement mechanism — you're not trusting the other side's future goodwill, you're trusting that at the moment of choice it's running close enough to the same computation as you, wich outputs cooperate. Reneging was never really on the table, same as how actual twins don't need a cop watching them to both cooperate.

(4) Dev said fine, that works for literal copies, but the whole point of the multiverse-wide version is that the other agent is NOT running your algorithm, it's some alien mind that just reasons "similarly enough." Once you weaken "same algorithm" to "similar enough," you've quietly let back in all the uncertainty about reciprocation that the shared-algorithm move was supposed to dissolve.

Nobody at paper club could close this out, and honestly the more i think about it the more i go back and forth. Can you adjudicate this move by move?
