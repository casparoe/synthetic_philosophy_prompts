As a retired astrophysicist who only started reading philosophy seriously after retiring, I've become stuck on the standoff between the Self-Sampling Assumption and the Self-Indication Assumption in anthropic reasoning. Each seems to solve one problem only by creating another that looks just as absurd: SSA seems to commit you to something like the Doomsday Argument, revising your credence about humanity's future merely from your rank among all humans who will ever live, while SIA escapes that conclusion only to license Bostrom's Presumptuous Philosopher, who claims to know which of two empirically indistinguishable cosmological theories is true purely by noting that one implies far more observers than the other. Write a dialogue, set in the corridor outside the laboratory in the minutes before the physicists in that thought experiment run their experiment, between a philosopher who defends SSA and one who defends SIA. Have each press the other specifically on this asymmetry, engaging with the actual counterarguments the other raises rather than restating their own view, and let neither side win cleanly. I'd like the exchange to end with the two of them trying to pin down whether their disagreement is a substantive one about probability and evidence, or ultimately just a difference in brute intuition about how to individuate and count observers.
