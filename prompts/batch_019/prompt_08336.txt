I've spent years reading LessWrong, Overcoming Bias, and the Alignment Forum, and one idea that has always stuck with me is Yudkowsky's old proposal that a friendly superintelligence should be built to pursue humanity's Coherent Extrapolated Volition (CEV) rather than our present, unreflective preferences: roughly, whatever we would converge on wanting if we were smarter, better informed, more the people we wish we were, and had undergone a great deal more moral and personal development than we actually have. It strikes me that this is, structurally, an internalist theory of reasons in something like Bernard Williams's technical sense: what an agent has most reason to want is whatever a sound deliberative route from that agent's own (suitably idealized) subjective motivational set would recommend, rather than anything answerable to considerations external to that set. Please describe the most important implications of the internalism/externalism debate about reasons, Williams versus critics such as Nagel, McDowell, Korsgaard, and Parfit, for CEV as a target specification for AI alignment. I'm especially interested in whether the classic objections to Williams's internalism resurface for CEV, and whether the sheer extremity of CEV's idealization procedure quietly reintroduces the kind of agent-independent convergence that only externalists are supposed to be entitled to.
