Ok so I've fallen down a bit of a rabbit hole this week. Bradley's regress keeps coming up as a throwaway reference whenever "the unity of the proposition" gets mentioned in the rationalist-adjacent stuff I read (old LW threads on truthmakers, the occasional ACX comment section where someone's showing off), and I always just nodded along with the two-line gloss: relations need further relations to tie them to their terms, forever, contradiction, done. This came up again in an argument with a friend about whether "states of affairs" in contemporary metaphysics are just a rebranded dodge of Bradley's problem, and I got annoyed enough at not actually knowing the argument that I pulled up Appearance and Reality (1893) on Wikisource, chapter 3, "Relation and Quality" (https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Appearance_and_Reality/Chapter_III), to read it myself instead of vibing off secondary summaries. Rookie mistake — the actual prose is way less clean than the SEP paraphrase makes it sound.

Here's the bit near the top of the chapter that's got me stuck:

"The object of this chapter is to show that the very essence of these ideas is infected and contradicts itself. Our conclusion briefly will be this. Relation presupposes quality, and quality relation. Each can be something neither together with, nor apart from, the other; and the vicious circle in which they turn is not the truth about reality. 1. Qualities are nothing without relations. In trying to exhibit the truth of this statement, I will lay no weight on a considerable mass of evidence. This, furnished by psychology, would attempt to show how qualities are variable by changes of relation. The differences we perceive in many cases seem to have been so created."

What's tripping me up: he says relation and quality 'presuppose' each other and calls the result a 'vicious circle,' but mutual presupposition just sounds like garden-variety holism to me, not an actual contradiction — plenty of things depend on each other for their identity without anyone calling that vicious. So what's the extra step that turns "these two need each other to exist" into "these two are incoherent"? And is this mutual-dependence move even the same argument as the infinite regress people usually mean when they namedrop "Bradley's regress" (relation R needs a further relation to tie it to a and b, which needs a further relation, and so on), or is that a seperate argument later in the chapter that just gets lumped under the same name in the secondary lit? I honestly can't tell if this passage IS the regress in embryonic form or just a warm-up act for it. And if you've got a take on why Russell apparently took this seriously enough for it to shape his thinking on the unity of the proposition, I'd love to hear it, because on first read it kind of looks like Bradley might just be equivocating on what "relation" means.
