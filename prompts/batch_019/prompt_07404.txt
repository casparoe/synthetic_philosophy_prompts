A friend and I spent most of a dinner arguing about Frege after she mentioned, almost in passing, that in the last year or so of his life he kept a private diary that is virulently antisemitic and openly contemptuous of democracy and the Weimar Republic — a diary that surfaced and was published only long after his death, and that, by all accounts, genuinely unsettled Michael Dummett once he had to reckon with it. Her view is that this is a sad biographical fact, sealed off from the philosophy: the sense/reference distinction, the case against psychologism, and the postulate of a 'third realm' housing objective thoughts are correct or not, valid or not, on their own terms, regardless of who happened to write them down. Mine is that this is too quick — that the same hunger for one single, objectively correct logical order, with no legitimate room for rival perspectives, sits uncomfortably close to the politics, even if it doesn't strictly imply it. Neither of us gave any ground. Write a dialogue between two philosophers taking these two sides, in which each has to engage the other's strongest argument rather than simply restate their own, and have it end the way ours did: neither position collapsing, but each side conceding at least one point it didn't want to.
