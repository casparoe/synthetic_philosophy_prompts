Intuitionistic logic's rejection of the law of excluded middle is often said to also rule out proof by contradiction, since inferring that a statement is true from the absurdity of its negation is just excluded middle in another guise. If that is correct, it would seem that constructive mathematicians must reject even elementary classical arguments such as Euclid's proof that there are infinitely many primes, which proceeds by assuming a finite list of primes and deriving a contradiction. Do constructivists in fact reject this proof, and if not, what does that show about which uses of proof by contradiction mathematical constructivism actually forbids?
