For my course on the philosophy of the public sphere, we have just read Kierkegaard's Two Ages alongside Habermas's Structural Transformation of the Public Sphere, and I want to see how far the comparison actually holds up under pressure rather than just noting the surface parallel. Write an imagined dialogue between Kierkegaard and Habermas in which Kierkegaard presses his claim that "the public" is a leveling abstraction — a phantom with no concrete existence that nonetheless dissolves individual responsibility — while Habermas defends the press-mediated public sphere as the very mechanism of rational-critical accountability that keeps power in check. Do not let the exchange settle into a simple standoff: have Habermas grant that his own account of the "refeudalization" of the public sphere under commercialized mass media sounds uncomfortably close to Kierkegaard's leveling, and have Kierkegaard press him on whether that concession vindicates the original diagnosis or merely describes a corruptible version of something Habermas still takes to be basically sound. Each should respond to the other's actual point rather than simply restate their own position.
