ok so I've been down a Humeanism-about-laws-of-nature rabbit hole for like a week now and need to get this out of my system. Lewis's best-system picture -- laws are just whatever axioms best balance simplicity and strength in summarizing the giant mosaic of local matters of particular fact, nothing is actually "governing" anything -- always felt like the correct, reductionist, LW-brained answer to me. Way more parsimonious than Armstrong's necessitation relations between universals, or Maudlin's "laws are just primitive, deal with it" move. But then theres the entanglement problem: if the complete state of the world really is just an arrangement of local, intrinsic qualities at points plus spatiotemporal relations, how do entangled states even fit in, given that the joint state of two particles isn't fixed by facts about each particle taken seperately? That feels like a real threat to the whole mosaic picture and not just a technical patch job, and I don't think most Humeans have a fully satisfying answer to it.
