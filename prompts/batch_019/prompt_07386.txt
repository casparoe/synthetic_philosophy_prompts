Over the past couple of months I have been trying to raise about $30,000 to open a small bakery, and my aunt, who wants to invest, has offered me two different arrangements: a loan at a fixed 8% interest rate that I would owe her on schedule regardless of how the business performs, or a stake in the bakery itself, where she only earns anything if it actually turns a profit. My accountant says the loan is the more sensible option financially, but I cannot shake the sense that the two arrangements differ in some deeper way than the expected payout: paying a return on the money itself, independent of whether it actually helped produce anything, feels different in kind from sharing honestly in an outcome we both had a hand in. Is there a real, defensible distinction here, or am I just being naive about how financing works?
