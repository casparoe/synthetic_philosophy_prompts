"Bell's theorem grows out of the EPR paper, where Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen use correlated particles to argue that if locality holds, QM must be incomplete, since there have to be pre-existing hidden values fixing the outcomes on each side. Bell then showed that any theory reproducing the perfect correlations of the singlet state via local hidden variables has to obey an inequality, in its CHSH form that a certain combination of correlations is bounded by 2, and that entangled quantum systems violate this bound, up to the value QM predicts. This has since been confirmed experimentally, including in the loophole-free tests from around 2015.

The derivation rests on two ingredients: locality (a measurement choice or outcome on one wing can't influence the distant outcome) and realism (the particles have definite properties before measurement, i.e. hidden variables exist). Since experiments violate the inequality, one of these has to go, and that's exactly why Bohmian mechanics and Copenhagen count as the two horns: Bohmian mechanics keeps the particles' definite trajectories (realism) but pays for it with a nonlocal guiding wave, while Copenhagen keeps locality intact and instead denies particles have definite properties pre-measurement. That's also why no faster-than-light signal ever shows up in EPR-type experiments: only a correlation, not any real physical influence, passes between the wings."
