So I'm about two-thirds through a novel where one of my leads is an aging opera director trying to restage Don Giovanni for a jittery board and a much younger cast, and for background reading I've been back in Bernard Williams' On Opera. Theres an essay on Giovanni specifically in there -- I think it's called something like "Don Giovanni as an Idea," dont hold me to the exact title, I don't have the book in front of me. The thing that's stuck with me is that Williams is pretty resistant to treating the opera as a morality tale about a rake getting his comeuppance; he seems much more interested in how the music and the sheer staged artificiality of the whole thing let it hold Giovanni's charm and his monstrousness together at once, without ever quite forcing you to pick a side, and he links this to Kierkegaard's bit in Either/Or about Giovanni being basically the purest expression of "the aesthetic" life, before any ethical reckoning sets in.

My director character is now stuck with exactly that problem, except forty-odd years later and online. His producers want him to cut or reframe the ending (the bit right after Giovanni gets dragged down to hell, where everyone left onstage sings a smug little moral about evildoers getting what's coming), give Anna, Elvira and Zerlina a lot more to actually do, and maybe add a program note about consent -- basically the standard post-#MeToo fixes you see cropping up in new productions now.

My partner, who actually studied philosophy, reckons Williams would've hated all this: a bunch of well-meaning people flattening something irreducibly ambiguous into an easy verdict. I think that's too quick, and maybe too flattering to him. Could you make the actual case, one way or the other, for what Williams would most plausibly have said about these #MeToo-era restagings -- drawing not just on the Giovanni essay but on his wider ethics too (the stuff on the "morality system," thick ethical concepts, his impatience with impartial, system-first moralizing)? And anywhere you're extrapolating past what he explicitly wrote, flag it as your own extrapolation rather than presenting it as settled.
