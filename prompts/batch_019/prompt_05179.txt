I write about science and ideas for a magazine that likes to run a short story alongside its features, and I'm currently working on a piece about the decision theory now surfacing in debates over trustworthy AI agents. The touchstone is Derek Parfit's "hitchhiker": a traveler dying of thirst is picked up only because the driver can tell he genuinely means to pay once they reach town, even though ordinary self-interested rationality tells him to renege the moment he's safe -- which is exactly why it's his disposition to pay, not any calculation made at the moment of paying, that gets him rescued at all. Could you write me a short story of around 1,000 words, set somewhere far from deserts and cars, that makes a reader feel the vertigo in that result: that it can be rational to turn yourself into a person whose own future actions will be irrational? No character should get to simply state the puzzle aloud -- keep the philosophy load-bearing rather than explained.
