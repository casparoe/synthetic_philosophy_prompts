I'm a PhD student in political science and sit on my graduate student association's governance committee. In this year's presidential race, three candidates split a similar base of support under our plurality rule, and the candidate who won would have lost a head-to-head contest against either of the other two -- a fact several members have cited all semester as proof that the system is broken. The general assembly has asked the committee to recommend a replacement procedure before spring nominations open, subject to two constraints it insists on: whatever we adopt must be explainable to the membership in a paragraph, and it must not be easily manipulated by an organized slate. Two of my fellow committee members are already campaigning for ranked-choice (instant-runoff) voting as the obvious fix. Please draft the committee's memo: recommend one specific procedure, state plainly which condition of Arrow's impossibility theorem it sacrifices and defend that as the least costly option available to us given the assembly's constraints, and give a fair hearing to the strongest objection a colleague could raise against your recommendation -- including, if it applies, to instant-runoff's own vulnerabilities in exactly this sort of vote-splitting scenario.
