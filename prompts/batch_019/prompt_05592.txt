So I've been reporting out a piece on this "Kierkegaard basically called social media" thing that keeps making the rounds, and for background I had two academics on a call together -- Elena (19th century philosophy) and Marcus (media studies) -- who ended up in a genuine argument that was honestly more useful than anything either of them said on the record. Trying to write it up fair and I keep going back and forth on who's actually right, so hoping you can referee.

(1) Elena's opening move: the real target in "The Present Age" is "the public" as a leveling abstraction, and the press is just the delivery mechanism -- so if you want to say Kierkegaard "called" Twitter, you owe a seperate argument that social media produces that same kind of abstraction, not just that it's another mass medium.

(2) Marcus pushed back that you can't peel the press off that cleanly: Kierkegaard keeps zeroing in on journalists specifically, their anonymity, their lack of accountability, as what makes instant, faceless leveling possible in the first place. Take away the press and there's no mechanism left, so calling it "just the vehicle" undersells how load-bearing it is in his own argument.

(3) Elena granted the press is necessary for that particular historical form of the problem, but said thats not the same as being the target: what he's actually condemning is people relating to an abstraction instead of to each other, which is also why he can say "the crowd is untruth" in a much more religious register that has nothing to do with newspapers. Collapse the two, she said, and you lose the ability to use him on things like algorithmic pile-ons, which don't run anything like an 1840s editor did.

(4) Marcus said that's a little too convenient, and brought up the Corsair -- the satirical paper that spent months turning Kierkegaard into the town joke after he more or less dared them to -- as evidence that this whole "abstract" theory of the public was really a grudge against journalists dressed up as philosophy after the fact.

They never really settled it. Can you go through it move by move rather than just declaring an overall winner?
