I'm a retired geneticist who's been sitting in on a philosophy discussion group at the local university since I shut down the lab, mostly to keep my brain busy. The therapy/enhancement debate hooked me right away because "species-typical functioning" is exactly the kind of thing population geneticists know is a messy, contingent, moving target, yet half the bioethics literature treats it like bedrock. Next month's speaker defends the standard Rawlsian line -- we owe people treatment to restore normal function as a matter of fair equality of opportunity, but nothing to enhance beyond it -- and apparently deflects hard cases by calling them "a seperate question about resource allocation," not a problem with her account. I get a few minutes before the floor opens -- can you write me 8-10 questions that don't just let her restate the view, with at least one built around a case like idiopathic short stature (where you genuinely can't tell if you're treating or enhancing), one on why an evolutionary accident should get to set the moral baseline at all, and one on what happens to her framework once germline editing lets us choose the baseline instead of inheriting it -- plus a follow-up for each, keyed to whatever dodge she's most likely to reach for.
