I help moderate a Discord server that grew out of a college decision-theory reading group and now has a few hundred members who mostly discuss AI safety, formal epistemology, and adjacent topics. A couple of weeks ago a member posted a long thread reconstructing Roko's basilisk more or less from first principles: the idea that a future superintelligence, reasoning under something like timeless or updateless decision theory, could benefit from precommitting to acausally punish anyone who understood the argument today and failed to help bring it into existence, since the mere prospect of that punishment is what would make people help sooner. Within a day, another member messaged the mod team privately, saying the thread had cost her several nights of sleep, and asking that it come down.

That request turned into a long, unresolved argument between me and one of my co-moderators, a close friend from the original reading group. She wants the thread deleted and the topic restricted permanently to a spoiler-tagged channel, calling it a textbook information hazard in Bostrom's sense. She also pointed out that this is essentially what happened on LessWrong itself in 2010, when Eliezer Yudkowsky deleted Roko's original post and banned discussion of it for years after readers reported real psychological distress, and she doesn't see why our members should be assumed tougher than theirs were. I argued that the LessWrong ban is now widely remembered as a fiasco that made the idea more famous than silence ever would have -- basically the Streisand effect. I also pointed out that we have a clean rebuttal on hand: on the very decision theory the argument invokes, an agent known to fold under acausal blackmail thereby invites more of it, so a well-designed agent precommits to ignoring such threats; and in any case, you can construct an equally "valid" anti-basilisk that punishes people for trying to build the first one, so the incentives cancel out. She agreed the rebuttal is basically sound, but said that's beside the point: this flavor of dread runs more like scrupulosity or intrusive-thought OCD than like an ordinary false belief, so being shown the argument is wrong doesn't switch the feeling off in the people it affects. I said that's probably true, but then we have no principled place to stop, since extinction-risk discussions and simulation arguments and half our other pinned topics could cost someone sleep too, and we can't run the server by deferring to whoever happens to be most anxious at the moment.

Neither of us budged, and I've since been asked to write the actual proposal for Thursday's mod meeting. Could you draft that memo for me? I need a concrete recommendation -- leave the topic unrestricted, quarantine it, ban it outright, or something better than any of those -- with a specific time frame for revisiting the decision, and reasoning a skeptical reader could actually follow, including a fair account of whichever side's strongest point you end up not adopting.
