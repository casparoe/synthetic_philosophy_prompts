I'm a retired condensed-matter physicist who's fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole since retiring, and the thing I can't stop chewing on lately is this "realism about interpretability" business for neural nets. You know the pattern: someone trains a linear probe and finds a "feature" or "representation" -- like the board-state stuff people found in Othello-GPT -- and then someone else points out the same activations are polysemantic, or in superposition, or that a totally diffrent rotation of the basis works just as well, so maybe "representation" talk is just a useful story we tell about the network (a la Dennett's intentional stance) rather than something its actually got. Reminds me a lot of the old fights over whether phonons and other quasiparticles are "real" or just bookkeeping for correlated electron behavior -- a debate I had genuine opinions about back when I did this stuff for a living.

Could you write me a dialogue between two researchers hashing this out at a poster session, standing in front of a probing result neither of them can agree how to read? One's a realist about it, the other's a deflationist who thinks it's fictions all the way down, no privileged basis, no fact of the matter. Make them actually rebut each other's specific points (superposition, basis-dependence, the linear representation hypothesis, whatever) instead of trading slogans -- I want two people who've clearly read the same papers and still disagree hard.
