So in my History of Modern Philosophy seminar we spent the last two weeks on Hobbes (mostly Leviathan, with some De Corpore excerpts), and at one point my professor mentioned, almost as an aside, that Hobbes spent something like the last twenty-five years of his life insisting he had squared the circle, kept getting mathematically demolished in print by John Wallis over and over, and never once admitted defeat, basically up until he died. Everyone in the room just laughed it off as "old man Hobbes being stubborn," but I mentioned it to my roommate (who's a math major) afterward and she said it's not actually just stubbornness — that it's supposedly connected to his whole materialist metaphysics in a pretty deep way. Now I can't stop thinking about it and the professor didn't really unpack it in lecture.

From the little bit of secondary reading I've done, it sounds like Hobbes couldn't accept the ordinary Euclidean idea of a point as something with literally zero extension, because for him everything that exists is a body with some magnitude - there's no room in his system for a truly dimensionless anything. So instead he wants points, lines and planes to be defined genetically, sort of, in terms of bodies and their motions (a line as the "path" of a moving point, a plane as generated by a moving line, etc.), rather than just accepting Euclid's abstract definitions at face value. Honestly I find this pretty hard to get my head around, since it still seems like you need some kind of idealization to even talk about a "point" moving in the first place, so I don't fully see what he thinks he's actually gained.

Could you help me get an intuitive, gut-level sense of why Hobbes thought geometry needed this kind of materialist foundation instead of just the standard idealized/abstract one, and how that connects to his stubbornness about the quadrature of the circle and the whole fight with Wallis? I'm not looking for the play-by-play of the actual mathematical dispute (I gather Hobbes proofs were just wrong), I'm more interested in the philosophical dependency - like, is the geometry error actually downstream of the metaphysical commitment in some real sense, or is that connection kind of overstated and it was mostly just ego by that point?
