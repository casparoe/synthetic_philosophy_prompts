I've been rereading discussions of Hempel's raven paradox and keep noticing that they all stop at the same point. Because "All ravens are black" is logically equivalent to "All non-black things are non-ravens" (the Equivalence Condition), Nicod's Criterion seems to force the conclusion that finding a white shoe in your closet confirms, if only minutely, a claim about ornithology. Most treatments simply report that Hempel accepted this conclusion and explain away the discomfort by pointing to how enormous the class of non-black things is compared to the class of ravens. I'd like to see a treatment that pushes past that stock move.

Please write a dialogue set in the ornithology wing of a natural history museum, in front of a mounted raven, between a Bayesian confirmation theorist and a Popperian falsificationist, each trying to convince the other that their own account of evidence is the one that actually dissolves the paradox rather than merely relocating it. The Bayesian should argue that once Nicod's qualitative criterion is replaced with degrees of confirmation, the paradox evaporates into an unremarkable (if initially counterintuitive) fact about how weak most evidence is. The Popperian should argue that this whole framework is misconceived: on his view nothing is ever confirmed, only tested and not-yet-falsified, and a well-corroborated theory is prized precisely for being improbable and easily falsifiable rather than probable, so that the puzzle only arises once induction has been smuggled back in. At some point, have the Popperian press the Bayesian with a fact due to the statistician I. J. Good: that one can specify perfectly coherent background assumptions under which spotting a black raven should lower, rather than raise, one's confidence that all ravens are black. The Bayesian should engage directly with why this does not simply reduce confirmation to whatever one's priors want it to be, rather than sidestepping it. Write both characters as equally capable: each should respond to the specific point the other has just made rather than delivering parallel monologues, and neither should be allowed to win by default.
