So I've been kind of obsessed with game theory as a hobby for the last few months, no formal background, just fell down a wikipedia/lecture-video rabbit hole starting from the prisoner's dilemma and Schelling points. Eventually I hit the centipede game and this whole idea of "common knowledge of rationality," and honestly it kind of broke my brain. The claim (I think this is basically Aumann's result?) is that if both players are rational, and both know the other is rational, and both know that they know, forever, then you can actually *prove* the only consistent outcome is that someone grabs the money on the very first move — even though both players would clearly end up richer if they just kept passing for a while. I brought this up with a friend who's doing a math PhD and she just shrugged and said "oh yeah, people fight about that forever," which was not helpful.

Because then I went and found out it really is contested. From what I can piece together, people like Binmore (and I want to say Reny too, but don't quote me on that) think Aumann's argument secretly eats itself, because to do the backward induction reasoning at all you have to figure out what a "rational" player would do at a point in the game that, by that same reasoning, a rational player should never actually reach. So the argument needs "common knowledge of rationality" to still be intact at a node that could only be reached by somebody having already violated it. That feels like it should be a real crack in the proof, not just a philosophers' quibble, but I genuinely can't tell — maybe it's a distinction without a difference and the math survives fine either way.

Could you write me a dialogue between two epistemic game theorists who actually disagree about this — one who thinks the Aumann-style argument is basically airtight, and backward induction is simply what common knowledge of rationality forces on you, and one who thinks the whole thing is undone by this off-the-path problem I'm describing. I don't want either of them to be a pushover for the other; I want real back and forth where they're each responding to the specific thing the other just said, not just restating their own side louder, and I'd rather it not end with one of them obviously winning, since it sounds like actual game theorists haven't settled this either.
