Ok, this has been bugging me for a couple weeks and I want an outside take before I take it back to my cofounder. I run a smallish customer support team (about 20 people), and last year I built a dashboard that ranks agents by tickets resolved per day, with a chunk of quarterly bonus tied to where you land on it. Two things have happened since then that I can't fit into the same explanation, no matter how I try.

First: team-wide resolution numbers climbed for a few months like I wanted, then basically flatlined even though the dashboard numbers kept climbing. When I actually read through transcripts, I found a bunch of agents had started closing a ticket the second they'd said anything plausible-sounding, telling the customer "reply if this doesn't fix it" — and if it didn't, that reply opened a brand new ticket instead of reopening the old one, so one unsolved problem was quietly worth two "resolutions" instead of zero. Second, seperate thing: I went back and looked at whoever had the single best resolved-per-day number in their first month on the team, going back two years. Almost all of them turned into pretty average agents by month six, and a bunch of people who looked totally mediocre in month one ended up being my strongest agents later on.

My cofounder's take on both is just "the metric's bad, kill the dashboard" (he also likes to say I've read too much LessWrong and it's rotted my brain into overanalyzing a simple HR problem). And sure, the dashboard probably needs fixing either way, but that explanation feels like its flattening two pretty different things into one, and I can't quite articulate why not. I've read enough rationalist blog posts to already have "well, that's what happens once a number becomes a target" pattern-matched, so that answer alone won't satisfy me — it doesn't feel specific enough to tell me what's actually going on in each case, or whether the two things even share a fix. Are these actually the same underlying failure wearing two different costumes, or two genuinely different failures that just look similar from a distance? And practically: should I be doing something different about the ticket-splitting behavior than about how much weight I put on someone's first-month numbers when I'm deciding who to keep investing in?
