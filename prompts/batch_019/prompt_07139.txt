I'm one of three citizen members of my county's Board of Assessment Appeals, and next Tuesday we have to decide a property-tax case that has split the board more than any we've handled before. Under our state's Agricultural Tax Relief Act, land qualifies for the preferential "agricultural use" tax rate if it is "used for the keeping of bees for the production of honey," the owner maintains at least twenty-five hives, and the owner "derives income from the sale of honey or other bee products." A preamble added to the bill by floor amendment states that the Act exists "to preserve and encourage the bee populations on which the pollination of the state's fruit and vegetable crops depends."

The appellant keeps forty hives on his property. Like a growing number of small commercial beekeepers, most of what he earns from them — a little over $9,000 last year — comes from trucking hives to orchards each spring and charging growers pollination fees; he also sells honey from an honor-stand by his driveway, but that comes to only about $300 a year, mostly surplus he doesn't want left in the hives over winter. The assessor denied him the agricultural rate, reasoning that pollination fees are payment for a service, not income "from the sale of honey or other bee products," and that a driveway honey-stand can't be what the legislature meant by that phrase. One board member wants to uphold the denial: the legislature wrote a specific, checkable eligibility test rather than a general "does this serve the Act's goals" standard, presumably so the assessor's office wouldn't have to make case-by-case judgment calls about who really counts, and a preamble added by later amendment shouldn't be read as overriding that test. The other wants to reverse it: it would be strange for an Act whose stated purpose is protecting pollination-dependent beekeeping to deny relief to the one beekeeper whose hives most visibly do that work, merely because pollination, rather than honey, happens to be what he is paid for.

Could you draft the written opinion I'll circulate to my colleagues before the vote? I need it to reach one concrete disposition — grant the exemption, deny it, or remand to the assessor for further fact-finding, with specific instructions about what to determine and a deadline for reporting back — and to state the reasoning so that whichever colleague loses could recognize it as a genuine engagement with their view rather than a formality on the way to an outcome I had already chosen. Since this is apparently the first appeal to test what "derives income" means under the Act, I'd also like the opinion to address how much weight we should give to the fact that our reasoning will likely become the de facto standard the assessor's office applies to every apiary case afterward.
