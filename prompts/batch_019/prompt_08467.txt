Okay so I'm low-key spiraling about this, bear with me. I somehow became the volunteer "field lead" for a city council campaign this fall, first time doing real organizing, and its been way more unsettling than I expected. Twice this month I've softened an honest answer at someone's door because it wasn't "on message," and our strategy calls are basically a live demo of a rooms opinion congealing before anyone's actually thought anything through. My housemate (philosophy MA, insufferable timing) handed me Simone Weil's essay on abolishing political parties and said "yeah, this is you now" — her argument that a party is basically a machine for manufacturing collective passion, for squeezing every member's mind into line, and whose real goal underneath any stated platform is just its own unlimited growth, is hitting way too close to home. But I also don't see how a campaign, or a legislature, runs at all without something party-shaped doing the coordinating. Could you write a dialogue between a Weil-style abolitionist and a career party organizer — maybe stacking chairs after a phone-bank shift — where the organizer actually grapples with her specific three-part critique instead of just reciting "parties are how democracy gets things done," and the abolitionist has to say something concrete about what politics without parties would actually look like? Want both of them genuinely sharp, no strawmen.
