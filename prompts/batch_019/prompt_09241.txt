So I'm a retired physicist (condensed matter, mostly) who fell down a philosophy-of-mind rabbit hole a few months back after my daughter gave me Philip Goff's Galileo's Error for my birthday, and I havent quite recovered since. Panpsychism itself doesn't bother me all that much - physicists posit all sorts of weird intrinsic properties for matter, so "electrons have some primitive spark of experience" doesn't strike me as weirder than half of whats in a standard quantum mechanics textbook. What actually keeps me up at night is the combination problem: even if you grant each electron some itty-bitty flicker of experience, how do trillions of those flickers ever add up into the one unified, richly textured experience I'm having right now, typing this? Apparently William James was already worried about exactly this back in the 1890s with his "mind-dust" objection - his point being that a hundred little feelings sitting side by side don't obviously add up into one big feeling, any more than a hundred people crammed into a room automatically become a single collective person. And then there's a further wrinkle I've seen called the "boundary problem": why does my particular clump of neurons get to count as one subject, but not, say, my brain plus yours, or just my left hemisphere on its own? I even ran this by an old colleague from my department over email, and he told me to stick to solid-state physics and leave the "woo" to the philosophers - not helpful, but fair enough.

I'd love it if you wrote me a short story that actually sits inside this problem instead of just gesturing at it. It doesn't need to solve it - I don't think anyone has - but I want it to take seriously how strange, maybe even incoherent, full-blown "combination" might be, rather than doing the usual hive-mind thing where everyone politely agrees to share thoughts and it all works out fine. Setting's totally up to you: a neuroscience experiment that goes sideways, first contact with some alien species that's naturally a combination-organism, a far-future society where people can opt to merge minds, whatever grabs you. I just want whatever's going on at the physical or biological level, and the philosophical crux underneath it, to feel like they were built by someone who has actually wrestled with the subject-summing question, rather than someone who just borrowed the trope. Something like 1000-1500 words would be perfect, though don't sweat the exact count.
