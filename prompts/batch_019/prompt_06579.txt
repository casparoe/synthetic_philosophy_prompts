here's her answer:

"Locke believed that children are born as blank slates (tabula rasa) with no innate ideas, so everything about a person's knowledge and character comes from experience and habit rather than being built in. This came out of the letters of parenting advice Locke wrote about raising his own son, which he later published as Some Thoughts Concerning Education. Because the mind is blank, Locke thought harsh punishment like beating a kid for messing up a Latin lesson was a bad idea, since it just teaches the kid to associate learning with pain and fear instead of making them actually love virtue and knowledge. Instead he wanted parents to motivate kids mainly through esteem and shame, praising them when they behave and showing disappointment when they don't, since kids care what others think of them and will shape their habits around keeping people's good opinion. He also thought lessons should be turned into a kind of play instead of feeling like a chore, and that habit and practice matter way more than just reciting rules at a kid. And since Locke thought literally any mind starts out blank, his philosophy was actually pretty egalitarian for its time — he pushed for this same gentle, esteem-based method to be used in the charity schools that were being set up for poor kids in England, not just for rich families."
