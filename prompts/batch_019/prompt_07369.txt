Since retiring after a long career in field biology, I have recently been reading Ian Hacking on the "looping effects" of psychiatric classification: his observation that labeling someone autistic, or as suffering from multiple personality disorder, can itself change their behavior, self-understanding, and even how the diagnostic category is later revised — a feedback loop with no counterpart for what he calls "indifferent kinds," such as rock strata or chemical elements. This looks to me like a fairly direct argument that psychiatric diagnoses, unlike diagnoses in the rest of medicine, do not track anything objectively real about the patient; they are simply labels that, once applied, self-fulfillingly produce the very condition they claim to describe. Is that the right conclusion to draw from Hacking's account, or does his notion of looping effects leave room for these categories still to pick out something real?
