I spent my career in atmospheric chemistry, and since retiring I've gotten hooked on philosophy -- right now it's Simone Weil's essay on abolishing political parties. It's got me rethinking a fight on the governing council of my old professional society, where I'm an emeritus member. A caucus of younger members wants the bylaws changed to allow organized slates in council elections, caucusing beforehand and voting the ticket once in, to finally unseat an old guard thats run things informally for decades. Others say this is exactly the dynamic Weil warns about: it turns technical votes into loyalty tests and makes the caucus's own survival more important than the society's actual scientific mission. We vote on the amendment in three weeks. Could you draft the short memo I'll circulate to council, recommending adopt, reject, or amend, with reasons, and a fair hearing for the strongest case on whichever side we don't take?
