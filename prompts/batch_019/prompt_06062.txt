I work in software rather than philosophy, but I have spent a fair amount of my spare reading time on AI safety material, and there is one question from it I still cannot settle: the so-called shutdown problem. I would like an essay that reaches a real verdict rather than simply surveying the field.

The setup, as I understand it, is this. A goal-directed agent with a fixed objective has an instrumental reason to resist being switched off, since shutdown generally stops it from achieving that objective. Two fixes keep coming up in what I have read. One keeps the agent as an ordinary expected-utility maximizer with a single, complete preference ordering, but patches its utility function with a correction term so that its expected utility is equalized across the shutdown and non-shutdown branches of the future. The other abandons completeness altogether, so that trajectories involving shutdown and trajectories involving continued operation are simply never ranked against each other -- there is no preference to act on either way, and hence nothing to resist.

Each fix has a real cost. The indifference patch is meant to remove any incentive to influence whether the button gets pressed, but getting the correction term to actually achieve that, rather than merely balancing utility after the fact, turns out to be delicate, and known versions of it leave the agent with residual incentives to manage, by indirect means, the events that feed into the operator's decision. Incompleteness avoids that particular failure, but agents with incomplete preferences are exactly what standard decision-theoretic arguments say can be money-pumped -- traded into a strictly worse position for nothing in return -- unless they adopt some further rule for choosing among options they cannot rank, at which point the original problem may simply have been smuggled back in through that rule.

Write an essay that weighs these two strategies against each other and against the possibility that neither works: that genuine corrigibility (an agent that neither resists nor engineers its own shutdown) is incompatible with being a coherent, general-purpose goal pursuer. Do not just lay out the considerations on each side; reach a real verdict (a qualified one is acceptable) and defend it against the strongest version of the opposing case.
