In my AI alignment seminar this term, we've been working through different accounts of how a trained model's objective can end up diverging from what its designers intended. Two ideas that keep being used almost interchangeably in the readings are "inner alignment failure," cashed out via the mesa-optimization framework as a mismatch between a model's internal mesa-objective and the base objective it was trained on, and "goal misgeneralization," where a model's capabilities transfer to a new distribution but its goal does not, so that it competently pursues the wrong thing. I suspect these are not quite the same concept, since one seems to be defined mechanistically, in terms of an internal search process with its own objective, while the other is defined behaviorally, in terms of a gap between train- and test-time performance on the intended goal. Could you compare the two frameworks and clarify their relationship: does inner alignment failure imply goal misgeneralization, does the reverse hold, and could a model that is not a mesa-optimizer at all still exhibit goal misgeneralization? Please also say which of the two concepts is doing more real explanatory work if our aim is to anticipate whether a capable model will pursue an unintended goal after deployment.
