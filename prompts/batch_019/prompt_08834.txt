My brother and I were raised in the same devout household. I left the faith in my twenties, while he has only grown more committed, and a long, difficult conversation over the holidays convinced me that we were no longer disagreeing about facts so much as arguing past each other, since neither of us could locate a premise the other would accept. Looking for a way to make sense of that impasse, I came across Wittgenstein's On Certainty and the idea of "hinge propositions" — commitments that are not themselves known or justified, but form the exempt-from-doubt bedrock that makes ordinary doubting and reasoning possible at all. It struck me that his faith functions as exactly this kind of hinge for him, and that, on reflection, my own confidence that he is simply wrong functions the same way for me. Have I understood the concept correctly, and does Wittgenstein's account give us a principled way of showing that my bedrock commitments are the genuine hinges and his are just mistaken beliefs dressed up to look unshakeable?
