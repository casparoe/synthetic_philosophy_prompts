A case has come up in my department that, despite years of teaching ethics, I haven't been able to think my way through cleanly. Two of my teaching assistants, both conscientious and experienced, cut the same corner: pressed for time before a midterm, neither randomized the seating chart as our exam policy requires. In one section, nothing came of it; in the other, two students who happened to end up sitting together used the chance to copy off each other, and were caught. Our undergraduate committee now wants a formal warning placed in the second TA's file, while the first, who did exactly the same thing, faces nothing. I can't shake the feeling that this is unfair, since neither TA behaved any differently and which students ended up adjacent was pure chance — but a colleague on the committee insists that real harm actually occurred in one case and not the other, so treating them differently is simply appropriate. Which of us is thinking about this correctly, and why?
