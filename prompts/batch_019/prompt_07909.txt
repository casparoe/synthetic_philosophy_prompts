I'm a PhD student in the history of political thought, and I have been trying to reconcile what looks like a tension between two threads in Pascal's Pensées. In one fragment, he argues that justice unsupported by force is merely powerless -- flouted at will by the wicked -- so that any order stable enough to actually function has had to let what is strong pass for what is just. Elsewhere, though, he treats tyranny not as injustice in the ordinary sense but as a specific category error: using the means proper to one quality (force compels the body) to extract what is properly owed only to another (belief is owed to demonstrated intellect, love to personal charm). Give a concrete example of an institution or practice -- real or realistic -- that satisfies Pascal's first condition, justice made effective through adequate coercive force, while also thereby committing the cross-order usurpation he calls tyranny in the second. Then say whether this shows a genuine tension internal to Pascal's own political thought, or whether coercive force could in principle stay confined to its own order -- compelling outward compliance without reaching for belief or love -- and so escape the charge of tyranny altogether.
