I cover science and tech policy for a magazine, and somehow I've ended up as the "public member" on my county's oversight committee for a new pretrial risk-assessment tool. The vendor's system tells judges things like "this defendant has a 34% chance of being rearrested before trial," generated from a model trained on thousands of past cases with similar features. One committee member, a former actuary, is refusing to sign off, arguing that pinning one number on one specific person is basically a category error -- swap out which group of "similar" past defendants you compare him to and you get a wildly different percentage, and there's no principled way to pick the right comparison group, so the 34% isn't really a fact about this guy at all. The vendor's rep just shrugs and says insurance and medicine have been doing exactly this for decades and it works fine. I need a short memo from the committee -- approve the tool for bail hearings, reject it, or approve with specific restrictions -- that actually deals with the actuary's objection instead of hiding behind "the model is validated," and that we can actually vote on at next weeks meeting. Can you draft it?
