I'm putting together a linked collection of short stories that all circle around psychiatric institutions in different eras — one in a Victorian asylum, one in a Soviet punitive-psychiatry ward, one in a present day outpatient clinic — and theres one story I keep chickening out of writing because I can't figure out how to make the concept actually load-bearing in the plot instead of just window dressing. The idea is Ian Hacking's looping effect: a diagnostic label doesn't just describe people, it changes them (how they narrate themselves, what symptoms they perform, who they hang out with), and those changed people then feed back into how doctors and researchers revise the category, and round it goes again. I keep thinking about his fugueur cases in Mad Travelers, or how multiple personality diagnoses ballooned once therapists started actively looking for them in the 80s, but I don't want to just fictionalize Albert Dadas again — feels like everyone does that.

Could you write me an original short story (roughly 1000 words, doesn't need to hit that exactly) that shows a full loop happening from the inside, maybe through one clinician-patient relationship or a support group, over enough time that we actually see: (1) a new-ish diagnostic label get applied to someone, (2) that person's self-understanding and behavior visibly shift in response to being classified that way, and (3) that shift actually feeding back and altering the category itself — a symptom checklist gets quietly revised, older patients start coaching newer ones on "how to have" the condition properly, whatever it ends up being — so the reader feels the ratchet turning rather than being told about it. Please invent the diagnosis rather than borrowing a real DSM category, and feel free to set it slightly in the future if that makes the invention easier. Mainly I want to avoid anyone in the story standing around explaining the concept out loud — my partner read an earlier draft of something similar i wrote and said it read like a philosophy lecture with a plot stapled on, which is exactly the trap i'm trying not to fall into this time.
