So I write long-form science and ideas features for a living, and something my editor said this week has been rattling around in my head. My last piece had something like 120 individually fact-checked claims in it (dates, quotes, figures, the usual), and every one of them went through our normal verification process. I'm genuinely confident in each claim on its own, call it 98% confident if you forced me to put a number on it. My editor, who did a philosophy minor decades ago and never lets anyone forget it, did the math and pointed out that 0.98 multiplied out over 120 claims gives you something under 10%, meaning theres over a 90% chance that AT LEAST ONE of those 120 claims is actually wrong somewhere. So her point is that I should currently believe the piece contains an error, even though I can't tell you which claim it is and I believe every single individual one of them. That feels like she's asking me to hold a flat contradiction in my head (claim 1 is true, claim 2 is true, ... claim 120 is true, and also: not all of those are true), which cant be right, can it?

Weirdly, a friend who does data journalism said this reminded her of a fraud-detection project she covered, where an algorithm flagged 500 small businesses for probably committing loan fraud, each one at around 95% model confidence, based purely on statistical pattern-matching with no individual investigation. Everyone treated that as obviously different from a confirmed, individually-investigated case, even though mathematically it implies the exact same thing my editor is pointing out to me: that roughly 25 of those 500 flagged businesses are probably clean.

So now I'm stuck on two things: is my fact-checking situation actually the same underlying puzzle as the fraud-flagging one, just with better sourcing, or is there a real, principled difference between "I individually verified this and I'm 98% confident" and "the model says 95% confidence" that would justify treating them totally differently? And either way, does my editor's argument actually mean I'm rationally obligated to hedge the whole piece before it runs, or is something going wrong in how she's reasoning about it?
