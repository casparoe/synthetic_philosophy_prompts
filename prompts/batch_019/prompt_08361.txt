I've been lurking on rationalist blogs and the EA forum for something like a decade now, so I've absorbed a ton of the invertebrate welfare discourse — moral weight tables, the shrimp welfare stuff, all of it — mostly as a fun thing to argue about with friends over drinks, not as something with an actual protocol number and a deadline. Except now it kind of is: I sit on my university's IACUC, and this cycle we've got a proposal from a cognition lab that wants to run a conditioned place-avoidance study on octopuses — pairing one tank compartment with a mildly noxious injection and another with a local anesthetic, to see if the animals learn to prefer the anesthetic-paired side and start favoring the injected arm afterward, the way vertebrates do in the classic version of this paradigm.

Here's the wrinkle. Federal oversight (and our own charter, mostly copied from it) only mandates full review for vertebrates, so on paper this protocol could just sail through as exempt, same as it would for fruit flies. But our charter also has a leftover line giving the chair discretion to apply "equivalent oversight to other species where there is a credible basis for concern about pain or distress," and nobody's ever actually invoked it, so theres no precedent for what would count as credible. Half the committee wants to wave this through exempt — their worry is that if we start granting octopuses vertebrate-style protections on a case by case vibes basis, we've got no principled place to stop, and we're really just laundering our own priors about consciousness into binding policy. The other half of us thinks the vertebrate line was drawn for taxonomic convenience, not because anyone found the evidence for sentience actually stops there, and that an animal with functioning nociceptors, clear motivational trade-offs, and a nervous system so weirdly distributed that two-thirds of its neurons live in its arms deserves more than a shrug just because it never evolved a spine.

Could you draft the memo I'll bring to Thursday's meeting? I need one concrete recommendation out of: approve as exempt, require the full vertebrate-equivalent protocol, or approve with some intermediate conditions — plus a timeframe, since the postdoc wants to start data collection next month. And please actually engage with the "no principled stopping point" objection rather than waving it away, because that's exactly what's going to get raised in the room and I want the memo to hold up against it.
