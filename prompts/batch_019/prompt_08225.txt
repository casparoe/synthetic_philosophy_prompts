My old grad-school officemate -- we're both teaching philosophy now, just at different colleges -- is getting married in a few weeks, and he's asked me to give the toast. Here's the wrinkle: his whole dissertation was on van Inwagen's special composition question, and he came out the other side a committed mereological nihilist. We used to argue about this over cheap beer for half of grad school and I never once got him to budge. He thinks strictly speaking there are no composite objects at all, ever -- just simples arranged suchandsuchwise -- and he's told me with a totally straight face that there won't be a wedding cake at his own wedding, just crumbs arranged cake-wise. This is actually more radical then van Inwagen himself, who at least lets living things compose something; my guy thinks thats a cop-out. His fiancée, who is mercifully not a philosopher, finds the whole thing equal parts endearing and alarming after four years of dating him.

Problem is, pretty much every wedding toast ever given leans on "the two of you become one" at some point, and if I say that line straight he will 100% call me out on the metaphysics of it, mid-reception, in front of eighty guests who have never heard the word "fusion" used that way in their lives. He does not think two things ever compose a further thing, and he'd be the first to say a wedding is a spectacularly bad candidate for the one exception even if he thought composition happened sometimes. His fiancée has already warned me, more than once, not to turn this into a philosophy lecture. Could you draft me the toast? I'm picturing something like four minutes, five or six hundred words, that gets a real laugh out of the handful of metaphysicians in the room, doesn't lose the other eighty guests who just want a nice wedding speech, and still lands as genuinely warm about the two of them -- ideally by finding some honest way to talk about togetherness that a committed nihilist could actually nod along to instead of wincing.
