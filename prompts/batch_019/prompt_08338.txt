I'm sketching a subplot for my next novel where a grad student digs up Newton's unpublished theology papers - the real stuff on the Trinity, not the physics everyone already knows about. The detail that's hooked me: in a treatise called 'Two Notable Corruptions of Scripture,' Newton argued that 1 John 5:7 (the 'three heavenly witnesses' verse) was a fraud slipped into the text to prop up trinitarian doctrine, and he never dared publish it himself because being outed as antitrinitarian could've cost him everything at Cambridge. Thing is, modern textual critics basically agree with him now - its absent from every early greek manuscript, and most modern bibles footnote it as spurious. But that same historical-critical method also treats the resurrection, the prophecies in Daniel, and scripture's inspiration generally as just as fallible and human, which Newton never doubted for a second. So if you dropped him into a seminar with a Bart-Ehrman type today, would he feel vindicated or realize his own weapon had turned on everything else he actually believed? Doesn't need to be in his voice, i just want the actual argument he'd make to wriggle out of that corner, not a summary of 'his views on the trinity.'
