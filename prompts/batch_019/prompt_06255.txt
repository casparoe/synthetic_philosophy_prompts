A university research integrity committee is finalizing its findings on a fabrication case. A paper's three co-first authors jointly produced its central dataset. Per standard practice, every coauthor -- including these three -- signed a statement accepting accountability for the integrity of the whole work, not just the sections they personally wrote. One of the three has since admitted to fabricating part of that dataset; the committee's investigation confirms the other two never saw the raw files and had no reason for suspicion. Members are split: some want a misconduct finding against the fabricator alone, clearing the other two as uninvolved; others argue the joint authorship statement bound the three to a commitment the group failed, regardless of individual knowledge. Draft the committee's findings memo -- what each co-first author is responsible for, and what follows -- and engage seriously with the objection that a 'joint failure' here just relabels ordinary negligence in trusting a colleague.
