I spent thirty-five years running a solid-state physics lab before I retired, and these days I fill my Tuesday mornings with a little philosophy reading group at the local library, much to my kids' amusement. A couple of weeks ago we got onto free will and moral resposibility, and I hit a wall: it turns out there are two Strawsons who keep coming up, P.F. Strawson and his son Galen, and as far as I can tell they're either disagreeing badly or talking past each other, and nobody in the group could settle it before we ran out of time and coffee.

Here's what I've pieced together since then, for what its worth. P.F. Strawson's essay "Freedom and Resentment" argues that we should stop letting the metaphysical free-will debate hijack our thinking about blame and praise: our "reactive attitudes" -- resentment, gratitude, indignation, and the rest -- are so woven into ordinary human relationships that they'd survive pretty much intact even if hard determinism turned out to be true, so whether determinism is actually true starts to look almost beside the point. Then along comes Galen with what people call the Basic Argument, which seems to conclude that nobody can ever be ultimately responsible for anything they do, because being truly responsible for an action would require being responsible for the mental character that produced it, and that character was itself shaped, all the way back, by factors nobody chose -- so "true," desert-grounding responsibility is flatly impossible, whether determinism holds or not. One fellow in my group is convinced the son is quietly demolishing the father's whole framework; another insists they're actually answering two different questions and could both be right at the same time.

Could you help me sort this out the way I'd want a colleague to sort out a puzzling result in the lab? Lay out each view in its own terms first, then tell me straight: are P.F. and Galen Strawson genuinely in conflict, or does "responsibility" mean something different enough in each argument that they're just talking past one another? Please don't leave me with a wishy-washy "both sides have a point" -- pick a verdict and defend it with the strongest reasons you've got.
