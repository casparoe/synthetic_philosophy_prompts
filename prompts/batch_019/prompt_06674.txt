My mom died two weeks ago and Im giving her eulogy Saturday. She was a hospice nurse for 30 years, and there's one night early in her career she never got over: she'd promised a dying patient with no family that she'd personally be there when he went, and partway through her shift he started to slip at the exact moment another patient's husband collapsed in the hallway and she was the only one who could act. She couldn't do both, so she went to the husband, and the old man died alone. Whenever anyone later told her "you did the right thing, you've got nothing to feel bad about," she'd say that missed the point -- there hadn't been a right thing available, just two real obligations and not enough of her, and going to the husband didn't make the promise stop being real, it just meant she broke it. She told my sister flat out she didnt want anyone "absolving" her of it at the funeral. I need the actual eulogy, maybe 6 minutes, for a room that's half hospice/ICU people who'll know if I fake the hard part and half family who just want to hear she's at peace -- and it has to be honest that being stuck owing two people something real with no answer underneath was just how it was, not a puzzle she failed to solve.
