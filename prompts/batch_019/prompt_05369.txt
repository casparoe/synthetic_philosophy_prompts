I've been turning over the idea of moral luck: the puzzle that two people who act with identical intentions and identical care can end up judged very differently, purely because of how events happen to unfold. The standard case is two equally reckless drivers, only one of whom happens to hit a pedestrian. The obvious fix, it seems to me, is to stop grading actions by their results and judge people solely by what was genuinely within their control — their intentions and effort — since that is the only part of the situation that is truly theirs. Once that adjustment is made, the whole puzzle seems to dissolve. Am I missing something, or have philosophers been making this harder than it needs to be?
