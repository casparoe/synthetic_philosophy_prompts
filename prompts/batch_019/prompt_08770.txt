A friend of mine and I attended the same company retreat last weekend, and spent most of the drive home arguing about something that happened there. On the first evening, the senior executive who organized the retreat arranged for the team to drink fairly heavily together, and defended the exercise as more than just a party: he argued that people's genuine character comes out once everyone has had enough to drink to stop performing for each other, and that doing this periodically doesn't merely reveal who has good judgment and self-command but actually strengthens those qualities over time, in much the way that repeatedly facing a manageable danger strengthens courage. My friend found this fairly persuasive; I didn't. It seems to me that how a person behaves while drunk is a different, chemically altered performance rather than an unmasking of some deeper truth about them, and I don't see what transferable capacity is supposedly being built, in the way that deliberately facing danger obviously builds something you can draw on later. We went back and forth for an hour without either of us budging. Who had the stronger case here, and does the executive's reasoning actually hold up under scrutiny?
