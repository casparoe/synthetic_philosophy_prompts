A few weeks ago my partner's company paid out an unusually large year-end bonus, and deciding what to do with it has turned into the most drawn-out disagreement we've had in years. My instinct was to put a substantial portion toward my younger sister, who lost her job earlier this year and is behind on a medical bill, and to set some aside for both sets of aging parents. My partner, who has become quite involved with an effective-giving group at work over the past year, wants to send nearly all of it instead to a charity whose published cost-effectiveness figures suggest it saves roughly one life for every few thousand dollars spent on basic medical supplies overseas.

What has actually kept the argument going, though, isn't the spreadsheet of estimates but a further point she keeps returning to. She notes that the pull I feel toward my own sister and parents is exactly what you'd expect biology to produce: ancestors who favored close kin over strangers left more descendants, so the feeling functions as inherited machinery for propagating genes rather than as a perception of who truly deserves help more. The idea that a stranger's suffering matters just as much as my sister's, she argues, is not something natural selection would ever have favored, since acting on it tends to work against one's own reproductive interests. So on the rare occasions we do arrive at that idea through calm, reasoned reflection, she thinks it is more likely to be tracking something real, rather than being one more evolutionary reflex like the family feeling is. She takes this difference to settle the matter: the family-favoring pull should give way whenever it conflicts with the impartial calculation, not the other way around.

I don't have any background in philosophy, so I may well be missing something obvious, but I haven't been able to explain why this still doesn't sit right with me, especially since I can't honestly deny that my attachment to my sister probably does have exactly the history she describes. I would like to know whether there is a genuine problem with an argument of this shape, or whether she is basically correct that a feeling forfeits any claim on me once I know it was installed in me for reasons that have nothing to do with what is actually right. And if the argument does fail, I would especially like to understand exactly where.
