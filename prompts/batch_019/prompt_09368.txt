So I'm about two-thirds through a hard-SF novel and I've hit a snag that's actually load-bearing for the plot, not just flavour text. My protagonist, a disgraced topologist, builds a device that uses the causal structure inside a rotating (Kerr) black hole so that one worldline can receive, in a finite stretch of its own proper time, the full signal record of another worldline's infinite future -- a "Malament-Hogarth" setup, basically -- and she uses it to settle a halting-problem-style question about a Diophantine equation that's been open for decades. Two people in my writers group say this is cheating, that it just breaks the Church-Turing thesis and is magic dressed up as physics, and the more I've tried to figure out if they're right the less sure I am what's actually at stake.

Here's what's got me stuck: some of what I found while researching this (there's a guy named Gandy who apparently tried to derive something like the thesis from pretty minimal physical assumtions -- bounded signal speed, finite information density per region of space, that sort of thing) makes it sound like the thesis is basically a theorem in disguise, so no physical setup could ever violate it, full stop. Other stuff treats the Church-Turing thesis as a genuinely open empirical claim about our universe, the kind of thing a weird enough spacetime really could refute, same as a new particle can refute a conservation law. And then a third camp seems to think "compute" doesn't pick out anything a machine could get right or wrong about in the first place, so my heroine's device wouldn't be refuting anything -- the word would just have quietly grown a new sense, and the two camps arguing about it would really just be talking past each other.

Could you write me something like a short essay on this -- not a survey, I want the three positions actually weighed against each other and then a landing spot -- that tells me whether a device like hers should count as "impossible in principle," "wildly improbable but not incoherent," or "not even a meaningful violation of anything"? Totally fine if the honest answer is "it depends," as long as you're precise about what it depends on. I just don't want the kind of both-sides shrug I could've written myself after an afternoon on wikipedia.
