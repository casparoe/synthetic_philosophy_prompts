His response, predictably, was "ok but what would Aristotle say about my liquidity mining position."

So here's what I actually want from you -- and please don't hand me the "Aristotle vs. capitalism" essay that's already been written a thousand times. Does the tokos argument even survive getting pushed into a case where there's no one on the other end of the "loan" for him to be disgusted with, or does that absence actually make it a purer, even more contemptible bit of chrematistike by his own lights? And does the nomisma stuff -- money existing by convention rather then nature, which is exactly why he thinks it can be "rendered useless" by a change in law or custom -- give him any extra angle on a currency that isnt backed by any state at all? Be honest about where his framework actually strains or just runs out of concepts to handle this, instead of smoothing it into something tidy.
