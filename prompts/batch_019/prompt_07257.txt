Ok this is a bit of a weird one but bear with me. I fell down a Descartes rabbit hole this weekend and learned that part of his case for animals being mindless automata (no real thoughts, no soul, nothing to feel bad about hurting) rests on a test he lays out in the Discourse on Method: a real thinking thing can use language flexibly to respond to whatever you say to it, on basically any topic, the same way even the dullest human can, whereas a mere machine (or an animal, in his view) can only react in fixed ways triggered by specific inputs. Animals definately fail that test for him, so no reason, so no mind. People point out this is basically a proto-Turing-test, centuries before Turing's actual paper. Now that LLMs clearly pass the "flexible conversation about anything" part of his test, I'm curious what you think Descartes would actually conclude if he sat down and used one. Would his own logic force him to grant it some kind of mind while still denying one to his dog, and if so does that expose a real crack in how he reasoned about animals in the first place -- or is there a consistent way for him to deny minds to both?
