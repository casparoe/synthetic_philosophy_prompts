ok so we're covering standing to blame this week in my moral responsibility seminar, and it reminded me of an argument that happened at Thanksgiving that I still haven't been able to settle in my head. My friend Leo skipped his sister's engagement party back in the spring because he had a final exam the next morning, which everyone agreed was pretty understandable at the time. Fast forward to last month: his roommate Theo skipped Leo's birthday dinner to study for the bar exam instead, and Leo was genuinely annoyed, telling him it was a selfish thing to do. When Leo told this story at Thanksgiving like it was obviously a fair thing for him to have said, my friend Ana wasn't having it, and the two of them went back and forth for like twenty minutes while the rest of us just watched.

(1) Ana said Leo has zero standing to call Theo selfish, since he did the literal same thing to his own sister a few months earlier. (2) Leo said standing doesn't make a claim untrue — Theo's choice can still just be a selfish one no matter who's pointing it out, hypocrisy is a fact about the messenger and not the message, so Ana isn't actually engaging with whether he's right. (3) Ana said that's missing the point: the issue isn't whether it's true, it's that Leo is holding Theo to a standard he lets himself off of, and that double standard is its own seperate wrong on top of whatever Theo did. (4) Leo said he isn't letting himself off anything — he felt so bad about the party that he apologized to his sister for weeks afterward, so there's no double standard, plus the two situations aren't even comparable: his exam was the next morning and non-negotiable, while Theo's bar exam was three weeks out and he could've studied literally any other night. (5) Ana said the apology thing is a little suspicious (if he really thought skipping was fine, why apologize for weeks?), and also that he's dodging: whether the two situations are factually alike is a totally different question from whether he had standing to say what he said in the first place.
