My sister is getting married next month, and I've been asked to give the toast at the reception. For the past dozen years I've co-owned a hardware store with my best friend, and somewhere along the way I got curious about why decisions we make "as the store" feel different from decisions we each simply happen to agree on personally, which eventually led me to read on my own about what philosophers call joint commitment. The idea that stuck with me, if I've understood it correctly, is Margaret Gilbert's: that once two people jointly commit to something, each one gains real standing to call the other back to it, and neither person can quietly let themselves off the hook alone, so the commitment holds even on the days the feeling behind it doesn't. I want the toast to actually rest on that point instead of the usual line about hoping they always feel as in love as they do tonight, but her fiancée is finishing a philosophy PhD and a good part of the room will be her cohort, so I can't afford to get the idea wrong, while my side of the family has never once heard the phrase "joint commitment" and shouldn't need to. Could you write me something short enough to deliver in three or four minutes that gets this right for both halves of the room, with at least one moment the philosophers can quietly enjoy without anyone else feeling left out of it?
