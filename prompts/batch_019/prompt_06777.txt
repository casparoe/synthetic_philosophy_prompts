I'm a software engineer at a small quantum computing startup, and I've just been added to the small internal panel that signs off on technical claims in our investor and press materials before they go out. Marketing wants our next pitch deck to explain our processor's speedup by saying it "checks every possible answer at once by running the computation in parallel across countless other universes," following physicists like David Deutsch, who has argued that quantum computing is itself evidence for many-worlds. Our lead hardware engineer objects that this quietly treats one contested interpretation of quantum mechanics (Everett's) as settled fact: Copenhagen and Bohmian mechanics recover the very same predictions without positing other universes, and the actual source of the speedup — interference between probability amplitudes — doesn't obviously require any of the three interpretations to be true. Write the panel's decision memo on whether this language can go out before next week's investor meetings — approve it, reject it, or approve a modified version — with a concrete recommendation and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration against it.
