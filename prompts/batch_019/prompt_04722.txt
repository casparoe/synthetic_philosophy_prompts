I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A after a talk by a Pascal scholar who, whenever pressed, insists that Pascal's fragments on justice and force are 'diagnostic, not normative' -- that he is describing how any fallen political order manages to hold together, not licensing the arrangement or counseling obedience to it. Draft eight to ten questions that make that deflection progressively harder to sustain. Include at least one question on the fragment where Pascal says that, unable to make what is just strong, we made what is strong count as just, and on whether this is compatible with calling the fragment merely descriptive when it doubles as a claim that authority rests on a fiction its founders know to be false; one on how his mockery of a justice that flips at a river or a mountain range fits with his own claim elsewhere that a true justice exists, inaccessible to us, in the order of charity; and one that weighs Pascal's own hardline refusal to compromise in the Jansenist formulary dispute -- where he broke with allies at Port-Royal willing to sign under a face-saving distinction -- against a quietist reading of these fragments as counseling obedience to established authority as such. For each, add a follow-up keyed specifically to the 'diagnostic, not normative' response she is likely to give.
