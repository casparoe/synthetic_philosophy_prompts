At work I help maintain the slur/toxicity filter for our app's chat, and two things about it keep bugging me: it flags words that are totally fine when the community that "owns" the word is using it on each other, and separately, our mod-report tool makes reviewers actually type out the slur when logging why someone got banned, which legal keeps flagging even though the report itself isn't insulting anybody. Went down a rabbit hole trying to figure out why this is so hard to get right, and it turns out there's a genuine philosophical fight over what a slur even is. One camp (Anderson and Lepore, I think?) basically says the word has no special nasty meaning built into it at all - its just a word that's socially banned, like a taboo token, and the offense comes purely from breaking that ban regardless of context. The other camp, "expressivism" (I keep seeing a philosopher named Jeshion cited for this), says no - using the word itself directly expresses contempt for the group every time, kind of like how "damn" expresses frustration rather than describing anything. Can you lay out both views properly, compare them, and then actually tell me which one better explains (1) why reclaimed in-group use isn't offensive and (2) why just quoting the word to report it still feels taboo even though nobody's attitude is being expressed toward anyone? Give me a real verdict and justify it, not a "both sides have a point" dodge.
