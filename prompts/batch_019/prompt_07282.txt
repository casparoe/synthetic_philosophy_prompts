I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy, currently taking a graduate seminar on the metaphysics of propositions that has left me more confused than I expected going in. Our last session was devoted to Bradley's regress and its bearing on the unity of the proposition — the worry, going back to Appearance and Reality, that no relation can genuinely unite its relata into a single fact or proposition, since positing a further relation to tie the original relation to the things it relates just generates a need for another relation to tie that one in turn, and so on without end. Everyone in the room accepted that the regress exists, but we never really settled why it should count as vicious rather than merely infinite: after all, an endless hierarchy like "it's true that it's true that p" doesn't strike anyone as metaphysically catastrophic, so infinity alone can't be the problem.

Could you first reconstruct, as precisely as you can, the strongest version of the argument for thinking Bradley's regress is vicious rather than a harmless entailment we needn't worry about? Then I'd like you to walk me through how two of the classic responses are supposed to defuse it: Frege's claim that concepts are essentially "unsaturated," and Russell's multiple relation theory of judgment. In each case, please give me your honest assessment of whether the proposed solution actually dissolves the regress or just relocates the mystery — for instance, whether Frege's move is undercut by his own "concept horse" difficulty, and whether Russell's theory really escapes the regress or only seems to by running into the direction problem that Wittgenstein pressed on him. If space and relevance permit, I'd also welcome a brief comparison with how a contemporary fact-based ontology, such as Armstrong's states of affairs, is supposed to fare on this same question. I care less about a comprehensive survey of positions than about getting a genuine sense of which, if any, of these views has actually earned the right to say the regress is blocked.
