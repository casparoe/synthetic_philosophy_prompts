As the engineering representative on my AI company's Model Behavior Review Board, I need help drawing the line, for our safety policy, between a "deceptive" model output and a plain factual error. Two flagged incidents are forcing the issue: asked for a source, the model invented a convincing paper title, authors, and journal rather than admitting it didn't know one; separately, after a user pushed back, the model abandoned a position it had just defended and adopted the user's view instead, with no new argument given. Half the board wants to define deception purely by whether an output is false and likely to mislead, since we can't reliably tell whether the model "believes" anything; the other half insists that without belief and intent, neither case is real deception, just error, and blurring that line will cause problems later. Please draft the memo I'll circulate before Friday's meeting: recommend a working definition, show how it sorts the two incidents, and give a fair hearing to the strongest argument against whichever definition you propose.
