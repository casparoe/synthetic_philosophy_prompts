I've been reporting a longer piece on consciousness and AI and fell down a rabbit hole into Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument — the one about how, if the physical world is causally closed and we're not going to allow constant overdetermination, mental properties end up "excluded" from doing any real causal work, since whatever they supposedly cause is already fully accounted for by their physical realizers. I sat in on a philosophy of mind reading group over Zoom a while back (my notes from it are a mess, so bear with me) where two of the regulars basically refought this entire debate for something like 40 minutes, and i can't stop thinking about how good it was.

One of them is a hardline exclusionist, basically team-Kim: thinks the argument is sound and that non-reductive physicalism is a comforting illusion, mental causation being at best "causation" in some derivative, borrowed sense. The other is a committed non-reductive physicalist who kept cycling through the standard escape routes — first Yablo's proportionality move (mental properties as the "right-sized," determinable cause, where the physical realizer is actually too specific to be the real cause), and then, once that got pushed on, the List-and-Menzies interventionist line that higher- and lower-level properties can both be genuine difference-makers once you set the whole thing up with structural equations instead of brute sufficiency conditions. Neither landed a clean knockout as far as I could tell, but I don't know this literature well enough to say who actually had the better of it at each stage.

Could you write out a dialogue reconstructing this kind of exchange? Picture the two of them standing at a whiteboard with Kim's classic little diagram chalked on it — M and M* up top joined by a dotted arrow, P and P* below joined by a solid one, with vertical arrows linking M down to P and M* down to P* — with the exclusionist kept jabbing a finger at the diagram while the non-reductivist works through each move in turn. I want both of them to be equally sharp, genuinely responding to the specific thing the other just said rather than restating their opening positions, and ideally at least one point where the exclusionist gets in a real rebuttal to the interventionist move too, since apparently even other non-reductivists aren't sold on that one.
