Quick philosophy question for a subplot I'm working on. My protagonist spends two years writing letters to who she thinks are two different people, "Nadia" and "Milo" -- then around the midpoint she finds out they're actually the same person, just writing to her under two names. I went down a rabbit hole reading about Frege's old puzzle about Hesperus and Phosphorus, and it sounds like Kripke basically closed the case decades ago by showing names are just rigid designators with no hidden "sense" attached -- so once you know "Nadia" and "Milo" pick out the same guy, theres nothing left to be surprised about, its really just an "a=a" statement wearing a trench coat. Given that, should I even bother writing my protagonist's realization as a big gut-punch reveal, or would a philosopher say that reaction is kind of unjustified now that we actually know how names work?
