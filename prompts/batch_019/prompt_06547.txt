I'm a retired marine biologist, and only in the last couple of years have I properly gotten into philosophy (my daughter keeps mailing me books, bless her). Something from my working life has been nagging at me in a new way. Back in the 90s, an oyster bed I'd studied for a decade collapsed the same summer a fertilizer plant upstream had a bad ammonia leak. Completely seperately, an invasive flatworm turned up in the same bay in huge numbers that same year. Our modeling afterwards showed that either one alone, at the levels recorded, would have wiped out the bed regardless of the other. When the town tried to make the company pay for restoration, their lawyers argued that since the bed was doomed anyway because of the flatworms, the leak wasnt really 'a cause' of the collapse, so they owed nothing. Everyone on our side felt like that was obviously a dodge, but nobody could say exactly why, and honestly neither can I, all these years later. Was the company actually right?
