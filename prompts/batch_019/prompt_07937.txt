I serve as a volunteer board member for a small nonprofit that runs an after-school resilience-training program for at-risk middle schoolers in my town, and the vote on renewing its $85,000 in annual funding is in eight weeks. After watching the power-pose study and a few other well-known psychology findings fail to replicate, I spent the past year teaching myself the basics of the dispute between traditional significance testing and Bayesian statistics, and it turns out to bear directly on an argument we're having on the board.

Our program director's evaluation found a statistically significant drop in disciplinary referrals (p = 0.045) among the 64 enrolled kids, but she had checked the data at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months and stopped once that number cleared the bar; three other outcomes she tracked -- grades, attendance, a wellbeing survey -- showed nothing. One board member who knows a bit of statistics ran a Bayes factor on the referral result and got about 2, which she says barely counts as evidence either way. Another member argues that insisting on a cleaner, preregistered replication before the vote will simply defund something that is helping kids right now.

Please draft the short memo I'll circulate before the vote: it should give a concrete recommendation -- renew, renew with conditions, or pause pending replication -- with a timeline, and it should take seriously the strongest argument against whichever option you pick, rather than just declaring the other side's statistics bad.
