So I went down a rabbit hole on the whole Galileo vs the church thing, and it's way less "science guy vs dumb priests" than they make it sound in school. From what I can peice together, Galileo's actual argument in that letter to the Grand Duchess Christina is basically: scripture doesn't have to be read literally about nature once something is actually proven, and he's borrowing that move pretty directly from Augustine. But then Bellarmine's reply to Foscarini is basically "sure, if you had a real proof I'd agree we'd have to go back and reinterpret Genesis, but you don't, you've just got a hypothesis that fits the data so far." And when I look at what evidence Galileo actually had going in 1615 (moons of Jupiter, phases of Venus, that kind of thing), it seems like all of that also fits Tycho Brahe's setup where the planets circle the sun but the sun still circles the earth, so it wasn't really proof of full heliocentrism either. Even the tides thing Galileo came up with later to try to nail it down turned out to be wrong anyway.

So what I actually want from you is an argument, not a "weigh both sides" essay: using Galileo's own rule about when scripture is allowed to be reinterpreted, make the strongest case you can that Bellarmine was actually the more careful and consistent one in 1615, and that Galileo hadn't earned the right to reinterpret Genesis yet by his own standard.
