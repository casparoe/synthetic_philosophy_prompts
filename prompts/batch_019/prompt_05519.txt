I'm a PhD student in philosophy. My dissertation advisor, under whom I studied for the last four years, died three weeks ago, and I've been asked to deliver her eulogy. Her career was largely devoted to defending moral realism against evolutionary debunking arguments, and she was best known for a reply to Sharon Street's Darwinian Dilemma: rather than accept either horn of it -- that natural selection somehow tracked independent evaluative truths, or that our getting them right was a wild coincidence -- she argued for a third factor that explains both why creatures like us evolved certain moral attitudes and why those attitudes are correct. The memorial will seat metaethicists who spent decades arguing with her about exactly this next to family members who have never heard of Sharon Street. She once told me, half-joking, that she didn't want to be sent off with language that made her decency sound like a lucky side effect of selection pressure rather than something she had actually gotten right. Please write the eulogy: it should move the non-philosophers in the room, satisfy the specialists, and not concede, even for warmth's sake, the picture of moral psychology she spent her career resisting.
