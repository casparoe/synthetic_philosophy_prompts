So a few weeks back I went down a total rabbit hole reading about Gottlob Frege -- the guy who basically invented modern formal logic and came up with the sense/reference distinction -- and I stumbled onto something that kind of rattled me. Turns out that in 1924, the year before he died, he kept a private diary that only surfaced and got published decades later, and it's genuinely ugly stuff: ranting that Jews have too much political and cultural influence and shouldn't have full political rights, contempt for the Weimar Republic and parliamentary democracy and the socialists, wanting some kind of strong "leader" to come sort things out. From what I read, even Michael Dummett -- who I gather pretty much single-handedly convinced English-speaking philosophy that Frege was one of the great philosophers of all time -- was really shaken when this came out, though he still insisted it didn't change the value of the logic itself.

That last bit is what I cant stop chewing on. Frege built his whole reputation on the idea that logical and mathematical truth is completely objective and mind-independent, on his attack on "psychologism," on this idea of thoughts existing in some timeless "third realm" outside both the physical world and any one person's head. Is there actually some real connection between that rigid, hierarchical picture of truth and the politics, or is it just two separate facts about one unpleasant guy that I'm forcing into a story because it makes such a satisfying narrative? Could you write me a dialogue between two people who both know Frege's actual technical philosophy cold? One of them thinks the diary is sad but has zero bearing on judging the Begriffsschrift or the logicist project, totally beside the point philosophically, and the other thinks that's too quick and keeps pushing on where a real connection might be hiding. I want them to genuinely engage each others strongest points instead of just repeating themselves -- neither one should be a pushover, and honestly it's fine if it doesn't end with some neat resolution.
