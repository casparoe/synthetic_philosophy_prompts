Here is the submission in full:

"Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen's 1935 paper argued that quantum mechanics is incomplete. They considered a pair of particles prepared in a correlated state such that measuring one lets us predict with certainty the outcome of the corresponding measurement on the other, however far away it is. Since, by their locality condition, no real change can be induced in a distant system by a measurement performed here, they concluded that the particle must already have possessed a definite value for that quantity prior to measurement — an 'element of reality' not captured by the quantum state. Bell's contribution was to ask whether any theory that respects both this locality condition and this kind of realism (definite values existing independently of measurement) could reproduce all of quantum mechanics' statistical predictions.

Bell, and later Clauser, Horne, Shimony, and Holt with the more experimentally practical CHSH version, derived an inequality that any such local-realistic theory must satisfy: a particular combination of correlations between measurement outcomes on the two particles, averaged over settings, cannot exceed 2. Quantum mechanics predicts that this same combination can reach 2√2 for suitably entangled states and suitably chosen measurement settings, and this prediction was confirmed experimentally, first by Aspect and collaborators in 1981–82 and then, with the remaining loopholes closed, by several independent groups in 2015.

The proper conclusion is that no theory which is both local and realist, in the senses just described, can reproduce the quantum statistics — and this is usually taken to mean that hidden variables themselves are the casualty, with the experiments effectively vindicating the standard indeterministic reading of the quantum state. This is also why Bohmian mechanics, notwithstanding the ingenuity of using a guiding equation to restore trajectories to quantum particles, is not a serious rival to orthodox quantum mechanics: because that guiding equation makes the velocity of each particle depend instantaneously on the positions of every other entangled particle in the universe, it is a hidden-variable theory of exactly the sort the Bell/CHSH inequality was constructed to rule out.

A further point worth flagging is that the violation is not merely a statistical curiosity: it reflects a real physical influence of the measurement on one particle upon the state of its distant partner, transmitted instantaneously regardless of the separation between them. This does not yet allow faster-than-light communication in practice, since existing experiments only reveal the correlation after the fact by comparing records from both wings of the experiment, but it remains a live question among physicists whether a sufficiently clever protocol could exploit this instantaneous influence to send a signal, and much of the continuing interest in entanglement within quantum information theory stems from probing exactly this possibility."
