I was just appointed the teacher representative on my state's instructional-materials review committee, and my first case is a dispute over the U.S. history textbook's unit on the Constitution and slavery. The current draft quotes only the young Frederick Douglass, who through the 1840s agreed with William Lloyd Garrison that the Constitution was "a covenant with death, and an agreement with hell." Two members want it revised to quote the older Douglass instead, who broke with Garrison in the early 1850s and later declared that "interpreted as it ought to be interpreted, the Constitution is a glorious liberty document." Others worry that foregrounding the later quote settles, rather than reports, a contested question of interpretation, especially since it echoes the argument some originalists now make that the Constitution was antislavery from the start. Please draft the memo I'll bring to next Tuesday's vote: approve the passage as written, reject it, or approve it with specific required revisions, giving reasons the textbook's authors could actually act on and taking seriously whichever side's strongest argument you don't adopt.
