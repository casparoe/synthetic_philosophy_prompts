I sit on the bioethics advisory committee for a fertility clinic that now gives IVF patients a polygenic report ranking their viable embryos by a composite score blending disease-risk variants (diabetes, several cancers) with softer predictors like educational attainment. Current policy discloses the ranking but leaves the transfer choice to the couple. One member wants us to default to transferring the top-ranked embryo, absent medical contraindication, on the grounds that knowingly transferring a lower-ranked one instead harms whichever child results. A second member disputes "harms": the child born from the lower-ranked embryo is not a worse-off version of the child who would have come from the top-ranked one; they are distinct, non-substitutable people, so no one's interests are set back relative to any alternative actually available to them. She admits, though, that this does not settle what the clinic should do. Please draft the short opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting, recommending disclosure-only, default-with-override, or mandatory top-ranked transfer, and engage honestly with the strongest argument against whichever option you choose.
