I'm a PhD student in philosophy working mainly on philosophy of language and metaethics, and this term I've also been TAing our department's metaethics seminar, which is how I ended up volunteering to moderate the Q&A after next month's colloquium talk. The invited speaker defends a hybrid expressivist solution to the Frege-Geach problem: on her view, every normative sentence has an ordinary descriptive content -- something like "fits the norms the speaker currently accepts" -- and it is this descriptive component that carries the full compositional burden in unasserted contexts such as negation, conditionals, and disjunction, while the sentence's distinctively normative flavor comes from a further conative attitude that only gets expressed when the sentence is used to make an outright assertion, not when it is embedded. Her stock answer to embedding worries is that Geach's puzzle only ever afflicted "pure" expressivists who tried to make the attitude itself carry the logical weight, and that dividing the labor this way dissolves the problem rather than just managing it.

I'd like the Q&A to press on that division of labor rather than let her simply restate it, since my own sense from the literature is that hybrid views buy their solution to embedding at a real cost elsewhere. Could you draft eight or nine questions, each with a short follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, covering at least: (1) whether putting the logical burden entirely in the descriptive component actually solves the negation problem -- the difficulty, pressed by Unwin and later sharpened by Schroeder, of explaining why "not wrong" is inconsistent with "wrong" rather than merely expressing its absence -- or just relocates that difficulty into the choice of descriptive property; (2) the worry that if the descriptive half already suffices to fix truth conditions and validity, the attitude becomes explanatorily idle, a decorative add-on that could be stripped from the semantics at no cost, in the way critics have pressed against Ridge-style "ecumenical" expressivism; (3) whether the view can still deliver the motivational-internalism payoff that made expressivism attractive in the first place, given that on her account one could in principle grasp the descriptive proposition while entirely lacking the attitude, which sounds very close to an amoralist who has lost nothing propositional; and (4) how she means to avoid the "creeping minimalism" charge -- that once the descriptive component is doing this much work, there may be no daylight left between her semantics and an ordinary naturalist cognitivism that simply declines to posit any expressed attitude at all. For the last question in the sequence, I'd like something that forces her to say what, exactly, would be lost -- semantically or normatively -- if the expressive layer were deleted wholesale.
