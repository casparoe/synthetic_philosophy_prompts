Dialetheists standardly motivate paraconsistent logics such as Priest's LP by observing that these logics can tolerate the contradiction generated by the Liar sentence without trivializing, since explosion (ex contradictione quodlibet) is invalid. Yet Curry's paradox is often held to pose a substantially harder problem for the same project, even though its derivation never invokes negation or an explicit contradiction of the form A and not-A. Please explain, at a fairly technical level, why a logic's being paraconsistent does nothing by itself to block the trivializing Curry derivation, and identify the further structural rule a dialetheist typically has to restrict, independently of anything concerning negation, in order to stop it. I'm interested in the underlying proof-theoretic reason for this asymmetry between the two paradoxes, rather than just a restatement of what Curry's paradox is.
