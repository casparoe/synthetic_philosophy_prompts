I am writing a novel in which Thomas Hobbes is brought back to witness the present day, and a pivotal scene needs to rest on genuinely Hobbesian reasoning rather than a modern paraphrase of it. Show him two puzzles: a leaderless protest movement that coordinates entirely through anonymous, encrypted channels, with no spokesperson, and a decentralized autonomous organization whose outputs are just the automatic tally of token-weighted votes, with no officer able to answer for the others. Drawing on his account, in Leviathan, of persons, authors, and actors—where a multitude becomes one person only through representation by someone whose authority each member has actually made his own—would he grant either group the standing of a genuine unified agent, or insist both remain a mere multitude of wills? Reconstruct the reasoning he would actually give, faithful to his own premises rather than to Gilbert's, Bratman's, or List and Pettit's accounts of group agency, in about 300 words.
