I cover philosophy and cognitive science for a science magazine, and I recently spent an afternoon at a small workshop on philosophy of language watching two speakers disagree, more sharply than I expected, over a distinction I had always assumed was settled: whether Quine's indeterminacy of translation is genuinely a different, stronger thesis than the ordinary underdetermination of scientific theory by evidence — the point associated with Duhem and usually folded into the "Duhem-Quine thesis" — or whether, looked at closely, it just is that thesis, dressed up in the more vivid language of rabbits and "gavagai." Quine himself insisted the two come apart: underdetermination, on his account, still leaves a single determinate physical world that our best theory simply fails to pin down uniquely, whereas indeterminacy of translation is supposed to mean something stranger — that there is no fact of the matter, not even one fixed by the totality of physical facts about the speaker, as to whether "gavagai" really means rabbit, undetached rabbit part, or rabbit stage.

I'd like to get this right for a longer piece rather than settle for the version that shows up in most popular summaries of Quine. Could you compare the two theses in some depth: reconstruct the argument Quine actually gives for treating indeterminacy as the stronger claim, and spell out what work his physicalism and his behaviorism about meaning are doing to generate that extra strength? I'm especially interested in your take on an objection one of the workshop speakers pressed — that the "no fact of the matter" language does no independent argumentative work, and that the apparent gap between indeterminacy and underdetermination is really just a restatement of Quine's separate, prior skepticism that "meaning" names a legitimate scientific posit at all. Does that objection succeed, in your judgment, or does Quine have the resources to resist it? Please write with enough precision that a specialist wouldn't object, but bear in mind that the eventual piece is for general readers, so flag along the way which moves in the argument actually bear on the stakes and which are just technical bookkeeping.
