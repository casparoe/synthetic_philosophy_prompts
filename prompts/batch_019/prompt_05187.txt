I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and next week I'm interviewing a philosopher who has made her name reviving P. F. Strawson's reactive-attitudes account of moral responsibility against the current wave of popular neuroscience-and-free-will books, Sapolsky's Determined chief among them. Her position, following "Freedom and Resentment," is that resentment, gratitude, guilt and their kin are not verdicts awaiting confirmation from physics; they're built into interpersonal life in a way the mere truth of determinism couldn't unseat.

Draft eight to ten interview questions that push past a restatement of that thesis. I want one question that presses the gap between "we can't help having these attitudes" and "these attitudes are therefore justified," with a follow-up ready for the likely retreat to dismissing further demands for grounding as mere "panicky metaphysics"; one that turns the excuse/exemption distinction back on her by asking how much we can learn about an offender's formative history before exemption threatens to swallow the rule; and one built on Gary Watson's problem of the exceptionally wicked, where indignation and the pitying "objective attitude" toward the same person seem to survive side by side rather than trade off cleanly. Give each question its own brief follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
