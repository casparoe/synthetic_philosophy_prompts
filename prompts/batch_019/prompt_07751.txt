I don't have any formal training in computer science or philosophy, but over the past several months I've been teaching myself about Solomonoff induction and Kolmogorov complexity, mostly through Wikipedia, a handful of blog posts, and a couple of recorded lectures I tracked down online. What drew me in was the promise that this framework turns Occam's Razor from a vague piece of advice into something mathematically precise: instead of just saying "prefer the simpler theory," you assign every hypothesis a prior weight of roughly two-to-the-power-of-minus-its-length, where "length" means the length, in bits, of the shortest program that would output it when run on some fixed universal Turing machine. Shorter programs get more prior weight, and that is supposed to be what "simplicity" really cashes out to.

The trouble is that when I try to apply this to an actual case, I get a result that seems to run backwards. Take any well-supported scientific theory, say the sort of thing you'd find explaining some physical phenomenon, which typically takes pages of equations, defined terms, and background formalism to state precisely, and compare it with something like "an unseen agent simply willed it to happen." The second one is maybe six words long. If Kolmogorov complexity is fundamentally about the length of the shortest description, and English sentences are descriptions too, it looks like "an unseen agent willed it" should get an enormously higher prior probability than any serious scientific account, purely because it is so much shorter to write down. That seems exactly backwards from how scientists and philosophers actually invoke Occam's Razor, which is usually to rule out those kinds of explanations, not favor them. A couple of people I've raised this with just said "that's not how it works" without explaining why, which hasn't helped much. So I'd genuinely like to know: is there something about how the length of a hypothesis is supposed to be measured that disqualifies something like "an unseen agent willed it" from counting as short in the relevant sense, or does this example actually expose a real weakness in treating Kolmogorov complexity as the rigorous mathematical backbone of Occam's Razor?
