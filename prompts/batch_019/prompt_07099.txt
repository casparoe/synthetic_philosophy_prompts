I've been working on my third novel for almost a year now, and it's written entirely from inside the head of a character who's pretty far from myself - an aging radio operator at a remote Arctic research station who's slowly convinced himself the mainland doesn't really exist anymore. To get the voice right I basically live inside him for stretches at a time: I keep a notebook where I only write as him, I let his rhythms bleed into how I think during the day, the whole method-actor thing writers do. Around the fourth month something started happening that's a little more unsettling then I expected: for hours at a stretch his memories and his sense of whats going on feel completely solid and real, and my actual apartment, my partner, my inbox, all of it starts to feel like the thin, made-up thing. Then I stop for the night, sleep, and wake up back in my own life, and for a day or so it flips - the station and the radio operator feel like something I dreamed, vivid but obviously not real.

I brought this up with my partner over dinner, half joking, and she said something like "well your actual life is the real one, obviously, you're just tired and need to touch grass," which is probably true in some boring sense but doesnt really touch what's bugging me. Because from inside either state there's no felt difference in solidity - it's not like the radio operator's world feels foggier or more dream-like while I'm in it, any more than my "real" life does while I'm living that one. The only reason I end up calling one of them real is that it's the one I default back to. So here's my actual question: if two states of mind can each feel totally coherent and complete from the inside, what actually gives me grounds to say one of them is real and the other isn't - and is the fact that this keeps nagging at me a sign that I'm even asking the right question to begin with? I can't shake the feeling there's something more interesting going on here than "you need more sleep," and I'd love your take.
