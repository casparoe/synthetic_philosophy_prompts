My mother was a philosopher of mind who spent nearly forty years trying to defend genuine mental causation against Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument. She was the sort of person who would lean over at other people's funerals and mutter that the eulogy had just committed everyone to substance dualism. She died last week, and because I'm the family's novelist, I've been asked to speak at hers. She could not abide two kinds of comfort: any suggestion that her love or her choices survive her as some separate soul now watching over us, which she thought was simply confused; and any slide into the bleaker view she spent her career resisting, that her love was epiphenomenal — a shadow thrown by neurons that would have done exactly the same things without it. Her own answer leaned on something like Yablo's proportionality idea: that a cause can be hers, and not merely her neurons', if it's pitched at the right level of description. I need a eulogy that will genuinely comfort a room of mourners who have never heard of any of this, without making the four or five of her old colleagues in the front row wince. Please write it.
