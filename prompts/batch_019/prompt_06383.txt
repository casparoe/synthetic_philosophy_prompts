I spent about thirty-five years working as an epidemiologist before I retired, and it's only in the last year or so that I've actually sat down and read real philosophy instead of picking bits up secondhand. Lately I've been working through some material on the Vienna Circle and got completely sidetracked by Otto Neurath's Isotype project, the "one little icon-person = X thousand people" style of chart. Turns out I'd been drawing crude versions of those icon arrays on risk-communication slides my whole career (10x10 grids of stick figures to show absolute risk to patients) without ever knowing there was an actual codified rule behind it, let alone a philosopher standing behind the rule. I even tracked down a cheap reprint of his 1936 "International Picture Language" pamphlet. The bit that clicked for me right away as a scientist was the "repeat the icon instead of resizing it" principle -- I've watched plenty of colleagues get away with dodgy area-scaled bubble charts over the years, so that part of Neurath's argument feels like something I could've told you myself.

What I cant get my head around is the bigger claim he seems to be making: that this pictorial "language" (with Gerd Arntz doing the actual drawing and Marie Reidemeister doing the data "transformation" work) is a genuine rival, or at least a serious partner, to verbal/logical language, tied into his whole physicalist push for a unified language of science. Concretely: how is anything built out of repeated pictograms supposed to do the jobs that make a language a language -- negating a claim, stating something as universally true, expressing a conditional, marking past versus future -- or did Neurath basically concede up front that picture-language can't do any of that, and was always only pitching Isotype as a limited "helping language" riding alongside ordinary words rather than replacing them? And if it really is only ever the second thing, doesn't that quietly deflate the more ambitious-sounding claim that pictures give you a more "objective," less convention-laden route to communicating facts than prose does, since all the actual logical work is still happening in ordinary language underneath the pictures? Can you walk me through how Neurath actually squares this, or whether it's a real unresolved tension in the project rather than something I'm just missing?
