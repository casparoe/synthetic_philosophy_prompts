So I'm a developmental psych PhD student (I run word-learning studies with toddlers) who's been auditing a philosophy reading group this semester on the epistemology of testimony, mostly becuase a friend in the philosophy department swore it would be relevant to my dissertation intro. We just worked through Coady and the two Frickers' worth of readings (Elizabeth, not Miranda — took me embarrassingly long to realize those are different people), and now I can't stop reinterpreting my own data through their categories. In my studies, 14- to 18-month-olds will happily adopt a brand new word or fact from an adult on what looks like zero track record of that adult's reliability — no time to build up trust, no capacity yet for anything I'd call an inductive check — and everyone in the reading group treated this as an obvious point for the anti-reductionists (testimony as a basic, default-entitled source on a par with perception, a la Reid, not something derivable from prior inductive grounds). I raised my hand and asked whether it could just as easily be read as showing this "trust" is epistemically cheap and unearned until something like monitoring comes online, and got a pretty mixed answer back.

Could you write me a dialogue between a committed local-reductionist about testimony (the Elizabeth Fricker line: no single global inductive argument for testimony-as-such, but the hearer still needs case-by-case positive reasons/monitoring for trustworthiness) and a committed anti-reductionist (the Coady/Reid line: testimony is an autonomous basic source, not reducible to or dependent on independent inductive support) who are hashing this out right after watching a clip from one of my toddler experiments? I want real back-and-forth, not two monologues — the anti-reductionist should get to press the regress/circularity charge (that any inductive case for testimony's overall reliability would itself lean on a mountain of testimony, making it viciously circular) and force the reductionist to actually answer it rather than just gesture at "monitoring," and the reductionist should get a real shot at turning my toddler data back on the anti-reductionist, e.g. by questioning whether pre-verbal credulity that isn't tracking any reliability cues is really the same phenomenon as the mature epistemic entitlement Reid and Coady have in mind, or just a different thing that happens to look similar from the outside. I don't want either of them to clearly win — I want to end up exactly as confused as I am now, just confused in a more precise way.
