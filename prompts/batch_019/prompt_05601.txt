A friend and I have pooled some money to make a series of independent, favorable bets together (each one wins 55% of the time and pays even money, and we expect to place several hundred of them over the coming year), and last night we argued for over an hour about how large our stake should be each time. I want us to wager the same fixed fraction of our current bankroll on every bet—specifically, whichever fraction maximizes the rate at which our money grows over the whole sequence—because I'm convinced that, after enough rounds, this fraction will, with near certainty, leave us richer than any other fixed staking rule would. My friend thinks that reasoning is muddled: he says the only defensible goal is to maximize our expected total wealth at whatever point we eventually stop, and since each individual bet has positive expected value, that goal calls for staking far more than I want on every bet—in the limit, our entire bankroll, every time. Neither of us managed to convince the other. Who has the stronger argument, and is there a principled way to adjudicate between these two ways of thinking about a long sequence of favorable bets, rather than just picking a side?
