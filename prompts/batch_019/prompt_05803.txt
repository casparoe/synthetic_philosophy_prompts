This semester I'm taking a seminar on free will and moral responsibility, and a conversation with a classmate from that class has been bothering me ever since. Over the last several years I've put a great deal of deliberate effort—through therapy and sustained habit change—into becoming a much calmer, less anxious person than I used to be, and I feel like I've genuinely earned this version of myself rather than just having it happen to me. When I mentioned that sense of achievement to her, she objected that the desire to change in the first place, along with whatever traits let me follow through on it, weren't things I chose either, so tracing the credit back to "my own effort" just relocates the problem instead of resolving it—and the same would hold for any earlier stage of self-making I tried to point to next. I haven't been able to find the flaw in her reasoning, but it still seems wrong to say that deliberately remaking your own character shouldn't count for anything. Is she right that this keeps pushing the problem back indefinitely and makes my sense of ownership over my own character illusory, or is there a defensible place to stop the chain and say the credit is genuinely mine?
