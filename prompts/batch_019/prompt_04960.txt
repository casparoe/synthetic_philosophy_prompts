Ok so I went down a Thomas Hobbes rabbit hole (yes, the Leviathan guy) and found out something kind of amazing. Turns out his whole argument for why geometry counts as perfectly certain knowledge is that WE construct the figures ourselves - we're not just observing nature like in physics, we're literally making the triangles and circles, so we get to know them completely. He liked this idea so much he wanted politics to work the same way too, since the commonwealth is also something "made" by us (via the social contract), so its supposed to be just as provable. Except - and this is the part that gets me - the guy spent like the last 20-something years of his life insisting he'd squared the circle, and kept getting destroyed in print by this Oxford mathematician (Wallis, I think?) who proved him wrong again and again, and Hobbes never once admitted it. So the dude whose entire argument for certainty was "we can trust it because we build it ourselves" apparently could not correctly build a circle-to-square construction to save his life. Can you turn this into an actual joke? Needs to be funny to someone who already gets what his argument was, not just a generic "old guy bad at math" joke - the punchline should hinge on the real irony here.
