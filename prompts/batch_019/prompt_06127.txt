Okay so this is a bit of a weird one but hear me out. In my school's philosophy club we stumbled on this concept called "moral trade" — I think a philosopher named Toby Ord came up with the term? The basic idea is that even if two people have totally different ethical views, they can still strike a deal that makes the world better by BOTH of their own standards, without either one changing their mind at all. Like say Maya thinks eating meat is seriously wrong but doesn't care much about carbon footprints, and Jordan loves his steak but feels kind of guilty about how much he flies. Maya could pay Jordan to eat less meat, and Jordan could pay Maya to fly less, and now each of them thinks less "wrong" is happening in the world overall — even though neither one budged an inch on their actual beliefs. It's basically regular trade (comparative advantage n all that) except your trading moral compliance instead of goods.

Me and my friend Divya got into a whole thing about whether this is actually good or kind of messed up. She thinks it's basically just buying indulgences from the church back in the day — instead of Maya having to actually convince Jordan that meat is wrong, she just pays him to act like he agrees, and Jordan's only doing it for the cash, not bc he thinks it's right, which feels gross to her. I was arguing back that it's actually a pretty smart response to the fact that nobody is 100% sure they've got ethics figured out, so hedging like this makes sense even if you can't resolve who's correct. But then I got stuck on something: does the whole thing only really work if both people are at least a little unsure they might be wrong? Because if Maya and Jordan are both fully confident they're right and the other one is just wrong, the trade stops looking like a "hedge" and starts looking more like paying someone to keep doing something bad.

Can you write me a dialogue between two characters arguing this out, one defending moral trade and one attacking it, where they actually respond to each other's strongest points instead of just repeating their own side — back and forth for a decent number of exchanges, not just two big speeches? You can set it up as Maya and Jordan themselves getting cold feet mid-negotiation, or as two totally separate people if that works better. I just really don't want either side to be a strawman, because after arguing with Divya for like an hour I genuinely still don't know who's right.
