My fiancé is finishing a dissertation defending narrative explanation in history against Hempel's covering-law model — he can explain, to any dinner guest unlucky enough to ask, why citing a covering law to explain a regime's fall is a category mistake, why Dray was right that we explain actions by reconstructing an agent's reasons rather than subsuming them under a generalization, and why Danto's "narrative sentence" (a sentence describing an early event only in terms that depend on something later and, at the time, unknowable — "that was the first skirmish of a war that would last thirty years") captures something a covering law never could. Four years of this and I can no longer sit through a wedding toast without noticing that most of them are covering-law explanations in a tuxedo: "you could tell from day one," as though there were a general law of soulmates our relationship simply instantiates. I'm giving the toast at our own wedding next month, to a room split between his History and Philosophy of Science cohort, who will hold me to the distinction, and family who mainly just want to feel something. Could you write a five-minute toast that stays honest to his actual view of explanation — narrating the two of us as the specific, non-repeatable story he'd insist we are, not an instance of anything general — while still landing as a love letter rather than a defense of a thesis?
