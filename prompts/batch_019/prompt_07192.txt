Ok so this happened in my film studies elective and it's been bugging me for days. We had to make a "top 5 films of all time" list with a short paragraph justifying each pick, and my friend Theo put Vertigo as his #1 and wrote this whole paragraph about how the cinematography "redefines the visual language of obsession," etc. Except he'd already told me privately that he's never actually finished it — he dozed off both times he tried and mostly just skimmed his older brother's old film class notes plus the fact that it topped some big critics poll (Sight & Sound, i think?) that a bunch of "greatest movies ever" lists point back to. My other friend Naomi overheard him talking about his paragraph and said thats basically fraud: you can't claim a movie is visually stunning or moving as YOUR OWN verdict if you didn't actually sit through it and feel any of that yourself, and that's different from just repeating a fact you took on someone's word.

Theo shot back that testimony is testimony — none of us personally verified that the earth is 4.5 billion years old either, we just trust the scientists, so if borrowing beliefs from experts is fine there, why not with critics and Vertigo, and if it's not fine, then everyone who's ever repeated a Rotten Tomatoes score at a party is also "lying." Naomi said the earth's age doesn't depend on how it strikes you personally, but whether something is moving or visually stunning is at least partly about your own reaction to it, so you can't just inherit that the same way you inherit a fact. Theo said ok, fine, but then explain why it's totally normal to go see a movie because a friend with good taste recommended it, without double-checking it yourself first — nobody calls that lying. Naomi said using someone's rec to decide what to try is a different move than standing up and asserting their verdict as your own settled opinion in an actual essay, that's not the same kind of claim. Theo then brought up how we let people defer to a trusted friend about whether some situation was messed up, morally, without redoing all the ethical reasoning themselves every time, so why hold art to a stricter standard than morality. Naomi said maybe moral deference has its own problems too, but that doesn't get Theo off the hook, and if anything artistic qualities seem even MORE tied to your own personal response than most moral questions are.

They went back and forth like this for way too long at lunch and neither one budged, and I just sat there eating my sandwich not saying anything.
