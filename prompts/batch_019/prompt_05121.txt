I'm preparing a graduate seminar on decision theory for agents that interact acausally, through mutual prediction and logical correlation rather than direct causal influence, and I want to pressure-test my grasp of the "surrogate goals" proposal for defusing acausal threats before I present it. As I understand the idea, an agent manufactures a cheap, otherwise inconsequential value and commits to responding to threats against it exactly as it would respond to threats against what it actually cares about, so that a threatener interested only in extracting compliance ends up indifferent between targeting the decoy and targeting the real goal. Please give me an intuitive account of why this is supposed to succeed, focusing especially on what I take to be the hardest objection: if the threatener is sophisticated enough to fully model the target's disposition, why wouldn't it simply see through the substitution and threaten the real goal directly, on the grounds that a threat against something genuinely valued is more credible leverage than one against a manufactured decoy? I'd also welcome your view on whether the same problem simply reappears one level up, with the commitment mechanism itself becoming the new target of threats.
