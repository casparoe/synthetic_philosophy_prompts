So a while back I fell down a rabbit hole reading about "scalable oversight" stuff for AI alignment. I'm a backend engineer, not an ML person at all, but a friend on our applied-ML team keeps sending me Alignment Forum posts and now I'm kind of hooked. The thing I keep chewing on is the original AI-safety-via-debate paper (Irving, Christiano, Amodei), which has this neat complexity-theory argument: with optimal play, a debate between two agents in front of a polynomial-time judge can supposedly settle questions in PSPACE, whereas if the judge just looks at one agent's answer directly, with no opponent around to poke holes in it, you're stuck at NP. I vaguely remember from a theory-of-computation class that PSPACE is generally believed to be a way bigger class than NP, so on paper that sounded like a genuinely free lunch: same judge, way more stuff you get to trust the outcome on.

Then I read the follow-up work from Barnes and Christiano on the "obfuscated arguments" problem, and it kind of deflated me. Turns out a dishonest debater can just bury the wrong answer inside some long, complicated chain of reasoning such that neither debater can actually locate the one bad step, so the judge (who's basically told "if nobody can point to a flaw, assume its fine") gets fooled anyway. That feels like it quietly breaks one of the assumptions the PSPACE proof needs, and I can't tell if it means the theorem is a nice mathematical story that just doesn't survive contact with real, non-omniscient judges, or if it's still buying us something important even once you admit that failure mode exists.

Can you walk me through the most important implications of that gap, between the clean PSPACE result and the messier obfuscated-arguments finding, for whether debate is actually a good bet for supervising future AI systems that end up smarter than whoever's judging them? I'm not after a history lesson on the papers themselves, more like: given this tension, what should someone actually conclude about debate as an alignment strategy going forward, and is there a way to tell which parts of the original theoretical case still hold up versus which parts were doing more rhetorical work than real work?
