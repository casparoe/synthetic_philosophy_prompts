Ok so I'm putting together notes for my epistemology seminar this week on the bootstrapping / easy knowledge stuff, and I want to try an argument out on you before I inflict it on students who will absolutely sniff out a hole if theres one.

Quick recap so we're working from the same setup: there's this gas-gauge case (I think it's originally Vogels, though Cohen runs with it too, and I always mix up which of them gets credit for what) meant to show that any view which lets perception hand you justification without first needing an independent reason to trust the source is going to end up licensing obviously bad reasoning. If all I ever do is read the gauge and note "gauge says full, tank's full," over and over, with zero independent check on the actual tank, I can't legitimately conclude the gauge is reliable from that alone -- even if, as it happens, the gauge really is reliable and every individual reading was justified. Cohen's version runs the same structure on perception generally: rack up a bunch of "that's red," "that's green" judgments, then infer "my color vision is reliable" from the track record. Same worry -- it's knowledge that's too cheap to be real, even though nothing in, say, reliabilism seems to actually forbid making the inference.

Here's the move I want to try on you, tell me if I'm just kidding myself: the gauge case and the perception case aren't really the same structure, because "perception" isn't one instrument, it's a loose bundle of only semi-coupled sub-faculties -- color vision, depth cues, touch, memory checks, other people's say-so -- that are at least somewhat independent of each other. So bootstrapping "perception in general" looks more like getting several different gauges to agree with each other than rereading one gauge over and over. That seems like it should count for something, even if it falls short of a fully independent check.

My worry is that those sub-faculties aren't independent in the way that actually matters here, since they could all go wrong together in the very same skeptical scenario, so maybe I've just pushed the circularity back a step instead of escaping it. Can you push on this for me: does the "several quasi-independent gauges" move actually buy Cohen anything against Vogel, and if it fails, can you say exactly where it fails?
