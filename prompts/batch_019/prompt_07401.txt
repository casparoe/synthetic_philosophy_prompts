I'm about two-thirds through a novel whose central character, Mira, is an aging philosopher of art who's built her whole career on a version of Bernard Williams's "manifest artifice" idea from On Opera -- the claim that opera's blatant unnaturalness (people singing instead of talking, singing while they're dying, singing to themselves alone on an empty stage) isn't an embarrassment the form has to live down, it's exactly what licenses opera to go for emotional extremity that spoken drama can't reach without looking ridiculous. In the chapter I'm drafting, Mira has just published a essay trashing a stripped-down, hoodies-and-park-bench production of Don Giovanni for trying to naturalize a piece that was never meant to be naturalistic, and a pretty sharp arts-radio host has booked her to defend herself live. My partner, who sang chorus at ENO for years, keeps telling me Mira sounds insufferable on the page so far, which I'm choosing to take as a good sign, but I need the actual interview to read like a real grilling and not eight variations on Mira restating her thesis.

Could you draft 8-10 questions for the host, each with a short follow-up keyed to whatever answer Mira is most likely to give? I definately want one question that pushes the "isn't this just Brecht's alienation effect with better tunes" objection and makes her spell out how sincere, undistanced artifice is supposed to differ from Brechtian distancing; one on whether the theory only really works for the aria and starts to fall apart wherever an opera leans on secco recitative or plain spoken dialogue; one that uses her Don Giovanni piece directly, since I gather Williams himself thought Giovanni is less a coherent psychological person than an idea the music is embodying, so the host should ask what that costs us if we still want to praise or blame him like a person; and one on translation, since Mira, like Williams, has spent time on an opera board and has to answer for whether singing Da Ponte in English is a betrayal of manifest artifice or completely beside the point. Could you end with one question that asks Mira flat out what kind of production or operatic experience would actually count as evidence against her view -- I don't want her walking off looking unfalsifiable.
