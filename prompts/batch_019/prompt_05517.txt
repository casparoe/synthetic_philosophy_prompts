Hey, I've been re-reading the moral perfection chapters of Franklin's autobiography for my ethics seminar and wanted your take on something. He sets up this whole elaborate system -- 13 virtues, a little chart for marking every fault, one virtue drilled per week so he gets through the whole list four times a year -- and he's remarkably candid that Order basically beat him for good, and that Pride kept creeping back even after a Quaker friend told him flat out that he was widely reputed proud (which is apparently why Humility got tacked on as virtue #13, almost as an afterthought). By the end he tells that story about the guy who wants his axe polished all over and eventually just settles for a "speckled axe," which reads like a quiet retreat from the whole perfectionist project, even though he insists he came out a better and happier man for having tried anyway. So, given how ambivalent Franklin himself ended up about the pursuit of moral perfection: what do you think he'd make of todays self-tracking culture -- habit apps, streak counts, commitment-device sites like stickK that literally charge you money for missing a goal? Would he see the gamification and added stakes as a smart fix for the willpower problems he complains about, or would he think it corrupts the whole point, since for him the value was supposed to come from building character on your own steam, not being externally forced into it?
