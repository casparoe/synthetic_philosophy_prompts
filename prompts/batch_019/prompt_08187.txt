Question:
Consider the following exchange between Priya and Sam.
Priya: "Are you coming to Daniyal's party on Friday?"
Sam: "I've got a big presentation Monday morning."

(a) [4 points] State, in your own words, what Sam implicates by his response.
(b) [7 points] Using Grice's Cooperative Principle and maxims, explain precisely how this implicature is generated. Which maxim is doing the bulk of the work, and is Sam best described as flouting that maxim or as observing it? Justify your answer with reference to Grice's account of the different ways a speaker can fail to fulfill a maxim.
(c) [5 points] Is this implicature generalized or particularized? Defend your classification.
(d) [4 points] Show that the implicature is cancellable, and explain what this shows about its status as a conversational, rather than conventional, implicature.

Student's answer:

(a) By mentioning the presentation, Sam is indirectly declining the invitation. He is implying that he probably won't be at the party, or at least can't commit to it, because he needs to be prepared and well-rested for Monday.

(b) This implicature is driven by the maxim of Relation ("be relevant"). Taken at face value, Sam's remark doesn't answer Priya's yes-or-no question at all, so on its face he flouts the requirement to be relevant. Since Priya has no reason to think Sam is opting out of the conversation or being deliberately unhelpful, she reasons that he must be flouting relevance on purpose, in order to communicate something indirectly rather than stating it outright — much as a speaker flouts the maxim of Quality when being ironic. To preserve the assumption that Sam is still cooperating overall, Priya supplies the missing premise that preparing for an important presentation is incompatible with staying out late at a party, and concludes that Sam means to decline. The implicature is calculable in the standard Gricean sense: it can be reconstructed step by step from the assumption that the Cooperative Principle remains in force even though the maxim of Relation is, on its face, being broken.

(c) This is a generalized conversational implicature. It doesn't depend on any special quirk of this particular exchange: citing a prior commitment in response to an invitation is, in general, taken by default to signal a negative answer, whatever the commitment happens to be. Because the inference goes through automatically from the structure of the exchange rather than from background assumptions specific to Sam and Priya, it counts as generalized rather than particularized, on a par with how "some" generally implicates "not all" whether or not anything is known about the speaker.

(d) The implicature is cancellable: Sam could add, "I've got a big presentation Monday morning — but I'll definitely still swing by for a bit first," and this is perfectly coherent, whereas denying something that was said, or something conventionally implicated, would produce a contradiction. Since the negative answer can be suspended in this way, it must be a conversational rather than a conventional implicature: it is not part of the fixed meaning of Sam's sentence, but an inference that depends on assumptions that can be overridden by context.
