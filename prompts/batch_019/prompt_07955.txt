For a feature I'm reporting on how economists formalize the idea of a "fair" negotiated outcome, I'd like to dramatize the central dispute between two classic bargaining solutions rather than just explain it. Write a dialogue between a proponent of the Nash bargaining solution and a proponent of the Kalai-Smorodinsky solution, set at the moment they realize their solutions prescribe different splits for the same negotiation. Each side should press the other specifically on the axiom it sacrificed to get its result — independence of irrelevant alternatives for the Nash camp, individual monotonicity for the Kalai-Smorodinsky camp — invoking the classic objections to each (that the Nash solution can leave a party worse off even as that party's own bargaining position improves; that the Kalai-Smorodinsky solution can change its prescribed outcome when options nobody wanted are dropped from the table). Have each character respond to the specific challenge just raised rather than simply restating their preferred axiom, and don't let either side win cleanly.
