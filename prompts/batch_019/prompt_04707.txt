I've been falling down a rabbit hole for a piece I'm writing about philosophers who snuck one genuinely weird thought experiment into an otherwise dry book, and right now G.E. Moore's Principia Ethica has me stuck. Theres a bit in there where he has you imagine two entire worlds side by side - one impossibly, extravagantly beautiful, the other basically a planet-sized garbage heap - and then he stipulates that no living thing will ever see, or could ever see, either one. He still insists its obvious that the beautiful world is the better one to bring about, like beauty carries some value that floats completely free of anyone ever being around to notice it.

Then later in the same book, when he gets to personal affection and friendship, he does this other move where the value of loving someone seems to hinge on whether the qualities you admire in them are actually real - so two people can have identical warm feelings toward a friend and still be doing something quite different, value-wise, depending on whether they're perceiving that friend accurately or just flattering a projection they've built up in their own head. I brought both of these up with a friend over drinks and we got stuck arguing about whether they even sit together consistently - one says value can be totally observer-independent, the other says value depends heavily on getting the object of your feeling right.

It's been rattling around in my head since, so here's what I'd love from you: a short story, any genre - sci-fi, fable, quiet literary realism, whatever fits best - that actually puts a character in a spot where they have to reckon with both of Moore's puzzles at once, not as background trivia but as something the plot turns on. Maybe she's the last person who will ever witness something extraordinary right before its only record gets destroyed, and around the same time she starts suspecting that the relationship she's built her life around rests on a misreading of who the other person really is. I don't want characters sitting around explaining Moore to each other - I want the actual tension (does beauty need a witness, does love need to be accurate) baked into what happens and what it costs her to choose.
