For my philosophy elective, we recently read the "Why I Am So Clever" section of Nietzsche's *Ecce Homo*, where he insists that questions of diet, climate, and daily routine matter more to a person's development than most of what philosophers and theologians have traditionally cared about. He mocks German and English cooking and beer-drinking as dulling the spirit, praises lighter southern fare, moved restlessly between towns in search of the dry air he thought necessary for clear thinking, and is openly skeptical that any single diet or regimen could suit everyone. Given all this, how do you think he would react to today's data-driven biohacking and longevity culture, with its blood panels, wearables, and standardized biomarker targets pursued by figures like the tech entrepreneur Bryan Johnson, and what, if anything, do you think he would find admirable in it despite his distaste for universal rules?
