The question they were given was: "Explain David Lewis's counterpart theory of modal predication, including how the problem of accidental intrinsics motivates it over a transworld-identity ('overlap') view of how individuals exist across possible worlds. Then present Kripke's 'Humphrey objection' to counterpart theory, and evaluate whether Lewis has an adequate reply."

And here's my classmate's answer, copied exactly: "On the transworld identity view, one and the same Humphrey exists in more than one possible world, so a counterfactual like 'Humphrey could have won the election' is made true by that very individual having a different outcome in some other world. Lewis's problem of accidental intrinsics is that this can't be right: if the very same Humphrey has, say, five fingers here and four fingers in a world where he lost one, then one thing simpliciter has two incompatible intrinsic properties, since intrinsic properties aren't the kind of thing you can have 'at a world' the way you can be a certain height 'at a time.' That's a contradiction, so Lewis drops the overlap picture. His alternative, counterpart theory, treats individuals as worldbound: nothing exists in more than one world, and 'Humphrey could have won' is true because some other world contains a counterpart of Humphrey — someone sufficiently similar to him along the contextually relevant respects — who wins there. Since the counterpart relation is just comparative similarity rather than strict identity, it's still an equivalence relation the way identity is, which is exactly what qualifies it to do identity's job in the truth conditions without disturbing the underlying modal logic. Kripke's Humphrey objection is usually read as making essentially this same point from the other direction: he argues that Lewis's own accidental-intrinsics setup backfires, because if a counterpart can have properties incompatible with Humphrey's, Lewis hasn't dissolved the contradiction, he's just moved it to a different pair of worlds. My best guess at how Lewis would answer this is that intrinsic properties were never really had simpliciter to begin with — they're implicitly world-relative — so there isn't a genuine contradiction for the counterpart relation to inherit."
