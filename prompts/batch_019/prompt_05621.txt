I'm TAing a grad seminar on modal metaphysics and could use a second pair of eyes on some grading, kinda buried this week. The take-home question was:

"Explain the disagreement between Lewis's counterpart theory and the 'genuine transworld identity' view of de re modality. Your answer should cover (a) Kripke's Humphrey objection to counterpart theory, and (b) at least one reason Lewis gives for preferring counterparts over transworld identity."

Here's the student's answer:

"Lewis's counterpart theory holds that individuals are world-bound -- each concrete thing exists in exactly one possible world -- so a claim like 'Humphrey could have won the election' is made true not by Humphrey himself existing in some other world, but by a counterpart of his, someone similar enough to him in the relevant respects, winning there instead. The rival transworld identity view says it's literally one and the same Humphrey, numerically identical, who wins in some worlds and loses in others.

Kripke's Humphrey objection is that this mislocates what we mean: when Humphrey wonders whether he could have won, he isn't wondering about the fate of some other guy who merely resembles him -- he doesn't care what happens to his counterpart. So counterpart theory seems to change the subject of our ordinary modal talk about particular people.

Lewis's main reason for preferring counterparts is the problem of temporary intrinsics: shape is supposed to be an intrinsic property, had by a thing simpliciter and not relative to anything else. But if Humphrey is bent in this world and straight in another, and the transworld identity theorist insists it's one and the same Humphrey in both, then one thing has two incompatible intrinsic shapes, which is a flat contradiction unless we give up treating shape as truly intrinsic. Counterpart theory avoids this because it's really two individuals, Humphrey and his counterpart, each with its own shape, so nothing has incompatible properties. Kripke, who like Lewis holds that possible worlds are concrete universes that really exist, would instead just relativize shape to worlds -- Humphrey is bent-at-this-world and straight-at-that-world -- so nothing's contradictory, just relative."
