My sister is finishing a PhD in comparative philosophy, and she is marrying a high school teacher who has never read a page of Confucius but has absorbed enough of her dinner-table monologues to make one very specific request for the wedding toast: no talk of "two souls becoming one," since neither of them believes a self exists prior to its relationships, but rather something grounded in Confucian role ethics, her actual research area. As the family's other philosophy PhD student, I've been given the assignment, along with the difficulty she embedded in it: she will not have her marriage blessed by a framework whose traditional account of the husband-wife relation — fūfù (夫婦), one of the wǔlún, the classical "Five Relationships" — is widely read as hierarchical, and a good third of the guests are her cohort, who will notice at once if the toast launders that hierarchy through unfamiliar vocabulary rather than actually confronting it. Could you draft the toast? It should run about five minutes, move grandparents who have never heard of Roger Ames, and still hold up under the scrutiny of those who have read him closely.
