Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have spent the past year reading through the philosophical literature on quantum foundations, and I have grown suspicious of a step that recurs in almost every treatment of Bell's theorem I encounter: the leap from "the CHSH inequality is violated" directly to "nature is non-local," without asking which notion of locality has actually failed. Bell's factorizability condition decomposes, following Jarrett and Shimony, into two independent constraints — parameter independence and outcome independence — of which only a violation of the former would permit superluminal signalling. Please argue for the position that entangled correlations violate outcome independence while respecting parameter independence, and that this is exactly why quantum mechanics achieves what Shimony called a "peaceful coexistence" with special relativity rather than a genuine conflict with it. In making the case, engage directly with Maudlin's objection that the mere absence of a signalling mechanism is too weak a criterion for relativistic compatibility, since producing correlated outcomes at space-like separation still seems to demand some non-local common structure that no-signalling alone cannot rule out.
