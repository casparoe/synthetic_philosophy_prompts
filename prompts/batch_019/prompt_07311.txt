So I've been chewing on Galen Strawson's "Basic Argument" for weeks now - his case that nobody's ever truly morally responsible for anything. The core move: you act the way you do because of the way you are, so to be truly responsible for an action you'd have to be responsible for the way you are - but the way you are is just genes plus upbringing plus luck, and even a deliberate attempt to reshape yourself was itself done by the "you" that existed before the reshaping, so the regress never bottoms out at a point where you made yourself. The part that really got me: this is supposed to hold even if indeterminism is true, since a random twist in how you turned out still isn't something you did. He even brings in (i think?) that Nietzsche bit about a guy trying to pull himself up out of a swamp by his own hair to become properly self-caused.

Every compatibilist i've read just pivots to stuff about identifying with your own desires (frankfurt-style) or being reasons-responsive, which feels like a dodge rather then an actual answer to the regress step. can you write me a dialogue between a hardline defender of the Basic Argument and a genuinely sharp compatibilist, where they actually go head-to-head on THAT specific move instead of the generic free-will-vs-determinism stuff? want the compatibilist to get a real non-strawman shot at it and the strawson-side person to actually respond instead of just repeating themselves.
