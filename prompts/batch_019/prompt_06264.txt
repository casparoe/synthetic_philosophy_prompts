I'm a science journalist who somehow ended up the "public member" on my university's IACUC -- the committee that signs off on animal research protocols. A renewal just came up for a basic neuroscience protocol that induces moderate, ongoing pain in several hundred mice to map chronic-pain circuitry -- no specific treatment being tested, just foundational data that might inform pain therapies someday. Half the commitee thinks that diffuse future benefit clears our harm-benefit bar; the other half says we're letting "more data is always worth it" do too much work, and would never accept that reasoning for comparable research on a human with similar mental capacities. Our resident bioethicist keeps bringing up Nozick's old aside in Anarchy, State, and Utopia about maybe doing what's often summarized as "utilitarianism for animals, Kantianism for people" -- plus his own worry that basing animals' lesser status on lesser psychological complexity has awkward implications for humans with unusually limited capacities too. I got stuck drafting the recommendation for friday's meeting -- can you write it: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and actually grapple with that worry instead of just reciting our usual checklist?
