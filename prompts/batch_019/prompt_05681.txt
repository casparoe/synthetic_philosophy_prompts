I sit on the Constitutional Review Committee of my university's Student Senate, and this term I'm also taking a seminar on twentieth-century French political thought in which we just read Simone Weil's essay "On the Abolition of All Political Parties." A senator has proposed banning organized 'slates' -- tickets of candidates who campaign jointly and caucus as a bloc once elected -- defending the proposal in Weil's own terms: that such a body is a machine for manufacturing collective passion; that it disciplines members' judgment until loyalty to the group displaces independent perception of truth and justice; and that it is ultimately oriented toward nothing but its own unlimited growth -- which, she held, is why every party carries the seed of totalitarianism even within a competitive multi-party system. Other committee members accept the diagnosis but doubt the cure: they argue that banning slates will only push factional coordination underground rather than eliminate it, and that it will disadvantage newer or less-connected candidates who depend on slates to pool volunteers and resources. I need to circulate a short decision memo before next week's vote. Please draft it: recommend adopting the ban, rejecting it, or some modified alternative, and justify the call through genuine engagement with Weil's argument rather than generic pros and cons, giving the strongest form of the opposing objection its due.
