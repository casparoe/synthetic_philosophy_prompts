So for the past couple months I've been down this rabbit hole reading about AI alignment stuff on my own time, and I keep running into this idea called the "Löbian obstacle" that comes up whenever people talk about whether a self-improving AI could ever fully trust a smarter/future version of itself. From what I can piece together the core issue is Löb's theorem — you can't have a proof system that generally adopts "if I can prove X, then X is true" as a rule about itself, because Löb's theorem says that if it could prove that rule for some X, it could already prove X outright, so the rule basically collapses into needing to already know the answer. That's bad news if what you wanted was an agent that can formally certify a successor (running on the same or a similar proof system) will only ever take good actions.
