In infinite ethics, one of the more technical problems facing total utilitarianism is that the "total welfare" of a world may not be well-defined once the population is infinite. Summing infinitely many positive and negative individual welfare contributions can yield different totals — or no determinate total at all — purely as a function of the order in which the contributions are added, much as a conditionally convergent series in real analysis can be rearranged to converge to any value whatsoever. Explain, at an intuitive level, why this order-dependence is a genuine feature of infinite aggregation rather than a mere technicality, and why it poses a deeper problem for total utilitarianism than the more familiar "infinitarian paralysis" worry that, in an infinite universe, no finite action can change the total amount of value.
