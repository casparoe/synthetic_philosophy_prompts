So after thirty-some years running a mass spec lab I retired and, of all things, fell into philosophy of mind, which is how I ended up wrestling with David Chalmers' zombie argument this past month. I get the basic thought experiment fine, my molecule-for-molecule duplicate with nobody home, so to speak, but what I can't shake is the more technical exchange that happens right after it, between what I gather is called "type-B materialism" and Chalmers' own comeback to it. Every writeup I find just states the two positions side by side and never actually makes them fight it out.

Here's the bit I mean. The type-B materialist says fine, zombies are conceivable, but conceivability obviously doesn't guarantee possibility - it was once conceivable that water might not be H2O, or that Hesperus might not be Phosphorus, and both of those turned out to be necessary once we learned the relevant empirical facts, so maybe the zombie case is the same kind of thing. Chalmers' reply (I've been reading around his two-dimensional semantics stuff, plus I dipped into Kripke's Naming and Necessity for background) is that the water case is disanalogous: "water" splits into a primary intension, whatever waterly stuff is around us, and a secondary one, H2O specifically, and its the gap between those two that makes the necessity feel contingent, whereas our phenomenal concepts supposedly don't split like that - there's no "watery-stuff"-style placeholder concept of consciousness that could turn out to pick out something else. So on his view the zombie case can't be explained away the same way.

What I'd like from you is a dialogue, real back and forth over several rounds, not just each side announcing its opening position, between a type-B materialist and a Chalmers-style dualist arguing this out directly. Have the materialist open with the water/H2O move, have the dualist give the two-dimensional reply above, and then keep them genuinely responding to each other's specific point rather then talking past one another. I'd especially like to see whether the materialist has a decent comeback to "phenomenal concepts don't split into two intensions" - I have a vague memory of something called the "phenomenal concept strategy" being relevant here, but I might be misremembering. Don't bother with scene-setting, a pub or a seminar room or whatever, I really just care about the substance, and both sides need to be sharp enough that I honestly can't tell going in which one you think wins.
