I got into reef keeping a few months ago (small tank, nothing fancy) and went to my first frag swap this weekend, where two regulars, Nate and Amir, got into an argument I can't stop thinking about. Someone had a table of frags all cut from one big colony, and Nate said "you're not buying twenty corals, you're buying twenty pieces of one coral that now lives in twenty tanks." Amir pushed back: each polyp has its own mouth, gut, and tentacles, so a colony is really thousands of separate animals sitting shoulder to shoulder, more like a city than a body - cutting one up doesn't injure some single "it" any more than splitting up a crowd does. Nate said that's nuts, since the polyps are physically fused by living tissue and in plenty of species share one gut cavity, so food actually moves between them - that's circulation, not neighbors. Amir came back with conjoined twins, who can share a blood supply and organs too without anyone inventing a third person to own the shared parts. Nate said twins each still have their own brain and can usually be separated and survive as two people, wheras a single polyp cut off from the colony usually doesn't make it - which he took as proof the colony's the real animal and the polyp's more like an organ. Amir's comeback: you can strain a sponge through cheesecloth into separate cells and it'll reaggregate into a working sponge again, and nobody thinks that proves a sponge isn't really made of separate cells - so "can't survive alone" doesn't settle what the real unit is either. Swap ended before anyone gave ground. I honestly cant tell who was actually ahead. Can you go through it move by move and say who had the better of each point, not just who wins overall - and is there an actual fact of the matter here, or is "one animal" vs "twenty thousand animals in a trenchcoat" basically just semantics?
