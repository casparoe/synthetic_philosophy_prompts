Ok, this has been rattling around in my head lately: Hume's whole is-ought thing says you can never validly derive a normative conclusion from purely descriptive premises. But the entire pitch of inverse reinforcement learning (and "value learning" more broadly) is exactly that move: infer the agent's reward function (an ought, basically their values) from nothing but observed behavior (an is). Thing is, IRL only actually works once you bolt on a rationality/optimality assumption, otherwise the reward is totally underdetermined by the behavior. So if you resurrected Hume and walked him through this, would he say that assumption is just more descriptive machinery, or that its secretly doing the normative work -- i.e. the exact "smuggled ought" his own argument warns about? Answer from Hume's actual views as best you can (reason as slave of the passions, the is/ought paragraph in book 3 of the Treatise), not just the bumper-sticker version of the guillotine.
