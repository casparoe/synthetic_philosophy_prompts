I've been reading about the cosmological argument and noticed it's often presented as having two independent versions -- the kalam argument and the Leibnizian argument from contingency -- but the contingency version really just looks like a more careful restatement of the kalam one, since both are ultimately trying to explain why the universe exists at all rather than nothing existing instead. Given that, a live possibility like a beginningless, eternal universe, which some cosmological models allow, should undercut both arguments equally, since nothing that has simply always been running would need anything outside itself to get it started. So why do philosophers of religion still treat the contingency argument as the more resilient of the two, one that supposedly survives even if the universe turns out to have no beginning at all?
