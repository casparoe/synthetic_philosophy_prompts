My best friend from undergrad is getting married in three weeks and she's asked me — her one remaining philosophy-nerd friend, now three years into a PhD in decision theory — to give the toast. I've been elbow-deep in L.A. Paul's book on transformative experience all semester for a paper on rational choice under uncertainty, and it occured to me that marriage is basically one of her go-to examples of what she calls a personally transformative experience, right up there with becoming a parent: you can't know from the outside what it's like to actually be married, your preferences are supposed to reorganize once you're in it, and normal expected-utility reasoning kind of breaks down because the "you" doing the evaluating beforehand isn't the "you" who ends up living with the outcome. Her vampire case (becoming an immortal blood-drinker is unknowable and irreversible from the human side, so you literally can't calculate your way into the decision) is the bit everyone who's read the book remembers, and there's a move she makes near the end where instead of asking "will this maximize my expected happiness," which you can't compute, she basically says the leap itself, the choosing to find out, is the rational core of the decision. That's the note I want to end on.

The catch is the room is maybe 40% my cohort, who will clock the reference immediately and expect it done properly, and 60% the couple's actual families, who have never heard of Laurie Paul and will visibly glaze over if I say "epistemically transformative" out loud. Could you write me a five-ish minute toast that uses the idea for real — the bit about not being able to preview who you'll become, and why you'd leap anyway knowing that — but that reads as a toast and not a guest lecture? I want at least one line in there that's genuinely funny to people who know the theory and not just a generic wedding joke wearing a footnote, and I don't want the overall thing to come off like I'm hedging on whether the rational move here is even to get married — it should land as obviously happy for them.
