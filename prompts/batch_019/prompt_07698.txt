So I'm in a philosophy PhD program and I'm TAing an intro ethics course this semester - we just spent two weeks on Augustine's writing on lying (De Mendacio and Contra Mendacium) and honestly it kind of wrecked me a little. I knew the headline version going in, "lying is always wrong, no exceptions," but I didn't realize how far he actually pushes it until I read the primary texts closely for discussion section. He's got this whole taxonomy ranking like seven or eight different kinds of lies from worst to least bad (doctrinal lies at the top, lying to save someone's life apparently near the bottom), and even the mildest category is still, in his view, a sin you have to answer for. He explicitly considers the case of lying to save an innocent person's life, or to protect someone's chastity from an attacker, and says nope, still not permitted, full stop, no consequentialist escape hatch anywhere.

This has been bugging me because it maps way too well onto something happening in my own family right now. My grandma has fairly advanced dementia and lives in a memory care unit. She asks for my grandpa multiple times most days - he died about two years ago - and at some point the staff (and her doctor) told my mom and aunt to just say hes out running errands or resting, instead of telling her the truth, because telling her means she relives loosing him from scratch, gets completely devastated, and then forgets the whole conversation within the hour, so nothing is gained except repeated trauma for zero benefit. Everyone does this now, my mom included, and it always struck me as obviously the right call, maybe even the only decent thing to do. Reading Augustine made me second-guess that, not because I think he's actually right, but because I couldn't articulate why he's wrong except "but the outcome is nicer this way," which feels like it's dodging his argument rather than answering it. Is there a real philosophical response to his position here, or does the view just kind of collapse once you apply it to a case like this? And separately, is what my family's doing even a "lie" in the strict sense he cares about, or is there some more honest way to get my grandma to the same calmer place without technically asserting something false? curious what you think - this has derailed like three separate dinners with friends at this point lol.
