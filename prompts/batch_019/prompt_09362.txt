Last night a friend and I spent hours arguing about mereology without at first realizing that was what we were doing. It began with the question of whether, over and above some particles arranged table-wise, there exists a further object -- the table -- and grew into the wider dispute between mereological universalists (who hold that even my left shoe and the Eiffel Tower compose some further object), nihilists (who deny that there are any composites at all, only simples), and everyone occupying the common-sense middle ground. Neither of us budged, and my friend eventually declared the whole debate merely verbal: each side means something different by "object" or "exists," and once that is disambiguated nothing substantive remains to disagree about. I wasn't convinced this dissolves so easily.
