I am an undergraduate currently taking a seminar on ethics, and the question below concerns a real decision I am facing rather than a class exercise. I have narrowed my summer plans to two options: a paid research-assistant position in a cognitive science lab that would strengthen my case for graduate school, and an unpaid organizing role with a local tenants'-rights group that I find far more personally meaningful, though it has no bearing on my career. When I try to weigh the two, I do not feel that I am missing relevant facts about either option, nor do they feel tied to me — if anything, each seems to outdo the other along its own dimension. What strikes me as odd is that even when I imagine sweetening one side (say, the lab position came with a guaranteed publication, or the organizing role involved a more urgent case), my sense of which is better barely shifts, which seems strange if one were genuinely superior, or if the two were simply equal in value. A friend has suggested that I simply list my criteria, assign weights, and total the scores, but this feels like it assumes away the very difficulty I am stuck on rather than resolving it. What is actually going on when two options are this hard to weigh against each other, and should it change how I approach the decision?
