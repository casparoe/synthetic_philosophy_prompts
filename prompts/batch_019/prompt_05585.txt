My friend and I spent much of last night arguing about the Löbian obstacle in the tiling-agents literature on AI self-trust. She works in formal verification and was adamant that the matter is settled: because Löb's theorem tells us a consistent theory T can only prove a reflection instance of the form "Prov_T(⌜φ⌝) → φ" when T already proves φ outright, no self-modifying agent can ever be built to soundly trust a successor that reasons in the same proof system. On her view, this makes the entire research program around stable self-improvement — parametric polymorphism, probabilistic reflection, towers of successively weaker meta-theories, all of it — a doomed attempt to evade a theorem, on a par with trying to trisect an angle with compass and straightedge. I couldn't produce a good rebuttal on the spot. Is she right that Löb's theorem forecloses the whole project in that strong a sense, or does her argument overreach somewhere?
