So, I spent almost 40 years running an experimental physics lab, and since retiring I've fallen hard into philosophy of logic, which is somehow how I ended up on the volunteer grants board of a tiny AI-safety foundation (don't ask me how). We're voting next week on a proposal to fund a toy AI that rewrites its own decision-making code, but only after proving the rewritten version will reason exactly as soundly as the original. Our resident logician on the board keeps invoking the Löbian obstacle: Löb's theorem apparently shows a system can't generally prove a soundness guarantee about a system as strong as itself without either already proving the specific facts outright, or risking that the whole system could be made to prove anything at all. The applicants say they've dodged this with a weakened proof standard between versions; half the board calls that a genuine fix, half calls it the same problem pushed down a level, since you cant weaken forever. Could you draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote -- fund, reject, or fund-with-conditions -- that actually grapples with whether their workaround escapes the obstacle, instead of just namedropping Gödel and Löb and moving on?
