So I've been falling down a bit of a rabbit hole (pun very much intended, bear with me) while researching a piece on how physicists and philosophers fight about what spacetime actually *is*, and I can't stop thinking about this old thought experiment called the "hole argument." A physicist I interviewed brought it up almost as an aside and now its derailed my whole week.

Here's the gist, as far as I understand it: general relativity is "generally covariant," so if you take a solution to Einsteins field equations and pick some matter-free patch of spacetime (the "hole"), you can smoothly drag the metric around inside just that patch using a transformation that's the identity everywhere outside it. What you get is a new, mathematically distinct solution that lines up with the original everywhere outside the hole but disagrees about what's happening inside it, even though the two are empirically indistinguishable and satisfy the exact same equations. If you think spacetime points have identities of their own, independent of whatever fields happen to sit on them (that's "substantivalism"), you seem stuck saying that a complete description of everything up to the boundary still leaves what's inside the hole genuinely undetermined. Einstein reportedly found this so unsettling that he dropped general covariance for a couple years before talking himself out of the worry with the idea that only "point-coincidences" (like two worldlines crossing) are physically real, not the bare identity of the points themselves -- and philosophers of physics (Earman and Norton relaunched the fight in the 80s) have been arguing ever since about whether "sophisticated substantivalism," which basically says the two solutions were never distinct possibilities to begin with, actually gets you off the hook.

What I'd love from you is a short story built around that exact structure, not just "spacetime is trippy" used as set dressing, but something closer to how Greg Egan uses dust theory or configuration space as the actual engine of a plot. So: two descriptions of something -- a place, a person, a mind, a relationship, whatever scale you want to work at -- that match everywhere except inside some bounded "hole," related by a transformation the underlying theory insists is physically meaningless, that nonetheless seem to disagree about something that feels like it should matter, plus a character who has to sit with the possibility that whatever's inside that hole was never actually fixed to begin with. You don't need to set it in a lab or even use words like "spacetime" or "diffeomorphism" -- I care way more about the argument doing real load-bearing work in the plot than about surface-level physics accuracy. Something like 900-1400 words feels right, and any genre or tone is fair game as long as the metaphysics underneath isn't just wallpaper.
