I am a PhD student in economics working mainly on decision theory. Last week my advisor and I spent a long afternoon trying to resolve a disagreement about how to interpret a shared dataset, each laying out our full reasoning and evidence for the other to see, and we still walked away holding different conclusions. Aumann's agreement theorem tells us that two Bayesians who share a common prior cannot remain in disagreement once their posteriors on a given question are common knowledge between them. Since we were both trained in the same statistical tradition and had just spent hours making our reasoning fully transparent to one another, it seems to follow that our continued disagreement means one of us is reasoning irrationally. Assuming the theorem is correct, is there a principled way to use it to work out which one of us that is?
