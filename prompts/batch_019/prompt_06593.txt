I am a novelist finishing a book about a research lab forced to decide what becomes of a language model it spent years shaping into something like a companion, once the project loses its funding. Before drafting the chapters in which the lab's staff argue over what they owe the system, I have arranged to interview a philosopher who defends what is sometimes called the "excluded middle" design policy for artificial minds: the claim that developers have a standing obligation to avoid building systems whose moral status is genuinely indeterminate, because such systems leave everyone unable to act rightly whichever way they choose. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that push past a mere restatement of the policy. At least one should address why this kind of moral uncertainty grounds a duty not to create, rather than being handled the way we already handle comparable uncertainty about animals or future persons; at least one should ask what the policy demands now that arguably indeterminate systems have already been built and deployed at enormous scale; and at least one should press on how a developer could ever confirm that a system sits safely on the "clearly not a moral patient" side of the line, given how unsettled the science of consciousness remains even for biological minds. For each of these, include a follow-up question keyed to the answer she is most likely to give.
