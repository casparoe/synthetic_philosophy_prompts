Two friends of mine who are way too into decision theory got into it after our reading group last week, about whether cyclic preferences are actually irrational. (1) Marcus said his friend Nadia's ranking of three lunch spots is cyclic -- she ranks the taco place over the noodle place, the noodle place over the sandwich shop, and the sandwich shop over the taco place -- and that's a textbook money pump: start her at the sandwich shop, charge her a couple bucks to trade up to the noodle place, a couple more to trade up to the taco place, then a couple more to trade back to the sandwich shop (which, on its own, she also ranks above the taco place) -- now shes paid three times and is back exactly where she started, just poorer, and you can run the loop forever. (2) Nadia said sure, if she's dumb enough to keep playing along -- but if she sees the whole loop coming, she can just refuse that last trade back to the sandwich shop, even though, asked about that pair alone, she genuinely does rank it over the taco place. (3) Marcus said that's a cheat: if she's willing to refuse a trade she supposedly prefers, she doesn't actually have that preference, so there's no real cycle left to defend. (4) Nadia said no -- she still ranks the sandwich shop over the taco place in isolation, she's just also got enough foresight to adopt a policy of not cashing that preference in step by step, and a preference plus a policy of not always acting on it sequentially isn't a contradiction. (5) Marcus said needing a bolted-on policy just to stop your own preferences from loosing you money forever is exactly the tell that they were broken to begin with -- genuinely coherent preferences shouldn't need that kind of patch. (6) Nadia switched tack: fine, but then completeness is on way shakier ground than transitivity here, since nobody can build a pump against someone who just refuses to ever pay for a swap between two options they've never ranked against each other, so 'maximize expected utility or get money-pumped' proves a lot less than people think.
