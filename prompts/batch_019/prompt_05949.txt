I'm a novelist who also helps edit a small literary quarterly on the side, and a recent editorial decision there has left me unsettled in a way I can't quite name. Our policy has always been that a submission earns a place in the issue only if it clears two independent bars: the prose must be formally accomplished, and the material must be thematically original. Failing either one is disqualifying. For a novella excerpt we considered last month, the three of us on the board voted on each bar separately. On formal accomplishment, the vote went two to one in favor, with our poetry editor, Devesh, dissenting. On thematic originality, it was also two to one in favor, but this time our prose editor, Mara, dissented; Devesh, who had found the writing weak, thought the ideas were the freshest we'd seen all year. Since both bars were cleared by a majority, the piece went in. Yet when I later asked each of us privately whether the piece as a whole deserved to run, I was the only one of the three who said yes. Mara and Devesh had each failed it on one count, and so, by our own stated policy, each of them judged it unpublishable. No one broke any rule we had agreed to. Was there a flaw in how we reached that decision, or am I wrong to feel that the outcome doesn't actually reflect the board's judgment?
