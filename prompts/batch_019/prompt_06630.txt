I spent my working life at the bench as a chemist, and now that I'm retired I've fallen down a logic and philosophy rabbit hole, starting with Gödel's incompleteness theorems. Lately I keep running into AI-safety folks talking about the 'Lobian obstacle' - basically that, following from Lob's theorem, a formal reasoning system can't generally prove things like 'if I can prove P then P is true' for every P without becoming inconsistent, so a self-improving AI (or arguably any of us) can never fully verify ahead of time that its future, smarter self will reason soundly - trust has to be handed off at some point without a watertight proof behind it. Gödel died in 1978, decades before anyone put it this way, but he obviously had strong opinions on what incompleteness meant for minds vs machines - that whole disjunction from his 1951 Gibbs lecture. So knowing what we actually know of his views, what do you reckon Gödel would've made of the Lobian obstacle as a limit on self-trust: more ammo for his side of the disjunction, or would he have found some clever way to squirm out of it?
