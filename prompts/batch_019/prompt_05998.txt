I spent thirty-odd years as an experimental physicist, and I always assumed that special relativity had settled the metaphysics of time along with its physics. Retirement has given me time to read more carefully; having now worked through the Rietdijk–Putnam argument and Penrose's Andromeda paradox, I am no longer sure that assumption survives. Please write an essay that reaches a definite verdict on whether the relativity of simultaneity refutes presentism in favor of eternalism. Engage seriously with the two presentist replies I have since come across: neo-Lorentzian presentism, which restores absolute simultaneity by positing a privileged but empirically undetectable reference frame, and Howard Stein's strategy of relativizing what is real, or what "becomes real," to a single spacetime point rather than to a global present. Judge whether either move is a principled response to the physics or merely an ad hoc rescue of a picture that relativity no longer supports, and close with a definite verdict: either presentism survives, in which case say which rejoinder is doing the real work, or eternalism wins outright.
