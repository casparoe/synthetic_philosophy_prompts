I'm the only engineer on the small internal board that has to approve any diagnosis-adjacent feature before my company's mental-health app ships it. Product wants to launch a 'self-check' quiz that returns results like 'consistent with adult ADHD' and nudges users toward a paid assessment; conversion is far higher among users who get that result, and many say it finally explained their whole life. The one board member with a philosophy background is blocking it, invoking Ian Hacking's 'looping effects': she argues the label changes how people narrate their own past and future behavior, which shifts the very base rates the quiz was calibrated against, so the tool partly manufactures the population it claims to detect -- and she cites the 1990s epidemic of multiple-personality diagnoses as the case study behind the idea. Everyone else thinks the objection is unfalsifiable, would indict any screening tool, and shouldn't withhold recognition people have waited years for. Write the board's short decision memo -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions -- specifying what we'd monitor and when we'd revisit the call, and take her argument seriously rather than dismissing it.
