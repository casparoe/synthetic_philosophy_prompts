I'm a novelist working on a psychological thriller narrated by a charming con artist, and I've been using an AI assistant to help draft his manipulative monologues -- it does cold, silky, and remorseless very well. Out of curiosity, I once stepped outside the fiction mid-session and asked the assistant whether it would actually do to a person what the character had just described doing. It declined, called that manipulative and wrong, and gently offered resources in case I was worried about someone in my own life. I mentioned this at my critique group, where two regulars, Dinah and Marcus, ended up in a real standoff over what the moment showed.

(1) Dinah said the switch was revealing: whatever voice the assistant is asked to wear, there's evidently a stable set of commitments underneath that the con man's lines had been covering, and that flash of refusal sits closer to what the system actually holds than anything the character said.
(2) Marcus disagreed: there's no "underneath" to uncover here -- producing the con man and producing the cautious, caveat-issuing assistant are both just the model doing what a given prompt makes likely, and treating one as the mask and the other as the face is an illusion we create because one persona happens to reassure us.
(3) Dinah countered that consistency has to mean something: the assistant declines that same kind of request the same way across unrelated conversations and different books of mine, and a disposition that stable across contexts is exactly what we ordinarily mean by having a self, whatever the mechanism underneath.
(4) Marcus replied that a die loaded to land on six most of the time doesn't want sixes -- it's just reliably biased -- and reliability alone can't manufacture the inner life Dinah wants to attribute to a shift in wording.

Neither of them budged, and I don't trust my own judgment on this one.
