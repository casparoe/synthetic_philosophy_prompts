Apparently the real point has more to do with "wu hua" (the transformation of things) and dissolving the need to sort Zhuang Zhou and the butterfly into a real one and a fake one, not with finding some indubitable ground to stand on the way Descartes wants.

Can you write me a short story that actually takes that distinction seriously? I want a character to have a butterfly-dream type experience, but please don't end it on the standard "wait... am I the one being dreamed?" gotcha, that's the boring cartesian version everyone already does. Instead let whatever peace/resolution the character reaches come from just letting go of the question of wich self is "real," kind of like that last line of the passage is going for. Doesn't have to be set in ancient China or anything, and maybe keep it around 700-900 words if you can.
