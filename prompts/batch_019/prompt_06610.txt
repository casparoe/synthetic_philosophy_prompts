ok, this has bugged me for ages (I've been reading LW/rationalist blogs since the Overcoming Bias days): everyone agrees that if MWI is true, you'll always subjectively land in a surviving branch after a quantum suicide bet, but the same crowd (Yudkowsky included -- iirc thats his term, 'reality fluid') insists it's still irrational because you should weight by measure/amplitude, not naive branch-counting. That's the part that never sat right with me: if every branch is equally real regardless of amplitude, why does measure get to sneak back in as the thing that determines what I should rationally expect or care about? Feels like people want 'no true randomness, all outcomes happen' as the ontology and 'but some outcomes count more' as the decision rule, without ever justifying the jump between them.

Write an essay that actually settles this instead of hedging: for someone with credence near 1 in many-worlds, is it rational to take a quantum suicide bet with (say) 999,999-to-1 odds of dying vs surviving, for a huge payout to the survivor? Give the strongest version of the 'measure is what matters, don't do it' side and the strongest version of the 'anthropic self-locating certainty, do it' side, then actually pick a winner instead of landing on 'reasonable people disagree.'
