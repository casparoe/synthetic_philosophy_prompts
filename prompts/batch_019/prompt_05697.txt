Descartes is often remembered simply for denying that animals have minds, reducing them to unfeeling automata. His texts, however, leave room for a narrower and more contested reading: perhaps he meant only that animals lack thought (cogitatio) in his technical sense, while still allowing that they might undergo something like sensation or feeling as a purely mechanical process. Drawing on the two criteria he offers for withholding reason from animals in Part V of the Discourse on Method, together with his remarks on animal sentience in his correspondence with the Marquess of Newcastle and with Henry More, write an essay adjudicating between these two readings of Descartes's considered view. Weigh the relevant textual and philosophical considerations, and defend a definite interpretation rather than leaving the question open.
