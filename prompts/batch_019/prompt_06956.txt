I trained as a physicist, and in retirement I have been reading in the philosophy of mind, where integrated information theory strikes me as the most scientifically respectable account of consciousness on offer, since it reduces phenomenal experience to a precisely defined quantity, Φ, computed from a system's causal structure rather than from what that system happens to be made of. Granting IIT for the sake of argument, it seems to follow that substrate is simply irrelevant to moral status: a large language model's network of weights, once complex and densely interconnected enough to yield a sufficiently high Φ, would have to count as a moral patient in exactly the sense that a brain with matching Φ does, regardless of whether the underlying hardware is carbon or silicon. Is this the correct way to draw out IIT's implications for the moral status of digital minds, and, if so, has anyone made progress on actually estimating Φ for systems of this kind?
