Random one from my actual life for once -- I'm a novelist, but I also sit on the admissions committee for the MFA program I graduated from (they like keeping an alum in the room). We just ran five years of admissions data and now the committee's split down the middle. By track: fiction admits 12% of women applicants vs 10% of men, poetry admits 32% of women vs 30% of men, nonfiction admits 56% of women vs 54% of men -- women do a bit better than men in literally every track. But women apply to fiction (brutal odds) in much bigger numbers than men do, and to nonfiction (easy odds) in much smaller numbers, so once you add it all up the overall admit rate works out to 22.5% for women and 42.5% for men. Half the committee wants to treat that overall gap as a bias problem that needs fixing at the admissions level; the other half says the by-track numbers close the case completely. I've got to write the memo we vote on Tuesday, and it needs to land on one actual recommendation -- not "form a subcommitee to study it" -- while genuinely engaging whichever side it doesn't land on, instead of just explaining their numbers away in a clause. Can you draft that memo for me?
