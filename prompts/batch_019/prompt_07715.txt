So in my phil of science seminar we just spent two weeks on structural realism, and now I'm trying to sketch a term paper on the Newman objection but keep tripping over what actually seperates the epistemic and ontic versions once you take the objection seriously instead of just name-dropping it. I get Worrall's basic pitch: after Fresnel's ether equations got carried over (in reinterpreted form) into Maxwell's electromagnetic theory even though the ether itself got junked, we're supposed to say science tracks structure across theory change while staying agnostic about the "nature" of whatever realizes that structure. But then Newman's point, as I understood the reading, is that "knowing the structure" of a domain is basically empty unless you already know which relations among the objects are the real/natural ones, since any set of objects with the right cardinality can always be hooked up by some relation-in-extension or other to satisfy the Ramsey sentence of whatever theory you like — so epistemic structural realism seems to collapse into a claim about how many things there are, not what the world's actually like. A classmate in my study group insists this only sinks the epistemic version, because ontic structural realists (Ladyman, French, the "structure all the way down" crowd) deny there's some further hidden "nature" behind the structure that we're failing to grasp in the first place — so there's nothing left for the Newman-style skeptic to complain we're missing. I'm not sold, since it seems like OSR still needs to say the world's structure is the physical/modal one and not just any old gerrymandered relation satisfying the same abstract pattern, which smells like the exact same move ESR needed and apparently couldn't make good on.

Could you write me a dialogue between an epistemic structural realist and an ontic structural realist actually hashing this out? I want the ESR character to press the OSR character hard on whether ditching "hidden natures" really blocks the Newman-style trivialization worry or just relocates it one level down, and I want the OSR character to give a real, specific answer instead of just repeating the slogan — bonus if the Psillos-ish complaint that "structure needs something to be the structure of" comes up somewhere along the way. Neither one should fold or get a strawman version of their view; make it a genuine back-and-forth, maybe 8-12 exchanges, where each line is actually responding to the specific thing the other person just said rather than both sides just re-stating their opening position.
