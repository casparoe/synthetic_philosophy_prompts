I'm four novels into a fantasy series built on one rule that has never bent: healing doesn't erase an injury; it relocates it, sending a wound of matching severity onto some other living creature somewhere in the world, chosen by nothing anyone has ever managed to predict or control. The pattern has held without exception across three books, and my editor wants to know, for book four's climax, whether a sufficiently clever mage could heal someone without the cost landing on a stranger. Answering that honestly means settling something I've been dodging: does the transfer happen because of some underlying mechanism — an unseen ledger the world somehow keeps, or a single quantity of vitality that simply cannot, by its own nature, be created or destroyed — or is the transfer itself the deepest fact about this world, true only because that's the pattern magic has always followed, with nothing further making it so? My co-writer thinks the question is confused: if the pattern has never once broken, that just is what it means for something to be a fixed feature of this world, and demanding an explanation underneath it asks for something nothing owes us. I'm not convinced, but I can't say what his view leaves out, and every mechanism I try to sketch just invites the same question about itself one level further down. I'd like to know whether he's right that there's nothing left to explain once a pattern is exceptionless, or whether he's missing a real distinction — and if so, what it would take for this rule to count as something that could, even in principle, be broken.
