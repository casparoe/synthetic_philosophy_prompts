My partner and I told our close friends last weekend that we're pregnant (first one), and somehow the celebration dinner turned into a whole seminar on antinatalism between two of our friends. It's been stuck in my head since. Roughly how it went:

(1) Colin, who just read Benatar's "Better Never to Have Been," said the real problem with having a kid isn't how happy the kid ends up -- it's the asymmetry. Pain being absent is good even with nobody around to enjoy that goodness, but pleasure being absent is only bad if there's an actual someone missing out on it. Since a kid who's never born can't be deprived of anything, you can only ever harm a future person by creating them, never benefit one, no matter how the life goes.

(2) Deb said that's gotta be wrong somewhere, because it implies even a spectacularly happy life is worse than never happening at all, and basically nobody living such a life agrees with that verdict about themselves. Colin said Benatar's actual answer is that we can't trust that verdict -- people are subject to a well-documented "Pollyanna" bias where we systematically over-rate our own well-being, so the fact that people don't feel like victims of a harm doesn't tell us much.

(3) Deb pushed back: fine, but the "absence of pain is good" half has the exact same problem Colin's accusing HER intuition of -- there's no one there for who its good either. Why does that side get to skip needing a beneficiary while "absent pleasure = bad" does need one? Looks like the asymmetry is just being asserted twice, not argued for. Colin said it's not asserted from nowhere -- we already believe something asymmetric in this vicinity, since everyone thinks you're wrong to knowingly have a kid who'll suffer badly, but no one thinks you're obligated to have a kid just because it'd be happy. That existing asymmetry in our duties is evidence for his deeper one.

(4) Deb said that move is too fast: "not obligated to create a happy person" is a much weaker claim than "creating a happy person harms them," and Colin needs the strong one to get antinatalism off the ground. Us already accepting the weak claim about duties doesn't mean we accept the strong claim about harm.

It kept going after that but mostly repeats. Nobody at the table budged, and now I've got a kid on the way and this argument living rent-free in my head.
