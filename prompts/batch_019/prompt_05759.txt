I've seen the idea of "evidential cooperation in large worlds" come up repeatedly on rationalist and EA-adjacent blogs, and I'd like to see its strongest form pressure-tested rather than just asserted. The core claim is that a sufficiently large or infinite universe almost certainly contains vast numbers of decision-makers who reason in ways relevantly similar to us yet end up holding different terminal values, and with whom we can never stand in any causal relationship. Proponents argue we should still act cooperatively toward those far-off value systems, since our own disposition to cooperate is evidence that similarly-reasoning agents elsewhere are simultaneously reasoning their way into cooperating with ours — so that a cooperative policy pays off in expectation across the whole ensemble, even though no single act of cooperation causes anything good to happen anywhere. Write a dialogue between a proponent of this view and a committed causal decision theorist who suspects the whole argument just smuggles in evidential decision theory's besetting sin — treating an otherwise uninfluenceable correlation as though it were a reason for action — through the back door. Have the two press each other on the least comfortable implications of their positions, and don't let either side concede too easily.
