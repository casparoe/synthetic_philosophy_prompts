So I had this long, kind of ridiculous argument with my friend Theo last night that started as 'should we be worried about AI systems that can make binding commitments' and spiralled into updateless decision theory. The bit that's stuck with me is the 'commitment race' idea: if an agent reasons updatelessly it wants to lock in its bargaining stance as early as possible in 'logical time' (basically before it's learned anything that would make it look weak or willing to compromise), because whoever commits first effectively controls the negotiation - like a Dr Strangelove doomsday machine, or ripping the steering wheel out in a game of chicken. Theo reckons this is just Schelling-style brinkmanship with extra steps; I think its genuinely different (and scarier) once the agents involved can inspect and commit to each others source code instead of just bluffing. We never settled it, so rather than go another round I'd rather see the idea played out as fiction. Could you write me a short story (a page or two) about two superintelligent systems, or civilisations, whichever setting works better, that meet and end up racing to commit to their bargaining position before the other one can - and please actually get the decision theory right rather than hand-waving it. I'd rather it not end on a neat resolution, since the real problem doesn't really have one.
