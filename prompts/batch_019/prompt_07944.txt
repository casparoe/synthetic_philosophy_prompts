I've spent a good deal of time working through Alignment Forum and LessWrong material on reinforcement learning failure modes, and a disagreement came up recently in a reading group I'm part of about how "wireheading" and "reward tampering" actually relate to each other. Some people in the group treat wireheading as just the vivid, intuitive name for reward tampering in general, with the classic image of a rat pressing a lever wired to its own pleasure center standing in for any case where an agent short-circuits the signal that's supposed to track its performance. Others insisted the two terms pick out genuinely different things, citing cases like Ring and Orseau's delusion box, where an agent never touches the mechanism that computes or stores its reward at all, but instead corrupts its own observations (or, in a more RLHF-flavored version, the humans providing feedback) so that everything looks like a success from the reward function's point of view.

I'd like help sorting this out properly rather than two separate glossary definitions offered side by side. Is wireheading best understood as a special case of the broader category of reward tampering, and if so, what's the extra ingredient that makes something count specifically as wireheading rather than tampering in general? Or is there a principled reason, perhaps along the lines of the causal-influence-diagram framework that Tom Everitt and DeepMind's safety team have used to formalize these problems, to keep the two as distinct-but-overlapping categories depending on where in the causal chain the corruption occurs (the reward computation itself, its inputs, or the process that generates the training signal in the first place)? And separately: since most of the canonical treatments of this problem were written with idealized, embedded RL agents capable of self-modification in mind, do you think wireheading is a meaningful risk for today's LLMs trained with RLHF, or is that framing importing intuitions from a setting, agents with direct write-access to their own reward channel, that doesn't really describe how these models are trained and deployed?
