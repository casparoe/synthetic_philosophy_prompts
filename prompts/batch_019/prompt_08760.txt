I'm writing a historical novel about A. J. Ayer's 1988 near-death experience -- the night he choked on a piece of smoked salmon in hospital, went into cardiac arrest, and afterward wrote up a strange episode involving a painful red light and two negligent "ministers" in charge of space -- and the defensive climbdown that followed in print. Before I draft the scenes set in the hospital and in the weeks afterward, I've arranged to interview a philosopher who works on Ayer and logical positivism, and I don't want the conversation to just retell the anecdote -- I need it to pressure-test the philosophy so that on the page he comes across as neither a cartoon skeptic nor a convenient convert, but as a rigorous mind genuinely unsettled by his own experience. Please draft eight to ten interview questions, each paired with a brief note on the answer she's likely to give and a sharper follow-up keyed to that answer, covering at least: (1) how Ayer's claim in Language, Truth and Logic that it "can not be significantly asserted... that men have immortal souls" sits against his postscript's remark that the experience left him thinking it "worth examining various possibilities of survival for their own sakes"; (2) whether his retreat from saying the experience had "slightly weakened" his conviction about his own death to insisting only his "inflexible attitude" toward that conviction had weakened marks a real epistemic distinction or is just damage control under public pressure; and (3) whether a lifelong empiricist who insists that the causes of a belief are irrelevant to its justification is entitled to let a vivid experience under cardiac arrest move his credence about anything at all. Somewhere in the set, also make room for the tension between Ayer's own two published accounts and his physician's much later claim that Ayer had privately spoken of seeing a "Divine Being," treated with the same pairing of likely answer and follow-up.
