I sit on my university's interdisciplinary dissertation fellowship committee, which funds one advanced PhD student each year for a full year of research free of teaching duties. The strongest logic proposal in this cycle, from a student jointly advised in mathematics and philosophy, requests support to spend that year arguing that the continuum hypothesis is, on balance, true. The case leans heavily on Hugh Woodin's "Ultimate L" program: if a canonical, large-cardinal-compatible inner model of that kind can be shown to exist, the continuum hypothesis holds in it, and the proposal treats this as the direction in which the evidence is currently moving. Two committee members who work in logic raised strong objections ahead of our vote next Tuesday. They point out that Ultimate L's existence remains an unproved conjecture facing serious open obstacles, that the proposal barely engages the rival forcing-axiom program (PFA, Martin's Maximum), which is at least as mathematically fruitful and implies 2^ℵ₀ = ℵ₂ — i.e., refutes CH — and that the whole framing sits uneasily next to Gödel's own guess, in "What Is Cantor's Continuum Problem?," that new axioms would eventually refute CH rather than confirm it. A philosopher on the committee responds that asking the student to hedge between rival programs would leave the dissertation without a thesis, and that adjudicating between axiom candidates on "extrinsic" grounds such as fruitfulness and unifying power is exactly the kind of philosophy of mathematics this fellowship exists to support.

Please draft the memo I will circulate to the full committee before the vote. It should come down on one concrete outcome — fund as submitted, reject, or fund contingent on specific revisions — and state the reasoning in terms the student could actually respond to if the proposal is sent back. I would rather not receive a memo that simply notes CH's independence from ZFC and calls the matter a wash; everyone on the committee already knows that much. What I need is an actual assessment: how much weight the proposal's confidence in Ultimate L can bear given the forcing-axiom program's comparable track record, and whether the student should be required to at least confront the alternative, associated with the set-theoretic multiverse picture, that there may be no single fact about CH awaiting discovery in the first place.
