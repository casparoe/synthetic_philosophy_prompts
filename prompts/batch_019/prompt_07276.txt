This has been stuck in my head for weeks and I want an outside take. At a work dinner, a coworker told us her cousin donated a kidney to a stranger a couple years back through one of those donor-matching programs. Pretty amazing, except she also said he's always been upfront that a big part of why he did it was wanting "a story" - he'd felt kind of invisible his whole life and liked the idea of being the guy at parties who saved someone. To be fair, he also seemed to genuinely want the recipient to be okay. Two people at the table (not me, i just sat there chewing) got into it over whether what he did was actually admirable:

(1) Dana said of course its admirable, someone's alive because of him, and its cold to sit there dissecting a guy's motives when the actual result is a stranger got a working kidney.

(2) Colin said a good result doesn't automatically make HIM good for it. If a guy pulls a kid from a pool only because a news camera's pointed at him, we don't call him brave, even though the kid's just as saved. Admirable is supposed to describe the person, not just what happened.

(3) Dana said fine, but the cousin wasn't ONLY doing it for the story, he wanted to help too, and nobody's motives are ever fully clean - if any bit of self-interest ruins the whole thing then nobody's ever done anything admirable, which seems like a nuts standard.

(4) Colin said mixing isn't the problem, what matters is which part was doing the work. Ask whether the cousin would've gone through surgery and weeks of recovery for a stranger if literally no one would ever find out. If he'd have to say no, the caring part wasn't enough to move him on its own, it was just riding along for free.

(5) Dana said that "would you still do it with nobody ever finding out" test is unfair to basically everybody - run it on almost anything nice anyone's ever done and most people would have to admit probably not, so either that can't be the real bar for admirable, or nothing clears it.

Neither budged and honestly i keep flip flopping myself. Can you go through it move by move and say who's got the better argument at each point, not just who wins overall? And is there something about what actually makes a good act reflect well on the person doing it that you think they're both missing?
