About two months ago I was hospitalized with a severe respiratory infection and, on the third night, went into cardiac arrest. The crash team told me afterward that I had been without a pulse for close to four minutes before they got me back; by the strict clinical definition, I gather, I was dead for part of that time. I remember nothing of the resuscitation itself, but I do retain a vivid impression from somewhere in that gap: an unbroken white light, a sense that sequence and duration had simply stopped applying to me, and an inexplicable feeling of being entrusted with correcting something, though I could not now tell you what. I have mentioned this to only a handful of people, partly because I am still unsure what, if anything, I am entitled to make of it.

The experience has proven more unsettling than I expected, for a reason specific to me. I have never believed in God or in any kind of life after death, and years ago I settled on a line of reasoning that I found genuinely conclusive: claims about surviving one's own death aren't merely under-supported; they aren't really the kind of claim that could be confirmed or refuted from where any of us stand while alive. If death is the end, no one is ever in a position to report that it was; and if it isn't, I suppose I would eventually find out, except that I could never have specified in advance what "finding out" would even amount to, or how I'd know that the thing doing the finding out was still me. On the strength of that reasoning I stopped treating the question as open at all. I still find the reasoning sound, and I have not become someone who believes in an afterlife. And yet the experience has left an aftertaste the reasoning doesn't seem to reach: a mild, stubborn pull toward reading it as meaning something, which sits uneasily beside my continued conviction that there is nothing in particular it could mean. My partner tells me this is simply what an oxygen-starved brain manufactures and deserves no more evidential weight than an unusually vivid dream. An old friend, who is devout, tells me that dismissing it on those grounds amounts to discounting the only first-person data I will ever get on the question. Neither response fully satisfies me, and I cannot tell whether that is because the right view lies somewhere between them or because both are missing a distinction I have not located.

What I would find useful is not reassurance in either direction but a clearer sense of the logic of my own position. Given that I still hold that claims about survival aren't confirmable or disconfirmable in the ordinary way, was there ever room for an experience like this to move my confidence even slightly while my reasoning was still doing something rational, or does noticing that pull just mean the reasoning has been overridden by biology in a way I should discount outright? And is there a coherent stance available somewhere short of both "this changes what I believe" and "this is strictly meaningless," or am I trying to force a middle ground onto a question that doesn't actually leave room for one?
