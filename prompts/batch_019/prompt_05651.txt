I have no formal training in philosophy or game theory, but I've spent a lot of my free time this past year working through Douglas Hofstadter's idea of "superrationality" and its application to the one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma. What keeps bothering me is the sharpest version of the puzzle: two agents who are exact, deterministic copies of each other, so that neither one can end up computing a different answer from the other. Superrationalists say that in this situation "I cooperate, you defect" simply isn't a live possibility, so the usual dominance argument for defecting never gets any grip, and the two copies should cooperate instead. Please make the strongest possible case for the opposite conclusion: that a rational agent should still defect even against an exact copy of itself, because the guaranteed match between the two choices is a fact about correlation rather than causal control, and so cannot be used to block the dominance argument. Address directly the superrationalist's own reply that unilateral defection was never really on the table to begin with.
