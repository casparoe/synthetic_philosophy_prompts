Question (25 points). In "Self-Locating Belief and the Sleeping Beauty Problem" (2000), Adam Elga argues that upon waking, Beauty's credence that the coin landed heads should be 1/3.

(a) Reconstruct Elga's argument for this conclusion, making explicit the two separate claims he needs and the reasoning that licenses each one. (12 points)

(b) In "Sleeping Beauty: Reply to Elga" (2001), David Lewis resists Elga's conclusion and maintains that P(Heads) = 1/2. Using Lewis's own credence assignment over the three relevant possibilities, calculate what Beauty's credence in Heads should become if, upon waking, she were told (and came to fully believe) that today is Monday. Show your work, and explain why this result has been treated as a cost of Lewis's position. (10 points)

(c) In two or three sentences, state which position you find more defensible and why. (3 points)

Student's answer:

Elga's argument proceeds in two stages. First, he asks us to imagine that upon waking, Beauty is somehow informed that today is Monday. In that situation, he argues, her credence that the coin landed heads should be 1/2: finding out that it is Monday tells her nothing about a fair coin toss, since on both the heads-protocol and the tails-protocol she is guaranteed to be awake on Monday. So P(Heads | Monday) = 1/2, and hence P(Heads & Monday) = P(Tails & Monday). Second, Elga invokes Lewis's Principal Principle to argue that, absent any distinguishing evidence, Beauty should divide her credence equally between the centered worlds Tails-and-Monday and Tails-and-Tuesday, since these are subjectively indistinguishable from the inside. Combining the two stages, since Heads-Monday, Tails-Monday, and Tails-Tuesday are exhaustive and mutually exclusive, and the last two are equal in probability to the first, all three get probability 1/3, so P(Heads) = 1/3.

Lewis's reply keeps Beauty's unconditional credence in Heads at 1/2: since she was certain all along that she would wake up regardless of how the coin landed, waking up per se cannot be evidence that favors either outcome, so her Sunday-night credence simply carries over unchanged. This gives the assignment P(Heads & Monday) = 1/2 and P(Tails & Monday) = P(Tails & Tuesday) = 1/4. Because the day of the week is, on this view, evidentially screened off from the outcome of the coin toss, learning that today is Monday should not move Beauty's credence in Heads at all: it should stay at 1/2, exactly as it was on Sunday night. This is one of the main reasons Lewis's view has seemed attractive to many: unlike Elga's, it does not let Beauty extract information about a fair coin from a fact -- the day of the week -- that has no bearing on how the coin landed.

I find Lewis's position more defensible: it is a strange consequence of thirdism that mere memory loss could change the correct credence in a coin that has already been tossed, and Lewis's view avoids this while still honoring the fact that Beauty cannot distinguish her possible awakenings from the inside.
