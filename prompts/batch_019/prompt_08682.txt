On LessWrong and similar blogs, hyperbolic discounting is often treated as having already dissolved the puzzle of akrasia: George Ainslie's discount curves for a smaller-sooner reward and a larger-later reward cross as the sooner one nears in time, so relapse is just a predictable, locally rational preference reversal. I don't think that settles the matter. Write a dialogue set at the moment a woman who installed a website blocker to keep herself off social media during work hours -- a textbook Ainslie-style precommitment device -- opens an incognito tab to get around it. One speaker is a hyperbolic-discounting theorist, who takes the incognito tab as further confirmation of the discounting story and treats "weakness of will" as just an old name for a preference that has predictably flipped. The other is a philosopher of action defending Richard Holton's view that a resolution is a distinct psychological state, neither belief nor desire, and reads the incognito tab instead as a resolution being reasoned around rather than a preference reversing. Each should engage directly with the other's last point, and neither should concede more than their position requires.
