I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have spent a long time working through, on my own, the pages on music in Schopenhauer's The World as Will and Representation, mainly to understand why he treats music so differently from painting, sculpture, and poetry. As I read him, those other arts give us copies of what he calls Ideas, the grades in which the will objectifies itself, while music alone bypasses that layer and offers a direct copy of the will itself — which is why genuine music, on his account, never expresses this or that particular joy or grief tied to a specific person or event, but joy or grief "in the abstract," with no occasion attached. On this basis he treats any piece that sets out to depict a specific object or happening — a storm, a battle, and so on — as a corruption of music, since depicting particular things is properly the business of painting or poetry, not of music. What he never supplies is a clean test for telling the two apart. Could you describe a real or plausible piece, technique, or genre of music — not one of the obvious storm- or battle-imitating textbook cases — where, by his own theory, it is genuinely unclear whether it expresses a feeling in the abstract or illegitimately depicts something particular? Please explain exactly what makes the case hard rather than easy.
