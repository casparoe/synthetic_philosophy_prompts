After forty years in theoretical astrophysics, I have spent much of my retirement working through David Lewis's modal realism, and I believe I have found an unexpected point of contact with my own field. Lewis argues that every way a world could possibly have been is a way some concrete world, causally and spatiotemporally isolated from our own, actually is — but this seems to me essentially the same picture that the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics and the eternal-inflation multiverse have already made respectable within physics, each holding that every alternative permitted by the relevant theory is physically realized somewhere. If two independent branches of physics are converging on exactly the ontology that Lewis defends on a priori philosophical grounds, does this not mean that modal realism has, in effect, already received empirical vindication, and that his "ersatzist" critics are now simply on the wrong side of the evidence?
