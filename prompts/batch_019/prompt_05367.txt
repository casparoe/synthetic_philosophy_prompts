My mother-in-law is deeply religious (church every Sunday, prays before every meal, the whole package), and every once in a while I try to have an honest conversation with her about it, not to convert her, just because I genuinely want to understand her. Whenever I bring up the evidence side of things, like how do we actually know the resurrection happened, or what about all the other religions out there claiming the exact same kind of certainty for totally different stories, she doesn't engage with it as an evidence question at all. She'll just say she feels it, or that its not really about proof one way or the other. My first thought was that she was dodging, but then I noticed my brother-in-law does the exact same thing, and he's an engineer who deals with evidence and probabilities for a living and is sharp about basically everything else.

At one point, half as a thought experiment, I asked her: "ok but if you somehow knew for certain, beyond any doubt, that none of it was true, what would you actually do differently tomorrow?" She didn't dodge the question, she just kind of blanked, like it didn't fully apply to her, not like I'd asked something hard but like I'd asked something in the wrong shape entirely. That's stuck with me since. It's not that she's weighing the same evidence I am and just landing somewhere different, it feels more like "believing" means something else to her here than it does when she believes the 5pm bus is coming.

So here's my actual question: is there something real going on where religious belief just works differently than ordinary everyday belief, in a way that means the whole "what would change your mind" test doesn't really apply to it? Or is that just a fancier way of describing plain old denial, and I'm giving my in laws too much credit because I love them and don't want to think they're being irrational? I mentioned this to a friend and she said I'm overthinking a simple case of people not wanting to give up something comforting, but that explanation doesn't fully sit right with me either, since it doesn't explain the "blank" reaction. Curious what's actually going on here.
