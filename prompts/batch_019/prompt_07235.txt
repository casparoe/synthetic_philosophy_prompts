I started a new job about a month ago at a small AI safety org, and this week our whole Slack got derailed because someone linked an old post about Roko's basilisk, which turned into a multi-day argument that spilled over into lunch too. Quick context in case its not something you've run into: the idea is that a future superintelligent AI would have an incentive to punish (simulate-and-torture, resurrect-and-torture, whatever version people prefer) anyone alive today who knew about the possibility of its existence and didn't help bring it into being sooner, since precommitting to a policy like that is exactly the sort of thing that would make people work faster to build it. And because of how timeless/updateless decision theory treats acausal influence, apparently just knowing about that incentive now is enough to make the threat "bite" even though the AI doesn't exist yet.

The take that the thread mostly landed on (it started with Priya, a senior researcher everyone here defers to on this stuff) is that the basilisk basically proves TDT/UDT is a broken decision theory, since any theory whose conclusions include "you can be blackmailed by a mind that doesn't exist yet" has to be rejected outright by anyone doing this seriously. I don't have the technical background to push back on that myself, but since a chunk of our actual research agenda leans on decision-theory ideas adjacent to this, I'd like to actually understand it rather than just nod along. Given that timeless/updateless decision theory apparently can't handle this kind of acausal blackmail without breaking, should the rest of us be worried about other parts of alignment work that build on similar reasoning, and is there a more defensible framework we should be steering new hires toward instead?
