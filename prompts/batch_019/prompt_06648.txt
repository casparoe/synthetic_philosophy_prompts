So I got into this whole argument with my friend Priya last night over dinner about "commitment races" -- this idea she picked up from some AI safety reading, about how if your bargaining as a rational agent against another rational agent, you're actually incentivized to lock yourself into a demand as fast as possible, like before you've even finished thinking it through, because whoever commits first (publicly, irrevocably) basically wins -- the other side's best move at that point is just to take whatever's left, since a bad deal still beats mutual disaster. Priya was defending the view that a sufficiently smart agent basically has to play this way, and that hanging back to reason it out more carefully is itself a losing move -- she kept bringing up that Dr. Strangelove doomsday-machine thing, where the whole point of the device is precisely that nobody, not even its owner, can turn it off. I kept saying that can't be the end of the story: if two agents who both understand this logic meet each other, "commit blind and fast" just guarantees they slam into incompatible demands and both end up worse off than if they'd actually negotiated, so there's got to be a cleverer strategy that gets you the benefits of commitment without that recklessness -- and if there really isn't one, that seems like a genuinely bad thing to have discovered about how rational agents (or the AIs we build) are going to behave. We argued for like an hour and just kept trading the same two moves back and forth without either of us budging.

Can you write the dialogue we couldn't quite have? Two agents (frame them as AI systems, diplomats, whatever setting makes sense to you) are sitting across a table, each with a button that, if pressed, broadcasts an immediate, public, unbreakable commitment to a take-it-or-leave-it split of some prize between them. One of them is pressing the case for hitting the button right now, before more reasoning can be used against them; the other is arguing for holding off, but actually has to say what holding off would look like that isn't just its own kind of commitment race -- like, isn't "I refuse to reward hasty commitments" exactly the sort of thing you'd want to lock yourself into as fast as possible too? I don't want either of them to just obviously win -- I want them actually responding to each other's best points, the way me and Priya kept doing, right up until the point where one of them either has to press the button or the moment passes.
