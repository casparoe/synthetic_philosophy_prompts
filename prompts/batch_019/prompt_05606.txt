My grandpa died on Saturday (he was 84, it wasn't sudden, he'd been sick a while) and my aunt decided I should do the "philosophy part" of the funeral on Thursday, which is great because I also have a physics test that same morning. Some background: grandpa wasn't a philosophy professor or anything, he drove a delivery truck for like 35 years, but after he retired he got really into this book called the Zhuangzi (he always called it Chuang Tzu, the old spelling, I guess from whatever translation he first found) and just never really stopped. He had four different translations on the shelf that he'd argue with each other in his head, it was kind of his whole personality by the end. I used to sit with him while he read bits out loud, so apparently that makes me the family expert now.

Here's the actual problem though. It's a normal church service, our pastor's doing most of it, and my grandma (his wife, still alive, very Methodist) is expecting the usual stuff about him being in a better place and watching over us now. I get why she needs that. But grandpa flat out did not believe that, like at all — his whole Zhuangzi thing was that death isn't a punishment or a door to somewhere else, it's just your shape getting handed back, life and death being one continuous thing instead of a good one and a bad one. I don't want to get up there and lie to make people feel better, I genuinely think he'd haunt me for it. Also please do not make it about the drumming-on-a-basin-after-his-wife-died story, my uncle already tried that at the wake and it landed so weird, everyone who's read one paragraph about this guy brings that story up. Grandpa had better material than that — there's a bit where he finds a skull on the road and later uses it as a pillow and it talks to him in a dream about how being dead means no boss over you and no seasons to suffer through or whatever, and a separate bit from when HE was actually dying where he tells his students not to bother burying him properly because heaven and earth are already his coffin and the stars are his jewelry, and something else about your body being more like a loan from the universe that you eventually give back and get to rest. I might be mangling the details since it's been a couple years since he read me the actual pages, so use your judgement on the specifics, I just need the spirit of it right.

Can you actually write the eulogy? Something like 4-5 minutes out loud, meant to go right after the pastor sits down. It has to work for my grandma and my little cousins who won't be tracking any of the philosophy and just need to feel okay by the end, but it also can't be secretly generic funeral-speak with a fortune cookie stapled on for the few people there (mostly just me, maybe my uncle) who would notice if it quietly betrayed what he actually thought. Don't turn it into a lecture where you explain what Daoism is or say "Zhuangzi" nine times, it just needs to hold both things at once without cheating on either one.
