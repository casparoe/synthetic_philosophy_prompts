For my philosophy elective's unit on antinatalism, I've been working through David Benatar's asymmetry argument, and I think I have the four premises straight: the presence of pain is bad, the presence of pleasure is good, the absence of pain is good even when nobody is there to enjoy it, and the absence of pleasure is only bad if there's someone for whom it counts as a deprivation. Given that setup, it seems like whether coming into existence counts as a harm should still come down to how much pain a given life contains relative to its pleasure, so someone whose life turns out overwhelmingly happy, with only minor and occasional suffering, ought to be a clear counterexample to Benatar's conclusion. Is that a fair objection to his argument, or am I missing something about why it's supposed to hold even for lives like that?
