A close friend and I have a running habit, mostly for fun, of putting explicit numbers on how confident we are about uncertain, everyday things: whether a mutual acquaintance's marriage will last, whether a package will arrive by a stated date, and so on. We take the exercise seriously enough to walk each other through our full reasoning rather than just blurting out a gut feeling, and we trust each other completely — neither of us thinks the other is being careless, dishonest, or deliberately withholding some piece of relevant information.

What I can't make sense of is a pattern that shows up almost every time our initial numbers differ. Suppose I say some event is 30 percent likely and she says 70 percent. We'll each lay out our reasoning at length, adjust a little, state our revised numbers, go a further round or two of the same thing, and consistently land somewhere like 40 percent versus 60 percent: closer than where we started, but still clearly apart. Neither of us can point to a specific fact, argument, or consideration that the other has overlooked — we've each just moved partway toward the other's view and then stopped. This happens too reliably, and across too many unrelated topics, to be a coincidence.

I'd always assumed that if two people trust each other's honesty and competence, and are willing to lay out their entire reasoning for one another as many times as it takes, a disagreement about a plain matter of fact or probability ought eventually to disappear completely. Since that isn't what happens, I'm not sure what to conclude. Is one of us, or are both of us, being quietly irrational every time this happens, in some way we could in principle correct? Or is it actually unsurprising, even between two people who trust one another fully and aren't hiding anything on purpose, for a genuine difference of opinion to survive as much honest back-and-forth as we're willing to put into it?
