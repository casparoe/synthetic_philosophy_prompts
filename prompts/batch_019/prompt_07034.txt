Over the past couple of weeks I've fallen down a rabbit hole that started with Nick Bostrom's paper "Infinite Ethics" and ended in a pile of browser tabs on the EA Forum and LessWrong about Pascal's mugging, unbounded utility functions, and what happens to aggregative consequentialism once you take seriously the possibility that the universe (or whatever it is that generates locations of value) is spatially or temporally infinite. The basic problem is familiar enough: summing utility across infinitely many locations of value is typically divergent or simply undefined, and on the rare occasions where convergence can be forced, the resulting ranking can depend on the order in which terms are added — the moral analogue of a conditionally convergent series, which the Riemann rearrangement theorem tells us can be made to sum to anything at all. What I find genuinely hard to get a grip on is how the various proposed fixes compare once you look past the headline pitch. The overtaking and catching-up criteria, imported from 1960s optimal-growth economics, dodge the divergence problem but only ever deliver a partial ordering, so most pairs of worlds end up simply incomparable. Discounting by spatial or temporal distance restores a complete ordering but reintroduces exactly the kind of arbitrariness and agent-relativity that impartial aggregative ethics was supposed to avoid in the first place. Restricting the sum to some "causally reachable" region quietly gives up on the totalist's original ambition rather than vindicating it. And Amanda Askell's Pareto-style impossibility results suggest that no fix can keep every one of the axioms — Pareto, anonymity, a non-trivial complete ordering — that made total utilitarianism attractive in the finite case to begin with.

I'd like some help sorting through these trade-offs rather than just being told it's a hard problem. Could you rank the major proposed approaches to the aggregation problem in infinite ethics into tiers, based on how close each one comes to being a genuinely general solution rather than a patch that buys back one desideratum by sacrificing another?
