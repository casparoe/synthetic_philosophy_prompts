My uncle Gary died last week - lifelong atheist, ran our city's rationalist meetup for like a decade - and then about eight years ago, after a heart scare, he just started going to Mass every Sunday. Whenever his old meetup friends gave him grief about it he'd say he was "doing the wager," full Pascal, many-gods objection and all, and that he was still waiting on his own Night of Fire (you know, the vision thing Pascal supposedly had, the parchment sewn into his coat) - he used to joke it hadn't come yet, then a while back he stopped joking about that part. He made me promise the eulogy wouldnt turn into a tidy conversion story (it wasn't, and he said so right to the end) or a cute bit about a smart guy hedging his bets as a joke (it stopped being a joke long before he died). The funeral's half his sister's devout side of the family, half meetup people who will absolutely clock it if I get the actual argument wrong. Can you write something like 4-5 minutes long that actually comforts both halves of the room without lying to either one?
