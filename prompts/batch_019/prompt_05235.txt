So I've been down a Vienna Circle rabbit hole for my diss and there's a tension in Carnap I can't shake. He's pretty explicit -- e.g. in Philosophy and Logical Syntax -- that value statements aren't cognitively meaningful: "X is good" doesn't express a proposition with a truth value, its basically a dressed-up imperative or wish, same basic move as Ayer's emotivism. But he was also genuinely gung-ho about using science to rationally plan society, and was tight with Neurath, whose whole thing was socialist economic planning -- their 1929 manifesto literally frames the "scientific world-conception" as being in service of social progress. So how do those two commitments actually fit together? If value and political claims aren't truth-apt, what licenses Carnap to treat planned, rationalized social arrangements as objectively better, rather than just a preference he and Neurath happened to share? Is there a principled way to read him as consistent here (some means/ends distinction that saves it, maybe), or is this just a real blind spot in that whole left-Vienna-Circle project?
