So a while back I built a little Kelly-criterion position sizer for a side project (just paper trading, nothing serious), and that sent me down a rabbit hole into "ergodicity economics" (Ole Peters' stuff, they always lead with the St. Petersburg paradox as the motivating example) and a LessWrong sequence I think by Scott Garrabrant called "Geometric Rationality." The pitch, roughly, is: normal expected-value/expected-utility reasoning secretly averages your outcome across a bunch of parallel copies of you (an "ensemble"), which is fine for stuff that doesnt compound, but wealth compounds multiplicatively, so the average you should actually care about is what happens to a single trajectory over time -- and once you switch to that time-average, Kelly-style log-optimal behavior just falls out as objectively correct, not as some arbitrary risk-aversion knob you bolt onto expected utility theory after the fact. Garrabrant's sequence then runs further with this and argues that rationality in general should be built out of geometric expectations instead of arithmetic ones.

I brought this up in our team's #random channel at work (a bunch of other engineers, no economists) and everyone was sold almost immediately, which honestly made me more suspicious -- its got that "one weird trick mainstream econ doesn't want you to know" energy. So I want a real steelmanned pushback rather than a knee-jerk one: please argue against the move from "Kelly betting maximizes long-run growth for repeated, reinvested, multiplicative gambles" to the much bigger claims that geometric/time-average reasoning is THE objectively correct standard of rationality in general, and that ordinary expected-utility theory is smuggling in a false "ergodicity" assumption it owes us an answer for. I don't want the standard Samuelson-style "just take the arithmetic mean, you fools" one-liner, since that feels like it talks past the ergodicity framing instead of engaging it -- I want something that actually grapples with the time-average vs. ensemble-average distinction on its own terms, and is honest about which part of the critique, if any, actually survives.
