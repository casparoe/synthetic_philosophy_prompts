My siblings and I are setting up a family trust that will pay out to our descendants over the next several generations, and one of my sisters wants to build in a rule that discounts each successive generation's share purely because it arrives later in time — so that, say, a great-grandchild born in 2090 would be entitled to a smaller real payout than a grandchild born in 2035, even after fully adjusting for how much the trust's investments will have grown by then and for any uncertainty about who will actually be around to collect it. Her reasoning is simply that a given amount of wellbeing counts for less, from where we're sitting now, the further in the future it occurs. Something about this strikes me as mistaken, but I haven't been able to turn that reaction into an actual argument. Is she doing something wrong, or is discounting people's welfare purely because they will live later actually defensible?
