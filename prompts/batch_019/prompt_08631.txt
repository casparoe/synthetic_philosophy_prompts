I'm the younger sibling giving the toast at my sister's wedding next month, and I've made this way harder for myself than it needed to be, because I've been reading rationalist blogs since the Overcoming Bias days and it's rotted my brain in a specific way. The standard toast move is some version of "what were the odds of you two meeting," delivered as a cute rhetorical flourish, and I physically can't say it like that anymore, because I know the question isn't well-posed until you fix a reference class or a sampling procedure -- it's basically Bertrand's paradox. Ask "what's the probability a random chord of a circle is longer than the side of an inscribed equilateral triangle" and you can get 1/3, 1/2, or 1/4 depending on whether you randomize over the endpoints, over a radius, or over the midpoint, and every method sounds perfectly "uniform" and "unbiased" right up until you compare it to the others. My sister already knows this bit -- we had a whole argument about it one Christmas, shes a data scientist and did not appreciate being told her intuitive prior was incoherent -- so if I just do a generic "isn't probability wild" toast she will clock the fudge immediately and be a little disappointed in me.

What I actually want is a toast that uses the real structure of the paradox -- the way the principle of indifference smuggles in a "natural" parametrization that was never actually privileged, and maybe even the wrinkle that Jaynes's whole invariance-based fix for Bertrand doesn't fully rescue the idea that there's one true way to be "equally uncertain" in general -- to land on something genuinely warm about the two of them, not a stats lecture and not a smug "well, actually, love can't be quantified" gotcha. About a fifth of the room will know exactly what I'm doing; everyone else just needs it to be a good, moving wedding toast, full stop. Can you write it for me? Something that reads aloud in under five minutes feels about right.
