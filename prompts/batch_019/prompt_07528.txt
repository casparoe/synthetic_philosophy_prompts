Quine's gavagai thought experiment is often taken to show that a radical translator can never be certain whether a native informant's term for a passing rabbit should be rendered as "rabbit," "undetached rabbit part," or "rabbit stage." This looks like an unremarkable evidential problem rather than a deep philosophical one: surely the linguist could settle the matter by asking further clarifying questions, such as whether the gavagai present now counts as the "same" gavagai encountered a moment ago, or by pointing to a detached rabbit's ear and asking whether the term still applies. Why, then, does Quine maintain that the choice between these translations remains indeterminate even in principle, rather than merely undetermined by the fieldwork conducted so far?
