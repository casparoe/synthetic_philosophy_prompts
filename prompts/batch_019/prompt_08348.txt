I've got this habit I picked up years ago from reading way too much LessWrong and Astral Codex Ten: I keep a running spreadsheet of "beliefs I hold" — everything from political predictions to random nutrition facts to stuff about my own field — and next to each one I write a confidence percentage. Its basically a much more obsessive, never-resetting version of the thing Scott Alexander does with his yearly predictions post. The whole point was calibration training: if I say I'm 90% confident about a hundred different things, roughly ten of them should turn out false, and I wanted to actually check myself against that instead of just vibing.

Here's what's been bugging me and my rationalist reading group lately. Say I've got 500 rows now, and just to make the math easy, imagine they're all sitting around 95% confidence — each one individually something I'd happily bet a lot of money on. Multiply that out, though, and the odds that literally every single row is simultaneously correct comes out to something like one in a hundred billion, basically zero. So I'm extremely confident in each individual claim AND extremely confident that my spreadsheet as a whole contains at least a handful of false rows, with no way to say which ones. Someone in the group pointed out this is the same shape as that old puzzle about a big fair lottery, where you're sure each specific ticket is a loser but also sure some ticket wins, and apparently there's a version involving a book author who trusts every sentence she's written but still writes in the preface that a book this size definately contains an error somewhere. I guess this stuff has an actual name in the epistemology literature — we just don't know the resolution, only that it's treated as a genuine paradox and not a rookie mistake.

So — could you lay out the most important implications of this for how I should actually treat my spreadsheet and my overall epistemic self-image? Once I notice the paradox, am I obligated to knock down my confidence in individual rows, is the whole "hold beliefs at X% confidence" framework secretly incoherent, or is there a principled way to keep "I'm 95% on each of these" and "I'm ~100% sure some of these are wrong" at the same time without that being crazy? I'd like the actual mechanics of how this is supposed to work, not just a hand-wave about nobody being perfect.
