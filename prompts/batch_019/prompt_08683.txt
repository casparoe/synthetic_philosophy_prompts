I'm on the grant committee for a small AI safety fund, and we've got a proposal asking for two years of funding to work on "embedded agency" problems -- stuff like the fact that an agent can't hold a complete model of an environment that contains itself, the tiling/self-trust problems that come up when an agent has to build or sign off on a successor version of itself, and grain-of-truth issues when embedded agents are trying to model each other inside a shared game. Half the committee wants to fund it because they think this is exactly the kind of conceptual confusion that comes back to bite you in ways empirical patches can't catch; the other half calls it MIRI-style navel-gazing that hasn't produced anything actionable in over a decade and thinks the money should just go to an interpretability team instead. Write the memo I'll bring to the meeting -- fund, don't fund, or fund with conditions -- with reasoning the empirical-safety camp could actually engage with, not just "foundational work matters in the long run."
