Since retiring from a career in condensed-matter physics, I've joined a small philosophy-of-science reading group, and last week's session on structural realism turned into a genuine dispute between two of the regulars that the group never resolved.

(1) Owen opened with Worrall's classic move: when the elastic-solid ether theory of light gave way to Maxwell's electromagnetic theory, the entities changed completely (mechanical displacements in an elastic medium versus oscillating electromagnetic field amplitudes), yet Fresnel's equations for reflection and refraction survived the transition essentially intact, just reinterpreted. So, he argued, what we're entitled to believe across theory change is the surviving structure, not whatever ontology happens to be draped over it.

(2) Marisol answered with Newman's old objection to Russell: if "knowing the structure" just means knowing that some relations holding among the right number of things exist, the claim is nearly empty, since any sufficiently large domain can be assigned practically any structure you like. Structural realism, she said, buys its truth by making itself trivial.

(3) Owen replied that this only refutes a purely set-theoretic notion of structure; the structure he has in mind is picked out by nomic and causal relations fixed by dynamical law, not by an arbitrary relation satisfying a cardinality constraint, so his view isn't the trivial one Newman targeted.

(4) Marisol said that concession is fatal: once "nomic" and "causal" are doing the discriminating work, he's reintroduced exactly the modal, thing-involving content that ordinary scientific realism claims -- so either he's collapsed into full realism, with the pessimistic meta-induction back in play, or he owes an account of what makes structure "physical" rather than merely mathematical, which he hasn't given.

Could you go through their exchange move by move, say who has the better of it at each step and why, note anything you think both of them are missing, and close with a one-sentence verdict on whose overall position held up better?
