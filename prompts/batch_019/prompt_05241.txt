So I'm a third-year in our HPS program and I've been chewing on the hole argument for my qualifying paper, and I want a gut check before I hand this in Friday since my advisor's at a conference all week. Quick recap of the setup so we're on the same page: you take a vacuum "hole" region H in a GR spacetime model (M,g), pick a diffeomorphism d that's the identity everywhere outside H but smoothly non-trivial inside it, and drag g along by d to get a new field g* = d*g. Because the field equations are generally covariant, (M,g*) solves them too, and its indistinguishable from (M,g) everywhere outside H, right up to the boundary, while disagreeing about what happens at particular points inside it. If you're a manifold substantivalist - points are real, individual things with identities independent of whatever fields are draped over them - you seem stuck saying these are two distinct possible histories of the universe, so a complete specification of everything outside the hole doesn't fix what happens inside it. That's the indeterminism Earman and Norton use as a reductio against substantivalism in their old "Hole Story" paper.

Here's the bit I actually want pushback on. The standard substantivalist dodge is to go "sophisticated" - deny that points have haecceities, i.e. deny that "which point is which" is a further fact over and above the qualitative/metrical structure, so g and g* aren't rival possibilities, just the same possibility described twice. My worry (maybe not original, but I haven't found anyone stating it this bluntly) is that this concedes the whole game to the relationalist: if sameness of a point across models just is sameness of qualitative role, points were never doing any independent metaphysical work to begin with, and "substantivalism" survives in name only. I contrast this with Maudlin's move of just accepting the extra indeterminism as a harmless cost of taking points seriously (something like his metrical essentialism), which seems way more honest, even if its a harder sell. To back up the "no independent work" claim I sketch an analogy to gauge freedom in electromagnetism (A vs A + ∇χ giving the same E and B fields), but im not 100% sure the analogy is carrying the weight I want it to.

Could you tell me: (1) am I representing Earman-Norton and the sophisticated substantivalist position fairly, or is there a standard reply that guts my objection and I just haven't run into it yet; (2) does the gauge-freedom analogy actually support the point I'm making or is it doing more rhetorical work than logical work; and (3) is "relationalism wearing a substantivalist costume" too cute/dismissive a framing for a quals paper, even if its basically right? Go ahead and be brutal, I'd rather you tear into it now than have my committee do it in April.
