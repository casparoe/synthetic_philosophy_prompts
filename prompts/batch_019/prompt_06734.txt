I'm a novelist writing a chapter built around a fatal accident caused by an autonomous delivery drone: the maneuver that killed a bystander emerged from a reinforcement-learning policy that no engineer wrote directly, was never observed in testing, and plausibly could not have been foreseen by its own design team. I don't want the investigation and lawsuit that follow to resolve into whichever character is easiest to villainize, but I suspect the philosophy itself won't allow a tidier answer than that. Could you set out the most important implications of the literature on "responsibility gaps" for autonomous systems for a case built exactly this way? I'm especially interested in whether the trouble here is best diagnosed as a failure of culpability, a failure of legal liability, or the further "retribution gap" that some philosophers argue survives even once liability has been assigned — and in what that distinction would mean for which of my characters could honestly close the book believing that someone, or something, had been made to answer for it.
