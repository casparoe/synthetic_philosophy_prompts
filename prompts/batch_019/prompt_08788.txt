My aunt died a couple weeks ago and I'm doing her eulogy this Saturday. She was deep into the effective altruism world, specifically the wild animal suffering stuff -- donated to Wild Animal Initiative, read all of Brian Tomasik's essays, would explain to anyone who'd listen that most wild animals are r-selected and die young in some awful way (starvation, parasites, getting eaten alive) so "nature" isn't the gentle balanced thing people picture, and nature docs are basically propaganda for skipping over that part. Before she died she told my mom, very specifically, that she did not want the eulogy to go anywhere near "she's out there at peace with the animals now" or any variation on nature-as-a-happy-place imagery, since that's the exact view she spent decades arguing against. Problem is it's mostly her neighbors and book club coming, not one of them has heard of suffering-focused ethics. Can you write the eulogy? It has to actually land as comforting for that room, not just technically avoid contradicting her.
