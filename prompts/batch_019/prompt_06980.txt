For nine years I've freelanced for a science magazine. My editor has just told me, kindly, that our arrangement ends after four more assignments, once her section moves entirely in-house. By design, the four assignments get bigger and better-paid as they go, building toward a cover story that pays several times what the first one will. Until now we've each routinely done more than the contract strictly requires -- she pushes my pitches upstairs and pays promptly, I put in reporting hours the fee doesn't cover -- because that is what earned each of us the next, better stage. A friend of mine, who is unsentimental about this sort of thing, points out that the logic changes now that the end is in sight: nothing stops the magazine from quietly handing the cover story to a staff writer at the last moment, since there will be no future pitches of mine left to protect by staying loyal. And if that risk is real, there was no point putting real effort into the assignment before that one either, and so on, back to the piece I am filing this week. It's a tidy chain of reasoning, and I cannot find the broken link in it, yet its conclusion -- that neither of us should ever have bothered building this relationship in the first place -- feels obviously wrong. Should I keep working these last four assignments the way I have the last nine years, or does my friend have a point that I'd rather not admit?
