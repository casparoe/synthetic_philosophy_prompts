I'm a PhD student in political science specializing in political behavior, and my dissertation uses age-period-cohort models to try to separate how much of the shift in people's policy views over a lifetime is a genuine effect of aging itself, as opposed to simply reflecting the generation someone belongs to (a cohort effect) or the historical moment they happen to be living through (a period effect). Lately this research keeps colliding with an argument my uncle makes at every family dinner. I'm considerably more sympathetic than he is to redistributive social spending, and whenever the subject comes up he says some version of: "You'll come around to my view eventually — everyone does, once they've got more to lose." The irritating part is that the honest answer from my own corner of the literature is that he isn't simply wrong: net of cohort and period effects, several studies do find a small but genuine aging effect pulling attitudes on exactly this question in his direction.

That finding has been bothering me in a way that seems to outrun the family argument. If I really do expect my own view on something to shift over the next thirty years for no other reason than that I will be thirty years older, doesn't ordinary consistency require me to move at least partway there now, instead of smugly waiting around for it to happen to me anyway? But that inference also seems to prove far too much: if it turned out that the aging effect runs entirely through, say, accumulating loss aversion or declining cognitive flexibility, rather than through exposure to better arguments or evidence, it would obviously be a mistake to update on it now — in the same way that predicting that a future stroke will leave me convinced the moon landing was faked gives me no reason to doubt the moon landing today. So I seem to have two cases that share exactly the same surface structure — "I can predict what I will come to believe" — yet pull in opposite directions, and I can't articulate what actually separates them. Is my uncle right that the mere predictability of his view's eventual triumph already counts as evidence for it, or is he quietly trading on a distinction he hasn't earned?
