Ok so this is going to sound like a weirdly specific thing to be stuck on, but here goes. A while back I found a free copy of Tolstoy's "What Is Art" online - had no idea he even wrote a whole book of theory, I only knew him for the novels - and it kind of messed with my head. He spends the whole book building this theory that real art is basically just "infecting" other people with a feeling you genuinely lived through yourself, and that you can judge how good a piece of art is by how sincere it is, how clearly and specifically it gets the feeling across, and especially by how many different kinds of people (not just educated types) it can reach. Then he turns around and uses that exact same yardstick to trash Shakespeare, most of Beethoven (including the ninth symphony), pretty much all of opera, and even his own books, War and Peace included - while going out of his way to praise stuff like Uncle Tom's Cabin and plain folk tales, becuase ordinary people everywhere can feel them without any special training.

Now I've got a nephew who is completely obsessed with the Marvel movies, we've probably watched a dozen of them together at this point, and I can't stop wondering what old Tolstoy would've made of the whole superhero-movie machine if he somehow saw one. On one hand these movies are about as universal as it gets - a kid in Ohio and a kid in Manila will tear up at the exact same scene, no education or special taste required, which is basically his whole standard for "real" art right there. On the other hand he was deeply suspicious of anything produced by a huge, expensive, profit-driven operation aiming at spectacle instead of coming from one person's genuine need to share a feeling, and he had zero patience for art that just recycles a crowd-pleasing formula instead of expressing something specific and individual.

So using his actual reasoning, not just a generic "beauty is subjective" answer - what do you honestly think Tolstoy would conclude about something like the Marvel movies, or blockbuster franchise movies in general? I want an actual verdict out of you, not a wishy-washy "he might have said this, he might have said that." But please dont just pick the flattering side either - engage with whichever consideration actually points against your conclusion, because if I'm being honest this one could plausibly go either way with him.
