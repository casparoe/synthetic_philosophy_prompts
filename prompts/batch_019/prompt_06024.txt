I'm writing a piece on the "you're a hypocrite, so you don't get to call me out" move that's everywhere in online arguments, and I fell down a rabbit hole in the philosophy of blame while researching it. There's a genuine split over whether hypocrisy strips someone of the *standing* to blame -- not whether they're right, but whether they get to say it. One camp thinks blaming presupposes something like equal moral footing between blamer and target, so a hypocrite forfeits the right to say it even when they're 100% correct on the merits. The other camp thinks that's dressed-up tu quoque -- "standing" is a philosopher's invention, and whether blame is apt depends only on whether the target did wrong, full stop, not on who's delivering it. Could you write a dialogue between two philosophers on opposite sides, really engaging each other's strongest moves rather then talking past one another? I want the skeptic to actually answer the "who are you to talk?" intuition instead of waving it away, and the standing-defender to answer the worry that their view means a sufficiently corrupt group could never legitimately blame each other for anything. Keep it tight and don't feel like you need a tidy resolution at the end.
