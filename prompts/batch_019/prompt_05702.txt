I write about science and ideas for a national magazine, and in three weeks I'm giving the best man's toast at the wedding of my closest friend from graduate school, now a quantitative trader who has spent the better part of a decade trying to convert everyone he knows to what he calls geometric rationality: the Kelly-criterion-derived view that a decision should be judged not by its expected value across some imagined ensemble of parallel worlds, but by the growth rate of the single path you actually live through time. He has made this case to me, unprompted, at two separate dinners, and he means every word of it. About a third of the guests are colleagues from his trading desk who will notice immediately if I get the idea wrong; the rest, including his new mother-in-law, have never heard of Kelly betting and just want a good toast. Please write it -- roughly three minutes, meant to be spoken aloud -- so that the distinction between ensemble averages and time averages is doing real work in what it says about commitment and marriage, rather than sitting there as trivia, and so that it never reaches for the two jokes that are easiest to make here and actually wrong on his own terms: praising him for "finally going all in," and calling the marriage his biggest diversification move yet.
