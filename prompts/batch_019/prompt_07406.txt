A guy I know, Ray, runs a small lawn-care business. Last spring he rolled out an aggressive 'lock in now' discount for the whole season, mainly because he'd just brought his two nephews on part-time and needed enough work lined up to justify paying them. Theres only one other full-time lawn guy in our town, Gary, who's been at it 20-some years, and Ray's discount pulled almost all of Gary's regulars away in a few weeks — Gary ended up selling his mower and taking a job at the hardware store. At a cookout, my friend Doug said what Ray did wasn't really different from setting out to ruin Gary on purpose, since there's no way Ray didn't know what would happen to the one other full-time guy in a town this size. Ray said that's not fair — he never once thought about Gary when he set his price, he was thinking about his nephews and his bills, and there's a real difference between doing something in order to hurt someone and doing something just knowing it'll hurt them. Doug said Gary's just as broke either way, so he doesn't see what work that difference is even doing. Ray shot back that if it doesn't matter, nobody could ever compete for a customer without being a bad person. Doug said fine, but this wasn't some abstract competitor lost to market forces — Ray's price is literally what pulled Gary's customers away, it was one specific guy everybody in town could've named in advance, and that's got to count for something even if Ray's right that he didn't want it to happen. Neither budged and it's been bugging me since. Can you walk through their back and forth and say who's got the better of it at each point, not just who wins overall, and whether you think they're both missing something?
