I spent about 35 years as an experimental physicist, and now that I'm retired I've been teaching myself philosophy, currently down a rabbit hole on voting and collective decision-making. Reading about the Condorcet Jury Theorem got me thinking about the lab: we'd never average a precise reading together with a couple of sloppy ones without weighting for precision, or the sloppy ones just add noise. So I'm suspicious of the usual takeaway people draw from the theorem, that stacking on more independent, better-than-chance voters can only help (or at least never hurt) accuracy. Could you build me a small, concrete example - real probabilities, say 3 jurors - where each juror is independent and individually still more likely right than wrong, but a plain unweighted majority vote of all three ends up LESS reliable than just going with the most competent juror alone? I want to see the actual numbers work out, not just be told its possible.
