Okay so this is going to sound like a weird ask but bear with me. Last night me and my friend Jess got into a genuinely heated two-hour argument that started over something dumb (a GoFundMe for a stranger's medical bills) and spiraled into her insisting that nobody can ever really know whether an act of "doing good" makes the world better once you follow it far enough downstream. Her point was basically: saving one persons life this year ripples into who they marry, who gets born because of that and who doesn't get born instead, who needs jobs and land and water a generation from now, and so on forever, so calling any action "net positive" is really just a guess wearing a fact's clothes. She actually used the word "clueless" for this, like its a whole recognized thing philosophers argue about and not just her being difficult. I went to bed annoyed and only half convinced.

Then this morning my uncle Ray died, and because I'm apparently the designated writer in the family, I've been asked to give the eulogy on Friday. Ray was about the most sincere effective-altruist type I've ever known in real life — gave away something like a third of his income for 25 years, almost all to bed net and deworming charities, kept a spreadsheet of estimated "lives saved" that he'd update every year like it was his taxes. But the maddening/wonderful thing about him is he was also the guy who'd argue late into the night, totally cheerfully, that even he had no real idea whether his giving made things better once you traced out everything it eventually touches — more kids surviving means more people needing land and jobs and water decades later, and who's to say how that nets out in the end. He actually left written instructions for the funeral (so on brand) asking that nobody get up and confidently declare he'd "saved X lives" as some settled tally, because he thought that kind of tidy accounting was basically dishonest.

So now I'm stuck writing this with last night's argument still ringing in my ears. Most of the room on Friday is going to be family and old coworkers who just want to hear "Ray made the world better," simple and comforting, and I don't want to hand them something false, but I also don't want to stand up and lecture forty grieving people about the unknowability of long-run consequences. Could you help me draft the eulogy, something like 4-5 minutes read aloud, that's honest about the uncertainty he genuinely held (the "cluelessness" thing, basically) while still landing as a warm tribute to a guy who kept doing the good he could see in front of him anyway? I feel like there's something quietly moving in that, I just can't get the words to come out right on my own.
