I mostly cover science and ideas for my paper, but I also sit on our standards and ethics committee, and we've got a nasty one to sort out this week. Back in March a reporter got a tip -- with the city's own inspection memos attached -- saying a support beam on the Ninth Street footbridge was corroded and had already been flagged twice internally. It sat behind two other investigations for about six weeks, nobody flagged it as urgent, and then a section of the deck gave way and hurt several people. Half the commitee wants a statement that says plainly 'our delay contributed to this,' plus real fixes -- an actual deadline system for safety tips, an escalation path, that sort of thing. The other half says that's hindsight bias playing dress-up: the city and the engineering firm sat on the exact same memos and did nothing, hundreds of people crossed that bridge every day and said nothing, and we don't have anything like a legal duty to report hazards by some deadline the way an inspector does -- so why is our six week delay 'a cause' and not just one more non-event among thousands of other non-events that also would've prevented this. I don't think that objection is crazy but it also feels like a dodge. Could you draft the actual memo I'd circulate before we meet -- a real recommendation, do we own some responsibility here or not, what specifically changes and by when -- and make sure it takes the 'why single us out' point seriously instead of just gesturing at it and moving on.
