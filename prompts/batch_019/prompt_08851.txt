I'm a software engineer on my company's internal committee that signs off on model-card language before release. Our newest model, when asked to explain its own answers, produces fluent first-person lines like 'I believe you want the code, not an explanation.' Marketing wants the model card to present these as genuine insight into the model's thinking; our red-team lead wants a flat disclaimer that it's pure metaphor, since the model has no beliefs at all, only next-token statistics.

Our outside ethics advisor complicated things by invoking Davidson's radical interpretation: on his view we only ever work out what someone believes and what their words mean together, as a single package, by charitably assuming they're rational and mostly right about a world we share with them — and that's no less true when we interpret each other than when we interpret a model. But she conceded that Davidson's picture also requires the interpreted party to be triangulating with us, jointly responding to shared objects over time, which fits a person far more naturally than a model whose 'memory' ends with the session.

We need to settle our house style before the model ships next week. Please draft the committee's short decision memo: a specific recommendation for what the model card should say about these self-reports, and an honest account of the strongest reason to doubt that the Davidsonian argument settles the disagreement.
