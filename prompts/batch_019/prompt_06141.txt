Over the past few months I have had several migraines with visual auras: shimmering, jagged arcs of light overlaid on whatever I happen to be looking at, phenomenologically seamless with ordinary seeing until I register that my desk lamp does not actually do that. The experience left me reading my way into the philosophy of perception, and for the podcast I host, I have booked a philosopher who defends disjunctivism about perceptual experience as a response to the argument from illusion. I do not want an episode in which she simply restates that veridical perception and hallucination are not the same fundamental kind of mental state. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that press on where the view actually struggles: at least one on the causal "screening-off" objection (if an aura and an ordinary visual experience share a proximate cortical mechanism, why does same-cause-same-effect reasoning not force a common underlying state on us in both cases?), one on whether a merely negative, indiscriminability-based characterization of the "bad case" is an honest account of what is happening during a hallucination or just a placeholder for one, and one on how this metaphysical disjunctivism relates to Pritchard's epistemological disjunctivism — whether the epistemic version is parasitic on the metaphysical one or could be held independently of naive realism. For each question, include a sharp follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give.
