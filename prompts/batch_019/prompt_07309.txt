So this went down in a rationalist-adjacent discord I'm in -- someone linked the old ProPublica "Machine Bias" piece on the COMPAS recidivism tool and it turned into a whole thing. Quick context incase it matters: ProPublica found that black defendants who didn't reoffend got flagged as high-risk way more often than white defendants who didn't reoffend, and Northpointe (the company that makes COMPAS) shot back that the tool's fine because it's equally well-calibrated across race -- i.e. among people given the same score, the actual reoffense rate is about the same regardless of race. Two regulars, call them Nina and Derek, went back and forth on it:

(1) Nina: the false-positive gap alone shows the tool is biased against black defendants. Innocent people are getting branded dangerous at very different rates depending on race, that's just bias, full stop.

(2) Derek: that's the wrong test though. If a 7 means the same thing whether you're black or white -- i.e. roughly the same fraction of 7s actually reoffend either way -- in what sense is the tool itself biased? Seems like whatever bias there is must be upstream of the tool.

(3) Nina: calibration's a pretty weak standard, it only says the tool isn't lying to you *on average within a bucket*. It says nothing about who eats the cost of its mistakes. You can be perfectly calibrated and still dump almost all the false positives on one group.

(4) Derek: fine, so fix that directly -- use different score thresholds for different races until the false-positive rates line up.

(5) Nina: except now the tool is explicitly conditioning on race, which is its own legal and moral mess, and a given score no longer means the same thing for a black defendant as a white one. You didn't remove the unfairness, you just relocated it.

(6) Derek: so calibration isnt good enough for you, and the direct fix for what you don't like about it isn't acceptable either. At some point it sounds like you're against risk-scoring defendants at all, which is a much bigger claim than "this particular tool is biased."

I don't know enough of the actual math here to tell who's winning at each step, as opposed to who just sounds more confident. Can you go through moves 1-6 and say where each of them is right or wrong, and then say whether Nina and Derek are actually disagreeing about facts at this point, or about something the facts can't settle?
