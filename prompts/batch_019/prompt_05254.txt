I'm a novelist working on a chapter built around a long-deferred argument between two characters who were inseparable in graduate school and have barely spoken since: one is now a poet who has always insisted that her best lines name a feeling she recognized before she had any word for it, and would recognize again if it returned; the other became an academic philosopher who has taught Wittgenstein's private language argument for twenty years and considers that very claim exactly what the argument is meant to rule out. Write their reunion as a real reconstruction of the diary case from Philosophical Investigations §258: he should press her, step by step, on what could possibly show she has correctly re-identified the old feeling rather than merely seeming to herself to have done so. She is not a pushover: she should turn her own craft against him — citing phrases she coined that felt exactly right for years before any reader ever confirmed them — and she should know the text well enough to remind him that Wittgenstein himself refused to call the sensation "a nothing." Write the full exchange, with each of them actually answering the other's last point rather than restating their own position, and end it with the two of them agreeing on what would have to be true for either of them to be right — without settling which of them actually is.
