So in my AI safety elective we spent a chunk of last week on this thing called the "commitment races problem," which as I understand it is basically: if two very capable agents (could be AIs, could be whole civilizations, doesn't really matter) both know that whoever locks in their bargaining position first and hardest gets the better deal, then each side has an incentive to commit to their stance as early and as irrevocably as possible, even before they've had time to actually think it through, because hesitating just means the other guy commits first and you're stuck accepting whatever they decided. The scary part is that if both sides reason this way, you can end up with two totally incompatible commitments that neither side can walk back, even though everyone would've come out ahead if they'd both just slowed down for five minutes. My professor kept comparing it to the doomsday device in Dr. Strangelove but said the underlying issue shows up anywhere credible commitment is possible, way beyond nukes.

I get the game theory of it ok (kinda reminds me of the Schelling stuff on credible threats we did earlier in the course), but I'm having trouble picturing how the "race" part would actually feel from the inside, like what its like to be one of the agents realizing mid-commitment that your maybe making a huge mistake but literally can't stop yourself. Could you write me a short story, maybe 700-1000ish words, that's really built around this dynamic instead of just being a generic conflict-escalation story? Set it wherever you want, two AIs, two alien civilizations making first contact, rival companies, doesn't matter, as long as the actual mechanics of the race (the pressure to commit fast, the point of no return, the mutual regret after) are what drives the plot. Bonus points if it has a bit of a darkly ironic edge to it.
