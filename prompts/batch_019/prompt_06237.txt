I recently came across an exchange in the comments under a LessWrong linkpost about Bostrom's work on infinite ethics, specifically the "infinitarian paralysis" problem: if the universe contains infinite amounts of both good and bad, as some cosmological models allow, then no finite action changes the total sum of value, so total utilitarianism seemingly can't say that curing a plague is better than doing nothing. Two commenters, whom I'll call M and K, went back and forth over it, and I'd like a neutral adjudication of their exchange.

(1) M argued that infinitarian paralysis is fatal to total utilitarianism: an axiology that cannot distinguish curing a plague from idleness, once a plausible infinite cosmology is granted, has failed at the one job an action-guiding theory of the good must do, and should be dropped for something like a person-affecting or agent-relative view.

(2) K replied that this is fixable without abandoning totalism: rank acts not by the total sum of the resulting world but by the finite causal difference they make, holding fixed whatever lies outside one's causal reach. On this construal, curing the plague still comes out determinately better.

(3) M answered that this is no minor patch: total utilitarians standardly refuse to discount anyone's welfare on grounds of causal or spatial distance, precisely because such boundary-drawing is arbitrary, so restricting the sum to one's own causal contribution imports exactly the move totalism rules out elsewhere, quietly converting a theory of the good into a theory that tracks only local differences.

(4) K responded that the boundary isn't arbitrary but just difference-making made explicit: any comparison of two acts, even for an ordinary finitist, already ignores whatever welfare doesn't vary between them, so nothing new is being conceded.

(5) M pressed once more: on standard cosmological assumptions, an infinite universe with only finitely many possible local arrangements of matter implies infinitely many exact causal duplicates of K elsewhere in space, making the same "locally good" choice for the same reasons; a totalist who sums over everyone's welfare has to sum over those duplicates' effects too, so paralysis reappears one level up, and K's fix has merely relocated the problem.
