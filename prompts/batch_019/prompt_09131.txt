Popular treatments of Roko's basilisk usually stop at noting its resemblance to Pascal's wager and conclude that it can be dismissed as a curiosity. But the thought experiment was originally framed within the acausal decision theories under discussion on LessWrong at the time -- timeless decision theory and updateless decision theory -- and the standard rebuttal offered from within that same tradition is not simply "call its bluff," but a specific technical claim about the disposition toward threats that those decision theories recommend. Please explain why proponents of updateless decision theory argue that a rational agent reasoning this way would not be moved by the basilisk's threat, and why this conclusion is supposed to follow from the formal structure of the decision theory itself, rather than from ordinary anti-blackmail bravado.
