I'm a novelist, and at the party after a reading from my last book, two friends of mine fell into a disagreement I still haven't been able to settle. It started because both of them had cried at the death, in the final chapter, of Teodor, the character who anchors the book's last third.

Helena said the tears embarrassed her a little in retrospect, because there was no one to grieve: what she'd actually felt, she suspected, was a grief-shaped reaction with nothing behind it. Marcus objected that no mere reaction is so finely tuned to who died, how, and after what four hundred pages of history — hers clearly was. Helena answered that being tuned to content only shows she was moved, not that what she felt was specifically grief: an instrumental piece of music with no story attached at all has reduced her to tears before, and nobody calls that mourning. Marcus said the cases aren't parallel, because the music represents no one in particular, while the novel had her holding a detailed, specific person in mind for hours, and losing that mental hold has the shape of a real loss whether or not she ever believed he existed. Helena replied that this can't be the whole story either, since she could sit down right now and invent an equally detailed death for a total stranger and feel nothing like what the book produced — so it isn't detailed representation doing the work, it's the fact that the text fixes, for her and Marcus alike, exactly who he was and what happened to him, which starts to look like a belief wearing a disguise. Marcus's last word was that believing something is true in the story is not believing that anyone actually died, that she can hold the first with total conviction while entirely lacking the second, and that this isn't an incoherence needing to be explained away — it's simply what fiction is for.

Could you go through their exchange move by move, saying who has the better of it at each specific point rather than declaring an overall winner, and close by noting what, if anything, both of them are still missing?
