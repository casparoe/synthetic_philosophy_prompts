I sit on the sourcing-and-standards committee at the outlet I write for, and I've had this weird bit of philosophy rattling around in my head since I did a piece last spring on trust and misinformation. Researching it sent me down a rabbit hole on the epistemology of testimony — the old Hume vs. Reid fight over whether believing what someone tells you has to be backed by independent reasons (Hume's side, roughly: you need grounds, like a track record, for thinking testimony in general is reliable, before any particular bit of it can justify belief) or whether trust in other people is just a basic, default source of knowledge that doesn't reduce to anything else (Reid's side: we're entitled to believe people unless we have specific reason not to). I think the label the philosophers I interviewed used was "reductionism vs. anti-reductionism," though don't hold me to the exact term.

Anyway, it stopped being abstract this week. One of our investigative reporters has a story about a mid-level city official leaning on a building inspector to soften a safety report. It rests almost entirely on one source — a former staffer with direct knowledge, visibly reluctant to talk, no obvious axe to grind, whose account fits some circumstantial details (an odd timeline, some email metadata) that are consistent with the story but don't independently prove it. Our written policy, inherited from the old Watergate-era playbook, says no story alleging wrongdoing runs without two independent sources. A second staffer did back up the key detail, except under questioning it turns out they heard it from the first source rather than firsthand — so its not really independent corroboration, more like the same testimony with a second name stapled to it. Half the committee wants to hold the story until we find someone genuinely separate; the other half says the two-source rule is being applied like a superstition here, that the first source's track record and the internal coherence of the account are already enough warrant on their own, and that insisting on "independent confirmation" no matter what doesn't actually track how justified we are. A competitor is reportedly circling the same tip, so we can't sit on this indefinitely.

Could you draft the memo I'll bring to Monday's meeting? I want a real recommendation — run it, hold it, run some hedged/attributed version, whatever's defensible — with a timeframe attached, not a here-are-both-sides summary. And I'd like it to take the "one sufficiently reliable source is enough" camp seriously as a position, rather than just citing the rule at them.
