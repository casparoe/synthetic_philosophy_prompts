For my school's effective altruism club, I've been reading about Toby Ord's concept of "moral trade," where two people with opposing moral views can each end up better off by their own lights by shifting their behavior toward what the other wants, in exchange for the other doing the same—something Ord presents as an attractive hedge against moral uncertainty, since none of us can be fully confident our own moral theory is correct. Please argue that moral trade is not actually well-defined as a form of mutually beneficial exchange, because determining whether a proposed trade leaves either party better off requires comparing amounts of value across rival moral theories, and no one has supplied a non-arbitrary way of making such intertheoretic comparisons. In particular, please address whether a merely ordinal ranking of outcomes within each theory could rescue the idea, or whether the objection really requires the comparisons to be cardinal and interpersonally meaningful.
