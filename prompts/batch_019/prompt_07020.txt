Kind of random context but I (34) got diagnosed with ADHD about two months ago — apparently it's super common for women to get missed until adulthood — and I've been low-key obsessed reading about it ever since. Anyway I brought it up over dinner last weekend and two friends, Mara and Dev, ended up in this whole back-and-forth about whether ADHD even counts as a "real" disorder, which went on long enough that I wrote down the gist afterward, partly because it wouldn't leave my head since its literally my own diagnosis they were arguing about.

It went roughly like this. (1) Mara said ADHD isn't a disorder in any deep sense, just a label for the low end of a continuous, totally normal distribution of attention and impulse control — there's no discrete biological line being crossed, just a cutoff some committee drew, so calling it a disorder is basically a values call wearing a lab coat. (2) Dev said that argument proves way too much, since blood pressure and bone density are also continuous traits with committee-drawn cutoffs and nobody thinks hypertension is fake — what actually matters isn't continuity but that the low tail measurably produces worse outcomes (more accidents, job loss, blown relationships), and that's true independent of anyone's values. (3) Mara came back that "worse outcomes" is exactly where the values are hiding — worse relative to what? Someone with a wandering, hyper-associative attention style might do totally fine, maybe better, as an artist or in a foraging society; it only reads as "bad" relative to a pretty specific, contingent setup (sit still in rows for twelve years, then answer emails for eight hours a day), so Dev's supposedly neutral biological fact is smuggling in a social arrangement. (4) Dev's response was that literally every disease is "bad" only relative to some goal — a heart defect is only bad if you want to keep living and walking around — but that doesn't make disease attributions arbitrary, since nearly everyone shares baseline goals like staying alive, unlike the specific demand to thrive in office work; plus, he said, plenty of ADHD symptoms (losing track of your kid at the playground, forgetting to eat for hours) cause real trouble no matter what the social setup is.

Nobody really conceded anything before we got pulled into other conversation, but I keep turning it over, probably for obvious reasons. Afterward I fell down a bit of a wikipedia hole and it seems like this is basically a version of the naturalism-vs-normativism debate about disease that philosophers of medicine have been having forever. Could you go through moves (1) through (4) and say, for each one, who comes out ahead — or whether it's a genuine wash — and why, rather than just declaring an overall winner? And then tell me what you think both Mara and Dev are still missing that would actually move the debate forward.
