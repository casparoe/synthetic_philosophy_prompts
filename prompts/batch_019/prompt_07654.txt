Ok, weird request but here goes. I started as a junior press aide for our mayor's office two months ago, and for a night class I had to read Hannah Arendt's essay on lying in politics (the Pentagon Papers one) -- it's kind of wrecked how I see my own job. Now my actual boss, Marion, the mayor's spokesperson for almost 30 years and basically the person who trained me, is retiring next month, and I got asked to give the toast.

Half the room will be beat reporters and comms people who've sparred with her for decades and know exactly what the job involves; the other half is her husband, kids, and 80-something mom, who just think she has a demanding job and are super proud of her. I can't say "she always told the truth," that's just false and the press crowd will actually laugh, but I also don't want it to curdle into a cynical inside joke about spin, not with her mom sitting right there.

Arendt's distinction between factual truth and the kind of "image" politics replaces it with, plus her point about the people doing the replacing sometimes fooling themselves too, feels like its actually about Marion's whole career and I can't shake it. Can you write me the toast (4-5 min out loud) where that framework is actually doing the work of praising her honestly, not just sitting there as an easter egg for the people who'll get it?
