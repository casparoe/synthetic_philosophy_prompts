Okay, this is a slightly weird ask but bear with me. One of my closest friends from the old Slate Star Codex comment-section days (like 2014, well before it turned into ACX) is having her first baby in a couple weeks, and she's roped me into giving a short speech at the baby shower this weekend. Backstory that actually matters here: she and her husband are rationalist/EA nerds through and through, and back when they were first deciding whether and when to try for a kid, they had this whole group-chat argument about Parfit's non-identity problem -- the idea that this specific baby could never have shown up as some slightly-improved version of itself, because a different week, even a different day, means a different sperm cell (and maybe a different egg entirely), and therefore a numerically different person, not a luckier draw of the same one. It turned into a running joke between them (theyve been calling the bump "the winning ticket" for months), and I want to build the toast around it, since a decent chunk of the guest list is our local rationalist meetup crowd who will absolutely catch the reference and love it.

Problem is the rest of the room is completely normal family and coworkers who have never heard of Derek Parfit and do not want a philosophy lecture at a baby shower, and underneath that there's a sharper problem: the whole reason this is called a "problem" and not just a cute fact about reproductive biology is that if you follow the argument all the way through, it seems to commit you to saying a kid can't really be wronged by the circumstances of their own conception, no matter how bad, as long as their life clears the bar of being worth living at all -- because the only alternative for that exact kid was never existing, not existing-but-better. That's a genuinely contested implication, and it is not a thought I want landing, even glancingly, at my friend's baby shower. I don't want the speech to sound like it's letting anyone off the hook for anything, and I don't want it to come across like I'm relitigating whether the timing was right for them to have a kid. What I want is just the one actually lovely part of the idea -- that this particular baby, whoever they turn out to be, was never a sure thing, that they're a wildly specific, unrepeatable draw rather than a slot any old kid could've filled -- turned into something warm and a little funny, maybe 3-4 minutes read aloud, that rewards the philosophy nerds for paying attention without ever tipping into a lecture for everyone else. Can you write me that speech?
