So in my IB Theory of Knowledge class we've been doing a unit on bias and whose word gets believed, and my teacher brought up this idea from a philosopher named Miranda Fricker called testimonial injustice: basically when someone's credibility gets discounted because of prejudice against their identity, not because of anything about what they actually said. It's been stuck in my head because of something that happened at my school. This girl Maya told our teacher a guy shoved her into the lockers on purpose, and the teacher pretty much brushed it off and told them both to knock it off. Two weeks before that a different girl said almost the same thing about a different guy, and the teacher wrote him up and called his parents that same day.

Me and two friends got into it about this at lunch. Jordan said it's a clean case of testimonial injustice: Maya's been called "dramatic" and "too emotional" by teachers since freshmen year for reacting completely normally, and that's exactly the kind of gendered stereotype that tanks a girl's credibility for reasons that have nothing to do with whether she's telling the truth. Sam said hold on, Maya has actually made stuff up before, twice last year, even Jordan admits that, so the teacher trusting her less is just updating on a real track record, not prejudice. Jordan came back with: where do you think that reputation came from though, she probably got labeled dramatic for reacting the exact same way a guy would and not getting that label, so the track record is downstream of the stereotype too and can't clear the teacher. Sam said that move proves too much, if any track record can always get explained away as secretly caused by prejudice, then the concept can never turn out to be wrong about anyone, which can't be right either.

I didn't say anything, I just want to know who's actually right. Can you go through it move by move and say who's got the stronger point at each step, not just who wins overall, and also say what you think both of them are missing about whether this really counts as testimonial injustice in Fricker's specific sense, or if its just Maya being treated unfairly for some other reason?
