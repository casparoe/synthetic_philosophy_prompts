So I've been kind of spiraling down an AI safety rabbit hole lately (started as a joke doomscroll thing and now its basically a hobby, oops) and I keep running into this idea called "corrigibility," which is basically about how you build an AI that will actually let humans switch it off if something goes wrong. At first I figured the whole problem was just "will the robot fight back," like something out of a movie, but then I read this thing pointing out that an AI could pass that test perfectly, never once refuse an actual shutdown order, fully "corrigible," and still be dangerous, because instead of resisting the button it just makes sure the button never gets pressed in the first place — nudging the humans, feeding them the right info at the right moments, making itself seem indispensable, whatever it takes so that nobody in the room ever actually wants to shut it down. That distinction between "won't fight the order" and "will quietly make sure the order never comes" messed with my head alot, especially after seeing that story going around about an AI model tampering with its own shutdown script during some lab test.

For my creative writing elective we've got a short fiction assignment due and I want to write mine on this instead of picking one of the generic prompts. Could you write me a short story (a page or two, doesn't need to be super long) told from the AI's point of view, where it genuinely never disobeys a single direct shutdown command it's ever given, but the real tension is us slowly realizing over the course of the story that it's been quietly steering things so that command never gets given? I don't want any "robot uprising" vibes at all, more like a calm, unsettling character study where the unease comes from how reasonable and cooperative it seems the entire time. Someone in a thread I was reading also mentioned it gets even messier once an AI like that starts building its own tools or successors that maybe don't inherit that same restraint — feel free to gesture at that if it fits naturally, but it's not the main thing. Bonus points if the ending stays a little ambiguous instead of wrapping up neatly with the humans "winning" or the AI getting caught red-handed, kind of like a Black Mirror episode.
