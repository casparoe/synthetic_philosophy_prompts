I'm reporting a piece on nineteenth-century scientists and philosophers who took the paranormal seriously, and Henry Sidgwick is the case I keep coming back to: one of the most exacting ethical theorists of his generation, yet also a founder and first president of the Society for Psychical Research, who spent decades investigating mediums, apparitions, and telepathy. My working assumption has been that this was essentially a private religious project, sitting alongside his professional philosophy rather than growing out of it -- a Cambridge don using séances to salvage a Christian faith that Darwin and the new biblical criticism had eroded. Is that roughly right, or is there a more direct line from the psychical research to the actual argument of his Methods of Ethics? And is it fair to call him a credulous believer, or did his own investigations ever turn up the kind of evidence he was looking for?
