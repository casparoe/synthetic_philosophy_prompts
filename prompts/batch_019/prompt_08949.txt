My closest friend and I have spent a decade trading merciless critiques of each other's romantic choices, and now that he is finally getting married, I have been asked to give the best man's speech. I want to use it to remind the room exactly how bad his judgment used to be -- except that my own record is at least as poor. Several guests from his philosophy doctoral program are likely to notice this immediately and wonder whether hypocrisy of this kind actually undermines my standing to blame him for a fault we share, or whether that worry is just the tu quoque fallacy dressed up as philosophy. The bride, meanwhile, has asked -- half-joking -- that the toast not turn into "a seminar on hypocrisy." Please write the speech so that it stays warm and funny for the non-philosophers in the room, while giving a real answer to that question, rather than either waving it away as a mere fallacy or concluding that I should simply stay silent.
