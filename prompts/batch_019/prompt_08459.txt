Ever since I retired from experimental physics I've been chewing through philosophy of science and probability theory, and I keep running into two ideas that seem like the same thing wearing different jargon. Theres van Fraassen's "Reflection Principle," which I gather comes out of Dutch-book arguments about credences, and theres "reflective equilibrium," which I ran into reading Rawls on justice. Both seem to boil down to the same core idea: a rational agent's beliefs should line up with whatever they'd conclude after reflecting further on them. So is the Reflection Principle basically just van Fraassen giving Rawls's fuzzier idea some actual mathematical teeth — a formal, probabilistic version of reflective equilibrium? Feels like two different fields quietly reinvented the same wheel and slapped different names on it, unless I'm missing something.
