Over a career in radio astronomy I relied on the maximum-entropy principle whenever I needed to pick a probability distribution, or reconstruct an image, subject to noisy constraints, and I had always assumed this was simply Richard Jeffrey's rule of probability kinematics under another name. Since retiring I have been reading in formal epistemology, and I came across van Fraassen's "Judy Benjamin problem": a soldier begins by assigning equal credence to four zones -- a Headquarters and a Second Company area within each of Red and Blue territory -- and then receives a radio message stating only that, if she is in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 on the Headquarters area. Updating by minimizing cross-entropy relative to her prior moves her credence that she is in Red territory at all away from one-half, even though the message seems to bear only on the split within Red, and not at all on Red versus Blue. I would like you to compare Jeffrey's original rule of probability kinematics with this cross-entropy-minimizing generalization of it. Does the Judy Benjamin result show that the generalization smuggles in a substantive assumption that Jeffrey's own rule never required, and should a rational agent in Judy Benjamin's position really accept the resulting shift in her credence about Red versus Blue?
