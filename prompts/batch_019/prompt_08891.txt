I don't have any formal training in philosophy, but I've spent the last few months working through John Rawls's Political Liberalism on my own, and one part of his argument keeps nagging at me. As I understand it, when citizens are deciding fundamental questions of justice, Rawls asks them to reason only from ideas that any reasonable person could accept, setting aside whatever their own comprehensive doctrine, religious or secular, tells them about the matter. That seems to cost a secular citizen nothing, since reasoning without appeal to God or scripture is just their default way of thinking anyway, while it asks a religious citizen to bracket the very considerations that matter most to them. So isn't Rawls's supposedly neutral political liberalism really just secularism wearing a more flattering label, and doesn't public reason end up imposing a burden on religious citizens that non-religious citizens never have to bear?
