For an independent-study project on AI alignment, I've been working through Eliezer Yudkowsky's proposal that a superintelligent AI should act on humanity's coherent extrapolated volition (CEV) rather than on our current, stated wishes — that is, on what we would want if we knew more, thought faster, and had reflected long enough to become more coherent versions of ourselves. The part I keep getting stuck on is the word "coherent": the whole proposal presupposes that idealizing each person's preferences will make them converge, and it's not obvious that it would, especially across people with very different values or life circumstances. Could you write a short story centered on the moment an AI (or the humans overseeing it) entrusted with computing this extrapolation has to confront the possibility that humanity's idealized will won't converge into one coherent volition, but instead fractures into several incompatible ones? I'd like the story to take the philosophical mechanics of the thought experiment seriously, rather than using the setup merely as a backdrop for a generic AI-takeover plot.
