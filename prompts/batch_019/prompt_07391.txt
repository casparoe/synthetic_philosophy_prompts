I cover the history of ideas for a science-and-culture magazine, and next month I am interviewing a historian of political thought whose forthcoming book rereads Pascal's Pensées fragments on force and justice -- including the line that, unable to make what is just strong, we made what is strong just -- as a Jansenist argument about fallen reason rather than a cynical anticipation of Hobbes. Draft seven or eight interview questions that do more than let her restate that thesis: include at least one on how she squares Pascal's still calling illegitimate force "tyrannical" with his claim that we have lost any solid grasp of true justice, one on what work the vignette about a killing that becomes "brave" rather than murderous simply because the victim lives across a river is doing in the argument, and one on whether the same forced-choice structure -- reason cannot settle the question, yet one must act -- is simply the wager again, now licensing obedience rather than belief. Give each question a follow-up keyed to the deflection she is most likely to reach for.
