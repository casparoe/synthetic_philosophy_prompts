So the puzzle is basically that I can never know for certain which function I really meant, since my past use underdetermines it. Kripke also rules out the idea that some disposition settles the matter, since dispositions are finite too (I'd never actually respond to numbers too big for my brain to hold) and dispositions only tell us how I would respond, not how I should respond, which can't ground the normativity of meaning. His 'sceptical solution' doesn't look for some fact that makes 'plus' mean plus; instead it says a claim like 'Jones means addition by plus' is justified when Jones's answers agree with the community's, and the whole practice exists so people can predict and rely on each other, not to describe some hidden mental fact. That's also why a totally isolated person, like Robinson Crusoe alone on his island, can still follow rules and mean stuff by his words on this view, since he's basically his own one-person community checking himself. Personally I think the sceptical solution really is a solution and not just a dodge, because it lets us keep using words like 'means' and 'correct' in everyday life without needing some spooky fact behind them."
