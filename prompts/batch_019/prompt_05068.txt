So my friend Priya is getting married next month and asked me to give the toast, and I want to get the framing exactly right instead of just winging it. Backstory that actually matters: Priya and I have both been in the rationalist blog-and-forum world since undergrad (that's half of why she asked me and not one of her cousins), and for years she ran her dating life like an honest-to-god optimal stopping problem -- she'd bring up the secretary problem and the 37% rule from Algorithms to Live By completely unironically, told everyone she was in a deliberate "calibration phase" of dating around and ranking people against each other before she'd let herself commit to anyone, and kept a spreadsheet to make the comparisons rigorous. Then she met Daniel well before her own calibration window was supposed to close, and just stopped searching and married him anyway. When anyone brings this up she just says she satisficed instead of optimized, like thats the end of the discussion, and it's become a running joke in our friend group.

My actual problem is this: maybe a third of the guests are from that same online world and will catch every reference and expect the toast to say something real about it, but most of the room -- her parents, Daniel's side, coworkers -- have never heard "satisficing" or "optimal stopping" in their lives. I don't want four minutes that quietly implies she settled, because that's the one reading that would actually land badly, and I don't even think it's true -- Herbert Simon's whole point with bounded rationality was that satisficing isn't a failed attempt at optimizing, it's what rationality looks like once you actually take search costs and irreversibility seriously, and a clean secretary-problem search doesn't really fit real dating anyway since you can't un-reject someone and your own ranking keeps shifting as you go. So I want the toast to actually use that distinction structurally -- make the case, through the joke rather than as a lecture, that stopping the search for Daniel was the rational move and not a consolation prize -- while still being warm and short enough that nobody's relatives feel like they wandered into a decision theory seminar. Can you write it?
