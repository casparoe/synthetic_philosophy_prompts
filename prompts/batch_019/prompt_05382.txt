My roommate swears shes an unusually patient person, and most of the time she really does seem pretty chill. But every single time something happens that would actually test that -- rent's late, dishes pile up for days -- she snaps, and always has a totally reasonable, totally different excuse for why THIS time doesn't count ('I hadn't slept,' 'my sister called two minutes before you walked in,' 'her boss emailed something stressful'). Her argument is that since each excuse checks out fine on its own, none of them individually counts as evidence against her being patient. I'm doing a phd in personality psych, so my first instinct was that this is just ordinary cross-situational inconsistency -- no trait predicts behavior perfectly -- but that doesn't feel right either, since it's not random noise, it's that there's always a fresh, independently plausible reason ready to go right at the moment the trait would actually get tested. Is she right that none of these count against her individually, or is that pattern itself doing some work she's not owning up to?
