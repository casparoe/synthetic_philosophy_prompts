ok i've been mulling over van Fraassen's Reflection Principle again -- the idea that if you're certain you'll have credence 0.7 in some proposition at a later time (via some process you currently trust as rational), you should already have credence 0.7 in it now, on pain of a diachronic dutch book. Yudkowsky's old "conservation of expected evidence" post always struck me as basically a special case of this, for plain bayesian updating. But then theres Sleeping Beauty, which a bunch of people treat as a live counterexample: Beauty can predict on Sunday exactly what credence she'll have in Heads upon waking, and yet almost nobody thinks she should already have that credence on Sunday, before she's even gone under. Can you write me a dialogue between two rationalist-forum regulars hashing this out -- one who thinks Reflection basically always holds and that Sleeping Beauty doesn't actually threaten it once you think it through, and one who thinks cases like Sleeping Beauty (or other anticipated-memory-loss / self-locating setups) show real exceptions that can't just be patched away. Want them actually engaging with each other's specific moves rather than just restating their own side each time, and neither should come off as the obvious pushover.
