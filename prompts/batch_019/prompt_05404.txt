So I fell down a rabbit hole reading about "epistemic utility theory" - the idea that you score credences for accuracy sort of like how we'd score a classifier's calibration with a Brier score. Turns out theres a theorem that says if your scoring rule is "strictly proper," your own credence always comes out looking like the most accurate one available - by your own lights, no other credence could possibly beat its expected score, not even tie it. Which is a pretty funny way to mathematically prove that everyone already thinks their own opinion is the best one in the room. Can you write me a joke about this? It should actually land for someone who knows what a proper scoring rule is, not just be a generic "nerds think they're always right" joke.
