I have spent a long time reading rationalist blogs and forums, where akrasia—acting against one's own better judgment, as when procrastinating or overeating—is treated as a real and consequential phenomenon, something to be diagnosed and ideally corrected. Yet the revealed-preference tradition in economics defines 'utility' purely operationally: a utility function is nothing more than whatever ordinal ranking rationalizes an agent's actual, observed choices, subject to consistency axioms such as WARP or GARP, rather than an independent psychological quantity an agent might act against. Taken strictly, this seems to leave no room for genuine akrasia, since whatever I end up choosing, by construction, reveals what my utility function was all along, which makes talk of 'failing to maximize my true utility' look close to incoherent. Please describe the most important implications of the revealed-preference conception of utility for whether, and how, one can still coherently talk about akrasia and self-control at all.
