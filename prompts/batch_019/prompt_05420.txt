I started a new job at a game localization studio last month, and on my second day two of the senior translators nearly came to blows over one line in a fantasy script — which somehow spiraled into a 60-year-old philosophy argument I can't stop replaying in my head. The line was genuinely ambiguous in the source language, and it set off a back-and-forth between Petra (who studied philosophy years ago) and Dov (a decade into this job) that went roughly like this:

1. Petra said this proves there's no such thing as "the" correct translation of anything, even in principle. She brought up Quine's old rabbit example: if a native speaker says "gavagai" every time a rabbit hops by, nothing about how they use the word could tell you whether it means "rabbit," "rabbit-part," or "rabbit-stage" — and thats not because we're missing information, there's just no fact there to find.
2. Dov said that's nuts. He edits translations for a living and some are obviously better than others, so something has to be making that true.
3. Petra said "better for our purposes" isn't the same as "the one correct meaning": two completely different translation manuals could match everything a speaker would ever say or do and still disagree about what the individual words pick out, and that gap is what indeterminate actually means.
4. Dov said fine, but that's only because Quine restricted himself to raw stimulus-response. Real translators lean on grammar, shared concepts, how human categorization actually works — bring that in and the ties get broken, so the whole thing is a leftover of 1960s behaviorism, not a fact about meaning.
5. Petra said that doesn't help, because any story about "how categorization works" is itself just more theory, and a rival theory could fit the same data equally well. You don't escape the circle by piling on more evidence of the same kind — you'd need evidence of a completely different kind, and nobodys produced that yet.

Lunch ended before either of them budged, and now I've got both of their voices in my head every time I have to pick a word. Could you go through the exchange move by move, say who's got the better of it at each point (not just an overall winner), and flag anything important that neither of them raised?
