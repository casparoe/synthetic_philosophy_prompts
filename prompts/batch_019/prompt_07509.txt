I'm a senior engineer on my company's Staff Engineer promotion committee, and we're reconsidering our "signature project" requirement: to be promoted, a candidate must lead a highly visible, technically ambitious project on top of their regular workload, usually over several months. Supporters argue it's the only reliable test of true Staff-level ability, since only someone genuinely capable would sustain that kind of costly effort; one committee member likened it to a peacock's tail, arguing that the sheer extravagance is what makes it credible. Critics respond that the requirement mostly rewards whoever has fewer caregiving duties, more job security, and a manager willing to hand them high-visibility work, so it may be measuring access to the signal rather than the underlying skill. We need to decide whether to keep the requirement as is, drop it in favor of manager and peer judgment, or redesign it, and I have to circulate a short recommendation memo before our next meeting. Please draft that memo: give a clear recommendation, and make sure the strongest argument against whichever position you take gets a fair hearing.
