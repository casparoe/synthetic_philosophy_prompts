ok so in my philosophy elective we're covering newton's bucket for the unit on space and time, and i think i found a hole in the whole debate. quick recap of the setup: you spin a bucket full of water. at first the bucket's spinning but the water hasn't caught up yet, so the surface stays flat even tho theres a ton of relative motion between the water and the bucket. then after a bit, the water gets dragged into spinning along with the bucket, and NOW the surface goes concave -- even though at that exact point the water and bucket have zero relative motion (theyre rotating together). so the concave-ness clearly isnt tracking relative motion to the bucket, which is why newton says it must be tracking rotation relative to absolute space instead.

but isnt this basically solved already? einstein's whole thing with relativity is that theres no privileged reference frame and ALL motion, including spinning, is relative. so doesnt that mean mach was right all along -- the water's really just rotating relative to the distant stars and matter in the universe, and general relativity actually confirmed this? if so why do philosophy of physics people still treat substantivalism vs relationism like its a live, unsettled debate instead of just admitting mach won and newton's absolute space lost?
