I've been reading LessWrong and the EA Forum on and off for close to a decade, and last year a few of us from my local rationalist meetup pooled our annual giving into a small donor-advised fund we call the Confluence Fund. A three-person committee, myself included, reallocates the pot every December, and this year's cycle has produced a genuine deadlock. I'd like help drafting the memo I'm supposed to circulate before we vote this weekend.

Our roughly $80,000 in annual giving has historically split between an anti-malaria bednet distributor and a cage-free corporate campaign, chosen on fairly ordinary cost-effectiveness grounds. This cycle, though, we received a proposal from a two-person team asking for the entire pot to fund a year of scoping work on what they call "trajectory-change" research — work aimed at marginally raising the odds that civilization's long-run future, if it turns out to be extremely long-lived or the reachable universe turns out to be infinite, ends up net positive rather than net negative in value. Their case leans on what I've seen called "infinite ethics" reasoning on the Forum: once there's any non-negligible credence that the future is unboundedly large, they argue, ordinary expected-value comparisons between their work and our current grantees break down, since finite, certain quantities are either swamped by or simply incommensurable with infinite ones, however small the relevant probability. One committee member finds this close to decisive and wants to fund the proposal in full. The other thinks the argument proves too much — that it would license funding any proposal that gestures at sufficiently astronomical stakes, which he calls Pascal's mugging by another name — and adds that "infinite expected value" isn't even well-defined once you notice that summing infinitely many positive and negative contributions can be made to yield any total whatsoever depending on the order you add them in, the way conditionally convergent series behave under rearrangement. I'm stuck in the middle, unconvinced by either full confidence or flat dismissal, and I don't trust my own reasoning here enough to write the memo alone.

Could you draft the short decision memo I'll actually send the committee? It should recommend one concrete allocation — fund in full, fund at some capped amount with a stated rationale for the cap, or decline and redirect the money to our existing grantees — and give reasons the applicants could genuinely respond to, rather than a bare appeal to common sense about mugging. Please also take seriously the strongest version of their objection: if there really is a non-trivial chance the stakes are that large, it isn't obvious that a fund our size is entitled to just ignore it either.
