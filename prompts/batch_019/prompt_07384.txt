I am a software engineer, and while reading about why machine learning models are justified in favoring simpler hypotheses, I came across two results that seem to be in real tension. Solomonoff induction is often presented as putting Occam's razor on rigorous mathematical footing: weight each hypothesis x by 2^-K(x), its Kolmogorov complexity, and the resulting predictor is provably optimal for any computable environment. But the No Free Lunch theorems prove that, averaged uniformly over all possible target functions, every algorithm — including one that just guesses randomly — achieves exactly the same expected performance, which seems to say that no inductive bias, including a preference for simplicity, can be generically better than any other. Given that, doesn't this mean the celebrated "proof" of Occam's razor via Solomonoff induction is hollow, and that when practitioners justify regularization or minimum-description-length model selection by appeal to it, they are really just dressing up an arbitrary preference in mathematical language?
