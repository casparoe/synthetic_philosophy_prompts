Her line (simplifying a bit -- this is from the chapter everyone's already read, so correct me if I'm garbling it) is that you don't need any special metaphysical glue to explain why "Dara swims" is one unified, truth-evaluable thing rather than just a list like {Dara, swimming}: no Fregean unsaturated concept, no primitive "nonrelational tie." Instead the proposition just IS a worldly fact -- the fact that, in some actual language, an interpreted sentence's syntax is used by competent speakers to encode that Dara has the property of swimming, period. Facts, on her view, come pre-unified in a way sets and sequences don't, so the regress never actually gets off the ground: your not adding one more relation to a pile, you're pointing at something that was never a pile of separate items to begin with.
