Write a dialogue between Galileo and Cardinal Bellarmine, set right after Bellarmine's 1615 letter to Foscarini (the Carmelite friar who'd been defending Copernicus). Skip the usual science vs blind faith framing and get into the actual substance -- Bellarmine's move of treating heliocentrism as just a calculating device instead of asserting it's literally true, what would actually count as the "necessary demonstration" he demands before scripture gets reinterpreted, and Galileo's comeback that the church has already reinterpreted plenty of literal-sounding passages before (the firmament, the pillars of the earth, etc.), so refusing to do it here looks pretty inconsistent. Don't make Bellarmine a pushover though, his worry about tying church doctrine to a theory that might get overturned later is a real argument, not just stubbornness. Make sure they're actually responding to each others points rather than just restating their own side.
