My wealthy uncle called me this week with an offer that has left me stuck. He wants to pay off the remainder of my student loans - a genuinely enormous relief - this coming Sunday, on one condition. Beforehand, during a conversation we're due to have, he has to become convinced that I truly intend to spend one full week next summer helping him with a tedious, entirely pointless project he's grown attached to for sentimental reasons (cataloguing decades of old family paperwork, as far as I can tell). Everyone in the family agrees he has an uncanny knack for telling genuine intentions from performed ones, and as far as anyone knows he has never once been wrong. Here's my problem: once the loans are paid off on Sunday, I will have no reason whatsoever to actually spend that week helping him next summer - the money will already be mine either way, and he's not the kind of person to check up on people or hold a grudge - and I already know all this right now, while I'm supposedly trying to work myself into sincerely intending to do it. Every time I try, the very fact that I can see I'll have no future reason to follow through seems to spoil the intention before it even forms. Is it actually possible to sincerely intend to do something under these conditions, and if so, how would I go about it, rather than just rehearsing a convincing performance for Sunday?
