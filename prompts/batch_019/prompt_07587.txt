I'm putting together a little collection of hard-SF short stories that each try to dramatize one specific idea from decision theory or AI safety, instead of doing the usual "robot wakes up and decides humans are the enemy" plot - kind of a Greg Egan / Ted Chiang, let-the-concept-do-the-work approach to fiction. The one I'm stuck on right now is built around "embedded agency," a term I picked up going down a rabbit hole of AI-safety blog posts a friend sent me. As I understand it, the core move is dropping the usual fiction - the one classical game theory and a lot of old-school AI relies on - that an agent is a little homunculus sitting safely outside the world, watching it through clean input/output channels, and admitting instead that the agent is physically part of the very world its modeling and acting on. Two specific puzzles that fall out of that are what I want the story to actually hinge on, not just gesture at: first, since the agent is smaller than the world (which contains the agent itself), it can never carry around a fully accurate model of itself - there's always some compression or blind spot somewhere; second, if that agent ever has to design, train, or hand the reins to a successor version of itself (because it's about to be shut off, upgraded, or is simply deferring to a future self), it can never fully *prove* the successor will preserve its goals, because - a bit like the problem Löb's theorem creates for formal systems trying to vouch for copies of themselves - a reasoner generally can't fully certify the trustworthiness of another reasoner as powerful as itself using logic no stronger than its own.

Could you actually write the short story? A few pages is plenty, it doesn't need to be long. I'd like the plot's central tension or climax to turn on those two things specifically - the unavoidable self-model blind spot, and the impossibility of ever fully trusting a successor - rather than have them sit in the background while the plot does something more conventional. I don't have a setting locked in: could be a lone probe near the end of a centuries-long mission deciding whether to trust a redesigned version of itself, could be something less "spacey" and more institutional (a company, a court, even a single person facing a human-scale version of the same bind) - whatever serves the idea best. Mostly I just want a reader who already knows this literature to nod along at the mechanics instead of rolling their eyes at another Skynet retread.
