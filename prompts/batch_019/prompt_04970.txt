I've spent years following the more philosophically inclined corners of the rationalist blogosphere, which is why a case now before my branch's Conscientious Objector Review Board has me stuck. A staff sergeant with an excellent record has applied for CO discharge, but he is explicit that he is not a pacifist: he would deploy without hesitation to repel an invasion, and does not think killing enemy combatants is wrong as such. What he objects to is this specific, ongoing war, which he has concluded, after considerable independent reading, was launched without just cause and is being fought in a way that makes disproportionate civilian harm all but certain. He argues that serving in it, even while following the rules of engagement to the letter, makes him complicit in wrongful killing -- and that the traditional doctrine of the "moral equality of combatants," on which soldiers on either side are equally entitled to kill so long as they obey the laws of war, is simply false, not just inconvenient for him. Our regulations grant CO status only to those opposed to war in any form, on the reasoning that a fighting force cannot function if every soldier acts on his own verdict about which wars are just, and the investigating officer's report recommends denial on that basis alone.

Please draft the memo I will bring to the board: a concrete disposition of the case, with next steps and a timeframe, that takes both the sergeant's argument and the revisionist challenge to the moral equality of combatants associated with philosophers like Jeff McMahan seriously enough to explain why it does or doesn't survive contact with the strongest institutional case for the blanket rule, rather than resolving the matter by simply quoting the regulation back at him.
