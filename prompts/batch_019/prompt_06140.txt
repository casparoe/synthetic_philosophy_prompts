So I fell down a bit of a rabbit hole this week and now I can't stop thinking about it. I'm a backend engineer, zero philosophy background, but I listen to a couple of rationalist/EA-adjacent podcasts, and one of them mentioned in passing that Henry Sidgwick -- the utilitarian philosopher those circles are always quoting for the "point of view of the universe" line -- was also the first president of the Society for Psychical Research back in the 1880s. Apparently he spent decades chasing down mediums, telepathy claims, and haunted-house reports, and was famous for being almost obsessively careful about ruling out fraud and sloppy controls. I brought this up with a couple coworkers at lunch and we ended up arguing for way too long about what a guy like that would say if you showed him what happened to the field after he died in 1900.

Here's what I mean specifically: J.B. Rhine's statistical card-guessing experiments in the 1930s, the Ganzfeld telepathy studies and the big Hyman-vs-Honorton fight over their meta-analysis in the 80s, and then, the one that really got me, Daryl Bem's 2011 "Feeling the Future" paper, where a mainstream social psychologist used completely standard, peer-reviewed statistics to get "significant" results for precognition -- a result that alot of people now point to as one of the first cracks that opened up the whole replication crisis in psychology. If Sidgwick could see all of that, what do you think he'd actually conclude? Would Bem's result read to him as his own painstaking approach finally starting to pay off, or would it read as damning evidence that ordinary significance testing is way too weak a filter, meaning psychical research (and honestly psychology in general) needed something a lot closer to his own standards of evidence all along?

And is there a real substantive connection between this and the thing he agonized over in The Methods of Ethics -- the "dualism of practical reason," if I'm remembering the term right, where duty and self-interest don't obviously line up unless the universe is somehow morally rigged to make them line up -- or was the psychical research basically just a separate hobby that happened to occupy the same guy? Don't just give me "he'd probably be skeptical," I want the actual reasoning laid out, and where you're genuinely unsure, say so instead of papering over it with a vague answer.
