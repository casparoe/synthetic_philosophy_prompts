Write a dialogue between a Buddhist philosopher defending anatta (no-self) in the classic Abhidharma style, and a Nyaya philosopher who thinks theres a real, persisting atman underneath all our experiences. Don't make it a lecture, make it a real argument -- the Nyaya side should press the classic worries about memory and recognition (how does a later moment of experience get to call an earlier one 'mine' if there's no subject persisting through both?) and about karma (whose karma is it, and who's around to collect on it in the next life, if nothing sticks around?). The Buddhist has to actually answer that stuff instead of just saying 'everything's impermanent' and moving on, and the Nyaya guy shouldn't be a pushover either. Both of them are equally sharp, and they need to keep responding to each other's actual points instead of talking past one another.
