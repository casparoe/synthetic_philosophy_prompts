I run a small podcast for listeners who follow the decision-theory corners of the rationalist blogosphere, and next episode's guest is a researcher who has spent her career defending evidential cooperation in large worlds (ECL): the view that, in a universe or multiverse large enough to contain many other agents reasoning in relevantly similar ways, one's own choices carry evidence about what those causally disconnected agents will do, licensing action aimed at securing mutual gains with them. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate that basic setup. I want at least one question on the epistemic access problem: how she thinks an agent could ever acquire justified credence about the values and dispositions of a partner it can never observe or contact. I want one on exactly where she draws the line between ECL-style trade and acausal blackmail, given that updateless agents are usually counseled to resist the latter while embracing the former, even though both trade on an agent's being predictable to others. And I want one that presses on the "measure" worry: without a principled way of weighting the enormous number of possible correlated partners, the expected-value sums ECL relies on may never settle on a determinate answer. For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, and close with a question on what she thinks would actually count as evidence against acting on ECL.
