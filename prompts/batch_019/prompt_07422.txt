Zahavi's handicap principle explains why extravagant, costly displays such as the peacock's tail can function as honest signals of underlying quality: their expense is precisely what prevents low-quality individuals from faking them. This suggests that a signal's reliability is essentially a function of its cost to produce — yet ordinary speech costs a signaler almost nothing. Why, then, does verbal communication still function as a broadly reliable way of conveying honest information, rather than being swamped by dishonest bluffing?
