I'm reporting a piece on the Liar paradox and have been reading about dialetheism, the position associated with Graham Priest that a sentence like "This sentence is false" is not merely paradoxical but literally both true and false. This strikes me as an obvious non-starter: classical logic tells us that once a single contradiction is true, absolutely any proposition can be validly derived from it, so if the Liar sentence really is a true contradiction, doesn't that commit dialetheists to the absurd view that every statement whatsoever is true? I haven't been able to find a clear explanation of why serious logicians still treat dialetheism as a live position rather than as a reductio of itself, and I'd like to understand what I'm missing before I write about it.
