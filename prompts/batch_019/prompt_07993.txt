Spent 35 years as an analytical chemist before I retired, and ran our lab's calibration and QC program for a good chunk of that time. Lately I've fallen down a philosophy rabbit hole, and keep bumping into this old chestnut from Plato's Meno: why should knowledge count for more than mere true belief, if both get you to Larissa just the same? Honestly seems like a non-problem to me -- a true belief thrown up by a reliable process (a calibrated instrument, a sound method) is obviously worth more than one thrown up by a lucky guess, becuase you can trust it to come out right again next time. So isn't 'reliabilism' basically just an easy answer to Meno's puzzle, and all the fuss philosophers still make over the 'value problem' more about keeping a research program alive than a real gap in the theory?
