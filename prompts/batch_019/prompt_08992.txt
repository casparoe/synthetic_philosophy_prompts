I've been down a rabbit hole reading about the fine-tuning argument (the one about how the physical constants of the universe seem incredibly improbable for life - like if gravity or the cosmological constant were off by a tiny fraction, nothing would exist) and I think I've basically convinced myself the whole debate is a non-issue once you bring in the anthropic principle. My reasoning goes like this: if the constants weren't life-permitting, nobody would be around to notice or ask the question in the first place, so of course any observers who do exist find themselves in a universe that looks fine-tuned. That seems like a plain selection effect to me, the same way its not shocking that you exist at all given how unlikely it was for your specific parents to meet - you just have to remember that only the "successful" outcomes ever get observed. So once you factor that in, isn't the fine-tuning "puzzle" basically solved, full stop, no need to bring god or a multiverse into it?

I brought this up with a friend doing a physics PhD, half expecting her to just agree with me, but she said the anthropic point is real but insisted it doesn't actually make the problem go away, which honestly confused me more since it feels like pretty basic probability and she couldnt really articulate what was wrong with my version of it either. Am I missing something here, or is this actually still an open disagreement among people who think about this stuff for a living?
