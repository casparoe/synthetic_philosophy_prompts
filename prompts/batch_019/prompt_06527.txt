In a graduate seminar on formal epistemology this term, we spent two sessions on Aumann's 1976 result that Bayesian agents who share a common prior cannot "agree to disagree" once their posteriors on some event are common knowledge, together with the no-trade theorems (Milgrom and Stokey, among others) that were later built on the same foundation. Most of my students came away treating these results as close to settling the normative question that animates the contemporary literature on peer disagreement: if two careful, honest reasoners still disagree after fully exchanging their views and reasoning, at least one of them must be making some identifiable error, since ideally rational Bayesian agents simply could not end up in that position. I suspect this moves too fast, and I would like help pressing my students on exactly where the argument's real work is being done.

I'd like you to construct a rigorous philosophical argument for the following claim: the common prior assumption is not an innocuous corollary of Bayesian coherence, and still less a harmless technical convenience, but an independent and substantive epistemic posit - so that once this is recognized, Aumann's theorem and its no-trade descendants show considerably less than they are usually credited with showing about the irrationality of persistent, acknowledged disagreement. In making the case, engage directly with Harsanyi's own rationale for holding that any two rational agents who differ in their beliefs must differ in their information rather than in some more basic, equally rational starting point, and explain what entitles a thoroughgoing subjectivist about priors to reject that rationale without thereby giving up anything else in the Bayesian package, including common knowledge and conditionalization. I'd also like you to take on, rather than sidestep, the standard rejoinder that one can always rescue a common prior by enriching the state space to include whatever it is the agents differ on (temperament, upbringing, priors about priors, and so forth): say whether that move saves the common prior assumption as harmless bookkeeping, or simply reintroduces by the back door the very assumption in dispute.
