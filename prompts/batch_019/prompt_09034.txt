Random context but I just started a job at a company that makes robotic companion pets for elderly people with dementia (think fluffy robot cats/seals that purr and react when you pet them). During onboarding one of the senior engineers mentioned they used to stress-test prototypes by literally kicking them around the lab to make sure the joints held up, and it kinda freaked me out even though I know full well the thing can't suffer. Reminded me of that whole internet meltdown a few years back over the Boston Dynamics videos of employees kicking their robots around to show off the balance recovery -- everyone felt weirdly bad for a machine that, as far as we know, can't feel a thing.

Anyway this sent me down a Kant rabbit hole. His famous argument for why cruelty to animals is wrong isn't actually about the animal's own suffering -- its that being cruel to animals corrodes your own character and makes you more likely to be cruel to people later on. Robots obviously werent on his radar, so: does that reasoning commit Kant to saying that kicking a robot pet around is ALSO wrong, even one we're 100% sure isn't sentient, or does the whole argument collapse once there's no real animal on the other end? Reconstruct his actual reasoning as best you can and give me a real verdict -- not a wishy-washy "well it depends."
