I'd like a joke to open the session on cosmological arguments in my philosophy of religion seminar -- one that treats the kalam and contingency versions as genuinely distinct, rather than as a single undifferentiated target. Please write a joke whose punchline turns on this real structural difference between them: the kalam argument needs the universe to have had a temporal beginning, so it collapses if the past turns out to be infinite, whereas the contingency argument does not, since on that argument even a beginningless universe would still be a merely contingent state of affairs in need of explanation via the principle of sufficient reason. It should only really land for someone who sees why "the universe might be eternal" refutes one argument while leaving the other completely untouched; please don't just recycle a generic "who created God" or infinite-regress joke.
