So I'm the mandated 'nonscientist member' on my university's IACUC (Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee) -- someone has to be. There's a protocol up for renewal at Thursday's meeting -- a terminal, mildly painful mouse study with a pretty modest expected payoff -- and the committee's working standard is just 'expected benefit outweighs animal cost,' straight aggregation. It reminded me of Nozick's old aside in Anarchy, State, and Utopia about 'utilitarianism for animals, Kantianism for people': he thought a bare positive balance wasn't enough to justify hurting an animal (extra taste pleasure doesnt cut it, on his view), but he also worried that any principled line between animals-as-aggregatable and people-as-inviolable is hard to draw given how gradual the relevant differences actually are. Draft the memo I'll bring to the vote -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions -- that actually grapples with that worry instead of just repeating 'the science is sound, this is routine.' Give the strongest case for our normal practice too, not just my own hobby horse.
