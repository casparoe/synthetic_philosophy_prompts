A colleague of mine, a freelance translator, has adopted a particular method for reassuring clients who cannot read the source language: she translates their document into English, then translates her own English version back into the source language, and presents the close match between the two source-language texts as evidence of her accuracy. She now wants to make this round-trip check a routine part of her practice, reasoning that a growing record of successful matches will vouch for her general reliability as a translator. Something about this strikes me as too easy a way to earn that assurance, yet each step in her reasoning seems individually sound, and I cannot quite locate where the trouble lies. Could you help me work out whether her confidence is warranted, and if not, exactly what has gone wrong?
