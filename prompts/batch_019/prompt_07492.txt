So I'm about two-thirds into a novel where the protagonist, Marguerite, quits a partnership-track law job to become a house painter (actual houses, not canvases, which was its own joke in the group), and my writing group basically detonated last week arguing about whether the ending "earns" the existentialist beat I was going for. My friend Dev said the arc is a clean case of bad faith: Marguerite spends the whole back half of the book telling herself "I was never really a lawyer, that wasn't the real me," and Dev's point is that's textbook bad faith because it treats "the real me" like a fixed essence she's uncovering under the lawyer costume, when the whole Sartre thing is there's no essence to uncover, you're just whatever you keep choosing. My other friend Priya said that's not fair to Sartre or to the character — she said the waiter in the cafe isn't in bad faith because he has a job, he's in bad faith because he collapses entirely into the role, plays at being a waiter the way an inkwell just is an inkwell, and Marguerite refusing to be defined by the lawyer job is basically the opposite move, so if anything it's closer to authenticity than to bad faith. Dev came back with: sure, but she's still smuggling in an essence, she's just relocated it to "painter" — she talks about painting like it's what she was "always meant to do," which is exactly the fixed-nature talk Sartre says we use to dodge the anguish of radical freedom. Priya's last word was that wanting to do something and mythologizing it as destiny are two different mistakes, and if you conflate them then literally any character who changes careers is guilty of bad faith, which cant be right, because then the concept isn't doing any real work anymore.

Nobody at the table settled it, me included, and I went home and reread the bad faith chapter of Being and Nothingness for the second time this month and came away less sure than when I started. Could you go through the exchange move by move and say who's actually got Sartre right at each step, not just hand down an overall winner? I'd also like to know honestly if the concept just doesn't apply cleanly to Marguerite either way, or applies for some reason none of us landed on. And if you can, end with one line on whether I should be quietly Team Dev or Team Priya next time this comes up at group, because I guarantee it will.
