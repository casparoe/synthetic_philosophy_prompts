"Objects seem to change their properties all the time without stopping being the same object -- like a banana that's green on Monday and yellow by Friday. But it's kind of weird that the very same thing could have two different, opposite colors, since normally something can't be two contradictory ways at once. This is called the problem of temporary intrinsics, which Quine first raised as a challenge to the endurantist view that objects are "wholly present" at every moment they exist.

Endurantists think the whole banana exists at each moment of its life, so the banana at t1 and the banana at t2 are numerically the same object, not just similar objects glued together. Perdurantists disagree -- they think objects are four-dimensional "space-time worms" made up of a series of temporal parts, kind of like frames in a movie reel, so the green-banana-part and the yellow-banana-part are two different (though related) temporal parts of the same worm. Since it's not literally the same part that's green and yellow, there's no contradiction for the perdurantist.

The endurantist has a few ways to try to escape the contradiction: they can say properties are always secretly relational, so the banana isn't just "green" but "green-at-t1," which dissolves the tension because "green-at-t1" and "yellow-at-t2" aren't actually incompatible. Presentists have an even easier time of it, since for them only the present moment is real, so the banana never actually has both colors at once in any robust sense.

Perdurantism also fits naturally with presentism, because you need all the different times where the object has temporal parts to genuinely exist and be equally real for the four-dimensional "worm" to be spread out across them -- otherwise most of the worm would be made of parts that don't exist. Overall I find perdurantism more convincing because it gives a clean solution to temporary intrinsics without needing extra moves, though I get why some people like the intuitive pull of endurantism, since it matches how we ordinarily talk about objects staying the same through change."
