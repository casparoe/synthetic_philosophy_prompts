I spent nearly four decades as an experimental physicist, and in retirement I've taken up re-analyzing some of my own old spectroscopic data using statistical tools that didn't exist when I first collected it. Last month I convinced myself that a faint second peak, once written off as noise, is real: I've checked the calculation repeatedly and can't find any flaw in it. Yet I also know, from long experience in the field, that researchers revisiting old data in the hope of finding something are notoriously prone to conjuring up patterns that a genuinely blind analysis would never turn up, and that no amount of re-checking can rule this out, since the checking is done by the very faculties in question. Having only recently taken up philosophy, I suspect there's a recognized puzzle lurking here that I don't know the literature well enough to track down. Should knowing that people in my position usually fool themselves lead me to distrust my own clear impression that the peak is real, or does that impression still count for something?
