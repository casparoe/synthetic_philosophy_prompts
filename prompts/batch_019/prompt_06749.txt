One of them thinks the Boltzmann brain problem is a genuine reductio: any cosmology whose future is an eternal de Sitter-like vacuum (whether that's our own universe if the cosmological constant never decays, or some pocket of an eternally inflating multiverse) predicts that randomly fluctuated brains vastly outnumber observers like us, and that's a real mark against the theory, not just a curiosity to shrug off. The other thinks the whole thing is basically an artifact of the measure problem -- whether Boltzmann brains "dominate" depends entirely on which measure you impose on an infinite spacetime (a causal-patch-style cutoff behaves very differently than a more global one), so until theres a principled reason to prefer one measure over another, the "problem" isn't really evidence against anything.

What I most want is for them to get into the move where one says "if this theory's right then I'm almost certainly a randomly fluctuated brain, so I can't trust the very reasoning that led me to accept the theory -- it refutes itself, measure or no measure" and the other pushes back that this is just an unprincipled way to rule out theories you find distasteful. Make them equally sharp, and have them actually respond to each other's specific objections instead of talking past each other -- no strawmanning either side.
