One of my closest friends has, over the past year or so, come to rely on astrology and tarot not as a diversion but as a genuine input into decisions that matter: she postponed sending off a strong job application because Mercury was retrograde, ended a promising relationship over a bad compatibility reading, and now consults her cards before anything she considers significant. I do not believe in any of it, and for a long time I treated the whole thing as a harmless quirk rather than something worth a real argument.

That changed recently, because we are in the middle of signing a lease together, and she now wants to delay for two weeks on her chart's advice, even though our landlord has told us plainly that the apartment will go to someone else if we wait. When I raise the kind of considerations that would normally settle something like this — the state of the rental market, the landlord's own track record, how often this sort of delay costs people housing — she does not dispute any of it; she simply does not treat it as outweighing the reading. When I pressed her on why she trusts the cards at all, she said they have been right for her often enough before, and then pointed out that my confidence in "statistics and landlords" is really no different: just another set of assumptions I have never had to defend, because everyone around me happens to already share them. I found I could not answer that without essentially restating that my way of weighing evidence is the correct one, which is an uncomfortable place to end up, since I still think she is making a bad decision and would like to be able to say why without simply begging the question.

I mentioned all this, half as a joke, to a friend who majored in philosophy, and she said it reminded her of an argument from a course on justification and belief, though she could not reconstruct it beyond the general idea that some disagreements go all the way down to assumptions neither side can independently prove. That has stayed with me longer than I expected. So I would genuinely like to know: is there a principled way to establish that my approach to deciding things is better than hers, or does this disagreement bottom out at a level no argument could touch? And if it is the latter, am I obligated to grant that her way of forming beliefs is just as legitimate as mine, or is there some way to be justified in thinking she is wrong that does not depend on winning a debate I apparently cannot win?
