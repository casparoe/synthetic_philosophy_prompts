I'm a retired physicist who's gotten hooked on philosophy since I hung up my lab coat, and lately I've been chewing on Aristotle's old sea battle problem from the De Interpretatione -- the puzzle about whether "there will be a sea battle tomorrow" is already true or false today, and how just accepting ordinary bivalence there seems to tip you into full blown fatalism. Reading around it I learned this thing has a whole afterlife: Diodorus Cronus apparently built a "Master Argument" out of similar premises (something like: truths about the past are now necessary, the impossible cant follow from the possible, therefore only what will actually happen was ever really possible), and then Arthur Prior tried to patch things up centuries later with branching time models and this Ockhamist vs Peircean distinction, plus a so called "thin red line" running through the branches that a lot of people say just smuggles the fatalism back in through the side door. Rather than an essay, could you write me a short story (a thousand words or so) with real characters -- a navigator, an oracle, a general, whoever -- where the plot actually hinges on which of these solutions is correct? I dont want a generic "is the future fixed" tale, I want it to really bite into the logical bind itself.
