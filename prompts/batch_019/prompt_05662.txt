I help maintain an open source formalized-math library, kind of like Lean's mathlib. Someone's PR adds a bunch of genuinely useful theorems -- every vector space has a basis, Tychonoff's theorem, existence of a non-measurable set -- that all depend on the axiom of choice, and proposes we just declare `choice` a blanket axiom for the whole library instead of tagging it per theorem. Half the maintainers are upset, since that quietly makes huge parts of the library noncomputable (no more extracting actual running code from those proofs) and smuggles choice into lemmas that constructive-math users downstream never opted into. The other half say refusing choice means we can't formalize most of real analysis or algebra, making the library useless for that. We vote at next weeks maintainer call and I have to circulate a recommendation first -- can you draft it? I need an actual call (adopt globally, opt-in tagging per theorem, or fork a seperate constructive-only library), with reasoning that would actually land with the anti-choice camp and not just 'choice is standard, deal with it.'
