Okay, this is going to sound like a weirdly specific relationship-logistics question but here goes. My girlfriend and I have pretty different ethical hangups around food and travel. I think factory farming is one of the worst things humans do and feel genuinely bad every time meat gets cooked in our apartment, but if I'm honest, carbon footprint stuff (flying, driving, whatever) just doesn't register for me morally — I believe the climate science, I just don't feel the guilt. She's basically the opposite: she feels legit bad about flying home to see her family like 6 times a year plus all her work travel, but eating meat doesn't bother her concience at all.

About four months ago, instead of just agreeing to disagree, we made a deal: she goes vegetarian Monday through Friday (mildly annoying for her, but by her own admission not a moral issue either way), and in exchange I stopped taking short domestic flights and take the train instead whenever it's under ~6 hours (mildly annoying for me, same deal — not something I actually think is wrong). So basically we're each doing the tedious thing that we personally don't think matters morally, specifically so the other person gets more of the thing they think matters a lot. It's worked great as an arrangement, four months running now, but it bugs me and I can't quite say why. I mentioned it to a friend from my old grad program who's deep into effective-altruism stuff, and she said this is basically just "moral bribery" and doesn't actually count as either of us doing anything good, which annoyed me but I didn't have a great comeback ready. I'm a backend engineer so my instinct is to just model this as a clean resource-allocation trade and move on, but applying that logic to actual ethics feels like it might be missing something important. Is this actually a legitimate way for two people to resolve an ongoing moral disagreement, or is my friend right that its kind of a hollow trick that doesn't really make either of us more ethical?
