Ok so I've been re-reading the Embedded Agency sequence (Garrabrant & Demski, the one MIRI put out with all the little diagrams of an agent that's stuck being part of the same universe its trying to model, instead of floating outside it like AIXI does) for probably the third time, and I keep thinking somebody should turn this into actual fiction instead of just diagrams and impossibility results. The thing that gets me isn't the generic "robot wakes up and either loves us or kills us" plot, it's the much smaller and honestly creepier problem: an agent whose self-model has to be strictly smaller than the world, because it's made of the world, so it can never fully contain itself the way a chess program fully contains the chess board. And then when that agent tries to figure out whether to trust some future, smarter, self-modified version of itself, it runs face first into the Löbian obstacle — you can't get a proof system to certify a strictly more powerful proof system's outputs without risking exactly the kind of self-referential mess Gödel and Löb already showed is fatal. There's this whole "tiling agents" literature on trying to get around that and it never quite fully works, which I find weirdly comforting actually.

Could you write me a short story, maybe 1000-1500 words, where a character (human, AI, uploaded mind, whatever, you pick) actually runs into one specific embedded-agency snag rather than a generic alignment-goes-wrong plot? Pick ONE of: the realizability problem (no hypothesis in its model is fully accurate, because a fully accurate hypothesis would have to be as big as the universe including the agent itself), the self-trust/tiling problem I mentioned above, or the weirdness in embedded decision theory where you can't cleanly separate "the decision" from "the environment," so counterfactuals like "what if I'd chosen differently" stop being well-defined rather than just being hard to compute. Feel free to set it somewhere small-scale and mundane, like a maintenance AI on a space station or a researcher talking to an earlier snapshot of their own upload — I don't want a galaxy-brained singularity epic, I want the claustrophobia of the specific logical trap. And please don't have it resolve neatly at the end, since it doesn't resolve neatly in the actual literature either; I'd rather the character just has to act under that irreducible fog. Bonus points if it reads more like Greg Egan or Ted Chiang than like a textbook with dialogue bolted on.
