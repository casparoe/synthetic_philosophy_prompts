My friend and I had a long argument last night about a decision his father just made, and I still cannot decide which of us had the better case. His father has an old sheepdog, Meg, who has herded faithfully on the family farm for eleven years but can no longer keep up with the work. Rather than go on feeding and treating a dog that no longer earns her keep, he is having her put down this week, quickly and without any suffering. My friend's position is that nothing wrong is being done to her at all, since Meg has no idea what is coming, no expectations about her own future, and no capacity to hold a claim against anyone — an act cannot wrong someone if there is no one there to be wronged. I could not locate the flaw in that reasoning, yet I still cannot shake the feeling that the father is doing something objectionable. Was my friend right that there is nothing here worth objecting to, or is there a genuine argument for why the father is doing something wrong — and if so, wrong to whom, or to what exactly?
