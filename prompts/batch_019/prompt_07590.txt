My grandpa's turning 90 in a few weeks and my aunt volunteered me to give the toast at his birthday dinner, which is honestly terrifying because he taught Latin for almost 40 years and Cicero's De Senectute (On Old Age) was basically his whole personality -- he's got this beat up Loeb edition he'd quote from any time someone complained about getting older, including at my own grandma's funeral, which was a whole thing. Probably a third of the guest list are former students of his, a few of whom could probably still recite bits of it in Latin, so I really can't just do the generic "wisdom comes with age" toast -- I want to actually structure it around his real argument, the four things people think make old age miserable and how Cato answers each one, instead of just vibes and a wine metaphor.

Heres the part thats been bugging me though. I started rereading his copy this week, partly because he had a bad fall last month and it kind of forced the issue for me, and I realized Cicero wrote the whole thing about a year before Antony had him killed, which is such a strange fact to sit with given that the entire back half of the book is Cato calmly explaining why an old man shouldn't be afraid of dying. It feels dishonest to just lift that section for an uplifting birthday toast like the irony isn't there, but it also feels wrong to make my grandpa's 90th about how Cicero got murdered. Can you actually write me a five minute toast that uses the real structure of his argument, not just the greatest hits quotes, and finds an honest way to hold that irony rather than just pretending I don't know about it?
