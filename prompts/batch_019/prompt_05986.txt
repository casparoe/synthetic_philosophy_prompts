I've been working through Bernard Williams's "Internal and External Reasons" for a seminar paper on practical reason, and I want to pressure-test the internalist picture with a case that isn't the usual amoralist or psychopath example. The case is Derek Parfit's "Future Tuesday Indifference": a person who is, in every other respect, a prudent, forward-looking egoist, except that he attaches no weight at all to any suffering that will occur on a future Tuesday, purely because it will be a Tuesday. He isn't confused about which day is which, isn't mistaken about any non-normative fact, and the indifference is stable under reflection rather than a symptom of some correctable error. Parfit thinks such a person is irrational in a way that a purely desire- or motivation-relative theory of reasons struggles to capture, since there is no false belief to correct and no failure of means-ends reasoning for a Williams-style "sound deliberative route" to fix.

Could you write a dialogue of some substance between two philosophers who run into each other after a conference talk on this material: one defends Williams's internalism and needs to say something about the Tuesday-indifferent man that doesn't just amount to disapproval dressed up as a reason claim, and the other takes the case as evidence for a categorical, broadly Parfitian or Kantian externalism about reasons, on which some requirements of concern for one's own future (and perhaps, by extension, for others) bind an agent independently of what is already in his motivational repertoire. I'd like each side to take the other's strongest move seriously rather than an easy one: the internalist should have to grapple with the suggestion that "sound" deliberation might itself be stretched to rule out purely arbitrary temporal discounting (and explain why that wouldn't just collapse the internal/external distinction), while the externalist should have to grapple with the reply that all the case really shows is that we're disposed to dislike or blame such a person, not that there is some further, reason-constituting fact about what he ought to care about. Please have the exchange end in a genuine standoff rather than a one-sided win, with both characters having had to revise or sharpen their position at least once in response to something the other actually said.
