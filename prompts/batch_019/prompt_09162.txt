So my mom is retiring next month after 38 years doing the books for a small plumbing supply company, and her coworkers are throwing her a little party at the warehouse and I somehow got voted into giving a speech. Few months back my brother-in-law, who's into finance stuff, sent me this old essay by John Maynard Keynes called "Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren," he wrote it back around 1930, and I honestly havent been able to stop thinking about it since. The short version as I understood it is that Keynes figured once humanity actually got rich enough that nobody had to scrape by anymore, the "economic problem" as he called it, the real challenge was gonna shift to something a lot harder: what do you actually DO with yourself once you don't have to work for a living. And he points out that the well-off people of his own time who already didn't need to work were basically a trial run for the rest of us, and a bunch of them were failing at it pretty badly, bored and kind of coming apart, because nobody had ever taught them how to live without the structure of a job. He's also got this bit about how caring about money for its own sake is basically a sickness he expects us to eventually grow out of, but that we still need it a little longer to drag ourselves out of scarcity, which felt oddly relevant for someone who's spent her entire career being the one keeping track of everyone else's money.

Anyway this has been stuck in my head because whenever anyone asks my mom if she's excited to retire she says something like "I don't know what I'm gonna do with myself all day" in this half-joking way that isn't really a joke. So she's kind of standing right where Keynes said the whole species would eventually end up, except its happening to her personally in a few weeks in a warehouse break room. I want to actually use this in the toast, but there's a wrinkle: half the room is her coworkers and my dad's side of the family who just want something warm and a little funny for a retirement party and could not care less who Keynes is, but my uncle Ray is also gonna be there, he almost finished a masters in economics before he dropped out to sell insurance, and he will 100% clock a Keynes reference and corner me about it later if I get it wrong.

Could you write the actual speech for me, not just notes, something around 4 to 5 minutes out loud? It needs to land as a genuinely sweet, slightly funny toast for the people who are just there for cake, hold up even if my uncle turns out to know what he's talking about, and use the Keynes idea to say something true about what she's walking into, without ever implying that the last 38 years of her life were just the boring warm-up before her "real" life starts, because she'd hate that and honestly it wasn't true either.
