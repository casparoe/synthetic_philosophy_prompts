So this has gone from an abstract "huh, weird" thought experiment to something I actually lie awake thinking about: my dad has had drug-resistant epilepsy for years, and his neurologist just brought up a corpus callosotomy as the next step if the new meds don't get things under control -- basically severing the corpus callosum, the bundle of fibers that lets the two hemispheres talk to eachother, so a seizure can't spread from one side of the brain to the other. Since that appointment I've fallen into a pretty deep hole reading about the old Sperry/Gazzaniga split-brain patients from the 60s and 70s -- the experiments where they'd flash something to just one visual field so only one hemisphere "saw" it, and that famous case where a patient's left hand (run by the silent right hemisphere) picked out a shovel card, while his talking left hemisphere, which had only been shown a chicken claw, instantly made up a completely confident and completely wrong reason for why the hand had done that. Gazzaniga calls that reason-making machine "the interpreter," and honestly it's messed with my head worse than the surgery talk itself -- if my own sense of being one continuous "me" who knows why she does things is partly just that same interpreter running on a fully intact brain, then what exactly would the surgery be cutting apart that isn't already, in some sense, two?

And apparently it's not even settled science, which doesn't help -- I found a 2017 paper (I want to say the lead author was Pinto?) that redid a bunch of the classic tests on two callosotomy patients and argued they still behave like a single, unified subject way more often then the textbook story lets on, not two little people stuck in one skull taking turns. So now I don't even know which version of the scary story I'm supposed to be scared of.

Could you write me a short story out of all this (something like 1000-1500 words)? I don't want an "evil twin living in your skull" horror piece, and I don't want a cute twist-ending gimmick either. I'd like it to actually use real features of split-brain syndrome as load-bearing parts of the plot -- the lateralized presentation setup, the confabulating "interpreter," maybe the alien-hand phenomenon where one hand seems to act against the other's wishes -- and I'd like it to end where I currently am, genuinely unsure whether there was ever just "one of me" in there to begin with, cable intact or not, rather than wrapping it up neatly. Doesn't have to be about my dad specifically, or even about someone getting the surgery -- that's just how I ended up down this particular rabbit hole.
