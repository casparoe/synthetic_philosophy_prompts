So there's this move Hilary Greaves makes to try to save consequentialism from the "cluelessness" problem (the worry that we can never really know the long-run effects of our actions, so expected value calculations are kind of hopeless). She splits it into "simple" cluelessness, where the unforeseeable effects are symmetric and just cancel out in expectation, and "complex" cluelessness, where we have some shaky systematic reason to think effects skew one way, but that reason is itself unstable. Can you argue that this distinction doesnt actually save expected-value reasoning for consequentialists, basically that her "simple" cases turn out to be just as complex/clueless as the ones she's worried about once you look closely?
