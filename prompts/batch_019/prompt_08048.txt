I'm about halfway through a novel set at a marine research station, and I spent a few days out there shadowing people for material. While I was there, two of the scientists got into an argument over lunch that I still haven't been able to shake.

(1) Marisol, a fish physiologist, brought up the old experiment where you inject something irritating into a trout's lips and it rocks back and forth, rubs its mouth against the gravel, and stops eating for hours -- and that most of this goes away if you give it a painkiller first. She said that's not a reflex, thats the behavioral signature of something that actually hurts. (2) Dov, a cognitive science postdoc, said none of that shows subjective pain, only nociception: fish don't have a neocortex, and in mammals that's the part that turns a raw danger-signal into a felt ache, so what she's describing could be a very sophisticated alarm system with no one home to feel it. (3) Marisol shot back that "no neocortex" can't be the test, since birds don't have a six-layered one either, and everyone now happily grants that crows and parrots have rich inner lives, run off a totally different structure (the pallium) doing analogous work -- so a missing part just means the wiring differs, not that no ones home. (4) Dov said the bird case earns that inference from convergent evidence across a ton of independent, hard-to-fake cognitive tasks -- tool use, planning, social reasoning -- while the fish case leans on basically one flavor of pain-behavior study, so treating them as equally solid is a bit of a switcheroo. (5) Marisol said that's convenient: whatever fish do, Dov can always relabel it "just a complex reflex," so nothing could ever count as evidence for him, which makes his skepticism unfalsifiable. (6) Dov said it's not unfalsifiable, just proportionate -- a claim this size (it bears on farming, angling, trillions of wild-caught deaths) needs more than "can't rule it out," or we'd owe the same caution to bees, or plants, or a thermostat.

I had to leave for the ferry before it resolved. Could you referee this move by move -- a quick score from -2 (clearly wrong) to +2 (clearly right) for each numbered move plus a sentence on why -- and then say if there's something bigger you think both of them are missing?
