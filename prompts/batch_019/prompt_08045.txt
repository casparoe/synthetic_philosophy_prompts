(a) Explain what reactive attitudes are and what role they play in his argument. (b) Explain the excuse/exemption distinction and why it matters for his reply to the pessimist about free will.

Katie's answer: "Strawson's basic move is to reframe the free will debate around what he calls reactive attitudes: things like resentment, gratitude, guilt, and indignation that we feel toward people depending on the quality of will their actions express toward us, rather than just the outcome of what they did. These are opposed to the 'objective attitude,' which is how we regard something to be managed or treated rather than someone to be held responsible. Strawson then splits the cases where reactive attitudes get suspended into two groups. Excusing conditions are things like 'he's mentally ill' or 'he's just a small child' -- these remove someone from the reactive framework altogether. Exempting conditions are things like 'he didn't mean to' or 'he was pushed' -- these apply to a single act rather than the whole person. This matters against the pessimist, who worries that universal determinism would work like one big excuse that empties moral responsibility out completely. Strawson's response is that since these attitudes are so deeply built into human relationships, their appropriateness doesn't depend on whether determinism is true, and the practice of holding people responsible is ultimately justified by how useful it is for cooperative life, so the metaphysical question turns out to be beside the point."
