My older sister (a philosophy PhD student) is getting married in three weeks and I got roped into giving the reception toast. Perfect timing: my moral uncertainty seminar just covered Toby Ord's idea of 'moral trade' -- people who disagree about ethics can still strike deals where each one comes out ahead by their OWN moral lights, not some shared standard, which is subtly different from just compromising. It's basically her whole relationship: she's an ethical vegetarian who thinks factory farming is a moral catastrophe, he thinks that's overblown but has quietly funded her animal-welfare donations for years, and in exchange she's dropped her grief about his Thursday poker nights (which she finds a little morally grimy and he finds totally fine) -- neither's budged on who's actually right, they've just found trades that work by each of their own standards. About half the wedding is her philosophy cohort, who'll clock the reference immediately; the other half is family who's never heard of Toby Ord in there life. Can you write the toast -- around 3 minutes spoken, warm more then funny, and it is NOT allowed to sound cold or transactional, even though the whole point is that it kind of literally is one.
