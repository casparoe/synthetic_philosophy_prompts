So in my philosophy elective we just read part of P.F. Strawson's Freedom and Resentment for our free will unit, and one move he makes is bugging me. He says we only drop reactive attitudes like resentment and blame for specific people (little kids, people who are mentally ill) by switching to what he calls the "objective attitude," and then argues that even if determinism were true for everyone, we couldn't actually make that switch globally, since reactive attitudes are too baked into normal human relationships to drop. He treats that as basically settling wether we're still justified in holding people responsible. That feels like a sleight of hand to me though, like he's only shown we WOULD keep reacting that way, not that its fair to if our actions are determined. Can you build the strongest version of the objection that Strawson is smuggling an "ought" out of an "is" here, i.e. that his naturalism just dodges the real justificatory question instead of answering it?
