I am an economics major taking a course on the history of economic thought this semester, which has led to some unusual dinner-table conversations. Over winter break, I mentioned to my grandfather, who ran a hardware store for forty years, that I had accepted a summer internship at a proprietary trading firm. He was unimpressed: his objection was not about risk or long hours, but that I would not actually be producing or selling anything real. In his view, trading is just "making money out of money" -- no different, morally, from charging interest on a loan, which he also regards with mild suspicion, even though he concedes that banks have to do it. My parents think he is being old-fashioned; they point out that trading firms provide liquidity and price discovery, and that interest simply compensates lenders for risk and for giving up the use of their own money. Still, I cannot shake the feeling that my grandfather is gesturing at something real, even if he cannot quite articulate it. Is there a substantive argument underlying his complaint, or is this just discomfort with an economy he does not fully understand?
