For my high school philosophy elective, I've been reading about Ian Hacking's concept of 'looping effects' in psychiatric classification, using his own case study of multiple personality disorder: diagnoses were almost unheard of before the 1970s, exploded once the category received sustained clinical and media attention, and became rare again once the therapeutic techniques behind it fell out of favor. Since that whole rise and fall tracks the diagnostic fashions of the time rather than anything else, doesn't it confirm that Hacking's term 'interactive kind' is just a more academic way of saying the diagnosis was fake and that nobody was ever really experiencing it? If so, I don't understand why he bothers with the softer, technical-sounding label instead of just saying outright that some psychiatric categories are inventions with no real referent.
