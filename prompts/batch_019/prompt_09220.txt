Write a dialogue between two epistemologists debating the KK principle after a colloquium on self-knowledge. One has just been persuaded by Timothy Williamson's anti-luminosity argument (from *Knowledge and Its Limits*) that no non-trivial condition is luminous — that is, no condition guarantees that whenever it obtains, one is in a position to know that it obtains — and concludes that KK, the thesis that knowing p entails knowing that one knows p, fails even for phenomenal self-knowledge. Have this character motivate the conclusion using the case of a subject who slowly warms from feeling cold to feeling hot, via margin-for-error reasoning. The other wants to save some version of KK but must do more than insist that introspection is infallible: have them either contest whether the margin-for-error principle really governs self-knowledge in the way Williamson needs, or press the reflexive worry that Williamson cannot know his own principle with the precision his argument requires. Each character should respond to the other's actual objections rather than restate their opening position, and the exchange need not end in agreement.
