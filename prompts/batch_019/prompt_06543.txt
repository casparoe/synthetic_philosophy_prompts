My podcast's next guest defends infinitism about the structure of epistemic justification -- the view that a belief is justified only if its backed by an endless, non-repeating series of further reasons, rather than the reasons terminating in some basic belief (foundationalism) or looping back into a circle (coherentism). This episode idea actually started because a friend dismissed the whole view as 'turtles all the way down with extra steps,' and I want the guest to get a real shot at earning back some credibility instead of just leaning on the 'incredulity isn't an argument' line, which is the part every summary of the view already covers. Can you draft me 8-10 interview questions that go beyond that opening move?

I definitely want at least one question on the objection that no finite mind could ever possess or run through infinitely many reasons, and how that's supposed to square with the 'the next reason just has to be available, not actually produced' reply; one on whether the non-repeating requirement can even survive contact with an actual finite person, since sooner or later a real human's reasons-for-reasons either loop back on themselves (which sounds a lot like coherentism) or run dry (which sounds a lot like foundationalism); and one pressing on whether a chain with no first member can confer justification on anything at all, or whether calling that 'holistic' support is just coherentism sneaking back in under a new name. For each of those, give me a natural follow-up keyed to whatever answer the guest is most likely to give, not just a rephrasing of the question. If it fits, throw in something on the meta-level version of the regress too -- does the principle that every justified belief needs a further reason need its own infinite backing, or does it just stop somewhere -- and maybe end on a question about what would actually have to happen for the guest to give up on infinitism for one of the rivals.
