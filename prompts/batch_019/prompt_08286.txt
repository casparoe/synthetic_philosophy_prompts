I brought this up mostly as a throwaway complaint, but it somehow led to a whole argument between two friends of mine, Marcus and Nadia, that ran through most of dessert and that I never actually resolved.

(1) Marcus said it was obvious that Jake's not watering the plants caused them to die -- if Jake had done what he'd agreed to, they'd still be alive, so what else would you even call that but a cause. (2) Nadia said hang on: by that exact logic, every other person on the planet who also didn't water my plants equally "caused" their death, herself included, since it's just as true of her that if she had flown over and watered them, they wouldn't have died -- and since we obviously don't want to say Nadia, or some random stranger in Peru, caused my tomatoes to die, mere counterfactual dependence can't be enough to make an omission a cause. (3) Marcus came back with: the difference is that Jake actually agreed to water them, so there was a real expectation on him that there wasn't on some random stranger, and that's what picks him out. (4) Nadia said that just relocates the problem -- now "cause" is tracking who made a promise, which is a fact about who's to blame, not a fact about who caused what, and those two can come apart (you can be blamed for something you didn't cause, and you can cause something you're not remotely to blame for). (5) Marcus pushed back that this isn't some special fix invented for omissions: ordinary causal talk works this way constantly, since we say the lit match caused the barn fire and never "the oxygen in the barn" caused it, even though the fire needed both -- picking out the abnormal factor against a backdrop of normal conditions is just how causal explanation always goes. (6) Nadia's last move brought in something else entirely: she said that, strictly speaking, nothing physical passed between Jake and the plant at all -- the plant died via a purely internal process, water failing to reach its roots, that was already underway -- so Jake didn't do anything to the plant, he just failed to interrupt a death that was already happening, and maybe the honest conclusion is that nobody "caused" it in the full sense at all.
