My younger brother is getting married next month, and as his best man I've been asked to give the toast. He's finishing a philosophy PhD, and his dissertation defends Molinism -- the view, developed by the sixteenth-century Jesuit Luis de Molina, that God possesses "middle knowledge" of what every possible free creature would freely do in every possible set of circumstances, and providentially governs history by choosing which circumstances to actualize, all without overriding anyone's libertarian freedom. He and his fiancée met at a dinner party that only happened because a mutual friend's original plans for that evening fell through, and the two of them have turned this into a running joke about which "counterfactual of creaturely freedom" happened to be true that night, out of all the ways things could have gone.

About half the guests are people from his department, including his advisor, who will notice immediately if I get the concepts wrong -- the difference between middle knowledge and ordinary foreknowledge, say, or the idea that not every logically possible arrangement of events counts as a "feasible world" once the relevant counterfactuals are fixed. The other half are relatives and old friends who have no idea what any of this means and just want a warm, funny toast about two people who clearly belong together. Please write the five-minute speech. It should use the actual machinery of Molinism, accurately, to say something true about the marriage being both providentially fitting and genuinely, contingently chosen by each of them -- and it needs to avoid the deflating implication that providence would have been just as happy pairing either of them with someone else, since the whole point of the toast is that it had to be these two people freely saying yes to each other. It should read as a toast that people can laugh at and be moved by, not as a philosophy of religion lecture.
