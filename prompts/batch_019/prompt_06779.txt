I'm teaching the epistemic-foundations-of-games unit in my decision theory seminar this week, and instead of throwing another wall of possibility-correspondence notation at my students before we get into the actual proofs, I want to give them a dialogue to chew on first.

Here's the bit I want dramatized. Everyone agrees plain backward induction says a rational player should grab the pot at the very first node of the centipede game, since the whole "keep passing along" arrangement unravels once each player knows the other will defect as soon as doing so is individually optimal. Aumann's famous result is that common knowledge of rationality forces this outcome even once you build in a story about what each player would do at nodes that never actually get reached -- and his own reply to the obvious objection is basically that if a deviation ever does happen, all that shows is you were never in a common-knowledge-of-rationality situation to begin with, so the theorem was never claiming to tell you what to do next. Binmore's side of it (this got a bit heated between the two of them in print back in the mid-90s) is that this makes the whole result strategically idle for exactly the situations where you'd want guidance, and that "rationality at a node whose very occurrence already falsifies common knowledge of rationality" was never a coherent notion to begin with. Rosenthal, who cooked up the game in the first place, seems to have had a version of this worry in mind too, well before the epistemic logic crowd got a hold of it.

Could you write me a dialogue of maybe 10-14 exchanges between an Aumannian and a Binmorean hashing this out -- ideally staged at the exact moment player 2 has just watched player 1 pass at node 1, so the stakes are concrete rather than purely abstract? I dont want either side to just restate their conclusion louder; I want the Binmorean to really press on what work "common knowledge of rationality" is even doing once its been contradicted by the history so far, and I want the Aumannian to have a genuine comeback to that instead of just gesturing at the theorem. Bonus if by the end they've managed to localize the disagreement down to one specific premise rather than just talking past each other.
