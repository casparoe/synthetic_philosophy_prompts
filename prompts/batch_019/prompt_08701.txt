In my philosophy elective, we have been studying Otto Neurath, the Vienna Circle philosopher who spent decades developing Isotype, an international picture language of simple, standardized icons meant to convey statistical and social facts across barriers of language and literacy. Three of his design commitments have stayed with me: a larger quantity had to be shown by repeating an icon at a fixed size rather than by enlarging it, the icons themselves were kept flat and free of shading so that they would not smuggle in emotional connotations, and the whole system was meant only as a supplement to ordinary language, since he thought pictures alone struggled with things like negation and hypotheticals. I have been wondering what Neurath would make of emoji, which are often described today as a kind of universal picture language. Judging strictly by the principles he actually set out, rather than his general enthusiasm for pictures over words, would he most likely see emoji as an imperfect but genuine continuation of his project, or as something that betrays what he was actually trying to achieve? Please justify your answer.
