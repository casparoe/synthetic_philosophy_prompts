so for my phil of religion seminar we've been reading this David Lewis paper (i think its called "Do We Believe in Penal Substitution?") where he tries to figure out whether penal substitution actually makes logical sense, not just whether its good theology. his main hangup is a debt analogy: if I owe you $50, literally anyone can pay it off for me and we're square, no questions asked about who actually paid. but punishment doesn't seem to work like that — if I'm sentenced to five years, it doesn't clear my record if some volunteer serves the time instead of me, even if they're begging to do it. so on his view "Christ was punished in our place" ends up being a genuinely puzzling claim, not just a mystery you're supposed to accept on faith.

I can't stop wondering how Calvin specifically would've responded to this exact objection, since he obviously never had to deal with 20th century analytic philosophy of punishment. does his actual theology have the resources to answer the transferability worry, or is it more of a blind spot that never got addressed because the objection just hadn't been posed this way yet? curious what you think his strongest comeback would've been.
