For an independent research paper on AI alignment, I have been reading Eliezer Yudkowsky's original 2004 proposal for Coherent Extrapolated Volition, in which a superintelligence is meant to act not on humanity's present, unreflective preferences but on the preferences a wiser, better-informed, and more unified version of us would settle on after much further reflection. What unsettles me is a worry that seems to generalize well beyond CEV to any "idealized preference" account of what is good for a person. It is a kind of alienation problem: the more radically you idealize someone, the less clear it becomes that the idealized version's preferences are still theirs to be governed by, rather than the preferences of someone new who merely happens to share their history. Could you write a short story that dramatizes this tension, centered on a character who is shown an AI's computation of their own extrapolated volition and must decide how much authority it deserves over their actual, unextrapolated life? I would rather the ending leave the philosophical question genuinely open than resolve it neatly, and I would like the story to avoid the generic "AI takes over the world" framing in favor of staying close to this personal-identity problem.
