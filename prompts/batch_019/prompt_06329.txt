So, I'm a retired biochemist (ribosome and translation biochemistry, mostly) and I only got serious about philosophy after retiring a couple years back, so bare with me if I fumble the terminology.

At a reunion dinner with old labmates last month, two former colleagues, Priya and Marcus, got into it over whether DNA is 'really' a code or just a metaphor us molecular biologists got too attached to. Roughly how it went:

(1) Marcus said DNA obviously is a literal code, not a metaphor: there's an actual table mapping all 64 codons onto 20 amino acids plus stop signals, and the mapping is essentially arbitrary -- nothing chemically forces GCU to mean alanine rather than something else. That arbitrariness, he said, is exactly what separates real codes, like Morse code, from ordinary causal processes like a river depositing silt.

(2) Priya said fine, that narrow translation step is code-like, but that's a much smaller claim than the one everybody actually relies on -- that DNA carries 'the information for building the organism.' That bigger claim is shakier, since identical genomes produce different outcomes depending on the cytoplasm and environment around them. If DNA counts as carrying information, she said, so should all the other developmental factors that are just as causally necessary, and then DNA isn't specially privileged.

(3) Marcus answered that there's a principled reason to single DNA out anyway: it's the one part of the system natural selection specifically shaped to be copied and read out reliably across generations, which is a stronger status than merely being a necessary ingredient, even granting the surrounding cellular machinery is selected too.

(4) Priya shot back that plenty of non-DNA stuff is also reliably copied and selected across generations -- she brought up prions and some epigenetic inheritance cases -- so his 'shaped by selection to be copied' test doesn't actually draw the line only around DNA like he needs it to.

Nobody at the table could settle it and it's been bugging me since. Could you go through the exchange move by move and say who's got the better of it at each step -- not just who wins overall -- and flag anything you think both of them are still missing?
