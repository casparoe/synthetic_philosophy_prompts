Quick thing I've been chewing on for a seminar on reasons. The wrong kind of reasons (WKR) problem usually gets raised against fitting-attitude theories of value -- the demon who'll torture you unless you admire some worthless object, so now you have a reason to admire it that has nothing to do with whether its actually admirable. It occured to me that this is basically the same structure as the old evidentialist vs pragmatist fight over belief -- Pascal's wager gives you a reason to believe in God that's totally disconnected from whether God exists. Are these really the same problem wearing different clothes, or am I forcing an analogy that doesn't hold? Compare the two for me -- does the usual move for separating right from wrong kind reasons in the value case (the object-given/state-given distinction, Parfit's right?) carry over cleanly to reasons for belief, or does it break down somewhere? And which version of the problem strikes you as harder to solve, and why?
