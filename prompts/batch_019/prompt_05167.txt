I sit on my university's honorary degrees committee (they let philosophy professors on these things too, mostly so someone can slow the process down). Current case: a trustee who made his fortune in biotech has let the provost know, not too subtly, that a $30M gift for a new public health building is 'a lot more likely' if we hand him an honorary doctorate at spring commencement. He's not undeserving on paper either - he funds vaccine distribution abroad and could plausibly clear the bar on merit alone. Half the committee thinks motive doesn't matter: hes accomplished, honors get timed for administratively convenient moments all the time for reasons that have nothing to do with merit, and this is no different. The other half thinks that once the reason we're actually acting on is the threatened gift, it doesn't matter that he's independently deserving too - the degree would just be a payoff that happens to land on a meritorious guy, not a response to his merits at all, and doing it now teaches every future donor exactly how this works. Our bylaws only say honorees should show 'sustained contribution to the public good,' nothing about gifts either way. Can you write the memo I'd bring to committee before we vote next month - a real recommendation (award now, defer, decline, or something else), the reasoning behind it, and something honest to say to the 'timing doesn't matter' camp instead of just muttering 'bribery'?
