I'm writing an extra-credit paper for my physics class on the history of the EPR debate, and I'd rather not just repeat the standard line that Einstein simply disliked quantum mechanics. He died in 1955, nine years before Bell published his theorem and six decades before the 2015 experiments that finally closed the locality and detection loopholes together and experimentally excluded local realism. Given what's actually documented about Einstein's specific position — his insistence in the EPR paper on locally definite "elements of reality," his later remark to Schrödinger that he felt Podolsky's write-up had buried his real point, and his talk of "spooky action at a distance" in the letters to Born — which of the two assumptions, locality or realism, do you think he would have sacrificed once local realism as a whole was ruled out? And do you think he would have accepted that outcome at all, or would he more likely have looked for another way out, such as denying that experimenters' measurement choices are truly independent of the hidden variables?
