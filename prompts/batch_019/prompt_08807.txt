So I spent something like 35 years doing condensed matter physics before I retired, and lately I've fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole. Right now I'm stuck on the endurantism vs perdurantism debate -- whether persisting things are "wholly present" at every moment they exist, or are really four-dimensional "worms" made up of temporal parts, the way a garden hose has different bits of hose at different points along its length. I wrote up an argument below that I suspect isn't original, though I can't find anyone laying it out carefully, and I'd like you to tear it apart a bit.

Here's the argument. Special relativity kills any single global "now" -- that's just the relativity of simultaneity, not really up for debate. Putnam and Rietdijk pointed out back in the 60s (and Penrose has a nice version involving an invasion fleet near Andromeda) that once you accept this, presentism is in real trouble: whether a distant event counts as "already happened" depends on your state of motion, so there's no principled fact about which 3D slice of the universe is "the real one." Fine, that just gets you eternalism, the block universe, all times equally real. But I want to push a second step: if all times are equally real and nothing picks out a privileged "now" to tell us which 3D cross-section of an object counts as the whole object, then surely the natural thing to say is that the object just is the entire 4D worm, and "the object at t1" and "the object at t2" are merely temporal parts of it -- exactly the way a cross-section of the hose at one spot is a spatial part, not the whole hose. That's perdurantism. So relativity doesn't just buy you eternalism, it pushes you the rest of the way to perdurantism, and endurantism starts looking like it's smuggling in exactly the kind of privileged, frame-dependent present that relativity already threw out.

I think this also dissolves Lewis's problem of temporary intrinsics for free -- the puzzle about how the same person can be bent while sitting and straight while standing without having contradictory shapes -- because the 4D worm never needs one shape, only its parts do, so no contradiction and no need for tense operators or relativized properties.

What I want from you: is the move from relativity of simultaneity to full-on perdurantism actually valid, or am I quietly sliding between two seperate debates (whether past and future are real, versus whether things have temporal parts) without noticing? If those really are seperable, is there a respectable way to be an eternalist and an endurantist at the same time? And is my temporary-intrinsics point doing the work I think it's doing, or is there a decent endurantist comeback I'm missing? Don't be gentle about it -- I'd rather find out this is a well-known (and already answered) move than feel clever about it.
