I've been reading LessWrong, the old Slate Star Codex, and now Astral Codex Ten for close to a decade, and a few of us from my city's ACX meetup eventually split off into a smaller monthly group that works through classic philosophy-of-language papers rather than the usual rationalist fare -- Frege and Russell on definite descriptions last year, and for the last three sessions, Kripke's Naming and Necessity.

One of our regulars turned out to be a philosopher who has spent much of her career defending the Kripke-Putnam causal-historical picture of reference -- rigid designators, natural-kind essentialism, the necessary a posteriori -- against essentially every objection raised in the decades since, and she's agreed to a live Q&A to close out our unit on the book. She's a sharp, practiced debater with a habit of answering objections by simply restating the modal argument against descriptivism (the point that "Aristotle might not have taught Alexander" is no contradiction) as though that alone settled the matter, so I want the questions specific enough that a bare restatement won't actually dispose of them.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions for the session? I want at least one that presses the "qua problem" for ostensive reference-fixing: if you point at a sample that's simultaneously a case of gold, of metal, of yellow stuff, and of matter, what makes the reference-fixing latch onto the kind gold specifically and not one of the others. I want one that uses the jade case -- jadeite and nephrite are chemically unrelated minerals that a single pre-scientific term picked out for centuries -- to ask what a natural-kind essentialist should say about terms that turn out to carve no real joint in nature. I want one that runs the Gödel/Schmidt case and asks her to say positively, not just negatively, what does secure reference for an ordinary name if not any description competent speakers associate with it. I want one asking whether the necessity of origin -- that this table couldn't have been made from a wholly different block of wood, that a person couldn't have come from different gametes -- is a considered commitment of her view or an embarrassment she'd rather quietly set aside. I'd also like a harder one that puts Chalmers's and Jackson's two-dimensionalist reconstruction to her directly, asking whether separating primary from secondary intension shows that the "necessary a posteriori" was never first-order metaphysics at all, just bookkeeping for how we individuate epistemic possibilities -- and then a closing question asking what she would accept as actual evidence against the causal theory, as opposed to a puzzle for the theory to absorb with further refinements. For each question, please add a short, specific follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give, rather than a generic prompt for elaboration.
