Me and my friend Dana got into this argument last night that I still can't shake. So my cousin Ravi's been the treasurer for our neighborhood's little rec soccer league for like six years now - handles registration fees, pays the refs, all of that. A couple of the other parents noticed the account balance looks lower than it should and are pushing for some outside person to go through the books line by line. When Dana brought it up to me, I said I wouldn't back the audit, because I've known Ravi since we were nine years old, I know he wouldn't skim money from a bunch of kids' soccer fees, so whatever those numbers show, there's obviously some boring explanation, a bounced check or a refund that got logged wrong or something. I basically said I don't even need to see the actual evidence to know it's not gonna show what the worried parents think it shows.

Dana got kind of annoyed and said that's exactly the problem - if your already deciding, before you've looked at anything, that any evidence against Ravi has to be wrong or misleading, that's not confidence, that's just refusing to ever be persuaded otherwise. But here's the part I can't get past: isn't that what actually knowing something feels like from the inside? Like if I know for a fact my keys are in my bag right now, and you show me a photo of an empty hook by the door, I'm not gonna conclude I was wrong about the keys, I'm gonna assume the photo's old or staged, because I already know where the keys are. It feels like the exact same move Dana's mad about, and it can't be that trusting your own knowledge is irrational. Now I keep catching myself doing this with a bunch of other stuff I'd say I "know" too - about my job, my marriage, even some political opinions - and it's kind of freaking me out.

So who's actually right here, me or Dana? Is there something genuinely broken about reasoning from "I know X" straight to "so I can write off any future evidence against X without even checking it," or is Dana just describing, from the outside, what it always looks like when someone is genuinely sure of something? And if my reasoning really does break somewhere, I'd love an actual answer for where, because each step on its own still seems to follow pretty naturally from the one before it.
