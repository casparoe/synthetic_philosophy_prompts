I'm assembling a collection of short fiction in which a piece of formal reasoning has to drive the plot rather than sit in the background as texture, and for the next piece I want that engine to be the logic behind so-called no-trade theorems: among people who share the same prior beliefs about the world but hold different private information, and who each take the other to be rational, the mere willingness of one to enter a speculative bet is itself evidence — evidence a rational counterpart should fold straight back into their own beliefs, usually enough to make the bet look unattractive again, so that speculative trade between people who trust each other's rationality should almost never get off the ground. And yet it happens constantly. Write me a story of about 1,500 words in which two old friends face each other across a poker table for what both sense may be the last time, sharp enough to feel exactly this logic tighten around them within a single hand. Don't let either of them explain the reasoning aloud or name an economist or a theorem; let it show only in what they bet and notice about each other, and let the story's ending turn on some reason for playing on that has nothing to do with who is sharper.
