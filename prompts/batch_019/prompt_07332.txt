So, retired physicist here, got into philosophy since retiring and I keep coming back to Bernard Williams's internal/external reasons paper. Being an old stats-mech guy, I'm naturally drawn to his internalism: reasons bottoming out in the agent's existing motivations (his 'S') is basically reasons-as-whatever-your-utility-function-spits-out. But 'sound' in his phrase 'sound deliberative route from S' seems to be doing all the real work: if any route counts, internalism is trivial (wishful thinking manufactures a reason for anything you like), and if he needs an independent standard of soundness, that standard isnt itself an element of S, so hasn't he smuggled back in exactly the external, motivation-independent standard he was trying to rule out? Give me the strongest case you can that Williams has the resources to define 'sound deliberation' without either trivializing internalism or quietly reintroducing external reasons.
