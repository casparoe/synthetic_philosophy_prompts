I fell down a rabbit hole this week while reporting a piece on the "chart crimes" discourse in data journalism — you know, the Alberto Cairo/dataviz-twitter crowd that gets mad when infographics resize an icon to show scale (like a bigger syringe meaning "way more vaccinations"), cause doubling the height of a 2D shape roughly quadruples its apparent area, so your brain reads the ratio as way bigger than it actually is. Turns out this exact problem was basically solved a century ago: Otto Neurath's Isotype system (born as the "Vienna Method" at his Social and Economic Museum in Red Vienna, later refined with Gerd Arntz doing the actual icon drawings and Marie Reidemeister doing the job they literally called "transformer" — the person who converts raw statistics into the picture grammar) had a hard rule against resizing: you show "more" by repeating the same-size icon, never by blowing one up. That wasn't just a style choice, it was tied to his whole Vienna Circle-ish insistence that public-facing "language," visual or verbal, shouldn't smuggle in false precision, and to his hope that Isotype could work as a picture-based cousin of Basic English — an international helper-language for people who couldn't be assumed to share a spoken one.

Neurath obviously died in 1945, decades before any of this became a mass medium, which is what's actually bugging me. Based on what's on record about his real aims for Isotype (the worker-education angle, the transformer/artist division of labor, the anti-illusionism, the internationalist ambitions), what do you think he'd make of today's infographic and data-viz culture? Would something like an Our World in Data chart or a COVID dashboard read to him as his project basically realized, or would he mostly be annoyed that most Instagram infographics break his most basic rule within the first five seconds? And seperately — would he count emoji, or the icon sets on airport signage and app UIs, as a genuine continuation of "picture language" in his sense, or is that a fundamentally different animal because it isn't doing the statistical/quantitative transformation work he actually cared about? I'd rather you commit to an actual reconstruction here than tell me it's unknowable — I think there's enough on record about the guy's commitments to make a real case either way.
