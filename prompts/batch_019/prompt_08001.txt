I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I'm finishing a piece on how far quantum computing has advanced since Google's 2019 "quantum supremacy" announcement. An editor pushed back on a line in my draft stating that Shor's algorithm and the speedups built on it have effectively disproven the Church-Turing thesis: a quantum computer, the argument goes, can solve certain problems in a feasible number of steps that no classical machine could ever match, so the thesis's claim about what counts as computable no longer holds. Given how mature the hardware has become, would it be accurate to tell readers that this is now a settled result, or is there a genuine reason serious computer scientists still hedge on the point?
