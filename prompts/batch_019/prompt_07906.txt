Last night a friend and I argued for two hours about whether Popper's idea of verisimilitude ever recovered from the Tichý–Miller result: the 1974 proof that, given Popper's own definitions of truth content and falsity content, no false theory can ever count as more verisimilar than any other false theory -- which gutted the whole point, since Popper introduced the notion precisely to rank theories we already know to be false (Newtonian mechanics is presumably closer to the truth than Aristotelian physics, even though both are strictly false). I argued the underlying intuition is worth salvaging with better formal tools; he argued that every repair proposed since 1974 just reproduces the same trouble in a new guise, for instance verisimilitude orderings that reverse under harmless-looking changes to a theory's vocabulary.

That argument has landed on my desk. I sit on the tenure committee in my philosophy department, and one candidate's case rests heavily on a monograph proposing a possible-worlds measure meant to repair Popper's definition -- until an outside referee's letter pointed out that her measure inherits exactly this kind of language-dependence. Half the committee wants to deny tenure on the grounds that a technical research program with a live, unresolved flaw at its core isn't achieved knowledge, whatever its elegance; the other half thinks refutation-and-repair is what real progress on a hard problem looks like, and that one flaw shouldn't erase six years of serious, well-regarded work. Please draft the memo I'll circulate before next month's faculty vote: a recommendation -- grant tenure, deny it, or recommend a one-year extension of the clock so she can respond to the critique in print -- with reasons the committee could actually debate, including a fair statement of the strongest case against whichever recommendation you make.
