For the past year I've worn a sleep-tracking ring that gives me a nightly readiness score out of 100, and at some point, without quite deciding to, I turned improving that score into a personal project. For a long while this tracked something real: as my weekly average climbed from the 60s into the 80s, I genuinely woke up sharper and less foggy. Lately, though, reaching the high 90s has meant lying rigidly still for an extra hour before getting up, skipping any evening exercise, and taking melatonin most nights - and I feel worse, not better, despite the higher numbers. My partner thinks the ring has simply stopped working for me, but that feels too convenient, since it's the same device, doing the same measurements, that used to track how rested I actually felt quite well. What's actually going on here, and is there a way to use a tool like this that doesn't eventually hit this same wall - or is any number doomed to become worthless once you start optimizing it directly?
