I'm the non-physician admin rep on my hospital's Medical Executive Committee, and we've got a genuinely nasty case coming to a head at Monday's meeting. A surgeon has had three different nurses independently report, over the last month, that he seemed unsteady and smelled of alcohol before two separate procedures -- but he was never actually tested in the moment, there's no toxicology on file, and his own explanation for both incidents (allergy meds, a hand-sanitizer mishap) is at least plausible taken on its own. About half the committee wants to put him on interim suspension today pending a full investigation; the other half says nurse impressions plus no positive test has never been our bar for suspending anyone's ability to work before, and that we'd basically be inventing a new, lower standard purely because the downside of being wrong -- a patient hurt mid-surgery -- sounds scarier than the downside in cases we didn't suspend for.

What's turned this into a two-meeting stalemate is that one of our members has a philosophy MA from before med school, and she keeps insisting this is a live case of "pragmatic encroachment" -- her worry is that if we let how bad it'd be to be wrong shrink how much evidence counts as "enough to act on," we're doing something different in kind from just being extra cautious with the same evidence, and the committee ought to be honest about which one it's actually doing. She's also brought up epistemic contextualism, pointing out that the language we'd use in an interim finding ("reasonable cause to believe") versus a final revocation finding ("the Committee finds") might already be smuggling in exactly the standard-shifting she's worried about, just dressed up as neutral legal drafting.

I got asked to write the actual decision memo since I'm suppose to be the neutral party here, and I don't want to just paper over her point to get to a recommendation faster -- but I also can't hand the committee three pages of epistemology with no bottom line. Can you draft that memo: a recommendation on the interim suspension with a concrete timeline for the fuller investigation, one that actually takes a stand on whether it's legitimate for the suspension decision to lower the evidential bar itself, versus holding the evidence fixed and doing all the stakes-sensitivity through the choice of action instead.
