I spent my whole career as a research chemist, and now that I'm retired I've finally got time to read philosophy instead of just journal articles, which has been surprisingly fun. Anyway, heres a real dilemma from my own living room I can't quite get my head around. My wife thinks it's wrong to keep shopping at the big box store near us because of how they treat warehouse workers, but she's never given a second thought to where our retirement savings are invested. I don't care much which store I shop at, but it's always bugged me that some of our index funds are sitting in weapons manufacturers and oil companies. We've argued for years about whose issue is the "real" one and gotten nowhere, so we finally struck a deal instead: I stopped shopping at that store, and she helped me move our savings into funds that screen out the stuff that bothers me. Neither of us actually changed our mind about the other's issue, we just each started acting on it. Works great in practice, but something about it feels slightly off, like we're just buying each other off rather than actually being more ethical. Are we onto something sensible here, or just letting each other off the hook?
