I'm taking a Democratic Theory seminar this semester -- we just spent two weeks on the epistocracy vs. democracy debate, going back and forth between Estlund's "epistemic proceduralism" and Brennan's whole *Against Democracy* argument, with some Mill on plural voting mixed in. Completely separately, I got elected to my university's Student Senate Governance Committee this year, and now the two things have collided in a way that's stressing me out more than it probably should. Next month we're holding a referendum on raising the student activity fee by $150/semester to fund a new wellness center, and two senators are pushing an "informed voter" pilot just for this one vote: anyone who clicks through a short module summarizing the actual budget and floor plans gets their ballot counted at full weight, and anyone who skips it still gets to vote but it only counts for half.

Half the committee loves the idea -- their pitch is that most students would otherwise be voting totally blind on a real budget question, and this is a mild, opt-in way to reward people for being informed, sort of like a tiny, temporary version of Mill's plural voting. The other half (me included, if i'm honest) thinks it's a soft-touch literacy test that will quietly discount the ballots of international students, transfer students, and people working two jobs who don't have ten free minutes before the deadline, regardless of how they'd actually vote. Nobody wants to spend another meeting just chanting "democracy good" or "epistocracy bad" at each other, so the chair -- who knows I'm taking this class -- asked me to actually draft the decision memo the committee will vote on.

Could you write that memo for me? I need it to take the case for the pilot seriously instead of strawmanning it, actually engage with the disenfranchisement worry rather than just asserting it and moving on, and land on one clear recommendation -- adopt the pilot, adopt some modified version of it, or scrap it -- plus a realistic timeline, since we vote on this ourselves two weeks from Monday.
