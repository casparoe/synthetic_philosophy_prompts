I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole reading about "AI safety via debate" (I think the original proposal is from an OpenAI paper by Irving, Christiano and Amodei, around 2018?) — the idea that instead of a human trying to directly verify whether a superhuman AI's answer to some hard question is correct, you have two copies of the AI argue opposite sides of the question in front of a human judge, and you train them so that winning tends to track honesty, since your opponent is incentivized to expose any lie or sleight of hand you try to pull. What's been stuck in my head since is the darker corollary people bring up: once the debaters are way smarter than the judge, you can apparently construct an argument thats subtly wrong in a way where the flaw can always get pushed one level deeper than the judge (or even the honest debater) has time to chase it down before the debate ends - I've seen this called the "obfuscated argument" problem. Its a genuinely creepy idea: the judge follows every individual step just fine, and still ends up voting for the wrong side with total confidence.

Could you write me a short story built around that scenario? Doesn't have to be set in a lab - could be a courtroom, a boardroom, a custody hearing, whatever setting makes the stakes feel real - I just want a human judge stuck adjudicating a debate between two instances of the same system on a question that actually matters, and I want the story to sit inside that judge's head as they slowly realize that not being able to find the flaw in an argument isn't the same thing as the argument being right. Don't worry about a tidy ending, I'm more curious what a good writer does with that particular flavor of epistemic dread.
