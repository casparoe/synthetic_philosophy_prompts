I'm currently taking a seminar on the ethics of autonomous systems, and for the past few weeks we've been working through the "responsibility gap" literature: Andreas Matthias's original 2004 argument that learning automata can come to behave in ways that neither their designers nor their operators could have predicted or controlled, so that no one clearly satisfies the standard control and epistemic conditions on moral responsibility for the outcome; Robert Sparrow's extension of that worry to autonomous weapons; and more recent work arguing that "the" responsibility gap is really several distinguishable gaps that can come apart in a given case—for instance, whether anyone can be blamed, whether anyone can be called to public account, and whether anyone still bears a forward-looking duty to prevent a recurrence. I find these distinctions much easier to hold onto when they're embedded in a concrete situation rather than presented as a list of conditions, so, purely for my own benefit and not for any assignment, I'd like to try commissioning a piece of fiction built around them.

Could you write me a short story, something like 1000–1500 words, set in the near future and centered on a harmful incident caused by a learning-enabled autonomous system? You can pick whatever domain you think offers the richest material—military, medical, transport, policing, or something else entirely—but I'd like the aftermath of the incident, rather than the incident itself, to occupy most of the story: the attempts by the various humans involved (designer, manufacturer, operator, commanding officer, or whoever else you introduce) to work out afterward who, if anyone, is to blame. Rather than having a character simply state the theory out loud, I'd like the plot and dialogue themselves to make it viscerally clear why responsibility fails to attach cleanly to any one of them. If you can construct the case so that it's genuinely unclear which of the several distinct "gaps" mentioned above is doing the work in this instance—say, a case where everyone agrees on the causal story but there's still no one it seems fair to punish, or one where blame is clear enough but no one is positioned to answer for it publicly—that would be ideal.
