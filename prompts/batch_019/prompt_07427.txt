I've been a longtime lurker of LessWrong and the old Overcoming Bias archives, and my local rationalist-adjacent reading group has spent the last few sessions working through Solomonoff induction and Kolmogorov complexity as a formalization of Occam's razor. Last time, someone new to the material summarized the whole idea as "the simplest program wins," and one of our more pedantic regulars shot back: "simplest to state, or simplest to run?" The resulting tangent, about Kolmogorov complexity versus Levin complexity and Schmidhuber's speed prior, ended up being the highlight of the evening.

I'd like a short joke for our next meetup that turns on exactly that distinction: between the universal prior's notion of simplicity (shortest description length, indifferent to runtime) and the alternative built into Levin complexity and the speed prior, which also penalizes slow programs. It needn't be more than a line or two, and it doesn't need to settle any real debate — but it should only really land for someone who already understands why "shortest" and "fastest" are different axes here, rather than read as a generic joke about nerds who like simplicity.
