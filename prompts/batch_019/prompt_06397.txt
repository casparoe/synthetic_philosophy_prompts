I've been reading Nietzsche's Ecce Homo for my philosophy class, and I was struck by how much space he devotes to seemingly mundane physiological matters -- nutrition, climate, and the choice of recreation -- treating them as more decisive for the shape of a person's spirit than most of the "great" philosophical questions, and even faulting German cuisine for what he takes to be the heaviness of the German mind. That struck me as odd next to the Genealogy of Morals, where he attacks ascetic self-denial as fundamentally life-negating and a symptom of physiological decline. So here is my question: is Nietzsche's own strict personal discipline around food and health a genuinely different sort of thing from the ascetic ideal he condemns, or is it really just asceticism by another name? If you think he has principled grounds for distinguishing the two, please spell out exactly what they are and say whether, in your judgment, they actually hold up.
