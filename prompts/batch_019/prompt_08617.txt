I'm a cognitive science major currently taking a seminar on philosophy of mind and AI, and this term I'm also the student representative on my department's undergraduate research fund committee, which awards small grants for independent projects. A strong proposal before us seeks funding to make progress on the eliciting latent knowledge (ELK) problem in a small open-source language model: the student would train a linear probe on the model's hidden activations against a benchmark of factual questions with checkable ground truth, then defer to the probe whenever it disagrees with the model's stated answer. Two faculty members on the committee are enthusiastic, calling this genuine progress toward trustworthy AI self-report; a third objects that a probe validated only on checkable questions cannot be distinguished from a "human simulator" that has merely learned to predict what a human grader would say, rather than a genuine "direct translator" of the model's own internal representation -- and that the two only come apart on the hard, unverifiable cases that motivate ELK to begin with. Please draft the committee's decision memo -- fund, decline, or fund with specific required revisions -- that I can circulate before next week's meeting. It should give the student concrete, actionable criteria rather than a vague verdict, and it should take the enthusiastic members' reasoning seriously rather than simply deferring to the objection.
