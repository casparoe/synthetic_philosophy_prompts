I'm taking a philosophy of medicine seminar this semester, and something going on at home has been bothering me in a way our readings haven't quite settled. My grandmother is 79, and her doctor recently flagged her kidney function, hearing, and bone density as all below normal, proposing to treat each as its own condition going forward. My grandmother's response is that she doesn't have three new diseases, she just has an old body, and that if these numbers are typical for someone her age, calling them "disease" is simply the wrong word. Her doctor countered that how common a decline is at a given age has no bearing on whether it's actually a disease, and pointed out that some of these measurements, like bone density, are deliberately judged against a young, healthy adult rather than against her age group, precisely so real problems don't get dismissed as "just aging." Both positions strike me as reasonable on their own terms, but I can't tell whether they're actually compatible or whether one of them rests on a mistaken picture of what disease even is. Is there a principled way to settle who has the better view here, or is the dispute confused from the start?
