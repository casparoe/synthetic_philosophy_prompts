I sit on the health technology assessment committee that decides which treatments our health system will fund, using cost-per-QALY thresholds to compare across unrelated conditions. We keep deferring the same methodological question: whether the utility weights behind those calculations should come from people currently living with a given health state, or from members of the general public asked to imagine living with it. The two sources reliably diverge—patients tend to rate their own state higher than the public expects, and the gap varies by condition—so the choice is not neutral: this cycle, two proposals competing for the same fixed budget would be funded in the opposite order depending on which source of weights we use. Draft the committee's decision memo recommending which source we should adopt going forward, or how to combine them, with an operational rule members can apply in future cycles and a candid account of the strongest objection to whichever recommendation you make.
