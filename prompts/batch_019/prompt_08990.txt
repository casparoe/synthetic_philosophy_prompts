ok so I've been on a bit of a Frank Ramsey kick lately -- not the decision theory / Ramsey sentence stuff for once, but this short essay called "Epilogue" that he read to the Apostles back in 1925. the gist is he flat-out refuses to feel humbled by the vastness of the universe: his mental picture of the world is drawn "in perspective" rather than to scale, so humans get to occupy the foreground and the stars are allowed to be tiny, because sheer size was never the kind of thing that generates importance in the first place -- thought and love and discovery do that, not bulk.

it struck me that this basically reads like a direct answer to the mood of Russell's "A Free Man's Worship" -- blind atoms, nobody intended us, everything eventually ends, and the most a person can do is defiantly build their ideals on top of "unyielding despair." Ramsey definitely would've known that essay, and I can't help but think he had it somewhere in the back of his head.

could you write me a dialogue between the two of them (or Ramsey vs. a Russell-voiced pessimist, if your don't want to put words in the real guy's mouth) actually hashing out whether the size, age, and indifference of the universe should diminish how much weight we put on human meaning and values? I don't want either one to be a pushover -- Russell shouldn't just be "sad guy who hasn't thought it through," and Ramsey's cheerfulness needs to survive Russell actually pushing back (like, is "just redraw the perspective" secretly motivated reasoning dressed up as an analogy?). want real back-and-forth here, not two monologues that happen to alternate.
