For the ten years we have run our business together, my partner has had a reputation among our friends for remarkable generosity: he covers the bill without being asked and quietly lends money to anyone going through a hard time. At the office, though, he is the one who nitpicks part-time staff's timesheets over a few minutes here and there and haggles suppliers down over fractions of a cent, and our employees have learned to dread negotiating money with him. I had always assumed that the generous version was who he really is, and that his office manner was simply a role adopted for practical reasons. A friend of mine recently argued the opposite: that there is no more reason to call one version the real him than the other, since he is merely responding, quite normally, to whatever pressures happen to operate in each setting. This is not an idle question for me, because he has now offered to run the finances of a new venture we are starting together, largely without my oversight, and I need to weigh his personal generosity against his professional stinginess in deciding whether to trust him with it. Is my friend right that asking which version is the real him is the wrong question, and if so, what should I be asking myself instead?
