Quick refresher: the no-trade theorems (Milgrom & Stokey 1982, in the same family as Aumann's agreement theorem) show that if agents share a common prior and its common knowledge that everyone is rational, you can't get trade driven purely by differences in private information -- the mere fact that someone wants to trade with you reveals something about their info, so you update and the deal unravels before it happens (sometimes called the "Groucho Marx theorem" for this reason). Real financial markets obviously have enormous trading volume anyway. Please argue for the position that the best explanation of this gap is failure of the common prior assumption specifically, rather than bounded rationality, failures of common knowledge, or purely non-informational trades like liquidity/hedging needs. Don't just list all the usual suspects -- actually defend common-prior violation as the primary driver and explain why the other standard explanations are less satisfying in comparison.
