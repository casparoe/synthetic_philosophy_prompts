So I'm a philosophy PhD student TAing a decision theory seminar this term, and I'm kind of done reaching for Newcomb's boxes as the discussion-opener every week. Can you write me a dialogue between two friends who hitchhiked out of the desert together, having promised the driver -- who's weirdly good at telling when people are lying -- that they'd pay her once they got to town, and are now on the sidewalk arguing about wether to actually go back and do it. One's a hardcore causal decision theorist: the ride already happened, nothing they do now can reach back and cause her to have picked them up, so don't pay. The other thinks that's exactly the reasoning that gets you left for dead, and wants to defend a kind of rational irrationality -- that the right policy can require doing, right now, what looks locally irrational, since rationality gets scored at the level of the disposition you carried into the car, not the isolated choice once you're safe. I want genuine back-and-forth, not two monologues -- the CDT friend has to actually deal with "you'd be dead," not just repeat "the past is fixed," and vice versa -- and neither should be a strawman. Maybe 500 words is plenty.
