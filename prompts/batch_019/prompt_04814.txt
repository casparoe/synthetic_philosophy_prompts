So I somehow ended up on my kid's school district's curriculum review committee (pretty sure I was the only parent who actually replied to the sign-up email). We've got a case in front of us that's way more annoying than I expected going in. A publisher wants approval for a supplementary insert to go with the 10th grade bio textbook's evolution unit, and buried in one of the sidebars is a quote from Karl Popper -- the falsifiability guy -- where he apparently calls Darwinism a "metaphysical research programme" instead of a properly testable scientific theory. The insert uses that line to argue students should be told evolution doesn't fully clear the bar of real science, and that "other explanations for biological complexity" deserve equal time (nobody actually types the words "intelligent design" but come on, we all know what this is).

Here's the part thats actually splitting the committee though: I looked into it and it's not a fabricated quote, Popper really did say something like that, and his whole complaint was basically that "survival of the fittest" is dangerously close to a tautology -- "those that survive, survive." What's more, I guess he half walked it back years later in some other lecture, saying he'd changed his mind about whether natural selection counts as testable after all. So now half the committee is like "it's a real quote from a genuinely famous philosopher of science, we can't just wave it off as creationism," and the other half is like "he recanted, so quoting the old version is just misleading, easy reject" -- and I don't think either side has actually sat with why Popper thought this in the first place, or whether the recantation actually makes the original worry go away.

Could you write the memo I need to bring to the full board next Thursday? I need it to land on an actual recommendation -- approve, reject, or approve with specific rewrites to the sidebar -- not a wishy washy "here are some considerations," and it should come with a timeline for getting this resolved. Whatever side loses needs to come away feeling like we took there strongest argument seriously, not like we just reached for "separation of church and state" and called it a day.
