so i'm on my city's charter review commision and i kind of started a whole fight last month by bringing up that Simone Weil essay on abolishing political parties -- the one from the 1940s, "On the Abolition of All Political Parties." i think i first ran into it years ago via some rationalist-adjacent blog and it just stuck with me. anyway, we're voting next month on a proposal to strip party labels off the ballot for city council and mayor races, and to ban candidates from running as part of an organized slate or ticket. half the commission is now into it because of Weil's point that a party's real function is manufacturing collective passion and policing what its members are allowed to think, with the party's own growth being basically its only real, unstated goal regardless of platform. the other half thinks that's naive -- labels exist because most voters don't have time to vet seven city council candidates, and if you ban the label you don't kill the faction, you just push it into dark money backed "independents" and backroom machines that are even less accountable than an actual party.

i have to circulate a short decision memo before the vote. can you write it? give an actual recommendation (keep labels, drop them, some disclosure based middle ground, whatever you think is right), make the reasoning genuinely engage with what Weil was worried about instead of a strawman version of "parties bad," and be honest about the strongest argument for whichever side you don't land on.
