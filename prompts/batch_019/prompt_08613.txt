I was a field biologist for 40 years, and I always trusted my notebooks—cross-referenced by site and date—more than my own memory. I'd flip through years of entries constantly while working out a problem, so the thinking felt like it was happening between me and the notebooks, not just in my head. Now that I'm retired and reading alot of philosophy for fun, something my grandson said won't leave me alone: he's doing computer science, and he told me all that notebook-flipping isn't really "knowing" something, its just good filing—my real knowledge, he says, is only whatever I can recall without looking anything up. That doesn't sit right with me, but I can't say why. Was he wrong, or is there a real sense in which the notebooks were part of my memory itself, not just a record of it?
