So this happened in my rationalist-adjacent reading group — we've been on a whole free-will kick ever since someone dragged up Eliezer's old sequence posts on determinism and responsibility, and this month's assigned reading was the SEP entry on Frankfurt-style cases. After the meetup, two of the regulars, Dev and Kate, kept going in the group chat for like two days and I honestly can't tell who's winning the arguement, so I want someone smarter than both of us to actually adjudicate it move by move instead of just declaring a winner.

It went like this. (1) Dev opened with the classic setup: Black has a device that will force Jones to shoot a senator, but only if Jones shows some sign of wavering first; Jones decides to shoot entirely on his own, so the device never has to fire. Dev's claim: Jones is exactly as blameworthy as he'd be with no device at all, even though, because of the device, he literally couldnt have done otherwise — so "you're only responsible for what you could've avoided doing" (the principle of alternate possibilities, PAP) is false. (2) Kate said not so fast, there's still a flicker of freedom in there: Jones could at least have produced the wavering sign the device is watching for, even if everything downstream of that sign was forced. That flicker is itself a genuine alternative possibility, so PAP survives, just in a much weaker form. (3) Dev's comeback was to strip the flicker out entirely: rig the case so the very same brain process that produces the "sign" also deterministically produces the decision itself, with no gap anywhere for a branch. No flicker left, and Jones still looks fully responsible. (4) Kate replied that this just helps itself to determinism — if the process really is deterministic, then "Jones is still responsible" is exactly what a compatibilist already believed and an incompatibilist already denied, so the souped-up case isn't proving anything new, it's just assuming one side of the underlying fight. And if the process isn't strictly deterministic after all, some real slack remains and PAP is back. Either way, no new ground gained. (5) Dev said Kate's dilemma doesn't actually save PAP, because the case doesn't need the sign to be deterministic — it only needs whatever flicker is left to be too thin and random to be the kind of thing responsibility could ever track, more like a coin flip somewhere in Jones's skull than an exercise of agency. Kate's last word (before I got dropped from the thread) was that "too thin and random to count" is doing all the real work in that move, and nobody's ever defined it except as "whatever's left once my argument goes through," which looks a lot like smuggling the conclusion back in.

Could you go through this move by move, (1) through (5), and say who had the better of it at each individual step rather than just who won overall? I'm especially curious whether Dev's move at (5) is a legitimate refinement of the case or a sneaky redefinition of what counts as a "real" alternative, and whether Kate's closing complaint about "robust"-type notions being underspecified actually lands, or is itself too quick given that compatibilists have presumably tried to answer exactly this. Flag it if either of them missed some obvious move, and close with a short paragraph on where this leaves PAP overall.
