Over the past month I have been trading actively on a couple of prediction-market platforms, and I recently learned firsthand what it means to be "Dutch booked": another trader noticed that my quoted prices across three correlated markets tracking the same election were not jointly coherent, and assembled a combination of positions that left me guaranteed to lose money regardless of which candidate ultimately won. Reading up on the philosophical pedigree of this argument afterward, I found the basic proof straightforward enough — incoherent credences admit a sure-loss book, coherent ones provably do not (the converse Dutch book theorem). What I have not been able to pin down is the further move the argument is usually taken to license: the inference from "these credences can be exploited into a guaranteed loss" to "these credences are thereby irrational," as an epistemic rather than merely a prudential verdict. Could you walk me through how that inference is supposed to go — what the attempt to "depragmatize" the argument actually looks like — and whether it survives the objection that the whole setup already presupposes that my fair betting price for a proposition simply is my credence in it?
