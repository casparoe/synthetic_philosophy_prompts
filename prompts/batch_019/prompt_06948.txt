For the last couple of years my younger brother has caused a lot of trouble because of a drinking problem: missed family events, money borrowed and never paid back, a couple of ugly scenes at holidays. For most of that time I was just plain angry with him, the ordinary way you're angry with someone who keeps letting you down, and I never thought there was anything complicated about that.

This year he finally went through a proper treatment program, and his counselor has been telling the family, in effect, that a lot of what he did is better understood as symptoms of a disease than as choices, and that we'll all do better if we stop taking his past behavior personally and start relating to him the way you'd relate to someone managing a chronic illness — focusing on what keeps him well rather than on what he "owes" anyone for the past. My mother has taken to this completely and has more or less stopped being angry at him at all. I find I can't quite follow her there, and I've been trying to work out why, because it isn't that I think the diagnosis is fake, or that I secretly want to keep punishing him. It's more that when I picture fully adopting her attitude, my brother stops being someone I could be hurt by, or disappointed in, or one day forgive, and turns into something more like a project the family manages — and that trade feels like too big a price for the peace it buys, even though I can't really defend staying angry either.

I looked around a bit for how this kind of thing is normally discussed and mostly found arguments about whether free will can survive in a universe of causes and effects, which isn't really my question — I don't especially care about the metaphysics. What I want to know is narrower: is there a real difference between excusing particular things my brother has done and no longer relating to him, generally, as someone who owes people anything at all, and if there is a difference, does a medical explanation like his actually tell us which of those two responses is the right one, or is there some further question — about him, or about us — that the diagnosis just doesn't touch? I would rather have that worked through properly than be told, yet again, that everyone deserves compassion.
