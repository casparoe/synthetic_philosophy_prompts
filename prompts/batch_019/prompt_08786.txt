Part of what draws me to him, I think, is that he was himself a working physician and astronomer, so unlike a lot of religious writers he's constantly hunting for a naturalistic, functional rationale behind the commandments instead of just "because God said so" -- which scratches basically the same itch science did for me.

Right now I'm stuck on his treatment of animal sacrifice. In Book III of the Guide he argues the whole korbanot system was essentially a concession: the Israelites were so steeped in the sacrificial cults around them that God couldn't just cut them off cold turkey, so he let the practice continue but redirected it toward Himself and loaded it up with restrictions (one Temple, one priestly line, and so on), as a kind of weaning-off strategy whose eventual endpoint is pure prayer with no slaughter involved at all. Then I went and read the bits in the Mishneh Torah on the laws of kings and of the Temple, where he says quite plainly that sacrifice resumes once the Messiah rebuilds the Temple -- which struck me as odd, since that's an strange thing to lock in as eternal law if the whole point of the practice was supposed to be training wheels everyone eventually outgrows. My study partner (a rabbi, very patient with my dumb questions) tells me this "contradiction" is a known sore spot in Maimonides scholarship and not something I stumbled onto myself, which was reassuring.

Anyway, here's what I can't stop turning over: if you dropped Maimonides into the present day and showed him the modern animal-welfare movement -- factory farming exposes, the ethical case for veganism, lab-grown meat, the whole works -- how do you think he'd actually process it, and how would he try to fit it into his own framework? Would his own "gradual weaning" logic basically commit him to treating modern vegetarianism/veganism as just the next rung on a ladder he already described (maybe even a rung past meat-eating generally, not only sacrifice), or would he say halakha fixes a hard endpoint at Temple service and none of this modern stuff is really his problem? There's also some detail I half-remember from his medical writings about steering patients away from heavy red meat toward lighter fare for health reasons, which feels like it should matter here somehow, though I could easily be misremembering the specifics.
