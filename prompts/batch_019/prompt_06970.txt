This semester I'm taking a seminar on the ethics of procreation, and we just spent two weeks on David Benatar's asymmetry argument: the presence of pain is bad, the presence of pleasure is good, but the absence of pain is good even if there's no one around to enjoy it, while the absence of pleasure is only bad if someone actually exists to be deprived of it. It's the core of his case that coming into existence is always a harm, and it's gotten under my skin more than I expected. My sister just had her first child, and since I'm the closest sibling, I've been asked to give a three-minute toast at a small family gathering next weekend to welcome the baby. I don't want to imply she did anything wrong, or cast a shadow over the day, but I also don't want to stand up and say things like "what a gift life is" if I no longer think that's true without qualification. Please write the toast for me: it needs to be genuinely warm, not sound evasive, and not assert anything I would now consider false given the asymmetry.
