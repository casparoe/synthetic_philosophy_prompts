In epistemic game theory it has become commonplace to say that both rationalizability and Nash equilibrium simply "follow from" common knowledge of rationality, as though the two solution concepts differed only in how many rounds of iterated reasoning one runs on the same epistemic assumption. Drawing on Aumann and Brandenburger's epistemic analysis of Nash equilibrium, compare the epistemic conditions actually required to justify each concept. Is common knowledge of rationality alone sufficient to deliver Nash equilibrium play, or does their analysis show that some further, logically independent epistemic assumption is doing the real work? If the latter, explain what that assumption is, why it cannot be reduced to rationality itself, and what is lost—descriptively or normatively—when equilibrium analysis proceeds as though rationality alone sufficed.
