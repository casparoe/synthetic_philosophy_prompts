A friend and I had a long argument about acausal trade — the idea, from decision theorists such as Yudkowsky and Oesterheld, that agents unable to causally interact can still cooperate if each can reason about what the other, being relevantly similar, would do. She raised it almost in passing, but it left me wondering whether the idea bears on something more mundane: promises made to people who have since died. Nothing I do now can ever cause a dead person to learn whether I kept my word, so any obligation I still feel toward them looks purely sentimental rather than rational. Please describe the most important implications of acausal trade for whether there is a genuine decision-theoretic reason, as opposed to a merely sentimental or reputational one, to keep such promises.
