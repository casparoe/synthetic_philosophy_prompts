In my school's philosophy club, we recently discussed Searle's Chinese Room argument, and I later came across a newer argument aimed specifically at today's language models: Emily Bender and Alexander Koller's "octopus test," in which a highly intelligent octopus intercepts an undersea telegraph cable linking two people, learns to produce fluent replies purely from the statistical patterns of their conversation, and then fails as soon as it is asked to respond to something that depends on real-world knowledge it was never given. Please compare these two arguments against machine understanding. Searle's argument is famously met with the Systems Reply, which grants that the man inside the room does not understand Chinese but insists that the room-plus-man system as a whole does. Does an equivalent systems reply succeed against the octopus test, or is Bender and Koller's thought experiment constructed specifically to block that kind of response?
