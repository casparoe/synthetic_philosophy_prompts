Going through my late grandfather's papers last month, I learned that he had served as a young conscript in the East German Grenztruppen in the early 1970s, and that he was among the border guards investigated — though never charged — in the Mauerschützen trials of the 1990s. It has sent me back to the Hart–Fuller exchange in a way that feels far less academic than it did in law school. Could you write a dialogue between a committed Hartian positivist and a Fullerian natural lawyer who disagree specifically about whether a guard like him could be justly convicted of manslaughter for shooting someone trying to cross, given that the Border Act's published text called for proportionate use of force while the actual shoot-to-kill practice rested on unpublished internal orders? I'd like the positivist to press the objection Hart raised against how the German courts handled the Grudge Informer case — that voiding a legal defense after the fact is retroactive punishment wearing a disguise — and the natural lawyer to push back using both Radbruch's formula and that gap between the statute's text and the regime's actual practice. Have the two of them work out, by the end, whether they really disagree about the guard's guilt, or only about how to justify the same verdict.
