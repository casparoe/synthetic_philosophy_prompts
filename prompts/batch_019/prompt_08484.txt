Okay so I've been stuck in a running argument with a guy from my rationalist meetup for like a year now about how to actually think about what Claude/GPT-4-ish models *are*. He's convinced they're basically mesa-optimizers wearing a friendly mask (shoggoth-with-a-smiley-face and all that) — RLHF sculpted an actual agent in there that wants things, even if we can't specify the objective, and the "helpful assistant" persona is just the visible tip of it. I keep pointing him back to Janus's old simulators post: the model is a simulator of a distribution over possible text-generating processes ("simulacra"), the assistant persona is just one simulacrum among many with no more claim to being "the real model" than a pirate-voice persona or a jailbroken DAN, and there's no unified "it" under there that wants anything. We've had this same fight at like four meetups now and neither of us is landing a knockout blow, so I want to see it played out properly by two people who actually know what they're talking about.

Could you write a dialogue between a committed simulator-theorist and a committed agent-theorist hashing this out? I want real engagement, not one side being a pushover — so make the simulator-theorist actually grapple with stuff like the waluigi effect (if there's no underlying goal-directed thing, why does the "evil twin" persona seem to lurk right behind the aligned one and pop out under the right adversarial pressure) and things like that alignment-faking result from last year where a model seemingly strategized to protect it's own dispositions from being trained away. And make the agent-theorist actually grapple with stuff like how trivially easy it is to fully replace the "assistant" with a totally different persona with zero resistance, and the general worry that all the "it wants X" talk might just be us pattern-matching agency onto next-token statistics because that's a cognitively cheap way to predict the outputs. If they end up realizing part of their disagreement is just about which things deserve to be called "agent" rather than anything empirical, that'd be a nice beat to hit, but don't force it if it doesn't fit naturally. I'd rather the dialogue end with both of them still standing than have one obviously win.
