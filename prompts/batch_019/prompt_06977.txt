I'm a novelist, and the book I'm currently drafting includes a chapter written as the transcript of a philosophy podcast. In it, a journalist character interviews a Spinoza scholar who insists that the unfinished Political Treatise, and not the more celebrated Theological-Political Treatise, contains Spinoza's real case for democracy. Whenever pressed, this scholar likes to say, "but he never got to finish that chapter," and lets the manuscript's incompleteness excuse whatever comes next. I'd like eight to ten questions for the journalist to ask, sequenced so that this deflection becomes progressively harder to use. Please include at least one question on how the equation of natural right with power is supposed to make democracy the most "absolute" of the three regimes rather than merely the most egalitarian one; one on how the claim that no one can really transfer their judgment to a sovereign is meant to make a democracy more stable than a Hobbesian commonwealth, and not just freer; and one that turns the scholar's own deflection back on him by noting that the exclusion of women from citizenship is among the last things Spinoza did manage to write before the manuscript breaks off. Close with a question asking what, short of a completed chapter, could possibly count as evidence against this scholar's reading of Spinoza.
