A situation in my own family has been nagging at me — the kind of puzzle I usually meet only in the abstract, not this close to home — and I'd welcome an outside view. My mother often says, with what seems like real feeling, that she is lonely and wishes my brother and I visited more. Yet whenever we do visit, she grows tense almost as soon as we arrive, criticizes small unrelated details of our lives, and only relaxes once we've left — and this has been the pattern for years, not just an occasional bad visit. My brother treats the behavior as decisive: he thinks we should stop planning visits around what she says and instead calibrate them to what her conduct shows she actually wants, which by his reading is less contact, not more. That inference strikes me as too quick, though I have trouble saying exactly what's wrong with it. Whose reading of the situation is more defensible? And, more generally, how should one weigh what a person says against what their behavior seems to show, when the two pull apart?
