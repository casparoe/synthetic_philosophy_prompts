I'm writing a piece on how Newton's Principia is often credited with giving the Enlightenment its "clockmaker God" -- a deity who designs a perfectly self-sufficient, law-governed cosmos and then withdraws, leaving it to run on its own. Since Newton himself supplied the physics behind that deist picture, I'm having trouble explaining a wrinkle for the piece: he spent his later years insisting that God still had to step in periodically to correct the planets' orbits. Is that best read as an inconsistency between his physics and his own theology, or did he simply not trust his own laws of motion to hold up indefinitely?
