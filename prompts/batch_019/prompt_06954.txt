I'm a backend engineer, and a few months ago I fell down a Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy rabbit hole on the metaphysics of dispositions: C. B. Martin's "electro-fink" counterexample, in which a device senses that a wire is about to be touched and flips it from dead to live (or live to dead) at the instant of contact, David Lewis's attempt to repair the simple counterfactual analysis of dispositions in light of it, and the companion "masking" cases, where a disposition survives completely intact but something external stops it from ever manifesting -- a fragile glass that comes through a drop unbroken only because it's wrapped in packing foam. Please describe the most important implications of the finks/masks distinction for how my team should design and interpret these tests -- specifically, how to tell whether a passing result means the underlying fragility or vulnerability is genuinely gone rather than merely intercepted, and what that should change about which risks we're actually entitled to sign off on as closed.
