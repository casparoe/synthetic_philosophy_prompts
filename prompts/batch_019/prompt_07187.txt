I co-host a podcast in the Overcoming Bias/LessWrong tradition, and next month's guest is a writer known for defending a strong, futarchy-style trust in prediction markets: the view that persistent public disagreement among well-informed people is itself evidence that someone is being irrational, and that betting markets are our best real-world stand-in for ideally rational, common-prior agreement. Whenever someone points out how much trading volume the no-trade theorems say shouldn't exist among rational traders with common priors and no hedging motive, he reaches for some version of 'that's just noise traders and hedgers -- the informed money still sets the price.'

Draft eight to ten interview questions that make that line progressively harder to reach for. Include at least one question on whether the common-prior assumption behind Aumann's agreement theorem is doing more work than he admits, since two people can start from different priors for principled reasons without either being irrational; one asking him to reconcile his trust in prices with the Grossman-Stiglitz point that fully efficient prices would remove the incentive to gather the information they're supposed to reflect; and one, saved for near the end, asking what specific pattern of prices or trades -- a bubble, a persistent mispricing -- he would accept as evidence against relying on markets this way. For each question, add a short follow-up keyed to the reply he's most likely to give.
