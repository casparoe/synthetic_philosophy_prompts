hey, so I'm a political theory PhD student (poli sci, not philosophy, though obviously adjacent) working on a dissertation chapter about Nozick's entitlement theory, and I've gotten stuck on the Locke passage his "Lockean proviso" is supposedly lifted from. Here's the bit I mean, from the Second Treatise, ch. 5, roughly §§27/31/33:

"...no Man but he can have a right to what that is once joyned to, at least where there is enough, and as good left in common for others." [§27]

"As much as any one can make use of to any advantage of Life before it spoils, so much he may by his labour fix a Property in: whatever is beyond this, is more than his share, and belongs to others. Nothing was made by God for Man to spoil or destroy." [§31]

"Nor was this appropriation of any parcel of Land... any prejudice to any other Man, since there was still enough, and as good left, and more than the yet unprovided could use... Nobody could think himself injur'd by the drinking of another Man, though he took a good draught, who had a whole River of the same Water left him to quench his thirst." [§33]

So Nozick glosses "the Lockean proviso" as basically a counterfactual non-worsening test: appropriating something is fine as long as no one ends up worse off than if the thing had just stayed unowned. But reading Locke's actual sentences, there seem to be two seperate limits running together here -- a spoilage limit and a sufficiency ("enough and as good") limit -- and neither one, just reading it cold, is obviously the same thing as a non-worsening test.

Can you help me pull apart (1) whether spoilage and sufficiency are doing independent work in Locke's own reasoning here, or whether one is really just a special case of the other, and (2) whether Nozick's proviso is a faithful reconstruction of Locke's sufficiency condition, or a substantially weaker requirement smuggled in under the same name -- and if it's the latter, exactly where the substitution happens. I don't want the standard "Nozick relaxes Locke's proviso" one-liner, I want the actual mechanics of the move.
