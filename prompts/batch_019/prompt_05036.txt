I've been reporting on a fight over a big AI data center a developer wants to build just outside a small town near me. The town's letting residents vote on whether to approve it, but only people on the official voter rolls inside town limits get a ballot. Problem is, the aquifer that data center would be pulling millions of gallons a day from also feeds wells in a bunch of unincorporated neighborhoods just over that line, plus a farm co-op a few miles downstream — none of who get any say, even though it's their water on the line too. When I asked the town clerk about it, she said you can't let every person who might be affected vote in someone else's election or nothing would ever get decided, which, ok, fair, but the line still feels totally arbitrary. Is there an actual principled way to figure out who should get a vote in a situation like this, or does it just come down to whoever happens to be holding the pen when the lines get drawn?
