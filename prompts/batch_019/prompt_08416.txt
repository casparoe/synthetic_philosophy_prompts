For my seminar on Rousseau and the Enlightenment, I've been working through the *Lettre à d'Alembert sur les spectacles* (1758), in particular the passage where he says the actor's whole talent lies in counterfeiting passions he does not feel, and the later one where he contrasts this with the outdoor civic festivals he wants Geneva to keep instead of a theater—gatherings where citizens are simultaneously performers and audience for one another, which he thought fostered mutual attachment rather than vanity. I can't stop wondering how he would size up today's social media influencer economy. In one sense it looks like his festival ideal realized at scale, since nobody needs a script and anyone scrolling can also post; in practice, though, attention concentrates on a handful of accounts, and the "authenticity" on display is curated, edited, and monetized in ways that seem to reproduce exactly the vice he pinned on actors. Reasoning strictly from the arguments Rousseau actually makes in the Lettre, would he count influencer culture as closer to his festival ideal or to the theater he opposed, and what would settle that for him?
