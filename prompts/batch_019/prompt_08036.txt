Ok, this is gonna sound niche, but I've been on a real rationalist-blog kick lately (worked my way through a big chunk of the old Overcoming Bias archive last month), and it's gotten me kind of stuck on something that happens with my dad every single time we talk politics. We read a lot of the same stuff — I actually got him hooked on a couple of the news sources I use — we've sat through the same debates and coverage for like fifteen years now, and I genuinely don't think either of us is dumb or arguing in bad faith. But we've never once actually converged on anything, even stuff where the facts are pretty well-established at this point. It's basically a running joke in our family at this point.

There's an idea that keeps rattling around in my head from all that reading: that two people who trust each others reasoning and are looking at the same evidence shouldn't really be able to stay in genuine disagreement — like, the mere fact that the other person still disagrees with you after hearing your whole case is itself supposed to be evidence you update on, and if you both keep doing that you're supposed to eventually land in the same place. Obviously that is not what happens with me and my dad, or with basically any two people arguing about anything, ever. So what's actually going on here — is that idea just wrong, or too idealized to say anything about real disagreements, are me and my dad secretly being irrational without noticing, or is there some other piece of the puzzle that "rational people shouldn't be able to agree to disagree" is leaving out? Would love an actual answer instead of the usual friend-group line that "politics just makes everyone stupid," which has never felt like its really engaging with what's weird about this.
