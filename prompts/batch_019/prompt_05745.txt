Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I've spent the last couple of years reading my way through modal metaphysics, and I think I've found a place where physics has already settled a long-standing philosophical debate. David Lewis's modal realism holds that every possible world is fully concrete and real, with the inhabitants of each world correctly calling their own world "actual." The many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics seems to establish exactly this: every possible outcome of every measurement is physically realized in some branch of the universal wavefunction. Doesn't this mean physics has effectively vindicated Lewis's metaphysics, so that the counterpart of me who accepted the job offer I turned down in 1987 is, in Lewis's own sense, really out there living that life?
