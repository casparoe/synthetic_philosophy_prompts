Oskar recently admitted that for three weeks he had quietly done less than his share, leaving Renata to absorb the extra work, without ever telling her. (2) Oskar replied that this proves too much: absent any explicit agreement about frequency, there was nothing to violate beyond ordinary courtesy, and a gradual loss of enthusiasm for a hobby isn't the kind of thing that needs anyone's permission or announcement.

What made the arrangement a joint activity in the first place, rather than two people gardening on separate tracks, was that each of them adjusted their own effort based on what they could count on the other doing. (4) Oskar answered that this shows at most that he should have stayed responsive while he was still gardening, not that he owed her notice before cutting back or stopping. Needing to coordinate while you're in something together doesn't imply a standing duty to remain in it, or to announce your exit — and that is the very obligation Renata still needs to establish.
