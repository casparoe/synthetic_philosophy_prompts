I'm taking a seminar on philosophy of language this term, and this week's unit covered hybrid theories of slurs on which a slur's descriptive content picks out the same group as its neutral counterpart, while its derogatory force is carried as a conventional implicature of the sort Potts describes for constructions like appositives or the connective "but." We also went over Grice's classic diagnostic that conversational implicatures, unlike what is said, can be cancelled without contradiction -- e.g., "some of the students, in fact all of them, passed." It seems to follow that if a slur's offensiveness is likewise merely implicated rather than asserted, a speaker should be able to cancel it just by adding a rider like "but I don't mean anything derogatory by that word" -- yet such riders are treated as completely ineffective at neutralizing the offense. Doesn't this failure of cancellability show that the conventional-implicature analysis makes the wrong prediction, and that the derogatory content must instead be part of the slur's asserted, truth-conditional meaning?
