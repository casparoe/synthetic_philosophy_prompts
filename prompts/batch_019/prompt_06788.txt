I write historical fiction, and my most recent book earned a far larger advance than any of my earlier ones. My agent and my accountant are both urging me to put a substantial share of it into advertising for my backlist, since our numbers show a clearly positive expected return on every dollar spent, even once failed campaigns are factored in. A friend of mine who spent years playing poker professionally warns me that however good the expected return looks, I should never commit that large a fraction of my current earnings to any single round of bets, and that a sensible amount to risk should scale with whatever I actually have at the time rather than being fixed in advance. I can't quite follow her reasoning, since a positive expected return seems like a positive expected return no matter how it's sized, but her instincts have generally proven sound. Who has the better grip on this, and how should I really think about the size of these bets across a whole career of releases, rather than one book at a time?
