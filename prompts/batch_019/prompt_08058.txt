I'm a backend engineer, and a fair amount of my work involves formally verified code, using tools like Coq and Isabelle. A friend recently sent me an article about how, back in 2013, two computer scientists (I believe one of them was named Benzmüller, though I may be misremembering the name) took Kurt Gödel's modal ontological argument for the existence of God and ran it through an automated higher-order theorem prover. As I understand it, the argument defines a notion of a "positive property," stipulates that a being possessing every positive property — a "God-like" being — is itself positive, and from a small set of axioms along these lines derives that such a being must exist necessarily, provided the concept is even logically possible. The central claim of the piece was that the theorem prover confirmed the derivation was airtight, with no hidden gaps or fallacies, and I recall the surrounding coverage running with lines like "scientists prove God exists."

This is the point that has continued to puzzle me. In my day-to-day work, once the proof kernel accepts a derivation, that's the end of the argument — no one goes back and insists the four-color theorem or the Kepler conjecture might still be false just because it "feels" doubtful; the checker either validates the chain of inferences or it doesn't, and there is no room left for human disagreement once verification succeeds. So if a machine did the same thing for Gödel's proof, mechanically confirming every step, it seems like it should settle the question of God's existence with the same finality as any other machine-checked theorem, at least for anyone who accepts classical logic in the first place. I mentioned this to a friend who is finishing a philosophy PhD, and she laughed, said that isn't really what the result shows, and had to leave before she could explain why. So I'm hoping you can: is there something about this particular case that makes machine verification less conclusive than it would be for an ordinary mathematical theorem, and if so, what exactly did the theorem prover establish, if not that the conclusion is true?
