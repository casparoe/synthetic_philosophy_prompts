I'm a student member of my high school's Honor Council, which hears academic-integrity cases and votes on findings. We're deadlocked on a case, and I'd like a memo to circulate before we reconvene next Tuesday.

A classmate's English essay was flagged by our school's AI-writing detector as 71% likely to be AI-generated. The council members who read it closely didn't notice anything that struck them as templated, his handwritten in-class drafts are stylistically consistent with it, and he denies using AI. Our handbook says a flag "shall be weighed alongside all other available evidence," but doesn't say how heavily. Half the council wants to treat the flag as close to decisive: we were told when the school adopted the tool that trained readers are barely better than chance at spotting AI prose by eye, while the detector's own accuracy is much higher, and nobody on the council can name a specific flaw in its read of this essay -- only their own impression that it seems genuine. The other half thinks our impression should outweigh a general accuracy statistic, partly because the detector can't explain itself case by case and is known to misfire more often on some writing styles than others, including that of non-native English speakers. One teacher summed up her own position bluntly: she doesn't believe the essay is AI-written, but thinks the council should rule as though it is, since the detector knows better than she does.

Please draft the memo I'll circulate. It should recommend a specific finding for this case and a standard the council can reuse for future flags, and it should take the strongest version of the "defer to the detector" position seriously rather than dismissing it.
