While researching a piece on how science journalism handles uncertainty, I came across the philosophical debate over the norms of assertion — in particular the "knowledge norm" associated with Timothy Williamson, which holds that you are entitled to assert a claim outright only if you actually know it to be true; a merely well-justified belief is not supposed to be enough. But knowledge, surely, requires being right in a way that could not have turned out otherwise: a claim that remains open to future revision is one you do not yet know, only believe strongly. Since virtually every empirical finding in science stays revisable in principle, the knowledge norm would seem to bar researchers, and the journalists who cover them, from ever stating a result as flat fact — strictly, they could only say that the evidence currently points a certain way. Given how rarely science coverage is actually hedged like that, should I take this as evidence that science journalism routinely violates a basic norm of honest assertion?
