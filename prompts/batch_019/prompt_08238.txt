A friend of mine and I spent much of a dinner last week arguing about whether Rawls's veil of ignorance actually earns him the difference principle, or whether the very same device just as easily supports something like average utilitarianism instead. My friend, who has been reading Harsanyi for a decision theory seminar, pointed out that if you set up essentially the same thought experiment Rawls does — rational parties choosing principles for their society without knowing which position in it they will occupy — but have those parties reason the way economists usually model choice under uncertainty, that is, by maximizing expected utility while treating every social position as equally likely, you seem to land on maximizing average welfare rather than on anything resembling the difference principle. Since Rawls's original position looks, on the surface, very close to this setup, I felt there had to be a real, substantive reason he steers his parties toward maximin reasoning instead of expected-utility reasoning, rather than this being a kind of hidden thumb on the scale, but I couldn't reconstruct that reason precisely enough to actually convince my friend at the table.

Could you compare the two arguments in some depth: Rawls's route from the veil of ignorance to the difference principle via maximin reasoning, and Harsanyi's route from a similarly structured veil of ignorance to average utilitarianism via expected-utility reasoning? I'm especially interested in exactly where the two constructions part ways — whether the crux is a disagreement about what parties behind the veil are entitled to assume about probabilities, a disagreement about which decision rule is rational for a choice of this magnitude and irreversibility, a disagreement about how to treat goods like self-respect and basic liberty that may not trade off smoothly against other advantages, or some further consideration I'm not carving out correctly. If you have a view on which side handles the veil-of-ignorance device more consistently on its own terms, I'd like to hear it, and why.
