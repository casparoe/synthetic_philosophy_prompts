I first encountered van Fraassen's Reflection Principle in a rationalist-community discussion of whether a self-modifying agent should defer to the credences of its future self, where it was glossed as a cousin of David Lewis's Principal Principle: both fit the schema Cr(A | X = r) = r, with X being your own future credence in one case and the objective chance in the other. Compare the two principles directly, weighing the "admissibility" restriction Lewis places on any additional evidence against the restriction van Fraassen needs — namely, that the agent not anticipate memory loss, manipulation, or a lapse into irrationality between now and then — and judge which restriction is doing more principled work rather than simply patching over known counterexamples. Then take a position, and defend it: is Reflection best understood as a special case of some suitably generalized Principal Principle, or are the two independent principles that merely happen to share a common form?
