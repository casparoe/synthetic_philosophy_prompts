I'm interviewing a decision theorist for our department's brown-bag next week -- she's one of the people defending 'surrogate goals' as a way to defuse acausal threats (threats from agents you can't causally interact with but who can still predict what you'll do). The idea is you publically commit to treat some arbitrary variable as though it were part of your real values, so a threatener who only wants concessions is just as happy threatening the decoy as the real thing, and you're actual goals never get touched. Her go-to move when pressed is that it doesn't matter if the threatener sees through the substitution, since that doesn't change what they'd rationally do -- so I want a run of 7-8 questions that make that move progressively harder to lean on. Give me at least one on what happens once the threatener terminally values your distress rather than just wanting concessions (which breaks the indifference the scheme relies on), one on how the commitment is even supposed to be credible with no causal channel and no track record to point to, and one on whether protecting a designated decoy just teaches every other agent to manufacture threats against whatever you seem to care about next -- then end on a question about what she'd accept as evidence the whole strategy fails, with a quick follow-up on each keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
