Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have taken up philosophy in earnest, and one episode in Karl Popper's work continues to puzzle me: his contention that natural selection, expressed as "survival of the fittest," risks being little more than a tautology, which led him to describe Darwinism as a metaphysical research programme rather than a strictly testable theory — a position he later partly recanted. Could you explain precisely what the tautology objection amounts to, why supplying an independent, non-circular measure of fitness proved so difficult, and what exactly changed in Popper's later view? I would also be curious whether the biologists who objected to him were genuinely engaging with this specific worry, or simply talking past it.
