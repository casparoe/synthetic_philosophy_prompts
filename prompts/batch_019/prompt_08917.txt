So I've been going down a rabbit hole reading Newton's secret theology stuff lately (the anti-trinity writings he never published in his lifetime). I found a transcription of a manuscript from around the 1710s-20s that people call the "Twelve Articles on God and Christ" (Keynes MS 8, if that means anything to you) — basically Newton laying out his own private creed. Article 5 is the one thats got me stuck. I cleaned up the weird insertion marks from the transcription but kept his spelling:

"The father is immoveable, no place being capable of becoming emptier or fuller of him then it is by the eternal necessity of nature: all other beings are moveable from place to place."

I get that he's saying God doesn't move around in space the way people or angels (or even Christ) do. What I don't get is why that's supposed to prove Christ isn't fully God — like why would being "moveable from place to place" have anything to do with your theological status? Feels like he's smuggling in something from his physics, maybe the absolute space stuff from the Principia, to do work in a totally different argument about who counts as God. Can someone unpack the actual logic he's running here, and why immovability specifically was such a big deal for him as an anti-trinitarian move?
