I'm a PhD student writing a dissertation chapter on hinge epistemology, and I've run into what looks like an internal tension in *On Certainty* itself, rather than just a disagreement between its interpreters. In §§341–343 Wittgenstein writes:

"That is to say, the questions that we raise and our doubts depend on the fact that some propositions are exempt from doubt, are as it were like hinges on which those turn. That is to say, it belongs to the logic of our scientific investigations that certain things are indeed not doubted. But it isn't that the situation is like this: we just can't investigate everything, and for that reason we are forced to rest content with assumption. If I want the door to turn, the hinges must stay put."

Here the bedrock items are still called "propositions," merely exempted from doubt — which is presumably why a reading like Crispin Wright's, on which hinges are things we are rationally entitled to accept without evidence, can treat them as truth-apt at all. But earlier, at §204, he writes:

"Giving grounds, however, justifying the evidence, comes to an end; — but the end is not certain propositions striking us immediately as true, i.e., it is not a kind of seeing on our part; it is our acting which lies at the bottom of the language-game."

That sounds much closer to Danièle Moyal-Sharrock's claim that hinges are not beliefs or proposition-shaped states at all, but something closer to practical bedrock — a matter of acting a certain way rather than holding anything true.

Can §204 and §§341–343 be read as expressing one stable conception of what a hinge is, or does *On Certainty* just leave the propositional and the practice-based pictures of the bedrock unreconciled? If there's a way of reading "proposition" in §341 that already builds in the non-doxastic, practical character §204 describes, I'd like to see how it accommodates Wittgenstein's own use of ordinary propositional-attitude vocabulary ("exempt from doubt," "assumption") for the hinges. I'm less interested in the more familiar question of whether hinges count as known than in this narrower one about whether they're even the right kind of thing to be believed in the first place.
