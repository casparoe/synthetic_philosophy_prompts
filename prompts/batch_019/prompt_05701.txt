Been chewing on this since a piece I filed on chatbots last week. Dennett's whole intentional stance move is that its fine to say a thermostat "believes" the room is cold, or that an LLM "believes" Paris is the capital of France, because treating a system as if it has beliefs is just the most useful way of predicting what it'll do next - doesn't matter what's "really" going on inside. But Brentano's the guy who put intentionality on the map to begin with, and for him being genuinely directed at an object was THE mark of the mental, not a convenient fiction for prediction. So if you sat Brentano down in front of an LLM confidently hallucinating a fake quote and then revising itself once corrected, would he sign off on Dennett's move and say sure, thats intentionality - or would he call it a category error, no real "directedness" happening at all? And does it matter that Brentano himself got cold feet late in his career about his own intentional-inexistence idea?
