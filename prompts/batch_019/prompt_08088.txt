So this went down in my rationalist discord server a couple weeks ago and I haven't been able to stop chewing on it. We'd been digging through some old Scott Aaronson blog posts on hypercomputation, and two regulars, Theo and Nadia, ended up going several rounds on whether the Church-Turing thesis is even the kind of thing that could turn out false.

Theo's opening move: the thesis is an empirical claim about the physical world, full stop. If someone built a device exploiting, say, a naked singularity or some closed-timelike-curve trick, that computed a function no Turing machine can compute, that would falsify it right there.

Nadia pushed back: that's the *physical* Church-Turing thesis, which is a seperate and much later claim. The original thesis is about "effectively calculable" functions -- what a human clerk mechanically following a finite rulebook with pencil and paper could do in principle -- it was never a claim about what physics permits. So whatever the exotic device is doing, we'd just conclude it isn't "computation" in the relevant sense, and the thesis walks away clean.

Theo said that's exactly the move that makes him suspicious: if literally no conceivable discovery could count against a claim, it isn't a thesis, its a definition wearing a discovery's clothes. He added that the famous convergence of Church, Turing and Post on equivalent formalisms, starting from totally different angles, doesn't prove much either -- maybe they just shared the same intuition pump, not a truth.

Nadia's comeback: the convergence *is* the evidence, just not the falsifiable-experiment kind. It's evidence about our shared informal concept of "algorithm," the same way several independent witnesses telling the same story counts as evidence even though there's no lab test for it.

Theo wouldn't let it go: "evidence for a conceptual claim" just means "a definition enough people find intuitive," and a bunch of rationalists of all people should be twitchy about unfalsifiable claims dressed up as substantive discoveries.

It fizzled out there with nobody conceding anything. Can you go through it move by move and say who's got the better of each exchange, and point out anything important that both of them are missing?
