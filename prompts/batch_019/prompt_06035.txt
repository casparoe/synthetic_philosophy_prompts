I'm a philosophy PhD student working on formal epistemology, and for this review I've been added as the student member of my university's institutional biosafety committee, weighing a proposal to release a gene-drive mosquito meant to suppress a local malaria vector. The three modeling teams the applicants commissioned won't converge on a probability that the drive breaches containment within five years -- their ranges (2-15%, 20-40%, and 1-5%) barely overlap -- and all three insist this reflects real disagreement about population-genetics assumptions, not noise that pooling would fix. The committee is split on how to decide given spreads like that: some want the release justifiable even against the single worst distribution among all three ranges; others think it should stand unless some stricter containment design beats it under every distribution still on the table; and a third group just wants to average the ranges into one number and treat this as an ordinary expected-value problem. Please draft the short memo I'll circulate before the vote -- approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions, with concrete conditions and a re-review date if conditional -- making explicit which of these ways of deciding under persistent disagreement you rely on, including a fair statement of what the worst-case faction gets right even if you don't side with them.
