In my AP Psychology class, we just finished a unit on classic experiments investigating what actually predicts whether someone will stop to help a stranger in need, and two of the studies have stayed with me. In one, seminary students were asked to walk across campus to deliver a short talk — for some of them, the talk happened to be on the parable of the Good Samaritan — and on the way each one passed a person slumped in a doorway, coughing and obviously in distress. What predicted whether a student stopped to help wasn't how religious they were or what their talk was about, but simply whether they had just been told they were running late. In the other study, people who happened to find a dime sitting in a phone booth were far more likely to help a stranger who dropped a stack of papers nearby than people who hadn't found anything. Our teacher's conclusion was that tiny, morally irrelevant situational nudges like these predict helping behavior much better than any personality trait does, and that this should make us suspicious of the whole idea that people have stable character traits like "kindness" or "honesty" in the first place.

I mentioned this to a couple of friends and we went back and forth without really resolving anything, partly because of something that happened with my friend Maya, who everyone (including me) would describe as the most generous person we know. A couple of weeks ago she walked straight past a classmate who was struggling to carry a huge stack of supplies down the stairs, apparently because she was anxious about being late to turn in an assignment; the very next day, in a calmer moment, a much less generous friend of mine stopped and helped that same classmate without hesitating. Does an episode like this mean my teacher is right — that "character" is basically a story we tell about people after the fact, so that I should stop thinking of Maya as especially generous and my other friend as especially self-centered? Or is there some way of holding on to the idea that people really do have character traits that doesn't amount to just ignoring the evidence? I would like an actual verdict on this, with the strongest reasoning on each side, rather than an answer that just splits the difference.
