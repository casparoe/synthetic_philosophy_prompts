I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, but an interest in Bayesian epistemology eventually led me to read about Rudolf Carnap and the Vienna Circle. One thing strikes me as odd: these were the same thinkers who held that metaphysical and ethical statements are cognitively meaningless because they cannot be empirically verified, yet Carnap and his colleagues were openly sympathetic to socialism and treated centralized, "scientific" economic planning as a natural extension of their program — their 1929 manifesto explicitly ties the "scientific world-conception" to progressive social reform. Please argue that Carnap's advocacy of planning is not merely a neutral, technical application of his philosophy of science but stands in real tension with his own verificationist rejection of cognitive meaning for value statements, because his political commitments smuggle in exactly the kind of unverifiable normative content his semantics is supposed to dismiss as meaningless, rather than dividing cleanly into verifiable means and independently chosen ends.
