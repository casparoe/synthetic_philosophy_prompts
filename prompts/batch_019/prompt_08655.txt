I'm outlining a novel with parallel timelines, and I ran a scene by my writing group where the MC finds out that in another branch of her life she became a murderer. It turned into a proper arguement between two people in the group and I can't stop turning it over in my head.

(1) Mara said the murderer isn't really her, just a double who happens to share her face and memories up to the point their timelines split — similar enough to be recognisable, but not the same entity, so it'd be strange for my MC to feel actual guilt over it, more like feeling bad about a stranger who looks like you.
(2) Dev said no, it's the exact same person, just a different branch of one life, and you don't need any causal thread linking the two versions for that to be true — we just stipulate it's her, same as how "if Napoleon had won at Waterloo" is still about Napoleon, not some Napoleon-ish stand in.
(3) Mara said fine, authors can stipulate whatever they like, but that just makes "same person" a decision made for the plot, not a fact about her that could make guilt appropriate — you can't get a real emotion out of an authorial ruling.
(4) Dev said the guilt doesn't need a metaphysical permission slip to make sense — people get survivor's guilt over stuff they had nothing to do with all the time — so the only question that matters is whether the two versions count as one self, and stipulating is exactly how identity questions get settled, in fiction and maybe in general.

Neither budged and now I don't know who to side with for the book. Can you go through it move by move and say who's got the better of it at each step (not just who wins overall), and call out anything important both of them are missing?
