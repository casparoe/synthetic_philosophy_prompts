I spent about thirty-five years as a bench chemist before retiring, and now I've been using the extra time to read my way through philosophy of science, which is basically a whole new hobby for me at this point. A few weeks back I got pulled into Larry Laudan's old argument against scientific realism, the "pessimistic meta-induction," where he lists a whole graveyard of theories that were genuinely successful in their day (phlogiston, caloric, the various ethers, etc.) but that we now think are just false, and uses that to argue we have no good reason to think our current best theories are true either, rather then merely useful. Seeing phlogiston on that list stung a little, professional pride and all, since it's basically my field's original sin.

Naturally I went looking for how realists answer this, and the reply that keeps getting billed as the strongest is John Worrall's structural realism: the idea that instead of being realist about the "stuff" a theory posits (ethers, calorics, fields, whatever), we should only commit to its mathematical structure, since that part often survives even when the underlying ontology gets thrown out entirely. The textbook example is Fresnel's ether-based equations for light surviving, more or less intact, inside Maxwell's electromagnetic theory. Clever, but I'm not totally convinced - it feels like it might just relocate the goalposts rather than genuinely escape Laudan's challenge, since who's to say the structures we currently believe in aren't just as historically contingent as the discarded ontologies were.

So here's what I'd like: a real comparison of these two, Laudan's pessimistic meta-induction and Worrall's structural realism as a reply to it. I want your honest take on whether structural realism actually gets the realist off the hook or whether it's vulnerable to basically the same historical pattern, just one level up. Throw in other case studies besides Fresnel/Maxwell if you've got good ones, and if there's some deeper technical snag that's made it hard for structuralists to even pin down what "preserved structure" really means, I'd like to hear about that too - my one philosopher friend hinted there's an issue there but wouldn't spell it out, said I should go look it up myself.
