A friend and I spent most of a long dinner arguing about teleosemantics, and neither of us budged. She thinks the whole research program is a shell game: dressing up "was selected to detect flies" in biological language doesn't remove the representational vocabulary it's supposed to explain, it just hides it one level down. I tried to defend the view by walking through the frog and the fly-detector, but I could feel myself reconstructing the theory's motivating story rather than actually answering her hardest objections, and I'd like to hear someone who works on this for a living get pushed past that story.

I help organize my department's philosophy-of-mind colloquium, and next month's invited speaker defends teleosemantics squarely within Ruth Millikan's biosemantic tradition -- I'd rather not script real words for a real person, so think of her as a composite defender of the view -- holding that a state's content is fixed by the selected function of the systems that produce and consume it. For the Q&A I'm moderating, I'd like eight to ten questions that don't just let her re-narrate the frog-and-fly story. I want at least one question built on Davidson's Swampman -- a molecule-for-molecule duplicate with no selection history whatsoever who nonetheless seems to reason and mean things -- and what the theory is committed to saying about him; one on the disjunction problem, pressing on why the frog's snap-mechanism should count as representing "fly" and not "small dark moving speck," given that both trigger it just as reliably; one on the distality or indeterminacy problem, about which link in the chain running from retinal stimulation through "nutrition" and "reproductive fitness" gets to count as the actual content, given that selection could just as truly be described as having favored any of the others; and one on how the producer/consumer machinery is supposed to secure genuine misrepresentation rather than quietly redefining the content, after the fact, as whatever actually caused the state on a given occasion. For each question, add a brief follow-up keyed to the move she's most likely to make in response -- an appeal to "normal conditions," to consumer-side proper functions, to a derived-versus-direct proper-function distinction, or to simply denying that Swampman has any content at all -- and have that follow-up press on whether the move actually earns its keep or just relocates the original puzzle.
