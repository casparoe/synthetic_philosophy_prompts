hi, I'm a phil phd student working on personal identity (Parfit, Buddhist reductionism, that whole literature) and I somehow ended up on the "practice safety" advisory board for a meditation research center that runs intensive retreats on classic Mahasi-style noting practice explicitly aimed at cessation (nirodha) -- the full progress-of-insight map, dukkha ñāṇas and all. Over the last 2 years we've had 4 participants land in states that look a lot like Britton's "dark night" cases: months of derealization, flattened affect, one hospitalization. The senior teachers on the board say this is a textually-mapped, expected stage and that softening instructions or pulling people out early just prolongs it; a couple of the clinicians say informed consent is basically incoherent here since the whole point of the technique is dismantling the self that would need to notice its own consent is being violated. I tried raising Parfit's point that reductionism about persons should make the "loss" feel liberating rather than damaging, but someone shot back that Parfit describes his own version as opening up, not as the flattened, cant-get-out-of-bed thing were seeing -- so maybe its not even the same "no-self" being realized.

Can you draft the actual memo the board needs to vote on -- keep, pause, or substantially modify the cessation track, with concrete conditions and a timeframe -- that actually engages the consent problem instead of just falling back on generic psychiatric caution or "trust the tradition"?
