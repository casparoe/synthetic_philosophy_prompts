I was elected to my town council this year as a candidate for one of the local parties, and the internal life of the caucus has started to bother me more than I expected. Before any vote, the caucus meets to settle on a common position; there is real argument in the room, but once a line is agreed, everyone is expected to vote it, and a member who votes their own conscience afterward is treated as a discipline problem rather than as someone who simply reached a different conclusion. What unsettles me most is that the arguments inside caucus are rarely about which policy is actually right or fair — they are about what keeps the group unified and what will let the party grow its seats next cycle, as though that mattered independently of the issue at hand. The other parties on the council operate in exactly the same way, so this does not seem to be a flaw of my party in particular. Colleagues tell me this is simply what representative politics requires once you organize at any scale, and that a council of unaffiliated members judging each issue on its own merits would be unworkable. Still, it feels like more than an ordinary compromise between my views and other people's — as if a judgment about what serves the group has quietly displaced my own judgment about what is true or just. Is my discomfort pointing at something real, or is this just what organized democratic politics inevitably requires?
