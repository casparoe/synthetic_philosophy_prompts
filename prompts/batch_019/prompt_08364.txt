Two of my former doctoral students met in my graduate seminar on the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā and are now junior faculty working on Madhyamaka ("Middle Way") Buddhist philosophy; they are getting married next month and have asked me to give the toast. Their one request, half in jest, is: no svabhava, no nihilism. They don't want soulmate language -- "meant for each other," love as some fixed essence the universe arranged -- since that smuggles inherent, independent existence back into the one context everyone is most tempted to believe in it, which is exactly what Nāgārjuna's dialectic is built to dismantle. But they equally don't want the toast to lapse into the smug "it's all empty, so none of this really matters" misreading that talk of emptiness invites at parties, since for Nāgārjuna dependent, changing, responsive things -- a marriage, for instance -- are possible only because they lack fixed essence, not despite it. They would like the tetralemma (catuṣkoṭi) put to actual work somewhere in the toast, not just named: perhaps some toast-appropriate question posed and then declined to resolve in the old fourfold way -- are the two of them now one person, two, both, or neither? About a third of the room are Buddhologists who will catch any sloppiness with the two-truths distinction; the rest are relatives who have never heard the word "Madhyamaka." Please write the toast: about five minutes, the doctrine doing real structural work rather than being name-dropped, landing by the end as a love letter rather than a seminar.
