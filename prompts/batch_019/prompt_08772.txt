I'm developing a collection of historical short stories, each centered on a moment when a celebrated rationalist's private convictions threatened to unsettle their public legend. For the entry on Newton, I'd like a short story set in the months after his death in 1727, narrated from the point of view of John Conduitt as he takes charge of Newton's papers and begins to reckon with what they contain: the argument, once sent to Locke, that the scriptural proof-texts for the Trinity are later corruptions; the readings of Daniel and Revelation identifying the triumph of Trinitarian orthodoxy in the fourth century as the very apostasy the prophets foretold; years of work reconstructing the cubit-measurements of Solomon's Temple as a cipher for those same prophecies. Conduitt is already quietly shaping the story the world will be told about the author of the Principia, and now has to decide how much of this to fold in, or bury, and from whom. Write the story so that it dramatizes that private reckoning rather than resolving it neatly.
