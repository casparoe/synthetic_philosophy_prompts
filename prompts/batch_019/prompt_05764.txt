Over dinner last weekend, I had a long and increasingly heated argument with a close friend about a legal dispute I've been stuck in with my next-door neighbor, and several days later I still haven't settled in my own mind who was right. About eighteen months ago, he and I disagreed over roughly six feet of overgrown land along our shared property line; a surveyor later valued the strip at somewhere between $3,000 and $5,000. Neither of us would concede the point, lawyers got involved on both sides, and between us we are now well over $40,000 deep in legal fees, with the trial still months away and an appeal already threatened by whichever side loses. I recognize perfectly well that none of that $40,000 is coming back regardless of how the case ends, and that even outright victory would leave me far poorer than if I had simply handed over the land the day he first asked for it. Even so, stopping now feels different to me than stopping eighteen months ago would have felt: settling at this point means that everything already spent bought me nothing, whereas seeing it through leaves open the chance that a judge eventually orders him to cover part of my costs, and avoids sending a signal, to him and to the rest of the block, that I can be pushed around in the driveway-easement dispute everyone expects is coming next.

My friend's position was that this is a plain case of letting money and effort already spent dictate a decision that should be governed only by the costs and benefits still ahead, and that I should settle immediately regardless of how much has already gone into the fight. I countered that the reputational stakes make my situation genuinely different from simply refusing to admit a mistake, but she wasn't moved, and she pointed out, fairly, that my neighbor is almost certainly reasoning in exactly the same self-justifying way I am, which may be a large part of why this has dragged on as long as it has. We went back and forth for over an hour without resolving anything, and I have a mediation session scheduled for next week that I would like to walk into with a clearer head than I have now. So I would like an actual verdict rather than a list of considerations on each side: given that my neighbor is presumably caught in the same trap and escalating right alongside me, is there any part of my reasoning for continuing that genuinely holds up, or is my friend simply right that I should cut my losses now, full stop?
