A friend of mine who is finishing a physics PhD and I spent most of dinner last night arguing about what Bell's theorem actually rules out, and I don't think either of us walked away satisfied. Her position was blunt: once you accept the experimental violations of the CHSH inequality, you have to accept some kind of superluminal influence, full stop, regardless of your preferred interpretation of quantum mechanics. I pushed back with something I half-remembered from a philosophy of physics seminar — that Bell's locality condition is really a conjunction of two logically separable assumptions, usually labeled "parameter independence" and "outcome independence" (I want to say this decomposition traces back to Jarrett, and that Abner Shimony, who also happens to be the "S" in CHSH, used similar terminology), and that only a violation of the first of these would actually conflict with relativity's prohibition on faster-than-light signalling. She thought this was a distinction without a difference, and got even more skeptical when I brought up the fact that Everettians describe their interpretation as fully local and deterministic — she suspects this is achieved only by quietly redefining "local," rather than by making any substantive physical claim.

I'd like your help thinking this through properly, since neither of us could reconstruct the argument with enough precision to settle it. First, could you lay out, in concrete physical terms, what it would mean for a hidden-variable model to violate parameter independence as opposed to outcome independence, and explain why only a failure of the former is supposed to be in tension with relativistic causality — i.e., why quantum nonlocality and relativity are often said to be in "peaceful coexistence" at all? Second, using that same vocabulary, tell me which of these two assumptions (or some third one entirely — say, the requirement that a measurement yield a single, determinate outcome) is actually being given up in the Everett interpretation, and whether my friend has a point that calling this "locality" amounts to a terminological maneuver rather than a genuine vindication of local physics.
