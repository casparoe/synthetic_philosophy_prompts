In my philosophy club, we've been discussing the "money pump" argument for why a rational agent's preferences must be transitive. The basic idea is that if I prefer A to B, B to C, but C to A, a clever trading partner can exploit the cycle, charging me a small fee at every trade until I've paid out a fortune while ending up exactly where I started. I also came across the idea of "resolute choice," where an agent resolves in advance to follow a fixed plan and simply refuses to defect from it even when later tempted to complete the cycle -- which seems to let the agent dodge the pump without ever giving up the cyclical preferences themselves. Could you explain how this resolute-choice response actually works, how it differs from the related "sophisticated choice" strategy of anticipating the cycle and declining the first trade outright, and whether decision theorists generally think either move genuinely refutes the money-pump argument for transitivity, or merely relocates the problem?
