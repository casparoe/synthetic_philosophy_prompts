A friend and I spent a long evening arguing about deflationism about truth and got stuck on one specific technical point neither of us could resolve. I was defending something close to Horwich-style minimalism: "true" is just a device for disquotation and for stating generalizations we couldn't otherwise express, and it does no further explanatory work. My friend, who knows more proof theory than I do, raised the conservativeness objection (a version of it goes back to Stewart Shapiro and Jeffrey Ketland): if truth is really that thin, extending a base theory like Peano Arithmetic with a truth predicate ought to be conservative — it shouldn't let you prove any new arithmetical sentences beyond what the base theory already proves on its own. Yet the compositional truth theories that actually deliver the generalizations deflationists care about are demonstrably non-conservative over PA: they let you prove PA's consistency, which by Gödel's second incompleteness theorem PA cannot prove of itself. I didn't have a good reply on the spot. Could you write a dialogue between a deflationist and a critic pressing this objection, in which the deflationist has a genuine, technically serious response rather than just reasserting that truth is metaphysically insubstantial, and the critic engages with that specific response instead of repeating the original point? Neither side should be a pushover, and it's fine if they don't reach a resolution — I'd rather see real engagement than a tidy ending.
