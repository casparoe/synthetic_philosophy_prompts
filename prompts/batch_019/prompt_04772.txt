I'm drafting a magazine piece on the strange second life Kurt Gödel's ontological argument for God's existence has had among logicians since his death. The version most people cite is Dana Scott's transcription of notes Gödel showed him in 1970; Gödel never published the proof himself, reportedly because he worried it would be read as a personal declaration that he had proven God exists, rather than as a demonstration of what follows from a set of axioms about "positive" properties.

What's slowing me down is the secondary literature on the argument's most damaging technical objection: modal collapse. As I understand it, Jordan Howard Sobel and, independently, Christopher Small showed that Gödel's own axioms -- specifically the ones defining an "essence" and "necessary existence" -- entail that whatever is actually true is also necessarily true, for every proposition, not just the existence of a God-like being, which looks less like proving a deity and more like erasing contingency from the world. I've also seen it argued that the "positive property" axioms amount to a disguised definition of an ultrafilter over properties, rigged so that a God-like element is guaranteed to fall out the other end -- which would make the whole argument close to question-begging. C. Anthony Anderson and, later, Melvin Fitting each proposed revised axioms meant to block the collapse while keeping the conclusion, but I can't tell from reading the papers alone whether those are principled repairs to a flawed formalization or just patches that relocate the same problem somewhere else in the system.

Since I'd rather represent this fairly than reduce it to "critics say the proof works too well," could you write me a dialogue of ten to fourteen exchanges between two philosophers of logic -- one defending a Gödel-style modal ontological argument, the other pressing the modal collapse objection as hard as it will go? I'd like them to do real logical work on the page rather than trade generalities: stating the relevant axioms or close paraphrases, walking through how S5 and the necessity of "positive"-hood generate the collapse, and arguing concretely about whether an Anderson- or Fitting-style emendation is a genuine fix or simply moves the question-begging into a different axiom. Please give the defender real moves rather than having them fold early, and don't resolve the dialogue with an authorial verdict at the end -- I'd like a reader to finish still having to weigh the exchange themselves.
