I'm working on a longer piece about whether the laws of physics actually "govern" the universe or just describe it after the fact, which turns out to be a whole live debate among philosophers of science (Sean Carroll's writing is what sent me down this rabbit hole). I feel like I've got a decent handle on the Humean side: a law is just whatever compact set of generalizations best balances simplicity and strength given the total pattern of events across history, past present and future, no mysterious "governing" required. What's tripping me up is the main objection to the rival, Armstrong-style view, where laws are supposed to be relations of natural necessitation between universals -- something like N(F,G), meaning "F-ness necessitates G-ness" -- which is meant to explain why every single F actually turns out to be a G, rather than that just being a wild cosmic coincidence.

A philosopher i interviewed for the piece said the whole picture basically falls apart because of something called "the inference problem," and mentioned that Lewis even had this line poking fun at Armstrong's own name -- something about how you don't get mighty biceps just by being called "Armstrong," i.e. slapping the label "necessitation" onto a relation doesn't actually earn you the right to infer that every single F is a G, unless the regularity was smuggled in some other way to begin with (I'm paraphrasing badly, didn't note the exact wording down). I sort of see the shape of the worry but can't get it to fully click for myself. Why can't there just be a brute, sui generis necessary connection between the universal F-ness and the universal G-ness, the way certain purely formal or mathematical properties necessitate others? Could you actually walk me through why philosophers think this move fails to earn Armstrong what he wants, and also whether the "dispositional essentialist" crowd (Bird and that lot), who ground the necessity in the properties themselves rather than in some separate external relation, really manage to escape the same problem or just relocate it one level down?
