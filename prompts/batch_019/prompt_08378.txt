So two people in my writers group got into it over a scene in my new manuscript last night and I honestly cant settle it. Setup: my character Reyes runs a mid-size drug company. She greenlights a rollout because it'll boost this quarter's numbers, and she knows -- and doesn't care either way -- that the same decision means the small adverse-event monitoring team gets quietly defunded, since that budget's going to marketing instead. The rollout ships, numbers go up, a rare complication goes undetected for months. In an earlier draft I had a mirror scene: same Reyes, same "doesn't care either way" attitude, except this time the freed-up money happens to fully fund a whistleblower hotline that had been running on fumes for years.

(1) Marcus says everyone will read the first Reyes as having intentionally let the monitoring team die, but basically nobody will read the second Reyes as having intentionally funded the hotline, even though her actual state of mind -- pure indifference -- is identical in both scenes, and that this is a reader inconsistency I should smooth over rather than lean into. (2) Aliyah says it's not inconsistency, its readers correctly tracking something real: the badness of an outcome is itself evidence about what an agent's reasons were, so people aren't being biased by the harm, they're being informed by it. (3) Marcus says that can't be right, because then whether Reyes acted "intentionally" ends up depending on the moral valence of the outcome, which gets the order of explanation backwards -- you're supposed to settle what she actually did intentionally first, from facts about her state of mind, and only then decide how blameworthy that makes her. Otherwise "she did it intentionally" stops being independent grounds for blame at all, it's just blame wearing a disguise. (4) Aliyah says that ordering is exactly the assumption on trial here, not something Marcus gets to help himself to, and there's no circularity anyway, since "the outcome was bad" and "Reyes is blameworthy" are different claims -- the badness is doing evidential work about what Reyes wanted, and actual blame comes later and depends on plenty else besides.

They argued about this for like 20 minutes at the bar and neither one budged. Can you adjudicate move by move?
