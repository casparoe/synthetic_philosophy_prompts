I'm about a third of the way into a campus novel set at Oxford in the late 80s, and there's a dinner party scene I keep getting stuck on. One of the characters, a crusty old philosophy don, is pretty transparently modelled on A.J. Ayer -- verification principle and all -- and the scene takes place not long after his real-life brush with death: choking on a piece of smoked salmon in hospital, heart stopped for four minutes, and coming back with a whole story about a blinding red light, "ministers" running the universe, and trying to cross a river he assumed was the Styx (he apparently wrote the whole thing up afterwards in an essay called "What I Saw When I Was Dead"). What I love is that he was pretty careful in public to say the experience had only "slightly weakened" his conviction that death is the end, and hadn't touched his conviction that there's no god -- but there's also this story going around that he privately told his doctor he'd seen a Divine Being and would have to revise all his books. That gap between the careful public hedging and the more dramatic private confession is exactly the kind of thing my sharpest, slightly cruel character at the table would pounce on.

So what I want is an actual joke, not just a "ha, the atheist saw a light" line, because thats not really a joke, its just an observation. It needs to land specifically for someone who knows what the verification principle in Language, Truth and Logic says about a sentence like "there is life after death" -- namely that it's not false but strictly meaningless, no more truth-apt than a grunt of approval -- and who also knows how carefully Ayer tried to hold the line afterward between "weird experience" and "evidence of survival." Could you write me two or three short versions, each just a line or two of dialogue that a character could actually say out loud at dinner, no extended setup? The character delivering it is dry and a bit mean, so it's fine if it stings a little.
