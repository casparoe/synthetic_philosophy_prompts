I'm the non-scientist community member on my hospital's institutional review board. On Thursday we're voting on a protocol that would give terminally ill cancer patients a single, supervised high-dose psilocybin session, paired with therapy, to treat the fear and despair some of them feel about dying. The drug isn't supposed to work by chemically calming people down; it's supposed to work by giving them an experience that changes what they believe and care about -- patients who've undergone it elsewhere often report valuing entirely different things afterward than they did beforehand, in ways they say they couldn't have anticipated. Half the board thinks our standard consent form is adequate as long as we disclose the physical and psychological risks, the same as for any drug trial. The other half thinks that when a treatment is meant to work by changing a patient's outlook in ways nobody, including the patient, can foresee, ordinary risk disclosure doesn't really amount to informed consent, and wants added safeguards, such as mandatory values-clarification counseling before enrollment. Please draft the short recommendation I need to circulate before the meeting -- approve as submitted, approve with conditions, or send it back for revision -- and make sure it engages the second worry seriously rather than treating it as merely a dramatic version of the uncertainty medicine ordinarily asks patients to accept.
