As a physicist, I spent my career largely ignoring consciousness studies, but retirement has finally given me time to work through the literature properly, and I have spent the last few weeks on Giulio Tononi's Integrated Information Theory. The objection that has stayed with me is not the familiar complaint that Φ, IIT's measure of integrated information, seems arbitrary, but rather the "unfolding argument" associated with Adrien Doerig, Aaron Schurger, and colleagues: any recurrent network can be unfolded, over any finite time horizon, into a purely feedforward network that computes the identical input-output function, and since IIT assigns feedforward networks zero Φ regardless of how high their recurrent counterparts score, the theory is committed to pairs of systems that remain forever behaviorally indistinguishable while differing completely in conscious experience. The authors conclude that IIT, and causal-structure theories of consciousness more generally, must therefore be either false or not genuinely scientific. I would like you to argue that this conclusion does not follow: that the argument only appears fatal if one has already assumed functionalism, which is precisely the thesis IIT was constructed to deny, and that treating the unfolding result as a consequence of the theory rather than a refutation of it does not, by itself, render IIT unfalsifiable or ad hoc.
