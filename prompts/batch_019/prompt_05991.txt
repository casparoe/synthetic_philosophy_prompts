I'm at work on a novel featuring two civilizations that arise on opposite sides of a cosmological horizon: owing to the accelerating expansion of space between them, no signal could ever travel from one to the other, in either direction. The plot turns on an instance of acausal trade. Each civilization reasons its way to an accurate picture of the other's values and, on that basis, unilaterally forgoes some practice — harvesting a particular resource, say — that its counterpart would find abhorrent, trusting that the other extends the same restraint in kind. I've been picturing this bargain as sealed once each side broadcasts a sufficiently detailed account of its own values and dispositions out into space, for the other to detect and model many ages later. What I can't work out, for the sake of the plot, is how either civilization could ever catch the other in bad faith: broadcasting a false picture of restraint, for instance, while quietly continuing to harvest the resource in secret. Is there a way, within the logic of acausal trade, for either side to guard against this kind of deception, or is blind trust simply the best either can do?
