I'm at work on a novel that includes a long scene between Einstein and Gödel on one of the walks between the Institute for Advanced Study and Gödel's house — walks that Einstein, by his own admission, kept up chiefly for the pleasure of Gödel's company. Before I dramatize the scene in my own voice, I want to see the underlying disagreement fought out honestly, so that when I compress it later I can be sure neither man has been flattened into a type.

Write the dialogue. Gödel should make the case he actually made in his letters to his mother: that the world's being rationally organized — a premise fundamental to his whole outlook — counts against a universe in which a mind spends decades cultivating itself only to be extinguished, its development never put to any further use, and he should explain why he took this to be a properly Leibnizian position rather than a Spinozist one. Einstein should answer from his own settled ground — an impersonal, law-governed God with no stake in anyone's survival or reward — and should press Gödel on whether an argument from the "waste" of a cultivated mind is doing any real metaphysical work, or simply borrowing the vocabulary of rationalism to dress up a wish. Have each take the other's strongest point seriously rather than talk past it, and don't let either man concede or land a clean win: I want the disagreement left exactly where it actually lives.
