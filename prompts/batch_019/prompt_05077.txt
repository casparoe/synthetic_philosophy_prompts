In my history and philosophy of science seminar, we've spent the last few weeks on the Vienna Circle, and this week's readings paired Carnap's intellectual autobiography with the 1929 manifesto he co-authored with Neurath and Hahn. What struck several of us in the discussion afterward is how openly both texts tie the "scientific world-conception" to a hope that society itself could eventually be reorganized along rational, planned lines, echoing Neurath's proposals for economic planning "in kind" rather than through market prices. Carnap seems to have held this as a genuine personal conviction even though he elsewhere insists, in line with his general views on ethics, that value judgments are not the kind of thing science can settle on its own.

Next week, we are contrasting this with Popper's The Poverty of Historicism, where he argues against exactly this sort of comprehensive, blueprint-driven planning and defends a piecemeal, trial-and-error style of social reform instead, largely because he thinks grand schemes for remaking society as a whole tend to slide into authoritarianism. It's a strange contrast to sit with: two thinkers shaped by the same Viennese milieu, both deeply committed to empiricism and hostile to metaphysics, yet seemingly worlds apart on whether science licenses large-scale social planning at all.

I would like to walk into that discussion with a clearer picture of what is really at stake. Could you compare Carnap's vision of the relationship between science, planning, and politics with Popper's critique of "utopian" social engineering? I am especially interested in whether their disagreement reflects something philosophically deep, such as differing views on the fallibility of scientific knowledge or the unity of the sciences, or whether it is better explained by differing political temperaments sitting on top of a fairly similar empiricist epistemology. Finally, of the two positions, which do you think holds up better in light of what we have since learned about large-scale technocratic planning, and why?
