So this is going to sound like a weirdly specific question, but I've been chewing on it for weeks and my writing group just went around in circles about it last night, so now I'm curious what you make of it. I'm three books into a fantasy series (my agent keeps saying the title needs work, but that's a separate problem) where the magic system runs on memory as currency: to cast a spell you permanently forget something, and how significant the memory is determines how powerful the spell ends up being. Over the three books I've written maybe nine scenes that establish this on the page — forgetting the smell of your childhood home lights a candle from across a room, forgetting your first kiss can knock a grown man off his feet, forgetting your mother's face can bring down a stone wall, that sort of escalation. While drafting I never once wrote down an actual formula, I just went with whatever felt emotionally right scene by scene, and it always felt like I was tracking one consistent underlying rule, even though I couldn't have told you what it was if you'd asked.

Now my editor has asked the obvious next-book question: could a caster forget their entire identity and, say, stop time, or bring someone back from the dead? And I honestly don't know, because when I go back through my nine scenes, I can build at least three seperate formulas relating "significance of the memory" to "power of the spell" that fit every single one of those scenes perfectly, and they diverge wildly on the identity-erasure case — one just keeps scaling up forever, one hits a hard ceiling well short of anything like resurrection, one has this weird jump right at memories that count as "identity-defining." Nothing I've actually written rules out any of the three, and there's no secret master formula sitting in a notebook somewhere that I've forgotten about — the "rule" only ever existed in whatever finite set of scenes I happened to write.

That's spiraled into a bigger question that's honestly bugging me more than the book itself: was there ever really a fact, even one only I had access to, about which rule I was "actually" using, or was I only ever committed to the handful of specific cases on the page, full stop, with everything else genuinely open until I decide, right now, what happens next? And is my situation as the person who invented this system actually different, in kind, from an ordinary person who's used some everyday word or followed some rule their whole life in only finitely many cases and then runs into a situation that's genuinely new? I'd love to know what's actually going on here philosophically, and whether "the rule I really meant all along" is even a coherent thing to be looking for.
