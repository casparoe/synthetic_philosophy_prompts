I sit on the clinical ethics subcommittee of a regional poison-control consortium. A chemical-plant accident has left six workers exposed to the same lethal toxin, and we have exactly one batch of antidote left, with resupply still a day and a half out. One worker needs the whole batch to survive; the other five each need only a fifth of it. Toxicology confirms the six are otherwise identical for triage purposes -- same prognosis, same certainty of death if untreated, no tie-breakers that our protocol recognizes.

Our protocol simply directs staff to save whichever option yields more survivors, but one member objects that this quietly treats five deaths as five times as bad as one, when each of the six loses everything regardless of how many others do too. This is essentially Taurek's 1977 case from "Should the Numbers Count?", right down to the fractions, and I don't want to resolve it by assuming the arithmetic just wins. The state health authority wants our tie-breaking rule on file by tomorrow morning, as standing policy, not just tonight's call.

Please draft the short opinion I'll circulate tonight: recommend one concrete rule, show how it resolves tonight's case, and give the reasoning, including a fair statement of the strongest argument against whatever you recommend.
