I've been reading Ned Block's old papers against functionalism for a phil of mind seminar, and one of his thought experiments has been stuck in my head for weeks. He imagines the entire population of China (something like a billion people, roughly the same order of magnitude as the neurons in a brain) each assigned to play the role of a single neuron, connected up by two-way radios, so that for an hour the whole population collectively realizes the exact same functional organization as a mind thats, say, in pain. Functionalism says a mental state just is whatever plays the right causal role between inputs, outputs, and other internal states, so if this "China-brain" has the right organization it should count as being in pain in exactly the sense you or I are. Block's intuition (and mine, though I keep second-guessing it) is that this is nuts - surely there's nobody home in that scattered, radio-linked system no matter how well it mimics the wiring - but then I worry that's just the same kind of chauvinism about multiple realizability that people probably showed toward octopus minds, or would show toward some alien or AI whose "neurons" are nothing like ours, before actually thinking it through.

Could you write me a short story (a few pages, doesnt need a tidy ending) that takes a scenario like this seriously as actual fiction rather than a dry intuition pump? Feel free to use Block's exact setup or your own variant - a hive of aliens, an upload, a very slow mind implemented by people passing notes to each other, whatever substrate you like - so long as the story genuinely lives inside the question of whether a system that's functionally identical to a mind, but built out of wildly unusual parts, has someone home having real experiences, or is just going through the motions. I want actual characters with something at stake, not an essay wearing a story as a costume, and I'd honestly rather it left me sitting with the uncertainty than have it resolved neatly either way.
