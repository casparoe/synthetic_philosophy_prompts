Ok, this is an actual committee I'm on, not a hypothetical -- feels like the moment all my years lurking on LW and the EA forum are supposed to pay off. I sit on the grants subcommitee for a small suffering-focused animal-welfare fund, and we've got a proposal to fund a pilot gene-drive project suppressing a wild rodent population on an island, justified not by conservation but by the claim that it cuts aggregate suffering from starvation, disease and predation. Half the committee says that's just our existing wild-animal-welfare logic taken to its obvious next step; the other half keeps raising the classic objection to negative utilitarianism -- that once suffering-reduction alone does the justificatory work, there's no principled place to stop short of 'fewer suffering-capable beings, full stop' -- and worries funding this quietly commits us to that logic either way. I need to draft the committee's actual decision memo for next week: fund, decline, or fund with conditions, and it needs to take the slippery-slope worry seriously rather than just pointing out that conservation biologists already do stuff like this.
