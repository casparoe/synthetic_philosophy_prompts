So I spent almost 40 years as a physicist watching one theory get superseded by the next "more accurate but still technically false" theory, and when I stumbled on Poppers notion of verisimilitude - the idea that a false theory can nevertheless be objectively closer to the truth than another false theory - it felt like exactly the right concept for what I'd been watching my whole career. Then I read that Tichy and Miller basically tore his original formal definition of it apart back in the 70s, showing it can't do the job it was built for. Could you cook up a small, concrete toy example (a handful of simple statements, in the spirit of Popper's own weather examples) that actually walks through how and why the definition collapses, rather than just asserting that it does?
