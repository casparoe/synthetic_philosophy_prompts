So I run a small appliance repair shop, and a few weeks back I got into a dumb argument with a customer over whether her toaster's heating element was "dangerous" even though the built-in cutoff switch meant it could never actually overheat on her counter. That argument wouldnt leave me alone, and it somehow sent me down a Stanford Encyclopedia rabbit hole about "dispositions" that I still haven't climbed out of. Turns out there's a whole running fight in philosophy over whether you can define a disposition -- fragile, poisonous, "live" for a wire, whatever -- as simply "would do X if Y happened to it," because people keep inventing cases that break the definition. Theres the "fink" case: a dead wire hooked up to a sensor that switches the current on the instant a screwdriver touches it, so the wire shocks you even though it wasn't live a second earlier. And there's the opposite problem, "masking": a poison that stays poison the whole time, but you swallow an antidote right along with it, so "would kill you if ingested" comes out false even though the stuff never stopped being poisonous. I gather David Lewis tried to patch the original definition by saying what matters is whether the same underlying "causal basis" stays in place and does the actual causing, but apparently even his patched version still struggles with the antidote cases, and there always seems to be some new device a person can dream up to break whatever the current patch is.

What I actually want, instead of one more explanation, is to watch this get fought out properly. Could you write me a dialogue between two people who both know this stuff cold: one who thinks the counterfactual analysis of dispositions can keep getting patched to survive the next fink or mask, no matter how clever, and one who's basically concluded the whole project is a dead end and that dispositions have to be treated as their own basic, unanalyzable kind of property, full stop. I want real arguing -- them actually responding to each other's specific examples (the wire, the poison, or new ones if that helps make a sharper point) instead of each side just restating its general outlook -- and I don't want either one to obviously come out on top. Ideally I end up finishing it still as torn as I am right now.
