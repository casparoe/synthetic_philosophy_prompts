So I went down a bit of a rabbit hole last week after watching some video essay about Descartes, and in the comments someone brought up this way older thinker, Avicenna (some Persian doctor-philosopher guy from like a thousand years ago), who apparently came up with basically the same "I think therefore I am" type idea, except his version is so much weirder and more vivid. It's called the flying man argument — you're supposed to imagine a fully grown adult man who just pops into existence out of nowhere, but suspended in mid-air, blindfolded, arms and legs spread out so they don't even brush against each other, no sounds, no smells, literally nothing touching him or reaching him from outside. And the claim is that even in that state, with zero sensory input of any kind, this guy would still just know, immediately, that he exists. Like his sense of "I am" doesn't come from his body or his senses at all, it's just there on its own.

I can't stop picturing this, it's honestly kind of haunting to me, this floating guy in the dark not even able to feel his own fingers touching each other. Could you write me a short story built around that scenario? It doesn't have to be literally a medieval man floating in a void, it could be updated into something modern, like someone in a sensory-deprivation tank, or a coma patient, or an astronaut whose suit fails in a way that cuts off all input, or even an AI/uploaded mind cut off from all its sensors, whatever you think works best. But I don't just want a creepy atmosphere piece out of it, I want it to actually dig into the philosophical question underneath, whether that immediate sense of "I exist" can genuinely be separate from the body and senses like Avicenna thought, or whether that feeling is an illusion that would eventually dissolve without a body feeding it. Keep it fairly short, flash-fiction length is totally fine, I'm more interested in the idea being explored well then it being some big epic.
