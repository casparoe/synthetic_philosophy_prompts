I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology finishing a dissertation chapter on self-locating belief, and I want to work through a connection I've only ever seen gestured at, never actually spelled out. Elga's thirder solution to the Sleeping Beauty problem is often glossed as an application of something like Bostrom's Self-Indication Assumption (SIA): you favor Tails because it entails twice as many awakenings, and hence twice as many candidates for "you." SIA is also the standard way of defusing the Doomsday Argument, since it is supposed to exactly cancel the anthropic shift that the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) generates from your birth rank, restoring the priors you would have held on purely non-anthropic grounds. Yet that same SIA is what licenses Bostrom's own Presumptuous Philosopher, who infers from the armchair that a big-universe cosmology is almost certainly correct, purely because it implies vastly more observers, without waiting on the experiment the physicists in the story were about to run.

What I'd like from you is the underlying mechanics rather than a survey of positions: why does stacking SIA on top of SSA produce an exact cancellation in the Doomsday case, rather than a partial correction or an overcorrection; why does that same reweighting go through unchecked in the cosmological case; and whether accepting the thirder verdict in Sleeping Beauty really commits one to the full-strength SIA that generates the Presumptuous Philosopher, or whether the within-world duplication of a single individual in Sleeping Beauty is different enough from the across-world population comparisons in Doomsday and cosmology to let one keep the former while resisting the latter.
