So this turned into a whole debate in my group chat last week and I want a take from someone who isn't already invested in one side. A few of us have been doing calibration practice for about a year now -- you write down a percentage for how confident you are that something will happen, then check later how you did, basically the thing Tetlock's forecasting-tournament research made popular, and that a bunch of rationalist bloggers do every January with their prediction posts. It works great for elections, sports, stuff like that, where you've got a huge pile of similar past cases to check your percentages against and can actually see whether your 70%s come true about 70% of the time over the long run.

The argument started because my friend Priya was waiting on results from a professional licensing exam -- one of those one-shot, pass/fail, can't-retake-it-for-a-year deals -- and said "I'm about 85% confident I passed." Her partner Marcus, who's normally just as into the calibration stuff as the rest of us, said that in this particular case the number was flat-out empty, not just shaky. His reasoning: a percentage only means something if its tracking how often things like this actually happen, and for "Priya passing this exact exam on this exact attempt" there's no honest way to pick which group of comparable past cases to measure her against -- everyone who took the exam that year? people who felt as prepared as she did? people with her specific study habits? -- since each grouping gives you a totally different pass rate and nothing tells you which one is the "real" one. So her 85% is just a feeling wearing a math costume, according to him. Priya's comeback was that when we flip a coin to decide who does the dishes, nobody thinks that 50% is somehow fake just because that particular coin only gets flipped once that night -- so why can't her gut sense, built out of years of taking tests and actually knowing what "ready" feels like from the inside, cash out as a real number too, even with no tidy pile of comparable cases lying around?

Neither of them budged and its honestly been bugging me all week. So: is Marcus right that Priya's 85% isn't really a probability of anything, or is he smuggling in some assumption that doesn't actually hold up?
