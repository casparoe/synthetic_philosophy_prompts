Last night a friend and I spent nearly two hours on a question about Kant's Groundwork that I expected to resolve in about five minutes: why do only two of his four famous examples—the false promise and the suicide case—fail what is usually called the "contradiction in conception" version of the universalizability test, while the other two—neglecting one's talents and refusing to help others in need—fail only the weaker "contradiction in the will" version? My friend's position was that this is really just a matter of degree, with Kant tracking how uncomfortable or self-undermining a universalized maxim would be, and the perfect duties simply marking the more extreme end of that spectrum. I argued instead that there is a genuine structural difference between a maxim one cannot even coherently imagine as a universal law of nature and one that is perfectly imaginable but could not rationally be willed, and that flattening these into a single spectrum misses what Kant is actually doing with the two tests.

The conversation also drifted into a dispute about whether the standard "logical contradiction" reading of the conception test even survives an obvious objection: if only a minority of people make lying promises while everyone else keeps theirs, trust in promising does not obviously collapse, so it is unclear what is supposed to be strictly contradictory about universalizing the maxim in the first place. Neither of us could reconstruct why Korsgaard's alternative reading is supposed to handle this better than the logical-contradiction view does. Could you help settle this properly? I would like a detailed account of what actually distinguishes the contradiction-in-conception and contradiction-in-the-will tests, why the four Groundwork examples divide as they do along the perfect/imperfect and self-regarding/other-regarding lines, and what, on Korsgaard's practical-contradiction interpretation, is supposed to be wrong with treating the conception test as a matter of straightforward logical inconsistency. I would rather not receive the standard "universalize the maxim and check for a contradiction" summary—that is exactly the level at which the two of us were stuck for two hours.
