I've spent a long time reading LessWrong and the wider rationalist blogosphere, and lately I've been working through some of the academic pragmatist literature that this community's epistemology gestures at without ever quite engaging directly. I drafted the notes below toward a post and would appreciate your comments before I put it up anywhere.

The Sequences treat concepts as tools: a category earns its keep by compressing predictively useful information about clusters in "thingspace," and the "carving reality at its joints" metaphor presumes that some joints are actually there, waiting to be found, independent of any observer's purposes. "The Simple Truth" makes the underlying commitment explicit: however sophisticated the objection, the pebble-and-sheep correspondence between map and territory is treated as the thing truth-talk is ultimately about, and rival accounts of truth as social agreement, as usefulness, or as illusion are dispatched for failing to notice that they still smuggle in some version of that correspondence to do their explanatory work.

Rorty's antirepresentationalism looks, from a distance, like it is making the same move: no scheme-independent fact settles whether Pluto is "really" a planet, so stop asking and use whichever taxonomy serves your purposes. I think the resemblance is superficial and collapses under a bit of pressure. The rationalist picture is still fundamentally representationalist; it has just relocated the correspondence relation from individual sentences to entire conceptual schemes, so that a scheme's goodness consists in how well it tracks mind-independent structure, real clusters, real causal joints, that would be there for any observer with the right setup, human purposes or none. Rorty denies there is any such backstop. His point in Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature, and again in Contingency, Irony, and Solidarity, isn't merely that many equally good schemes could fit a joint-having world; it's that the picture of a joint-having world sitting outside every vocabulary, ready to be fit, is itself an artifact of the representationalist picture he wants dropped entirely, not a neutral fact both sides can appeal to.

That leaves what I take to be the standard objection to press, some version of which I gather shows up in the exchanges with Putnam, though I know those mostly at second hand: physics-flavored vocabularies reliably out-predict folk or occult ones in a way that seems to cry out for explanation, and "we have culturally settled on caring about prediction, and these vocabularies happen to deliver it" reads like a redescription of the explanandum rather than an explanation of it. As I understand Rorty, his considered response is that demanding a further, vocabulary-transcendent explanation of this differential success is just the representationalist itch reasserting itself, and that a pragmatist is entitled to stop right there. I can't tell whether that is a defensible place to plant a flag or a well-dressed refusal to answer the question.

Have I represented Rorty fairly here, or is there a strawman hiding somewhere in this reconstruction? And is there a genuine Rortyan answer to the differential-success worry that I'm overlooking, short of simply declining the demand for explanation?
