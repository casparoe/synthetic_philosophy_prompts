Ok so this has been driving me and my friends a little crazy for like a week now. In my ethics elective we did this classic lifeguard case: Lifeguard A pushes a swimmer under and lets him drown, and Lifeguard B just doesn't bother throwing the rope to a totally different guy whos drowning and could easily be saved. Half the class said these are basically the same thing morally, because not throwing the rope obviously *causes* the death too (if he'd thrown it the guy lives, so the omission clearly makes the difference), and that treating "letting die" as less bad than killing is really just us being squeamish about blaming inaction. But then my friend Theo pulled up this philosopher, I think her last name was Beebee (might be misremembering), who apparently argues omissions can't actually be causes at all, since "nothing" can't cause "something" — like there's no actual causal oomph coming from a non-event.

Now half my friend group has basically decided that the entire killing vs letting-die debate just comes down to whichever side wins that one metaphysics argument: if omissions really do count as causes, there's supposedly no real moral difference between Lifeguard A and Lifeguard B, and if omissions aren't causes, then letting die is basically fine since technically nothing caused it. Is that actually how the philosophy works, or are we missing something big here? We have to turn in a position paper on this by Friday and I don't want to build the whole thing on a premise that's secretly wrong.
