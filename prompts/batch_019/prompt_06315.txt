The formal ethics reading group I'm part of has spent the last few weeks working through Harsanyi's aggregation theorem, and I want to sanity-check a framing I'm planning to use in a talk for our department's graduate workshop next month, since a couple of the group's members are decision theorists who won't let a sloppy statement of the theorem pass unchallenged. As I understand it, once both individuals and the social evaluator are required to have preferences over lotteries satisfying the von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms, the Pareto indifference condition forces the social utility function to be an affine combination of the individual utility functions. I've been presenting Harsanyi (1955) to the group as, in effect, a mathematical vindication of classical utilitarianism: any rational social planner who respects unanimous indifference among expected-utility maximizers is thereby committed to summing everyone's utility with equal weight, on pain of violating one of the axioms. On this reading, prioritarian or Rawlsian departures from equal-weight aggregation are not merely contestable value judgments but outright violations of rationality once uncertainty enters the picture.

One of the economists in the group objected that this smuggles in more than the theorem actually delivers, gesturing at something about the weights being arbitrary depending on how each person's utility scale is normalized, and mentioned a "Harsanyi-Sen debate" that I have only encountered in passing footnotes. Before I build part of the talk around an "utilitarianism is mathematically mandatory" framing, could you help me work out whether the theorem really pins down equal weighting across individuals, or whether I am reading something into the axioms that is not there? If it is the latter, I would appreciate a precise account of what further assumption is needed to move from "some affine combination of individual utilities" to "the equally weighted sum," and how that gap bears on whether the theorem genuinely forecloses non-utilitarian views as irrational.
