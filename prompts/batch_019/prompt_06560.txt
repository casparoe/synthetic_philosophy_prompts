For the past year or so I've been reading rationalist and effective-altruism forum posts about an idea usually called "evidential cooperation in large worlds" — I have never seen it addressed in any book I own, so everything I know about it is self-taught. As I understand the argument: if the universe or multiverse is large or infinite, it almost certainly contains an enormous number of other reasoning agents whose decision-making resembles ours closely enough to be correlated with it, even though we will never meet them, never exchange so much as a signal, and hold different ultimate values than we do. Because of that correlation, choosing to act with some weight on the values of that whole distant population, rather than only my own, is itself evidence that similar agents are doing the same on my behalf — even though nothing causal passes between us and neither side could ever check whether the other kept its end of things. People sometimes describe this as a form of acausal trade, though apparently the researchers behind it are careful to insist it is not trade in the ordinary sense, since no negotiation or communication is involved at all.

I would like to see this made concrete in fiction rather than left as an abstract argument. Could you write me a short story, somewhere around 1000 to 1500 words, in which a character works out this reasoning and then acts on it? Please make sure it is clear from what actually happens in the story why the act depends on correlated reasoning rather than on shared identity or shared values, why it has nothing to do with hoping for any causal payoff, and why it is not secretly just a rebranded threat.
