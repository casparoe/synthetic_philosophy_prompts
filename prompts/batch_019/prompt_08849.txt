I've served on the board of our neighborhood food co-op for a couple of years now, and in my spare time I've gotten interested in an idea I came across online called "futarchy" — cooked up, I believe, by an economist a couple of decades back. As I understand it, the pitch is something like "vote on values, bet on beliefs": you get everyone to agree on one number that stands for how well the organization is doing overall, and then, instead of debating a decision the usual way, you open a betting market where members wager on what that number would end up being under each option on the table. Whichever option the market prices higher is the one you go with, the reasoning being that real money forces people to reveal their honest best guess, and that trading pools together all the scattered bits of information different members happen to have.

We now have an actual decision where a few of us want to try this out: whether to open a second, smaller storefront across town or instead put the same money into expanding our existing store. A landlord's offer on the strip-mall space expires in about two months, so the board has to decide either way by then. The proposal is a pilot: define some combined score for co-op health — say, store revenue plus our annual member-satisfaction survey — and let members trade contracts on what that score would be a year out under each option, with the higher-priced option winning. Some board members are enthusiastic that this would settle the question more honestly than another round of meetings; others think it's little more than a gimmick, on the grounds that we only have around 600 members who mostly already talk to each other and already know roughly what everyone else thinks, so there's no hidden information left for a market to dig up — and that whatever small number of people do trade could easily distort the price, deliberately or not, in a market that thin. I've been asked to put together a recommendation before the board votes.

Could you draft that memo for me? I want it to land on an actual recommendation — run the pilot on this decision, skip it, or run it only with specific safeguards attached — together with a realistic timeline given the two-month deadline, rather than just a list of considerations on both sides. Please take the skeptics on the board seriously instead of waving them off: I'd especially like the memo to grapple with whether a group our size can really supply the kind of market this idea depends on, and with whether folding "how well the co-op is doing" into one tradeable number quietly ends up deciding, through the market, exactly the value questions the vote was supposed to settle beforehand.
