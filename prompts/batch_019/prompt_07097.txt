Something happened a few weeks ago that I can't stop turning over, and I would value a careful outside perspective on it. My partner of four years — we have been quietly discussing getting engaged this year — became extremely drunk at my closest friend's wedding and, over the course of the reception, said a series of genuinely cruel things: to me, that my career ambitions are unrealistic and always have been; to my mother, about her cooking; and, entirely unprompted, a long, bitter speech about resentment over how we split money, something he has never once raised in four years of otherwise candid conversation. By the next afternoon he was mortified, apologized repeatedly, and insisted that none of it reflected what he actually believes — that, in his words, "it wasn't me talking."

My friends have split into two camps over what to make of this. One camp holds that someone that intoxicated is barely an agent at all, so the words are closer to noise than to testimony, and holding them against him would be like blaming someone for what they mumble under anesthesia. The other camp insists that alcohol doesn't invent opinions out of nothing, only strips away the filter that normally keeps them unsaid, so what came out is, if anything, more honest than his usual tact. I've read a little about disinhibition and "alcohol myopia" in psychology, but that only explains the mechanism, not whether what gets disinhibited deserves to be believed. Since this bears directly on whether I actually know the person I am planning to spend my life with, I would like your considered opinion: when someone says something wounding while drunk that he would never say sober, is that good evidence of what he truly thinks and feels, or is my partner right that it simply was not him talking?
