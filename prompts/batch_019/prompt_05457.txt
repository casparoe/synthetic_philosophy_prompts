I'm drafting a novel where one of my characters, a translator named Marisol, promised her dying mentor she'd never reveal that he faked the results in a decades-old study -- and now a grad student is about to build her entire dissertation on those same faked results. I workshopped the chapter where Marisol talks herself into breaking the promise anyway, explicitly running her reasoning through Kant's universalizability test, and afterward two people in my writing group (not me, I just listened and typed notes on my phone like a creep) got into a long side-argument about whether her reasoning actually holds up. It went on for a while so I've got most of it.

Nadia said Marisol's maxim is fine as stated: "I will break a promise of silence when keeping it would let serious, avoidable harm happen, and only then." Universalize that, Nadia said, and promising in general doesn't collapse, it just gets one narrow exception, so the test is satisfied and Marisol's in the clear. Theo said that's a dodge: you can rescue literally any maxim from the test by tacking on enough qualifiers until it only describes your own case, and if thats allowed the test can never forbid anything, which can't be what Kant was going for. Nadia came back that leaving the reason out isn't more honest, its less honest -- a maxim is supposed to state what you're actually doing and why, and "I will lie" full stop just isn't an accurate description of Marisol's actual policy. Theo said sure, but then no self-interested liar is ever caught by the test either, since they all sincerely believe their case clears whatever bar they name -- so the test only works if the exception clause is checkable by something other than the agent's own say-so, and nothing in how Nadia's stated the maxim provides that. Nadia's last word was that this is a real problem, but it's a problem about how to specify the test correctly, not a problem with universalizability as a method -- and Theo never actually produced a fixed-up version that avoids it.

Could you go through their exchange move by move and say who's got the better of it at each point, not just who "wins" overall? And then tell me what you think the actual answer is here -- is there a principled way to write the universalizability test so it blocks Theo's self-interested liar without also blocking Marisol, or does the specification problem run deeper than either of them realize?
