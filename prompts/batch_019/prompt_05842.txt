Something's been rattling around in my head since a dinner party a couple weeks back, and my colleagues in the department have been useless about it ("not really my area, sorry," from three different people so far). A friend of a friend, a genuinely well-read guy, was holding forth about how that big literary novel everyone was talking about last year is "the best thing published this decade." When I asked what got him about it, it turned out he'd stalled out around page fifty, but four or five critics whose taste he trusts, plus most of Book Twitter, had called it a masterpiece, so that became his verdict too, and he's been repeating it at parties for like a year now. I don't have a problem with using reviews to decide what to read or where to eat, thats just practical, everybody does that. What's bugging me is the next move, where he's not just picking the book because of the reviews, he's asserting that it's a masterpiece, full stop, like that's his own settled judgment of the thing and not a report of what other people think. Someone else at the table said this is no different from me telling people my cholesterol is fine because my doctor says so, even though I've never looked at the bloodwork myself, and I didn't have a snappy comeback ready, but it still felt different to me in a way I couldn't pin down that night.

I teach intro epistemology, and normally testimony cases are the easy unit: somebody tells you something, you had no independent access, you believe it anyway, and under ordinary conditions that's just knowledge, no big drama, next slide. But this case keeps refusing to fit that template, and now that I've started noticing it I see it everywhere, people calling an album "a masterpiece" off one Pitchfork score, or a restaurant "transcendent" off a listicle, with nothing of their own doing any of the work. So here's what I actually want from you: is there a principled difference between the cholesterol case and the novel case, or is my discomfort just snobbery with a philosophy degree behind it? And if there is a real difference, what exactly has my friend-of-a-friend failed to get, does he not actually know the book is good, or does he know it and is missing something else besides, something we'd still want him to have?
