I spent thirty-five years doing experimental physics before I retired, and lately I've fallen down a modal metaphysics rabbit hole -- specifically the fight between David Lewis's counterpart theory and the 'genuine transworld identity' camp. My first thought was that this is basically many-worlds with a new coat of paint, but the more I read the more I realize thats not it at all -- the actual disagreement turns on something oddly specific, which is Kripke's complaint that if 'Humphrey' wins the election in some other possible world, on Lewis's view that isn't really a claim about Humphrey, its a claim about some other guy who merely resembles him, so it tells us nothing about what could actually have happened to Humphrey himself. Maybe its because I'm at the age where you start thinking about roads not taken, but I'd love a short story (800-1500 words) where a character runs headlong into that exact distinction -- not a generic parallel-universe romp, but one where the plot genuinely turns on whether some 'other version' of them in a possible world is literally them, so their guilt or regret actually transfers, or merely a similar counterpart, so it doesn't. Bonus points if you can work in the fact that the counterpart relation is just similarity and is context-dependent, so it doesn't even have to pick out one unique double.
