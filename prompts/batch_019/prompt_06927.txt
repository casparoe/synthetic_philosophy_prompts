so this is gonna sound weird but I've been low-key obsessed with the "why is there something rather than nothing" question since I was like 19 (I'm a bartender, no college, so don't expect any fancy philosophy background from me lol). it started as one of those 2am stoner thoughts but it never really left me, and over the years i've gone down a bunch of rabbit holes reading stuff and watching youtube — Jim Holt's book was a big one, and I burned through a ton of those "Closer to Truth" interview clips where that host guy asks like fifty different philosophers and physicists the same question and they all give totally different answers.

anyway somewhere along the way I started keeping a running list in my notes app of all the different "answers" i've run into, because I noticed I have pretty strong gut reactions to some of them — some feel like they're actually trying to solve the puzzle, and some feel like total cop-outs that just relabel the mystery instead of actually touching it. here's my list so far (heads up, I might be butchering some of the names/terms, this is all self-taught):

- the classic "God is the one necessary being that doesn't need a further cause, everything else does" answer (cosmological argument stuff)
- "it's just a brute fact, there IS no further reason, stop asking" — pretty sure this is basically what Russell said in that old radio debate with the priest guy
- the idea that the universe exists because its existing is somehow required by "goodness"/value itself (i've seen this called axiarchism, some guy named Leslie came up with it i think)
- "literally every possible universe exists, including empty ones, so there's nothing special about ours needing an explanation" (I think this is Nozick's move, kind of the same neighborhood as that "modal realism" thing)
- this weird move where you imagine deleting every object in the universe one at a time to show an empty universe was always a real possibility, so "nothing" isn't as impossible as people assume (the "subtraction argument," maybe a guy named Baldwin?)
- the answer that says the whole question is rigged from the start, because we're just assuming "nothing" is the natural default that doesn't need explaining while "something" does — drop that assumption and the mystery mostly deflates (pretty sure this is Grünbaum's whole thing)
- Parfit's very long essay where he basically gropes around for some deep "selector" principle (fullness? simplicity? goodness?) that could pick out which possibility gets to be the real one, without ever fully landing on an answer
- the straight up physics answer — universe tunneling out of a quantum vacuum, or Hawking's "no boundary" thing where there's no starting point to even explain in the first place
- "the question is just meaningless/confused, kind of like asking what's north of the north pole"
- Heidegger's move of treating it as THE basic question of all metaphysics, but insisting you can't answer it the normal "because X" way science answers things, since any "because" already assumes something exists to do the explaining
