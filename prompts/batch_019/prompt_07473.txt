okay so for my Phil of Science seminar we've spent the last two weeks on laws of nature, and this week was specifically the "quantum entanglement is a problem for Humean Supervenience" debate, which honestly broke my brain a little. Basic setup as I have it in my notes: Lewis's Humeanism says laws are just whatever regularities in the total "mosaic" of local matters of particular fact come out as the best system (simplest + strongest summary of the pattern) — no necessary connections between distinct existences, just Hume's old point dressed up as a metaphysical thesis about the whole world. Anti-Humeans like Armstrong want laws to be necessitation relations between universals, and Maudlin goes further and just says laws are primitive, ungrounded governing facts. Maudlin's specific argument against the Humean side is that entangled particles have correlations that can't be cashed out as intrinsic local properties plus spatiotemporal relations between them, so seperability (which the "mosaic" picture needs) is just false as a matter of physics. Apparently Lewis's actual response to this in "Humean Supervenience Debugged" is kind of just a shrug — something like "so much the worse for quantum mechanics" (I'm paraphrasing, don't quote me) plus some hand-waving about wavefunctions in configuration space still technically counting as "local."

Here's what's been bugging me since class though, and what I want your take on: what would Hume HIMSELF think about this, as opposed to a modern Lewisian "Humean"? Hume's actual project seems way more epistemic/skeptical than Lewis's — he's not asserting a positive metaphysical thesis that the mosaic is the whole truth about mind-independent reality, he's making a point about what we can ever be entitled to infer (no impression of necessary connexion, causation reduces to constant conjunction + habit of expectation). So would Hume even feel obligated to defend "his" supervenience thesis against Bell-type correlations, or would he think that's not really his fight since he never signed up for the full package (mind-independent mosaics, possible worlds, etc) that got built on top of his name later? Would his empiricism make him suspicious of inferring spooky nonlocal structure from Bell violations in the first place, or would he be totally fine just adding entanglement to the pile of brute regularities, since for him even garden-variety billiard-ball causation already had zero hidden necessity behind it? Basically: reasoning from what Hume actually committed himself to (not just what people citing him now believe), whose side is he really on here, and is there something a little awkward for contemporary Humeans in how much extra unHumean baggage (modal realism etc) they've had to attach to his name to even run this debate?
