So I went down a rabbit hole this week because of all the AI chatbot news lately, and ended up actually reading Turing's original 1950 paper, the one where he comes up with the "imitation game" that everyone calls the Turing test now. I wasn't expecting much beyond the usual setup about fooling a judge into thinking a machine is human, but near the end there's a whole section where he runs through objections to his own idea, and one of them is literally about ESP, telepathy and clairvoyance and all that. He doesnt just mention it and brush it off either, he actually writes that the statistical evidence for telepathy specifically is "overwhelming," and that it's inconvenient for our normal scientific worldview but we basically have to accept it anyway. Then he goes on to seriously propose that you'd need something like a "telepathy proof room" for the imitation game to actually be valid, because otherwise a human contestant could just read the interrogator's mind and cheat their way through.

This kind of broke my brain a little, not gonna lie. Turing isn't some random crank, he's basically the founding father of computer science, cracked Nazi codes, one of the most rigorous logical minds of the last century. If a guy like that looked at whatever data he had access to back then and concluded the case for telepathy was overwhelming, doesn't that carry a lot more weight than some youtuber today just going "ESP isn't real, everybody knows that"? It's not some obscure misquote either, it's sitting right there in the actual published paper. But whenever the Turing test comes up in documentaries or pop science articles, this part never gets brought up, like it's an embarrassing bit everybody quietly agreed to skip over. I brought this up with my friend and he said I was reading way too much into some random throwaway aside, but when I pushed him on what exactly Turing got wrong, he couldnt really say, just that he probably was wrong somehow. So genuinely, was Turing's reasoning here actually solid given what he had in front of him, and should it be shifting how confident we are today that telepathy is pseudoscience? Or is there something about this whole episode that both of us are missing?
