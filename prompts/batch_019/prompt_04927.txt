Something happened in my friend group over spring break that turned into a real argument at lunch this week, and I still haven't figured out how to settle it.

My friend Ana had promised another friend, Jonah, that she'd water his plants and grab his mail while he was away for two weeks. She never went a single time. It only worked out because Jonah's aunt, without telling anyone, decided on her own to check on the house and take care of everything; the plants were fine, the mail was fine, and Jonah never learned that Ana hadn't kept her word, since the aunt never mentioned it to him either. Ana only admitted the truth to a few of us, in confidence.

When the story came out at lunch, two friends who had nothing to do with any of it got into an argument about whether Ana had actually done anything wrong:

(1) Maya said no: nobody was harmed and nobody's expectations were disappointed, since Jonah never found out, so there's nothing left to blame Ana for.

(2) Chris disagreed: Ana wronged Jonah the moment she blew off her word, regardless of how things happened to turn out. That, he said, is exactly what separates a promise from an ordinary prediction about what you'll probably do.

(3) Maya answered that if the wrong doesn't depend on any actual cost to anyone, then "I promise" is just a magic phrase that can turn a normally trivial omission into a moral violation out of nowhere. She added that Jonah clearly hadn't been relying on Ana's word alone anyway, since his aunt had quietly been checking the whole time.

(4) Chris replied that Jonah having a backup doesn't show Ana's word carried no weight. People reasonably hedge against the chance that others will flake; needing the hedge shows how fragile a bare promise already is, not that breaking one costs nothing. The real cost, he said, is to the kind of trust that would let people stop needing backups in the first place.

Neither of them budged. Could you go through moves (1)-(4) and say who has the better of it at each stage, rather than just declaring an overall winner? I'm especially curious whether there's some deeper question about why promises bind at all that both of them are talking past. Please close with a one-line verdict in exactly this form: Verdict: Maya / Verdict: Chris / Verdict: split.
