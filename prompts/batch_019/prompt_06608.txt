Gödel's modal ontological argument has become an unexpected preoccupation of mine over the last month. I'm a second-year graduate student in philosophy, and the topic first caught my attention when an instructor mentioned, almost in passing, that the proof had since been fully formalized and checked for validity with an automated theorem prover. That offhand remark sent me down a fairly deep rabbit hole: I worked through Dana Scott's notes on the argument and then through the secondary literature by Sobel, Anderson, and Oppy. I now feel reasonably comfortable with the formal apparatus itself — positive properties, the definition of a God-like being as one possessing every positive property, essences, and necessary existence as the necessary exemplification of one's essence, all set within an S5 framework in which possible necessity collapses into necessity.

What I still haven't managed to internalize is an intuitive grip on the so-called modal collapse objection. I can follow, line by line, how the axioms — particularly the stipulation that positivity is itself a necessary feature of properties — combine with the definitions of essence and necessary existence to entail that every actually true proposition turns out to be necessarily true, so that the argument seems to abolish contingency altogether rather than merely proving that God exists. But I can't yet explain, in plain language and without leaning on the symbolism, where exactly contingency gets smuggled out of the system, or why this particular axiom, rather than some other premise one might have suspected (the closure condition on positive properties, say, or the claim that being God-like is itself positive), turns out to be doing the damage. I'd also like to understand whether Anderson's proposed emendation, which revises Gödel's definitions around essence to block the derivation, actually neutralizes whatever mechanism you identify, or whether it just relocates the same problem one level up.

I'm presenting on this in a seminar shortly and would much rather understand the mechanics than simply cite modal collapse as a well-known objection. Could you walk me through, as intuitively as possible and without a full formal derivation, why Gödel's axioms force this collapse, and offer your own assessment of whether it's a repairable defect of one particular formulation or something closer to an unavoidable cost of trying to secure God's existence through essences and necessary existence in the first place?
