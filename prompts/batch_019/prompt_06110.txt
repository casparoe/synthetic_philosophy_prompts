I'm a junior on my high school's student council, and this year I ended up in charge of running the vote for our Spring Formal theme, partly because I had just written a current-events report on ranked-choice voting after Maine and Alaska adopted it, and I convinced the rest of the committee that ranking our three finalist themes — "Midnight in Paris," "Under the Sea," and "Retro Arcade" — would be fairer than simply picking whichever got the most first-choice votes. Almost 500 students turned in ranked ballots, and out of curiosity I also tallied every possible head-to-head matchup between two themes at a time. Now I'm not sure "fair" means what I thought it meant: 54% of voters preferred Paris to Arcade, 52% preferred Arcade to Under the Sea, and 51% preferred Under the Sea to Paris — a perfect cycle, so that every theme loses to some other theme once you compare them one-on-one. Paris also happens to have the most first-choice votes outright (38%, against 35% for Under the Sea and 27% for Arcade), which only makes the disagreement more confusing rather than less.

The committee meets again this Monday, decorating and catering deposits are due Friday, and rumors are already circulating in our grade's group chat that the vote must be rigged, since it seems like any of the three themes could be presented as either the clear winner or the clear loser depending on how the ballots get counted. Our faculty advisor wants two things out of Monday's meeting: a decision on this year's theme, and a written counting procedure for future student councils to use, so that the same fight doesn't happen every spring. A few committee members want to just revert to plain first-choice plurality — most first-choice votes wins — arguing that it's simple, familiar, and can't plausibly be accused of being cherry-picked to produce a particular winner; others point out that this throws away information the students gave us when they ranked all three themes, and isn't obviously the fairer method just because it's the simplest one.

Could you draft the short memo I'll bring to Monday's meeting? It should recommend a specific theme for this year and a specific standing counting procedure for future councils, explained in terms a skeptical classmate could actually follow, and it should take the case for sticking with plain first-choice plurality seriously rather than waving it away, even if your final recommendation is to adopt something else.
