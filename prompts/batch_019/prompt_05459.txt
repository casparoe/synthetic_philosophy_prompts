Write a dialogue between Simone Weil and a defender of party democracy on whether political parties should exist at all -- maybe set it as testimony to some commission drafting a new democratic constitution, or whatever framing feels natural. The other side should be making something like the "responsible party government" case -- think Schattschneider's line that modern democracy basically can't function without parties. Weil should make her actual case from her wartime essay on suppressing political parties: that a party is essentially a machine for manufacturing collective passion, that it pressures members into fitting thier opinions to the party line instead of reasoning things out themselves, and that a party's built-in goal is really just its own unlimited growth. She should insist this problem shows up just as much in a healthy multiparty system as in a one-party state, not only under authoritarianism. Have the other side actually engage with that "collective pressure on individual judgment" charge specifically instead of just reciting the standard case for parties as vehicles of accountability and coherent governing, and have Weil respond to whether her alternative isn't secretly just parties under another name (factions, movements, whatever). Keep both sides equally sharp and give real back and forth, don't strawman either one.
