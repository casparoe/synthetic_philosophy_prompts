A couple of friends of mine got into an argument over dinner a few weeks ago about split-brain patients -- the ones who had their corpus callosum (the big fiber bundle connecting the two brain hemispheres) cut, usually to control severe epilepsy -- and I still can't decide who came out ahead. It started because we'd watched an old clip where a patient's two hands seem to work against each other, like one hand buttons a shirt while the other undoes it. (1) My friend Nadia, who's in a clinical neuropsych program, said this basically settles it: these patients have two seperate minds in one skull, two genuine centers of consciousness sharing a body, since each hemisphere can see, want, and act on stuff the other has zero access to. (2) Her friend Owen, who did a philosophy MA, said that's way too quick -- plenty of processing in an intact brain is modular and walled off too (blindsight, subliminal priming, stuff like that) and nobody concludes there's a second person hiding in there, so disunity of 'information' can't by itself get you a disunity of 'subject.' (3) Nadia answered that it's a difference of degree that becomes a difference in kind: a stray unconscious module doesn't perceive a whole novel scene, form a goal based on it, and carry out a flexible intelligent action on that basis, but a disconnected hemisphere does exactly that, and that just is what having a point of view amounts to. (4) Owen then brought up something he half-remembered from a psych class, that later testing on these same patients found odd bits of cross-integration that shouldn't be possible if the two sides were cleanly cut off, and that not one split-brain patient has ever reported feeling like two people or feeling divided. (5) Nadia said that proves nothing, since only the language-dominant hemisphere can talk at all, so of course you'd never hear the other side's version even if it's fully conscious -- silence from a witness who's been struck mute isn't evidence the witness doesn't exist. (6) Owen shot back that this is suspiciously convenient: if literally no verbal report from these people could ever count against the two-minds view, then the view has quietly stopped being something you could find out was false.

Neither of them gave an inch, and honestly I was too out of my depth to referee it myself. Could you go through the exchange move by move and say who's actually got the better of it at each numbered step, rather than just handing down an overall winner? I'd also love it if you flagged anywhere Nadia and Owen seem to be using words like 'mind' or 'unity of consciousness' to mean subtly different things without noticing it, and told me whether Owen's unfalsifiability charge in (6) really lands, or whether Nadia has a principled comeback available to her.
