I recently took over as the visual-communication designer for a science museum's new permanent exhibit on global inequality, which needs to be legible to visitors regardless of which language they read, and this has led me into an extended review of the history of pictorial statistics. That research led me straight to Otto Neurath's Isotype system, and I was struck by how closely our brief echoes his original ambition at the museum of social and economic statistics he directed in Vienna: to let ordinary people grasp complex statistical facts through repeated, standardized icons rather than words, in a form he explicitly pitched as more neutral and universal than any national language could be. The more I've read about Neurath the philosopher, though, the harder it has become to take that "neutral and universal" framing at face value, since this is the same Neurath who, in his exchanges with Carnap and Schlick over the status of protocol sentences, denied that any statement — visual or verbal — could function as a neutral, foundational given, and who compared the growth of knowledge to sailors forced to rebuild their ship plank by plank on the open sea, with no dry dock available where they might start again from scratch.

That juxtaposition has been bothering me enough that I would like help thinking it through properly, rather than settling for whichever reading of Neurath's design work happens to be most flattering. Please make the strongest case you can for the position that Neurath's own anti-foundationalist, coherentist epistemology actually undermines his stated ambitions for Isotype as a neutral, universal picture language, rather than sitting alongside them as a separate, unrelated strand of his career. Engage with the real substance on both sides: his specific claims about what repeated icon-based pictograms could achieve that verbal statistics could not, and his specific arguments against any class of statements being epistemically privileged or theory-independent. I would rather you press the strongest version of the tension than dissolve it with the generic observation that "all representation is theory-laden."
