So my neighbor Priya is finally getting sworn in as a US citizen next month, and she's asked me to say a few words at the backyard party afterward (the actual oath ceremony is a federal thing with a judge presiding, this is just the family-and-friends toast after). Here's my problem: I'm a philosophy professor and most of my research is on the boundary problem -- the fact that nobody has ever worked out a non-circular, principled way to say who should count as part of a demos in the first place, since whatever procedure you'd use to draw that line already has to presuppose some prior group entitled to draw it. Whelan pointed this out decades ago, Dahl basically admits democratic theory can't settle it from the inside, and there's newer stuff, mainly Abizadeh, arguing that the whole idea of a nation as a natural pre-political unit just waiting to be handed a state is incoherent for exactly the same reason. I definitly believe most of this. Which makes writing a warm, sincere toast about Priya "officially becoming one of us" weirdly hard, because the honest version of my views says that "us" was never drawn on principled grounds to begin with -- the line she's crossing is closer to a historical accident we've all agreed to keep enforcing than some natural kind she's now correctly sorted into.

I don't want to be a downer or turn this into a guest lecture at her own party, obviously, and most of the room is going to be her family, her book club, and my spouse, who will rightfully murder me if I make this weird. But two people from my department are going to be standing right there, and I'd feel like a fraud doing the standard "land of opportunity, welcome home" toast and pretending I don't think the boundary's arbitrary. Can you write the actual toast for me? Something like 4 minutes read aloud, genuinely warm and a little funny, that works fine for the non-academics in the room but that a democratic theorist listening closely would recognize as quietly built around the real problem instead of dodging it. Extra credit if you can make the arbitrariness of the line feel like part of what's moving about the occasion rather than something that undercuts it, but I'll leave it to you to figure out if thats actually possible or if I'm just asking for a square circle.
