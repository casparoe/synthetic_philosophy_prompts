I'm a novelist and sit on the board of a small nonprofit press that publishes literature in translation — the kind of house that first brought my own work out abroad. We've been offered a three-year grant from a foundation tied to the cultural ministry of a government notorious for jailing its own writers, earmarked specifically to translate and publish the very writers it has silenced — work we could otherwise never afford to do justice to. Our director calls this a clean case of 'moral trade': each side sacrifices something it values less to secure something it values more, without either endorsing the other's conduct. Two board members call it reputation-laundering and want us to decline outright. Please draft the memo I'll bring to next month's vote: a specific recommendation — accept, decline, or accept with named conditions — and reasoning solid enough that whichever side loses would recognize its concern was genuinely engaged, not waved away.
