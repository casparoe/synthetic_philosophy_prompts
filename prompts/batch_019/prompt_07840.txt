When critics call a resource bound like 'polynomial time' an engineering detail rather than a philosophical one, her stock reply is some version of 'the engineering is the argument.' Draft eight to ten interview questions that make that reply progressively harder to fall back on: at least one on whether requiring the map from physical states to computational states to be efficiently computable really earns its keep against the standard argument that a rock can be seen as implementing any program you like; one on her reading of Gödel's 1956 letter to von Neumann as an early statement of the P versus NP question, or whether that's hindsight talking; and one on what a literal giant-lookup-table version of the Chinese Room shows once you count the bits it would need. For each, add a follow-up keyed to the deflection she's likely to reach for, and close with a question about what result would convince her that complexity considerations are philosophically idle after all.
