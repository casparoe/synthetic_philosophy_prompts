The instructor's point was that popular expositions tend to speak of "the argument" for the thesis as though there were only one, when the literature (including this week's readings, which touched on Gandy's 1980 paper and several chapters from the volume *Computability: Turing, Gödel, Church, and Beyond*) actually contains quite distinct lines of justification, resting on conceptual analysis, mathematical convergence, physical constraints on mechanisms, historical track record, and outright stipulation, respectively.
