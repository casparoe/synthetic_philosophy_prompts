During my career as a theoretical physicist, decision theory entered my thinking only through game theory and the foundations of quantum mechanics. Since retiring, I've been reading philosophy in earnest, and I recently came across the notion of "acausal trade" from the literature on Newcomblike problems: the proposal that two agents who can never causally influence one another -- because, say, they are separated by a cosmological horizon in an infinite universe, or because they inhabit branches of the wavefunction that decohered from one another long ago -- can nonetheless have genuine reason to cooperate, provided each can reliably predict the other's reasoning and each knows itself to be so predicted. Please describe the most important implications of taking acausal trade seriously for how I ought to conduct myself now. I'm especially interested in whether it gives me any real additional reason, beyond ordinary ethics, to act well even where nothing and no one -- including any causally inaccessible version of myself -- could ever detect or reward it, or whether the whole idea just dresses up an old answer to Gyges' ring in decision-theoretic clothing.
