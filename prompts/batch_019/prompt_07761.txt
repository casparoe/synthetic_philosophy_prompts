I am currently taking a graduate seminar on epistemology, and over the past two weeks we have moved from Plato's Meno to the contemporary literature on what our professor calls "the value problem." In the Meno, Socrates argues that a true opinion is just as useful a guide to action as knowledge for as long as it lasts, but that it lacks knowledge's stability: true opinions are compared to the animated statues of Daedalus, which are so lifelike that they wander off unless someone ties them down, and what ties a true opinion down into knowledge is supposed to be something like working out an account of the reason why it is true. We then read Linda Zagzebski's argument that reliabilism cannot explain this extra value: if a reliable process is only instrumentally valuable as a means to true belief, then once the true belief is already in hand, the reliability of the process that produced it is "swamped" and adds nothing further -- she illustrates the point with the thought that it should not matter whether the machine that made your coffee is generally reliable, if the cup in front of you is already good. Zagzebski's own proposed replacement is a credit theory of knowledge, on which knowledge is true belief that arises from, and is creditable to, the exercise of intellectual virtue or ability, in the way a courageous act is more creditable than a merely lucky good outcome.

At the end of the last session, our professor remarked, somewhat in passing, that Zagzebski's credit theory is "really just Plato's tethering story dressed up in the vocabulary of virtue epistemology," and then moved on before anyone could push back. I would like to examine that claim properly for my term paper. Could you compare Plato's solution to the value problem with Zagzebski's credit theory in some depth: do they actually agree about what has to be added to true belief to make it valuable in the further way that knowledge is, and does Zagzebski's own swamping argument have the same force against Plato's account that it has against reliabilism? Finally, please say which of the two solutions comes closer to genuinely explaining why knowledge is more valuable than mere true belief, and justify your answer.
