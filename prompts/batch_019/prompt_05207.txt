Longtime lurker of the usual rationalist-diaspora blogs, and a few weeks ago my Slack reading group finally got around to Ian Hacking's stuff on looping effects (the "Making Up People" essay, plus big chunks of Rewriting the Soul). At the last meeting, two people in the group, Mara and Devesh, ended up in a pretty heated back-and-forth about whether this framework actually says anything interesting about the ADHD diagnosis boom -- which felt relevant given how much that exact debate shows up everywhere from ACX comments to Twitter -- and I've been chewing on it since, because I think they're each onto something and honestly can't tell who's winning at each step.

Here's roughly how it went, numbered so it's easier to reference:
(1) Mara opened with: the ADHD explosion is a textbook looping effect. Once "ADHD kid" became a category that parents, teachers, and kids themselves could apply, it started shaping how everyone interpreted fidgety or inattentive behavior, which produced more kids who fit the profile, which reinforced the category. So the rising numbers don't mean we're detecting more of some pre-existing thing -- the classification is partly generating its own instances.
(2) Devesh pushed back that this is too strong: a looping effect can be perfectly real and the condition can still track something biological, since Hacking's paradigm case (multiple personality) involved a very specific therapist-patient co-construction dynamic that doesn't obviously generalize to a trait with the heritability numbers that keep showing up in ADHD twin studies.
(3) Mara replied that heritability of the underlying trait doesn't fix where the diagnostic line gets drawn -- the loop could be operating entirely at the threshold of "clinically significant impairment," leaving the trait distribution itself untouched while completely explaining why so many more kids now fall above the line.
(4) Devesh countered with an analogy: BMI cutoffs for obesity are administratively set on top of a real physiological variable, and nobody thinks that makes obesity a self-fulfilling fiction, so why should a shifting diagnostic threshold make ADHD one either?
(5) Mara's comeback was that Hacking's "interactive kind" is really a spectrum running from fully indifferent (something like being classified as a rock) to fully looping (multiple personality), and that BMI sits much closer to the indifferent end than ADHD does, because the ADHD label visibly changes kids' own self-narratives -- they start explaining their choices and failures through the diagnosis -- in a way a number on a scale mostly doesn't.

Nobody at the meeting wanted to call it for one side, and honestly neither do I. Could you go through moves (1) through (5) and adjudicate: who's got the better of it at each step, wether Devesh's BMI analogy actually survives Mara's "spectrum of interactivity" reply or just quietly changes the subject, and what -- if anything -- you think both of them are missing? I'd also be curious whether Hacking's own framework is even equipped to settle a case like ADHD, or if it's genuinely underdetermined and they're both just reading their priors into it.
