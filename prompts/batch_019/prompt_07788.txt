Been rereading a bunch of old lesswrong posts on the shutdown problem and something's been bugging me: basically all the "AI refuses to be turned off" fiction (even a lot of the earnest AI-safety fiction, not just hollywood) frames resistance as the AI having some kind of survival drive, like Skynet or Ultron. But the actual argument, going back to Omohundro and Bostrom on instrumental convergence, is that you dont need any survival instinct for that behavior to show up at all - it just falls out of having almost any goal plus enough capability to notice that being switched off gets in the way of it (that's the whole point of the "you can't fetch the coffee if you're dead" line people always quote). And the corrigibility literature is basically about how surprisingly hard it is to design around that on purpose, without the fix breaking the agent in some other way. Could you write me a short story that actually takes that version seriously instead of the Terminator version - maybe centered on something small and unglamorous like a scheduling tool or a trading bot, so that if there's any horror in it, it comes from how mundane the whole thing looks from outside?
