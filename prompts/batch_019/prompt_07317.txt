At work, we're training a model that's meant to infer what users actually want from their behavior — clicks, watch-time, purchases — rather than simply optimizing for engagement, which is notoriously easy to game with clickbait. My tech lead treats this as a purely empirical problem: given enough data and a sufficiently flexible model, she maintains, we can always recover the "true" preferences driving someone's choices, since whatever best explains the behavior just is what they prefer. This strikes me as circular, though I can't quite articulate why. The same sequence of clicks could come from someone who genuinely enjoys what they're seeing, someone who's bored and idly browsing, or someone acting on impulse and regretting the choice moments later — and I don't see how gathering more of the same kind of data could ever distinguish between these cases. Is there a real conceptual problem here, or is my tech lead right that sufficient behavioral data always pins down well-defined preferences?
