So this happened at my local ACX meetup last month and its been bugging me since. Someone brought up the classic money-pump argument for coherence: if your preferences aren't representable as maximizing some expected-utility function, someone can string together a series of trades that leaves you strictly worse off, so any agent smart enough to notice this pattern in itself has reason to become coherent -- which is a big chunk of why people expect advanced AI to end up with convergent instrumental goals like resource acquisition and self-preservation. Two regulars, Theo and Ilana, argued about it for like half an hour and neither one backed down. Roughly, in order:

(1) Theo: the money-pump argument is basically decisive. Any agent whose preferences violate transitivity or completeness can be exploited out of resources, so a sufficiently smart agent will iron that out of itself. That's the whole basis for expecting advanced AI to behave like it has a coherent goal.

(2) Ilana: but the theorem only guarantees that *some* utility function rationalizes your choices once you're coherent, and that's way weaker than people treat it as. Literally any fixed behavior -- even a thermostat's -- can be redescribed as maximizing a trivial utility function that just rates whatever it was already going to do as best. So "coherent" doesn't get you "has a real goal over future states of the world"; that part is smuggled in separately.

(3) Theo: fair objection to a dumb, fixed behavior, but it doesn't touch the case that matters, which is an agent that already has to trade off resources and time to do its job. For that kind of agent incoherence really is exploitable, so there's genuine pressure toward coherence as it gets smarter and starts noticing the exploitable patterns in itself -- it's not just a relabeling after the fact.

(4) Ilana: that just relocates the question -- you still need an independent argument for why capable agents end up with broad, temporally-extended preferences in the first place, and coherence doesn't supply that. And even granting broad preferences, "must become fully coherent or get pumped" assumes your preferences are complete. An agent with merely incomplete preferences can dodge the standard pump just by refusing any trade that isn't a strict improvement, so I don't think "smart enough" gets you all the way to "basically an EU-maximizer."
