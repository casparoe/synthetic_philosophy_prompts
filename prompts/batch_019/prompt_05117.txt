I'm running our department's graduate pragmatism seminar this term, and this week we paired Peirce's 1878 "How to Make Our Ideas Clear" with Lecture VI of James's 1907 Pragmatism, "Pragmatism's Conception of Truth." One of my sharper students cornered me after class on Tuesday with a question that's been nagging at me since, mostly becuase I don't think I answered it well on the spot. Her claim, roughly, was this: James just took Peirce's pragmatic maxim -- meaning is cashed out in terms of practical, experiential upshot -- and pushed it one step further, from a theory of meaning into a full theory of truth, and since Peirce signed off on that extension as basically the natural next move for his own semiotics, there's no real substance to treating "the final opinion fated to be agreed upon by all who investigate" (Peirce) and "the true is whatever proves its worth by working in experience" (James) as two different theories. On her reading they're the same insight in two dialects, one logical and one psychological, and the standard move of contrasting them is more about vibes and reputation (Peirce the rigorous logician, James the loose popularizer) than about actual doctrinal daylight between them.

I'd like to set her straight next week if she's off base, but honestly I want to make sure I'm not the confused one here -- a couple of the editorial intros in the anthology we're using kind of wave their hands right at this point instead of settling it, which isn't helping me prep. So: is she basically right that Peirce endorsed James's extension of the maxim into a theory of truth, making the two views continuous, or is there a real disagreement underneath the shared vocabulary (and if so, where exactly does it bite -- realism vs. something more instrumentalist, the individual believer vs. the community of inquirers, the "final opinion" as an ideal limit versus an actual episode of verification, or something else entirely)? Happy to be told I'm overcomplicating a genuine convergence, but my hunch is that the "Peirce signed off on it" part is exactly where this goes wrong.
