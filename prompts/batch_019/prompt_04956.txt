ok so this got personally relevant fast: my partner just joined the chorus at our local opera company, so now i go to basically everything, and this same semester i'm taking a night class on aesthetics where we got assigned a couple of Bernard Williams essays on opera from that "On Opera" collection of his. last week a few of us from the class went to see Cosi fan tutte together and it turned into a whole argument at the bar afterward that i haven't been able to shake since.

(1) Jae said the opera is basically dressed-up cruelty: the guys bet on their fiancees' fidelity, prove the women will fall for literally anyone in a disguise within a day, humiliate them for it, then everyone pairs back up and sings a cheerful finale like nothing happened -- so whatever point its making, it's made at the women's expense and free for the men.

(2) Priya pushed back with the Williams reading: his point about this opera, she said, is that treating the ending as either a cynical put-down or a sentimental return to true love both flatten it -- the opera is staging that tension on purpose, not resolving it, which is more honest than picking a side.

(3) Jae said "staging a tension" is a nice way of saying the guys never apologize, and that calling the ambiguity sophisticated is exactly how you launder content that would get someone side-eyed if they said it out loud at dinner.

(4) Priya said you can't read the plot like a realist morality play to begin with, because the whole form is already stylized past that point -- people singing oaths of eternal devotion mid-disguise -- so judging whether it "endorses" cruelty misreads what kind of thing it even is.

(5) Jae said fine, but the finale is major-key and bouncy and unmistakably comic, so whatever kind of thing it is, it's telling the audience to laugh along, which is the zero-cost part she's complaining about in the first place.

i was just the ride home, so i stayed out of it. can you go through this move by move and say who's got the better of each point, and what -- if anything -- you think they're both missing?
