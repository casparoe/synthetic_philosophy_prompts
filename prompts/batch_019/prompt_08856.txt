A friend and I recently had a long argument about mechanism in the philosophy of mind. I argued that for any consistent formal system F powerful enough to express basic arithmetic, Gödel's first incompleteness theorem yields a sentence G_F that F cannot prove but that we can recognize, from outside F, to be true. When my friend objected that we can't actually be sure our own reasoning is consistent, I pointed out that this shouldn't matter: whichever formal system might be proposed as a model of a person's mathematical reasoning, Gödel's theorem still lets us exhibit a truth that system cannot reach. Since the Lucas–Penrose argument therefore shows that minds cannot be equivalent to Turing machines, why do so many logicians and philosophers of mind still treat computationalism as a serious, open possibility?
