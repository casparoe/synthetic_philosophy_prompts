I spent close to forty years doing population-genetic fieldwork on social insects. I still remember the storm that broke out in 2010, when Nowak, Tarnita, and Wilson published a Nature paper arguing that inclusive fitness theory was mathematically dispensable and could be replaced by a more direct, group-structured accounting of fitness; within the year, well over a hundred colleagues had co-signed a published rebuttal in defense of Hamilton's rule. At the time I filed the whole affair away as a turf dispute over which bookkeeping method was more convenient, and went back to counting larvae. Now that I am retired and have joined a small philosophy-of-science reading group at the local university, I have been working through Sober and Wilson's Unto Others, Okasha's Evolution and the Levels of Selection, and the Kerr and Godfrey-Smith paper contrasting individualist and multi-level perspectives on selection — and I have realized that this "bookkeeping" framing is exactly the philosophical question I never had to settle as a working scientist, and that I cannot get an intuitive grip on it.

Here is what puzzles me. Kerr and Godfrey-Smith show, convincingly as far as I can tell, that any apparent case of group selection can be redescribed in purely individualist terms, simply by letting each organism's fitness depend on the local group context in which it sits, so that the same numbers fall out of the model however you carve it up. And yet Okasha, having laid out essentially that same equivalence result, still insists on distinguishing MLS1 scenarios — where "group fitness" is just a convenient average over individual fitnesses — from genuine MLS2 scenarios, in which groups themselves undergo birth, death, and heritable fitness differences as individuals in their own right, the way a honeybee colony does when it reproduces by swarming. He seems to think that, in MLS2 cases, the individualist redescription loses something real about the world. What I would like is an explanation, pitched at the level of intuition rather than of Price-equation formalism, of what exactly that "something" is supposed to be — ideally illustrated with a simplified colony-level example, since that is the terrain I know best — and of why a committed individualist could not simply respond that MLS2 is just a more elaborate relabeling too.
