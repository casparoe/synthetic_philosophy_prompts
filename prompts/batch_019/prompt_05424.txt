A few nights ago, a friend and I got into a surprisingly protracted argument over dinner, and I still cannot decide which of us was right, if either. Here is what started it: I had confided a piece of personal news to exactly two people, Priya and Dan, both of whom I explicitly asked not to tell my sister. Somehow, she found out anyway. Since I am certain I never mentioned it to anyone else, and since one of the two of them has to be responsible, I said to my friend, "Well, if Priya didn't tell her, Dan did." My friend nodded along and offered back what she took to be the same thought in different words: "Right — if Priya hadn't told her, Dan would have." That rewording struck me as wrong the moment I heard it, though I couldn't immediately say why. Her version seemed to accuse Dan of something considerably worse than mine did: my sentence simply drew the obvious conclusion from the fact that the news got out and only two people could have let it slip, whereas hers seemed to claim that Dan was, in effect, primed to tell regardless of what Priya did — a far more specific and far less charitable claim about him.

We argued about this for a long time, each of us sure the other was missing something obvious. It is not the first time one of us has accused the other of splitting hairs over phrasing, but neither of us could let this one go. My friend insisted she had merely restated my claim more vividly; I insisted she had smuggled in something new and much less certain. What I cannot get past is that the two sentences are built from exactly the same ingredients — the same two suspects, the same leaked secret, and the same basic if-then structure — with only the mood and tense of the verbs changed, and yet they seem to me capable of coming apart in truth value, with one true and the other false. I would like to know whether that impression is actually correct, and if so, what general principle explains why such a small grammatical shift does that much work. More practically: if you had been sitting at that table, whose reading of the situation would you have sided with, and why?
