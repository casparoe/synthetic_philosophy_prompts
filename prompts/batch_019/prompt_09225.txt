I'm a retired radio astronomer -- spent about thirty five years doing Bayesian parameter estimation on telescope data, where the working rule was always "trust the posterior after the next data release over your current hunch." Since retiring I've fallen in with a little reading group of ex-colleagues who used to argue about instrument calibration and now argue about epistemology instead, and this month we hit Bas van Fraassen's Reflection Principle. As I understand it, the principle says that if your certain right now that your credence in some claim A at a later time will be some specific value r, then coherence requires your credence in A right now to already equal r -- on pain of being Dutch-booked across the two time-slices the same way an incoherent prior gets Dutch-booked in the ordinary synchronic story. When I first read it, it felt like exactly the intuition behind my old working rule, just dressed up formally.

But then one of my reading-group friends (a very annoying man, formerly a solid-state physicist) brought up the obvious nasty case: suppose I know for a fact that next week a cult is going to kidnap me and brainwash me into being 95% confident the moon landing was staged. Reflection seems to say that since I'm already certain my future credence will be 0.95, my credence right now should shift to 0.95 too -- which is obviously wrong, since I currently have great evidence the landing was real and I know perfectly well that the future shift will come from brainwashing rather than evidence. That's made me suspect the whole diachronic Dutch book argument is basically a dud, and honestly it's shaken my confidence in the ordinary synchronic Dutch book argument for probabilism too, since I'd always mentally filed the two as the same kind of argument wearing different clothes. Am I right that this kind of case sinks Reflection pretty much outright, and does it drag the more basic coherence arguments down with it, or is there something about my telescope-data intuition that doesn't actually license the unrestricted principle the way I thought it did?
