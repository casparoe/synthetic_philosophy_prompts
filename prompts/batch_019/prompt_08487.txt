I'm working on a literary novel whose two central characters, Ilse and Daniel, were together briefly in graduate school and have stayed close friends ever since, despite ending up on opposite sides of the same unresolved argument. Ilse, who works in modal metaphysics, is a modal rationalist in the vein of David Chalmers: she holds that ideal, fully rational conceiving of a coherent scenario is strong evidence of its genuine possibility, and she leans on this to defend the zombie argument against physicalism about consciousness. Daniel, who moved into philosophy of neuroscience, is a Type-B physicalist: he thinks the zombie intuition is exactly what you'd expect to have even if physicalism were true, since our phenomenal concepts could be a posteriori tied to physical properties in ways we cannot discover from the armchair, much as "water" turned out to be tied to H2O.

For a scene set on the long drive home from a mutual friend's funeral, I'd like a dialogue between them that earns its length rather than restating the zombie argument in outline. It should get into the real mechanics: Kripke's necessary a posteriori cases (Hesperus and Phosphorus, water and H2O) and what each of them takes those cases to show about the zombie case; Chalmers' distinction between primary and secondary conceivability and possibility, and whether phenomenal concepts are truly the sort of concept for which primary and secondary intension coincide, as Ilse's argument requires; and the phenomenal concept strategy Daniel reaches for to block the inference from conceivability to possibility without simply denying that zombies are conceivable at all. Neither should win by default. These are two people who have had this argument for twenty years and know each other's moves cold, so I want each of them landing real objections against the strongest version of the other's view, conceding points that actually cost something, and the scene ending with the disagreement sharpened rather than resolved. Something in the range of 900 to 1300 words would suit the pacing of the chapter.
