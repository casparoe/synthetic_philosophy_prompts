I've been asked to give the toast at my newspaper's retirement dinner for our science editor, who is stepping down at eighty after fifty years on the job and has spent much of that time citing Cicero's De Senectute to any junior reporter who suggested he slow down. I'd like the toast to engage Cicero's argument on its own terms rather than just gesture at it: work through Cato's four counts against old age -- that it withdraws us from active affairs, weakens the body, deprives us of almost all sensory pleasure, and brings us too near to death -- and his answer to each. But our editor built his career on not letting a well-turned line stand in for a checked fact, so wherever current research on aging actually undercuts one of those specific answers, the toast should say so plainly rather than smoothing it over. Keep it to about five minutes, for a room split evenly between veteran colleagues who know the text and interns who have never heard of it.
