For an independent project extending what I learned in AP Statistics, I've been working through van Fraassen's Judy Benjamin problem, in which a soldier who is equally likely to be in any of four zones receives a radio message stating that, given she is in Red territory, she is three times more likely to be in the second company's zone than the first. Since introductory logic treats 'if A, then B' as the material conditional, I formalized the message by simply eliminating the 'Red and first company' possibility and renormalizing the remaining three zones equally. That isn't how the papers I've found handle the problem, though: they keep all four zones possible, and the overall probability of Red territory itself ends up shifting to somewhat below one-half, even though the message never compares Red to Blue. Is the published treatment overcomplicating something that elementary logic already settles, or is my material-conditional approach missing something important about how this kind of evidence should be incorporated?
