So this happened at my cousin's barbecue last weekend and its been bugging me since. My aunt was talking about two of her neighbors - one guy who's been on permanent disability since a hit and run that was 100% not his fault and needs a home aide now, and another guy who quit a decent job a few years back to try to make it as a musician, it didn't work out, and now he's broke and on and off unemployment. My uncle Dave said obviously the disability guy deserves way more support, since he never chose what happened to him, while the musician guy basically gambled with his own life and it's on him that it didn't pay off. My cousin's husband Ray pushed back and said the whole "he chose it" thing is way shakier than it sounds, because a lot of people who "choose" to chase some big dream were basically raised their whole life to think that was the only real option for them, so how free was that choice really.

Dave said ok but if literally any choice can get explained away like that once you dig into somebody's upbringing, then nothing is ever really a choice, and we'd owe everybody the exact same comfortable life forever no matter what they do with it - like if some guy blows his whole share of money at the casino every single year we'd still owe him just as much as anyone else forever, which cant be right. Ray said no, there's a real difference between a one time gamble and something more like a compulsion the guy never really signed up for and can't just shake off, and anyway, even ignoring the blame question completely, handing two people the exact same pile of money doesn't make them equally well off, since the disability guy needs a lot more of that money just to get around and live anything like a normal life - so maybe arguing over who chose what is missing the real issue, which is that money itself isn't the thing that should be equal in the first place.

Dave shot back that "compulsion vs one time gamble" is exactly as fuzzy as the thing Ray was just complaining about a minute earlier, so now Ray's the one who needs a way to draw that line that isn't totally made up, and whoever gets to decide which cravings count as a "real" compulsion is basically just deciding who they feel sorry for and calling it a principle. Neither of them budged and I honestly don't think either one is fully wrong. Could you go through the whole exchange move by move and say who's actually got the stronger point at each stage, not just hand out an overall winner? And if you think there's some option they're both overlooking that would get them out of this mess, I'd genuinely like to know what it is.
