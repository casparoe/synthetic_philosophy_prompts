Her argument, as I understand it, is that Sidgwick genuinely thought the rational egoist and the utilitarian have no non-question-begging way to settle their dispute unless the universe is morally governed, and since he'd already lost his orthodox faith (he famously resigned his own Trinity fellowship over a religious scruple), psychical research was his attempt to get actual empirical evidence for that kind of moral order rather than just postulating it on faith. As an old lab hand this whole episode fascinates me, because Sidgwick was about as far from a crank as it gets, and yet his own circle caught the Creery sisters using a signal code and later caught Eusapia Palladino cheating outright in Cambridge, and none of it seems to have shaken him loose from the project -- if anything he just kept refining the controls.
