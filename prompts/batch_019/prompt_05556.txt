I spent about thirty-five years running an analytical chemistry lab (a lot of it squinting at spectra and thin-layer plates), and one thing that always nagged at me was how much what I "saw" in a smudgy result seemed to depend on what I already expected or hoped to find. Now that I'm retired I finally have time to read philosophy properly, and it turns out there's a whole technical debate about more or less exactly this, filed under "cognitive penetration of perception" -- basically, can your beliefs, desires, or expectations reach down and change your actual perceptual experience, not just your later judgment about what you experienced? I brought this up at my philosophy meetup group last week and it turned into a two hour arguement that never really got resolved, so now I'm curious what you'll do with it.

From what I've been reading, the case for penetration leans on things like the old Bruner and Postman trick playing-card experiments, the Delk and Fillenbaum finding that a cardboard heart cut-out looks redder than a same-colored non-heart shape, and the newer Witt/Proffitt "action-specific perception" work claiming a softball looks bigger to a batter who's hitting well that day. The skeptics -- Firestone and Scholl's big 2016 Behavioral and Brain Sciences paper seems to be the text everyone argues against these days -- say basically all of this is contaminated by response bias, memory, shifted attention or eye movements, anything except a genuine change in early perceptual processing, and that the encapsulated "early vision" Fodor and Pylyshyn talk about stays stubbornly immune to what you believe.

Could you write me an essay that actually wades into this instead of just landing on "it's probably a bit of both"? Walk through the strongest evidence and arguments on each side as you understand them, then tell me straight: does genuine cognitive penetration of perceptual experience (as opposed to memory, judgment, or attention) actually happen, and if so, roughly where's the line -- which processing stages, which senses, which sources (belief vs. desire vs. emotion) are and aren't susceptible? I'd rather you commit to a real, defensible answer than hedge the whole way through.
