I've been falling down a rabbit hole reading old LessWrong and EA Forum posts about "evidential cooperation in large worlds" (I think the original version, by a guy named Caspar Oesterheld, was called something like multiverse-wide cooperation via correlated decision-making). The pitch as I understand it: reality branches into literally every possible outcome, many-worlds style, so there are countless branches out there with alien civilizations or advanced AIs whose choices correlate with mine even though we can never causally interact - kind of like Hofstadter's superrationality idea, but scaled up to the whole multiverse. And apparently that's supposed to give me an actual reason to act more cooperatively/altruistically right now, since doing so is evidence that my correlated counterparts elsewhere are doing the same for my values.

Thing is, I brought this up with my housemate who's doing a physics phd, and she pointed out that many-worlds is just one interpretation of QM among several live options (Copenhagen, pilot wave, objective collapse, etc), and its definitely not settled science. That kind of deflated me, because it made the whole ECL argument sound like its hostage to a physics debate that's nowhere near resolved. So am I right that the entire case for evidential cooperation collapses if many-worlds turns out to be false, or is there some way the argument survives even if reality doesn't literally branch like that? Basically - am I even understanding what "large worlds" is supposed to mean here in the first place?
