Ok, decision theory nerd-snipe time. I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole on the Death in Damascus puzzle lately -- the Gibbard and Harper case where a guy runs into Death in Damascus, gets a wierd look, panics, and hightails it to Aleppo, except Death was already on its way there because it predicted he'd flee. I feel like I've got Newcomb's problem down pretty solid at this point (yes, I one-box, no I don't want to relitigate it), but Damascus messes with me more, because there's no dominance argument for causal decision theory to lean on -- staying looks bad exactly to the extent fleeing looks good, and vice versa, so you can't just point at one option that wins no matter what. Apparently the standard patch is "ratifiability," whether an act still looks best once you condition on having actually chosen it, except in Damascus neither pure act is self-ratifying and only some mixed strategy is, which feels like a genuinely strange thing for a theory of rational choice to have to say. Meanwhile evidential decision theory just calmly tells you to stay put and save the cab fare to Aleppo, since by its lights your fate's already sealed either way -- stable, at least, but with a fatalism to it that a lot of people find hard to swallow.

I also vaguely remember a paper by Levinstein and Soares, "Cheating Death in Damascus," that tries to use something like FDT-flavored reasoning to wriggle out of the whole setup, and I've seen people disagree about whether that actually resolves the puzzle or just quietly changes the subject. Could you write me an essay that stakes out an actual position instead of just cataloguing the views? Explain why Damascus is supposed to be harder for CDT than plain Newcomb, take the ratifiability/mixed-strategy patch, the EDT answer, and the FDT-ish dissolution all seriously as competitors, and then actually tell me which one (or which combination) gets it right and why the others don't. Don't let it end on a "reasonable people disagree" note -- I want a verdict I could go pick a fight with someone about at a meetup.
