I'm putting together a new unit on Turing for my philosophy of mind seminar, and I went back and reread "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" for probably the tenth time in my life. I'd completely forgotten how much space he gives, near the end, to extrasensory perception as an objection to the possibility of machine thought. He runs through the usual suspects first, the theological objection, Lady Lovelace's, the idea that machines can only do what they're programmed to do and can't originate anything, and swats most of them away pretty quickly and confidently. Then he gets to telepathy and clairvoyance and the tone shifts completely: he basically grants that the statistical evidence of the time was hard to argue away, and starts worrying, in this very dry, deadpan way, that a telepathic human contestant could wreck the whole imitation game, since they'd be picking up information the machine has no way of faking. I definately did not expect a logic paper from 1950 to end up worrying about telepathy.

His proposed patch, if I'm remembering right, is more or less to seal the contestants off in a room shielded against telepathy so the test stays fair. I brought this up with my grad seminar last week and half of them assumed I was making it up. Its been rattling around in my head since, and I'd rather see it turned into fiction than just lecture about it.

Could you write me a short story, a page or two is plenty, built around an imitation-game-style setup where ESP turns out to be genuinely real? Feel free to keep it period appropriate, 1950s Britain, maybe with Turing himself lurking in the background, or drag the whole apparatus into a present-day AI lab with a language model sitting where his digital computer would've gone, whichever gives you more to work with. Just don't use the telepathy as a cute plot twist and leave it at that, I want the story to actually sit inside the problem Turing backed into: once minds can leak into each other, what is a test like this even measuring anymore, and does that leakage make it easier or harder for a machine to pass as human?
