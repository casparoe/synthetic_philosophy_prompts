Last night a friend and I got into a long argument, prompted partly by some of Sean Carroll's writing on entropy that one of us had been reading, about whether the thermodynamic arrow of time really requires the kind of cosmological boundary condition that David Albert calls the "Past Hypothesis": the stipulation that the universe began in an extraordinarily low-entropy macrostate, from which the Second Law and the felt directionality of time are supposed to follow by ordinary statistical-mechanical reasoning about macrostates and their relative phase-space volumes. My friend defended what I take to be the orthodox line: the Past Hypothesis is unexplained, maybe even unexplainable, but it is the only option we can rationally hold, because the alternative — reviving Boltzmann's own suggestion that we are simply a rare statistical fluctuation out of an otherwise eternal equilibrium background, a "Boltzmann brain" complete with false memories of a coherent low-entropy past — is not merely bizarre but self-undermining. If that hypothesis were true, she argued, we would have no business trusting the very statistical-mechanical reasoning that led us to take it seriously in the first place, so it can be dismissed before we ever get to comparing it with the Past Hypothesis on the merits.

I didn't think that "cognitive instability" move was as decisive as she made it sound, but I couldn't pin down exactly why at the time. I'd like you to succeed where I failed: construct the strongest argument available that we are not entitled to reject the fluctuation/Boltzmann-brain hypothesis in favor of the Past Hypothesis on cognitive-instability grounds alone. In particular, engage with how typicality reasoning is used both to justify ordinary statistical mechanics in the first place and to generate the Boltzmann-brain problem, and with whether there is a genuine distinction between a hypothesis being self-undermining to assert and a hypothesis being false or unwarranted. Please argue for taking the fluctuation hypothesis seriously as persuasively as you can, rather than surveying both sides evenhandedly — I can reconstruct the orthodox rebuttal myself if I need it.
