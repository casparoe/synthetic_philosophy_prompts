So this happened after my decision theory seminar last week and I haven't stopped thinking about it. One of my PhD students and her housemate, who trades for a living, got into it over beers about Kelly betting while I mostly just listened. He said sizing your bets with the Kelly fraction isn't just one option among others -- its the objectively rational thing to do, since if you bet noticeably more than Kelly on a repeated favorable bet you're mathematically guaranteed to go broke eventually, no utility function required. She said that's a cute fact about what happens almost surely in the long run, but it doesn't make it irrational to just maximize expected value instead, especially if you're not actually going to face the bet a thousand times -- and she brought up that Samuelson actually wrote an entire paper using only one-syllable words to mock the idea that "maximize expected log wealth" is some universal law of rationality rather than just what you'd do if you happen to have log utility. He came back with the "geometric rationality" line: wealth is multiplicative, multiplicative processes aren't ergodic, so the time-average growth rate is the objectively correct thing to maximize for someone who actually lives through time, whatever utility function you hand them. She said that's log utility wearing a physics costume -- the time-average growth rate just is the log-utility expectation, so he hasn't found some new rational requirement, he's relabeled an old one and dressed it up as physics. Neither budged. Can you go through it move by move and say who's actually got the better of it at each step, and whether there's something they're both missing?
