As a psychology PhD student currently designing an experience-sampling study, I've run into a probability puzzle that has sparked a real disagreement between my advisor and me. We plan to randomize participants 50/50 into two conditions. Those in Condition A receive exactly one push-notification survey, delivered at a random time over a two-week period. Those in Condition B receive an identical-looking survey at a random time on each of the fourteen days that make up that period. The app is designed to give no indication of the date, a running survey count, or a participant's past responses; for the purposes of this question, assume participants also have no way of recalling how many prompts they've already received. Suppose a participant has just opened one such survey. At that exact moment, using only the information available to them, what probability should they assign to being in Condition B? My advisor maintains the answer must be 50%, since that is the true randomization rate and nothing has happened to change it. I keep suspecting that the fact Condition B generates fourteen times as many such moments has to matter, though I can't justify that intuition, or even say confidently whether it's asking the same question as what fraction of all participants are in Condition B. Whose reasoning is correct, and why?
