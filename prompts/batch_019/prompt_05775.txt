Ok this is gonna be a bit inside-baseball but bear with me. You know the Othello-GPT saga from the mech interp world? Kenneth Li's original paper found the board state was only really decodable with a nonlinear probe (iirc), and then Neel Nanda showed that if you recode the board as "mine vs yours" (relative to whos turn it is) instead of "black vs white," a totally linear probe nails it almost perfectly. This got passed around a lot as evidence that the network really does have a world model sitting in there, just represented in a basis nobody had thought to check yet.

I want the opposite case argued, as rigorously as you can manage: that the mine/yours result is actually evidence *against* robust realism about neural network representations, not for it. The intuition I want fleshed out properly is this: if whether some quantity counts as "really represented" flips entirely depending on which of several extensionally equivalent, human-chosen recodings we happen to probe for, then "the network represents the board state" isn't tracking some basis-independent fact about the network's internals — it's tracking facts about which featurizations the interpreter bothered to try. Bring in the privileged-basis/superposition stuff if it helps. I don't want the cheap "well it's all just interpretation" version, I want the strongest version of this that actually takes the linear representation hypothesis seriously and still comes out skeptical of realism.
