Now retired after forty years as an experimental physicist, I have only recently begun reading philosophy of science in earnest, starting with Kuhn's *Structure of Scientific Revolutions*. What troubles me is his argument that terms such as "mass" mean something subtly different within Newtonian and Einsteinian mechanics, which is central to his claim that the two paradigms are incommensurable: there is no paradigm-neutral vocabulary in which to state what, if anything, has been gained by moving from one to the other, and hence no algorithm by which one theory could be shown to be simply better than the other. That seems to make the shift to relativity something closer to a conversion experience than a piece of reasoning, yet this sits awkwardly with the fact that GPS satellites require relativistic corrections merely to function, which looks like about as objective a demonstration of relativity's superiority as one could ask for. Have I misunderstood what Kuhn means by incommensurability, or does an example like this show his account of theory change to be untenable?
