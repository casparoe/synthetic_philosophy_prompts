I've owned a cottage on Birch Lake for eleven years, and for the last three I've served as one of five trustees on the water and lake-management district that governs the place -- we set water rates, dock permits, and shoreline rules for about four hundred parcels. Our bylaws, written back in 1968 when this was still basically a summer colony, give exactly one vote per parcel to whoever holds recorded title, regardless of how many people actually live on the property or whether the owner has ever set foot on it. That arrangement made a rough kind of sense when nearly everyone here was both owner and full-time resident, but it no longer describes the place.

More than a third of our houses are now rented out year-round to tenants who have no ownership interest in the units they occupy, several longtime owners have died and left their parcels to adult children who live out of state and have never seen the lake, and the man who runs the marina just outside our boundary depends entirely on our water-quality decisions for his livelihood despite owning no land inside the line we draw. A group of the year-round tenants has petitioned the board to extend a vote to any adult who has resided in the district for at least twelve months, on the grounds that they are the ones drinking the water and living with the consequences of our decisions, whatever the deed says. Several absentee owners -- including a couple who spend barely six weeks here each summer but reliably vote by mail -- are pushing back hard, arguing that ownership, not residence, is what creates the long-term financial exposure the vote is meant to control, and that tenants have every incentive to vote themselves short-term benefits at the owners' expense and then move away.

I don't have any background in political theory, just a lot of time spent turning this over at the kitchen table, and what strikes me is that there doesn't seem to be a neutral place to draw this line at all: ownership, residence, being affected by a decision, and paying into the district budget are all real candidates, and whichever one our bylaws currently use was itself chosen by people who were already on the inside of that boundary. I'd like you to write the short memo I'll circulate to my fellow trustees before we vote on the tenants' petition next month. Please recommend a specific, concrete change to our voting rules -- or a deliberate decision to keep the current one -- including how any change would be phased in and by what date, and please take the absentee owners' "no lasting stake" argument seriously rather than waving it away, since at least two of my fellow trustees will raise it and I want something better to say than that renters deserve a voice too.
