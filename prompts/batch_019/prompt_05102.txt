Working on a piece about AI bargaining risk and went down a rabbit hole on the "commitment races" problem that keeps coming up in AI safety circles — the worry that bargaining agents (human or AI) who can credibly pre-commit to threats have a big incentive to commit early and hard, which is great if you're the only one doing it and a disaster if everyone does it at once. One proposed fix doing the rounds is the "safe Pareto improvement": a tweak both sides can agree to in advance thats guaranteed to leave each of them at least as well off in expectation, without either side having to reveal the private info that made racing to commit tempting in the first place. Schelling basically built a career on this terrain — focal points, burnt bridges, threats that leave something to chance — decades before anyone was worrying about AI agents. If he could see safe Pareto improvements pitched as a partial fix for the commitment race problem, what's his most likely verdict, and which of his own ideas is doing the work in that verdict?
