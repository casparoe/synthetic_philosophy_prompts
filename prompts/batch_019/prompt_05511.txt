I'm on my condo association's governance committee, rewriting our board election rules after a rough race this spring. Four owners ran for our one open board seat: the winner took it with 34% of the vote, while the other three—all running on similar reform platforms—split the remaining 66% almost evenly (24%, 22%, 20%), and a post-election survey asking owners to rank all four candidates found that a clear majority had actually placed the winner last. Committee opinion is now split three ways: some want ranked-choice voting (instant runoff), some want a Condorcet method that would elect whichever candidate beats every other one head-to-head, and a couple want to keep plain plurality because, as they put it, "it's the only system people here will actually understand." I've spent months reading about voting theory on my own, including Arrow's impossibility theorem, so I know no method can satisfy every fairness condition at once—but I still need to recommend one. Could you draft the memo I'll circulate before the bylaw vote? It should name one specific method for future elections, state plainly which fairness condition or conditions it gives up and why that trade-off is reasonable for a group our size, and take the plurality holdouts' objection seriously rather than just telling them no system is perfect.
