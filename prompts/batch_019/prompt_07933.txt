I've been reporting a piece on whether spacetime points are "real" things or just bookkeeping devices, interviewing a couple of philosophers of physics for it next month, and I fell down a total rabbit hole (sorry, couldn't resist) on Einstein's old hole argument -- the one he flirted with around 1913 and that got dragged back into philosophy of physics decades later by Earman and Norton. I think I've more or less got the shape of it: because GR is diffeomorphism invariant, you can take a solution, pick some empty "hole" region H with no matter in it, and smoothly drag the metric around inside H while leaving everything outside it (including all the initial data you'd specify before you even reach the hole) untouched, and you get a second solution that's mathematically distinct from the first but empirically indistinguishable from it. If spacetime points are genuine individuals with there own identity (substantivalism), that seems to mean GR can't even determine what happens inside an empty hole, which feels like a cheap, manufactured kind of indeterminism rather than a real one. What I can't get straight in my head is the "sophisticated substantivalist" escape hatch -- keeping points as real while dropping the assumption that they carry their identity with them from one solution to the other -- and whether that ends up being a different position from relationism at all, or just relationism wearing a substantivalist nametag.

So here's what I'd love: a dialogue between a sophisticated substantivalist and a relationist working through the hole argument together, maybe standing at a chalkboard with the two diffeomorphic solutions sketched out. Don't let the relationist win just by lodging the "nametag" charge -- make the substantivalist actually answer it, ideally by saying something substantive about what's left for points to be once you've dropped primitive identity across solutions (is it that there's a determinate number of them, prior to any labeling? that they ground the qualitative facts rather than the other way round?). And don't let the substantivalist win either -- give the relationist a real comeback, not a token one. If the two of them end up circling the question of whether there's anything left to disagree about, that's fine, but only if it grows out of the actual argument rather than being announced from nowhere.
