Here's the part I actually care about getting right: it needs to be an *informational* cascade, not a peer-pressure story. Nobody should be conforming because theyre embarrassed or scared of the group -- every character is doing perfectly good Bayesian reasoning, combining their own private evidence with whatever they can infer from the choices (not the reasons) of everyone who went before them. At some point fairly early on, the accumulated weight of other people's choices has to make it strictly rational for someone to just ignore their own private signal, even a good one -- that's the moment the cascade actually starts, and I want it to be almost visible on the page. Give me at least one character with genuinely strong private evidence pointing the other way who still, rationally, goes along with everyone else anyway. Bonus points if you can gesture at why the whole thing is fragile -- it's really only resting on one or two people's original signals and could in principle get knocked over later by one new piece of public info -- but don't force it if it fights the story.

Doesn't have to be finance (that's the boring default). Under 1000 words is fine, and it doesn't need a tidy moral tacked on at the end.
