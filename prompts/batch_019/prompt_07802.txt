For an English class project, I have been reading Borges's short story "The Garden of Forking Paths" alongside some general-audience explanations of the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics, an idea that only became a prominent topic of scientific and popular discussion well after Borges's death in 1986. Several sources I found simply assert that Borges "anticipated" many-worlds with his forking-paths device, but this seems too quick once I consider his essay "A New Refutation of Time," where he draws on Berkeley and Hume to argue that time itself, and even the persisting self, is not fully real. Given that background, do you think Borges would have welcomed many-worlds as a vindication of his fiction, or would his underlying idealism about time have made him resist the idea that every branch is a literal, objectively existing physical fact rather than a metaphor? I would like a reading that genuinely grapples with the tension between these two texts rather than repeating the standard "Borges predicted the multiverse" claim.
