I'm deep into the fourth draft of a novel whose narrator, a former concert pianist named Imre, undergoes a corpus callosotomy in his fifties after decades of intractable epilepsy, and the whole back half of the book turns on a single set piece I want to get right: a long, escalating argument between the "two Imres" that his surgery is supposed to have produced. Working up to it, I went back to Sperry's and Gazzaniga's original split-brain papers and also read around in the philosophical literature that grew out of them — Nagel's old piece on brain bisection, and some more recent work of Tim Bayne's on the unity of consciousness. My sense, coming out of all that, is that the scientists and philosophers have essentially converged on the view that severing the corpus callosum leaves two distinct, mutually aware centers of consciousness sharing one skull, disagreeing only on the fine print of how to individuate them; and that Gazzaniga's idea of the left hemisphere as an "interpreter" names the way that hemisphere watches what the other half of the body is up to and narrates it, often needling or accusing it, rather like one sibling continuously mocking another's behavior in real time. On this basis, my plan is to have Imre's left hemisphere accuse the right of trying to sabotage his marriage through small, unauthorized gestures of his left hand, with the right hemisphere firing back through equally deliberate if silent actions, the whole thing playing out as a fully lucid, occasionally verbal shouting match at the dinner table, years after the operation and long after any hospital tachistoscope has left the picture.

Because this scene is load-bearing for the whole novel, I would rather find out now whether I have badly misread the science and the philosophy than after another hundred pages. Did Sperry's and Gazzaniga's results, together with the philosophical debate they provoked, really settle on two distinct, mutually aware subjects as the right way to describe these patients, such that a real-time dinner-table argument between "two Imres" is a fair extrapolation of what has actually been observed? And have I understood the "interpreter" correctly, or does that idea actually depend on the left hemisphere lacking, rather than having, this kind of ongoing access to what the right hemisphere is doing and why?
