For my seminar on distributive ethics this term, we spent the last two weeks working through John Taurek's "Should the Numbers Count?" together with Derek Parfit's reply, "Innumerate Ethics." The discussion got surprisingly heated: about half the room thought Taurek's position was borderline unintelligible, and the other half thought it was the only way to take the separateness of persons seriously, and by the end our professor admitted, half-joking, that the disagreement basically comes down to whether you find aggregating harms across people intuitively obvious or intuitively question-begging. That offhand comment got me wondering whether the debate could be turned into an actual joke, rather than just being a joke about how heated our seminar got.

Could you write a short joke for me—a one-liner or a brief two-line exchange is fine—that turns on the specific content of Taurek's argument? For instance, it could play on his denial that the losses suffered by five separate people can be summed into one greater loss, or on his suggestion that flipping a coin is a defensible way to decide whom to save when you can't save everyone. I don't want a generic trolley-problem joke where the numbers just happen to be five and one; I'd like the humor to depend on actually knowing what Taurek claims, so that someone who has read the paper laughs for a different, better-informed reason than someone who hasn't. If it helps the joke land, feel free to add a one-sentence gloss afterward identifying exactly which move in the argument it's riffing on.
