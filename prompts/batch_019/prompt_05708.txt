I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on formal epistemology. A friend from my program and I have a running bet -- dinner, at even odds -- on whether the Collatz conjecture is true. She's since had second thoughts, arguing that since the conjecture is either a necessary mathematical truth or a necessary falsehood, a fully rational reasoner must assign it credence 0 or 1; anything in between, on her view, leaves you exposed to a sequence of bets that would each look fair on their own but are jointly guaranteed to lose you money, no matter how the conjecture is eventually resolved. So by her own reasoning, at least one of us was irrational to agree to even odds. That seems too strong to me -- neither of us has a proof -- but I can't locate the flaw. Is she right, and if not, where exactly does the argument go wrong?
