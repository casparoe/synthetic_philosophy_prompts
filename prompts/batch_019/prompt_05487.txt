I'm a backend engineer at a mid-size startup, and outside of work I co-host a small podcast where we talk to people doing serious work at the intersection of software and ethics; our listeners are mostly other engineers, so we're expected to go a bit deeper than the usual "AI ethics" small talk. Next week's guest is an AI safety researcher who has built something of a following in alignment circles for arguing that advanced AI systems should be designed as expected-utility maximizers that stay deliberately uncertain about their own objective and defer to human oversight, rather than being bound by hard-coded deontological rules. The topic actually mirrors an argument my own team had a few months ago, when half of us wanted to hard-code refusal rules into our product's assistant and the other half wanted it to weigh consequences case by case; I don't think either side really won, which is part of why I want to hear this argued out properly by people who've thought about it more than we have.

Could you draft nine or ten interview questions that don't just let her restate the position?
