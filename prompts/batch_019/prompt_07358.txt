Ok here's one that's been bugging me. The Kelly criterion says you should bet or invest whatever fraction of your bankroll maximizes expected log wealth, and the classic selling point (Kelly, then Thorp, and revived lately by Ole Peters' "ergodicity economics" crowd) is that this beats literally any other strategy in the long run with probability 1 -- so growth-rate optimization is just what a rational agent should do, full stop, not merely the preference of someone who happens to have log utility. Samuelson thought this was a straight-up fallacy (he even wrote a rebuttal using only one-syllable words to mock people who fell for it), and argued, along with Merton and others, that probability-1 dominance over an infinite horizon tells you nothing about what maximizes utility at any given time for an agent whose utility isn't log -- so theres nothing actually privileged about "geometric rationality."

Write an essay that takes an actual side on this. Does long-run growth/Kelly-optimality deserve some special claim to rationality beyond just being what log-utility maximizers want, or is Samuelson basically right that its an arbitrary preference dressed up as objective? Weigh the strongest form of both arguments, but don't be wishy-washy -- commit to a verdict and defend it.
