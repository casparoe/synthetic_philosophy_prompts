So I spent about 35 years running an experimental physics lab, and now that I'm retired I've fallen into reading philosophy, mostly free will stuff since it feels like unfinished business after a career spent thinking about causation. I recently read P.F Strawson's 'Freedom and Resentment,' where he argues that our whole practice of praising and blaming rests on natural 'reactive attitudes' -- resentment, gratitude, indignation -- and that whether determinism is literally true barely touches any of it. Then I found out, almost by accident, that his son Galen Strawson has spent decades arguing pretty much the opposite: his 'Basic Argument' says true moral responsibility is impossible for anyone, determinism or not, because to really be responsible for what you do you'd have to be responsible for the way you are, and nobody gets to be causa sui, the self-caused author of their own character. Funny that they're father and son, but now I honestly can't tell if they're actually disagreeing or just talking past eachother. Could you compare the two accounts for me and tell me which one, if either, comes closer to a genuinely satisfying answer to the problem of moral responsibility -- and justify your pick?
