I'm taking a seminar on free will and moral responsibility this term, and we just spent two weeks on Strawson's "Freedom and Resentment," which has made me start noticing this sort of thing everywhere. This weekend, two friends of mine -- Grace and Felix, neither of whom has read a word of the paper -- got into an argument about a third friend, Naomi, who had promised for a year to be maid of honor at a wedding, then backed out by text two days before and never showed up. It later came out that she has a longstanding, diagnosed anxiety disorder, and that the wedding had reopened old wounds from her own parents' divorce.

Felix's view was that you can't really hold this against Naomi: given the diagnosis and the acute trigger, this looks like exactly the kind of case Strawson thinks should push us from resentment toward what he calls the objective attitude, rather than treating her as a normal target of it. Grace said that was too quick -- Strawson's own examples of a full exemption are things like severe derangement or being a small child, not "has an anxiety disorder and a rough week"; at most this supports an excuse rather than an exemption, and the excuse only covers the panic attack itself, not the weeks beforehand when Naomi avoided telling anyone she was struggling, which is where the real resentment is aimed. Felix replied that the avoidance is itself a textbook symptom of the disorder, so you can't excuse the breakdown while still holding the avoidance against her -- if one condition explains the whole pattern, the line Grace wants to draw looks arbitrary. Grace answered that this argument proves too much: everyone's dispositions have some causal history, and blocking exactly that inference from the "pessimist" is the whole point of Strawson's naturalism about the reactive attitudes -- otherwise nobody could ever be resented for a standing character trait, since character always has a history.

Neither of them budged. Could you go through the exchange move by move, say who has the stronger Strawsonian case at each point, and flag anything you think both of them are getting wrong or leaving out?
