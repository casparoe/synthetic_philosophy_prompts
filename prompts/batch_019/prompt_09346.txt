As a PhD student working on nineteenth-century social philosophy, I am drafting a chapter on Kierkegaard's account of "the public" in the closing section of *Two Ages*, usually excerpted as "The Present Age." What strikes me most is his claim that the daily press conjures a genuinely new social entity: an anonymous collectivity capable of producing opinion and gossip but never able to commit to anything or be held answerable for it. This abstraction, he argues, "levels" individual distinction not through any positive, revolutionary passion but through idle reflection and envy. I would like to see these specific mechanisms dramatized rather than merely summarized. Please write a short story of around 1,000 words whose plot turns on the formation of such a "public" and its leveling effect on one central character. Resist the easy move of simply restaging this as a social-media pile-on; find a subtler setting or occasion that still stays faithful to the underlying psychology Kierkegaard describes — the substitution of talk for action, the diffusion of responsibility into anonymity, and envy as the only passion the age still permits itself.
