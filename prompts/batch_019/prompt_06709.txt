I'm writing a dissertation chapter on the philosophical underpinnings of AI risk arguments, and I keep running up against what looks like an internal tension between two theses that are usually presented as complementary. Bostrom's orthogonality thesis holds that an agent's level of intelligence tells us essentially nothing about its final goals, since almost any goal is compatible with almost any degree of cognitive sophistication. Yet the instrumental convergence thesis is then invoked to predict, with considerable confidence, that any sufficiently capable agent will tend toward self-preservation, resource acquisition, and goal-content integrity. If we genuinely have no grip on what a superintelligent system will ultimately want, I don't see how proponents of these arguments can simultaneously claim to know so much about how it will behave. Doesn't the predictive confidence of the convergence thesis effectively undercut the central claim of orthogonality, or at least show that the two theses sit in tension rather than mutually supporting one another?
