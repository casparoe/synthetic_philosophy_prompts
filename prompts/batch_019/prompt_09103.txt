I'm one of the elected worker-trustees on the board of a mid-sized multi-employer pension fund (a handful of unrelated employers pooled into one plan, so we've got pretty different cohorts -- new hires in their 20s all the way up to guys retiring next year). We're about to vote on a new framework our actuarial consultants want us to adopt for setting investment and risk-sharing policy across those cohorts. Their pitch, roughly: survey each cohort to back out something they call a von Neumann-Morgenstern utility function over retirement income (had to look that up), then set the fund's whole investment policy to maximize a weighted sum of those cohort utilities, with weights set by each cohort's share of total membership. When a couple of the older trustees pushed back -- the near-retirement cohort is small and can't recover from a bad decade the way a 25-year-old could, so a membership-weighted policy basically hands a young majority the wheel -- the actuaries said this isn't really a judgment call at all, it's "Harsanyi's aggregation theorem," and any rule that doesn't take this weighted-linear form is provably incoherent. Basically: the math has spoken, and if you don't like the outcome you're objecting to the weights, not the method.

I got kind of deep in the weeds trying to figure out on my own wether that's a fair characterization of what the theorem actually shows, and I'm still not confident I can pin down exactly where (if anywhere) the overclaiming happens. I'm on the small working group that has to bring the full board a recommendation next month, so could you draft that memo for me? I want an actual bottom-line call -- adopt the framework as-is, adopt it with specific modifications, or shelve it for a different approach -- plus concrete next steps and rough timing, not just "both sides have a point." And I want the actuaries' position represented at its strongest, since we'll have to explain whatever we decide directly to them, not just to the trustees who already agree with us.
