I spent my career as a behavioral ecologist studying signaling systems in birds, and one idea I only appreciated properly after retiring was Zahavi's handicap principle: a signal stays evolutionarily honest only when cheating on it would cost the signaler more than it's worth, which is why stotting gazelles and peacock trains can be trusted even though both signaler and receiver have every incentive to lie or be fooled. Lately I have been reading about attempts to define honesty and deception for AI systems, and I keep running into a disanalogy I cannot resolve on my own.

In animals we already distinguish two quite different grades of "deception." A stick insect or a sexually deceptive orchid deceives in a purely functional sense: no belief, no intention, just a phenotype shaped by selection to trigger a false inference in a predator or pollinator. Primatologists such as Byrne and Whiten reserve "tactical deception" for something stronger, requiring the deceiver to have something like a representation of what another individual believes, deployed on purpose to mislead it. Most of the definitions now being proposed for AI systems seem to assume something closer to this second, stronger sense: they build in that the system induces a false belief in the user on purpose, or that its output is decoupled from some internal "belief" state it could be said to hold. But it is not obvious to me that a large language model clears that bar any better than the orchid does, or that it needs to, if the purely functional notion already captures what we actually care about: that people end up believing false and costly things because of what the system produces.

Write an essay that weighs whether honesty and deception in AI systems should be defined functionally, as we are content to do for orchids and stick insects, or reserved for cases meeting the stronger, belief-and-intent-involving standard we use for primate tactical deception. Defend a verdict rather than leaving the matter tied; if the two standards genuinely converge, or diverge only in identifiable edge cases, say which cases and why.
