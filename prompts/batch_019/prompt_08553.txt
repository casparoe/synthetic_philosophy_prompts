I'm working on a piece about scientific fraud, and it led me into research on how well people detect deception face to face. Even trained professionals — police interrogators, customs officers, judges — turn out to be barely better than chance at this, and their confidence has almost no relationship to whether they're actually right. That unsettled me: a few weeks ago I interviewed a researcher whose data colleagues have quietly begun to question, and I came away convinced she was telling me the truth. I've since gone back over my notes and the recording more than once, and I still can't identify anything in her answers or manner that should have raised a flag. So am I rationally obligated to lower my confidence in this particular case simply because judgments like it are generally unreliable, even though I haven't found anything wrong with this one? Or can I reasonably keep trusting my own read of her while fully accepting that the research is right? Holding both at once feels like it shouldn't be possible, but I can't tell which one is supposed to give.
