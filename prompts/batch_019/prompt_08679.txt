Random side gig: I'm a comp lit PhD student (dissertation's on Tolstoy), and a family friend who chairs a tiny regional arts foundation roped me onto their grant panel this year, since apparently nobody else on the board has read past the Cliff notes. The foundation's charter, written by the original donor decades ago, says the yearly grant has to go to work that fits "Tolstoy's definition of true art in What Is Art?" We've got two finalists and the panel's deadlocked, we vote friday.

Finalist A is a formerly incarcerated folk singer whose songs are plain, autobiographical, and land like a punch -- people with nothing in common with him openly cry at his shows. But a couple of his best songs make revenge for his brother's murder sound like plain justice.

Finalist B is a composer who writes gentle, technically polished pieces for a kids' choir, explicitly about forgiveness and togetherness -- exactly the "universal" content Tolstoy's always on about. But several panelists find the music kind of inert, and suspect she's writing to formula rather than out of anything she's actually lived through.

Can you draft the panel's decision memo -- who gets it, neither, or both -- using Tolstoy's actual criteria and not the bumper-sticker 'art = sincere feeling' version? And be honest about the strongest case for whichever finalist you don't pick.
