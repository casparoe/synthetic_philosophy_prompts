Lewis's setup: nobody thinks a guilty offender's friend serving his prison sentence lets the offender off the hook, and he explains why -- punishment isn't like a debt, there's no "creditor" who just wants to be made whole again. But then he points out we're all totally fine with a friend paying someone's fine instead, which is just as much a case of one person's penalty landing on somebody else. He concludes that everyone, not just Christians defending the atonement, is quietly inconsistent about this, and ends by half-suggesting the shared inconsistency is itself weak evidence penal substitution can somehow make sense, even though he can't say how. My guest's paper is basically an attempt to supply the "how."
