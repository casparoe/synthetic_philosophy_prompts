Ok so this has been bugging me for months: I am absolutely terrified of flying, like white-knuckle-gripping-the-armrest terrified, even though I've read all the stats about it being basically the safest way to travel there is and I genuinely do believe those numbers. My brain is 100% convinced its fine, and my gut does not care one bit what my brain thinks the second there's turbulence. A therapist once told me to just calmly remind myself of the statistics mid-flight, which, spoiler, did not make the fear go away even a little. I brought this up at dinner with some friends and one of them, who reads a ton of philosophy, said this is actually a known problem for people who think emotions are basically the same thing as judgments about the world (so fear would just be the judgment "this is dangerous") - because if that were true, shouldn't my fear vanish once I genuinely stop judging flying to be dangerous? That sent me down a rabbit hole where I found there's a whole other camp that says emotions aren't judgments at all, but more like perceptions of value, sort of the way an optical illusion can keep looking crooked even after you've measured it with a ruler and know for a fact its straight - the perception just outlasts the belief that contradicts it. Apparently cases like mine even have a name, "recalcitrant" emotions, though I might be misusing that term a bit since I only picked it up secondhand.

What I'd really love is to watch these two camps actually go at each other instead of just getting each position explained one after another, which is all I could find when I looked into it myself. So could you write me a dialogue between a philosopher who thinks emotions are at bottom judgments and one who thinks they're at bottom perceptions, where they specifically hash out a recalcitrant case like my fear of flying (feel free to swap in a different example, like the classic spider-behind-glass one, if it works better)? I don't want either side to be a pushover - the judgment person especially needs an actual comeback to the recalcitrance problem instead of just folding in round one, since that's where every summary I've read conveniently stops. I want them genuinely responding to what the other just said rather than restating their own view louder, and honestly if by the end I still can't tell who came out ahead, that's exactly what I'm hoping for.
