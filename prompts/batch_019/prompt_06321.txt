I've been reading about epistemology on my own, and I recently came across Timothy Williamson's anti-luminosity argument, which he uses to reject what's known as the KK principle. As I understand it, he shows that because we can never pinpoint the exact moment at which we stop feeling cold as we gradually warm up, we don't really know that we are cold even in the most obvious, clear-cut cases. If a rigorous philosophical argument can undermine something as basic as that, doesn't it follow that introspection in general is unreliable, and that I should be far more skeptical of my own snap judgments about my beliefs and feelings in everyday life?
