My younger brother and I have spent the better part of the last month arguing about whether our hometown football team will make the playoffs, and the disagreement has started to bother me in a way that has little to do with football. Both of us are the sort of people who track advanced statistics for fun — he works in data analysis professionally, and I follow the same kinds of models as a hobby — so this has never really been a matter of gut feelings or loyalty. We have drawn on the same injury reports, the same strength-of-schedule figures, and the same playoff-odds calculators, and each of us has walked the other through his complete reasoning, more than once, over the phone. After our first couple of conversations we both moved: he came down from roughly eighty percent to sixty-five, and I came up from twenty-five to forty. But we have been stuck at those numbers for two weeks now, even as we keep trading the same arguments back and forth, without either of us turning up anything the other hasn't already considered. Somewhat impulsively, we ended up putting forty dollars on it at those odds, which in hindsight strikes me as a little strange: if I genuinely believe he is reasoning just as carefully as I am, from essentially the same information, why did either of us expect to come out ahead on that bet?

I mentioned all this, mostly to vent, to a friend from graduate school who now studies game theory. She told me, half-joking but apparently half-serious, that there is a well-known argument for the conclusion that two people who regard each other as equally rational, and who are working from the same basic starting assumptions, should not be able to remain in this kind of openly acknowledged disagreement — and certainly should not be willing to take opposite sides of a bet on it. I had never come across anything like this before and am not sure whether to take it seriously. Is there really something to the idea that our disagreement, and especially our willingness to wager on it, shows that one or both of us isn't reasoning as well as we like to think? Or is there a more mundane explanation for how two people who trust each other's judgment, and who have gone over the same ground repeatedly, can still end up settled on such different numbers?
