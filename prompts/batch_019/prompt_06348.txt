I'm a philosophy major currently taking a seminar on Nietzsche, and this week we worked closely through the Second Essay of *On the Genealogy of Morality* -- the material on guilt, bad conscience, and the strange, much-debated passage on the "sovereign individual." Independently of the course, I also sit on my university's Honor Council, which hears academic integrity cases and is right now drafting a reform of our disciplinary code. The proposed reform would replace the current system -- suspensions, permanent transcript notations, and a searchable internal registry of violations -- with a restorative-justice model centered on facilitated dialogue, restitution, and no lasting record, on the grounds that the existing code manufactures shame and self-loathing rather than genuine responsibility. A few Council members who are otherwise sympathetic to reform raised an objection this week that struck me as drawn directly from the seminar readings: Nietzsche argues that punishment and the memory it burns into us are what first make an animal capable of standing behind a promise at all, so if we strip out consequence in the name of compassion, we may not be liberating students from guilt so much as depriving them of the very mechanism that lets anyone be held -- or hold themselves -- to an obligation in the first place.

Could you draft the short memo I'll circulate to the Council ahead of our vote next Tuesday? It should take a clear position -- adopt the restorative model, keep the current one, or propose a specific hybrid -- and it should do real argumentative work with Nietzsche's own account: the distinction between bad conscience (cruelty turned inward) and whatever is supposed to be admirable about the sovereign individual's "memory of the will," the etymological link he draws between Schuld and Schulden, and the question of whether anything in his genealogy of punishment survives once you subtract the cruelty he otherwise diagnoses so unsparingly. Please also address the objection above in its strongest form rather than brushing past it -- if you conclude that it ultimately fails, I want to know exactly where and why.
