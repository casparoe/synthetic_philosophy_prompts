For my school's Ethics Bowl regionals this year, one of the case studies involves a small rural clinic that has just enough antivenom on hand to save either one patient or a separate group of five patients, with no way to split the doses so that everyone survives. While we were prepping, my coach mentioned that this is basically a real philosophical dilemma going back to a 1977 paper by John Taurek called "Should the Numbers Count?", so I looked up a summary out of curiosity. I was honestly surprised to find that Taurek's answer is basically no: since no one actually experiences the "loss of five lives" as a single loss — each of the five people only loses their own one life, exactly as the lone patient does — he argues that adding the five deaths together into a bigger harm that automatically outweighs the one death is illegitimate, and he proposes something like a coin flip instead of just treating the larger group. Apparently this got a lot of pushback: Derek Parfit and T.M. Scanlon each wrote responses defending the idea that numbers should count, though supposedly Scanlon tries to get there without simply adding welfare up the way a utilitarian would, and I couldn't really follow how that's supposed to work.

Could you write me a dialogue between two doctors at the clinic, standing over the last vial of antivenom with only moments left to decide, who represent these two opposing positions? One should hold that you obviously treat the larger group, ideally reasoning more like Scanlon than like a flat "five outweighs one" calculation, and the other should be a convinced Taurekian who thinks a chance procedure is the only way to respect each patient's separate claim to be saved. I don't want either doctor to be an easy target: each should engage with the specific argument the other just made — including the separateness-of-persons idea behind the coin flip — rather than talking past each other or simply restating their own view, and I'd like the exchange to end with both positions still standing, since from what I can tell, real philosophers haven't settled this either.
