What keeps bugging me is that the theorem is entirely about ex-ante expected utility over lotteries, so it guarantees the social utility function respects everyone's preferences before any uncertainty resolves, but it says nothing about whether things look fair once you actually see how they turned out. Can you cook up a concrete example (not the classic 'flip a coin for the one indivisible good' case, already seen that one) where a Harsanyi-style planner maximizing a weighted sum of individual vNM utilities ends up totally indifferent between two policies that most people would call clearly different in terms of fairness once the dice have actually landed? Bonus points if it still works when everyone involved has the exact same utility function, so it's not secretly just an interpersonal-comparability problem in disguise.
