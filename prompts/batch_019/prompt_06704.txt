Random one for you: G.E.M. Anscombe basically revived the doctrine of double effect from Aquinas and wrote the book -- literally, Intention (1957) -- that gives us the modern intended-vs-merely-foreseen distinction philosophers still lean on. She also thought it showed a "corrupt mind" to even entertain hypotheticals about judicially executing an innocent person for the greater good, and made an actual public stink when Oxford gave Truman an honorary degree in '56, publishing a pamphlet ("Mr Truman's Degree") arguing Hiroshima was murder rather than a merely-foreseen side effect. So: what's your best reconstruction of what she'd say about the whole self-driving-car trolley industry -- MIT's Moral Machine surveys, engineers debating wether the car should swerve into the one guy to save the five? Two things I actually want worked through, not just gestured at: whether swerving into the one counts as intending his death as a means (which double effect rules out) versus merely foreseeing it, and whether her account of intention -- tied as it is to the agent's own practical reasoning -- even applies to a piece of code, or if the whole double-effect framing is a category error once there's no "agent" there in her sense.
