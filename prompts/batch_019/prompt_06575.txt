So this happened on a camping trip two weekends ago and I honestly can't stop thinking about it. Me and my friend Jake went hiking somewhere kind of remote (not even my idea, he wanted to "explore" off the marked trail) and we ended up lost after dark with my phone completely dead, so there was no way to call anyone. Eventually this older guy doing trail maintenance came by on an ATV, and he said he'd drive us back to the parking lot, but he made it pretty clear he wasnt gonna bother unless we swore we'd Venmo him $50 once we had signal again, since his gas and time weren't free. He actually said something like "I've had hikers promise me money before and never pay up, but you two seem like the type who actually would" right before he let us climb on. Obviously we said yes. He dropped us at the lot, never asked our names or numbers or anything, and drove off back onto the property, so there was literally zero way for him to ever track us down or know if we'd paid.

While we were waiting in the parking lot for Jake's mom, Jake said the "smart" move now was to just not pay. His reasoning was that the ride already happened, nothing bad happens to us either way at this point, and paying doesn't get us anything anymore, so sending the money would just be throwing away $50 for literally no reason. I couldn't poke a hole in the logic but it still felt wrong somehow, so I paid the guy anyway, and now Jake keeps saying I basically set $50 on fire out of guilt for nothing. So who's actually right here — was Jake being the truly rational one and I just did something dumb, or is there something off with his reasoning that I can't quite put into words? I brought it up with my older brother, who's kind of a philosophy nerd, and he got weirdly excited and said this is apparently a well-known type of problem, but he just smirked and said he'd rather I work out what's actually going on than have him spoil it, which, thanks a lot.
