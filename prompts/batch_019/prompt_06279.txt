I've followed rationalist blogs and forums for years, and a comment thread I recently read under a post about the ethical implications of a spatially infinite universe has stuck with me. Two regular commenters, Owen and Ilana, went back and forth in a way I found genuinely hard to referee.

(1) Owen opened by arguing that if the universe is actually infinite -- which is what a flat or open universe implies on standard cosmological models -- then it already contains an infinite amount of both wellbeing and suffering. Under total utilitarianism, he said, this means no finite action, such as pulling one more person from a burning building, can change the total sum of value: infinity plus or minus any finite quantity is still the same infinity, so "maximize total welfare" quietly stops issuing verdicts on anything we actually do.

(2) Ilana replied that this treats infinity as a number you can subtract from, which is the real error. The right comparison, she said, isn't of totals but of dominance: ask whether, in every sufficiently large region surrounding the action, the running total in the with-rescue world permanently exceeds the running total in the without-rescue world. On that test the rescue does make things better, since the two worlds differ only in a bounded region, and the "with" total leads everywhere beyond it.

(3) Owen answered that this smuggles in an arbitrary choice of how to carve up and order the regions. Since the underlying sequence of local values isn't absolutely convergent -- unbounded good and unbounded bad both occurring infinitely often -- the very same facts can be summed in different orders to make either world come out ahead, just as a conditionally convergent series can be rearranged to converge to any value you like. So which world "dominates" is an artifact of the partition, not a fact about the world.

(4) Ilana answered that the rearrangement worry only bites against arbitrary mathematical reshufflings, not against the one ordering actually available to an agent embedded in the world: regions defined by the expanding future light cone of the action itself. We aren't free to sum in just any order; the causal structure of spacetime hands us a privileged one, and demanding invariance under reorderings nobody could ever physically implement is demanding too much.
