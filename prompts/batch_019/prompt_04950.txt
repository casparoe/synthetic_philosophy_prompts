Whenever she's pressed with the obvious objection, that CH is a perfectly determinate claim about a fixed collection of real numbers and so must be true or false whether or not we can prove which, she has a practiced way of turning it back on the interviewer: "tell me what fact you think forcing left out." It's an effective move, and I don't want the whole conversation to consist of her deploying it four different ways.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions that push past that move rather than inviting it? For each of those three, please add a short follow-up question keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
