I'm doing a PhD in philosophy (bioethics-ish) and I sit on the ethics committee at the memory-care unit where I do my practicum. We're about to vote on whether to formally sign off on "therapeutic fibbing" for dementia residents -- like telling a woman her dead husband is "running late" instead of re-breaking the news to her every time she asks, since she can't retain it and it just retraumatizes her all over again. Our chaplain is dead against it and keeps bringing up Augustine's bit about the Hebrew midwives: even though God rewarded them for lying to Pharaoh to save the babies, Augustine still insists the lie itself was sinful, because a good motive doesn't make a lie not a lie, reward or no reward. Half the committee thinks thats exactly the lesson -- we shouldn't institutionalize something this absolutist a thinker wouldn't even allow to save a life -- and half thinks importing 5th-century moral absolutism into a dementia ward is kind of nuts. Can you draft the memo the committee will actually vote on this Thursday? Pick an actual recommendation (approve, reject, approve with limits) with real reasons, and make sure it seriously engages the chaplain's Augustinian objection instead of just nodding at it and moving on.
