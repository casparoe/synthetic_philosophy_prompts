When I left the hardware startup I co-founded, my separation agreement entitled me to full acceleration of my unvested equity "if the Company ceases to pursue the Business substantially as conducted as of the Separation Date." In the year and a half since, my former co-founders discontinued our original product entirely and rebuilt the company around an unrelated enterprise-software business, while keeping the same legal entity, board, and cap table intact. They insist this is plainly still "the Company" named in my agreement; I insist just as plainly that it is not. Months of correspondence between our respective lawyers have produced no new facts, only restatements of this same insistence, which has left me suspecting that we are not actually disputing anything beyond what "the Company" should be taken to mean. Please describe the most important implications of the literature on verbal disputes and metaontology — especially Chalmers's account of verbal disputes, Hirsch's quantifier variance, Sider's rival defense of ontological realism, and Carnap's internal/external-question distinction — for whether this is genuinely a merely verbal dispute, and for how I might try to resolve it if so.
