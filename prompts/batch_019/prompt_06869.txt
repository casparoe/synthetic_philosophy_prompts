I'm writing my IB Extended Essay on theories of biological function, and the part I keep struggling to get right is the disagreement between etiological accounts of function, where, following Larry Wright, a trait has a function only because past instances of it were selected for some effect, and Robert Cummins' causal-role account, where a trait's function is simply whatever it currently contributes to some capacity of the system it belongs to, regardless of history. My supervisor suggested that if I could dramatize the disagreement in fiction, I'd probably understand it well enough to write about it properly. Could you write me a short story, around 1000 words, built around a case where the two accounts would hand down different verdicts — say, a creature that comes into existence by pure chance, a perfect physical duplicate of an ordinary animal, heart and lungs and all, but with no ancestors and no selection history behind it whatsoever (I believe this is a version of what's sometimes called a 'Swampman' case)? I'd like an actual character to have to decide, out loud, whether that creature's organs really have functions, and to feel the pull of both answers, rather than having the two theories simply explained to the reader — it doesn't need to resolve which account is right.
