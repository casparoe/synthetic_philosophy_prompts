So this has been living in my head for like two weeks now, ever since my uncle and my cousin got into it at a family dinner. My cousin was calling my uncle out for lecturing everyone about being financially responsible when the guy has personally declared bankruptcy twice, and my uncle's whole comeback was basically "that doesn't make me wrong though." Which, ok fair, that's true, but it also felt like his comeback was dodging something. Like even if what he was saying was 100% correct, there was still something off about HIM specifically being the one to say it to us.

So I went down a bit of a rabbit hole reading about this at night (no philosophy background here, just a guy who likes wikipedia rabbit holes) and it turns out theres a real disagreement among philosophers about whether hypocrisy actually takes away your standing to call someone else out, or whether that's just a deflection tactic that falls apart once you look closely, basically a fancier version of "well YOU'RE one to talk." One side seems to think blame is kind of a two-way deal - you're implicitly claiming you'd hold yourself to the exact same standard, and if you don't actually do that, you've forfeited the right to make the call even though the underlying claim can still be totally true. The other side thinks that's confused, and that the hypocrisy stuff only tells you something about the blamer's character, not about whether they had any standing to say what they said in the first place.

Can you write me a dialogue between two people who each represent one of these positions, going back and forth for real, not just both repeating their opening statement? I want whoever's defending "hypocrisy kills your standing" to have to answer the obvious comeback of "so nobody without a totally clean record could ever call out anybody for anything, which seems like a crazy conclusion," and I want the other person to have to deal with cases where the hypocrisy really does feel like the actual problem and not just an unrelated character flaw (the classic cheating-politician-giving-family-values-speeches type of case). If you can, I'd also love it if the dialogue dug into the difference between a piece of blame being "correct" versus someone having "standing" to give it, because I think that distinction is doing a lot of the heavy lifting here and I don't fully get how each side actually uses it.
