Uncle Dave died nine days ago — cardiac arrest, totally sudden, he was 58 — and I'm giving the eulogy on Saturday because out of the whole family I was the one who actually used to sit and listen to his effective altruism stuff instead of changing the subject. He gave away something like 40% of his salary for twenty years, had a genuinely unhinged spreadsheet with a tab literally called "$/QALY by charity," and once spent an entire Christmas dinner arguing with my dad about whether an Xbox for my cousin was defensible spending when the same money could've been malaria nets instead. Classic Dave. Everyone who's speaking Saturday is going to talk about how generous he was, and they're not wrong, but I want to actually get him right.

Here's my problem. At the shiva, one of his meetup friends mentioned that Dave had spent his last year quietly stuck on something called "cluelessness" — not the everyday kind, an actual argument, I guess theres a whole Hilary Greaves paper about how even our best-informed donations have consequences decades out that we can't sign positive or negative, and how this "complex" version doesn't politely cancel out the way ordinary uncertainty does, it just sits there unresolved. His friend said Dave had basically stopped being sure the spreadsheet proved what he used to think it proved, and kept donating anyway. So now I've got two audiences who'll hear this very differently: my grandma and his college roommates, who just want "he was generous and it mattered," and his EA friends, who will absolutely notice if I get up there and imply his giving definitely nets out to some tidy number of lives saved, like that was ever a settled thing for him by the end. I don't want to turn a funeral into a seminar, and the word "cluelessness" doesn't need to appear anywhere in it, but I do need the eulogy to be honest about a guy who was rigorous enough to doubt his own numbers and did the thing anyway. Can you actually write it — about five minutes read aloud — in a way that's true to that instead of either the greeting-card version or the philosophy-department version?
