Over the past couple of months I've been working through a philosophy-of-logic podcast during my commute, mostly on the recommendation of a friend from my old computer science program who is now doing a philosophy PhD. I have no formal background in philosophy or logic beyond a single discrete-math course in undergrad, so most of this is genuinely new to me, but one episode -- on dialetheism and paraconsistent logic -- has stuck with me longer than the others. Part of the appeal is that it maps onto problems I actually deal with at work: what a system should do when it receives genuinely contradictory input, like two conflicting writes to the same field, a feature flag that reads as both on and off depending on which cache you hit, or a test that passes and fails depending on timing.

As I understand it, classical logic treats a single contradiction as catastrophic: once you have both P and not-P, the "principle of explosion" (ex falso quodlibet) lets you derive literally anything, true or false. Paraconsistent logics are built specifically to block that collapse, so a contradiction can sit inside a theory without taking every other belief down with it. What I'm still turning over, though, is the further step that some paraconsistent logicians -- Graham Priest's name kept coming up -- actually take: not merely that contradictions can be tolerated without explosion, but that some of them, like the Liar sentence ("this sentence is false"), are literally true. That feels like a much bigger commitment than just adopting a more forgiving logic, and the host and guest spent a good ten minutes on whether you can coherently hold one position without the other.

I'd like you to write a joke that actually turns on that distinction -- between merely refusing to let a contradiction explode into triviality and outright asserting that the contradiction is true -- rather than a generic joke about logic being weird or contradictions being funny. It should be the kind of joke that lands harder the better you understand the principle of explosion and the difference between a paraconsistent logician and a dialetheist, not one that would work just as well with Schrödinger's cat substituted in. Any format is fine: one-liner, a scrap of dialogue, a fake forum post. Please don't append an explanation afterward -- if it needs footnotes to be funny, it hasn't worked.
