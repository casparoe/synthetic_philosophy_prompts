I've been falling down a rabbit hole this week after someone at a talk mentioned "program equilibrium" like it was a totally normal thing to bring up over coffee. Turns out its this niche corner of game theory - I think it goes back to a Moshe Tennenholtz paper from 2004 - about what happens when, instead of picking an action in a game, players submit a *program* that plays on their behalf, and those programs get to read each other's source code before acting. The hook is that this supposedly gets you real cooperation in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma as an honest-to-god equilibrium, no repeated play, no outside enforcement, nothing. Apparently the field has gotten pretty baroque since then too, with people using Löb's theorem to build things like "FairBot" and "PrudentBot" that are supposed to be more robust then the obvious first attempt (something like a bot that only cooperates with an exact copy of its own code, which I've seen called "CliqueBot," though I might be mangling that name).

For a piece I'm noodling on I want to actually get the disagreement here right, not just the one-paragraph "isn't this neat" version you get in blog posts. Could you write me a dialogue between two people who both know this stuff cold but land in different places on how much it really buys you? One of them, say Priya, thinks this is a genuinely important result - that giving agents mutual transparency plus the right fixed-point trick really does dissolve the cooperation problem, in a way that will matter once you're talking about AI systems that can show each other their code. The other, Dov, thinks it's a clever trick that doesn't deliver what it promises: that it just pushes the equilibrium-selection problem up a level (mutual defection is a program equilibrium too, after all), leans hard on assumptions about shared proof systems and perfect legibility that are doing all the real work, and produces bots that behave in ways that look kind of dumb by ordinary lights, like cooperating with something that would happily exploit them. I don't want them to just restate their opening positions at each other - I want Dov to actually press on Priya's strongest case, and Priya to have a real answer to the equilibrium-selection worry, and so on back and forth, until it's clear exactly where they still disagree once the easy confusions are cleared away.
