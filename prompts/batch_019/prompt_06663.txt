I've spent a long time following the LessWrong-adjacent literature on anthropic reasoning and self-locating belief — Sleeping Beauty, the Doomsday Argument, UDT, and so on — and a friend from my decision theory reading group recently pointed me to Piccione and Rubinstein's 1997 "absent-minded driver" problem as a cleaner cousin of Sleeping Beauty, since it manufactures genuine self-locating uncertainty purely out of an agent's own mixed strategy rather than out of an external coin flip. I've since worked through the standard setup on my own, but I've gotten stuck on precisely the piece my friend promised would be the payoff of the whole exercise.

To fix the numbers: a driver on a highway must take the second of two consecutive, indistinguishable exits to get home, which is worth 4; taking the first exit by mistake is worth 0; missing both and driving on is worth 1. Because the driver has genuine imperfect recall, he cannot tell which intersection he is currently at, and so is forced to apply the very same, possibly mixed, rule — continue with probability p — at both. Maximizing ex ante expected payoff gives p = 2/3. The trouble starts when you try to reconstruct that same figure "from the inside," standing at an intersection and reasoning via ordinary expected-utility maximization: everything then turns on the credence you assign to being at the first intersection rather than the second, and the credence that actually reproduces p = 2/3 turns out to be 3/5, not the 1/2 that a principle-of-indifference reasoner would presumably reach for. Substituting 1/2 doesn't just give a slightly worse answer — worked through consistently, it recommends continuing with probability only 1/3, a policy exactly as good as never even attempting to get home.

What I'd like from you is a genuinely gears-level account, not a restatement of the equilibrium condition: why does the credence have to be derived from the very policy it is meant to justify, why is treating "which intersection am I at" as a fair coin actively self-undermining rather than merely a coarser approximation, and does this cast any doubt on whether ordinary conditionalization is even the right tool for updating on purely self-locating evidence? If you have a view on how this bears on the halfer/thirder dispute over Sleeping Beauty — whether the driver case is genuine evidence for thirding, or whether it is disanalogous in some way I should be tracking — I would be glad to hear it, though that part is very much secondary to the main question.
