Quick one for a legal thriller I'm drafting. I've got a 3-judge appeals panel deciding a case where liability hinges on two seperate findings — whether there was a valid contract, and whether it was breached. Went down a rabbit hole reading about the "discursive dilemma" (aka the doctrinal paradox), where a panel can be individually totally rational but still spit out a group verdict thats logically incoherent, depending on how the votes get tallied. But it seems like that only bites you if the judges vote issue-by-issue and then mechanically apply the rule to reach a verdict — so if I just have each judge privately weigh the whole case and cast one final liable/not-liable vote, majority wins, that sidesteps the paradox for good, right? Feels like the clean fix, and I want to write the scene that way, just don't want to bake in a logic error that a philosophy-minded reader would catch.
