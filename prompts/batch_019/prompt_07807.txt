One response to Pascal's mugging that comes up a lot in these circles is Bostrom's leverage penalty: discount the prior probability of any hypothesis in proportion to the number of people it implies you are uniquely positioned to affect, since being the pivotal actor among so many is itself antecedently improbable. This is usually presented as a targeted defense against muggers and simulated Matrix Lords, but the same reasoning seems to bite just as hard on the standard longtermist case for prioritizing existential risk reduction, which likewise depends on a comparatively small number of present-day actors being able to influence the astronomically large number of people who will ever exist. Please argue for the position that the leverage penalty, applied consistently rather than special-cased to stop at muggers, undermines the epistemic case for existential-risk-focused longtermism to roughly the same degree that it undermines susceptibility to Pascal's mugging in the first place. I'd rather see the strongest version of this argument you can build than a literature summary, so please also work through the most promising way a committed longtermist might try to block the analogy, and say why that move does or doesn't succeed.
