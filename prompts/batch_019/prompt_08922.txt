Quick context: I'm taking an intro game theory course this semester, and a couple weeks ago in lecture the professor mentioned, almost as a throwaway line, that two people who both think the other is rational shouldn't be able to knowingly keep disagreeing about the odds of something once they've actually told each other everything they know. Didn't think much of it at the time, but then it started happening to me in real life.

My friend Deepak and I have been arguing for like two weeks about whether our mutual friends food truck is gonna survive its first year. At this point we've genuinely shown each other everything — his sales numbers, the lease terms, how burnt out hes seemed in texts to both of us, all of it — and we've talked it through for hours, more than once, with neither of us holding back or trying to "win." And yet I'm still around 70% it survives and Deepak's still around 70% it doesn't, and last night he actually offered to bet me $150 on it. When I brought it up to my roommate she just said "well, people are allowed to see things differently," which honestly didn't feel like an actual answer.

So two things I could use help with: should I take the bet? And, more importantly, if we're both reasonably rational, we trust each other's judgement, and we've genuinely pooled all our information, why haven't we converged on roughly the same number by now? Something about the fact that he still wants to bet against me feels like it should be telling me more than I'm using it for, but I can't pin down what exactly. Is one of us being irrational without realizing it, is there some information gap neither of us is noticing, or is this actually a case where two honest, sane people can just keep disagreeing indefinitely?
