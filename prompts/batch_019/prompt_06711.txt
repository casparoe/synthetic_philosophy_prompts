My friend and I had a long argument this weekend that I still haven't resolved. He follows markets far more closely than I do, and offered to sell me his shares in a small, publicly traded company at the current price, saying he simply needs the cash. I was ready to accept, having done my own research and judged the stock cheap. But it nagged at me: if he's as rational as I take him to be, and knows I'd wonder why he's selling to me privately instead of simply placing the shares on the open market, shouldn't his offering anyway make me more suspicious, not less? And once he anticipates that suspicion and still wants to sell, doesn't the same worry just recur, again and again, until neither of us should want the trade at all, whatever either of us actually knows about the company? He thinks his need for cash settles the matter; I think I've stumbled onto a genuine paradox about trading with people whose judgment you respect. Who's right, and what exactly is his stated reason for selling doing to the argument?
