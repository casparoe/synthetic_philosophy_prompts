"Explain Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument against non-reductive physicalism. Your answer should (a) state the argument's key premises, (b) explain why its conclusion is thought to threaten mental causation, and (c) briefly describe how Kim himself tries to avoid the argument's most troubling implications."

Here is the classmate's answer:

"Non-reductive physicalism holds that mental properties are distinct from physical properties — a conclusion Kim himself established by pointing out that a state like pain is multiply realizable, since it could be implemented in carbon-based neurons or in some functionally equivalent silicon system just as well — while still holding that every mental property is dependent on, and instantiated in virtue of, some physical property. The exclusion argument then asks what happens when a mental event, such as a decision, causes a physical event, such as an arm rising. Because the physical domain is causally closed, that arm-rising already has a sufficient physical cause. So the decision and its physical base both look like causes of the very same effect. Kim resists saying this is a genuine case of overdetermination, on the model of a firing squad where each of several shots is independently sufficient to cause death: that sort of coincidence might be tolerable as a one-off, but it is not credible as the standing explanation for every instance of mental causation. Once systematic overdetermination is rejected, and the physical cause is already sufficient by itself, the mental property is squeezed out: it turns out to be epiphenomenal. That is a serious problem, because non-reductive physicalism was specifically trying to preserve mental properties as genuinely causally efficacious, not merely along for the ride.

Kim's own way of avoiding this outcome is to bite the bullet on reduction rather than accept epiphenomenalism: he argues that a mental property should be functionally defined — picked out by its typical causes and effects — and then identified with whichever physical property actually plays that causal role in a given system. Because the mental property just is its physical realizer under this functional specification, there is no longer a second, distinct cause competing with the physical one, so mental causation is secured after all. This functional reduction is Kim's answer for the mental realm quite generally, including our subjective, qualitative experience, which gets reduced to its physical realizer in the same way once its causal role has been specified."
