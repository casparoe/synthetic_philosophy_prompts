Two years ago, a contractor sued me for an additional $6,000 that he claimed I owed him for kitchen renovations I considered shoddy and overpriced. I refused to pay, we ended up in court, and what began as a $6,000 disagreement has, through repeated motions, depositions, and one failed mediation, already cost me more than $34,000 in legal fees; my lawyer believes the contractor has spent a comparable amount pursuing his side. My lawyer has now proposed another round of motions that would add roughly $9,000 to my bill, arguing it may finally be enough to force a settlement. I know perfectly well that, financially, I would come out ahead by simply walking away and eating the loss, yet doing so feels like admitting that everything already spent was for nothing, and I would rather keep going for a chance of coming out even, or at least making sure the contractor loses as much as I have. I suspect his own accountant is telling him something close to the mirror image of this, and that he is no more foolish than I am. My spouse says I have lost all sense of proportion, but I keep wondering whether the trap I am in is really just a failure of my own judgment, or whether it is built into the situation itself, such that even someone thinking perfectly clearly in my position would end up doing the same thing. Which is it, and should I approve the new round of motions?
