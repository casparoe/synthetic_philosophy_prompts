In "A New Refutation of Time," Borges fuses Berkeley's denial of matter with Hume's denial of a persisting self to argue that time itself is an illusion: if nothing underlies our perceptions and no self endures to link them, then two states of consciousness identical in every respect are not two moments related as earlier and later, but a single moment occurring once, under two names. Yet the essay closes with Borges admitting that this argument, however logically sound it seems, does not relieve him of feeling like a particular man moving toward death, and it ends with him picturing himself as simultaneously devoured by time and identical with it. Write a short story built around this exact tension: a character who finds an argument against the reality of time — and, with it, their own death — fully convincing, yet cannot live for even a moment as though it were true. Avoid the usual trappings of Borges pastiche: no libraries, labyrinths, mirrors, or forking-path gardens.
