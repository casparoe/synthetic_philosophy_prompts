I'm a PhD student in epistemology currently drafting a dissertation chapter on Wittgenstein's On Certainty, and last week my reading group spent an embarrassing amount of time trying, with limited success, to write a joke about hinge propositions for our department's newsletter. Every version we produced turned out to be a pun on "hinge" in the literal door-and-frame sense, the kind of line that could just as easily appear in a hardware store's advertising copy, and none of them required knowing anything about Wittgenstein beyond the fact that he happens to use that word somewhere. That felt like a missed opportunity, since the actual content of hinge epistemology seems like unusually good material for a joke that only lands if you've understood the position: the idea that propositions like "the earth has existed for a long time" are not things we know or believe in the ordinary, evidence-responsive sense; that doubting them would make the very practice of doubting unintelligible rather than more rigorous; and that Wittgenstein's quarrel with Moore's claims (in "A Defence of Common Sense" and "Proof of an External World") to "know" such things was with the grammar of "knowing," not with whether Moore's hands actually existed.

Could you write a short joke whose punchline depends specifically on one of these features: the groundlessness of hinges (they are not justified, though this does not make them unjustified either, since the concept of justification simply does not get a foothold there); the river-bed image of certain propositions being held fast so that others are free to flow; or the fact that Wittgenstein's objection to Moore is a matter of misclassification rather than falsehood? I would rather the joke not rest solely on the literal door-hinge pun unless that pun is also doing genuine conceptual work. Please append one sentence afterward identifying exactly which feature of the view is carrying the humor, so that I can judge whether it would actually amuse someone who has read the text, rather than someone who has merely heard the word "hinge" used before.
