For a story on global vaccine access, our graphics desk is split over how to show that one country has treated twice as many patients as another: one designer wants to keep a single syringe icon at a fixed size and simply place two of them side by side, while another wants to keep one icon per country but scale it up until it looks twice as large. The scaled-up version strikes me as more misleading, even though both are meant to encode the same ratio, and I can't quite articulate why. The designer who favors scaling also argues, more broadly, that icon-based graphics are essentially neutral and pre-linguistic, so they read as more objective to an international audience than sentences full of adjectives and framing, and that we should default to pictures over prose wherever we can. That claim feels too convenient to me, but I can't pin down exactly where it goes wrong. Could you help me work through both halves of this disagreement?
