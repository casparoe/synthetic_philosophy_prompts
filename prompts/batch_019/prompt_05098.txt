I'm about two-thirds into a historical novel set in Restoration London, and there's a scene coming up where I want Thomas Hobbes and John Wallis to finally have it out, face to face, over geometry (in real life it was mostly dueling pamphlets for something like twenty years, but for the book I need them in one room). I've read enough now to know the real argument isn't just that Hobbes tried to square the circle and got shown up -- thats just the flashpoint everyone remembers. What its actually about, I think, is that Hobbes won't call something known unless you can say what motion generated it (a line is the path a point traces, a plane the path of a line, and so on), because that's tangled up with his whole matter-in-motion view of the world, whereas Wallis is totally fine getting correct results out of indivisibles and infinite series even when nobody, him included, can really say what those things are. Could you write the scene as an actual dialogue, maybe a page long, where each of them lands real blows on the other's weak spot instead of Wallis just smugly listing off Hobbes's mistakes -- and don't let either one win or back down. I'd like it to end with them still talking past each other, because from what I can tell, that's basically what happened in real life.
