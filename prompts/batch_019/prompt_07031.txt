Ok this one's been living in my head since the spring—I'm noodling on a piece about the dire wolf "de-extinction" saga from Colossal Biosciences: they edited something like 20 genes in gray wolf DNA to give a litter of pups some dire-wolf-ish traits, then annouced the species was basically back (they even named two of the pups Romulus and Remus). A bunch of biologists pushed back hard, saying these are really just gray wolves with a handful of tweaks, not resurrected dire wolves, since actual dire wolves are genetically pretty distant from gray wolves despite the resemblance. It occurs to me this fight is basically the old "species problem" from philosophy of biology playing out in real time—ancestry, interbreeding, phenotype, and ecology all pull apart in a case like this, and nobody's ever agreed which one actually settles what a species is. So: what are the most important implications of the species problem for how I think through (and explain to readers) whether Colossal's animals count as real dire wolves? Not looking for an "it's complicated" cop-out, I want the actual shape of the disagreement.
