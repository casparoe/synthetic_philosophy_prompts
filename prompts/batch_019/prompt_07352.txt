So I went through a Spinoza phase a while back (the Ethics kind of wrecked me, in a good way), and the thing that stuck with me most is his rant against treating humans as a "kingdom within a kingdom" - some separate little sovereign sitting outside nature pulling its own strings, rather than just being one more finite piece of nature doing what nature does. Lately I've been reading about AI safety and ran into this "embedded agency" idea, which is basically the same complaint aimed at AI: you can't cleanly model a real agent as sitting outside its environment, sending actions out and getting observations back like a little cartesian mind, because the agent is actually made of the same stuff as the world its stuck inside, which is apparently where you get headaches like not being able to fit a full model of yourself inside yourself, or having to trust future versions of you that you can't fully predict yet. Is that an actual parallel or am I just pattern-matching on vibes? And if you gave Spinoza a crash course in this stuff, would he see it as his own point getting rediscovered, and would his hardcore determinism (no free will the way people normally mean it) change how he'd think about "agency" here at all?
