I spent my career doing gravitational-wave data analysis, and until I retired I regarded general covariance as a technical constraint on admissible coordinate choices rather than a source of genuine metaphysical trouble. Working through the philosophy of physics literature since then has changed my mind, especially once I reached the hole argument. I follow Earman and Norton's original dilemma well enough: the hole diffeomorphism seems to leave manifold substantivalism committed to a radical, physically idle indeterminism. What I can't yet judge is how the various rejoinders to that dilemma actually compare in strength.

Could you rank the main responses to the hole argument by how successfully each actually defuses the threatened indeterminism, as opposed to merely relabeling it or trivializing the underlying substantivalist/relationalist dispute into a verbal one?
