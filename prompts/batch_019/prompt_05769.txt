I'm on the fiction jury for a well-regarded literary prize, and we're deadlocked over a finalist narrated entirely from inside the mind of a cult leader whose self-justifications talk one of his followers into accepting a death he frames as a "release." The prose is skilled enough that several jurors admit to nodding along with his logic for pages before catching themselves. Two jurors want the book struck from the shortlist on the grounds that a novel has no business making a reader that complicit; two others say unsettling sympathy is exactly what serious literature is supposed to risk, and that our discomfort proves nothing except that the writing works. I keep suspecting our real objections are some unexamined mixture of considered moral judgment and plain disgust wearing moral language, and I can't find a principled way to tell the two apart in this particular case. Please draft the short memo I'll bring to the jury's final session, recommending whether the novel stays on the shortlist, is removed, or advances with a dissent attached, and give reasons the losing side could actually engage with rather than dismiss as either squeamishness or its opposite.
