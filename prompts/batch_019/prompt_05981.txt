Okay so in AP Physics we spent like two days on the EPR paradox and Bell's theorem (the basic "spooky action at a distance" version) and it kind of broke my brain, so afterward I went down a youtube rabbit hole and ended up watching a bunch of Sabine Hossenfelder videos about something called superdeterminism. From what I can tell, the normal story is that Bell's theorem backs you into two options if you want to explain the weird correlations: give up locality (like Bohmian mechanics does, where the pilot wave acts instantly across space) or give up "realism"/hidden variables and just accept particles don't have definite properties until they're measured (Copenhagen-ish). But apparently theres a sneaky third option where you get to keep BOTH locality and hidden variables, by instead giving up something Bell just assumed without really arguing for: "measurement independence," basically the idea that the hidden variables and whatever setting the experimenter randomly picks to measure are statistically independent of each other. If those two things are actually correlated instead (because everything traces back through the same causal past, like the initial conditions of the universe), you can supposedly build a fully local, fully deterministic model that still matches the Bell inequality violations we see in real experiments.

I brought this up to my teacher and she basically laughed it off as unfalsifiable conspiracy-theory physics, which is honestly what most places I looked it up say too, but Hossenfelder has this whole response where she says calling it a "conspiracy" kind of begs the question, since in any fully deterministic universe everything ends up correlated with everything else through shared causal history, so thats not automatically sinister or made up just to dodge Bell's theorem. I don't know enough to tell who's actually right. Could you write me a real, detailed argument -- not just a summary of both sides -- for the position that giving up measurement independence is actually a MORE reasonable response to Bell's theorem than giving up locality? Please genuinely engage with the two objections that make me most skeptical (that it requires a weirdly fine-tuned conspiracy, and that it seems to wreck the whole idea of doing a controlled experiment if the settings aren't really free/random) instead of just waving them away like everyone else seems to.
