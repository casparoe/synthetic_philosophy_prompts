The question I set:
Kelly's growth-optimal betting fraction can be gotten at in two different ways: (i) as the fraction that almost surely maximizes long-run wealth growth over repeated bets (Kelly's own argument), and (ii) as the fraction that maximizes single-period expected utility for one specific utility function. (a) Name that utility function and explain what's special about it that makes (i) and (ii) coincide. (b) Sketch Samuelson's objection to using that coincidence to recommend Kelly betting to investors generally. (c) "Geometric rationality" tries to justify maximizing a geometric expectation as a general rationality norm rather than just a betting heuristic, partly via an analogy to Nash bargaining. Sketch the analogy and give one problem with it.

The student's answer, verbatim:
(a) It's log utility. Log is the only utility function where the fraction that's optimal for a single bet stays the same no matter how many more times you're going to bet -- that's exactly what lets a log-wealth bettor ignore the future and just re-optimize greedily each round, and it's why Kelly's rule gets called "the log utility strategy" even though Kelly's own derivation never mentions utility at all.
(b) Samuelson's objection was basically that "you'll almost surely end up ahead if you bet this way forever" is only a fact about the limit as the number of bets goes to infinity, and real investors only get to make finitely many bets in a lifetime, so a long-run argument can't tell a finite investor what to do today.
(c) The Nash-bargaining move treats each possible world you might end up in as a separate party with a stake in the outcome, and instead of averaging their payoffs (ordinary expected value) has them bargain to the solution that maximizes the product of their gains -- a geometric-mean-flavored objective that spits the Kelly fraction back out as a special case. The obvious problem: outside genuinely repeated, poolable bets there's no law-of-large-numbers story backing up why the parties should settle for a geometric split rather than an arithmetic one, so the move risks just relabeling the conclusion you wanted as a "bargaining solution."
