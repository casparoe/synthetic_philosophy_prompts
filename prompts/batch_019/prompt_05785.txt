I've fallen down a bit of a rabbit hole with old infographics lately, and that's how I first ran into Otto Neurath and his Isotype project from Vienna in the 1920s and 30s: rows of small, identical pictograms -- workers, ships, sacks of grain -- used to show statistics to people who couldn't read a line graph, or who didn't even share a common spoken language. What caught me off guard is that Neurath wasn't primarily a designer; he was an economist and philosopher tied up with the Vienna Circle, and from what I can tell he had an actual philosophical case for why pictures, rather than words, should carry a good deal of everyday factual communication, since he thought words were vague, loaded with connotation, and tied to one's own language and schooling in a way pictures didn't have to be. At the same time, he seems to have been careful never to claim that Isotype was a full language in its own right -- he called it something closer to a "helping language" -- and as far as I can tell it mostly stuck to showing quantities and simple comparisons, through repeating a small icon rather than resizing it, rather than anything resembling a negation, an "if...then," or a general law.

What I can't work out on my own is whether that self-imposed limit says something about pictures in principle, or whether it was just the modest, practical scope Neurath happened to choose for a project aimed at factory workers and museum visitors. Could a picture-based sign system ever, even in theory, do the logical work that sentences do -- express negation, conditionals, generality -- or is any such system doomed to stay parasitic on a verbal language to fill in exactly that work? I'd like you to write me an essay that takes this question seriously: lay out the best reasons Neurath, and anyone sympathetic to his project, had for distrusting ordinary language and favoring pictures for conveying facts, and then lay out the best reasons for doubting that a purely pictorial system could ever match a verbal or symbolic language's logical range. Please don't leave it at "both sides have a point" -- reach an actual verdict on whether picture languages of Neurath's kind are limited only in the execution he happened to choose, or limited in principle, and defend that verdict.
