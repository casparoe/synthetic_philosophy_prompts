This term I'm taking an upper-level epistemology seminar built around Timothy Williamson's *Knowledge and Its Limits*, and we have just spent two weeks on his argument against the luminosity of mental states. I follow the mechanics of his own case well enough: because your belief that you are in some state has to remain safe across the neighbouring moments you cannot discriminate from the one you are actually in, a long enough chain of such indiscriminable moments (he runs his through a room that gradually warms over the course of a morning, tracking whether you still feel cold) will eventually force a point at which you are in the state but not in a position to know it, on pain of contradicting the fact that you are clearly no longer in it by the end of the chain. In seminar, one of my classmates dismissed the whole argument as a trick that happens to work only for continuously-varying perceptual states like temperature, and that tells us nothing about knowledge itself — i.e., about the KK principle Williamson also wants to reject, according to which knowing that p entails knowing that one knows that p. Our instructor demurred rather than settling the dispute, so I would like to pursue it myself for this week's response paper.

Could you first construct a genuinely new example, drawn from a domain Williamson does not use in the book — so not temperature, and not any other continuously-varying sensory magnitude such as length, loudness, or weight — of some state that a margin-for-error argument of this style would show to be non-luminous? Please spell out the sorites-style chain of cases explicitly, state the margin-for-error premise you are relying on and why it is plausible for the state you have chosen, and carry out the reductio against luminosity step by step, the way Williamson does with his own example. Then, in a closing paragraph, tell me whether that same construction can be run one level up — with "knowing that p" itself as the target state, rather than some more basic condition like feeling cold — to produce a direct counterexample to KK, or whether there is some principled reason the construction stalls once knowledge itself is the target.
