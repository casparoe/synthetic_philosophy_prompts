I volunteer with a small ham-radio-adjacent search-and-rescue outfit, and lately I've been chewing on something that came up during a training exercise. We use a pretty standard Bayesian search-theory setup: a probability grid over quadrants where a missing hiker might be, updated as tips come in, with search effort allocated to maximize expected probability of detection. During a tabletop drill, dispatch relayed a witness tip that was conditional rather than a direct sighting — something like "if she carried on past the fork instead of turning back, she almost certainly took the ridge trail over the creek trail." Half the team wanted to fold this in with straightforward Jeffrey conditionalization on the conditional statement; the other half wanted a minimum-cross-entropy update that keeps the rest of the grid "as untouched as possible." Our resident rationalist-blog-reader (guilty) piped up that this is just a real-world instance of van Fraassen's old Judy Benjamin problem, and that the max-entropy update is notorious for sliding probability mass around in quadrants the tip never mentioned, in a way that's hard to defend to a search commander who just wants to know where to send volunteers at 6am.

We now have to write an actual SOP for how the team handles conditional tips like this before the next callout, and I got roped into drafting it since I'm the one who won't shut up about the philosophy of it. Could you write the memo for our methodology working group? I want it to recommend one concrete updating procedure (or a clear decision rule for choosing between procedures depending on how the tip is phrased), justify the choice against the alternative, and not pretend the losing method is stupid — it should say honestly why smart people keep reaching for minimum-cross-entropy updating even knowing about the Judy Benjamin result, and where our SOP should compensate for it.
