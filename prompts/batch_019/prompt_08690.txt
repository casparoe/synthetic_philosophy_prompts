I'm the philosopher on the ethics panel that our armed forces' procurement command consults before certifying autonomous weapons systems. In a recent field trial, a targeting classifier flagged a vehicle as hostile; the operator, working inside a mandated four-second window and shown only a "92% confidence" readout, authorized the strike. The vehicle was a civilian tractor whose heat signature the classifier handled poorly -- a known failure mode the developer had never flagged for disclosure. The operator followed protocol exactly; the officer who set the four-second window was implementing standing doctrine; and the developer notes the system performed within its documented statistical tolerances. Some panel members conclude that since no one meets the ordinary conditions for being held responsible, the system cannot be certified, whatever its battlefield value; others think this proves too much, since comparable gaps already attend ordinary chains of command. Draft the panel's recommendation -- certify, certify with conditions, or decline to certify -- with reasoning the developer and the chain of command could each actually respond to.
