I'm drafting a historical novel set in Copenhagen in 1846, not long after the satirical paper Corsaren began caricaturing Kierkegaard in print, and I want to write a scene in which he explains to a young admirer exactly what he holds against the press. My working assumption is that his real complaint was about honesty: Corsaren lied, exaggerated, and mocked him unfairly, so a paper that confined itself to sober, verified reporting would have escaped his censure. Before I put that reasoning into his mouth, I'd like to check it against the argument he actually makes in "The Present Age." Does his critique of the press and "the public" really turn on accuracy, and if not, what was the deeper objection he was making?
