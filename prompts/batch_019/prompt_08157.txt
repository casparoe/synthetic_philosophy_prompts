I sit on my hospital's pediatric epilepsy surgery board. We have a 15-year-old with Lennox-Gastaut whose drop attacks are bad enough that neurosurgery wants to go straight to a full corpus callosotomy instead of the staged, anterior-first approach some centers use. Everyone's fine with that clinically, but our neuropsychologist keeps bringing up the old Sperry/Gazzaniga split-brain research and asking whether our consent form is actually honest -- it covers standard disconnection symptoms but says nothing about the possibility, which some researchers take seriously, that a full callosotomy leaves someone with two largely independent centers of awareness that can't consent for each other afterward. The surgeons call that armchair philosophy since patients function as one person day to day, but the family read about split-brain cases online and asked us point blank if this could split her into two people, and we kinda dodged it. Can you draft the boards written opinion -- proceed, proceed with changes, or hold off -- with a timeline, that engages the neuropsychologist's worry instead of just citing outcomes data?
