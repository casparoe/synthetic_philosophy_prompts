I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, but I've been working through Plato's dialogues on audiobook during my commute after a friend with an actual philosophy degree got tired of me only ever being able to reference the cave allegory. I recently got through the opening two books of the Laws, and one thing caught me completely off guard: the Athenian Stranger spends a remarkable amount of time defending regulated wine-drinking, arguing that supervised symposia can train self-control, reveal a person's true underlying character (it reads like an early, more careful version of the "in vino veritas" idea), and even help older citizens loosen up enough to take part in choral singing and dancing as a form of moral education. That felt hard to square with my hazier memory of the Republic, where Socrates seems much warier of drunkenness, going so far as to argue that the guardians shouldn't even be trained to imitate or perform characters who are drunk, on the grounds that imitating something long enough during your upbringing turns it into a permanent trait of your character.

Rather than just assuming Plato contradicted himself, I'd like to actually understand the two positions on their own terms. Could you compare his treatment of drunkenness in the Republic with his treatment of it in the Laws, laying out the specific arguments he gives in each case? I'm especially curious whether the two views are genuinely in tension or whether they can be reconciled once you take into account the different aims and audiences of the two works, e.g., guardians being shaped from infancy through tight control over what they're allowed to imitate, versus ordinary adult citizens of a more realistic "second-best" city being deliberately tested and trained. And if you had to judge one of the two treatments as more philosophically defensible, or as fitting more comfortably with Plato's broader moral psychology (what I recall being called the tripartite soul, plus his views on habituation and the role of pleasure and pain in shaping virtue), which would you pick, and why?
