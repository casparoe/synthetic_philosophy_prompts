Two people in my rationalist meetup's reading group got into it after a session that covered both the Euthyphro dilemma and Yudkowsky's old Coherent Extrapolated Volition (CEV) posts, and I haven't been able to stop turning it over since (very on-brand for this group, I know). Roughly how it went:

(1) Josh said CEV doesn't dodge Euthyphro, it just inherits it wholesale: either "good" means "whatever CEV outputs when you run the extrapolation on humanity," in which case a monstrous convergence would count as good by definition, which is nuts -- or "good" is independent of CEV's output and CEV is just a guess at tracking it, in which case it isn't actually a solution to value specification, it's an unverified proxy, and we still haven't said what it's a proxy *for*.

(2) Mira said that's a false choice: "what I'd want if I knew more, thought faster, and was more the person I wished I were" isn't arbitrary the way "whatever the gods happen to love" is, because its preference under idealized epistemic conditions, not brute approval -- nobody calls "what a fully informed rational agent would believe" arbitrary just because its stated in terms of an agent.

(3) Josh said sure, but who sets the idealization parameters -- how much extrapolation, whose volition counts, how disagreement across billions of people gets resolved even after idealizing? None of that's forced by logic, so whoever picks it will pick parameters that spit out answers they already like. That just relocates the arbitrariness from "the gods' say-so" to "the choice of extrapolation procedure" -- and this is a real, standard objection to ideal observer theories in academic metaethics, not something he's inventing to be difficult.

(4) Mira said fine, but that shows every substantive theory of value pays a similar cost somewhere -- moral realists owe a story about how we ever access the independent facts too. If Euthyphro-level purity is the bar, nothing clears it, including whatever Josh would propose instead. So at most this is a reason for CEV's proponents to be modest about what it guarantees, not a reason to ditch it for some other target.
