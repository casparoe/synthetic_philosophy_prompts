A friend and I spent most of a dinner arguing about whether Jean-Jacques Rousseau would have treated prestige streaming drama, unscripted reality television, and influencer livestreaming as just more of the theatre he fought to keep out of Geneva, or whether some of them might actually satisfy the participatory civic festival he proposed instead. I'd like your reasoned verdict, argued from the actual case he makes in the Letter to d'Alembert on the Theatre: that drama cannot reform morals because playwrights must flatter the tastes of the audience they depend on, that professional performance -- especially by actresses -- corrupts the performer's own character, and that his preferred public festivals surpass theatre precisely because they dissolve the line between spectator and performer. Apply those three arguments to each of the three formats, moving from the most straightforward case to the least, flagging where an argument stops applying or holds only in a modified form, and end by saying which format he would have condemned most fiercely and which, if any, comes closest to his festival ideal.
