My great-uncle Gerrit died three weeks ago at eighty-six, and because I'm "the writer in the family" I've somehow ended up on the hook for the eulogy, which is a problem, because Gerrit spent almost fifty years forging paintings -- mostly minor Dutch Golden Age stuff -- and was good enough that at least one museum still has a piece of his hanging under a real painter's name and has no idea. I've been quietly mining his life for a novel for a couple of years now (my writing group has heard way more about him than they signed up for), so I know his views better than I'd like to. He left a note, found by my aunt, saying he didn't want whoever spoke to apologize for him or treat his life's work like a string of victimless pranks. He was proud of it right to the end, and his standing line was that a forgery good enough to fool the experts had achieved everything the original achieved, and that "authenticity" was basically a story the market tells about provenance and money, not something you can actually see or feel in the paint itself. He genuinely believed that -- it wasn't just a con man's excuse.

The trouble is the room. Half of it is family and old neighbors who just want to hear that he loved us and cry a normal amount. The other half includes people he actually fooled, among them a retired auction-house specialist and the conservator who spent two years proving that one of his "Cuyps" was fake before anybody believed her, and they will absolutely be listening for whether I let him off the hook. I don't want to lie for him up there, and I don't want to turn a funeral into a seminar on weather he was an artist or just a very patient liar, but I can't just ignore the note either. Could you write the eulogy for me? Five to seven minutes spoken, it has to actually comfort the ordinary half of the room, and it can't dodge the real question his whole life poses -- what, if anything, is lost when a copy is good enough that no one, including the eye, can tell -- by pretending that's an easy question, or by refusing to answer it at all.
