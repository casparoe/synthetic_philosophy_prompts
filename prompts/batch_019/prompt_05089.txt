Write a dialogue between Kepler and a more traditional astrologer colleague, set while the two are stuck drawing up a nativity together for a patron whos waiting on it. Kepler wants to chuck the zodiac signs, the houses, and planetary rulerships as arbitrary bookkeeping with no real basis in the sky, but he still insists the aspects (the angles between planets) are the real deal, because he thinks the soul resonates with harmonic geometric ratios. He always framed his own position as a kind of third way between astrology's despisers and its true believers, so make him actually earn that label when his colleague presses him on why "angles good, signs bad" isn't just as arbitrary a cut as anything the old astrologers drew. Give it a real back-and-forth -- both of them sharp, actually responding to each other's points instead of restating their side or caving.
