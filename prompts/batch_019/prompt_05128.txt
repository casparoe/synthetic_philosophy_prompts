Quick one for a piece I'm noodling on about the arrow of time. I've been down a rabbit hole on the 'Boltzmann brain' problem in cosmology — basically, if the universe just sits in a high-entropy, near-equilibrium state forever, random fluctuations should eventually cook up way more isolated, deluded brains with fake memories than real evolved observers like us. Funny thing is, Boltzmann kind of invented this move himself: to explain why we're in a low-entropy patch at all, he floated the idea that we might just be living inside a rare statistical dip in an otherwise equilibrium universe. So if someone explained the modern Boltzmann-brain twist to him — that his own logic makes him way more likely to be a momentary fluctuating brain than an actual guy with a real past in Vienna — what do you reckon he'd say? Would he bite the bullet, ditch the fluctuation idea, or wriggle out some other way, and how would that square with how he actually argued back agaisnt Loschmidt and Zermelo?
