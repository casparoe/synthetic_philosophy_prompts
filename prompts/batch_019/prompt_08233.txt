Ok, this has been bugging me since I first read through the quantum physics sequence. If many-worlds is true, why isn't quantum suicide a totally free lunch? Rig a device that vaporizes you instantly unless a radioactive atom decays in the next second -- since a dead brain can't have experiences, "you" should always find yourself in the branch where it decayed and your still alive, every single time, no matter the odds. So betting your life savings on a quantum coin-flip where tails means instant vaporization should be a great deal from a first-person point of view, independent of the payout. Every rationalist writeup I've found just says "don't actually try this" for reasons about grieving relatives, not because the reasoning itself is wrong. Is there an actual flaw in the logic, or is everyone just too chicken to bite the bullet?
