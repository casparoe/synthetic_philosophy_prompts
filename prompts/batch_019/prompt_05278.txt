My grandma died on Monday and my mom asked me to give her eulogy this weekend, which is kinda terrifying because half the room is gonna be her old philosophy department collegues and the other half is just family who's never even heard the word epistemology. She spent her whole career arguing against luminosity (the idea that if you're in a mental state, you automatically know you're in it) and against the KK principle (if you know something, you know that you know it) -- lots of margin-for-error stuff about how you can't always tell you're even the tiniest bit less sure of something than you were a minute ago, so nobody gets perfect self-knowledge, not even about their own mind. Once, half-joking, she told my mom that if anyone stood up at her funeral and said "she knew how much she was loved" or "deep down she always knew we'd be fine," she'd come back and haunt us, because that's literally the exact kind of certainty claim she spent 40 years proving nobodys entitled to make. So I need a eulogy that actually comforts the non-philosophers in the room without me ever saying she "just knew" something in a way her old colleagues would catch immediately, and that would honestly make her mad. Can you write it for me?
