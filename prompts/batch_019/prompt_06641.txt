I'm a software engineer at a mid-size mobile game studio, and I sit on the small internal group — two designers, a data scientist, and an outside clinical psychologist — that must sign off before we ship anything touching player mental health. We've built a model that flags accounts as elevated-risk for what the WHO's ICD-11 labels "Gaming Disorder," combining play data with an in-app questionnaire worded to track the official criteria (impaired control over play, prioritizing it over other interests, continuing despite harm); flagged accounts get a nudge toward setting play limits. Our psychologist warns that once players know the flag exists, some start describing ordinary play as compulsive on the questionnaire, apparently because being told they're "at risk" changes how they narrate their own habits, while others steer their answers away from anything resembling the criteria to avoid being tagged again — either way, the flagged group's self-reports stop being independent evidence of what the model is supposed to measure, and next quarter's retraining will bake that distortion back into the model itself. We decide this Friday whether to ship the feature as designed, shelve it, or redesign the questionnaire and retraining pipeline. Please draft the memo I'll circulate beforehand: take a clear position with a concrete plan and timeline, and engage honestly with the strongest argument against whichever option you choose.
