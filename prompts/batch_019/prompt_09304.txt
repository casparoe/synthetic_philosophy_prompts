I have always been drawn to David Lewis's thesis of Humean Supervenience: that, fundamentally, the world is nothing but a vast mosaic of local qualities distributed across spacetime, with laws, causation, and everything else supervening on that pattern and nothing more — a thesis Lewis himself thought faced its most serious threat from the long-distance correlations responsible for the confirmed violations of Bell's inequalities. Supposing Bell's theorem is correctly read as foreclosing any Einstein-style local hidden-variable rescue of separability, please describe the most important implications of the theorem for someone who, like me, would like to preserve something recognizably Humean in the metaphysics of laws and causation. In particular, I would like your assessment of whether strategies such as relocating the Humean mosaic onto a high-dimensional configuration space on which the wavefunction itself counts as a local quality, admitting entanglement facts as new primitives within an otherwise Humean base, or falling back on a modest best-system account of laws that stays neutral on separability, amount to genuine rescues of Humean Supervenience or merely relocate the original problem — and which, if any, you find most defensible.
