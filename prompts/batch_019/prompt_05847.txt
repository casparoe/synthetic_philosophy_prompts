So my mom passed away back in February and left her lake house — the one me and my brother Danny grew up going to every summer — to the two of us jointly, 50/50, with no other instructions in the will about how to actually divide it up. We've spent weeks going back and forth on whether to sell it or keep it, and if we keep it, who gets which weeks, and it's honestly gotten way more tense than I expected. Yesterday Danny's wife (who works at a law firm, so she's basically fluent in this stuff even though she isn't the lawyer on our case) told him he should send me and the estate attorney a written statement today saying he "will not accept anything less than exclusive July and August, full stop, non-negotiable" — basically staking his claim in writing, in front of witnesses, so he can't be talked down later without looking like he folded.

My gut reaction was to just do the same thing back — fire off my own "this is my final position" email before he does, so I'm not the one caught without a stake in the ground. A friend of mine who did a couple years in corporate finance is straight up telling me to do exactly this, that whoever locks in their number first basically wins the negotiation. But something about both of us racing to nail ourselves to a position before we've even really hashed anything out feels less like strategy and more like a countdown to a wreck — like, we obviously cant both get exclusive July AND August, so if we each fire off our "non-negotiable, no take-backs" letter this week, we'll just end up with two commitments that literally can't both be honored, neither of us able to back off without loosing face in front of the whole family, and then we're paying a mediator (or worse, lawyers) to sort out a mess that didn't even need to exist. Or it just permanently wrecks things between us, and my kids stop seeing their cousins over a cabin that isn't even worth that much.

Am I overthinking this, or is there something genuinely off about the "whoever commits first wins" advice we're both getting? And practically — should I go ahead and fire off my own non-negotiable letter too, just hold off and see what he does first, or is there a totally different way I should be approaching this so we don't both end up backed into corners we can't get out of?
