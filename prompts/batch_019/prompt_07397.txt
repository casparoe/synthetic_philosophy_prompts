ok so I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole on infinite ethics lately, started with Bostrom's old paper on what he calls "infinitarian paralysis" and then fell into a bunch of old LW/EA-forum threads riffing on it. one thing that keeps bugging me is the move people call the "reordering problem" -- basically, if the universe contains infinitely many pockets of value and disvalue, the "total" amount of good you get by summing them all up depends on the order you add them in, kind of like how a conditionally convergent series can be rearranged (Riemann's rearrangement theorem) to sum to literally any number you want, or to diverge. people usually treat this as basically a death blow to any additive/aggregative theory of value once you take spatial or temporal infinities seriously, and I think thats too quick.

can you make the strongest case you can that the reordering problem does NOT actually sink aggregative consequentialism in an infinite universe? I don't want the easy dodge of "just restrict the theory to your causal neighborhood" -- I want an argument for why theres some principled, non-arbitrary way to fix (or avoid needing) an ordering even under full infinitude. lean on whatever actually works -- physics, math, decision theory, whatever -- just engage with the problem head on instead of hand-waving it away.
