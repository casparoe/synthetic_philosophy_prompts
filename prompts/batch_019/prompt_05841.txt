So for my democratic theory seminar we've been stuck on the 'boundary problem' - basically, democracy is supposed to be about the people deciding things, but you need an answer to who counts as 'the people' before you can even hold that vote, so democracy cant be the thing that settles its own membership. This actually came up again last night when I was arguing with my roommate about immigration policy. Anyway, there's two views in our reading that I think would make a really good back-and-forth: one side says that since a state's border and immigration enforcement literally coerces outsiders too (turning them away, detaining them, deporting them), those outsiders have some claim to a say over that policy, meaning no state gets to unilaterally decide its own membership rules. The other side says turning someone away isn't 'coercion' in the sense that generates that kind of claim - it's just not facilitating their plans, more like a club not letting you join than like threatening you - so a bounded people can make that call about itself, same as it makes plenty of other calls about itself without asking outsiders.

Could you write me a dialogue between the two of them hashing this out? Stage it somewhere concrete, like both of them giving competing testimony to a committee that's about to vote on a stricter border/asylum bill, where none of the people who'd actually be turned away get any say in that vote. Don't let the "it's not coercion, it's just non-facilitation" move just sit there unchallenged - have the other side really try to take it apart (bring in the fences, the guards, the literal force involved in deportation, whatever) and make the self-determination side answer that specific challenge instead of just restating their opening line, so that one move gets like two or three real rounds before the dialogue moves on. Aim for something like 15-20 exchanges total, and I'd honestly rather it end with both sides still standing than have one obviously win, since I don't think anyone's actually closed this argument off.
