A friend of mine, Daniel, is celebrating twenty years sober next month, and he's asked me to say a few words at the party. Shortly before he asked, another friend and I got into a long argument neither of us won. She maintained that what Daniel overcame was weakness of will in the strict philosophical sense: he judged, correctly and continuously, that drinking was worse for him, and simply failed, again and again, to act on that judgment. I argued that hyperbolic discounting describes it more accurately, and more kindly: there was never a real contradiction in him, only a present self and a future self with mismatched discount curves, so what sobriety changed was which self got to decide, not how much willpower he had. She replied that my account quietly excuses every relapse as 'rational, for whichever self was having it,' and can't explain why Daniel, sober, describes his drinking as a betrayal of what he actually wanted, rather than another person's reasonable choice. I had no good reply. Some guests are in recovery and talk about will and surrender every day; others have never considered any of this. Please write the toast. It should run about five minutes, stay warm, and genuinely honor what Daniel did, and work for both halves of the room without quietly settling our argument by treating either picture of him as simply the true one.
