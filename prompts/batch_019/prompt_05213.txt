So in my phil of religion class we just went through Schellenberg's hiddenness argument - basically that if God were perfectly loving, there wouldn't be people who are genuinely open to believing in him but just don't, because the evidence isn't there for them, and since those people clearly exist that's supposed to count as evidence against a perfectly loving God existing. Right after class i remembered Pascal has that whole bit in the Pensées about God being deliberately hidden - enough clues for people who are earnestly seeking, not enough for people who aren't - and he treats that as totally fitting instead of a problem. It's bugging me bc it feels like Pascal is basically granting Schellenberg's whole setup (that hiddenness is real and God could reveal more if he wanted) and then just asserting its fine, without really explaining why a loving God would let genuinely honest seekers get stuck on the wrong side of that line through no fault of their own. Does Pascal actually have the resources to answer Schellenberg here, or is he kind of handing him most of what he needs without realizing it?
