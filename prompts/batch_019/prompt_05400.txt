I'm teaching a seminar this term on early modern women philosophers, and this week we did Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz's "Respuesta a Sor Filotea de la Cruz" alongside the letter that provoked it -- the one the Bishop of Puebla published under that nun's pseudonym, praising her mind but nudging her to redirect it from secular study toward sacred things. One of my students flattened the bishop into "just a sexist who got outargued," and I think that's too quick -- his position reads to me as less "women can't reason" and more a worry, something like the old curiositas vs. studiositas distinction, about whether dazzling secular erudition in a nun is vanity poorly ordered toward God rather then a proper use of her gifts.

Could you write me a dialogue that takes his side more seriously than that? Put Sor Juana and "Sor Filotea" in the same room -- pretend that instead of trading letters a year apart, he actually shows up at the locutorio grille and they have it out face to face, so it's real back-and-forth rather than two speeches stapled together.

On his side, I want him to press both the Pauline "let women keep silence in the churches" line and the vanity worry, not just one of them. On her side, she has to actually answer both, not just handle the Paul verse and change the subject -- I want her argument that the sciences form a chain or ladder so that you cant really do theology well without logic, physics, rhetoric and the rest, plus her point that women teaching women privately is safer and more proper than the available alternative, not less. End with him conceding whatever he'd honestly have to concede on the arguments, but still holding on to a residual practical worry about what the Church can be seen to tolerate from a nun in public -- don't let him just lose the scene, since historically he didn't (she gives up the books and the scholarship a few years after this). A page or so is plenty.
