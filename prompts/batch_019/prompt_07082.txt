Got into a two-hour arguement with my friend Priya at the bar last night about something that's definately going on in my life right now, not just a hypothetical. She's finishing a philosophy MA and the second I described my situation she goes "that's literally the Wilt Chamberlain thing from Nozick" and wouldn't let it go for the rest of the night.

Here's the situation: I'm on the five-person steering council of a walking-tour co-op a few of us started years back (we call ourselves Groundwork Tours, mostly historic-district and cemetery tours). Our founding charter splits all tour revenue equally across all nine guides every month, no exceptions, basically because half of us quit the old commercial tour company over management playing favorites with whoever was the "star" guide that season. Now one of our guides, Marisol, blew up on TikTok for her cemetery tour, and tourists book weeks out asking specifically for "the Marisol tour," happily paying a $15 premium over the normal ticket to get her instead of whoever's actually scheduled that day. She and a couple others think that premium is basically hers -- a tip for her specifically, since nobody's forcing anyone to pay it. The rest of the council is furious: she's doing this through our shared booking site, under our name, covered by our insurance, riding a reputation the whole group spent years building, and if this is allowed everybody's going to start angling for their own side premiums within a year. Priya's take was that once you admit tourists are free to pay whatever they want for something that's genuinely Marisol's to sell, you can't also insist the payout stays equal after the fact -- liberty upsets patterns, entitlement wins, end of story. I pushed back that it's not that clean: tourists pay our booking system, not Marisol's hand, so it's not obvious the premium is a transfer "to her" rather than to the co-op for a specific product, and our charter actually has a clause letting the council reclaim earnings "attributable to inequities in access to co-op resources or reputation" -- none of us can agree whether that clause was ever meant to cover something like this.

I got stuck writing the actual memo our council votes on in three weeks, so figured I'd get a real draft from you instead of winging it hungover. I want it addressed to the council, recommending one specific course of action (not a shrug of "it depends"), with an actual timeline for phasing it in, and whichever way you land, I want it to seriously engage with the strongest version of the losing side's case -- Priya's liberty/entitlement point if you side with pooling, or the shared-reputation point if you side with letting Marisol keep it -- instead of just namedropping Nozick and calling it a day.
