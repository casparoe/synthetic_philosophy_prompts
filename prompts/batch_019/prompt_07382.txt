I've spent the better part of a decade lurking on LessWrong and Astral Codex Ten, which probably explains why I can't just accept a generous family gesture without turning it into a decision problem. Here's the situation. My grandmother, seventy-eight and sharp as ever despite a couple of recent health scares, has offered to pay off what's left of my student loans — a little over sixty thousand dollars — in one lump sum, transferred this week. There's no formal string attached, in the sense that she's promised she will never bring it up again, never ask for proof, and never hold it against me if things change later. Her one condition is that I actually mean it, tonight, when I tell her that I intend to make the six-hour drive to see her every single weekend for as long as she's alive. She's oddly, almost unsettlingly good at telling the difference between someone sincerely intending something and someone just saying it to get what they want — she's been right about this with everyone in our family for as long as I can remember, to the point where nobody bothers trying to put one over on her anymore — and she's told me plainly that if she senses I'm only telling her what she wants to hear, the offer disappears.

Here's what's bothering me. Say I sit with her tonight and, as far as I can tell, genuinely form the intention to make that drive every weekend. She's satisfied, and the loans are gone by Monday. On her own account, nothing further will ever hinge on whether I actually go: she's explicitly taken tracking, guilt, and any future mention of the money off the table for good. When I try to picture an ordinary Saturday six months from now, after a long week at work, I honestly can't locate a reason I'd have at that point to get in the car rather than stay home — the debt will be gone regardless, and she's deliberately removed every other form of leverage. My sister's advice is to just tell her whatever gets the money moving and "figure out the visiting part later"; but given how reliably my grandmother reads people, I'm not sure that's even a strategy available to me — it isn't obvious I could fake this even if I wanted to. My partner's advice is the opposite: that this is no different from any other promise, that people commit to things now that they won't necessarily feel like doing later, and that's simply what commitment is. Neither answer sits right with me, and I can't quite say why.

So I'd like your read on this. Is my partner correct that this is just ordinary promise-keeping and I'm overanalyzing a nice gesture? Is my sister's plan even a coherent description of a mental state I could actually be in tonight, as opposed to just a way of describing lying? And if I do, honestly, manage to form the intention this evening, does the fact that I can already predict my future self will see no reason whatsoever to follow through mean that the intention isn't rational — or, for that matter, that it was never really a genuine intention to begin with? Practically speaking, I'd also just like to know what you think I should say to her tonight.
