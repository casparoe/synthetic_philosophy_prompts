So I went down a rabbit hole on Newton's bucket and the whole absolute-vs-relative space debate (apparently theres a fancy name for it, substantivalism vs relationism, which I only learned like yesterday). Everyone always tells it the same way: bucket spins, water goes concave, the bucket's walls obviously aren't causing that, so space itself must be a "thing." But there's two lesser-known bits I like way more: Newton also has a version with two globes tied together by a rope, spinning in an otherwise completely empty universe, where the tension in the rope supposedly reveals the rotation even with nothing else around to compare against. And Mach's actual reply to the bucket wasn't just "nah, it's all relative" -- it was more like "fine, keep the bucket still and spin the entire shell of fixed stars around it instead, and tell me the water doesn't still curve."
