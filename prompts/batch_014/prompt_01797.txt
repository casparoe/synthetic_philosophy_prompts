ok so i've been on a bit of an epistemology kick lately (no formal philosophy training, just a guy who went down a wikipedia/SEP rabbit hole one night and now can't stop) and there's two ideas that keep getting mentioned together like they're basically the same thing: "pragmatic encroachment" and "epistemic contextualism." Both of them are apparently reactions to the same weird puzzle: how can two people have literally the exact same evidence for something, but one of them "knows" it and the other one doesn't, just because more is riding on being right for one of them? The go-to example is always the bank hours thing — if it doesn't matter whether the bank's open Saturday you'll happily say "yeah I know, I was there last week," but if your rent check bounces unless its open, suddenly your a lot less comfortable claiming you "know" that, even though the evidence didn't change one bit.

From what I can piece together, contextualism says the word "know" works kind of like "tall" or "empty" — the standard shifts depending on who's talking and what conversation they're in, so when the stakes-conscious person says "I guess I don't really know," they're just invoking a stricter standard for the word, not reporting some actual change in the other guy's situation. Pragmatic encroachment, meanwhile, seems to want to say the person's actual situation changes — that knowing genuinely gets harder when more is on the line for you, period, independent of who's asking or judging. I asked a friend who did a philosophy minor in college and he just shrugged and said "eh, they're basically the same vibe," which did not help at all. Meanwhile half the "explainer" type stuff I find online just restates the bank example without ever pinning down what's actually at stake (pun intended) between the two views.

So here's what I'm hoping you can help with: can you lay out, in plain english, where these two views actually agree and where they genuinely part ways underneath the shared vocabulary? And — this is the part I really can't figure out on my own — could you construct a case where the two theories would actually spit out different verdicts, since every example I've run into seems to fit both equally well? Finally, if you had to bet on one of them being closer to the truth about how knowledge actually works, which one would you pick, and why?
