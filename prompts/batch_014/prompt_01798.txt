Two colleagues of mine, Renata and Owen, got into a real argument at dinner last week over a story about a mutual acquaintance, Dana. Dana had spent much of a recent faculty meeting denouncing a colleague who had presented a postdoc's idea as his own, calling it "straightforward professional theft." Owen then mentioned—not maliciously, just as a fact several people at the table already half-knew—that Dana had done something similar herself years ago: she had quietly presented an intern's suggestion as her own at an internal workshop, something she has never acknowledged to anyone.

What followed went roughly like this. (1) Owen said this meant Dana had no standing to deliver that verdict on her colleague, whatever its content. (2) Renata answered that the act's wrongness doesn't depend on who names it, so if the charge was true, it was true regardless of Dana's history. (3) Owen granted that, but held the two questions apart: being right that something is wrong and being entitled to press the charge are different matters, and it is Dana's never having owned up to her own case that disqualifies her on the second score. (4) Renata asked what work this "owning up" requirement was really doing, since Owen couldn't show that Dana had ever consciously drawn the parallel to her own past conduct—can you be a hypocrite about a comparison you never actually made? (5) Owen replied that what matters is the objective gap between the standard Dana invoked and the standard she herself met, not what she happened to be thinking at the time; Renata's parting shot was that the two acts aren't obviously comparable anyway—an aside at an internal workshop is not a colleague's credit-taking in a meeting where a promotion is on the line—and that without some principled account of how similar two cases must be, Owen's view collapses into "you may never criticize a wrong you have ever committed in any form."

Neither of them budged, and I genuinely can't tell who comes out ahead, not least because this bears on real disputes in the philosophical literature on the standing to blame that I would rather test against a concrete case than the stylized ones I reach for in my seminar.
