For my IB Extended Essay in philosophy, I've been reading about Kripke's claim that 'water is H2O' is necessarily true yet only knowable a posteriori, since it took empirical chemistry to discover that water is H2O. I've also been reading David Chalmers's work on two-dimensional semantics, where a term's primary intension is supposed to be graspable a priori, just from understanding the concept, without needing to know any empirical facts about which world is actual. Since we can therefore work out the primary intensions of 'water' and 'H2O' from the armchair, it seems that comparing those two primary intensions should let us establish a priori whether the terms pick out the same thing, making 'water is H2O' knowable a priori after all and collapsing Kripke's category of the necessary a posteriori rather than explaining it. Why, then, do philosophers still present two-dimensionalism as vindicating Kripke's examples instead of undermining them?
