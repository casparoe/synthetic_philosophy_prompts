I spent thirty years as a condensed-matter physicist, and only since retiring have I had time to read seriously in the philosophy of physics - Sean Carroll's *From Eternity to Here* among other things. This has left me puzzled by something. A system in thermal equilibrium is, by definition, at or near its maximum entropy, and the universe just after the Big Bang was an almost perfectly uniform, extremely hot plasma sitting in thermal equilibrium; so the early universe must actually have been a high-entropy state. Given this, I don't understand why the "Past Hypothesis" - the claim that the universe began in a state of extraordinarily low entropy, which underwrites nearly every modern account of why entropy, and hence time, has a preferred direction - is treated as settled rather than as a claim that gets the thermodynamics backwards. What am I missing, or do physicists and philosophers of time need to rethink this foundational assumption?
