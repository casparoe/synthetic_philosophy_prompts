A few weekends ago, I had a long, at times fairly heated, dinner conversation with my friend Priya that I haven't been able to stop turning over since. We both work in early-stage venture capital and had, independently, been evaluating the same startup as a possible investment. Over the course of the evening we walked each other through essentially everything either of us had seen: the same data-room documents, the same calls with the founders, the same market research, so that neither of us was withholding some private fact the other lacked. We even iterated on the question a few times: I would state my probability that the company eventually returns the fund, she would state hers, each of us would explain our reasoning, and we would both revise our numbers. After three rounds we plateaued at 35 percent (me) and 65 percent (her), and neither of us could point to a single step in the other's reasoning that looked like a clear mistake. Priya eventually shrugged and suggested we had probably just started out with different gut instincts about the founders, and that it's perfectly fine for two sensible, honest people to land in different places even after comparing notes that thoroughly. Then, half-joking, she offered to bet me on it at even odds — she'd take the side that the company succeeds, I'd take the side that it doesn't.

Her explanation has nagged at me ever since. I have a hazy memory from an economics class years ago that two honest people who trust each other's judgment and have shared all their evidence aren't actually supposed to be able to settle into stably different probabilities like that, and that a willingness to bet on the disagreement should be an even bigger warning sign than the disagreement itself. So I would like your take: is Priya's "we just started from different gut instincts" defense a legitimate way to account for what happened, or does the fact that we still disagree, and that she now wants to bet on it, mean that one of us has to be reasoning badly somewhere neither of us can detect? And, purely as a matter of self-interest, would it be a mistake for me to take her bet?
