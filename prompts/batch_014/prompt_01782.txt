Ok here's a weird one about Frege that's been rattling around in my head. Most people who've heard of him know him as the ultra-rigorous father of modern logic, sense vs reference, all that. Fewer people know that Michael Dummett, the philosopher who basically single handedly revived Freges reputation in English (and who was himself a lifelong anti-racism campaigner), dug up a diary Frege kept in his final years -- one that wasn't published til decades after he died -- full of hard-right stuff: anti-parliamentary, anti-democratic, wanting Jews stripped of political rights, sympathetic to the nationalist fringe that fed into the Nazi party. By all accounts Dummett was floored.

Ever since, there've been two camps on what to do with this. One says: totally irrelevant, genetic fallacy, a great logician's private prejudices have zero bearing on whether his arguments about the objectivity of logic and the mind-independence of "thoughts" actually hold up. The other says: not so fast -- the very same absolutist streak that made Frege so hostile to psychologism, to the idea that logic could ever bend to human minds or majorities, is arguably the same impulse behind the politics. Two expressions of one worldview, not an unrelated personal failing.

Write me a dialogue between one philosopher on each side -- real back and forth, each one actually landing blows on the other's position, nobody strawmanned -- as they try to hash out whether the diary should change how we read Frege's philosophy.
