Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Mara and Elias, who are debating whether P. F. Strawson’s distinction between participant reactive attitudes and the objective attitude can survive the institutional mediation of moral accountability. Mara defends what she calls “mediated accountability”: social practices—such as facilitated restorative circles, victim-impact panels, and structured reparative protocols—through which a community addresses wrongdoing without requiring each affected party to feel direct, unfiltered resentment. She argues that these systems preserve the core Strawsonian insight that offenders are treated as members of the moral community, not as objects of clinical management, because the emotional investment is not eliminated but distributed across roles; she introduces the notion of a “vicarious reactive attitude,” in which a facilitator or representative genuinely feels indignation or disappointment on behalf of those harmed, maintaining interpersonal address even within procedural structure. Elias contends that this is precisely the slide into the objective attitude Strawson described: once resentment is channeled through bureaucratic mediation, the wrongdoer is addressed not as “you who wronged me” but as “the case requiring resolution,” and Mara’s “vicarious” emotion is merely sentimental ornamentation layered atop administrative control. The dialogue should contain genuine back-and-forth: Elias should press Mara on whether her vicarious attitude can distinguish itself from a social worker’s professional concern, and Mara should respond by challenging Elias to explain how direct, unmediated resentment can be ethically appropriate in cases of severe cognitive disability or systemic harm without conceding that we have adopted the objective stance toward the offender. Both should treat Strawsonian concepts—the moral community, exemption conditions, the participant and objective attitudes—as living commitments rather than museum pieces, and neither should be reduced to a strawman. Keep the discussion away from generic free-will determinism; focus instead on whether institutional design can reshape reactive attitudes without dissolving them. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or verbatim quotations from Strawson’s texts; the exchange should read like an advanced seminar argument that turns on conceptual distinctions rather than textual authority.
