Please explain a conceptual tension I have been developing in recent seminars on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness, and treat it as genuinely unsettled rather than resolved by standard moves in the literature. The familiar illusionist claim is that introspection systematically misrepresents neural processes as possessing phenomenal properties—properties that are, in fact, absent—thereby dissolving the hard problem by removing the explanandum. I want to distinguish two ways this misrepresentation might work. On the ontological reading, phenomenal properties do not exist at all; introspection functions like a hallucination that posits entities with no real counterpart. On the epistemic reading, phenomenal properties do exist, but introspection represents them with structurally wrong features—say, as simple, intrinsic, and non-physical rather than as complex, relational, and physical—so that the hard problem survives in modified form: not why is there phenomenality, but why do these particular properties generate precisely this misrepresentation. My question is whether illusionism, if it is to be a substantive metaphysical alternative to physicalism rather than a rhetorical redescription of it, must commit to the ontological reading and reject the epistemic one. I am also interested in whether a coherent intermediate position—let us call it structural illusionism—is available, one that denies the existence of phenomenal properties as traditionally conceived (intrinsic qualitative feels) but accepts the existence of functional-phenomenal hybrids that introspection misclassifies as purely qualitative. Please lay out the conceptual geography with precision: what distinguishes the ontological from the epistemic reading at the level of metaphysics rather than mere rhetoric; whether the epistemic reading collapses into a form of representationalism about consciousness; and whether structural illusionism is stable or merely physicalism in illusionist clothing. I should add that if the framing I have offered rests on a confused premise—for instance, if the ontological-epistemic distinction does not apply cleanly to phenomenal properties, or if structural illusionism is conceptually incoherent—please say so plainly and do not construct an answer to fit a broken question.
