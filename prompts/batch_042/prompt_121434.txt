Consider the standard practice in training large language models known as reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF): a model is optimized not against a pre-specified external objective—such as accuracy, logical validity, or task success—but against ratings of approval assigned by human evaluators. What philosophical commitments about rational agency and value does this design practice presuppose? Further, distinguish what the practice strictly requires philosophically from what it merely accommodates, and indicate what would have to change in the alignment methodology if one replaced approval-based optimization with direct, verifiable goal specification.
