I keep noticing a pattern in how people treat objective-chance claims once they learn the generating story behind them. I do not know whether it has an established name, so I will call it "normative-modulation by mechanism transparency" for the purpose of this question, but I want you to tell me if there is a recognized concept and, if not, to coin one with precise boundaries.

Here are two concrete instances.

First instance: A clinician is told that, for patients with a specific genetic profile, there is an objective 30 percent chance of developing a condition within ten years. She updates her belief to match that figure, treating the chance as authoritative. Then she learns the biochemical pathway: the gene activates only in the presence of an environmental trigger found in 80 percent of the population. Rather than conditioning her belief on the presence of the trigger for this particular patient, she treats the pathway revelation as undermining the authority of the original 30 percent figure itself—she calls it "just a population artifact"—even though the unconditional risk for her patient has not changed.

Second instance (the mirror image): An engineer learns that a quantum-random generator produces outcomes with exactly 50 percent frequency. She defers fully to that chance. Then she learns about the decoherence mechanism that produces the randomness. Rather than treating the mechanism as explaining why the chance is 50 percent, she treats her understanding of the physics as an additional endorsement—she becomes more confident in deferring to the 50 percent figure, as if metaphysical transparency added normative weight, even though no new statistical information has arrived.

The shared structure is not ordinary Bayesian updating on new conditional probabilities, because the statistical information available to the agent has not changed in either case. It is also not plain explaining-away, because the clinician is not claiming the correlation is spurious—she still expects 30 percent of similar patients to develop the condition. Rather, the agent's willingness to treat the chance claim as rationally binding oscillates asymmetrically based on whether the causal story feels "respectable" or "transparent": some mechanisms diminish deference, others amplify it, with no consistent rule governing which does which.
