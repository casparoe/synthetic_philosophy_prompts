Write a poem presented as exactly five numbered stanzas — 1 through 5 — with no title, no introduction, and no lines outside the numbering; each stanza should contain one or two complete sentences in blank verse (unrhymed iambic pentameter). The speaker is a slow-switch Twin Earth traveler who has lived for years among Twin-English speakers and now, in solitude, tries to introspect her own concept “water.” Keep the meter regular in stanzas 1 and 2 to represent her Earth-born confidence that meaning is transparent to first-person access; in stanza 3, fracture the line lengths and caesuras visibly to mark the realization that her mental life holds only the hollow form of deference, while the reference — H2O on Earth, XYZ here — lies entirely outside her head. In stanzas 4 and 5, restore the regular meter but replace the nouns she once trusted about interiority (“mind,” “source,” “thought”) with terms of community dependence (“usage,” “expert,” “division”), so that the absence of rhyme becomes an image of content that never lived inside. The poem should be philosophically precise enough that someone who knows semantic externalism recognizes Putnam’s division of linguistic labor and the problem of first-person authority over reference, without either thinker being named, and it should leave open whether her introspective failure is merely incomplete or altogether empty.
