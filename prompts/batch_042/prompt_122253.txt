My thesis is that the standard separateness-of-persons objection to utilitarianism should be extended—without appealing to controversial metaphysical theories of personal identity—to what I call diachronic separateness: just as interpersonal aggregation wrongly treats one person's welfare as exchangeable for another's, intrapersonal aggregation across temporal stages wrongly treats an earlier stage's interests as exchangeable for a later stage's, violating the normative boundary between separately entitled centers of concern within a single life. Order the objections by the damage they would actually do in this setting, phrase each in the vocabulary that group would use (welfare-aggregation language for the consequentialists, agency and respect language for the Kantians, empirical framing for the psychologist), and for each provide: a reply I can deliver in about two minutes, what I should concede up front, where that reply is weakest, and whether to hold firm or soften.
