In philosophy of music discussions that treat Schopenhauer, I notice a recurring interpretive move. The interpreter begins by emphasizing that for Schopenhauer music is special among the arts: it does not copy particular things or general forms the way painting and poetry do, but is instead a direct manifestation of some deeper, non-representational reality—the fundamental force or drive Schopenhauer calls the Will. Having established this metaphysical uniqueness, the interpreter then asks what music actually conveys, and answers by listing specific emotional conditions—deep longing, sudden joy, tension and release, melancholy—and describing these emotions as the direct content of musical form. The difficulty is that within Schopenhauer’s system, emotions are themselves representations: they are how the Will appears to conscious subjects within the phenomenal world. By identifying music’s direct content with emotional states, the interpreter therefore turns music back into a representational art, merely one that operates through sound patterns rather than through images or concepts. I have seen this structure repeated with different emotional vocabularies—romantic, existential, even neuroscientific descriptions of affective arousal—but the pattern is constant: the claim that music transcends representation is preserved intact, while its substance is filled in with exactly the representational material it was supposed to bypass. Is there an established name for this specific failure mode—not a generic psychologizing of Schopenhauer, but the systematic retention of his metaphysical framing alongside a substitution of representational content? If no established term exists, can you propose one, together with precise boundary conditions: for instance, how should it be distinguished from a straightforward misreading that simply ignores Schopenhauer’s distinction altogether, and from legitimate analogical uses of emotional language meant only to illustrate the Will’s movements for listeners without metaphysical training?
