I'm outlining an essay that treats Einstein's hole argument as a test case for theories of reference rather than as a direct argument for relationalism about spacetime. The standard presentation assumes that coordinate expressions like 'point p in region R' are purely descriptively defined by their metrical and topological relations within a model. On that descriptivist reading, applying an active diffeomorphism inside the hole produces a genuinely distinct mathematical representation of the same physical situation, and determinism fails because the field equations don't fix which representation corresponds to reality. I want to argue that this conclusion depends on a hidden philosophy-of-language premise. If, instead, we treat spacetime points as having their reference fixed causally-historically—through continuous worldlines extending from a region where the metric is already determined, or through an initial baptismal fixing of local frames—then the diffeomorphism-related models are not competing descriptions of one possibility but descriptions of different possibilities with different reference-fixing histories. I distinguish three readings: (1) pure descriptive structuralism, where only global invariants are real; (2) full causal-historical direct reference for every point, which seems implausible given that points aren't observable; and (3) a hybrid structural-causal view where global topology is descriptively fixed but local points are anchored by their causal connections to a boundary region. The essay asks whether adopting (2) or (3) dissolves the hole argument's threat to determinism without committing us to relationalism, and whether this shows that diffeomorphism invariance is a gauge redundancy about notation rather than a deep physical symmetry. Could you comment on whether this framing misrepresents how physicists understand diffeomorphism invariance in classical general relativity—especially regarding whether they already treat it as a gauge symmetry rather than an ontological ambiguity—and whether the injection of reference theory actually clarifies the argument or just relocates the problem into philosophy of language? I'd also welcome suggestions on tightening the three-way comparison, and specifically on whether my notion of baptismal fixing for spacetime points is coherent given that individual points lack direct observational access. This is an outline with a central thesis, not a request for citations or a bibliography.
