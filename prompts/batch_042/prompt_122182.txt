Im giving opening remarks at a workshop for my mentor Dr. Elena Vasquez's retirement. She spent forty years on metaphysics of dispositions—finks, antidote masks, all the usual suspects. Half the room are philosophers who will catch every allusion; the other half are family and old friends who don't know a disposition from a habit.

I want the speech built around an idea I'm calling a 'social fink': a collective capacity that doesn't vanish when stress hits, but gets structurally rerouted so the triggering conditions never actually reach it. The underlying ability is still intact—it's masked by the way the institution reshuffles roles under pressure. I think her best work showed that what looks like institutional failure is often exactly this: not the loss of capacity, but a fink-like neutralization that hides it in plain sight.

The idea should shape what I can and can't say—so I shouldn't claim she 'changed the field' in some simple causal way, since that'd be treating influence like a direct disposition rather than a socially finked one. The philosophers should hear the parallel to standard fink-and-mask debates; everyone else should hear a tribute to someone who refused to confuse rerouting with disappearance. About four minutes, warm but not sentimental, no paper citations, no direct quotes from texts, and please don't turn her into a ghost watching over us. Write the remarks.
