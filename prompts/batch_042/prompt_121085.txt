I'm working through some conceptual issues for a course on AI safety and value alignment, and I've become stuck on a problem that sits precisely at the boundary between formal engineering and normative philosophy. The question is whether the alignment objective of "avoid side effects" can be coherently specified for a low-impact artificial intelligence without first supplying the system with a complete, or near-complete, specification of human values. Any attempt to distinguish harmful disruptions from benign changes seems to require a normative baseline: if we define side effects as deviations from the counterfactual trajectory the world would have followed absent the agent, we must already decide which counterfactual worlds are relevant and which outcomes count as damage; if we instead appeal to structural properties—such as physical reversibility, informational minimalism, or the preservation of option value—we must justify why those particular dimensions should be privileged without smuggling in substantive judgments about which states of affairs are worth protecting.

Write an essay that weighs these competing lines of thought and reaches a defended conclusion. In particular, examine the tension between an instrumental interpretation, on which side effects are merely obstacles to achieving a given task, and an intrinsic interpretation, on which the agent must avoid altering its environment in ways that are not strictly necessary for task completion, even when those alterations are irreversible only relative to contingent preferences rather than to physical law. Consider whether value-neutral proxies—such as entropy bounds, causal footprint minimization, or reversibility conditions—can genuinely serve as stand-ins for a full value function, or whether selecting any such proxy already commits us to implicit normative claims about which features of the world matter and which may be treated as expendable. Your essay should argue either that a genuinely value-independent formalization of "no side effects" is possible and can be captured through structural or physical constraints alone, or that the concept is irreducibly normative and therefore collapses back into the broader problem of value alignment. Defend your verdict with reasons, and address the strongest objection to your position.
