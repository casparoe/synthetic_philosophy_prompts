Write a two-page research proposal for a semester-long independent study in comparative philosophy of language and metaphysics. Your subject is the White Horse dialogue associated with the School of Names, conventionally read as a paradox about class membership: the claim that an object possessing both horse-form and white-color is not, strictly speaking, a horse. Existing scholarship generally resolves this into either a logical error about sets, a semantic ambiguity in the term horse, or a rhetorical exercise in statecraft. Your proposal should advance a less familiar framing: treat the dialogue as advancing a thesis of ontological composition failure, specifically that a color-property can be sortally invasive—meaning its presence alters the kind-membership conditions of the substance it modifies rather than merely adding an accidental feature. State your precise research question in these terms: does the dialogue provide internal arguments for the claim that whiteness and horseness stand in a relation of non-compositional dependence, such that their combination produces an entity whose kind-identity is disrupted? Explain why the three standard interpretations leave this ontological reading untouched: they either presuppose Aristotelian substance-accident frameworks that the text may reject, or they treat the argument as purely linguistic and therefore miss its metaphysical target. Set out your method: a premise-by-premise reconstruction of the dialogue's reasoning that avoids importing modern logical notation; a comparison with contemporary phase-sortal theories, but restricted to testing internal consistency rather than claiming historical influence; and a clear failure condition—namely, that the reconstruction requires attributing premises that have no textual basis in the dialogue's own structure. Assess scope honestly: what makes the project feasible within one semester, where the risk of collapse into general intellectual history lies, and what a determinate philosophical payoff would look like if the composition-failure reading holds. Do not request a bibliography or list of citations; refer to interpretive positions and comparative theories by descriptive name only.
