I serve on the allocation board of a regional cooperative-housing authority that must redesign eligibility rules for a new tranche of permanently affordable units. A proposal before us would implement a modified veil-of-ignorance protocol: applicants submit anonymized profiles stripped of race, gender, current income, and family-status data, and a committee assigns units according to a maximin rule that maximizes the prospects of the worst-off profile without knowing which real-world applicant corresponds to which profile. One faction argues this violates our charter's transparency and anti-paternalism clauses; another faction urges replacing the maximin criterion with an expected-welfare rule that weights each profile by its probability of representing a genuinely disadvantaged household. Draft the board's decision memo — recommending adoption, conditional adoption, or rejection — with a concrete implementation timeline if the protocol is approved. The memo should engage the transparency objection on its own terms rather than dismissing it, explain whether and why maximin remains appropriate when the assignment is probabilistic rather than contractual, and include a candid assessment of the strongest argument for the opposing recommendation.
