i sit on the planning committee for our city's eastside transit corridor and we're deadlocked over the evaluation framework. half the committee wants us to stay "value-neutral" in the Carnap vein — just map out empirical trade-offs between the three route options (cost, travel time, displacement) without baking in a welfare metric, since Carnap thought rational planning should seperate means-ends analysis from value commitments that cant be empirically verified. the other half says thats naive, that picking which metrics even count already smuggles in values, and that without an explicit "equal access" weight we'll just default to cost-minimization which is itself a hidden value choice.

our chair asked me to draft a memo for thursday's vote recommending one framework — either the value-neutral presentation or explicit welfare-weighting — plus a concrete decision timeline for picking the route. it needs to take the Carnap-inspired position seriously rather than just calling it impractical, and it should include the strongest counter-argument to whatever side i reccomend. can you write it? formal enough for circulation but no need for citations or a reading list.
