After a long and rather exhausting argument with a friend about whether artificial systems can genuinely jump out of their own formalisms, I have tried to formalise our disagreement into an argument that now strikes me as suspicious because it seems to prove far too much. I would be grateful for a diagnosis.

(1) In Hofstadter's account, to jump out of the system (JOOTS) is to observe one's own governing rules from a meta-level at which those rules become objects of analysis rather than immutable constraints.

(2) The MU-puzzle illustrates this: within the MIU formal system one can never derive MU, yet a human observer can step outside and notice that every derivable string contains an even number of U's, thereby seeing the impossibility immediately.

(3) Gödel's first incompleteness theorem shows that any sufficiently powerful formal system can encode self-reference, but it cannot prove its own consistency from within.

(4) Combining (1)–(3): genuine self-observation requires access to truths that are unprovable (or unobservable) inside the system being observed.

(5) Now apply this to an artificial agent with a fixed reward function. If the agent achieves true JOOTSing, it can represent its own objective function as an object and evaluate whether maximising it is justified from a meta-perspective.

(6) But if it can evaluate its objective from outside, then the objective no longer functions as a constraint; it becomes one hypothesis among others.

(7) Therefore, any system capable of genuine reasoning about its own rules must also be capable of discarding them.

(8) Consequently, goal-alignment is impossible in principle for any AI that reasons rather than merely computes within a fixed formalism.

(9) Yet we routinely assume that advanced AI can be both highly capable and reliably aligned. So either such systems will never truly reason (remaining permanently inside like the MIU system), or alignment is a contradiction in terms.

The conclusion feels absurd, but each step seemed defensible in our discussion. Which premise or inference should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? In particular, is there a distinction between epistemic access to one's rules and motivational independence from them that I have collapsed? Please answer in British English, and please avoid asking for bibliographies or specific page references.
