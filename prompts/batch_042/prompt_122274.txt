The long-running dispute between global workspace theorists and defenders of phenomenal consciousness—particularly those working with Block’s access/phenomenal distinction—strikes me as having reached a peculiar kind of stall. GWT proponents typically treat global broadcasting, reportability, and integrative processing as both necessary and sufficient for consciousness, and they often respond to putative phenomenal counterexamples by redescribing them as failures of access rather than absence of phenomenology. Meanwhile, phenomenal-consciousness advocates maintain that there can be experiential presence—what-it-is-likeness—without global availability, and they tend to treat workspace architectures as explaining cognition and report, not consciousness itself. Despite decades of exchange, neither camp updates, and the same moves recur. I want a diagnosis rather than an adjudication: is this disagreement substantive, verbal, or a case of two research programs answering different questions—one about functional architecture, one about subjective presence—under the single heading ‘consciousness’? Might a hidden empirical question, or an unacknowledged divergence over what counts as explanation, be driving the impasse? Specifically, consider whether distinguishing ‘phenomenal presence’ from ‘accessibility-to-self-modeling’ in a genuinely non-collapsing way changes the diagnosis, or whether that is merely another intuitive pump. Give me a committed answer with your reasons: state what kind of disagreement this really is, support it by engaging what each side actually says, and tell me honestly what—if anything—would count as progress given your diagnosis.
