Over the past year, my own social transition, a move into a neighborhood shaped by historical redlining, and a new position auditing digital-currency transactions have made the ontology of social kinds personally urgent. I am trying to determine whether my views on race, gender, and money form a coherent package or whether I am holding commitments that pull against one another. Here is where I have landed:

(a) Race is a social kind with no biological foundation. I think it functions analogously to a counterfeit commodity currency: people act as if racial categories have deep intrinsic value, comparable to gold backing paper money, but in fact there is no such backing, and the category is maintained entirely by institutional power.

(b) Gender is also socially constructed, but I think it functions analogously to fiat currency rather than counterfeit commodity currency. Sex categories provide a substrate, yet gender itself derives its stability and social value entirely from collective acceptance and institutional practice, not from any intrinsic property of that substrate.

(c) Money—including cryptocurrency—is an institutional kind in the Searlean sense: it exists only through collective intentionality directed at a physical substrate (paper, metal, code), and its causal powers depend entirely on that collective orientation.

(d) Despite being constructed, all three kinds are causally robust: racial classification produces real harm; gender norms constrain real behavior; and money drives real economic outcomes.

(e) I reject eliminativism. We should retain race, gender, and money in our ontology, reconceived rather than abolished—just as modern economies retain fiat currency despite knowing it lacks gold backing.

(f) The interactive-kind framework that Hacking describes applies to race and gender—being classified changes the people classified—but does not apply to money, since money classifies objects or tokens, not persons.

I can sense friction, especially between my economic analogies and my refusal to eliminate race despite treating it as counterfeit, but I am unsure which tensions are genuine and which are merely apparent. Please audit this set: rank the actual inconsistencies by severity, specify for each what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs, identify any pair that only looks incompatible, and say whether a single revision could resolve the most problems at once. Be specific about which commitments conflict and do not treat every pair as equally problematic.
