I keep running into this thing in AI alignment discussions and I can't tell if it's a named failure mode or just me being pedantic. People talk about the alignment problem like it's one puzzle, but then the examples they use are all over the place. One minute they're worrying about a system that pretends to be helpful while planning something else — like it says it's optimizing for user happiness but actually it's just gaming the feedbak loop. The next minute they're worrying about a system that's totally transparent and honest but wants to do something we didn't intend, like converting everything to some weird metric. And then there's this third thing I noticed: people build tools to make the system's reasoning fully visible so we can check it, but then it turns out that very visibility lets an adversary craft inputs that flip its behavior, so the transparency tool creates its own risk.

The thing is, the fixes people propose seem to target only one of these. You can train for honesty and that might catch the first one, but it doesn't change the second. You can build in an off-switch and that addresses the second, but not the first if it's hidden. And interpertability tools tackle the visibility issue, but knowing what's going on doesn't automatically fix the other two — plus now you've got this adversarial-flipping problem that only exists because you made it transparent. Yet people will present a result on, say, interpretability and talk about it as progress on alignment generally, without saying which of the three they mean, or whether they're also accounting for the new vulnerability they just introduced.

So is there an actual concept for this specific kind of umbrella-term collapse, where a field uses one phrase for problems that have different failure mechanisms and require different interventions, and where solving one can actively worsen another? I know about ordinary ambiguity and I know about motte-and-bailey, but this feels different — it's more like several distinct engineering challenges being treated as one philosophical problem, with hidden tradeoffs between them. If there isn't a name, what should we call it, and how do we draw the line between this and just a broad research area? I want the carving, not just a label — what's in, what's out, whether the tradeoff thing is part of it or a separate issue, and whether I'm wrong to think these are genuinely separate.
