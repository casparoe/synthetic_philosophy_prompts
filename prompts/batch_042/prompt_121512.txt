I've been trying to apply the idea of safe Pareto improvements to collective decision-making for a course I'm taking, and I keep running into an argument that sounds airtight but ends up saying no group can ever rationaly change its mind. I was discussing it with a friend and we couldn't find the exit.

Here is the argument I sketched out (sorry for any typos, I'm writing this on my phone between classes):

(1) A proposal is a safe Pareto improvement over the status quo S iff every group member is weakly better off in the proposal and at least one is strictly better off, according to each member's own preference ordering.
(2) If a proposal is a safe Pareto improvement, then no member has a rational objection to adopting it.
(3) If no member has a rational objection, then the group as a whole lacks any reason not to adopt it.
(4) So whenever a safe Pareto improvement is available, the group is rationaly required to adopt it immediately.
(5) Now suppose the group has a collective ordering built by agregating individual rankings. If S' is a safe improvement over S, then S' ranks above S.
(6) But for any proposed S', there is always some hypothetical future state S* that would be a safe improvement over S' only if the group first rejects S' and remains in S. In other words, adopting S' destroys the option of reaching S*.
(7) Therefore, for every available improvement S', there exists a reason to stay in S — preserving the path to S*.
(8) By (4) the group must adopt S'; by (7) the group must reject S'. Contradiction.
(9) So at least one premise is false.

My friend thinks the problem is in (3) — that 'no objection' doesn't equal 'positive reason to act together' — but I think the real slip might be in (7), treating an option-value argument as a rational objection to the improvement itself. I want a definite answer, not a survey: which step actually breaks, why did it look fine, and what's the cost of dropping it? If you think I'm wrong about where the flaw is, say so directly. Be committed — pick the step, defend it, and tell me exactly what I have to pay if I reject it instead of your choice.
