I co-facilitate an advanced undergraduate philosophy reading group—twelve students, evenly split between philosophy and physics majors—who will meet for a single seventy-five-minute session on the ethical implications of treating sentience as a fundamental physical property. Rather than explaining panpsychism itself, I want you to plan the explanatory arc for one specific, derivative problem: the possibility of “distributed valence,” in which suffering-like phenomenal properties exist in physical processes that lack the biological integration typically required for moral patienthood. Begin by mapping the exact dependency order of the conceptual pieces: which distinction—perhaps between ubiquitous micro-phenomenal properties and macro-integrated subjects—must be firmly in place before the ethical question can even arise, why the combination problem must be addressed before rather than after the normative framing, and how the notion of an “integration threshold” depends on prior clarity about phenomenal binding. Next, design the single carrying example that should anchor the entire session: I am considering a structured comparison among a dissipating thermal gradient, a coherently maintained quantum system, and a mammalian nervous system, but specify precisely what properties this trio must exhibit and what they must deliberately exclude—particularly any biological or anthropomorphic framing that would smuggle macro-subjectivity back into the micro-level—so that the students encounter the idea’s force without retreating to animism. Then, anticipate the misconceptions they will bring or generate in sequence: my own expectation is that they will first conflate micro-phenomenal properties with micro-subjects, then conclude that ethics collapses into universal paralysis, and perhaps finally treat coherence as an automatic ethical filter; indicate exactly where in the dependency sequence each misconception should be intercepted and how. Provide one closing diagnostic question that would reveal whether a student has genuinely grasped the dependency between integration and moral status rather than merely assenting that “everything might suffer.” Finally, state openly what should be left out at this level—such as specific quantum-mechanical formalisms, the full taxonomy of Russellian versus constitutive panpsychist positions, or neuroscientific models of global workspace—and explain why omitting it protects the session’s conceptual focus. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation suggestions.
