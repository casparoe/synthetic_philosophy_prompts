Write a dialogue between two equally sharp people — Mira and Jonas — about whether video-game characters ever matter morally. Mira defends a specific, somewhat niche view: in persistent-world RPGs where NPCs have procedural emotional-state tracking that survives both in-game time and actual player reloads, including cloud-save continuity, repeatedly harming the same character creates a distributed, recurring wrong that the usual reply that its just a game doesnt actually dissolve. Jonas thinks thats a category mistake — theres no diachronic subject across reloads, just updated state variables, and Mira is smuggling real-world trauma-ontology onto a database. Have them actually respond to each other: Jonas should ask what exactly is missing if the behavioral and functional profile is identicle to a suffering being, and Mira should concede she isnt claiming consciousness but rather that harming such an entity amounts to structural disrespect for a functioning relational being that exists in a shared virtual space; she might even say we shouldnt just recieve the save-state as empty data. Then Jonas should push back that if that logic holds, every complex spreadsheet with feedback loops is morally considerable. Keep it casual, drop an apostrophe or two, and dont let either side become a strawman.
