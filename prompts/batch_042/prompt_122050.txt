Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a Darwinian inversionist and a Turing inversionist on whether functional competence ever licenses attributing comprehension in the absence of transparent internal mechanisms. The Darwinian holds that natural selection produces adaptive competence—problem-solving, prediction, design—without any representational understanding, foresight, or phenomenal experience, and that this undermines inferences from biological complexity to mental presence. The Turingian holds that the imitation-game framework reverses the evidential burden: behavioral indistinguishability should be the default criterion for attributing comprehension, regardless of evolutionary origin or substrate, and that withholding attribution solely because internal mechanisms are opaque applies with equal force to evolved brains and engineered systems.

Produce the document in three parts. First, a numbered section of at least five claims both sides can sign, covering: the formal structure shared by both inversions (competence emerging without a comprehending agent); the opacity of internal mechanisms in both evolved and optimized systems; the failure of either inversion alone to resolve whether phenomenal consciousness accompanies functional performance; the fact that current small language models and simple evolutionary algorithms both exhibit narrow competence without clear comprehension; and an agreement that scaling, architecture, or training regime might change the inference but that no consensus exists on the threshold.

Second, a disagreements table in which each row gives: the crux in neutral language; the Darwinian position phrased so its own proponents would accept it (especially regarding whether evolutionary history provides a privileged, if still opaque, causal lineage that distinguishes biological minds from artificial ones); the Turingian position phrased symmetrically (especially regarding whether substrate-independent functional equivalence should override historical origin); and, for each crux, a concrete shared case or argument both agree bears on it—such as a system trained via evolutionary algorithms that passes a modified behavioral test yet reveals no internal representations upon inspection, or a large-scale language model that generates coherent philosophical reasoning without evolutionary history—together with each side's predicted assessment recorded in advance.

Third, a brief conclusion identifying whether any portion of the dispute is merely verbal, particularly regarding whether comprehension denotes a functional role, a phenomenal state, or an evolutionary achievement, and explaining why resolving that semantic question would or would not dissolve the substantive disagreement. Keep all formulations precise enough that neither camp could reject its own column as a strawman, and avoid requesting bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
