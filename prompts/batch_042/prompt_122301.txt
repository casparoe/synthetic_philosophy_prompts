I hold a qualified intentionalist position on literary meaning: the semantic content of a text is fixed by the author's architectonic intentions — the overall design, structural commitments, and explicit public directives that are recoverable through the linguistic conventions and historical context available to a competent reader at the time of publication — but not by executive intentions, meaning the author's private associations, subconscious motives, retrospective reinterpretations, or moment-to-moment compositional choices that leave no public trace. Where a text displays features that appear to conflict with recoverable architectonic intention — an irony the author denies, a symbolic pattern never acknowledged, a thematic coherence that seems unintended — I resolve the conflict in favor of the text's publicly accessible structure, unless the author's explicit directive is unambiguous and part of the public record. What would count against this view? I want the defeaters mapped by severity: which arguments or cases would be fatal, which merely awkward, and which I could absorb with further qualification. In particular, are there texts whose meaning genuinely depends on private intentions that are not publicly recoverable, such that denying them access changes the interpretation in ways readers should care about? Does the distinction between architectonic and executive intention collapse in practice — for example, when an author's later commentary reveals a design plan that was not publicly recoverable at publication, or when subconscious patterns are shown to be causally necessary to the text's actual structure? Do results in the philosophy of language about speaker meaning versus sentence meaning, or practices in legal interpretation where authorial intent is sometimes explicitly subordinated to textual autonomy, show that my middle position is unstable and tends to collapse either into full intentionalism or into anti-intentionalist textual autonomy? Finally, I want an honest assessment of whether this view has been insulated into unfalsifiability: can any counterintentional feature be dismissed as "not part of the recoverable architectonic intention," and if so, is that insulation a theoretical virtue or a sign that the position risks explaining everything and therefore nothing?
