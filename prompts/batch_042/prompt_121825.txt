okay so me and my roommate got into a huge argument last night about epiphenominalism and this paradox where if consciousness doesn't cause anything then how can you even judge that you're conscious? he kept saying consciousness is like the glow on a lightbulb - electricity does the work, glow just hangs there. i want to come back with a poem that doesn't just summarize it but actually performs the problem. write me a villanelle in the voice of someone trying to report their own pain, with the two refrains being 'this hurt is real to me' and 'this hurt is only said by me'. let them rotate through the stanzas and finally sit side by side in the final quatrain. the second refrain needs to carry the reflexive bite: if my judgment that i'm hurting is just a physical event with no causal link back to the feeling itself, then saying 'this hurt is real to me' is itself just more physical noise, so the claim kind of eats its own tail. make the form mirror that - the refrains should keep passing each other like the judgment and the experience never actually touch. don't write a textbook summary, but make it accurate enough that someone who knows the paradox recognizes the exact instability. and keep it casual, no citations or reading lists.
