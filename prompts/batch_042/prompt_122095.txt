Q: Contrast Mencius and Xunzi on human nature (xing). Cover (a) what each thinker takes xing to be, (b) whether they think people are born with moral tendencies, and (c) how moral development actually happens.

Student answer:
Mencius and Xunzi both use xing but split hard on its moral status. For Mencius, xing includes the four sprouts—compassion, shame, deference, and the sense of right and wrong. These arent full virtues yet, but they have a built-in teleology: left in decent conditions they grow automatically into full moral character. So education for Mencius is mostly passive, like gardening; you just remove corrupting influences and let the heart reflect inward.

Xunzi says xing is just our raw biological drives—hunger, desire for rest, profit, and envy. He calls it evil because following these drives produces conflict, not because people are wicked. Sages then invented ritual (li) and law (fa) to suppress these impulses. For Xunzi the heart has no innate moral compass at all, so development is entirely external: ritual carves the person like wood, mainly through punishment and enforced compliance, and any inner approval comes only after long conditioning.
