For an advanced philosophy seminar on moral worth, design a branching taxonomy—functioning as a diagnostic decision tree—that classifies positions according to how they treat actions done for reasons that relate, sometimes obliquely, to what actually makes them right. Each internal node must present a concrete case judgment or claim that a respondent can accept or reject without knowing any doctrinal label; the leaves should then be mapped onto named or unnamed positions. Begin at the root with the tracking question: must an agent’s motivating reason correctly identify the feature that actually confers rightness on the act for the act to possess full moral worth? From there, split on the meta-reason error case—an agent performs a genuinely right action for a reason that correctly directs her behavior but rests on a false meta-belief (for instance, she returns lost property because she erroneously believes a specific religious text commands it, though impartial respect for ownership is what in fact makes it right). Next, split on the structural-substitution case: can moral worth be preserved when an agent acts through institutional or procedural compliance—say, a civil servant applying an equitable distribution rule—without personally grasping the justice of the rule, provided the social architecture reliably aligns her behavior with rightness? Then split on the fragility condition: if the agent’s correct reason is counterfactually brittle—she would have withheld help had one incidental belief about risk been slightly different—does that brittleness diminish or erase moral worth? The tree should include leaves corresponding to strict Kantian rationalism, Aristotelian virtue-based habituation without explicit reason-access, a Humean sentimentalist view modified to accommodate structural roles, consequentialist reason-irrelevance, and an unnamed hybrid that accepts tracking but holds that meta-reason error never undermines worth. For every leaf, provide a one-sentence statement of its most awkward commitment. Identify at least two combinations of answers that the logical space permits but that no named position occupies, and briefly diagnose whether each gap reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere unexplored territory. Ensure the tree contains between six and eight internal nodes and at least seven leaves, that no node asks a respondent to identify themselves by doctrine, and that the instrument can be traversed entirely from case judgments alone.
