Construct a conceptual example—whether involving an artificial cognitive architecture, a neurologically atypical subject, or an abstract representational system—that exposes a tension internal to higher-order representational theories of consciousness. Specifically, the case should turn on whether a second-order representational state can be genuinely present and non-degenerate, in the sense that it targets a distinct first-order state without being identical to that state or merely causally downstream of its phenomenal effects, while leaving it genuinely ambiguous whether the conditions for consciousness are satisfied. The example must not depend on introspective report, verbal behavior, or standard neural correlates for its resolution. Instead, design the scenario so that both the presence and the absence of the relevant second-order structure seem defensible, and explain precisely what structural feature of the architecture makes the case ambiguous and what this ambiguity reveals about the conditions that higher-order theories must specify in order to avoid collapsing into first-order accounts or over-generating phenomenal consciousness.
