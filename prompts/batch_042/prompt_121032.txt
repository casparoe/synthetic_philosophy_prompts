I've been working through the ethics of agricultural pest control—particularly the widespread use of neonicotinoids—using a framework that tries to combine epistemic humility about invertebrate sentience with scale-sensitive moral reasoning. I'd like you to rank the following five ethical stances on broad-spectrum insecticide use, from most defensible to least defensible, assigning each a score out of 10 and giving a one-sentence justification. The stances are: (1) pure anthropocentric instrumentalism—only human welfare is directly morally relevant, so pesticides are justified solely by agricultural efficiency; (2) naive expected-value utilitarianism—assign near-zero weight to insects because we lack proof of consciousness, then aggregate over the affected population; (3) a suffering-focused rationalist position—assign a small but non-negligible moral weight per insect, multiply by the astronomical numbers affected, and treat the uncertainty as a reason for precaution rather than dismissal, yielding a strong presumption against broadcast application; (4) ecological virtue ethics—judge pesticides primarily by their disruption of pollinator networks and soil ecology, treating systemic ecological integrity as an intrinsic good independent of individual sentience; (5) a rights-based deontology that grants insects direct moral rights scaled to cognitive complexity, thereby prohibiting lethal control except in direct defense of human life. Please evaluate each specifically against three constraints that I think standard discussions neglect: (i) the scale asymmetry—each application affects billions of insects for every human beneficiary; (ii) the epistemic asymmetry—behavioral indicators of nociception are present, yet we have no settled neuroscientific criterion for subjective experience in distributed nervous systems; (iii) the land-use counterfactual—banning chemical pesticides typically requires expanding cultivated area, displacing wild animals through habitat loss. For the middle-ranked stance, explain what it captures that the extremes overlook; for the lowest-ranked, identify which of the three constraints most directly undermines its central premise. Please avoid appeals to authority or citation; I'm interested in the reasoning structure, not bibliography.
