I am preparing a seminar paper on cross-world identity and have sketched the argument below. Rather than asking for a summary of the standard literature, I want targeted conceptual feedback on a specific distinction I am trying to introduce.

Thesis sketch: The debate between Lewis-style counterpart theory and the transworld-identity view (Plantinga and others) is usually treated as exhaustive. I want to defend an intermediate position—call it relational anchoring—according to which an object is identical across worlds just in case some core relational structure is preserved, even when intrinsic properties and parts differ. This is not merely a weighting scheme for counterpart relations, because on my view the absence of the anchoring relation means there is literally no object in that world to stand in any resemblance relation at all.

Argument outline:
- Counterpart theory permits overgeneration: if qualitative resemblance is weighted, then a bridge in world W could have a counterpart in world V that connects different islands, provided the intrinsic properties match closely enough. But if the bridge's identity conditions are essentially tied to connecting island A and island B, then in V (where those islands do not exist) there should be no bridge-entity whatsoever—not merely a poor counterpart.
- Transworld identity avoids overgeneration but faces a circular identification problem: to say that this is the same bridge in W seems to presuppose the very modal properties we are trying to ground.
- Relational anchoring claims that cross-world identity is secured not by intrinsic similarity or bare haecceity, but by the persistence of a specific relational structure. For artifacts with functional essences tied to contingent environments, this means they exist only in worlds containing the relata of that structure.

Request: Please evaluate whether relational anchoring is genuinely distinct from a Lewisian view that assigns maximal weight to relational properties. In particular, does my bridge example show a real divergence, or can the Lewisian absorb it? Also, is the identification problem for transworld identity fairly characterized, or have I missed a standard reply? I want your comments on the coherence and interest of the argument, not a literature survey, bibliography, or list of citations.
