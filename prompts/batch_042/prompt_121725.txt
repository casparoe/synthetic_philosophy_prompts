I've been thinking about Schrödinger's view—drawn from Vedanta—that consciousness is not plural but singular, and that our individual minds are like waves on a single ocean. Separately, I've always been puzzled by his cat: if nothing is definite until observed, and every brain is made of atoms like everything else, then it seems no physical observer can ever stand outside the system to collapse it. So I started wondering whether the cat paradox was actually a philosophical argument in disguise. Maybe Schrödinger was suggesting that the only genuine observer is that one universal consciousness he described, which isn't located in any brain but resolves superpositions from outside the physical world altogether—while our personal awareness is just a local ripple that never actually observes anything. Is that a defensible reading, or does it conflate his quantum mechanics with his metaphysics in a way he wouldn't have accepted?
