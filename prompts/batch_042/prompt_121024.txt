I'm constructing a decision-tree taxonomy of philosophical responses to the Rietdijk--Putnam argument for the block universe, intended for a graduate seminar in philosophy of physics. The tree should begin from a commitment that special relativity denies absolute simultaneity and should branch through concrete metaphysical questions rather than through named positions.

Please structure the tree with exactly eight internal nodes. At each node, pose a single question that a student could answer without knowing which philosophical label it leads to; allow two or three answer branches per node. The branching logic should distinguish, at an appropriate point, between the Rietdijk-style formulation (which relies on the spatial separation of observers and the finite velocity of light) and the Putnam-style formulation (which relies on the determinateness of future events relative to an observer's present). The tree must also include a branch that asks whether a privileged foliation of spacetime can be recovered in a Lorentz-invariant manner, and whether any such recovery is metaphysically relevant rather than merely epistemically hidden.

The leaves should represent at least the following positions: (i) the four-dimensionalist eternalist block; (ii) presentism combined with a privileged but observationally inaccessible foliation; (iii) the growing-block view modified for Minkowski spacetime; (iv) a local-presentist or fragmentalist view restricting reality to an observer's past light-cone; and (v) one combination of answers that currently has no named defender, with a brief diagnosis of why the space is empty—whether because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

For each leaf, provide a one-sentence statement of the view and identify its most awkward commitment. At the end of the taxonomy, flag any pair of leaves that the tree fails to separate cleanly, explaining whether the overlap is metaphysical or merely terminological.

In your response, clearly separate what is settled physics or logic—such as the Lorentz transformation structure and the absence of a globally invariant simultaneity relation in flat spacetime—from what is genuinely contested among philosophers of physics, such as whether frame-dependence undermines objective temporal becoming. Do not present any contested metaphysical interpretation as if it were the consensus view.
