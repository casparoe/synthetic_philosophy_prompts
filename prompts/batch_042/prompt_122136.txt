I am moderating a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who has recently advanced what she calls “threshold-locked functionalism.” On her view, mental-state types are fixed by functional roles, but multiple realizability is constrained by what she terms an “integration floor”: once a system’s architecture crosses a threshold of recursive, error-correcting self-modeling, the corresponding high-level states—such as conceptual reasoning and normative judgment—can no longer be realized in substrates that preserve all first-order functional relations but lack the requisite temporal dynamics, whereas sub-threshold states like basic perceptual affect remain widely realizable. She contends this explains why artificial systems might replicate pain or sensory discrimination without achieving genuine propositional thought.

Draft six to eight questions suitable for an academic audience, going well beyond asking her to restate the view. I require at least one question pressing on whether the integration floor marks a conceptual boundary or merely an empirically contingent one, together with a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that the boundary is discovered rather than stipulated. Include at least one question on how her position avoids collapsing into standard biological chauvinism, with a follow-up anticipating her appeal to abstraction within the locked class. Include at least one question on the implications for artificial consciousness if only sub-threshold mental states are substrate-independent, and at least one question on whether her account of high-level cognition ultimately reduces to a form of type physicalism, with a follow-up keyed to her probable defense that functional identity is still preserved, but only within a restricted realization class. Maintain a formal, rigorous tone throughout.
