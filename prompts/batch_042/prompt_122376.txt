A common philosophical move, evoked by Hofstadter in the dialogue involving Aunt Hillary and the ant fugue, compares an ant colony to a single brain or cognitive agent. On this analogy, individual ants correspond to neurons; pheromone trails and tactile signaling correspond to synaptic weights and action potentials; caste specialization corresponds to modular brain regions; and colony-level regulation of foraging, temperature, and reproduction corresponds to homeostatic control and unified goal-direction. Proponents use this mapping to argue that if consciousness requires only the right kind of integrated information processing, then an ant colony is not merely a society but literally one subject of experience—perhaps even with its own point of view.
