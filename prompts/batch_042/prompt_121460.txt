Write a short story, in a polished literary style, exploring a specific puzzle in infinite ethics. In the story, an entity called the Allocator must distribute a single, indivisible unit of care across a moral population arranged not in a linear sequence but in a branching tree: each node has ℵ₀ children, and tree depth is indexed by countable ordinals. The Allocator discovers that any distribution reaching every node at a given depth necessarily leaves uncountably many nodes at that same depth untouched, while concentrating care along one infinite branch reaches arbitrarily high ordinals but abandons almost all siblings at each level. Center the narrative on the Allocator’s internal deliberation—do not resolve the puzzle—showing how the tree’s geometry makes standard aggregation principles yield contradictory prescriptions. Include a moment in which the Allocator realizes that its own identity may be distributed across the nodes it selects, so that self-preservation and altruistic distribution become conceptually indistinguishable. Do not cite philosophers, theories, or texts by name; let the tension emerge entirely through the narrative situation and the Allocator’s reasoning.
