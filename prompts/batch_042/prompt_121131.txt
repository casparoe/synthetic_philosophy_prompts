I'm trying to figure out my actual view on agregating tiny harms, and my gut is pretty messy. Here are my verdicts. One person tortured for an hour is worse than every human alive getting a dust speck in the eye — thats clear. But that same torture vs every sentient being that ever lives getting that speck once a day for a hundred years? Now I think the specks win, definatly. One person losing a finger is worse than every current human getting a paper cut, but I'm not sure about one finger vs every being in the 24th century getting that paper cut. Then there's this: a million people each feel a pain so small they barely notice it, and I think thats collectively worse than one person feeling a moderate headache. But push it to a trillion people feeling something so faint it doesnt even register in consciousness, and suddenly I think its basically nothing — not worse than the headache at all. So I seem to think tiny harms aggregate, but only if they're above some threshold of noticeability, except that in the century-long speck case they aggregate even though each instance is barely noticed. Is there one principle that saves all these, or do I have to drop one? And what does it say about the case I keep flipping on: one child being bullied lightly every day for years vs every child ever born having one second of anxiety so brief they never remember it?
