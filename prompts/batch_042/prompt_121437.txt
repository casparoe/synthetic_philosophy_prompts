My department's been arguing about populism and "the will of the people" for months and we're going in circles. One camp says populists are describing a real collective preference that liberal institutions suppress—like there's an actual group-agent with interests that polling just approximates badly. The other camp says "the people" is purely performative: saying it exists makes it exist, so asking whether populists are "right" about the will is like asking whether a wedding vow describes a pre-existing marriage. They both cite the same elections and neither budges.

My hunch is we're mixing up three different things: (1) whether collective agents exist in any metaphysically serious sense, (2) whether democratic legitimacy needs a pre-political will or can build it procedurally, and (3) whether "will" here means a statistical aggregate or a diachronic identity that survives preference change. But I'm not sure if that's the real stall or if it's just bedrock intuition about whether democracy is discovery or invention.

Can you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is—verbal, substantive, or different questions under one label? And tell me what would count as progress: a conceptual distinction, a formal model, an empirical test, or is this one of those debates that cant move because the sides are defending different pictures of politics? Be concrete about what each side is actually committed to, not just "populists vs liberals."
