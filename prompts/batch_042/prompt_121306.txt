I'm a doctoral student in political philosophy working on partiality and global justice. At a recent workshop, two senior colleagues argued over whether patriotism is a virtue or a vice, framing the dispute around what they called indexical loyalty—the claim that loving one's country is morally valuable only if it is essentially tied to this particular nation and not substitutable by any equally worthy one. The first colleague argued that this makes patriotism structurally analogous to nepotism: it privileges the near and dear without sufficient moral justification, and in an interconnected world it functions as an atavistic barrier to impartial justice. The second replied that all meaningful loyalty is indexical—promises, friendships, and marriages derive their value precisely from being non-substitutable—and that patriotism is best understood as large-scale associative loyalty, not as arbitrary preference. The first countered that family and friendship generate special obligations through reciprocal relationships, whereas citizens rarely stand in direct reciprocal relations with the state; without that mechanism, the analogy collapses and patriotism reduces to ungrounded favoritism. The second responded that this confuses the mechanism generating obligation with the form of loyalty itself: one can maintain associative loyalty to a cultural-historical community without direct reciprocity, just as one might honor an ancestral tradition, and that demanding universal substitutability eliminates the goods that make loyalty worth having at all.

Please adjudicate this exchange move by move: rate each step from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify your rating. Say who is more right overall, and identify what both are missing—particularly whether there is a principled distinction between permissible and impermissible forms of indexical loyalty that neither has articulated. Also, please state explicitly what would change your verdict: which argument or evidence would lead you to reverse your assessment, and where in your reasoning you are most likely to fail.
