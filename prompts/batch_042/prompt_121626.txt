I was recently implanted with a de novo engineered ventricular patch—induced pluripotent stem cells differentiated into contractile tissue with no evolutionary lineage—following extensive myocardial damage. The clinical team describes its function as restoring synchronized contraction, but I have since encountered the philosophy-of-biology literature and now wonder whether that functional attribution is conceptually legitimate. Would you compose a rigorous dialogue between two equally informed interlocutors, one defending an etiological account of biological function in the tradition associated with Wright and Millikan, the other defending a causal-role account in the manner of Cummins, specifically addressing whether my patch can genuinely be said to have the function of rhythmic contraction given its absence of selection history? The exchange should avoid straw-man representations: the etiologist should press the concern that causal-role analyses overgenerate functions for artifacts and pathological states, while the causal-role proponent should reply that etiological criteria arbitrarily exclude engineered and novel biological entities from functional discourse altogether. Please have them respond directly to one another’s arguments, and conclude by delineating whether my normative expectation—that the tissue ought to contract in synchrony with native myocardium, and that desynchronization counts as malfunction—depends on a defensible notion of function or collapses without an evolutionary backstory.
