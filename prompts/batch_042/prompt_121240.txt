I am drafting a philosophical dialogue for my novel in which an ethicist and a conservationist argue whether omnivores can consistently oppose wild-animal suffering, and I need a rigorous taxonomy to ensure their positions are genuinely distinct rather than rhetorical variants. Please construct a decision tree that sorts ethical stances on meat consumption and wild-animal suffering by concrete commitments rather than by familiar labels. The root node should ask whether wild-animal suffering carries direct moral weight comparable to farmed-animal suffering. From there, branch on whether crop-cultivation deaths are included in the moral calculus; on whether the beef-versus-chicken comparison should be evaluated by suffering per calorie, by land-use displacement and trophic-level cascade effects, or by neural-complexity weighting of different species; and on whether the naturalness of predation provides a defeasible reason against intervention, or whether our record of ecological intervention gives a decisive practical reason against it, or whether epistemic humility about ecosystem dynamics does the work instead. Each leaf must name the position if it has a standard name, otherwise mark it unnamed, summarize its core claim in one sentence, and state its most awkward commitment—particularly where a view must accept massive wild suffering as permissible or must endorse large-scale ecological intervention despite uncertain outcomes. Where the logical space contains an unoccupied combination of answers, diagnose briefly whether it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. The tree must separate views that standard surveys collapse, such as positions that reject beef but accept chicken on neural-complexity grounds rather than efficiency grounds, and positions that treat wild suffering as weighty yet deny intervention on epistemic-humility grounds rather than on natural-value grounds. Please also flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate. Finally, state how confident you are in the taxonomy's completeness and identify the specific branch or leaf about which you are least certain—especially any judgment about whether a particular unoccupied position is genuinely incoherent rather than merely neglected.
