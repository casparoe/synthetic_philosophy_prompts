Construct a thought experiment to specifications within the debate over individual contributions to collective harms and the inefficacy objection. Design a realistic, everyday scenario—presented in one paragraph, with no science-fiction elements—in which an ordinary agent performs a discrete action that feeds into a large-scale harm such as climate damage or industrial animal agriculture. Engineer the case so that three conditions hold: first, the agent's contribution is genuinely causally inefficacious in isolation, and no visible threshold is crossed by this particular act; second, the aggregation of harm operates through a structurally opaque, non-linear mechanism (for example, hidden supply-chain intermediaries or feedback loops) rather than through simple linear addition, so that statistical insignificance is not equivalent to causal irrelevance; third, despite the first two conditions, both a Kantian analysis of the maxim and a virtue-ethical analysis of the agent's orientation yield a clear wrongness verdict that does not rely on symbolic expression, personal character corruption, or membership in an identifiable unjust institution. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that the design works: state the verdict and reasoning of (a) a threshold-sensitive consequentialist, (b) the Kantian analysis, (c) the virtue-ethical analysis, and (d) a defender of the pure inefficacy objection. Identify the exact structural feature—distinguishing structural overdetermination from mere statistical noise—that allows the non-consequentialist verdicts to stand, and explain how removing the non-linear aggregation mechanism collapses the case back into a standard linear inefficacy problem. Conclude by stating how the inefficacy defender would attempt to redescribe the aggregation mechanism to restore the appearance of statistical insignificance.
