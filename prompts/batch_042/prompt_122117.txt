Write the complete specification of desiderata that any philosophical reconstruction of Buddhist moral evaluation must meet in order to accommodate consequentialist reasoning about suffering reduction—reasoning that aggregates welfare across large numbers of beings and extended temporal scales—without reducing the view to either a pure outcome-maximizing calculus or to a pure intention-based karma-theory in which only present mental state (cetanā) determines moral value.

For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or doctrinal distinction behind it, indicate whether it is non-negotiable within mainstream Buddhist commitments or negotiable only within specific interpretive camps (such as Theravāda Abhidharma analysis versus Mahāyāna bodhisattva ethics), and flag any pair you believe is jointly unsatisfiable. In particular, your list must cover:

1. The temporal-structure problem: how the theory must distinguish immediate karmic fruit from matured result (vipāka) when assessing long-range suffering reduction, and whether this distinction blocks consequentialist evaluation of distant outcomes.

2. The aggregation problem under anātman: whether welfare can be summed across beings given the denial of a permanent self, and if so, what replaces individual identity as the unit of moral accounting.

3. The upāya constraint: how skillful-means exceptions are to be bounded so that they do not render all outcome-based calculation vacuous, and what distinguishes a legitimate expedient from rationalized harm.

4. The scope of moral patients: the status of currently non-existent future beings and of wild-animal suffering in ecosystems, given that traditional frameworks center on intentional action directed at present sentient subjects.

5. The interdependence requirement: consistency with pratītyasamutpāda (interdependent origination) without committing the naturalistic fallacy of deriving normative weight solely from ecological or causal interconnectedness.

Then identify which single desideratum eliminates the largest number of recent hybrid accounts, and say whether the full set is jointly satisfiable or whether a subset must be abandoned. Do not supply a bibliography, citation list, or named reading recommendations; work only from the criteria themselves.
