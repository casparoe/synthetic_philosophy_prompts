I cover philosophy and cross-cultural intellectual history for a science-and-ideas magazine, and I am preparing a feature on how relational metaphysics is taught in advanced philosophy seminars outside the usual Euro-American canon. For the piece, I want to include a concrete example of assessment design that cannot be satisfied by summary or recall. Please draft a final-exam essay question—together with a model answer of roughly 700 words and a detailed marking scheme—suitable for an upper-level graduate seminar in comparative metaphysics and moral philosophy.

The domain should be Ubuntu and relational conceptions of personhood, specifically contrasted with Western individualist ontologies of the self. The question must not be answerable by reciting the familiar proverb about a person being constituted through other persons, nor by summarizing standard textbook distinctions between communitarian ethics and liberal individualism. Instead, it should force students into a precise conceptual crux: ask them to evaluate whether a genuinely relational ontology of personhood can consistently account for individual moral responsibility in cases of radical relational rupture—for instance, prolonged solitary confinement, exile that severs communal recognition, or severe cognitive decline that dissolves the social networks said to constitute the person. They should be required to consider whether preserving responsibility in such cases forces the relationalist to smuggle back in a non-relational, individual core (perhaps as a bare potentiality or a dispositional substrate), and if so, whether that undermines the view’s distinctive ontological claim or merely qualifies it.

The model answer should articulate the specific moves that distinguish an excellent response from a merely competent one: for example, distinguishing constitutive relational dependence from merely causal social influence; assessing whether appeals to residual or potential relations collapse into a form of dispositional individualism; and evaluating whether the view can be qualified to survive hard cases without becoming unfalsifiable. The marking scheme should allocate 25 marks total, specify the point value of each required move, and identify the characteristic errors that should cost marks—such as conflating relational constitution with causal dependence, treating Ubuntu as purely an ethical doctrine rather than an ontological thesis about what a person is, or assuming without argument that moral responsibility requires a metaphysically independent self. Please write in a formal, polished register appropriate to an editorial brief for an academic audience.
