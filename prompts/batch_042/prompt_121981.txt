I am moderating a colloquium Q&A with an unnamed philosopher who defends a position she calls layered potentialism about mathematical infinity: she accepts Cantor's power-set argument and the internal coherence of Hilbert's hotel within a fixed formal framework, but denies that these establish actual infinities because she treats each successive cardinal as existing only in a modal or constructive potential relative to the prior layer, not as a completed totality. She also rejects strict finitism's strategy of denying Cantor outright, insisting instead that the error lies in conflating intra-framework cardinal comparison with cross-layer ontological commitment. Write me a sequence of eight to ten questions that turn on the actual mechanics of this view. I want at least one question pressing how her framework avoids collapsing into either standard platonism or strict finitism; one on what she makes of the Banach-Tarski paradox as a challenge to the physical intuition behind her layered ontology; one on whether her modal-layer strategy can be applied consistently to the ordinals without generating a hidden actual infinity at the limit; and one on what she would say to a mathematician who claims that her view renders the statement that there are infinitely many primes meaningless rather than true-in-potential. For each, include a follow-up keyed to the defensive reply she is most likely to give—especially her tendency to say that framework-relativity dissolves the paradox rather than solving it. End with one question asking what empirical or logical result could, in principle, force her to abandon the layered-potentialist framework entirely. Avoid generic biographical questions; keep every question anchored to the content of her position.
