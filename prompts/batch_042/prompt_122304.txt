I cover science and philosophy for a magazine, and I somehow ended up on the advisory board for the City Natural History Museum's new permanent gallery on evolutionary biology — don't ask how. We're meeting Thursday to vote on the label framework, and I said I'd circulate a short memo before then but I'm totally stuck. The gallery spotlights 19th-century figures like Francis Galton and a couple colonial naturalists whose statistical methods are still taught, but who also pushed eugenics and racial taxonomy as if it were empirical science. One faction wants every display to carry a "retrospective ethical index" that grades the figure against current human-rights norms; the other faction says that's pure presentism, that historians can never fully escape the moral vocabulary of their own era, and that forcing modern judgments onto past actors corrupts the archive. I was arguing about this with my roommate over burnt toast last night and realized my stance is basically just vibes. Could you draft the board memo? It should recommend a concrete labeling and framing policy — I'm leaning toward a tiered caption system with seperate framing tracks for in-period context and modern distance, but I'm open — and include a rollout timeline within the quarter. The memo needs to take the anti-presentist side seriously rather than dismissing it as denialism, and it should flag exactly where curators still have to make a judgment call because the principle can't settle every edge case. Keep it formal enough for the board — half scientists, half museum staff — but readable, and please, no bibliography or citation lists, since half the board wouldn't read them anyway.
