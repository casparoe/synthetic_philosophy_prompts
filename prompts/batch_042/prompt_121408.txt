Hey, I'm stuck on an argument my friend cooked up about Scheffler's afterlife conjecture and I can't find where the slight of hand is. Could you diagnose it? And please answer in American English.

Here's the chain:
(1) Scheffler claims that much of what gives our present lives meaning is that they contribute to an ongoing human future -- the afterlife of civilization carrying on.
(2) If that's correct, then the value of any given project partly depends on whether there's a future that extends or receives it.
(3) Now imagine a totally self-contained act: you compose a symphony, play it once with no audience, then burn the score and every recording. You intend no legacy.
(4) Even this private act seems to depend on a world where music, ears, and the concept of beauty exist; if humanity dies immediatly after your performance, the framework that makes it music rather than noise collapses.
(5) So every value, even the most ephemeral and hidden, is anchored to an indefinite human future.
(6) But the human future is necessarily finite -- eventually the sun expands, or entropy, or something.
(7) Therefore, on Scheffler's view, all human value is temporary, and temporary value that ends in total annihilation is ultimately pointless.
(8) That lands us in nihilism, which feels like a much bigger problem than just worrying about extinction.
(9) So premise (1) must be wrong, and extinction being bad can't rest on this afterlife idea.

Which step should I reject? I'm tempted to blame (4) -- maybe I'm smuggling in dependence where there isn't any. Or maybe it's (6)-(7), since temporary doesn't mean meaningless. I really want to keep (1) without becoming a nihilist. What's the least costly fix?
