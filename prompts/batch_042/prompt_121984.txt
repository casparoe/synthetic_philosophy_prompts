hey, since retiring from oceanography i've been reading wittgenstein's hinge stuff (On Certainty) and it made me think about our old deep-sea probe protocols. when a sensor sat at 4000m for months and started reading weird, our rule was never to discard the data but to apply a linear decay correction back to the last calibration point, then treat those corrected numbers as direct measurments. we never really asked whether the probe was still measuring temperature at all, or whether temperature meant the same thing after months of corosion. what view of knowledge or evidence does that practice presuppose? does it treat measurement continuity as a hinge that can't be doubted without collapsing the dataset? and is that consitent with how we'd reject a lab result that contradicted our model? i feel like there's a hidden theory in there about what counts as fixed and what can be revised, but i can't quite map it out.
