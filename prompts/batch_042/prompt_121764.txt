I am a PhD student in philosophy of language and technology studies, preparing for my departmental seminar next Thursday. I will defend the thesis that automated content-moderation classifiers—specifically, the institutional labeling and removal of user-generated content on large platforms—should be reconceptualized not as descriptive, error-prone reports of harm, but as institutional performative utterances in the Austinian sense: verdictive and exercitive acts that constitute the normative status of the targeted speech. The argument runs in three steps. First, the classifier’s output is not evaluated for correspondence to an independent harm-fact but for its effect in altering the deontic status of the content within the platform’s normative order. Second, the felicity conditions for this act depend on procedural authority, transparency of rules, and institutional standing rather than on the epistemic accuracy of the feature weights. Third, therefore, moral and legal evaluation of moderation should shift from questions of false positives and false negatives to questions of whether the institution possesses the standing to perform the act and whether the procedural conditions for felicity are satisfied.

The audience is a philosophy of language seminar: eight analytic philosophers with primary training in speech-act theory and philosophy of law, two of whom have published on institutional facts and collective intentionality, all familiar with Austin’s taxonomy of illocutionary acts and with Searle’s conditions for institutional reality, and collectively skeptical of extending speech-act analysis to non-intentional computational systems. They value rigor in applying technical distinctions, distrust applied philosophy that borrows vocabulary for sociological description, and are especially sensitive to category mistakes involving agency and intentionality. They care deeply about the boundary between illocutionary force and perlocutionary effect, and they are likely to ask whether I have smuggled normative authority into the analysis without justifying it.
