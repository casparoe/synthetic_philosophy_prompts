I sit on a county citizens' review board with no academic background in philosophy or science, and every other month I have about forty-five minutes to vote on consultant reports—flood-risk, air-quality, traffic models—that always contain glaring unrealistic assumptions such as perfectly uniform soil, identical driving habits, or purely rational commuters. I have been working through Milton Friedman’s F-twist—the idea that unrealistic assumptions are not automatically flaws if the model still predicts well—along with my own half-formed distinction between assumptions that actually do structural work inside the model and ones that are merely decorative. I need you, acting as an expert philosopher of science who reasons slowly and checks every translation against the original principle, to turn this into a reusable decision procedure I can run in thirty minutes without calling an academic. Before you construct it, spend a moment brainstorming which distinctions must survive the simplification and which can be dropped. Then deliver the procedure as an ordered sequence of tests with the exact question to ask at each step, what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure stops with a clear rejection, where it stops with approval, and where it must hand the decision to human judgment because the issue is values rather than technical fact. Be explicit about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as rules, and state plainly what the procedure cannot do. Apply it to three cases: a flood model assuming uniform clay soil county-wide; an air-quality model assuming every resident drives exactly the same annual mileage; and a traffic model assuming commuters always take the shortest-time route, a result that happens to support the consultant’s politically preferred road expansion. Make sure one of these yields an answer I will not like. Lead with your bottom-line verdict—in one sentence, with no preamble—on whether Friedman’s F-twist can actually be operationalized this way for a non-expert board member; then provide the reasoning and the worked applications. Do not restate my question, do not add a preface, and do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
