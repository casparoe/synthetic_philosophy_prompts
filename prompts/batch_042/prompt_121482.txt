Read King Lear through Cicero's De Senectute, but skip the obvious "Lear just lacks wisdom" reading. Cicero says old age frees you from bodily lust and ambition if you've built good habits, and he takes for granted a Roman social order where elder authority is protected by law and clients. Lear lives in a feudal world where authority is personal and can be divided away with a map. So ask whether Lear's collapse is a moral failure in Cicero's terms, or whether the play proves Cicero's comfort depends on instutional backing that Lear doesn't have. Point to the storm scene and the division of the kingdom, and say if Lear's madness comes from vice or from the structural precarity of aging without that backing. Don't reduce the play to an illustration, and don't twist Cicero to save him—say honestly where the drama resists the philosophy. I'm prepping a discussio on aging and power, not a lit essay, so I care whether the theory survives the play.
