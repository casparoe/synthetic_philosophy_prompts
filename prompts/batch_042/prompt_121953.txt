Write a dialogue, presented as exactly five numbered exchanges with no introduction or conclusion, between a historian who interprets Kepler’s astrology as purely instrumental patronage lacking any sincere belief in celestial causation, and a historian who interprets it as grounded in Kepler’s genuine conviction that planetary configurations emit harmonic physical resonances capable of attuning the human soul, where accurate judgment depends on the observer’s own mental alignment with those rhythms. In each exchange—limited to two sentences, one per speaker—the instrumentalist should press Kepler’s private complaints about predictive failure and economic dependence, while the causationist should defend the coherence of harmonic cosmology and the observer-attunement condition; both must respond directly to the prior point rather than talk past it. Avoid citations, bibliographies, or references to specific editions; keep the dispute conceptual, treating whether mental attunement is a causal mechanism or a rhetorical escape clause as the central crux.
