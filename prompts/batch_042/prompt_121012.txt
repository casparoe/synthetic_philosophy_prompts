I am a novelist constructing a dialogue about justice. In my reading I have observed a recurring argumentative posture that I have not been able to name, and I should like to know whether philosophy has a term for it.

Picture a defender of moral internalism responding to the ring of Gyges. She insists that she would remain just even if invisible, because justice has value for her independently of reputation or punishment. Yet when invited to specify what she would actually do with the ring, she rejects only the vulgar injustices — theft, violence, open fraud — while calmly accepting subtler advantages: reading private letters to anticipate a rival in negotiation, arranging committee memberships so her allies receive the more favourable positions, or guiding a discussion so that an opponent's contribution is quietly ignored, all without violating any explicit rule. She regards these actions not as unjust but as prudent, and she draws the boundary of true injustice precisely where her own behaviour stops. She condemns anyone who imagines gross crime with the ring as lacking moral refinement, yet she does not recognise her own refined exploitation as falling within the scope of injustice at all.

This posture seems different from ordinary hypocrisy, because she genuinely believes she is moral and does not pretend to virtues she lacks. It is not standard self-deception, for she is fully conscious of her actions and does not suppress them. It is not the motte-and-bailey, since she is not retreating to a defensible core and then advancing an indefensible extension; she maintains one consistent position that simply relocates the boundary of injustice. Nor is it exactly moral licensing, because she does not first perform a good deed to authorise a later bad one; the licence is built into her very definition of what counts as injustice.

Is there an established concept — in discussions of Glaucon, in moral psychology, or in the analysis of rationalisation — that captures this specific configuration: the defence of internal moral reasons coupled with the tacit acceptance of rule-compliant, refined exploitation under conditions of invisibility? If the nearest established terms are moral licensing, self-deception, or a particular form of rationalisation, please identify them and explain with precision how the structure I have described differs from each. If there is no established term, please coin one, provide a clear definition with boundary conditions, and distinguish it from the adjacent phenomena I have mentioned.
