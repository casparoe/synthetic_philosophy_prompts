After a long argument with a friend who insisted that Fomenko's New Chronology and ancient-astronaut narratives deserve the same evidential standing as mainstream historiography—simply because they invoke "methodological symmetry"—I need a practical tool rather than more debate. I work as an acquisitions editor for a general-reference encyclopedia, and I need a reusable decision procedure to evaluate submissions proposing radical chronological revision, whether through statistical textual analysis, technological-anachronism arguments, or claims of suppressed records. The procedure should be grounded in philosophy-of-science demarcation criteria, ask ordered questions, specify what counts as a sufficient answer to each, stop with a clear reject, accept-with-caveats, or escalate-to-expert, and flag precisely where the decision is one of values rather than facts. Apply it to three proposals: a Fomenko-style claim that the Almagest was compiled in the sixteenth century via statistical parallel-text analysis; an argument that megalithic construction precision proves extraterrestrial engineering; and a proposal to revise Bronze Age collapse chronology using new radiocarbon calibration curves, including one case where the procedure yields an answer the asker will dislike. Take a committed position: state definitively whether methodological symmetry—the idea that pseudohistorical claims should be evaluated by standards identical to mainstream ones—has any legitimate place in the decision tree, and defend that choice with reasons rather than options. Finally, include a brief note on where the procedure takes liberties with standard demarcation theory or relies on a contested reading of it.
