I work in robotics, designing hierarchical autonomous systems with reactive controllers, deliberative planners, and meta-cognitive monitoring layers that arbitrate between them. Translate the philosophical debate about which varieties of free will are worth wanting — specifically the compatibilist claim that determinism is compatible with meaningful control, the hierarchical model in which higher-order desires regulate lower-order ones, and the evolutionary-functional view that free will is an adaptive decision architecture — entirely into the vocabulary of robotic autonomy (control loops, reference signals, feedback gains, predictive models, error dynamics, layer arbitration, robustness margins). For each variety, specify the exact architectural analog. Then deliver a residue report: which philosophical claims map cleanly onto engineering specifications, which translate only by distorting normative evaluation into optimization objectives, and which have no counterpart in autonomy design at all. In particular, determine whether the judgment that one variety is more worth wanting than another corresponds to a measurable property like stability or efficiency, or whether it marks a boundary where philosophical evaluation exceeds what can be expressed in control-theoretic terms — and say whether that residue reveals a limitation of the engineering vocabulary or a lack of substantive content in the normative distinction.
