Hi there — I'm drafting material for a graduate seminar on the philosophy of social science, and I've run into a pattern in the literature that I can't quite map onto the standard taxonomy. I'm wondering whether there's already an established name for it, or whether I should just treat it as a particular failure mode of methodological individualism.

Here's the pattern. A researcher begins by rejecting holism outright: they say we must never reify societies, institutions, or cultures, and that any proper explanation of a collective phenomenon — say, the sudden collapse of a cooperative norm, or the geographic clustering of a religious practice — has to bottom out in indvidual beliefs, preferences, and local interactions. So far, so standard. But then they build a model in which the macro-level distribution of those beliefs is treated as an exogenous parameter. They calibrate the individual decision-rules against the observed cluster, and once the micro-behaviour reproduces the cluster, they declare the macro pattern explained. The collective configuration is therefore doing silent explanatory work: it sets the boundary conditions and shapes the utility functions, yet it is never itself explained, and the researcher still insists that only individuals are causally real. I've seen versions of this in agent-based work on language shift and in some rational-choice accounts of institutional persistence, but I'm not asking about those fields specifically. What I'm after is the logical shape of the move itself — using a holistic distribution as an unexplained explainer inside a framework that denies holistic entities any explanatory autonomy. Is there a recognised term for this? It feels adjacent to crypto-holism and to downward causation, but it isn't either: there's no attribution of group intentions, and no claim that the macro level literally causes the micro. It's more like explanatory double-entry bookkeeping, where the same collective pattern apears both as the explanandum and as the hidden premise. If there isn't a standard label, could you define the phenomenon precisely — including its boundary conditions, how it differs from an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis, and whether it's genuinely inconsistent with methodological individualism or just an embarrassing omission?

Oh, and please write your reply in British English — I'd like to keep the terminology consistent with the course materials.
