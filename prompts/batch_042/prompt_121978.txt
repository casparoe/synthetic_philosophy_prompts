I have been trying to think through my own stance on work, rest, and fairness without following any particular school, and I want to know whether the pieces I have settled on actually hold together. This is roughly where I stand:

(1) Leisure is a negative liberty: employers and the state should not block people from resting, but they should not mandate rest or treat it as a reward for labor already done.

(2) The Protestant idea that work is a spiritual calling is wrong, but I still feel there is genuine dignity in making things that other people actually need.

(3) Paid work should be capped at 35 hours per week for everyone, with no special exemptions for managers, professionals, or people who say they are passionate about their jobs.

(4) Karoshi — dying because you worked too much — is not just a personal tragedy; it is an institutional failure, because the employer sets the hours, the targets, and the culture.

(5) If a grown adult truly understands the risks and wants to work more than 35 paid hours, the law should allow it, but the employer should still be fully responsible if that work harms them.

(6) Calling leisure a "right" is empty unless jobs are actually redesigned so that taking time off does not quietly punish you with lost promotions or lower pay.

Do these commitments hang together? I am looking for an audit, not a lecture: go through the pairs, say which ones really conflict, which ones only seem to, rank the real conflicts by how bad they are, and for each say what the cheapest fix is and what I would have to give up. Then tell me which single change — dropping or revising one of these — would clean up the most problems at once.

Please return your answer as a JSON object with exactly these fields:
- "overall_verdict": a single sentence saying whether the set is consistent and, if not, how badly.
- "tensions": an array of objects, each containing "pair" (e.g., "3 vs 5"), "status" ("real" or "apparent"), "severity" ("high", "medium", or "low"), "reason" (one sentence), "cheapest_fix" (which commitment to revise and how), and "cost" (what is lost by that fix).
- "severity_ranking": an array of the "pair" strings, ordered from most severe to least severe.
- "best_single_revision": an object with "commitment" (the number), "revision" (a brief description of the change), and "tensions_resolved" (an array of "pair" strings).

Be sure to include at least one pair whose tension dissolves once you look closely, and at least one conflict that is not obvious on the surface.
