Construct a thought experiment in which a subject using a transparent neural-interface display discovers—while remaining fully conscious—that her experience of redness tracks not a specific wavelength but a compressed boundary-encoding data structure, and she can report this functional correspondence from the first-person without any loss of qualitative character. The scenario should isolate whether treating consciousness as a user-illusion (phenomenology as a functional interface rather than a direct representation of physical properties) accommodates this transparency better than a representationalist account that ties qualitative content to representational accuracy. Constraints: avoid inverted-spectrum and zombie scenarios; restrict the technology to present-day or near-future plausibility; specify precisely what the subject knows, what she reports, and why the representationalist must either deny her continued qualitative experience or reconstrue its content. After presenting the case, indicate which structural feature forces the divergence between the two theories, and explain what collapses if the transparency condition is removed.
