I am working through my own position on whether accelerating technology can be governed, using Von Neumann’s distinction between exponentially growing technical capability and linearly adapting social institutions as my starting point. My commitments are: (1) the central risk is structural drift—systems that outrun the feedback loops designed to regulate them—rather than malicious intent; (2) managing this requires a global coordinating body with enough centralized authority to impose rapid moratoria, but also enough internal value pluralism to prevent a single normative framework from locking in; (3) I reject any commensurable metric for trading off safety, innovation, and liberty, holding instead that these values are genuinely incommensurable; (4) under radical uncertainty about long-run outcomes, I think maximin is rational: pick the option with the least catastrophic worst case, even at the cost of forgoing probable benefits; (5) I take this to be consistent with accepting that technological growth is, in some domains, effectively exponential and not artificially stoppable. Do these hang together? In particular, does the centralized authority in (2) presuppose a unitary decision procedure that (3) denies? Does (4) entail a near-total moratorium that contradicts (5)’s acceptance of unstoppable growth, and if so, does that make (1)’s diagnosis of drift incoherent—since drift presupposes ongoing growth that maximin would halt? Rank the tensions by severity, distinguish real conflicts from apparent ones, propose the least costly revision for each, and indicate which single change would resolve the most. If this package rests on a misinterpretation of Von Neumann’s actual view, or if maximin and value pluralism are being combined in a conceptually confused way, tell me directly rather than accommodating the confusion.
