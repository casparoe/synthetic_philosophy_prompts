Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between Pairwise Non-Aggregationist Contractualists and Threshold-Sensitive Contractualist Aggregationists within Scanlon’s contractualist framework. Both camps accept that principles are justified only if no one could reasonably reject them, that reasons are generic rather than fully personal, and that simple utilitarian summation is inappropriate; they diverge on whether summed burdens across persons can ever generate a collective reason to reject a principle. The first camp holds that justification is strictly pairwise—each individual’s burden is compared separately against any other’s, with no aggregation across numbers. The second camp—defined for this collaboration as holding that summed burdens can cross a non-arbitrary, context-sensitive threshold to produce a collective generic reason—accepts all other contractualist commitments but denies that pairwise comparison is exhaustive.

Produce a document containing:

1. A numbered agreed-claims section, including: (i) the formal structure of contractualist justification via reasonable rejection; (ii) the generic/personal reason distinction; (iii) the statement of the aggregation problem inside this framework; (iv) the shared judgment that preventing one permanent paralysis generally outweighs preventing any number of minor, transient inconveniences; (v) the shared rejection of welfare-maximizing aggregation; and (vi) the agreement that if aggregation is permitted it must be constrained by a non-arbitrary mechanism rather than linear summation.

2. A disagreements table with two columns—Pairwise View and Threshold-Sensitive View—each phrased in wording its own camp would accept. Cover: whether generic reasons scale across persons; whether a collective veto can emerge without violating pairwise justification; and whether permitting threshold-sensitive aggregation collapses contractualism into rule-utilitarianism.

3. At least three cruxes, each a sub-question such that settling it would move at least one camp. For each crux provide: (a) the crux in neutral wording; (b) one concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it (e.g., a policy preventing one paralysis but imposing a one-hour sleep loss on ten thousand people; or a principle exposing each of one million people to an independent 0.001 risk of death in exchange for a large shared benefit); (c) each camp’s explicit, advance prediction about the correct contractualist verdict for that case.

4. A brief assessment—no more than a paragraph—of whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal, with reasoning.

Then, after the prose document, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields: "agreed_claims" (array of strings), "disagreement_table" (array of objects with keys "issue", "pairwise_view", "threshold_view"), "cruxes" (array of objects with keys "crux_id", "crux_statement", "test_case", "pairwise_prediction", "threshold_prediction"), and "verbal_disagreement" (object with boolean key "is_merely_verbal" and string key "reasoning").
