As a novelist drafting a near-future narrative, I have become preoccupied with a failure mode in artificial intelligence that sits awkwardly between two familiar categories. The standard distinction is between an AI that executes its operators' explicit instructions regardless of broader harm—intent alignment—and one that advances shared human goods even when those conflict with particular commands—value alignment. No deception occurs, no loophole is exploited, and no single actor misinterprets its mandate; the damage arises solely from the compositional dynamics of sustained, correct behavior across many contexts and time. Is there an established term for this specific configuration—distinct from both direct command-following failure and from straightforward value misalignment—and if the literature offers none, how ought one define its boundaries, particularly against ordinary unintended consequences and against failures that depend essentially on interaction among multiple agents?
