Hey, I'm a retired field ecologist — spent thirty years tracking bird populations — and since I retired I've gotten weirdly obssessed with philosophy of science and social epistemology. I was discussing with my friends over coffee about a pattern we all lived through but never had a word for.

In the 90s our whole subfield switched to this one multivariate analysis package, not because anyone locally tested it against alternatives, but because you kept seeing it in high-profile journals and then grant reviewers started expecting it in proposals. Once funding agencies wrote it into the 'standard methods' section of call texts, grad studnets just learned it by default and stopped asking if there was a better fit for small samples. Every individual choice made sense: you assume the big labs did the homework, and you need the grant to feed your family. But then decades later people discover the package had a hidden bias with small sample sizes, and now it's locked in everywhere — not because anyone decided it was best, but because the cascade got baked into the bureaucracy. I know about information cascades and rational herding, and this feels adjacent, but I think it's different because the herding isn't just belief-copying; it's belief-copying plus institutional reinforcement that punishes anyone who tries to break out. Is there an established concept for this exact configuration? If there isn't, can you coin a clear term and map out precisely where its boundaries sit — how it's not quite a standard cascade, not quite path dependence, and not just groupthink? I'm not asking for a bibliography, just whether the carving already exsists.
