Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers of biology—Jordan and Sam—debating whether symbiotic microbiomes can constitute a unit of selection genuinely distinct from both genes and individual hosts. Jordan should defend a "functional-integration" position: when microbial communities are stably transmitted across host generations and jointly determine host fitness, they form what Jordan terms a "developmental consortium" that selection can act upon as a unit, even in the absence of a shared pool of genetic replicators. Sam should defend a "causal-democracy" gene-level view: evolutionary selection always tracks differences in replicator fidelity and abundance, so microbiome effects are always reducible either to selection on microbial genes or to selection on host genes mediated by environmental conditions, never to selection on an emergent multi-species unit. Require real back-and-forth: Jordan must respond directly to Sam's replication-objection by invoking "transgenerational niche construction" as the mechanism that binds host and microbe into a single selective unit, and Sam must counter by distinguishing mere causal influence from unit-hood through the criterion of "discrete heritable variance." Keep the exchange formal, focused, and free of strawman arguments, and present the answer in American English.
