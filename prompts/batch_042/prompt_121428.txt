Write a poem of about eighteen to twenty-two lines in unrhymed iambic pentameter that enacts, through its own metaphorical architecture, the thesis that cognition is fundamentally analogy-making rather than definition by necessary and sufficient conditions, in the spirit of Hofstadter and Sander's treatment of surfaces and essences. Take as your central comparison a structural parallel that possesses no surface resemblance: the branching geometry of river deltas and the branching structure of logical disjunction. Let the first half present the two domains separately in concrete, sensory language; around line twelve, execute a volta that collapses them into a single relational frame, so that the reader grasps the mapping through shared form rather than shared appearance. End without resolving the comparison—instead, let the final lines generate an unexpected third category that the analogy itself opens up, leaving the reader with an unresolved conceptual slippage. The poem should be precise enough that someone familiar with the analogical view of categorization will recognize its mechanics, but do not cite texts, name chapters, or provide references.
