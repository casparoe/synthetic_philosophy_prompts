For my graduate seminar on symbolic architecture and physical implementation, I want students to derive the evaluation standard before they assess particular models. Define for this purpose the careenium as the discrete, syntactically structured symbolic layer that encodes rule-types and representations, and the simmballs as the continuous, dynamical substrate processes that physically realize computation. The guiding question is whether symbols in the careenium genuinely push the substrate around—that is, whether token-level symbolic states exert downward causal influence on simmball dynamics—or whether all causal work is done bottom-up, with symbolic descriptions merely tracking substrate outcomes.

Write the desiderata: the full specification of what an adequate theory of the careenium-simmball interface must deliver to count as solving the interaction problem. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or conceptual consideration that makes it necessary, and flag whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference (for example: physical closure of the substrate, an anti-homuncular constraint on interpretation, preservation of computational tractability, consistency with hierarchical predictive processing in neuroscience, and the distinction between causal influence and mere correlation). You should specifically address:

- The error-correction and rule-deviation cases that appear to require symbolic intervention in substrate dynamics
- The distinction between token-level causal efficacy and type-level supervenience, which must be preserved rather than collapsed
- The conditions of substrate autonomy—situations in which simmballs operate, adapt, or deviate without symbolic oversight
- Whether any subset of your desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable given standard causal-exclusion principles or other no-go considerations

Finally, score only briefly how three generic positions—pure substrate reductionism, strong symbolic realism, and a weak-emergence hybrid—fare against the list, keeping the emphasis on the criteria and their tensions rather than on the evaluation itself.
