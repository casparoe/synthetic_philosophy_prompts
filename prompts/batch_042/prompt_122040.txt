I'm trying to pin down when using AI actually eats away at understanding vs just saving time. Here are my gut verdicts. If I have ChatGPT draft a work email I was gonna write anyway and I just tweak the tone, my understanding of what I want to say is untouched — its labor outsourcing, not mind outsourcing. If I ask it to summarize a long report I never open, then parrot the summary in a meeting, thats fake understanding, not fine. If I use Copilot for boilerplate code I could write in my sleep, no erosion. But if I use it for a regex I dont understand and never learn, my debugging skills feel hollow, like Im renting knowledge I dont own. Now the tricky pair: I study calculus with an AI tutor that uses analogies that are technically wrong but get me through problem sets. I can solve derivatives now, but I dont feel the limit concept in my bones. Meanwhile my sister uses AI to translate her journal into Mandarin, then checks each word against her original intent with a dictionary. She says she understands her feelings better, not worse. Both involve AI mediating between her and something she wants to understand, with some distortion in the middle. So why does calculus feel eroded and the journal feel deepened? Is there a principle like "understanding erodes only when the AI occupies the reasoning bottleneck you needed to traverse yourself, and theres no recovery path"? Or is that too neat? What does that principle say about the calculus case — am I actually understanding derivatives, or am I just running a rented algorithm? I keep going back and forth on that one.
