I am trying to determine whether this is a substantive causal claim about which layers constrain which, or mostly a methodological lens for organizing narrative, and I keep reaching for analogies from my own background.

I am a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums with a working familiarity in reinforcement learning and multi-agent dynamics — enough to reason about environment architectures, slowly adapting opponent policies, reward-structure equilibria, and single-step transition noise — but I am not a trained historian. Please restate Braudel's three-level model entirely in that vocabulary: map the geographical and climatic layer to static or slowly mutating environment parameters, the social and economic layer to intermediate-timescale dynamics like meta-policy shifts or equilibrium drift, and the event and individual layer to episode-level noise. Then report the residue: which parts translate cleanly, which survive only if I distort the RL framework (for example, treating irreversible geographical transformation as a non-resettable state rather than a fixed map), and which parts have no counterpart at all — particularly any normative weight Braudel assigns to the deeper layers being causally prior or hermeneutically fundamental. I want to know whether the residue reveals that RL vocabulary is missing something essential to historical explanation, such as the idea that deep structures can be genuinely irreversible and that individuals may be causally impotent at certain scales, or whether it suggests that Braudel's distinction between rhythms is less formally sharp than it appears once you try to translate it into hierarchical system dynamics.
