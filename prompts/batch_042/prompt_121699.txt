I edit a small lit magazine and lately we're recieveing first-person essays written by AI systems, like synthetic memoirs. Our board wants a decision procedure — something my copy editors can actually run without a philosophy degree — to tell whether the word 'I' in a submission is doing real Hofstadter-style strange-loop self-reference, where the symbol loops back and generates the self, or if it's just a grammatical indexical like anyone saying 'I went to the store'. I need it laid out as ordered steps: what to check first, what counts as passing, where we stop and hand it to a senior editor because it's a values question not a structural one, and what misfires to watch for. The hard cases it has to cover are an AI calling its own code repairs 'I healed myself', an essay with nested narrators all using 'I' at different levels, and a piece that claims 'there is no I' but keeps using the pronoun anyway. Run the procedure on all three, including at least one where it gives an answer I probably won't like. Also be honest about what the checklist can't catch — for instance if Hofstadter's loop is just a metaphor for self-awareness rather than a literal mechanism — and flag any step where we're really just dressing up judgment as procedure.
