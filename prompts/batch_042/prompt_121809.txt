At a workshop on modern Stoicism, the leader repeatedly used the line “what’s in your control is only your judgment; everything else is indifferent” to shut down any talk of changing external conditions—workplace bias, economic precarity—telling participants to “work on your reaction instead.” Before I push back, I want the slogan disambiguated. Please lay out, as a menu of distinct precise readings, what “only judgment is in our control” could claim: (1) the trivial point that you cannot directly will the past away; (2) the classical Stoic thesis about prohairesis and assent versus externals; (3) the claim that all externals are literally value-neutral (adiaphora) in every context; (4) the stronger claim that you have zero causal efficacy over the world; and (5) any other precise formulation that belongs in the space—such as distinctions between direct control, indirect influence, and mere preference over outcomes. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, whether it is defensible, whether it actually licenses dismissing structural concerns by telling someone worried about discrimination to “control your reaction,” and whether it matches what Epictetus or modern revivalists like Holiday or Pigliucci actually hold. Do not settle on one “correct” interpretation; map the logical space, flag unoccupied combinations if any are incoherent or unexplored, and tell me which reading the facilitator was almost certainly invoking.
