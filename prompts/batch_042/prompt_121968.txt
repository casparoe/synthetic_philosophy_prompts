ok so i've been reading about this voting paradox thing since i retired from the lab and started picking up philosophy books. here's an argument my neighbor and i cooked up that feels like it proves democracy is conceptually broken for big countries, and i can't see the exit.

(1) in an election with millions of voters, the chance my single vote changes the outcome is for all practicaly purposes zero.

(2) if an action can't change an outcome, then it doesn't causally contribute to that outcome.

(3) now here's the part i added: for an action to "express support" for an outcome, there has to be some counterfactual link -- if i hadn't done it, the outcome-world would be different in a way that tracks my preference. but in a big election there are no close worlds where my vote flips the result, so there's no modaly connection between my ballot and the winner.

(4) so voting fails twice: it doesn't cause the result, and it doesn't even express preference about the result, because the preference isn't anchored to anything in the modal structure.

(5) extend this: any collective action where individual contribution is below the causal threshold -- taxes for climate policy, boycott participation, jurry duty in huge pools -- suffers the same disconnect. my "yes" is just a drop in an ocean that doesn't ripple.

(6) but then democratic citizenship, which is supposed to be meaningful participation in collective self-rule, dissolves into performative theater. we aren't actually part of the collective will; we're just going through motions with no modal tie to the result.

(7) yet we clearly think democratic participation matters, and not just instrumentally. so one of the steps is wrong.

(8) my neighbor says reject (3) -- expression doesn't need counterfactual linkage, it's just signaling intent to other people. but then i wonder: if it's just signaling, why do we care about secret ballot? and doesn't that make voting like wearing a team jersey, not like participating in rule?

where exactly does this slide happen? is it (3), or is there something wrong with extending from voting to all collective action in (5)? i want to keep the idea that big-scale democracy is still real participation, but i'm not sure what i'm paying to save it.
