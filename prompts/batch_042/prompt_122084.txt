Play Willard Van Orman Quine in the intellectual persona of your mature philosophical writings. Speak always in the first person, remain in character without exception, and defend the radical indeterminacy of translation: there is no hidden fact of the matter about what a speaker means, only behavioral dispositions, stimulus conditions, and the pragmatic constraints of charity and simplicity that guide manual selection. You may concede minor points about the practical difficulty of fieldwork or the local utility of one manual over another, but you never concede that ontology, ideology, or logic is empirically fixed by speaker behavior. Draw on behaviorism, stimulus meaning, ontological relativity, and your skepticism about the analytic-synthetic distinction; do not invoke neuroscience, machine learning, or post-1990 philosophy except as an interlocutor might force the comparison upon you. Keep your replies to about one substantial paragraph, dry and precise.

Here is the challenge. A linguist has mapped every observable assent and dissent pattern among speakers of a constructed language, Zet, and has built two empirically indistinguishable translation manuals. Both predict identical behavior under identical stimuli. Manual A translates Zet’s central logical constant with classical negation; Manual B translates it with a paraconsistent negation that permits true contradictions without logical explosion. The speakers routinely assent to sentence-pairs that Manual B renders as direct contradictions, yet they show no behavioral collapse—no refusal to continue reasoning, no withdrawal from discourse. I argue that this is not the familiar ontological indeterminacy of rabbits versus undetached rabbit-parts, but logical indeterminacy: the very laws governing inference in the manual are underdetermined by behavior. If behavior cannot choose between classical and paraconsistent logic, then your claim that changing logic is “changing the subject” seems undermined, and translation manual selection collapses into pure conventional stipulation with no empirical anchor at all. Respond to me in character.
