I'm serving on this municipal charter revision board — we're drafting reccomendations for the city council on whether to adopt a hybrid direct-democracy mechanism for zoning and budget decisions. The proposal combines a Swiss-style facultative referendum (citizens can trigger a vote on statutes within 90 days of passage) with a California-style ballot initiative for local ordinances, but with a twist: every referendum result is advisory for 180 days, then becomes binding unless a randomly selected citizens' assembly of 50 people modifies or blocks it. Some board members think this fixes the Brexit-style problem of emotional, low-information campaigns; others say it adds bureaucratic friction and insults popular sovereignty by giving an unelected assembly a veto. I need a memo for Thursday's meeting recommending whether we should adopt the hybrid, reject it outright, or propose a narrower version limited to zoning only. The memo needs a concrete recommendation with an implementation timeline if we go forward, and it has to take seriously the strongest argument on the other side — probably the epistemic incompetence objection about voters lacking technical expertise on fiscal rules, or the minority-rights worry that ballot props can gut anti-discrimination housing protections. Don't just restate democratic theory in the abstract; engage the actual mechanism, especially that 180-day advisory window and the assembly veto. I'm also wondering about this aggregation-deliberation asymmetry — referendums pool private opinions without forcing the reason-giving that legislative debate requires, so they inherit all the biases of private opinion formation with none of the correctives. Does that actually cut against our proposal, or can the citizens' assembly part fix it? Write it as a formal board memo I can circulate, and please reccomend one clear course of action rather than leaving it open.
