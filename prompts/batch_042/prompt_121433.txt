I have no formal training in philosophy, but I've been turning over an idea on my own: that a good life might not be built by accumulating pleasures or achievements, but by systematically removing craving and suffering until there is almost nothing left to disturb you. I've heard this expressed in slogans like "the only victory is the end of wanting" and "tranquility is not a feeling but a vacancy," and I've started using them myself to justify simplifying my life. But I realize they could mean very different things, and I want to see the menu of precise claims hiding inside them. Could you lay out the distinct readings these slogans might carry — from the trivial psychological observation that relief feels pleasant, to the ethical thesis that craving is the sole source of harm and its absence the only genuine good, to something almost metaphysical about consciousness being purified or emptied? For each reading, say whether it is obviously true, defensible but controversial, or false, and tell me which version actually supports the practical conclusion that one should organize life around subtraction rather than accumulation. I don't want bibliographies or author names; I just want the disambiguation so I can recognize which version I actually believe.
