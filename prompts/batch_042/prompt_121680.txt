You are a scholar of Renaissance political philosophy with particular expertise in Machiavelli's The Prince and Discourses on Livy. Before you begin the audit, briefly outline the correspondence table you intend to examine.

In textbooks and popular commentary, Machiavelli's account of fortune and virtù is routinely captured by the river analogy from The Prince: fortune is compared to violent rivers that flood the plains, while virtù is compared to the advance construction of dikes and embankments, so that foresight in calm times limits later damage. Commentators use this image to argue that Machiavelli is essentially an optimist about human agency—contingency can be systematically managed through institutional preparation, much as hydraulic engineering tames nature.

Audit how far this analogy holds. First, lay out the mapping explicitly: what in Machiavelli's political world corresponds to the river, the flood, the dikes, the engineer, and the calm season? Then identify where the mapping breaks. Focus especially on three disanalogies: (1) Machiavelli's gendered personification of fortune as a woman who must be struck and beaten—how does that feature map onto an impersonal river, and what does its absence in the analogy suppress? (2) The cyclical, historical dimension of fortune in the Discourses, where republics undergo long processes of corruption and renewal—does the sudden external shock implied by flooding suppress this temporal structure? (3) The fact that Machiavellian virtù is frequently improvisational, violent, and personal, whereas hydraulic engineering is collective, rational, and incremental—does the analogy smuggle in a modern technocratic conception of agency that Machiavelli lacks?

Finally, assess whether the optimistic conclusion—that contingency is systematically masterable—survives once these disanalogies are on the table. If the river analogy is carrying that conclusion, suggest what alternative analogy, if any, would better capture the actual balance between fortune and virtù in his thought. Be precise, do not invent direct quotations, and flag any genuinely ambiguous points rather than forcing a resolution.
