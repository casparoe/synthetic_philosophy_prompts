Write a deadpan parody of a corporate human-resources ethics-training module — formatted with numbered sections, learning objectives, and compliance checklists — that introduces the “teleological suspension of the ethical” as an executive-leadership framework. Invent a fictional “Institute for Existential Governance” that maps Kierkegaard’s three stages onto management tiers (the aesthetic manager, the ethical manager, and the religious manager) and codifies a “Faith-Based Exception Protocol” allowing a leader to suspend institutional codes of conduct upon receiving an unverifiable, private mandate from a higher telos. The satire must depend on getting the philosophical structure right: preserve the distinction between suspending and abolishing the universal ethical; maintain the paradox that the subject remains simultaneously a potential murderer and a knight of faith; respect the incommunicability of the religious stage by requiring the manager to withhold all justification from peers and auditors; and include the anxiety of infinite resignation as a mandatory “pre-certification emotional audit.” Exaggerate the bureaucratic impulse to formalize Abraham’s solitary, silent ordeal into peer-review forms and weighted-risk matrices. About 600 words. Do not ask for bibliographies or citations, and do not invoke obscure secondary literature; the module should speak in its own jargon, paraphrasing Kierkegaard’s concepts without verbatim quotation.
