Access to advanced education, artistic patronage, and even algorithmically mediated romantic pairing is then calibrated according to this index, with the explicit rationale that the state ought to maximize aggregate emotional stability by filtering out distressing information and engineering pleasant expectations. The architects of the system insist that this is not manipulation but enlightened welfare, since citizens retain formal autonomy and are never informed that their informational environments are curated.

Given du Châtelet's conviction that happiness is not reducible to passive pleasure, and that both study and genuine attachment are indispensable to it, how do you think she would evaluate this institutional architecture? Would she see the engineered illusions as a legitimate extension of her own view that some false beliefs sustain well-being, or would she condemn the system for substituting manufactured affect for the rational and emotional activities she takes to be constitutive of human flourishing? Please reach and defend a specific verdict—whether she would reject the design outright, accept it only under strict conditions, or endorse it with modifications—grounding your conclusion in the particular relationship she draws among illusion, reason, and love. In doing so, explain what institutional safeguards, if any, she might demand before consenting to any such arrangement, and clarify why her philosophy of happiness either supports or undermines the claim that an opaque biometric score can serve as a legitimate proxy for human flourishing. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I am interested in the philosophical argument itself.
