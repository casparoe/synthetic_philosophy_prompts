Exam question: In the Sleeping Beauty problem, Beauty knows she will be awakened once if a fair coin lands Heads and twice—Monday and Tuesday, with full memory erasure between them—if it lands Tails. Upon finding herself awake, should her credence that the coin landed Heads be 1/2 or 1/3? Analyze both the "halfer" and "thirder" positions, with particular attention to whether the relevant update involves self-locating belief—belief about one's own temporal position within a centered world—or only uncentered belief about the coin. Address whether the thirder's appeal to three equally likely centered possibilities (<Heads, Monday>, <Tails, Monday>, <Tails, Tuesday>) requires an independent principle of indifference, and whether accepting that appeal forces Beauty to abandon her Sunday-night credence of 1/2.

Student answer: The thirder position is correct: Beauty should assign 1/3 to Heads upon waking. She is now in one of three centered worlds, and because the protocol is deterministic and the coin fair, these worlds are equiprobable by indifference over self-locating possibilities. Applying Bayes's rule with P(awake | Heads) = 1 and P(awake | Tails) = 1 gives P(Heads | awake) = (1 × 1/2) / (1/2 + 1) = 1/3.

The halfer position claims Beauty should remain at 1/2 because she acquires no new uncentered evidence about the coin. This is mistaken in a deeper way: halfers treat "I am awake now" as lacking any truth-conditions whatsoever, as though self-locating propositions were not genuine beliefs. But centered worlds are the proper objects of credence in diachronic reasoning, so refusing to update is simply refusing to apply Bayes's rule to the correct sample space.

One might object that if Beauty's rational credence is 1/3 upon waking, she should have held 1/3 on Sunday night, contradicting her knowledge that the coin was fair. This confuses the ex-ante prior over uncentered worlds with the ex-post conditional over centered worlds. The thirder does not say the coin's objective chance changed; she says the conditioning event—being awake—selects a subset of the original space in which Tails is twice as represented. Hence her Sunday-night prior of 1/2 is preserved, and the update is consistent—provided we recognize that self-locating belief operates on a logical structure distinct from uncentered belief, one that does not fall under the principal principle linking objective chances to rational credence.
