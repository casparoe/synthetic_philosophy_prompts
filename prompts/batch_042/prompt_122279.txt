For an article on adversarial collaboration in heritage ethics, draft the joint statement that two opposing camps—State-Succession Restitutionists and Living-Community Preservationists—would sign after good-faith engagement on the restitution of colonial-era museum holdings, treating the Benin Bronzes and the Parthenon Marbles as reference cases rather than isolated disputes. To keep the exercise from collapsing into familiar talking points, inject one constructed boundary case: artifacts removed in the nineteenth century from a polity that no longer exists, whose descendants are now dispersed across three modern nation-states with competing custodial claims, so the parties can test whether their disagreement is genuinely about state continuity, present custodial capacity, or a deeper principle.

The document should open with a numbered section of agreed claims—covering the general historical circumstances of the Benin punitive expedition and the removal of the Parthenon sculptures, the current conservation and security capacities of major holding museums, the conceptual distinction between corrective justice (addressing the wrong of the original taking) and distributive justice (allocating present custodianship), and the physical risks attendant to deaccessioning and long-distance transport—phrased neutrally so both camps can endorse them.

This should be followed by a disagreements table: each camp’s precise residual position worded in language its own proponents accept and the opposing camp recognizes as accurate, with verbal disputes explicitly separated from substantive ones.

Then list the cruxes—specifically, whether legitimate state succession is a necessary condition for restitution; whether a present cultural community’s living practice tied to the objects creates an independent custodial claim regardless of state borders; and whether risk of damage in transit can ever be a decisive moral reason for retention.

For each crux, specify one concrete comparison or empirical case both sides agree bears on it—such as the post-return conservation record for repatriated Benin works versus comparable retained works, or institutional-stability metrics for potential recipient museums—together with each side’s explicit prediction of what that evidence shows.

Write in the formal, concise voice of a journalist covering science and ideas. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or appeals to obscure theoretical texts; keep the prompt fully self-contained for a model without web access.
