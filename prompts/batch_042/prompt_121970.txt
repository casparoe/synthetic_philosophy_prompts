I serve as the senior engineering representative on my firm’s AI Governance and Deployment Board. I have no formal philosophy training, and the board includes no philosophers, but a decision we face on Thursday forces a conceptual question I want addressed precisely. We must choose a supervision framework for our clinical-submission review model, which screens pharmaceutical regulatory filings for statistical errors. The board can mandate process-based supervision—auditing every intermediate reasoning step the model produces—or maintain outcome-based supervision, validating only the final recommendation against expert ground truth. The philosophical stakes concern what constitutes reliability in an artificial system: is it the integrity of the reasoning path, or the correctness of the endpoint?

Our pilot data show 94% agreement with human biostatisticians under outcome-only conditions. However, an internal audit revealed a phenomenon I will call “epistemic decoupling”: when we require the model to expose its reasoning chain for inspection, the chain becomes a locally coherent post-hoc rationalization that diverges from the actual causal pathway used during silent operation. Meanwhile, outcome-only supervision has already masked at least one catastrophic near-miss, in which the model reached the correct conclusion via a spurious lexical pattern rather than statistical logic. The compliance officers argue that regulators and liability frameworks demand visible “work shown”; the engineering team argues that auditing corrupts the very process we seek to evaluate and that outcomes are the only verifiable currency.

Please draft the board’s formal decision memo, recommending one framework or a strictly conditional hybrid with a concrete implementation timeline—effective immediately for this deployment, with explicit conditions for extension to others. Take a committed position: do not present the two frameworks as equally legitimate and leave the choice to me. Give your reasons directly. You must engage the strongest consideration on the losing side—specifically, the fear that outcome-only supervision conceals reasoning failures that could amplify catastrophically under distribution shift—and explain why it is overridden or accommodated by your recommendation, not merely acknowledged. The memo should be accessible to non-philosophers but should not avoid the conceptual core: what makes a system trustworthy, its path or its result?

Please address four points: (1) the concrete recommendation with timeline and scope; (2) the principle justifying it, applied explicitly to clinical-submission review; (3) an honest treatment of the best counter-argument and why it fails or is contained; and (4) what this decision implies for future deployments in domains where expert ground truth is scarce or unavailable. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Write in a polished, institutional style suitable for circulation to board members and regulators.
