I serve as an ethics mediator at a clinic offering deep-brain stimulation for severe, treatment-resistant depression, an intervention that frequently transforms patients' core values and self-narratives in ways they could not have anticipated beforehand. Because standard informed consent assumes patients can evaluate outcomes using their present preferences, I need a reusable procedural checklist—grounded in the philosophical literature on transformative experience—that my team can run on any consent evaluation without requiring philosophical training. The procedure should proceed in ordered steps: first, determine whether the decision involves a first-person epistemic gap about subjective value change; second, test whether the patient’s current preference ranking can coherently project onto the post-intervention state; third, check for what I will call "value-inheritance failure," where the post-intervention self rejects the reasoning that led the pre-intervention self to consent; fourth, assess whether substitute decision criteria—such as narrative continuity with peer reports or stability of higher-order preferences—are available and adequate; and fifth, specify the exact point where the procedure yields a procedural stop (consent insufficient) versus where it must hand the decision to a mediator’s values judgment rather than to a factual check. Apply the procedure to three cases: a patient whose pre-intervention self values artistic creativity and fears emotional flattening, but peer reports from post-intervention patients indicate that flattening restores their creative drive; a patient who insists that only her present self can authorize changes to her future self regardless of outcome; and a patient facing a novel variant of the intervention for which no peer cohort exists. For each case, show where the procedure lands, including one result that conflicts with the common clinical intuition that consent should be granted. Finally, state explicitly what the procedure cannot resolve—such as whether personal identity survives transformation—and flag every step that remains a judgment call dressed up as procedure.
