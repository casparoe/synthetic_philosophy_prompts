I am a high school student working on an independent essay about Nietzsche’s philosophy of health, and I would appreciate conceptual comments and suggestions on the argument I am developing. Rather than treating health as the mere absence of disease, Nietzsche—particularly in his autobiographical reflections and in The Gay Science—advocates for what he calls great health, a robust, affirmative vitality that embraces risk and recovery. My essay connects this philosophical ideal to his documented personal practices: his preference for light Italian and French cuisine over heavy German food, his avoidance of alcohol and strong stimulants, and his insistence that digestion and climate directly shape the quality of one’s thought.

The central claim I want to defend is that Nietzsche’s dietary regimen represents what I term a physiological transvaluation: a disciplined attention to the body not for the sake of denying it, as in the ascetic ideal he critiques in On the Genealogy of Morals, but for amplifying its creative and intellectual power. Where the ascetic priest directs bodily discipline toward spiritual salvation and life-denial, Nietzsche directs it toward enhanced affirmation—using dietary restraint as a technology of the self that serves this-worldly greatness rather than other-worldly redemption.

My draft argues three points. First, that Nietzsche’s rejection of German culinary heaviness symbolizes a broader rejection of cultural heaviness and resignation. Second, that his specific food choices—lean meats, simple preparations, avoidance of beer and sweets—constitute a practical ethics of energy management. Third, that this practice avoids the trap of the ascetic ideal precisely because its telos remains immanent: greater thought, stronger art, more vigorous life, rather than transcendence.

I am particularly interested in feedback on the following: whether the distinction between physiological transvaluation and the ascetic ideal holds up textually, or whether I am forcing a contrast that Nietzsche himself would reject; whether the symbolic reading of cuisine is defensible or overreaches; and whether there are obvious inaccuracies in how I characterize his dietary views. I do not need a bibliography or reading list—only comments on coherence, accuracy to Nietzsche’s philosophy, and suggestions for sharpening the argument.
