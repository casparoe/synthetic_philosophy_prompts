I serve on the appeals panel of a state architectural licensing board. We must decide whether to uphold the suspension of an architect, M., whose approval of structurally deficient plans—generated under a proprietary AI design mandate her firm required—led to a partial building collapse. The board's charter permits suspension only for conduct for which the licensee bears genuine moral responsibility, not mere causal involvement. M. appeals by invoking Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: because every cause of her action traces back to her education, the firm's protocol, and the algorithm's output—none of which she authored—she cannot satisfy the condition of being causa sui, and therefore cannot be the proper subject of blame. Some panelists regard this as determinism that licensing law should ignore; others think it exposes a real tension in our responsibility standard. Draft the panel's written opinion recommending either upholding or overturning the suspension, with a reasoning framework the board can apply in future cases. The memo should treat the strongest version of M.'s argument seriously, explain why it does or does not defeat our charter's standard, and specify a concrete time frame or condition for reinstatement or permanent revocation. Do not include legal citations, philosophical bibliographies, or reading lists; the reasoning should stand on its own.
