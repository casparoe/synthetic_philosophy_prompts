hey, grading some phil papers for a friend and want to sanity check my read. exam asked: Tranquilism holds that only the absence of craving and suffering matters for wellbeing, not presence of pleasure or achievement. Apply this to the null-state case: a being engineered to have zero preferences, zero suffering, and zero positive affect. Does it have maximal wellbeing? Give the tranquilist answer and one serious objection, then reply.

student answer:
Tranquilism is basically negative utilitarianism for individuals - wellbeing is just suffering subtracted, pleasure doesn't add anything. The null-state being has no cravings and no pain, so wellbeing is maxed out; there's literally nothing missing. This is close to Epicurus's idea of ataraxia, and also connects to Buddhist anatta where dissolving the self removes dukkha. The rock objection says this makes wellbeing cheap since rocks also lack suffering. The tranquilist reply is that wellbeing only applies to entities that can suffer, so rocks are out of scope. So the null-being has peak wellbeing, even if it feels weird to call that good in a positive sense.
