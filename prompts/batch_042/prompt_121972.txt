I'm giving the retirement toast for my chair, who ran the macro lab for thirty years and always kept Keynes's essay about our grandchildren in his desk drawer — he once said Keynes's claim that greed is basically a mental illness is the only honest thing ever said about university funding culture. Alot of the room are economists who know the essay cold and half are family who dont. Write me a three-minute toast, max 400 words, that uses Keynes's split between absolute needs (real, satiable) and relative needs (status-driven, insatiable) to actually shape what I can say about his career: he spent decades on the means — budgets, growth metrics — precisely because he knew they are never the ends, since relative needs always outrun them. Don't pretend he solved the economic problem; do say he kept the lights on so others could chase contemplation and friendship. Start with the bottom line in one sentence — what should this room actually remember about him? — then build out. Keep it warm, not a lecture, and dont make me sound like I'm reading a textbook.
