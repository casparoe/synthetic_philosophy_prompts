Please draft a final-exam essay question for an upper-level undergraduate philosophy of mind seminar, together with a model answer of approximately 1000 words and a detailed marking scheme. The exam is 90 minutes, and all parts of your response should be in British English. The question must concern functionalism and multiple realizability, but it should not be answerable by summarising the standard distinction between mental-state functionalism and substrate independence, or by listing canonical examples from Putnam and Fodor. Instead, develop the following conceptual tension within the question itself: the level-individuation problem. Ask students to evaluate whether a functionalist can consistently maintain that mental states are individuated purely by their causal-functional roles while also claiming that those roles may be realised in physically diverse substrates, given that specifying the roles with sufficient precision to exclude pathological realisers—systems that replicate macro-level input-output patterns without the appropriate micro-level causal coherence—appears to require fixing a privileged level of functional abstraction. The question should require students to argue whether fixing such a level either smuggles in substrate-dependent constraints, thereby undermining multiple realizability, or renders the functional description so abstract that it loses empirical content and cannot distinguish genuine cognition from sophisticated simulation.

The model answer should address both horns explicitly, explain why an appeal to typical or normal realisation conditions risks circularity rather than resolving the difficulty, and defend a specific resolution—whether by supplementing functionalism with a non-functional constraint, by embracing the abstraction as a feature, or by rejecting one horn of the dilemma—while remaining clear on whether that resolution succeeds. The marking scheme should allocate 20 marks in total and identify five specific moves that separate an A-range answer from a B-range answer: correctly diagnosing the tension as a dilemma about individuation rather than merely describing functionalism and multiple realizability separately; engaging with the circularity worry rather than naming it in passing; offering a substantive, argued resolution rather than concluding that the issue depends on further empirical investigation; showing how the problem affects both token-functionalism and type-functionalism; and avoiding the misconception that multiple realizability entails that any physically different system with identical behavioural outputs necessarily possesses the same mental states. For each move, state the marks available and note the typical error that causes candidates to lose them. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations in the question, model answer, or marking scheme.
