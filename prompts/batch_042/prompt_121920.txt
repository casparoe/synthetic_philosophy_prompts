All the individual clicks are anonimised; nobody sees who shared what, just the velocity score and the headline. I got into an argument with the product manager because this seems to treat 'the public' as one giant mind that can be excited or calm, when acutally it's just thousands of isolated people tapping screens. A friend mentioned Kierkegaard had issues with the press inventing an abstract 'public' that talks about everything and is responsible for nothing, which sounds relevant, but I'm not sure.

So here's my question: what philosophical commitments does the 'pulse' design actually presuppose? Is that smuggling in a particular epistemology? And is there a contradiction in using the public as an authority (we follow its velocity) while treating it as nobody in particular (because everything is anonimised and aggregated)?
