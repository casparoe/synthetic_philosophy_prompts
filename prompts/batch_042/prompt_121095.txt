I sit on our citys Public Welfare Allocation Board. Next Tuesday we vote on adopting the planning offices "Well-Being Budget Framework," which is basically a lexical-threshold negative-utilitarian setup: any policy that leaves even a small group in severe chronic pain is automatically out, no matter how much moderate happiness it buys for everyone else. The twist is their "banded commensurability" amendment—they want to allow pleasure-pain tradeoffs inside three bands (mild, moderate, severe) using different exchange rates, so moderate pleasure can offset moderate pain at 2:1, but severe pain is shielded from lower-band gains. Half the board says this preserves the lexical floor; the other half says once you allow any cross-band weighting youve smuggled ordinary utilitarian calculus back in and the floor collapses.

Please draft the memo Ill circulate before Thursdays meeting. Recommend whether we adopt the pure lexical version, the banded version, or reject the framework and keep our current case-by-case review. Include a concrete timeline if you pick one, and take seriously the strongest argument on the losing side—especially the worry that treating pleasure and pain as even partly commensurable undermines the whole point of having a lexical threshold. Write it like a real board document, not an essay, and dont worry about citing philosophers—just reasoning we can vote on.
