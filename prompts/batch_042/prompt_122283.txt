I'm writing up my position for a course on perpetrator studies and want a consistency check before I finalize my essay. Here is where I stand. (a) I think Browning's analysis of Reserve Police Battalion 101 is essentially correct: for most of the ordinary policemen, situational dynamics—peer pressure, authority, incremental escalation, and career concerns—explain participation far better than deep prior ideological hatred. (b) I think Goldhagen is wrong to claim that a uniquely German eliminationist antisemitism was necessary for these killings, though I grant that cultural background can shape which actions appear morally permissible without being sufficient on its own. (c) I believe the small minority who refused to participate show that pre-existing moral character can act as a genuine resistance point against situational pressure. (d) I hold that explaining perpetration causally does not diminish moral responsibility: understanding why someone killed is not the same as justifying it. (e) I think moral education should therefore prioritize institutional and procedural safeguards over character-based virtue training, since robust character evidently failed for the vast majority. (f) I want to introduce a further factor—moral framing—by which bureaucratic language and role-based descriptions restructure the moral salience of violence, so the same physical act is perceived differently; this is meant to be neither purely situational, like peer pressure, nor purely dispositional, like hatred. (g) But I also worry that if moral framing operates through institutional structures, then relying on institutional design as in (e) might actually reinforce the very framing mechanisms that enable violence, producing a self-undermining loop. Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to conflict? Rank the real tensions by severity. For each, identify the cheapest revision and its cost. Is there one revision—perhaps abandoning (c), (e), or (f)—that would clean up the most problems without collapsing the rest? In particular, tell me whether my rejection of Goldhagen in (b) is compatible with my acceptance of cultural influence there, and whether the distinction drawn in (f) actually does explanatory work that Browning's situationalism already covers.
