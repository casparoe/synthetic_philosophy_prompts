Write a dialogue between Mara, a phenomenological pluralist, and Elias, a teleonomic monist, on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism—whether every pleasure shares a single essential nature. Frame the debate around a precisely described hypothetical case: a direct-stimulation protocol targeting the nucleus accumbens produces in subjects an intense, sustained positive affect that is entirely objectless (no taste, no achievement, no aesthetic object), carries no motivational impetus to seek or avoid anything, and is not integrated into episodic memory. Mara should argue that this null-content state proves pleasures form only a family-resemblance class: gustatory, intellectual, and now this synthetic pleasure share nothing beyond a bare feeling-tone and a loose functional tie to reinforcement, so the search for a common essence is misguided. Elias should reply that the case is not an outlier but a purified instance of the single underlying mechanism—reward-prediction-error signaling—stripped of the cognitive and sensory scaffolding that normally differentiates pleasures phenomenologically; the absence of object and motivation is exactly what one expects when the core signal is isolated. Require genuine back-and-forth: Mara must directly address Elias’s claim that phenomenological differences are merely superficial modulation of one signal, and Elias must directly address Mara’s charge that if the core signal can exist without any of the usual phenomenological or functional accompaniments of pleasure, then pleasure is being defined circularly by the presence of that signal rather than discovered. The dialogue should be formally structured, with neither speaker reduced to a strawman, and should conclude with each conceding a specific point—Mara admitting that a unified neural substrate likely exists, Elias admitting that substrate alone may not exhaust the normative or phenomenological concept of pleasure—while leaving the heterogeneity question genuinely unresolved.
