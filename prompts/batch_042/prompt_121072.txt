A refrigerator making ice cubes: local entropy went down, but I still feel time runs forward inside, not backward. If the whole universe were in perfect thermal equilibrium except for one tiny pocket that randomly fluctated into low entropy, I'd say there's still an arrow inside that pocket, but I'm unsure if there's a global arrow for the whole thing. The fact we remember yesterday not tomorrow feels explained by entropy increase, but I also think that if existant beings lived in that pocket and formed memories of whatever came 'before' them, they'd call that past too -- so maybe the psychological arrow just tracks entropy's local slope. I think the low-entropy Big Bang is the root explanation, but I also think that if the universe started high-entropy and contracted toward a low-entropy Big Crunch, the arrow near the crunch would point toward it, which makes boundary conditions seem to do all the work. Yet quantum decoherence feels like an independent arrow, since branching looks one-way, though maybe that's just thermodinamic entropy in disguise. Is there one principle that gets all these, or close? Which of my verdicts would it force me to drop, and why?
