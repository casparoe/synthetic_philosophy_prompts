I've been arguing with a colleague about whether "easy knowledge" objections expose a fatal flaw in reliabilist and closure-friendly epistemologies, or whether they are merely symptoms of misapplying a principle of transmission across levels. Before I commit to a position for an upcoming graduate seminar, I want a rigorous specification of adequacy: what must a theory of epistemic justification actually deliver to count as having genuinely dissolved the bootstrapping problem, rather than simply relocated the circularity into a different vocabulary?

Write the full list of desiderata. For each requirement, state the motivating case or conceptual distinction it protects, and indicate whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. The list should cover at least: (i) Moore-style circular reasoning, in which a subject deploys a perceptual belief to justify the reliability of the very perceptual faculty producing it; (ii) closure-based accumulation, in which knowing p and knowing p→q yields knowledge of q without any independent check on q; (iii) cross-source bootstrapping, where testimonial and perceptual streams validate each other in a loop with no external anchor; and (iv) temporal bootstrapping, where anticipated future knowledge is treated as present justification for the beliefs that will generate it. The theory must preserve a robust distinction between first-order knowledge and second-order knowledge about one's own faculties; it must remain consistent with fallibilism rather than requiring Cartesian certainty; and it must not collapse the boundary between justification and mere coherence or pragmatic success. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable—particularly whether blocking (ii) forces an outright rejection of closure, and whether that rejection should be treated as a theoretical cost or as an independent virtue. Then identify which single requirement does the most damage to standard contenders—reliabilism, basic coherentism, pure evidentialism, and hybrid views—without turning the exercise into a mere scorecard. The conceptual work should lie in the criteria and their interrelations: which demands are genuine constraints and which are prejudices of one epistemological camp, and whether the space of adequate theories is narrower than it initially appears. I need the formulation precise enough to defend in discussion without reference to any specific text, so keep it free of bibliographic dependencies and phrase every node in terms of cases and structural conditions rather than authorial attributions.
