Write a villanelle that enacts Sen’s liberal paradox through its formal architecture. Use two refrains—one articulating the Paretian principle that unanimous individual preference should determine the social choice, and one articulating the minimal liberal principle that at least one person retains a protected sphere over a purely personal matter. Ground the poem in a specific, non-classic scenario: a small collective of neural-interface users must vote on whether to impose a universal filter that removes violent imagery from all shared sensory streams. Every member except one mildly prefers the filtered collective experience, yet that single dissenter claims an inviolable right to unfiltered perception for artistic work. Let the two refrains alternate stanza by stanza, each time showing how consistent application of one principle blocks the other, so that the poem’s turning lines become the logical deadlock itself. In the final couplet, place both refrains side by side without synthesis or resolution, letting the villanelle’s required convergence demonstrate that the conflict is structural rather than contingent on the particular example. The verse should be precise enough that a reader acquainted with social-choice theory recognizes the paradox immediately, and compelling enough that the tension is felt even without prior knowledge of Sen’s argument. Do not resolve the conflict with an external moral; let the form bear the full weight of the impossibility.
