I was arguing with a friend about whether Rorty's antirepresentationalism actually helps with real-world culture wars, and we kept circling back to the Scopes Monkey Trial in 1925. I know the broad strokes—John Scopes breaks the Butler Act by teaching evolution in a Dayton, Tennessee high school, William Jennings Bryan prosecutes with this literalist Biblical vocabulary, Clarence Darrow defends by framing science as the authority that represents nature correctly, and the whole thing becomes a spectacle about who gets to define reality for public education. But I want to push this further through Rorty's neopragmatism.

Specifically, can you treat the trial as a clash not between truth and falsehood, but between two competing final vocabularies—one religious, one scientific—both of which are wrongly claiming to mirror nature? Rorty rejects that knowledge represenats reality; he thinks vocabularies are tools for coping and that progress is redescription, not closer approximation. So map the actors onto that framework: are Bryan and Darrow both trapped in representationalist postures? What would a Rortian liberal ironist have said about the conflict? And does Rorty's emphasis on solidarity over objectivity explain anything about why the trial turned into a battle over instutional control—who writes the curriculum—rather than a genuine philosophical debate?

Then run it the other way: what does the Scopes episode reveal about the limits of Rorty's view? Because even if we drop the mirror-of-nature metaphor, people in 1925 fought viciously over which vocabulary got taught in schools, and the legal outcome (Scopes convicted, law stayed) wasn't resolved by anyone saying oh, these are just different tools. Does that show that antirepresentationalism ignores the material and legal power embedded in vocabulary choices? Or that Rorty's distinction between normal and abnormal discourse doesn't capture how public conflicts actually work? I want an honest account of where Rorty illuminates the case and where the case breaks the theory—no need for citations, I just want the conceptual mapping done carefully.
