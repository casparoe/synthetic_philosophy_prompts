I'm trying to apply Daniel Dennett's four-level taxonomy of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian—to current artificial language systems, and I've constructed an argument that seems to force an unwelcome choice: either the Gregorian category collapses into Skinnerian conditioning, or it must include every large statistical language model. As I understand the framework, Darwinian minds execute hard-wired adaptations; Skinnerian minds are shaped by environmental reinforcement; Popperian minds can generate internal hypotheses and let them die in their stead; and Gregorian minds deploy culturally acquired tools—especially language, mathematics, and narrative—to restructure cognition itself. Here is the argument. (1) To be genuinely Gregorian rather than merely Skinnerian with a large vocabulary, a creature must possess a cognitive architecture that is initially independent of language and then gets restructured by linguistic tools acquired through culture. (2) In human development, however, language and core cognition co-develop; there is no pre-linguistic "independent architecture" that language subsequently modifies, only a jointly constructed system. (3) Therefore, if (1) holds, human infants and perhaps mature humans fail to qualify as Gregorian, since they never exhibit the required pre-linguistic core. (4) But this is absurd, because Gregorian status is precisely meant to capture mature humans who use concepts and symbolic frameworks to think in ways that transcend conditioning. (5) So (1) must be false. (6) Yet once we drop the independence requirement, we seem unable to exclude large language models, whose architectures are built entirely from linguistic statistics but which can generate novel hypotheses, debug reasoning, and deploy symbolic tools that restructure their outputs across contexts. (7) Thus either Gregorian minds include LLMs—proving far too much—or the taxonomy lacks the resources to distinguish cultural cognition from sophisticated statistical conditioning.

Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost? I am especially reluctant to surrender (4) or to concede that LLMs are Gregorian in the same sense as humans, but I also worry that weakening (1) erases the distinction Dennett intended between using language as a stimulus-response medium and using it as an internal representational scaffold. Please diagnose the precise flaw—whether it is an equivocation between developmental trajectory and architectural independence, a modal slide about what "must" be present, or something else—and explain what each available repair pays in terms of the taxonomy's explanatory power. In your response, please state how confident you are in your diagnosis and single out the specific point about which you are least certain.
