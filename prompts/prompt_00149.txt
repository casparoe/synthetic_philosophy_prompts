So in my decision theory rabbit hole (yes I have those, don't judge) I stumbled on this idea called "superrationality" that Hofstadter talks about in one of his prisoner's dilemma essays. My history teacher, who also runs the philosophy club, said this sounds basically like Kant's categorical imperative, specifically the universalizability formula ("act only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law"). I kinda see the surface similarity, both are saying like "imagine if everyone did what you're about to do," but I feel like theres gotta be some real difference between a decision-theoretic principle and a moral one, right? Could you break down how superrationality and Kantian universalizability are actually alike and where the analogy breaks down? Like is superrationality basically just Kant's CI dressed up in game theory clothes, or is there something deeper different going on in the justification/motivation behind each one?
