In my linguistics club (we train for NACLO, so if you've heard of that, this is basically like that but with philosophy attached) our coach went on this tangent about a philosopher named Donald Davidson and something called "radical interpretation" -- basically the puzzle of how you could ever figure out what a total stranger's words mean if you share zero language and zero background with them, given that you supposedly have to figure out what they believe and what their words mean at the exact same time, since you can't really pin down one without the other. He said the only way Davidson thinks you can even get a foothold is by assuming the person is being truthful and roughly rational by your own lights (the "principle of charity"), and that even after you've used absolutely all the evidence there could be, there might still be more than one totally different scheme for interpreting the person that fits everything equally well, so there's no single fact about what they "really" mean deep down. I also skimmed a bit about a later paper of his with a pretty funny title, "A Nice Derangement of Epitaphs," where he argues there's actually no such thing as a fixed shared "language" in the way people assume, just two people improvising a "passing theory" about each other on the fly each time they talk (his big example is that you can totally still understand someone even when they mess up a word, like a malapropism, so you can't have just been matching their sounds to a rulebook you both memorized).
