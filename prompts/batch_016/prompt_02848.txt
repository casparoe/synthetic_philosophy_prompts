So Nyaya logic has this theory of four pramanas, i.e. valid ways of knowing something: perception, inference, comparison, and testimony (shabda). But testimony only counts as a genuine, independent pramana when it comes from an "apta" -- someone who actually has real knowledge of the thing AND genuinely intends to pass it on without distorting it. The Naiyayikas, especially in the Navya-Nyaya tradition that Gangesha Upadhyaya kicked off, even spelled out the exact conditions a sentence has to meet to produce valid verbal knowledge: expectancy (akanksha), semantic fitness (yogyata), proximity (asatti), and the hearer grasping the speaker's intent (tatparya).

Here's the thing I can't stop chewing on, especially with all the hand-wringing over people trusting chatbots for medical and legal advice: what would Gangesha say if you asked him point blank whether an answer from an AI chatbot could ever count as shabda pramana? Does the apta condition even make sense applied to a statistical model that has no perceptual or inferential "jnana" of its own, and arguably no intention to communicate anything at all? Or is there enough give in the Navya-Nyaya machinery that reliability could stand in for genuine knowledge here? Or would he just say the whole framing is confused, and what's really happening when you trust a chatbot's answer is you're performing an anumana (inference) from it's track record, not recieving testimony at all?

Give me your actual best reconstruction of his view, and defend it -- don't give me "on the one hand, on the other hand," I need something with a spine, since that's the only kind of answer that would be any use for the piece.
