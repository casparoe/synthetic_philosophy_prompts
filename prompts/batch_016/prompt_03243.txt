I've spent the past few weeks reporting on the new wave of generative-AI music tools—Suno, Udio, and their rivals—which can turn a short text prompt into a fully arranged, sung pop song, complete with mixed vocals and instrumentation, in under a minute. While interviewing musicologists for the piece, I kept coming back to an older and, I think, underused framework: Schopenhauer's strange and quite specific metaphysics of music. Unlike every other art, which he thought merely copies the Platonic Ideas (the timeless patterns that particular things imperfectly instantiate), Schopenhauer argued that music alone bypasses the Ideas altogether and stands as a direct copy of the will itself—the same blind, striving force that, on his account, underlies all of nature and drives human desire and suffering. That is supposedly why great music expresses not any individual's particular joy or sorrow but joy and sorrow as such, in the abstract; why he was contemptuous of merely imitative "tone-painting" that depicts external events, like a thunderstorm, rather than channeling the will directly; and why he placed music in a category of its own, outside and above the hierarchy of the arts that otherwise culminates in tragedy. He was also emphatic that the composer does this unconsciously, revealing what he called the innermost nature of the world in a language their own reason does not understand—more sleepwalker than engineer.

Given all that, I'd like your help working out, as rigorously as you can, what Schopenhauer would have made of AI-generated music. Is there a case to be made, on his own terms, that a generative model composing without conceptual understanding is simply doing—mechanically—what he already thought human genius does non-rationally? Or does his whole theory hinge on music channeling a will that is actually striving and suffering, in which case a system with no body, no desire, and no stake in its own existence couldn't be composing "real" music at all, only recombining the formal residue that past acts of human willing happened to leave in its training data? And if it's the latter, would Schopenhauer have needed an entirely new category for this kind of output, or would he simply have filed it under something he already had room for—mere craft, imitation, or perhaps even the "charming," his term for art that panders to appetite rather than enabling will-less contemplation? I'd like your best-supported reconstruction, argued from the actual mechanics of his aesthetic theory rather than from his general reputation as the philosopher of pessimism.
