My brother-in-law, who follows financial news closely but has no formal training in economics, has become convinced that our country's persistent trade deficit is a slow-motion catastrophe. His reasoning runs roughly as follows: each year, more money leaves the country to pay for imports than returns through exports, and this steady outflow must, over time, be quietly draining the nation of real wealth, much as a household would be impoverished by continually sending cash abroad with nothing to show for it. He now insists that the government should impose tariffs and choke off the outward flow of money before, as he puts it, "there's nothing left to drain." I suspect his picture of how money, prices, and trade interact over time is mistaken, but I can't quite locate the error myself. Is his alarm justified, and if not, precisely what has he overlooked?
