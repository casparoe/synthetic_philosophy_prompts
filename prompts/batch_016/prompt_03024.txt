So I'm doing a PhD in developmental psycholinguistics, and I've been sitting in on a philosophy of mind reading group for fun. We just covered Wittgenstein's private language argument, and honestly it feels like this got empirically settled decades ago and nobody told the philosophers? Theres a huge body of work starting with Vygotsky, then Berk and others through the 80s/90s, showing kids' private speech -- talking out loud to themselves while working through a hard task -- is totally functional, follows a predictable developmental curve, and helps with self regulation. If a private language were really incoherent in the way Wittgenstein argues, why would this self-directed speech work so well and develop so systematically instead of just being noise? Feels like the private language argument has basically been falsified and philosophers just kept debating it like it's still an open conceptual puzzle. Am I missing something, or is this just a case of philosophy not updating on relevant empirical work?
