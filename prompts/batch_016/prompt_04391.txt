Last month my employer made my copywriting role redundant after adopting generative AI tools that now produce most of our first drafts, and while I work out what comes next I've been re-reading Keynes's essay "Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren." Writing in 1930, Keynes coined the term "technological unemployment" himself but treated it as merely "a temporary phase of maladjustment" on the road to solving the "economic problem" for good, predicting that by around 2030 living standards would be four to eight times higher and humanity's real difficulty would become psychological: our ingrained "purposiveness," the habit of always preferring jam tomorrow to jam today, which he thought would have to be unlearned before anyone could actually enjoy the abundance and leisure on offer. He pictured that abundance arriving through agriculture, mining, and manufacturing, not through software that can draft prose before it can drive a tractor. Given his own arguments about compound growth, purposiveness, and the psychological strain of unstructured time, how do you think Keynes would judge the specific way automation is unfolding today, and what would he tell someone in my position to actually do with the months ahead?
