Miranda Fricker holds that testimonial injustice occurs when a hearer's identity prejudice unfairly deflates a speaker's credibility. She explicitly considers, and rejects, the further claim that a corresponding excess of credibility enjoyed by a privileged speaker could itself constitute a testimonial injustice. José Medina denies that this restriction is warranted: on his proportional view, credibility judgments are inherently comparative, so an undeserved excess granted to one speaker can amount to an undeserved deficit imposed on another, making credibility excess relevant to testimonial injustice after all—but as a wrong done to a third party rather than to the speaker who receives the excess.

Give an example of a scenario that instantiates Medina's proportional case: a hearer's excess of credibility toward a speaker, A, driven by identity prejudice in A's favor, produces an unfair deficit for a different speaker, B, where B's deficit cannot be explained by any identity prejudice the hearer independently holds against B. Explain why your example turns on genuine comparative proportionality rather than a disguised, ordinary case of prejudice against B, and say what this shows about whether Fricker's hearer-prejudice-against-the-victim framework is too narrow.
