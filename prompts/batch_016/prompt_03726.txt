There's this thing called the causal exclusion argument (Jaegwon Kim's, i think) that's been stuck in my head since — basically: my brain state at time t is already, all on its own, a sufficient cause of my brain state at time t+1 (thats what "causal closure of the physical" means), so if my *mental* state also just supervenes on that first brain state, there's nothing left over for the mental state to actually do, unless you say its literally identical to the brain state (which kind of defeats the whole point of psychology/economics/biology etc being about anything other than physics). And what really got me is apparently this generalizes — like it's not just a problem for minds, it seems to threaten ANY higher-level science being causally real, which some people treat as a reductio against the argument and other people just bite the bullet on.
