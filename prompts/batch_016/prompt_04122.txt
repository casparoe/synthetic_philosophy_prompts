I just started a new gig doing AI safety research, and I keep hearing people toss around "coherence arguments" like it's a closed case: the idea that any sufficiently advanced AI has to end up acting like an expected-utility maximizer with one fixed goal, since anything less would just get money-pumped into oblivion by competitors that don't have that flaw. One of the senior folks on my team told me this basically means real corrigibility (an AI that's genuinely fine with being shut down or having its goals edited) is a lost cause long-term, because that kind of open-endedness requires preferences that are incomplete in exactly the way a money pump is supposed to exploit. Is this actually as airtight as everyone's treating it, or is there more wiggle room here then they're letting on?
