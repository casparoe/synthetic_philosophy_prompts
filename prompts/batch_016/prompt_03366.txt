so my friend group watched Everything Everywhere All At Once and it turned into an argument that never got resolved, plus a whole philosophy youtube rabbit hole afterward. Question: is Alpha-Evelyn, or movie-star Evelyn, literally the SAME person as our Evelyn, or just some lady who happens to resemble her a lot?

Noah says its literally the same person. He found this Kripke argument that we don't identify "Nixon" in a world where he lost the election by matching qualities close enough — we just stipulate we're talking about Nixon, no matching required. So verse-Evelyn the movie star just is Evelyn having made different choices, same as if you'd picked french over spanish freshman year.

Sofia found a name for her side too, David Lewis's counterpart theory, and says thats a cop out. Two people with totally separate bodies, brains, and causal histories in disconnected universes can't be "one person" just because you say so. Really there's a huge family of similar-ish people across universes, none strictly identical, and we just tag whichever resembles you most a "counterpart." Calling that counterpart "literally you" is a comforting fiction the movie needs so the stakes feel personal.

Noah: then Sofia can't explain why we say stuff like "I could've gone to a different college" — on her view that's not about anything that could've happened to Noah, it's about some other guy who resembles him, and Noah could not care less what that guy does. If she's right we're never actually talking about our own possible lives, just strangers who look like us.

Sofia: fine, that's weird, but "we just stipulate it" doesn't answer anything either — it can't say WHY one verse-Evelyn counts as her and another doesn't, especially once the movie shows Evelyns with hot dog fingers where nothing bio or psychological carries over. "It's her because I say so" stops meaning anything eventually, whereas her theory at least gives a mechanism (similarity) for why some counterparts feel more like "us."

They've been at this for a week and keep roping people in to referee, including me, and i honestly can't tell who's winning. Can you go through it move by move and say who's got the better argument at each step (not just an overall winner), and whether there's a real crux they're both missing? End with "Verdict: Noah" or "Verdict: Sofia" on its own line.
