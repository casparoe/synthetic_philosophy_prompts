I'm building a dissertation chapter on Quine's naturalism and want to pressure-test an inference before I commit to it. In "Two Dogmas," the case against the analytic/synthetic distinction rests entirely on holism: no sentence is checked against experience except as part of the whole web of belief, so the line between truths grounded in meaning and truths grounded in fact cannot be principled — it's a matter of centrality, not kind. In "On the Nature of Moral Values," Quine seems to run the same argument on the fact/value line: moral standards, like physical hypotheses, are revised piecemeal under pressure from recalcitrant experience and feeling, and no single moral judgment is checkable except against the rest of one's values. He does hedge by saying science keeps "some title" to a correspondence theory of truth while a coherence theory is "the lot of ethics," but that looks to me like a restatement of the asymmetry rather than a reason for it, now that both domains are conceded to run on the same holistic engine. So once Quine grants ethics the very holism he used to dismantle the first dogma, what in his own framework stops the fact/value line from collapsing the same way the analytic/synthetic one did — or is that correspondence/coherence hedge doing more work than it looks like at first pass?
