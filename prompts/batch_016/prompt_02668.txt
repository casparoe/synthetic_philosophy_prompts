In Principia Ethica's chapter on "The Ideal," Moore argues that loving or admiring something evil/ugly is intrinsically bad, and hating or despising something good/beautiful is bad too — but he thinks the badness scales asymmetrically between the two. For "loving evil" cases, whether the evil thing actually exists barely matters, but correctly recognizing it as evil while still enjoying it makes things worse. For "hating the good" cases it's flipped: whether the good thing actually exists matters a lot, and falsely believing it's actually bad makes your hatred less vile, not more. (Also, Moore stipulates "ugly" pretty broadly, as roughly "whatever it's evil to admire," so cruelty counts as enjoying something "ugly" in his sense, not just aesthetic stuff.)

Using that logic, rank these 6 states of mind from least to most intrinsically evil, and flag any you think are exact ties:

1. Watching a real person suffer, knowing full well it's cruel, and enjoying it anyway.
2. Reading a novel where a character suffers, knowing it's pure fiction, and enjoying that suffering just as much (fully aware it'd be cruel if it were real).
3. Watching a real person suffer but genuinely, innocently mistaking it for a harmless prank, and laughing along.
4. Feeling seething contempt and envy toward a real friend who's genuinely brilliant and admirable, and you fully know it.
5. Feeling that same contempt toward the same friend, except you've convinced yourself (wrongly) that they're actually a talentless fraud.
6. Feeling intense contempt for a "legendary genius" you've heard about, who you'd correctly recognize as truly admirable if they existed - except this person is entirely made up.

Give me the answer as JSON: a "ranking" array from least to most evil (use item numbers 1-6, nesting any exact ties together in their own sub-array), plus a "reasons" object with a one-line justification for each number.
