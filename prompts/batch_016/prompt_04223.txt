I am a retired analytical chemist who has recently taken up philosophy as a serious hobby, and there is a situation I cannot quite work out. I am booked to attend a ten-day silent meditation retreat next month, and from talking to former participants, along with some of the literature on these retreats, I have noticed a strikingly consistent pattern: almost everyone who completes one emerges with total, unshakeable certainty that the experience revealed to them the true nature of reality, no matter how skeptical they were beforehand, and despite being offered no new argument or evidence along the way, only prolonged silence and practice. At present I am quite doubtful of such claims. Yet given that I can already predict, essentially with certainty, what my own opinion will be a month from now, it seems to follow that I ought to simply adopt that opinion in advance, before I have even arrived, which strikes me as getting things backwards, though I cannot say exactly why. Should I begin shifting my view now, or am I entitled to keep my present doubts unless and until I actually go through the experience myself?
