So this happened at my ACX meetup a while back and it's stuck with me. Two people who are usually pretty sharp got into it about quantum suicide while I just sat there eating chips.

Ravi opened with the standard move: if MWI is true and you rig a device that either kills you instantly on a quantum event or does nothing, you should keep playing round after round, since there's always a branch where you survive, and you can never experience being dead -- so subjectively that's as good as immortality. Sana said thats conflating 'a branch exists where you're alive' with 'you should want this': anticipation should be weighted by branch measure, and that measure gets snuffed out every round, which is bad the same way it'd be bad for your kids even in the branches where you're gone. Ravi shot back that this is just ordinary probability discounting, so why should 'I'll never experience being the dead branch' count for literally nothing? Sana then brought up the 'quantum torment' idea (Lewis, by way of some philosopher named Aranyosi I think) -- since there are way more ways to survive maimed than clean, repeated quantum suicide should make you expect to end up increasingly crippled, not fine, so his 'immortality' is closer to a curse. Ravi said that only bites for devices with in-between outcomes, and a genuinely binary, instant kill switch leaves no room for 'maimed' branches, so it doesn't generalize to his setup. Sana's last word was that the real disagreement isn't physics at all -- it's downstream of unresolved anthropic reasoning (self-sampling vs. self-indication assumption) and whether identity even tracks branches the way either of them is assuming.
