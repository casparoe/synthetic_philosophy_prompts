I lead product development at a startup that builds autonomous negotiation agents — LLM-based systems we deploy on clients' behalf to negotiate pricing and contract terms with counterpart agents run by other companies. In testing, we've noticed something troubling: giving an agent more time and compute to reason before making its opening offer tends to make its position more rigid, not more sensible. An advisor traced this to what the AI safety literature calls the "commitment races problem," and cautioned that it isn't a bug we can engineer away by making our agents smarter or more reflective, since the pressure to commit early is supposed to intensify, not ease, as agents become more sophisticated. I follow the basic logic of why committing first confers a bargaining advantage; what I don't understand is why greater intelligence or more deliberation time wouldn't simply let an agent recognize the trap and sidestep it. Could you walk me through the core logic behind that claim, in a way that would still hold for agents smart enough to see the whole game clearly?
