I've been teaching myself Bayesian probability and recently came across van Fraassen's Judy Benjamin problem, and I think the fuss over it is a bit misplaced. Here's the setup: Judy Benjamin, a soldier, is dropped somewhere in a region split into four areas that she initially considers equally likely — Red Headquarters, Red Second Company, Blue Headquarters, and Blue Second Company — so she assigns a probability of 1/4 to each. Command radios her that if she is in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 that she is in the Headquarters area rather than the Second Company area. Since that message is entirely about the internal split within Red territory, it seems obvious to me that it cannot tell her anything about whether she is in Red or Blue territory in the first place, so her credence in each Blue-territory area should simply stay fixed at 1/4, with only the two Red-territory probabilities reshuffled between themselves in a 3:1 ratio. Given that, I don't understand why this is treated as a genuine puzzle about rational belief revision rather than a routine bit of conditionalizing — am I missing something?
