To sound informed at these meetings I started reading up on voting theory, which took me from Condorcet (the 18th-century French mathematician with the paradox where majority preferences can cycle - A beats B, B beats C, C beats A) all the way down into this thing called the Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem, which as far as I can tell basically proves that any voting rule with three or more options is either a flat out dictatorship or can be gamed by someone voting insincerely - there's no clever "fair" system that dodges both.

That kind of wrecked me a little, not gonna lie. Then, about two centuries later, it turns out the exact thing he was chasing - a voting rule that's decisive, non-dictatorial, and immune to strategic manipulation - is provably impossible, not just difficult in practice.
