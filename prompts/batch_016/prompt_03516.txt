Popper wanted a formal notion of verisimilitude, or "closeness to the truth," precise enough to justify saying that Newtonian mechanics is closer to the truth than Aristotelian physics, even though both are strictly false. His qualitative definition, cast in terms of a theory's truth content and falsity content, was shown by Tichý and Miller (1974) to entail that no false theory can ever have more verisimilitude than any other false theory — exactly the kind of judgment the concept was meant to license. Compare Popper's definition with one specific "likeness" (similarity-based) successor account of your choosing, such as those of Tichý, Niiniluoto, or Oddie. (Tichý's 1974 paper is, fittingly, where he both delivers this very refutation and sketches the germ of a replacement.) Explain precisely why the Tichý–Miller argument is fatal to Popper's definition, and identify the specific technical move by which your chosen account is designed to avoid the same fate. Then assess whether it genuinely escapes unscathed, or merely trades Popper's problem for one of comparable severity.
