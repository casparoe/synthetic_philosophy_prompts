A disagreement with my partner last week has been bothering me more than I expected, and I would welcome an outside perspective on it. We have been together for four years, so this is not some early-relationship jitteriness — it is a fairly specific disagreement about what my own behavior means. She caught me on my laptop after dinner, the same evening I had told her, quite sincerely, that I wanted us to just talk instead. Rather than treating it as a one-off lapse, she took it as evidence of something more reliable than anything I say: in her view, what I actually end up doing, evening after evening, simply is what carries the most value for me in that moment, and my stated intentions beforehand are little more than noise layered on top of that fact. She has been reading a book that states the point bluntly: you do not discover what you value by introspecting on it, you discover it by observing what you choose, and when the two disagree, the choosing wins.

What unsettles me is that I cannot dismiss this outright, yet it does not feel like the whole story either. She has pre-empted the obvious escape route, too: it is not as though I am swamped with deadlines or too drained to do anything else, since on several of the nights in question I had nothing pressing and drifted to the laptop anyway. So I cannot simply blame circumstances, and I am left with the uncomfortable thought that perhaps I do not want what I sincerely believe I want. And yet it also seems wrong to collapse wanting into doing so completely: the whole experience of resisting a habit, of doing the very thing you would tell anyone you wish you were not doing, seems to require that wanting and choosing can come apart — otherwise words like temptation or backsliding would not mean anything at all.

I would like help thinking this through properly. Is my partner essentially correct once the argument is stated this carefully, so that my "real" preference is simply whatever my laptop habit reveals it to be? Or is there a principled, non-circular way to say that someone can genuinely want something — as opposed to merely wanting to want it — while reliably failing, night after night, to choose it?
