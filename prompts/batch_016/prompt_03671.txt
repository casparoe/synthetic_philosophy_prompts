Been down a panpsychism rabbit hole this week and can't stop thinking about the "boundary problem." Basic panpsychism: even electrons have some tiny flicker of experience, and the combination problem asks how all those micro-experiences add up into the one unified experience I'm having right now. Weird but ok. The boundary problem is the part that actually broke my brain though -- even if experiences do combine, why do they stop exactly at my skull? Theres no api boundary in physics like there is in software, nothing that cleanly draws a line around "one person," so why don't my neurons and my cat's neurons sum into some shared mega-mind, or cascade all the way up into a universe-sized experiencer? We just assume the boundary is obviously "me," but nothing actually forces that.

Write me a short story where this exact problem drives the plot, not just background "ooh everything's conscious" vibes -- something where the characters have to actually confront why experience is bounded where it is, and what happens when that boundary breaks down.
