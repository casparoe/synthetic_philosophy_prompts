hey, i'm writing a dissertation chapter on 19th-century philosophy of music and i've been stuck on the same couple pages of Schopenhauer for like a week now. its from The World as Will and Representation, book 3, §52 (Haldane/Kemp's old public-domain translation, which iirc is technically titled "Will and Idea" but same book).

so first he sets up a contrast: painting, poetry, sculpture etc. all copy the Platonic Ideas, but music supposedly skips the Ideas entirely and goes straight to the will itself:

"Music is as direct an objectification and copy of the whole will as the world itself, nay, even as the Ideas... Music is thus by no means like the other arts, the copy of the Ideas, but the copy of the will itself, whose objectivity the Ideas are."

then almost in the same breath he adds:

"there must be, not indeed a direct likeness, but yet a parallel, an analogy, between music and the Ideas whose manifestation in multiplicity and incompleteness is the visible world."

...and spends the next three pages spelling out that analogy in wild detail: the bass = unorganized nature ("the mass of the planet"), the inner "complemental parts" of the harmony = plants and then beasts, the melody = "the intellectual life and effort of man," and (my favorite part) impure discords are apparently like "the monstrous abortions produced by beasts of two species, or by man and beast."

so which is it? if music genuinely bypasses the Ideas with zero mediation, why does he need this whole point-for-point correspondence to "the whole gradation of the Ideas" to explain that? is the "parallel, not a direct likeness" hedge doing enough work to save him from just contradicting himself two sentences earlier, or is this a real tension in the theory that people have written about? i keep flip-flopping and could use a sanity check before i commit to a reading in the chapter.
