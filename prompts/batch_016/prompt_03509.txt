A friend and I spent an entire evening arguing over the closing essay in Frank Ramsey's The Foundations of Mathematics, titled "Epilogue." I argued that when Ramsey describes his picture of the world as drawn in perspective rather than to scale — so that the stars shrink to the size of an old threepenny coin while human beings fill the foreground — he is really conceding that the scale model, on which humanity is a negligible speck beside the stars, is the honest picture, and that his human-centered perspective is just a comforting fiction he has chosen to live inside instead. My friend insisted this gets Ramsey exactly backwards, but I couldn't see how else to read a passage that openly grants the stars are enormous. If Ramsey is really engaged in this kind of clear-eyed, pragmatic self-deception, is that a defensible way of finding meaning in an indifferent universe, or is it just denial dressed up in confident prose?
