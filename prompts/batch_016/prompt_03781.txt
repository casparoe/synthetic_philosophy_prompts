Van Fraassen's Judy Benjamin problem is this: Judy is dropped into a region divided into Red and Blue territory, each of which is further divided into a Headquarters area and a Second Company area, and she initially assigns probability 1/4 to each of the four resulting quadrants. A radio message then tells her only that, if she is in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 that she is in the Headquarters area there — a constraint fixing P(HQ | Red) = 3/4 and nothing more. Updating by minimizing relative entropy (Kullback–Leibler divergence) with respect to her prior, subject to this constraint alone, drives her credence in being in Red territory down from 1/2 to approximately 0.467, even though Blue territory's internal Headquarters/Second-Company split remains exactly even. Grove and Halpern, and later Douven and Romeijn, take this drop as evidence that minimizing relative entropy is not an acceptable way to update on conditional information of this sort, since the message seems, intuitively, to bear only on the internal composition of Red territory and to say nothing that should favor Blue over Red; both propose alternative rules on which P(Red) is left fixed at 1/2. Please argue, against this line of criticism, that the drop in P(Red) is exactly what a rational agent should conclude from the message, and that the "irrelevance" intuition motivating the objection does not withstand scrutiny.
