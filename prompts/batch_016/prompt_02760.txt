Ok, I've been falling down a rabbit hole for a piece on the Galileo affair, and I keep bumping into that quote (Galileo credits it to Cardinal Baronius) about the Bible teaching us how to go to heaven, not how the heavens go -- like that's the mic drop that wins the whole scripture argument for him. But when you actually dig into the Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina, a big chunk of his case leans on Augustine's caution in De Genesis ad Litteram: don't lock scripture into a reading you're not fully sure of, cause you might have to eat your words later. Which is... basically Bellarmine's whole point in the letter to Foscarini too -- no real demonstration yet (no stellar parallax, no felt motion, and Tycho's geo-heliocentric model saved the appearances just as well as Copernicus's did), so leave scripture's traditional reading alone for now.

So here's what I want: argue, hard, that judged by Galileo's OWN hermeneutic rules -- not the Inquisition's -- he wasn't actually entitled to push for a scriptural reinterpretation back in 1615, and that Bellarmine was the one being more consistent with Galileo's own logic. No wishy-washy both-sides stuff, I want you to commit to the thesis.
