My girlfriend's doing a philosophy MA and I tag along to her department's reading group sometimes, even though I usually only follow about half of whats going on. Last week they spent almost two hours on something called the "special composition question," which some philosopher named van Inwagen apparently came up with -- basically, given a bunch of objects, under what conditions do they actually add up to a further, bigger object? Like do the particles that make up a chair really compose "a chair" as a real further thing, or is it more accurate to say there's just particles arranged chair-wise and "chair" is just a convenient word we made up? I'd heard of the Ship of Theseus before but had no idea there was this whole separate, deeper question underneath it. People in the room were defending totally different extreme positions -- one guy was fully committed to "universalism" (literally any objects whatsoever, no matter how random or scattered across the universe, compose something), someone else was defending "nihilism" (nothing ever composes anything, period, there's only ever simples), and a third camp wanted some restricted, in-between answer, like composition only happens when the parts are caught up in what they kept calling "a life."

As a backend dev this weirdly hooked me, because I spend my whole day thinking about when a pile of files/services/modules actually counts as "one system" versus just being a bunch of loosely related junk sitting in the same repo. Could you write me an essay that actually digs into the special composition question -- lay out the strongest versions of nihilism, universalism, and the restricted/"life"-based answer (or any other serious contender I'm missing), give their best arguments and the strongest objections against each, and then actually commit to one and defend it, instead of doing the "there are good points on all sides" cop-out? If it turns out your honest answer is some kind of "it depends on what we mean by object" hedge, that's fine, but I still want you to bite a bullet somewhere rather than staying neutral.
