Ok this got personally relevant fast: two months ago my partner and I set up a "moral trade" (her idea, from a podcast). She agreed to go basically vegetarian, which she'd never do on her own since she doesnt think theres anything wrong with eating meat, and in exchange I redirect my yearly work bonus to the Against Malaria Foundation instead of the animal charity I'd normally pick. I care way more about factory farming, she cares way more about poverty relief.

I mentioned it at dinner and two friends, Reza and Kat, got into a real argument I keep replaying:

(1) Reza: paying each other off just lets you both off the hook -- if you really think factory farming is a serious wrong, you should want her convinced, not bribed.

(2) Kat: the bonus isn't wasted -- her diet change serves the cause you rank highest, so redirecting money you value less is optimizing across your own values, not abandoning them.

(3) Reza: still, turning "do I eat animals" into a line item with a donation attached is basically buying indulgences -- funding real good works didn't make that less corrupting, and once her diet's a purchased service, it stops expressing anything.

(4) Kat: it was never expressing anything for her, she was indifferent -- that's the whole premise. Nothing got downgraded, and actual animal suffering drops, which her indifference alone never achieved.

(5) Reza: then flip it to you. You think factory farming's a genuine wrong, and you're tolerating more of it in exchange for money to a cause you rank lower. Doesn't that give even your principle an exchange rate?

(6) Kat: anyone with finite money and more than one value already has an implicit exchange rate, admitted or not -- refusing to ever trade one off just means it happens anyway, untracked.

Neither budged. Can you referee it point by point -- who's right at each step, or partly right -- and say what you think both of them are missing?
