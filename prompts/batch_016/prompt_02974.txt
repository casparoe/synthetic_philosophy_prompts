I have spent the past few months corresponding with the translators preparing the French, German, and Japanese editions of my new novel, and I gave all three of them identical passages and identical notes on exactly what I intended. A short phrase — not a pun, and not meant to be ambiguous — recurs at several pivotal moments and is meant to carry one very specific meaning built up by everything around it in the text, yet each translator has rendered it differently. What troubles me is that each has also made small, compensating adjustments elsewhere in the book, so that every version, taken as a whole, is fully self-consistent and fits everything I have written and explained, even though the three versions cannot all be capturing the same thing. Is it possible that there is genuinely no fact of the matter about which translator has it right, rather than this being something more careful notes from me could eventually settle, and if so, what does that suggest about how well I actually know what I meant?
