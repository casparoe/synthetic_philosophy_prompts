I'm writing a seminar paper on the conditional analysis of dispositions and want to check a claim before committing to it in writing. Martin's (1994) electro-fink — the live wire that won't shock you because contact triggers a device that instantly cuts the current — and Johnston's (1992) masked glass — fragile, but wrapped in packing material so it won't break when struck — both strike me as the same basic counterexample: some intervening factor blocks the expected manifestation even though the disposition itself is still present. Given that, once Lewis's (1997) reformed conditional analysis handles the finkish case by requiring the causal basis to persist through the stimulus, it seems like it should automatically cover Johnston's masking case as well. Yet the secondary literature keeps treating masking as a separate, still-open problem for Lewis's analysis, as though his fix for finks left masking completely untouched. Is there a principled structural difference between finks and masks that explains why Lewis's solution works for one but not the other, or is the persistent distinction mostly just terminological housekeeping?
