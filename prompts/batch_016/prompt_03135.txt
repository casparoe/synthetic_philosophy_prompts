On David Lewis's counterpart theory, no individual exists in more than one possible world, so Humphrey himself exists only in the actual world. When we say that Humphrey could have won the 1968 election, what makes this true, on Lewis's view, is that some distinct counterpart of Humphrey—not Humphrey himself—wins in another world. Does it not follow that, according to Lewis's own theory, ordinary claims about what a person could have done are never really true of that person, but only of someone else who merely resembles them?
