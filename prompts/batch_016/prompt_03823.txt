So a friend who's really into AI safety stuff was explaining this idea to me called "commitment races" (I think it's originally a Kokotajlo post from way back on LessWrong, could be misremembering the name). The gist as I understood it: if you've got two consequentialist agents bargaining with each other and commitments are possible and binding, whoever locks in their commitment first basically gets to dictate the outcome, since the other side has no reason to keep fighting a done deal. Which means both sides are incentivized to commit as early and as hard as possible, ideally before the other guy does and maybe even before they've fully thought through whether the commitment is actually a good idea, and apparently that's a real recipe for disaster if the commitments end up incompatible or just dumb. It kept reminding me of the doomsday device in Dr. Strangelove, where the whole point of a deterrent is undone by a dumb technicality, though maybe that's not quite the same failure mode.

Anyway, can you write me a short joke about commitment races? One or two lines is fine, doesn't need to be a whole bit. The main thing is I want the punchline to actually turn on the real mechanics of it -- the fact that committing early is a bargaining advantage AND that racing to commit before you've thought it through is exactly what makes the whole thing dangerous -- rather than just being a generic joke about stubbornness or indecisive people that happens to have the word "commitment" in it.
