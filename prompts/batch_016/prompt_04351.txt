I recently completed jury service on a sexual-assault trial, and although I obviously can't discuss the specifics, the deliberations left me unsettled: several jurors seemed to treat the complainant's hesitant, non-chronological account, and perhaps her accent, as signs of dishonesty rather than as the ordinary texture of recounting something traumatic. A friend who is writing a dissertation in social epistemology told me this sounds like a textbook case of what Miranda Fricker calls testimonial injustice. That's left me wondering how the concept is meant to translate into practice: if testimonial injustice just consists in a speaker's credibility being unfairly deflated by a hearer's prejudice, then presumably the remedy is for jurors to apply a standing correction, adding a fixed increment of credence back onto testimony from witnesses in groups known to face this kind of discounting, to offset the bias. Is that the right way to operationalize Fricker's proposal, or am I missing something about how the corrective is actually supposed to work?
