Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have joined a small philosophy discussion group that meets monthly. At our last session, two of the more argumentative members fell into an extended exchange that the rest of us simply watched unfold. It began with an offhand remark that a completed proof of the twin prime conjecture would show that "infinitely many twin primes exist."

(1) Peter said that accepting this sentence, and any proof of it, commits you to believing that numbers exist: "exists" does the same logical work whether it quantifies over primes or over galaxies, so you cannot consistently assert the sentence while denying that its subject matter is real.

(2) Miriam replied that this is only compelling if "exists" means the same thing inside and outside a framework of discourse. Within arithmetic, "there are infinitely many primes" is simply a theorem, made true by the rules of the system. Whether numbers "really" exist, asked as a question standing outside arithmetic altogether, is not a further fact waiting to be discovered; it is a practical question about whether to adopt number-talk, to be settled by its usefulness rather than by being true or false.

(3) Peter answered that this move proves too much: belief in electrons or distant galaxies rests on exactly the same kind of grounds -- theoretical simplicity, explanatory power -- yet Miriam surely does not want to say that the existence of electrons is merely a convenient manner of speaking. So either unobserved entities of every kind are in the same boat, or the line she has drawn between an "internal" theorem and an "external" practical choice is not doing any real work.

(4) Miriam said the cases are not parallel: claims about electrons belong to a framework that is in principle answerable to observation and could turn out to be mistaken, whereas "there are infinitely many primes" is fixed by logic alone and would hold no matter how the world turned out. That difference, she said, is exactly why one side can be a factual, revisable claim while the other is not, and it has nothing to do with dodging inconvenient commitments.
