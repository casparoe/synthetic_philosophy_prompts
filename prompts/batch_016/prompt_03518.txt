Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I've been reading my way through the metaphysics of persistence, and I've come to think that special relativity has effectively settled the long-running disagreement between endurantism and perdurantism. The endurantist claim that an object is "wholly present" at each moment of its existence seems to presuppose exactly the kind of absolute, observer-independent present that the relativity of simultaneity does away with, whereas perdurantism, on which an object persists by having distinct temporal parts the way it has distinct spatial parts, fits naturally with a genuinely four-dimensional spacetime. Given this, why do philosophers still write as though the question were open, rather than treating it as one that physics quietly closed decades ago?
