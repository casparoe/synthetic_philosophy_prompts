So i've been re-reading Schlick's "On the Meaning of Life" for my seminar and can't stop thinking about how his framework would collide with stuff that didn't exist in 1927. Quick reminder of his setup: for Schlick, "play" is any activity that carries its own purpose and value within itself, with no goal outside it, while "work" is work specifically because its governed by some purpose that lies outside the activity. Meaning comes from smuggling the spirit of play - which he thinks basically dies off after childhood for most people - back into as much of adult life as possible; he even says real scientific or artistic work counts as play when it's done for its own sake.

Here's my question: what would he say about stuff like infinite-scroll feeds, loot boxes, Duolingo streaks, and gig-work apps with little quest systems bolted on - interfaces explicitly engineered, using variable-reward psychology, to produce the exact phenomenology of spontaneous, purposeless play, even though the actual point of the design is a totally external goal (engagement time, ad revenue, retention) that belongs to the company, not the user? Is that "work" in his technical sense wearing a play costume, no matter how playful it feels from the inside - or does the user's felt purposelessness make it count as real play by his own lights, company's motives be damned? Reason it out from his actual criteria rather than a vibes-based guess, and flag it if you think the case exposes a real gap in his theory.
