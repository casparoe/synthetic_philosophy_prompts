hey, longtime lurker here (mostly LW and the ACX comment section, if that tells you anything about my priors). went down a rabbit hole this week and want to sanity check it before i post it anywhere.

everyone in these circles knows about Godel's ontological proof of God, its the fun party-trick version of his philosophical side. but i only just learned about the *other* thing he apparently believed: that he was basically certain there's an afterlife, and actually laid out an argument for it in letters to his mom in 1961, after she flat-out asked if he thought they'd see each other again after death (found this via an old Aeon essay, in case it rings a bell). here's my summary of the argument, tell me if i'm mangling it:

the world is rationally organized -- not chaotic, but governed by discoverable order, which is exactly what makes science possible. since we're part of the world, our lives should have that same rational structure. but actual human lives obviously never come close to realizing their potential -- people die having barely scratched the surface of what they could've learned or become. a rational, meaningful world wouldn't hand out that much unrealized potential for nothing, the same way you wouldn't pour a foundation for a house and then just walk away and never build on it. so there has to be a next round where the potential gets cashed in. Leibniz is obviously humming in the background -- Godel idolized him, and this is basically the principle of sufficient reason applied to a whole life instead of a single event.

the part i find most interesting, and where i think it plugs into the stuff he's actually famous for: this is his incompleteness theorem wearing a metaphysical trenchcoat. the Gibbs lecture argument says either the mind outstrips any finite formal system, or there are absolutely unsolvable math problems -- either way materialism loses, since a "meaningless heap of atoms" (his actual phrase) can't produce that kind of result. so the mother-letters and the theorem are really the same argument, just aimed at two different audiences.

where i get off the train: even granting the universe is lawlike (fine, science works), that's way weaker than "the universe is meaningful in a way that makes wasted potential impossible." regularity isn't purpose. plenty of lawful processes waste potential constantly -- most fertilized eggs never implant, most stars never end up with planets anyone enjoys -- and nobody thinks the universe owes those a sequel. feels like Godel is smuggling in a value-laden, almost theological sense of "rational" (rational = purposive, non-wasteful) that his science-is-orderly premise doesn't actually license.

three things id love comments on: (1) am i overstating the incompleteness-theorem connection, or getting the mother-letters argument wrong somewhere, (2) is the equivocation-on-"rational" thing the standard objection here, or is there a stronger one i'm missing, (3) is this even worth turning into a real post, or too far in the weeds for anyone who isn't already a Godel completionist. thanks in advance, any typos are original content.
