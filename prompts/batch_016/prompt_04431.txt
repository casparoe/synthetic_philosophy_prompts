I'm drafting a scene in a novel in which two Frege scholars, once close collaborators, fall out over how to read the diary Frege kept in 1924, the year before his death—a diary calling for Jews to be stripped of political rights and voicing his contempt for parliamentary democracy, which so unsettled Michael Dummett when he first came across it in the Nachlass. One character maintains that Frege's achievements in logic and the philosophy of language—the sense/reference distinction, the case against psychologism, the positing of a "third realm" of objective thought—are wholly insulated from the diary, exactly as a theorem is insulated from the vices of the man who proved it. The other insists on real continuities, pointing to Frege's own use of his logical theory of the ancestral to define "Aryan descent" genealogically rather than in terms of blood, and to his worry that vague predicates would frustrate the drafting of antisemitic law. Could you write their exchange, a dozen or so turns, so that neither simply carries the day and each is made to answer the other's strongest point rather than merely restate their own?
