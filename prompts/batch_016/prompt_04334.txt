Something about how even with zero cheating involved, if your metric is only correlated with the real goal (not identical to it), pushing into the extreme tail of that metric will actually make the real goal worse past some point, not just plateau — I think she called it "extremal Goodhart" or the "optimizer's curse" or somethin like that. And separately, she said once people know they're being scored on a specific number, they'll go hunt for the exact gap between that number and the real goal and exploit it, which she called "specification gaming" and said was a whole separate and worse failure mode stacked on top of the first one. Marcus, another engineer on the team who's a total pragmatist, was NOT having it. He said that's way too fatalistic, that Goodhart's law mostly just describes what happens when you're careless about metric design, and that you can dodge most of it with the same statistical hygiene we already use to fight overfitting elsewhere — holdout metrics, multiple complementary measures, actually reasoning causally about why a proxy tracks the goal, rotating targets, keeping a human in the loop. His whole point was that treating it like some unbeatable law of nature is itself a kind of cop-out.
