I'm reporting on 'coherence arguments' in AI safety, which hold that any sufficiently advanced agent will converge on transitive, complete preferences, since an agent that lacks them can be money-pumped. Please argue that this inference is too quick: a diachronically rational agent can retain genuinely cyclical preferences over outcomes taken pairwise, yet, by choosing at each step in light of the whole anticipated sequence rather than the local comparison alone, decline ever to complete the cycle and so avoid the pump entirely. Then take on the obvious rejoinder: that this way of choosing doesn't refute the coherence argument so much as relocate its demand for consistency, from preferences over outcomes to preferences over entire plans.
