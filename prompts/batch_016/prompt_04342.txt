So this happened after my decision theory seminar last week and I still can't settle it in my own head -- figured I'd get an outside read since I'm too close to the material.

We'd just covered money pump arguments, and two of my grad students, Owen and Kat, kept going at each other walking out of the room. The setup on the board: someone prefers car A to car B, B to C, but C to A -- not crazy, since they're weighing mileage, price and reliabilty differently in each pairwise comparison. The standard move is to offer them C for A plus a dollar, then B for C plus a dollar, then A for B plus a dollar -- they end up back with A, three dollars lighter, and would happily go round again.

(1) Owen said this settles it: if a set of preferences lets someone be drained of money for nothing, with their full willing participation at every step, that alone shows the preferences are irrational, full stop.
(2) Kat said that only follows if the agent chooses myopically, trade by trade. An agent who sees the cycle coming and resolves in advance to stop after two trades never gets pumped at all -- so the pump indicts a choice disposition, not cyclic preferences as such.
(3) Owen replied that "resolving to stop" just means refusing, at the last step, a trade the agent still strictly prefers taking in that moment -- so the resolute agent is being just as irrational as the pumped one, only the irrationality's been moved somewhere else.
(4) Kat said no: sticking to a sensible plan against momentary temptation is exactly what we praise in dieters and promise-keepers, and judging plans is its own legitimate rational exercise -- it doesn't just reduce to judging each momentary option on its own.
(5) Owen said fine, then run the pump one level up: offer to sell them a different plan before they even start (since by their own cyclic ranking they'll sometimes prefer the plan ending on C to the plan ending on A), and now they need to refuse that trade too. So the irrationality hasn't been explained away, just pushed back a level.

Both of them want to be told they won. Can you actually adjudicate this move by move rather than just picking an overall winner?
