So I've been rereading Scott Garrabrant's "Geometric Rationality" sequence from a couple years back (I've been lurking on LW since like 2013 and this is genuinely one of the few sequences that made me sit up and reconsider stuff I thought was settled). The pitch is that a bunch of seemingly unrelated things -- Kelly betting, Nash bargaining, Thompson sampling, even Bayesian updating -- are secretly the same move: maximizing a geometric expectation instead of the usual arithmetic one. The bold part of the claim is that this means giving up actual VNM rationality, specifically the independence axiom, because a geometrically rational agent can strictly prefer a randomized mixture of two options over either option on its own. His main worked example is a married couple (Alice wants Atlanta, Bob wants Boston, then a job offer that would send them to California instead shows up with 80% odds) where "fair" bargaining behavior between the two of them ends up violating independence.

Here's what's been bugging me though: Kelly betting is supposed to be the other flagship example of geometric rationality throughout the sequence, but when someone in the comments asks how Kelly betting actually fits his formal definition, Garrabrant basically concedes that Kelly betting is just ordinary expected-utility maximization with a log-wealth utility function, so it doesnt violate VNM at all -- the "reject independence" argument, he admits, is really only carried by the bargaining-style cases. That feels like a pretty big crack in the framing? If your two headline examples of "a new kind of rationality" turn out to be (a) completely standard VNM behavior wearing a costume and (b) a bargaining case that maybe can be patched back into VNM too (e.g. some people in the comments argued you can rescue it just by making the merged couple's utility function sensitive to whichever fact is being conditioned on), then its not obvious there's any daylight left between "geometric rationality" and plain old expected utility theory anywhere.

I keep going back and forth on this with a couple of people in my discord and we can't land anywhere. Can you write me an essay that actually takes a stance: first, does Kelly betting lend any real support to the claim that geometric rationality is a genuine rival to (rather than just a disguised special case of) expected utility theory? And second, is the Nash-bargaining-style argument strong enough on its own to justify rejecting independence, or does it collapse the same way once you're willing to gerrymander the utility function/outcome space? Go through the strongest considerations on both sides, but actually commit to an answer at the end instead of leaving it at "it depends on your definitions" -- a conditional verdict is fine, but I want a real position I can bring back to the chat.
