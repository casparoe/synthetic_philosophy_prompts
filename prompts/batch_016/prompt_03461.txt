Write a dialogue between a philosopher who defends the multiverse response to the fine-tuning argument and one who thinks that response commits the inverse gambler's fallacy. The critic should press the charge that inferring a multiverse from our universe's apparent fine-tuning is no better than concluding, from watching a stranger roll double sixes, that the dice must have been rolled many times before; the defender should counter that our own existence constitutes a selection effect that blocks the analogy. Have the two of them test each other's examples for disanalogies until their disagreement narrows to one precise point of contention.
