So this actually happened at a game night a couple weeks back and it's been rattling around in my head since -- I've been lurking on the EA forum and rationalist blogs for years and had run into the term "moral trade" before, but never actually seen anyone try to run it in real life until now.

Quick background: two friends of mine, Milo and Nadia, have had this running arrangement for a few months. Milo's a vegetarian who's pretty into animal welfare but has always been kind of dismissive of local volunteering -- he's got a whole spiel about how showing up at a shelter once a month is more about the volunteer feeling good about themselves than actually being the best use of anyone's time. Nadias the opposite: she volunteers at a homeless shelter every week and could not care less about factory farming, eats meat at every meal without a second thought. At some point the two of them struck a deal: Milo shows up and volunteers with her one Saturday a month, and in exchange Nadia goes vegetarian Monday through Friday. Neither had been doing the other's thing before.

Nadia had read Toby Ord's old paper on "moral trade" -- the idea that people with different moral priorities can strike deals where each side thinks the world ends up better by their own lights, same underlying logic as regular trade but for ethics instead of goods -- and brought it up at dinner to describe their arrangement. Milo pushed back and it turned into a whole thing, roughly like this:

(1) Milo said this isn't really "moral trade" in the fancy sense, it's just a favor swap with a philosophy label slapped on top. He doesn't think skipping the shelter is wrong and she doesn't think eating meat is wrong, so neither of them is actually satisfying a moral obligation any better than before -- they're just exchanging things they each happen to want.

(2) Nadia said that's backwards: the only reason there's anything to trade at all is that their moral views differ. If they'd agreed eating meat was fine, she'd have had no reason to offer to cut it out; if they'd agreed shelter time didn't matter, he'd have had no reason to show up. She said the paper covers exactly this -- it doesn't require both people to see the act as obligatory, only that the deal is possible because of a gap in how they weigh things morally, and that this is supposedly its own recognized category ("mixed" trade) rather than some loophole.

(3) Milo said okay, but here's the real problem: Nadia already loved that shelter and used to go sometimes on her own before they ever made the deal, so how does either of them actually know the arrangement is doing anything? If she'd have shown up regardless, he's not "buying" any extra good from her at all, he's just getting a free pass on the vegetarian guilt-tripping for nothing.

(4) Nadia said that's a fair worry about her side specifically, but it doesn't sink the whole deal -- Milo definately wasn't vegetarian on weekdays before this and has kept it up since, so real behavior is changing on at least one end, and "we can't cleanly verify the counterfactual" isn't the same thing as "nothing real is happening," it just means this kind of trade is harder to audit than a receipt from a store.

Milo's comeback to that, which nobody really followed up on, was that even granting all of it, it still bugs him that he's only showing up at the shelter because he's being paid in vegetarian-dinners-for-Nadia currency rather than because he actually cares, and that this feels uncomfortably close to each of them just selling the other indulgences.

I couldn't figure out who actually came out ahead here, or whether "moral trade" is even the right frame for what they're doing versus Milo's "it's just a labeled favor swap" take. Could you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about what at each point (not just declare an overall winner), and flag anything you think both of them are missing?
