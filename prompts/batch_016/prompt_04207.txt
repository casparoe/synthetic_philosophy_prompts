Over a long career in molecular biology, I established gene function largely through knockout experiments: knock out a gene, observe which phenotypes disappear, and infer that the gene is a cause of those phenotypes. Since retiring, I have taken up reading philosophy, and it struck me that this bit of laboratory common sense is essentially what philosophers call the interventionist theory of causation. It seems to me that this theory marks a clean advance over the older counterfactual theory of causation associated with David Lewis, since it replaces his talk of similarity among possible worlds with something empirically respectable: a fact about what would happen if we actually intervened. Am I right that interventionism has superseded counterfactual theories of causation by trading their possible-worlds metaphysics for real experimental practice?
