so this happened at a rationalist meetup a few weeks back and it's been bugging me since. two people i know (long-time lurkers, not new to this stuff) got into it over sleeping beauty -- not the basic "is it 1/2 or 1/3" version, but the souped-up one where on tails they wake her up a hundred times instead of two.

(1) kat opened with the hundred-awakenings case: if you'd wake up either once (heads) or a hundred times (tails), on any given waking your credence in tails should obviously jump to like 99% -- if your intuitions don't move that far, they're broken.

(2) sam called that a shell game: the coin already landed before she went under, and she's certain going in she'll wake up either way, so waking up rules out nothing and cant be evidence about the coin. he said an epistemology that lets you manufacture 99% credence about a real coin just by asking yourself more often should worry you -- like bostrom's presumptuous philosopher, who isnt supposed to deduce facts about the world for free just from noticing he exists.

(3) kat said it's not free: she's conditionalizing on real evidence, namely which waking-moment she's currently in, and once you grant self-locating evidence is genuine -- which you have to, since realizing what day it is updates people's beliefs constantly -- an indifference principle over her indistinguishable moments forces the lopsided number.

(4) sam said mundane cases work because something actually distinguishes your current moment, like a newspaper or a clock. beauty's moments are indistinguishable by design, so there's nothing to conditionalize on -- "be indifferent across moments she literally cant tell apart" smuggles in the very assumption that was supposed to be earned by evidence.

they argued in circles for 40 minutes, both clearly knew the material cold, neither budged. can you go move by move and say who's got the better of it at each step -- not just who wins overall -- and flag what you think both are missing?
