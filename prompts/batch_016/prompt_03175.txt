Ok, weird thing that's been bugging me since a scientist friend told me about it — might turn it into a column if i can figure out what's actually going on. Her paper just got rejected, and the way the journal reached that verdict feels off, though I can't quite say why. The rule was: accept only if both (a) the stats hold up AND (b) the finding is genuinely novel. Reviewer 1 said stats fine, novel -> accept. Reviewer 2 said stats fine, not novel -> reject. Reviewer 3 said stats shaky, but novel -> reject. Straight up/down vote: 2 of 3 reviewers said reject, so the paper got rejected. But my friend noticed that if you tally the two questions seperately, a majority (1&2) thought the stats were solid, and a different majority (1&3) thought it was novel enough — so issue by issue, the paper actually clears both bars. She feels like she got rejected by the bookkeeping, not the actual science. Is she right to feel cheated, or is the straight up/down vote just as legit? Is there a principled reason to prefer one tally over the other?
