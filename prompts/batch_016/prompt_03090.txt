Hi there. I'm a retired marine biologist (35-odd years doing reef fish surveys before I hung up my fins), and since retiring I've fallen into a bit of a philosophy rabbit hole, mostly around the Vienna Circle and logical positivism. An old diving buddy of mine who's taking an adult-ed philosophy course got me started on Otto Neurath, and I have to admit I got kind of fixated on the Isotype/picture-language side of his work rather than the "unity of science" stuff everyone seems to go on about more. I already half-knew the visual style from old ecology textbooks — rows of little repeated fish icons instead of one big resized fish — but I had no idea there was a whole philosophical program behind it: the "sailors rebuilding the ship at sea" business, the collaboration with the artist Gerd Arntz, the 1936 International Picture Language book written in Ogden's Basic English, the idea of it being a kind of international "helping language" for people who might not read well, and, maybe most interesting to me, Neurath's own admission that picture language basically can't handle abstract or hypothetical content, only concrete "this many of this, that many of that."

Anyway, here's my actual problem, and it's not a hypothetical one. I got roped onto a tiny volunteer commitee (mostly other retirees plus a couple of keen teenagers) redoing the exhibit panels at our local aquarium on coral bleaching and fish stock decline. Everyone likes the friendly icon-chart look, and I've become the annoying one at meetings going "wait, is that repeated icons or are we just resizing one fish, because Neurath would not approve of the second." But some of what we actually need to show — projected decline under different warming scenarios, uncertainty bands around a stock estimate, that sort of thing — sounds a lot like the comparative, hypothetical content Neurath more or less flagged as outside what picture language alone could responsibly do.

So could you lay out what you think are the most important implications of Neurath's specific views on picture language for how a commitee like ours should go about designing (or maybe, in some places, deliberately not designing) these kinds of exhibits? I'm not after a Neurath-101 refresher, I think I've more or less got the history down at this point — I'm much more interested in what actually follows from taking his particular commitments seriously, including anywhere you think his whole approach just runs out of road for something like what we're trying to do.
