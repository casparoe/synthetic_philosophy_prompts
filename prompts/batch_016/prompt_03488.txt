I am a PhD student in history and philosophy of science, and a disagreement arose in our reading group last week after we had worked through Neurath's essay "Protocol Statements" alongside his book International Picture Language. I would like an outside adjudication of it.

(1) Sam argued that Neurath's picture-language project sits uneasily alongside his mature epistemology. The point of the boat image is that no statement, protocol statements included, is a neutral, presuppositionless starting point; everything is revisable and holistically entangled with everything else. Yet when Neurath writes about Isotype, he treats pictures as having a more direct, less conventional grip on the facts than words do: he consistently aligns pictures with scientific, fact-tracking representation and aligns words with the kind of loose, metaphysics-prone talk he wanted unified science to purge. The whole pedagogical rationale of the project, moreover, is to let ordinary citizens read social facts off a chart without the distortions that, on his view, rhetoric introduces. That, Sam said, is a foundationalist move in disguise: it treats one representational medium as epistemically privileged, which is exactly what the boat metaphor was meant to rule out.

(2) Noor replied that this conflates two distinct Neurath doctrines. The holism is a thesis about the revisability of truth-claims, verbal or pictorial, and nothing more. The preference for pictures is a thesis about pedagogy and resistance to rhetorical manipulation. As evidence, she noted how conventional and rule-bound Isotype actually is: there is an entire rulebook — down to the principle that a larger quantity must be shown by repeating an icon rather than enlarging it — an institute devoted to fixing the standard symbols, and Neurath's own insistence that Isotype was merely a "helping language" that always needs words alongside it. None of this, she said, is what one would expect from someone who thought pictures gave unmediated access to reality.

(3) Sam responded that this does not explain why Neurath singles out the pictorial as a class, rather than simply holding that some conventions, of whatever kind, end up clearer than others. The consistent pairing of pictures with fact and science, and of words with metaphysics, suggests that he thought resemblance itself was doing epistemic work, and not merely that his team had engineered a good convention.

(4) Noor maintained that this shows at most that Neurath was rhetorically loose in his enthusiasm for images in a handful of passages, not that his considered position is inconsistent: a slogan does not undo a whole rulebook built on conventionality.

Could you work through this exchange move by move, indicating where each of the four points holds up and where it doesn't, and then offer an overall verdict: is Neurath's practice with Isotype actually in tension with his protocol-statement holism, or do Sam and Noor's later points show that the tension is merely apparent? Please also flag anything bearing on this question that neither of them raises.
