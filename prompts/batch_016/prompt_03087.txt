In my abstract algebra course this semester, we used Zorn's Lemma to prove that every vector space has a basis by applying it to the poset of linearly independent subsets of the space, ordered by inclusion. What confuses me is that the hypothesis of Zorn's Lemma—that every chain in the poset has an upper bound in the poset—only seems meaningful if the poset itself is totally ordered, since "chain" and "upper bound" otherwise presuppose that any two elements you pick are comparable. But the poset of linearly independent subsets clearly isn't totally ordered under inclusion: two singletons containing different independent vectors are incomparable. Doesn't this mean the standard basis-existence proof is quietly relying on an assumption that Zorn's Lemma doesn't actually license?
