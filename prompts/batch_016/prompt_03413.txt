Ok, this has been the big argument in my fantasy football league's group chat for like two weeks now and I could use an outside opinion. The league's in its 9th season, and back when it started, the four guys who set it up wrote up this whole document (they call it "the league constitution," only half joking) laying out scoring, roster sizes, playoff format, all of it. They also put in a clause saying none of the "core" rules can ever be changed unless 75% of owners vote yes, and only in the off-season. From the old emails I've seen, their reasoning was basically that they didn't want the league turning into chaos, with someone proposing a rule change every time they had a bad week, or a clique of buddies teaming up mid-season to nerf whoever's in first place.

Fast forward to now, two of those four original guys are long gone, replaced by owners who just bought an empty spot in the league and never voted on any of this. Right now there's a pretty solid 8-out-of-12 majority that wants to switch to a scoring system basicaly everyone agrees is more balanced, but the rule needs 9, and four holdouts (only one of whom is an actual original founder) keep voting no every single off-season, so nothing changes. Half the group chat thinks it's kind of messed up that four guys get permanent veto power over something a decade-old document decided, especially since most of the current owners never agreed to that 75% threshold in the first place, they just joined a league that already had it. The other half says that's the whole point, if you could lower the bar the second you had the numbers, the rule wouldn't actually be protecting anything. I feel like there's a real disagreement buried in here and it's not just fantasy football guys being petty about scoring settings, but I can't quite put my finger on what the actual disagreement is. What's going on here, and does one side genuinely have the stronger case?
