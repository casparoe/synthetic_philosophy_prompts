Whenever I'm offered a choice between a guaranteed sum of money and a coin flip with the same average payoff — say, a certain $300 versus a coin flip for $600 or nothing — I take the guaranteed sum, even though the amounts involved are small enough, relative to my income, that they wouldn't meaningfully affect my life either way. A friend who studies economics tells me this is irrational: at these amounts, an extra dollar is worth just as much to me as the last one, so there's no financial reason to treat the safe option as better than the gamble, which means my consistent preference for certainty must just be squeamishness rather than anything I could actually defend. I don't feel like I'm making a mistake, but I can't articulate why. Is she right, or is there a genuine, defensible reason for choosing the way I do?
