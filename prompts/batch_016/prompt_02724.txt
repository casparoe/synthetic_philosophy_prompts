Ok this is going to sound super specific but I've been chewing on it for like a week and my friends are sick of hearing about it lol. For my AP Euro end-of-year paper I picked Simone Weil's essay "On the Abolition of All Political Parties" (the Simon Leys translation) as my primary source, and her argument is basically that EVERY political party, not just the obviously scary ones like the Nazi party, should be gotten rid of completely. She says a party is basically defined by three things: its built to whip up "collective passion," its built to pressure its own members into not thinking for themselves, and its structured so that growing bigger becomes the whole point, with no natural limit. From that she concludes every party is "potentially" totalitarian, even if it never actually gets the chance to act on it because the parties around it are checking it.

Here's where it gets confusing though: in French class we're translating bits of Rousseau's Social Contract, and my teacher pointed out that Weil is obviously working in his shadow with the whole "general will" thing, since Rousseau also has this passage about how factions ("partial associations") mess up the general will by turning millions of individual opinions into just a few big competing ones instead. But Rousseau's actual fix seems way more moderate than Weil's — he says the ideal is no factions at all, but if you're stuck with them, the second-best option is to just have tons of small ones that are all roughly equal in size and power, so they end up cancelling each other out kind of like individuals do. Weil doesn't even really engage with that "lots of small equal parties" option, she just skips straight to full abolition, no exceptions.

So I guess my actual question is: does Rousseau's "multiply the factions and keep them equal" fix actually solve the problem Weil is pointing to, or is she diagnosing something that fix wouldn't touch at all — like is the "collective passion machine" thing a totally different kind of problem than the vote-counting/aggregation problem Rousseau seems to care about? Basically, is Weil just a more extreme Rousseau, or is she making a genuinely different argument that happens to reuse his vocabulary? And which of the two do you think holds up better as an actual account of what's wrong with parties — can you actually walk me through the reasoning instead of just asserting an answer?
