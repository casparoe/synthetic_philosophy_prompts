Since retiring after thirty years running a cell-biology lab, I've joined my old university's research-integrity committee, which is currently reviewing a junior scientist accused of quietly discarding a few inconvenient data points before publishing her results. A colleague on the committee mentioned, not unkindly, that this was roughly how data were often cleaned up in my own early papers, long before anyone spoke of 'questionable research practices.' She wasn't suggesting I step aside, but the remark has stuck with me. I still think what the young scientist did was wrong and deserves censure, but I'm no longer sure I have any right to be the one saying so. Does my own history disqualify me from judging her, or am I free to set it aside?
