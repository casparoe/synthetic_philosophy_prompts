I'm a backend engineer, and for the past few months I've been trying to quantify my own focus at work, partly inspired by Cal Newport's writing on deep work. I built a small menu-bar tool that I tap the moment I notice I've dropped into deep focus, and over time I noticed that what I was tapping for wasn't one uniform experience but two distinguishable ones. Both feel like total absorption in a problem, but one has an effortless, almost trance-like quality, while the other carries a background strain, as though I'm holding a slippery task in place through sheer will. I've never come across a description that matches either state precisely, so I started privately tagging them "Flow-A" and "Flow-B" in the tool, logging whichever one I believe I'm in based purely on how it feels from the inside at that moment.

I described this setup to a friend who studied philosophy, expecting her to find it interesting, and she raised an objection that has stuck with me. She said that even I have no way to distinguish between correctly reapplying the same tag over months of use and simply drifting in how I use the labels without ever noticing, since I have nothing to check my impression against except itself; "correct," on her view, can't mean anything more here than "whatever strikes me as right at the time." I pushed back that the two logs behave quite differently in aggregate: Flow-A sessions cluster on days when I've slept more than seven hours according to my wearable, and code from those sessions tends to survive review with fewer revision requests, while Flow-B sessions cluster on high-caffeine days with looming deadlines. Surely, I argued, that pattern shows I'm tracking something real and applying my own labels with genuine consistency. She wasn't fully persuaded that this settled the matter, and I couldn't quite articulate why it should or shouldn't. I don't have any formal philosophical training, so I'd like help thinking through whether she's pointing at a genuine problem with what I'm doing, and whether the patterns I've found actually answer her worry or merely appear to.
