As a retired experimental physicist now working through the philosophy of science literature, one puzzle keeps recurring. Laudan's pessimistic meta-induction and the no-miracles argument for scientific realism appear to pull in opposite directions, and John Worrall's structural realism is offered as a way of having both. His central case study is drawn from my own field: Fresnel's elastic-solid ether theory of light was discarded wholesale, yet the equations he derived for reflection and refraction survive, reinterpreted, inside Maxwell's electromagnetic theory. Worrall concludes that realist commitment should attach to mathematical structure alone, not to the nature of the underlying entities -- but this move runs directly into Newman's old objection to Russell, namely that a purely structural claim, once cashed out as a Ramsey sentence, is satisfied by any domain of the right cardinality and is therefore nearly empty of content.

Write an essay assessing whether structural realism can survive Newman's objection while still doing the job it was designed for: rescuing scientific realism from the pessimistic meta-induction. You may consider whether shifting from an epistemic to an ontic structural realism helps. Weigh the relevant considerations, but close with a definite verdict rather than leaving the matter open.
