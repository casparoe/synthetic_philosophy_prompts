So I went down a rabbit hole a couple weekends ago that started at Godel's incompleteness theorems and ended with me reading old MIRI papers on self-modifying AI and this thing called the Lobian obstacle. My rough (probably slightly off) understanding: if a formal system could prove the blanket rule "whenever I can prove X, X is actually true," then Lob's theorem says it could prove X outright, for literally any X, which basically wrecks the system. The upshot ppl draw is that a self-improving AI can't certify a successor by checking "I can prove that anything my successor proves is true" the way you'd naively want, becuase that exact move is what blows up. I tried explaining this to a coworker over lunch using a story about two accountants signing off on each other's books and it did not land at all.
