I'm about to collaborate on a one-off paid project with a stranger abroad whom I have no way to sue if things go wrong — the kind of deal that normally collapses, since whoever finishes first has every incentive to vanish rather than deliver, and both sides know it. Instead, we've agreed to route the exchange through code that neither of us, nor any company, controls, though we can both inspect it beforehand. It releases each side's payment only once it verifies that the other has delivered, and no authority stands behind it to enforce anything further. A friend says this is just a fancier promise, since anyone determined to cheat would simply avoid the deal. Is she right that nothing has really changed, or does being able to verify each other's commitments in advance actually change what's rational for us to do?
