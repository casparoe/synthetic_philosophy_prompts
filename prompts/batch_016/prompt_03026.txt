I sing second tenor in a community choir, and last month's season finale was Beethoven's Ninth — my first time performing the "Ode to Joy" movement, and by all accounts a genuine triumph for the group. At the reception afterward, our conductor mentioned, almost as a throwaway line, that Tolstoy had used that very symphony as one of his stock examples of bad art. I assumed she was exaggerating for effect, but when I looked into it that night I found she wasn't: in "What Is Art?" (1897), Tolstoy really does single out the Ninth as a paradigm case of a work that fails on his own terms, because it cannot be understood by, or genuinely unite, ordinary listeners.

That sent me down a longer rabbit hole than I expected. It turns out Tolstoy's standard in that essay was so exacting that he went on to disown nearly the whole of his own fiction — including "War and Peace" — as failing to meet the very bar he had just set. Our choir runs an informal book club alongside rehearsals, and we've assigned ourselves "What Is Art?" for next month's meeting, where everyone is expected to defend a verdict on a shortlist of specific works rather than just react to the essay in general terms. I'd rather not show up having only skimmed summaries that recycle the same two anecdotes (Beethoven, and Tolstoy trashing his own novels) without actually running his stated criteria — the "infection" of an audience, which depends on the individuality, clarity, and sincerity of the work, and then the separate question of whether the feeling transmitted is one of religious or universal brotherhood, or something narrower — against less commonly discussed cases, including a couple of his own later, more didactic short stories that I understand he judged quite differently from his novels.

So here is what I'd like help with: ten works, to be sorted using Tolstoy's own categories from the essay rather than generic "elite vs. accessible" intuitions.
