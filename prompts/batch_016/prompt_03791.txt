In my seminar on Rousseau and the Genevan Enlightenment, we've just finished the Lettre à d'Alembert sur les spectacles (the Letter to d'Alembert on the Theatre). What interests me is less the general claim that theater corrupts morals than the specific mechanism Rousseau proposes: tragedy lets spectators feel indignation and pity at a fiction, discharging their moral feeling and flattering their vanity precisely because no real sacrifice or action follows. His preferred alternative wasn't silence but the open-air Genevan festival, where the line between actor and audience dissolves and citizens perform for one another as equals.

Reasoning strictly from these premises, how do you think Rousseau would judge contemporary social media influencer culture? It's an awkward case for his framework: platforms like TikTok collapse the actor/spectator divide he prized, since everyone is, at least formally, both performer and viewer, yet the content is individualized self-display rather than collective solidarity, seemingly engineered to gratify amour-propre. Would he see this as a corrupted echo of his festivals, an intensified version of the vice he condemned in the theater, or something his framework simply doesn't capture?
