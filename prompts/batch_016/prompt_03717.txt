I'm a PhD student in philosophy finishing a dissertation chapter on whether rational preference must be complete, and I've spent the last few weeks working through Johan Gustafsson's "forcing" money-pump argument for the completeness axiom. The setup, briefly: an agent has a genuine preferential gap between two prospects A and B — neither is at least as preferred as the other, and, unlike mere indifference, the gap survives mild sweetening or souring of either option. Gustafsson constructs a multi-step decision problem in which, at each choice node, the agent's preferential gap leaves it liable to accept a trade that, carried all the way through by backward induction, results in a prospect that the agent can foresee, in advance, is dominated by the prospect it would have secured by simply refusing every trade from the outset. What turns this into a "forcing" money pump — one that supposedly cannot be escaped just by choosing carefully — is a further premise Gustafsson calls Decision-Tree Separability: what an agent is rationally permitted to do at a given node cannot depend on parts of the decision tree that are no longer reachable from that node. It is precisely this premise that rules out the agent's escaping exploitation by committing, at the very first node, to a resolute policy of declining every subsequent trade.

I raised this in a work-in-progress seminar, and my advisor was unpersuaded that resoluteness is a genuine escape route: she argued that letting an earlier plan govern a later choice just is choosing against one's operative preferences (or lack of them) at that later node, and that this counts as irrational no matter how it is dressed up in the language of policies or dispositions. I suspect this reply moves too fast, effectively presupposing the very separability premise at issue, but I would like to see the strongest version of the opposing case worked out properly rather than just gesturing at the old Ulysses-and-the-mast intuition.

Please argue for the position that an agent with a genuine preferential gap between two prospects can rationally adopt a plan-dependent (resolute) choice disposition in order to avoid Gustafsson-style forcing money pumps, and that doing so is compatible with, rather than a covert violation of, the demands of rationality. I would like the argument to engage directly with why Decision-Tree Separability specifically should not be granted the status of a rationality requirement, rather than resting on the weaker, more general observation that money pumps can sometimes be dodged given enough foresight.
