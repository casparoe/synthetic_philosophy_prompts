For context in case its not as familiar as Sleeping Beauty: in the absent-minded driver problem (Piccione & Rubinstein, with a whole response paper from Aumann, Hart & Perry basically arguing there's no real paradox), a driver has to take the second of two exits off a highway to get home. Exiting at the first intersection pays 0, exiting at the second pays 4, and sailing past both pays 1. The twist is he can't tell the two intersections apart and can't remember if he's already passed one, so whatever probability of exiting he picks, he's stuck using it at both. Apparently there's a genuine dispute over whether the exit-probability that's best to commit to ahead of time is the same one that looks best once you're actually sitting at an intersection updating on the fact that you haven't exited yet.
