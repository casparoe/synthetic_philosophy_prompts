Our high school math team runs a weekly exercise in which each of us puts a probability on an open conjecture — for instance, whether the Collatz conjecture is true, or whether a specific large number none of us has checked yet is prime — and afterward we compare whose confidence looked best once the matter is actually settled. One teammate insists the whole exercise is confused: a mathematical claim, he argues, is already either necessarily true or necessarily false, so calling it "70% likely" makes no more sense than asking how likely it is that seven is an even number. That doesn't sit right with me, though, since we clearly do grow more or less confident as we check more cases, find partial arguments, or learn what mathematicians we trust believe, and adjusting our confidence accordingly seems perfectly reasonable. Is my teammate right that the whole idea is some kind of mistake, or is there a coherent way to hold — and update — a "probability" for something whose truth was already fixed before any of us ever guessed at it?
