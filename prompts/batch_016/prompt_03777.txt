I'm a retired experimental physicist (laser spectroscopy, mostly — nothing to do with gravity, which is a bit funny given what follows), and I've been teaching myself philosophy since I retired a couple years back, partly because my grandaughter is finishing up a philosophy PhD and keeps sending me papers I only half understand. Somehow I've fallen down a rabbit hole on the substantivalism vs relationism debate, starting from Newton's bucket. I've got the basic setup cold: spin the bucket and the water climbs the sides even though nothing nearby is moving relative to it, so Newton says the rotation has to be relative to absolute space rather than to other bodies, while Leibniz says space is nothing but the relations among things and there's no "absolute space" left over for it to be relative to. Every popular explanation I find stops more or less right there, like that's the whole argument, and having spent 40 years in physics I have a hard time believing an argument that's been running for 300 years is still that clean.

So here's what I'd like: a dialogue between a substantivalist and a relationist, standing right next to the spinning bucket, each one trying to talk the other out of their view. Skip the 101 version I just gave you and have them get into where the live disagreement actually is now — I'd like the relationist to press Mach's principle and the question of whether frame-dragging counts as vindicating it, and I'd like the substantivalist to have to answer for the hole argument, since from what I can tell that one is supposed to be a headache for THEIR side these days rather than the relationist's, which threw me a bit given how these debates usually get set up. Most important: make both of them equally sharp, neither one a strawman, and have them actually responding to what the other just said as it goes, rather than each just taking a turn to state their case and moving on.
