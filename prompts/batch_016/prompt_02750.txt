I recently agreed to co-chair the conduct committee for my club ultimate frisbee team, mostly because nobody else wanted the job, and I'm already facing a decision that's bothering me more than I expected. Our team's code of conduct handles violations in two very different ways. Minor ones — showing up late to a tournament, forgetting to bring jerseys — are punished with a small fine into the team's social fund, and nobody cares who actually pays it; teammates cover each other's fines all the time without a second thought. Serious violations work differently: our teammate Priya recently got into a shoving match with an opponent, which is a serious matter in a sport that prides itself on self-officiating and what players call "Spirit of the Game," and both the league and our own committee gave her a two-tournament suspension, barring her from playing.

Here's the wrinkle. Priya's closest friend on the team, Dana, has formally asked the committee whether she can serve the second suspension in Priya's place — sitting out our biggest regional qualifier herself so Priya, who happens to be our best defender, can play in the one tournament that actually determines whether we make Nationals. Dana wasn't involved in the incident at all; she just doesn't want the team to lose Priya for the game that matters most, and insists she genuinely doesn't mind sitting out instead. A couple of committee members argue this is no different from letting someone else cover a fine, so we should just approve it. I have a strong intuition that a suspension isn't the same kind of thing as a fine, and that this swap doesn't actually accomplish what the suspension was for, but every time I try to explain that to the committee I end up sounding like I'm just holding a grudge against Dana rather than making a principled point. Before we vote next week, I'd like a clear-headed outside opinion: is there an actual, defensible reason to treat the two cases differently, or am I overthinking what is really just a generous, harmless act of team loyalty?
