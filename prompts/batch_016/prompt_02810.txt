Been retired a few years now and I've been reading alot of philosophy in my spare time, which is probably why this old memory keeps nagging at me. Back when I was doing field geology, our truck broke down way out past the last town in the Atacama, no radio, real trouble. A trucker who happened by agreed to drive the three of us back to civilization, but only after we promised him a few hundred bucks, payable once we hit a bank - obviously none of us had cash on us out there. He drove us in.

Once we were safe, my postdoc at the time argued we should just slip the guy a small tip instead of what we'd actually promised: the trip was already done, he had no way of making us pay more, and we'd never cross paths with him again anyway, so what would paying up in full actually get us? I overruled him and we paid every cent we owed, and I still think that was the right call, but forty years on I still can't put my finger on exactly why his logic was wrong, if it even was. Was my postdoc actually being the smart one, or is there some flaw in that kind of reasoning that I'm missing?
