Hey, quick one — I'm putting together a short story collection around odd corners of philosophy, and I want to try one on "causation by omission." Theres this puzzle that's been rattling around my head: a gardener is hired to water someone's flowers, forgets, and they die — everyone agrees he caused it. But the neighbor didn't water them either, and neither did the Queen, and neither did literally anyone else on earth, and if any of those people had grabbed a watering can the flowers would've lived just the same — so in strict counterfactual terms they're all equally "responsible." Yet nobody thinks the Queen caused the flowers to die. Philosophers have been arguing for like 20 years over what singles the gardener out (duty? normalcy? something else?) and nobody's really nailed it — some even think omissions aren't genuine causes at all, just something we tack on after the fact for blame purposes.

Can you write me a short story, a page or two, that lives inside that gap — where one person's failure to act gets treated as THE cause of some disaster while a bunch of equally-absent others walk away clean? Don't give me a tidy moral-responsibility lecture, I want it to actually sit inside the philosophical weirdness, so the reader feels how unstable the line between "cause" and "not a cause" really is.
