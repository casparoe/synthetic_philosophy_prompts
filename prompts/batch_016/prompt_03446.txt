Not a coding question, but this happened at work last week and I haven't been able to let it go. I'm on the backend team, not data science, but our service is basically a wrapper around a loan-default risk model, so I get pulled into the fairness reviews. Two of the data scientists who own that model, Owen and Divya, got into it during the review and neither backed down. Trying to reconstruct it fairly:

Owen: the model's fine, its calibrated separately within each group - group A and group B, split along a protected characteristic - if it says 10% risk, about 10% of people at that score actually default, in either group. A score means the same thing no matter which group you're in, and that's the bar that should matter.

Divya: sure, but here's the thing - among people who never would've defaulted, group B members get scored high-risk a lot more often than group A members do. So two equally creditworthy people, one from each group, don't have equal odds of getting approved. Calibration doesn't touch that problem, so "the model's fine" isn't really true.

Owen: that gap isn't a bug though, its forced by the math - once you require calibration and the two groups have different actual default rates, you literally can't also get equal error rates on both sides, not unless the model is a perfect predictor. If we pushed false-positive rates to match, either calibration breaks (group B's "10%" stops meaning 10%) or the imbalance just shifts onto the false-negative side instead. Also legal already shot down group-specific thresholds anyway, since that's using a protected characteristic directly as an input to the decision.

Divya: the "we don't use it as an input" defense is kind of a shell game though. The base-rate gap between the groups exists partly because of stuff like whose credit history got messed up by discrimination further back in the pipeline, and that history is all over the other features we're allowed to use. The model doesn't need the protected field to reconstruct group membership, it just infers it from the correlates. So "we're neutral, we don't use it" isn't really true, it just looks true.

That's where they left it, meeting ran out of time before anyone answered that last point.
