I'm working on a piece about whether AI translation and language models can be said to "understand" meaning at all, and every explainer I've read leans on Quine's old gavagai example — native points at a rabbit, says "gavagai," and the linguist can't tell if that means rabbit, undetached rabbit-parts, or a passing rabbit-stage, since all three fit the evidence equally well. Fine, cute example, everyone loves it. But this weekend I actually sat down with the relevant chapter of Word and Object plus Chomsky's old "Quine's Empirical Assumptions" essay (the one in that Words and Objections volume from the late 60s), and it turns out theres a much gnarlier fight hiding under the cute rabbit story: Quine insists indeterminacy of translation is NOT just the ordinary underdetermination of theory by evidence that any physical theory faces — he wants it to be something categorically worse, where there's no fact of the matter at all about which translation manual is correct, even given literally all possible evidence. Chomsky basically says Quine never earns that extra step, he just helps himself to it. I even dug up a 1973 Dialectica paper by some guy named Føllesdal that's apparently the classic response on this exact point, but it's paywalled everywhere I looked.

I don't want to just write "Chomsky refuted Quine" in the piece if that's actually too quick, so before I commit to that framing: can you build me the strongest possible defense of Quine's side? I.e. make the real case that he's entitled to treat translational indeterminacy as different in kind, not just degree, from ordinary scientific underdetermination — and please actually engage with why he thinks facts about meaning and synonymy don't get to count as "further facts" the way facts about electrons or gravitational waves do, rather than just gesturing at his behaviorism and calling it a day. I've got a fairly philosophy-literate audience for this piece so it can handle real density, I just can't find anyone out there laying out the actual argument instead of a plot summary of the debate.
