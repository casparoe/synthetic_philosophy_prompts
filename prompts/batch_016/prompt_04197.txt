Been down a rabbit hole reading old LessWrong posts on decision theory lately and I can't stop thinking about "updateless decision theory" (Wei Dai's thing from like 2009). The part that messes with my head is the idea of an agent commiting, in advance, to a full policy covering every possible thing it might later learn -- and then actually sticking to that policy even in the specific moment where updating on what it now knows would obviously recommend something else. Classic illustration is the "counterfactual mugging": a perfect predictor flips a coin; tails, it asks you for $100; heads, it would've given you $10,000, but only if it had predicted you'd pay up in the tails-branch. So if you're standing there and it actually comes up tails, there's no causal way that handing over the $100 helps you anymore -- the heads-branch where you get rewarded isn't real anymore, it already didn't happen. And yet the "updateless" agent pays anyway, because it's the type of agent that pays, and it turns out that being that type of agent is what gave it a shot at the $10,000 in the first place. Definitly a genuinely strange kind of rationality -- honoring a commitment that your present, fully-informed self gets zero benefit from and can't be causally coerced into.

Anyway I think there's a good short story hiding in this, something like what Ted Chiang or Greg Egan (Permutation City-era) do with a cold decision-theoretic idea -- make you actually feel it instead of just having it explained to you. Could you write me one? Maybe 1000-1500 words. I don't need "updateless decision theory" mentioned by name or any of the above spelled out explicitly -- I'd rather it all live in the plot: something like a bargain struck with a predictor-type figure (Omega-ish, or a more mundane stand-in -- an oracle, an AI, a person with an uncanny knack for reading people, whatever works) where a character has to decide whether to honor a commitment made by a self that, strictly speaking, no longer has any skin in the game once all the relevant facts are in. Bonus points if the ending doesn't resolve too neatly into "and that's why updatelessness is right/wrong" -- I'd rather just sit with the discomfort than get a tidy resolution.
