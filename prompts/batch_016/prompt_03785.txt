So I spent thirty-some years as a field biologist, most of it out in Kenya recording vervet monkey alarm calls, and now that I'm retired I've been reading my way through philosophy of mind on the side. Naturally the "teleosemantics" stuff grabbed me right away, since its basically the same move I used to make about alarm calls: you figure out what a signal means by asking what it was built by natural selection to do, not just whatever happens to set it off. Made alot of sense to me as a general story about mental content, until I ran into this "Swampman" business, which I gather comes from a philosopher named Davidson, though he was originally making a slightly different point with it. The setup: lightning strikes a guy in a swamp and vaporizes him, and by pure freak coincidence a second bolt reassembles a nearby dead tree into a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of him, who climbs out, seems to recognize the guy's friends, chats with them in English, and goes back to writing his old papers. Since this duplicate's brain has no selection history at all -- it just fell together by chance -- a teleosemanticist seems forced to say he has no beliefs or desires whatsoever, however convincingly he's chatting away. From what I can tell, people who like plain causal or informational theories of content instead (no history needed, just the right hookup between head and world right now) treat this case as close to a knockdown argument against making content depend on history at all.

That's about where my reading runs out, and I'd rather watch two people who actually hold these views go at each other than read another neutral textbook summary. Could you write me a dialogue between a committed teleosemanticist and an equally committed causal/informational-content rival, staged right at the edge of the swamp as Swampman squelches off toward town? I want real back and forth -- the teleosemanticist shouldn't get to just wave away the intuition that a talking, apparently-thinking creature must have SOME mental content, and the rival shouldn't get to just assert that Swampman obviously has beliefs without saying how you fix determinate content at all, on a historyless view, given stuff like the classic problem of frogs snapping at BB pellets the same way they'd snap at real flies. Neither one should get an easy win.
