I got into a long argument with a friend last night about whether "incomplete preferences" really solve the AI shutdown problem, and I would like a more dispassionate take on which of us has the better view.

Briefly: an agent with complete preferences over trajectories (its possible future action-sequences) generally has some incentive to resist being shut down or to manipulate its timing, since shutdown forecloses whichever continuations it values most. I brought up Elliott Thornley's proposed fix, which trains agents to have preferences only between trajectories of equal length and none at all between trajectories of different lengths, so that the agent ends up strictly neutral about when it is shut down. My friend raised the standard objection to any such incompleteness: money-pump arguments seem to show that a preferential gap can be exploited by a sequence of trades into either paying for something it could have had for free, or behaving exactly as if the gap had been closed — defeating the whole point of the proposal. I countered that the agent could avoid this simply by refusing, as a matter of policy, any trade that would leave it worse off by its own prior lights — choosing "sophisticatedly" rather than "naively." He thinks that such a policy is itself just a completed preference in disguise.

Please explain whether the sophisticated-chooser reply genuinely preserves incomplete preferences while blocking the money pump, or whether avoiding exploitation in this way effectively commits the agent, in every decision-relevant respect, to acting as if its preferences were complete after all.
