so I'm a few years into a metaphysics PhD and my mereology reading group has been stuck for like three sessions now on the Special Composition Question chapter of van Inwagen's Material Beings. I get the overall shape of the argument -- he wants to rule out "moderate" answers like Contact or Fastening because they'd make it vague exactly when composition occurs, and therefore vague how many things exist, which he thinks is just incoherent (there can't be a vague number of things in a room). That's supposed to push you toward the two answers that dodge vagueness, nihilism and universalism, but then he doesn't actually pick either one -- he goes with organicism instead, where some simples compose something iff their activity constitutes a life. Every time I reread that move I can't decide if "life" is really the principled, non-arbitrary joint in nature he needs it to be, or if its just smuggling vagueness back in through the side door (viruses, comas, slime molds, whatever the exact moment is that a zygote starts counting as "alive" in the relevant sense).

Could you write me a short dialogue between two people who actually know this literature cold -- a committed universalist (unrestricted composition, happy to bite bullets, the Lewis/Sider crowd) and a defender of van Inwagen's organicism -- hashing this out? Bonus points if you set it somewhere dumb and mundane, like they run into each other at a diner and one of them ordered the trout while the other got a turkey club, so the food on the table becomes the occasion to relitigate the old trout-turkey worry -- the stock objection to universalism that Lewis just bites the bullet on in Parts of Classes. I want actual back-and-forth, not two monologues sitting next to each other: the universalist should really press on why life gets to be exempt from the vagueness problem that sinks every other moderate view, and the organicist needs a real comeback rather than just citing van Inwagen, plus a chance to turn it around and make the universalist own up to what unrestricted composition actually commits them to. Neither one should be a pushover -- I've got a seminar paper on this due soon and honestly watching two people who actually believe this stuff go at each other would help me more than another summary of the positions would.
