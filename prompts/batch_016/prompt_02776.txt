A friend and I spent a long car ride arguing about Goodman's grue paradox. She thinks that Goodman's own proposed fix, entrenchment -- favoring 'green' over 'grue' because 'green' has actually been used successfully in the past -- is hopelessly parochial, and that Solomonoff induction, which ranks hypotheses by their Kolmogorov complexity, puts induction on much firmer footing, since it grounds simplicity in computation rather than in the contingent history of our language. I argued that this doesn't escape Goodman's problem so much as relocate it: Kolmogorov complexity is machine-independent only up to an additive constant, and since that constant can be made arbitrarily large, there is always some equally universal machine relative to which 'all emeralds are grue' comes out simpler than 'all emeralds are green' given identical evidence -- so 'simple' turns out to be just as conventional as 'entrenched.' Neither of us budged. Could you describe the most important implications of this -- particularly of the invariance theorem's dependence on an unbounded, machine-relative constant -- for how much confidence we should place in a machine learning model's preference for 'simpler' hypotheses when it extrapolates beyond its training data?
