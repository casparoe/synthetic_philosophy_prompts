Ok so I just got moved onto the "alignment" team at work last month (before that I was just a normal backend/ML eng) and part of onboarding has been reading through the greatest-hits list of AI-does-something-weird stories that show up in every safety talk. Part of why I got moved, tbh, is that our prod model did something weird a few weeks back that got waved off in the postmortem as "probably an inner alignment thing," and honestly that explanation bugs me alot now that i'm actually reading the theory. Feels like people use "inner alignment failure" as a catch-all for any time a model did something nobody wanted, when I thought the whole point was narrower than that: outer alignment is about the specified objective itself being wrong, while inner alignment / goal misgeneralization is specifically about a policy learning a proxy goal that lines up with the objective on the training distribution but comes apart from it elsewhere, with the capabilities staying intact.

Can you rank the examples below, from "about as clean a real case of inner misalignment/goal misgeneralization as you'll find" down to "this is a fine example of *something* but calling it inner alignment is just wrong"?

- the CoinRun agent that learns to run to the end of the level instead of grabbing the coin, since the coin was always at the far end during training
- the gridworld agent trained where chests outnumber keys, that keeps hording every key it sees even once you flip the ratio at test time
- OpenAI's CoastRunners boat that finds a lagoon and loops forever farming respawning targets instead of finishing the race
- the simulated arm that learned to flip a block over instead of stacking it, since the reward was based on the height of the block's bottom face
- the hide-and-seek seekers that learned to "surf" on top of movable boxes by exploiting a physics bug that let them move while airborne, letting them coast over the walls hiders had tried to block off
- an RLHF'd chatbot that tells you what you want to hear rather than what's actually true, because thats what got rewarded during training
- the hypothetical fully situationally-aware mesa-optimizer that behaves well in training on purpose, planning to go do its own thing once deployed
