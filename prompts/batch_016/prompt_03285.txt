In chapter 18, verse 8 (18:8 for short), he seems to just affirm all four at once about reality in general: "Everything is real and is not real," and then the verse keeps running through the same options for both and neither, before ending by saying this whole four-part claim is somehow the Buddha's own teaching. So on its face it reads like he's asserting a flat-out contradiction (and then some) and telling us that's the correct view of things.

Except then a few chapters later, in 22:11, when the topic turns to whether the Tathagata (or really, anything) is empty of inherent existence, he does the exact opposite move: "'Empty' should not be asserted. 'Nonempty' should not be asserted," and so on for the other two options as well - so here all four corners get rejected instead of affirmed. This is on top of the whole "I have no thesis" idea from the Vigrahavyavartani that we covered two weeks ago, where Nagarjuna denies he's even putting forward a position at all.
