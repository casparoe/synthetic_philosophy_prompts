So my sister just got diagnosed with bipolar I, and she's working with her psychiatrist on what's apparently called a "Ulysses contract" -- she signs something now saying that if she goes manic again, the hospital should override her and treat her even though manic-her will insist nothing's wrong. It's a lot, but it also sent me down a rabbit hole about whether that's even rational -- binding your future self against their own future judgment like that. Turns out theres a whole philosophy thing about exactly this structure: van Fraassen's Reflection Principle, which says your current credence in something should basically match whatever you expect your future credence to be, otherwise a bookie could run a set of bets against you that you're guaranteed to lose. Except that flatly contradicts what we're doing with my sister, where present-her is deliberately overriding future-manic-her. Van Fraassen apparently even wrote a whole follow-up paper built around this exact Ulysses-tied-to-the-mast setup, and people also cite the Sleeping Beauty problem as a counterexample to Reflection. Can you write me a dialogue -- two sharp characters, one defending something like Reflection even in cases like this, one arguing it needs serious qualification or should just be dropped -- who actually engage with each other's strongest moves instead of talking past each other? Neither one should be a pushover, and it doesn't need to end in tidy agreement -- I just want to see where the real pressure points are.
