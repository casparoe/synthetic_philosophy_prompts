Ok so I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole on this "illusionism" idea about consciousness for the last couple weeks (started with a Keith Frankish talk on youtube, then some Dennett interviews, then a bunch of people arguing about it in forum comments), and theres one specific move in the argument I keep chewing on and can't decide if its a real problem or if I'm just missing something.

As I understand it, the illusionist isn't saying "nothing happens when you touch a hot stove" -- they're saying your introspection takes some messy functional/computational stuff going on in the brain and represents it as having these special properties: ineffable, intrinsic, immediately and directly known, that sort of thing (Frankish has some term for it, "quasi-phenomenal" properties or something close to that), and it's that representing-it-that-way, the seeming, which is the "illusion." Ok fine so far. But then I keep getting stuck here: doesn't representing something as ineffable and immediately known have to itself seem like something to whoever's doing the representing? Because if it doesn't seem like anything at all, I don't see how it counts as an illusion rather than just some false belief sitting there doing nothing in particular. And if it does seem like something, haven't they just pushed the phenomenal feel up one level, so the "illusion" ends up having exactly the kind of feel it was supposed to be explaining away?

I brought this up with my housemate, who's doing a cog sci PhD, and she said it's basically a known objection (something about the seeming not being able to go "turtles all the way down") but got pretty hand-wavy trying to explain Frankish's actual response to it, so I never came away with a clear picture of whether illusionists have a real answer here or are just kicking the can down the road. Could you write me a dialogue -- an actual back-and-forth, not two monologues stapled together -- between a committed illusionist and someone who thinks phenomenal consciousness is fully real, where they really hash out this exact "seeming regress" point? I want the realist pushing the objection as hard as it legitimately goes, the illusionist giving their genuine best replies rather than a strawman version, and then the realist coming back with something real instead of the conversation just fizzling out with both sides repeating their opening speech. Don't make either one an easy winner -- I want to see both positions at their strongest.
