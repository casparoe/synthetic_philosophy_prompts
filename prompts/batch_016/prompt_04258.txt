In "Mr. Truman's Degree," G.E.M. Anscombe argued that the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki amounted to murder, since, in her words, "choosing to kill the innocent as a means to your ends is always murder." Central to this argument was the doctrine of double effect: what matters morally, on her view, is whether civilian deaths are intended, including as a means to some further end, rather than merely foreseen as a side effect of attacking a legitimate military target. Suppose Anscombe were confronted with contemporary lethal autonomous weapons systems, which can select and engage targets without a human making the lethal decision in real time. Applying her own framework of intention, foresight, and double effect, how do you think she would assess the moral status of such systems — and would removing a human decision-maker from the moment of killing make them easier to justify on her terms, or would it instead undermine the very possibility of the moral agency her argument presupposes?
