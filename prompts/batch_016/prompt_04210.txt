A friend and I spent much of last night arguing about Frankfurt-style cases and whether the "new dispositionalist" response to them actually succeeds. Her position was that the counterfactual intervener in such cases doesn't remove an agent's ability to do otherwise but merely finks or masks it, in the way that packing foam masks a teacup's fragility without eliminating it. I argued that importing the fink/mask apparatus from the metaphysics of dispositions to rescue a conditional analysis of "could have done otherwise" just relocates the original problem: whatever move protects an ability-ascription from Martin's and Johnston's counterexamples seems available just as easily to a thoroughgoing determinist description of the very same case. Neither of us budged. Please describe the most important implications of the finks-and-masks literature on dispositions for the new dispositionalist defense of compatibilism against Frankfurt-style counterexamples to the Principle of Alternate Possibilities.
