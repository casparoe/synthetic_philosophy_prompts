My novels have now been translated into a dozen languages, and I've grown used to translators writing to me with rival renderings of the same sentence, each insisting that theirs is truest to the original. I recently came across Quine's parable of the linguist who, on hearing a native speaker exclaim "Gavagai!" at the sight of a rabbit, can never determine from any amount of evidence whether the word means "rabbit," "undetached rabbit part," or "rabbit stage," and it struck me as confirming something I had always suspected: that if no translation manual can ever be more correct than any rival, then my translators' quarrels over the right word are strictly meaningless, and I ought to stop taking sides and simply let each of them do as they please. Given that this kind of indeterminacy is apparently built into the very nature of translation itself, how should I, as their author, adjudicate these disputes without pretending that one translator's choice is somehow righter than another's?
