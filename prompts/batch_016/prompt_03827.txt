So a few weeks back I fell down this wikipedia hole about a rule from Leibniz that says if two things share literally every single property, they actually have to be the same thing (I guess it's called the identity of indiscernibles). That led me to this old thought experiment by a philosopher named Max Black, where he says just imagine a universe with nothing in it except two identical iron spheres, same size, same everything, sitting a couple miles apart, and the whole point is that this is supposed to break Leibniz's rule, since you'd have two genuinely separate objects sharing every single property there is to share. I brought it up with my friends Nate and Mara at trivia night, since they're both way more into this armchair-philosophy stuff than I am, and it turned into a real argument that I haven't been able to stop chewing on since.

Nate's opening move was that the spheres example just settles it, Leibniz was wrong, full stop: two things can be identical in every property and still be two things. Mara said not so fast, they're not actually identical in every property, because sphere A has the property "is two miles from sphere B" and sphere B has "is two miles from sphere A," and those are different properties, so the rule survives. Nate called that a cheat, since you're only allowed to say those are different properties if you've already decided A and B are two different things, which is the whole question at stake, and if you take the labels away, both spheres just have the exact same property, "is two miles from the other one." Mara's comeback was that no matter what you call it, being-two-miles-apart is a relation nothing can have with just itself, so the fact that it's satisfied here at all, instead of each sphere just sitting there related only to itself, proves there's two of something, not one thing wearing a costume. Nate said that's the same trick again: "satisfied between them" already assumes a "them." Mara's last move was that the only way it's really "one thing described twice" is if space itself loops back around on itself, like a wraparound map, and if we just stipulate this is ordinary flat, infinite space with no looping, that escape hatch is closed and your stuck admitting there's two totally indiscernible things sitting right there.

Neither of them gave any ground and we had to get back to the trivia round before it went any further, but it's been bugging me ever since.
