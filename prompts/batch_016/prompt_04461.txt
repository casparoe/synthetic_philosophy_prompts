been slowly working through Garfield's translation of Nagarjuna's Mulamadhyamakakarika for a reading group and I'm stuck on chapter 22, the "Examination of the Tathagata." He spends the whole chapter arguing that the tathagata (basically an awakened being, like the Buddha) can't be shown to be identical to, different from, dependent on, or containing/contained by the five aggregates, so that grasping and the grasper both turn out to be empty, exactly like everything else in the preceding 21 chapters. Then right when you'd expect him to just wrap up and conclude that the tathagata is empty, he instead turns his own tetralemma on that very claim and says: "Empty" should not be asserted. "Nonempty" should not be asserted. Neither both nor neither should be asserted. He adds that these are only used as labels, then asks how any tetralemma - permanent/impermanent, finite/infinite - could even apply to something at peace like this.

isnt that basically him retracting the thesis the whole text has been building toward? if he won't even assert emptiness of the tathagata, in what sense has anything actually been established here, as opposed to gestured at and then taken back? is this a real contradiction nagarjuna is just biting the bullet on, or is there some move available (two truths, conventional vs ultimate assertion, "emptiness of emptiness," whatever) that keeps this passage from being the text quietly admitting it self-destructs?
