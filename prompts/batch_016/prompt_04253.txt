Spent about 30 years running a population genetics lab before retiring, so I've used the Price equation and Hamilton's rule more times than I can count -- but always just as calculating tools, never really asked what they're supposed to tell us metaphysically. Now that I've got time on my hands I've been working through some real philosophy of biology (Okasha's book on levels of selection, at the moment), and one argument keeps bugging me.

The gene's eye view crowd (Dawkins, Williams) say individuals and groups are just "vehicles" and the gene is the real unit selection acts on -- any group selection story can supposedly be rewritten in genic terms after the fact. Critics call that move "bookkeeping," just re-partitioning the same covariance term in the Price equation rather than making a causal claim, and Sober and Wilson return the favor, accusing the genic camp of what they call the "averaging fallacy." So I honestly can't tell anymore if this fight is a substantive disagreement about what's causally doing the work in evolution, or just two bookkeeping conventions arguing over who's column headers are more fundamental.

Can you compare genic selectionism to multilevel/group selection theory properly and help me sort this out -- and if you think there's a genuine, non-terminological disagreement buried in there, give me one concrete case where the two would actually generate different predictions, not just different-sounding descriptions of the same outcome?
