so i've been slowly reading through pascal's pensees on my own time (got into it after hearing about the "wager" thing and went down a rabbit hole) and i hit a couple of fragments in the justice section that are messing with my head. this is the trotter translation, fragments 297-298 (whatever numbering that even is, different editions seem to number these differently):

"Veri juris.—We have it no more; if we had it, we should take conformity to the customs of a country as the rule of justice. It is here that, not finding justice, we have found force, etc.

Justice, might.—It is right that what is just should be obeyed; it is necessary that what is strongest should be obeyed. Justice without might is helpless; might without justice is tyrannical. Justice without might is gainsaid, because there are always offenders; might without justice is condemned. We must then combine justice and might and, for this end, make what is just strong, or what is strong just.

Justice is subject to dispute; might is easily recognised and is not disputed. So we cannot give might to justice, because might has gainsaid justice and has declared that it is she herself who is just. And thus, being unable to make what is just strong, we have made what is strong just."

two things i can't untangle. first, in that opening bit, what is "it" even referring to, and why would HAVING it make people fall back on a country's customs as the rule of justice? feels backwards to me, if you actually had real justice why would you need custom as a stand in at all. second, is pascal actually saying justice is basically fake and "just" just means "whatever the strong say it is" (a cynical might-makes-right take), or is he making a narrower claim that WE cant access real justice even though it might exist, which seems like a pretty different thing to be arguing? and given hes this hardcore religious guy, does that change how "we have it no more" should be read, like is this a theology point dressed up as a political one? i don't want a "pascal thought might makes right" one liner, i want someone to actually walk through the logic of how these two fragments connect.
