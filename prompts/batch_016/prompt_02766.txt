I've spent the past few months on the interpretability beat for a science magazine, working through the sparse-autoencoder "monosemantic features" literature and, further back, the Othello-GPT lineage of papers: the original finding by Kenneth Li and colleagues that a transformer trained on nothing but legal move sequences appears to build an internal model of the board, and Neel Nanda's widely cited follow-up showing that this board state is even linearly decodable, provided you probe for "mine/theirs" rather than "black/white." Researchers increasingly describe results like these as genuine discoveries of what a network represents, in roughly the sense in which a neuroscientist might say a population of neurons represents spatial location. That rhetoric sent me back to William Ramsey's Representation Reconsidered, where he argues that most representation-talk in cognitive science — and, he suspects, in connectionist and neuroscientific models especially — really trades on a thin "receptor" notion (a state that is simply a reliable detector of some condition), and that only "structural representations," which maintain an exploitable structural correspondence to their target and are used by the system as a surrogate for reasoning about that target (his own preferred illustrations run along the lines of a cognitive map), deserve the name in any full-blooded sense.

For a piece I'm drafting, I'd like a rigorously argued case for the deflationary side of this dispute. Specifically, please argue that even the linear, causally manipulable "mine/theirs" board probes recovered from Othello-GPT-style models fail to establish that these networks contain genuine Ramsey-style structural representations, so that the interpretability community's realist talk of "discovering" representations outruns the evidence it can currently claim. I'd like the argument to take the strongest form of the opposing view seriously rather than dodge it: the obvious reply is that exploitable, causally efficacious linear structure just is what structural correspondence amounts to, so please explain specifically why passing a linear-probe-plus-intervention test still falls short of Ramsey's bar — or, if you don't think it does fall short, tell me so and say what would need to be added before it did.
