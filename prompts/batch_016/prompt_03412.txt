I'm a science and ideas journalist currently reporting a longform piece on the vocabulary we've inherited for talking about large language models, and how much of it may be quietly begging the question of what these systems actually are. Over the past couple of months I've interviewed cognitive scientists, philosophers of mind, and AI safety researchers for the piece, and what has struck me is how few of them treat "what kind of thing is a chatbot?" as an open question: each camp has a preferred picture and treats the rest as either naive anthropomorphism or willful mystification. One researcher showed me, apparently in complete seriousness, a cartoon of a shoggoth wearing a smiley-face mask; a philosopher I spoke to that same week waved the entire discourse away as warmed-over homuncularism.
