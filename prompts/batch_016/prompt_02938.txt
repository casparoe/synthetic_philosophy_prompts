In my seminar on Hume's political philosophy, we've just covered his essay "Of the Balance of Trade" and the price-specie-flow mechanism: a nation running a persistent trade surplus accumulates specie, which drives up domestic prices until its exports become uncompetitive and the surplus corrects itself, with the mirror process unwinding any deficit elsewhere. I've been trying to work out how Hume might read the situation in the Eurozone, where a shared currency removes the exchange-rate channel entirely, yet Germany has run a large, persistent current-account surplus against southern members such as Greece, Spain, and Italy for well over a decade, with almost all of the adjustment falling on the deficit countries through internal wage and price cuts rather than any comparable correction on Germany's side. Would Hume treat this asymmetry as merely a slower, stickier version of his mechanism eventually working itself out, or would he see it as exposing a hidden assumption in his original argument — for instance, that prices and wages adjust symmetrically in both directions, or that capital flows don't systematically offset trade flows?
