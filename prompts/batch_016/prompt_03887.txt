I've been reading about decision theory and long-termist research into "s-risks" (risks of astronomical future suffering) on forums associated with the rationalist and effective altruism communities, where there is recurring discussion of how an agent might protect itself from acausal threats: cases in which a threatening agent, despite having no causal channel of influence, can nonetheless credibly threaten to harm what a target agent cares about, simply by being able to model or simulate that target accurately (in the spirit of Newcomb's problem, or of agents reasoning about one another across otherwise disconnected regions of a large universe or multiverse). Because such threats can in principle be aimed at whatever an agent is known to value most, and because a bare refusal to yield may be neither credible nor even well-defined for some kinds of agents, several distinct countermeasures have been proposed. Perhaps the best-known is the "surrogate goal": an agent deliberately cultivates, and makes legible to others, some attachment to an additional and otherwise unimportant value, in the hope that threats will be redirected onto this decoy rather than onto what the agent truly cares about.
