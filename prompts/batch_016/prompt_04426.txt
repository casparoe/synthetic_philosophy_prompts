My buddy has fallen pretty hard down the rationalist-blogosphere rabbit hole, and a few weeks back at a bonfire he was trying to explain "acausal trade" to me. From what I understood: two minds that can never communicate or physically affect each other in any way -- like us and some alien civilization forever beyond the cosmic horizon, or two different branches of the universe -- can supposably still benefit each other, just by each one figuring out what the other wants and doing it, on the theory that the other is running the same kind of reasoning and will do the same back. He even brought up some researcher (Oesterheld? something like that) who apparently wrote papers arguing that civilizations across the whole "multiverse" could be quietly cooperating with each other this way, without a single signal ever passing between them.

I told him this sounds suspiciously like doing something nice and then calling it a "trade" after the fact so it sounds rational instead of just being nice for no reason. Real trade, I said, involves some way of checking the other side actually delivered, or at least some risk of getting burned if they don't -- thats kind of the whole point of it being trade instead of charity. He said the "checking" gets replaced by the fact that your decision and their decision are logically wired together somehow, so that defecting on your end just is defecting on theirs, but honestly I couldn't tell if that's a real answer or just the original assumption said more fancily. We also couldn't agree on whether this needs each side to basically simulate the other's whole mind, or whether some much cheaper "we both reason similarly enough" is actually enough -- he kept flip flopping on that one himself.

Instead of explaining it to me straight, could you write the whole thing out as a dialogue -- one character who's genuinely convinced acausal trade is something any sufficiently rational agent has to take seriously, and one who's a real skeptic of it, going back and forth for something like 8-10 exchanges. Don't make the skeptic a pushover -- have them really hammer on the "there's no enforcement or verification, so in what sense is this trade and not just wishful cooperation" objection, and make the proponent actually earn their answer instead of just repeating "it's logically correlated" like that settles things. Somewhere in there I'd like them to actually hash out the simulation-fidelity question from above, and what changes about the argument depending on the answer. Neither one should just fold at the end -- if anything, I'd rather they land on the actual crux of where they disagree than have one side clearly win.
