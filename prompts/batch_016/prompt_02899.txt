Last weekend, my friend Owen and I spent the better part of an evening trying to track down an anniversary gift that my partner had hidden somewhere in our city's botanical garden as an elaborate scavenger hunt. Before any clues arrived, all we knew was that the garden is bisected by a canal into an East Wing and a West Wing and, independently, split by a raised boardwalk into a Formal Garden section and a Wild Meadow section. That gives four regions of roughly equal size, and with nothing else to go on, we treated each of them, East/Formal, East/Meadow, West/Formal, and West/Meadow, as equally likely: a flat 25 percent apiece.

The only clue we ever received was a single text from my partner, sent just before she went quiet for the rest of the night: "If it's on the West side, I'd say the odds are something like 3 to 1 that it's tucked into the Wild Meadow rather than the Formal Garden." Nothing was said about the East Wing at all. Owen insisted that the message could bear only on how we divided our confidence within the West side, and that our overall confidence in the East Wing had to stay exactly where it started, at 50 percent, still split evenly between its two sections; letting the East Wing shift, he argued, would mean revising our view of a region the clue never even mentioned, which struck him as a plain violation of relevance. I remained unconvinced. His argument felt suspiciously quick, almost as though it were assuming the very thing in question, and I had a nagging sense that a more careful, principled way of incorporating a conditional clue like this one might not leave the East Wing untouched at all, precisely because the clue told us the West-side split was uneven rather than balanced. We ended up sketching the four regions on a napkin and arguing over that single text message for a solid hour, neither of us able to fully justify our own position or dislodge the other's.

I would like some help settling this properly. Who had the more defensible position, if either, and why does such an apparently innocuous conditional clue turn out to be so genuinely contentious to update on? More generally, I would appreciate it if you could draw out the underlying principle at stake and explain what it implies for how I ought to revise my beliefs whenever I am handed evidence of the form "if A, then the odds on B are such-and-such," rather than as a plain statement of fact.
