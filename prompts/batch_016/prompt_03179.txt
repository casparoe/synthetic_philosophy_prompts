I'm a PhD student in statistics, and a strange inference occurred to me recently that I can't quite shake. A close friend just accepted a position as the 23rd person hired at a new startup, and she is confident the company will eventually grow into a giant employing hundreds of thousands of people. It struck me that if I treat her rank in the hiring order as a random draw from the full list of everyone the company will ever hire, then her landing so early on that list is itself mild evidence against the company ever growing that large, since a random draw from an eventual roster of a million employees would rarely come out as low as 23. That would let me form a confident view about the company's ultimate size from nothing more than her hire number, before learning anything else about the company. When I mentioned this to her, she dismissed it as a parlor trick with no bearing on her company's real prospects, but I could not immediately explain why the underlying reasoning should be wrong. Is this inference actually sound, and if not, where exactly does it break down?
