I am writing a historical novel featuring a fictional student of al-Ghazālī, and I want to write a scene in which she arrives at what seems, startlingly, to be the very insight for which Hume would later become famous. Having absorbed her teacher's occasionalist argument that cotton's burning is never necessitated by its contact with fire—since experience shows us only one event following another, never one event producing another—she feels a vertiginous kinship with a thinker she could not possibly have read. Could you draft the passage in which she reasons her way, step by step, to the recognition that she and Hume, despite six centuries and entirely different religious worlds, had each arrived at exactly the same conclusion about causation?
