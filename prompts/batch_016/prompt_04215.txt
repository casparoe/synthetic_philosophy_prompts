ok so I've fallen down a bit of a rabbit hole and want your help finishing an argument. I've always liked Yudkowsky's old anti-zombie posts ("Zombies! Zombies?" and the Generalized Anti-Zombie Principle) — the move where once you've fully explained, in mechanistic terms, why a system says and believes it has ineffable private qualia, there's nothing left over for a "hard problem" to be about. Someone in a discord I'm in linked Chalmers' actual 2018 "meta-problem of consciousness" paper a while back, and I finally sat down and read the whole thing instead of just absorbing the vibes of it secondhand. Funny enough, there's a footnote where Chalmers directly cites Yudkowsky and paraphrases that bit about a "deranged" zombie-Chalmers writing true philosophy papers purely by coincidence, as an example of what he calls the coincidence argument for illusionism. Small world.

That argument is the part I can't stop poking at. Roughly: (1) there's a full explanation of every judgment, report, and intuition we have about consciousness that never has to mention consciousness itself, (2) if that's true and those judgments are nevertheless correct, their correctness is basically a coincidence, and (3) it's not plausible that something this basic and universal is just a coincidence — so (4) the judgments aren't correct, i.e. illusionism. Chalmers' preferred way out for a realist is what he calls "realizationism": say that consciousness itself does causal work in producing those judgments, rather then merely correlating with whatever does, so their truth isn't accidental. But he pretty explicitly admits this doesn't fully close the case, because you'd still need an independent reason why it has to be consciousness, specifically, doing that causal work, rather than some non-conscious functional stand-in that would've produced the exact same reports anyway. Without that extra step, realizationism just relocates the coincidence one level down instead of removing it.

So that's the gap I want you to actually fill in, instead of just gesturing at like he does. Construct the strongest, most principled version of realizationism you can manage — one with a real, non-question-begging reason (not just "well, by stipulation, consciousness is what's doing it") why phenomenal consciousness, and nothing merely functional, could occupy that causal role, tight enough that positing a zombie twin of us — something producing identical reports minus the phenomenal ingredient — stops even making sense as a coherent possibility. I don't need a whole theory of consciousness out of this, just that one load-bearing move. And if you genuinely don't think such a move is available, I'd rather read your best honest attempt at it followed by an explanation of exactly where it breaks, instead of a shrug.
