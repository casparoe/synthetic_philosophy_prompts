In ch. 15, where he lays out the "infection" test for art, he says:

"And not only is infection a sure sign of art, but the degree of infectiousness is also the sole measure of excellence in art. The stronger the infection the better is the art, as art, speaking now apart from its subject-matter, i.e. not considering the quality of the feelings it transmits."

ok fine, that seems clear enough on its own. but then in the very next chapter he spends ages ranking feelings as good or bad depending on whether they flow from the "religious perception" of universal brotherhood, and ends up calling stuff like patriotic anthems and erotic/sexual content "bad art," even though he'd admit plenty of people find that stuff genuinely moving.

so which is it? if degree of infection is literally "the sole measure of excellence" in art, how does he get to dock a highly infectious work later just for its subject-matter, without contradicting ch. 15? is "excellence"/"goodness" doing two totally seperate jobs across these chapters that he never really flags, or is there some way it's actually one coherent standard and i'm just not seeing it?
