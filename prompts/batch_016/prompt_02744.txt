So for the last year or so I've been flipping limited sneakers as a side hustle, plowing basically all my profit back into inventory for the next drop. My instinct has always been simple expected-value reasoning: if a pair looks like a near-sure flip, throw as much cash as you can at it, since thats obviously how you make the most money on average. A guy who mods the resell discord I'm in says that's wrong — he thinks I should never spend more than some fixed % of whatever cash I currently have on any single drop, and that the actual dollar amount should shrink automatically after a run of bad flips and grow back once I recover, instead of me sizing up or down based on gut confidence. Two things bug me: it seems to leave money on the table on my best calls, and it feels backwards that right after eating some losses is exactly when I shouldn't swing bigger to make up the difference. He insists that just going with "average payout is higher, so bet more" logic is basically guaranteed to wipe out my float eventually, even though every individual drop really is a good bet on its own. Is he actually onto something, or is this just risk-aversion dressed up as a rule?
