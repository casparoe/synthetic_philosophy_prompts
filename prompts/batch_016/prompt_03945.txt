ok so i've been down a bit of a higher-order evidence rabbit hole lately (peer disagreement stuff, the whole epistemology-of-self-doubt literature). once you take seriously that higher-order evidence can be genuinely misleading, there's a real fork in the road: either keep the enkratic principle (no rationally believing P while also believing your evidence doesn't support P) and give up strict evidentialism about credence, or keep evidentialism and just accept that akratic-looking states can sometimes be perfectly rational. Lasonen-Aarnio basically bites that second bullet. most people's gut reaction is that this is nuts, mostly because of the bootstrapping worry -- if "akratic" states are fine, couldn't you just use a string of lucky first-order judgments to build up a track record and explain away any higher-order doubts about your own reliability? i want the strongest possible case for biting the bullet anyway: that we should ditch the enkratic principle rather than evidentialism, and that the bootstrapping objection doesnt actually sink the view. don't just list considerations on both sides, actually argue for it.
