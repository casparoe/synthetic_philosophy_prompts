A friend of mine and I are taking a seminar on the metaphysics of relations together, and after last week's session we spent most of a long train ride arguing about Bradley's regress without ever really getting anywhere. It started because I'd been reading Russell's discussion, in the Principles of Mathematics, of the difference between a relation "actually relating" its terms and that same relation considered merely as a term in its own right (his example is "A differs from B"), and I tried to explain that difference by appealing to something like Frege's idea that the relating item is inherently "unsaturated" -- it has a built-in incompleteness that lets it latch onto its relata directly, so that no further relation is needed to tie it to them and the regress never gets started. My friend, who had been reading about Bergmann's "nexus," argued that this was a kind of shell game: whether you call the unifier an unsaturated concept, a non-relational tie, or a nexus, you are just renaming the mystery rather than explaining why this particular item, unlike an ordinary relation, doesn't itself need something further to connect it to what it unites. I countered that her own fallback view -- that facts or states of affairs are simply brute, non-mereological unities, genuinely more than the sum of their constituents -- looked open to exactly the same complaint. Neither of us budged, and I have to admit that neither position struck me as obviously the weaker one.

I would like an essay that actually adjudicates this dispute rather than just surveying the field. Specifically: can any proposed unifier of a relational fact -- an unsaturated concept, a primitive non-relational tie, a brute non-mereological complex, or some other candidate -- explain why it escapes the need for a further connector, without simply re-describing the very fact that needed explaining in the first place? Or is the honest conclusion that this whole strategy fails, in which case say whether the better response is to treat the regress as benign (a harmless explanatory regress rather than a vicious ontological one) or to give up on the idea that a fact like aRb is built out of separately existing constituents at all. Please commit to a specific verdict rather than a balanced survey -- something like "unsaturatedness escapes the charge because of X, while the nexus and brute-complex strategies don't, for reason Y" would count, and so would "no proposed unifier actually works, and the regress is fatal to the whole constituent picture of facts," but "philosophers disagree and there's much to be said on both sides" would not.
