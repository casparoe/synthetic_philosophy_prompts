A colleague of mine from graduate school -- now on the faculty at another university -- and I have spent the better part of a decade discussing the same body of evidence on a long-standing question in our field, each of us fully briefed on the other's reasoning at every stage, and yet we still arrive at opposite conclusions. Neither of us regards the other as careless, dishonest, or unaware of some decisive consideration. A mutual friend who works in game theory recently insisted that this kind of stable, mutually respectful disagreement is mathematically impossible between two people who genuinely take each other to be rational: repeated exchange of our reasoning should have driven our views together, unless we started out, before either of us had seen any evidence, with fundamentally different assumptions about the question -- which we both deny having had. I can't tell whether she's right that one of us must be reasoning badly, or whether her argument is quietly relying on some assumption that doesn't obviously hold between two real people. What should I make of her challenge?
