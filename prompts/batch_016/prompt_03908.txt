I'm a PhD student in metaphysics, and something that came up in our seminar on powers and dispositions has been nagging at me ever since, even though the rest of the group waved it away as a merely presentational wrinkle rather than a real problem. Start from the familiar set-up: the Simple Conditional Analysis (SCA) says x is disposed to M when C iff x would M if C obtained, and C. B. Martin's electro-fink cases are the standard refutation — a dead wire wired to a device that makes it live the instant a conductor touches it, plus the reverse-cycle version that kills a live wire's charge at the very moment of contact. David Lewis's Reformed Conditional Analysis (RCA) patches SCA by requiring that x have some intrinsic causal basis B such that, were x to undergo C and retain B for a little while longer, C and B would jointly cause M — which correctly counts the reverse-cycle-finked wire as live, since its free electrons would produce conduction if only the fink let them persist. Crucially, though, Lewis can run this move only by also insisting on the Intrinsic Dispositions Thesis (IDT: intrinsic duplicates under shared laws share all their dispositions), since without IDT nothing stops the deflationary response, associated with Choi and Gundersen, of simply saying the finked wire isn't dead to begin with, so that whether it counts as live or dead is fixed extrinsically by whatever it happens to be wired to.

My worry concerns what Lewis then has to say about Bird's and Johnston's masking cases: a fragile glass shielded by packing material, a poison neutralized by an antidote. Here the intrinsic basis is left completely intact — the glass keeps its molecular structure throughout — while something further downstream blocks the manifestation, so RCA's basis-retention clause does nothing to help. Lewis's way out — what's sometimes called "getting specific" — is to deny that the packed glass is disposed to break when struck simpliciter, insisting instead that it is only disposed to break-when-struck-without-the-packing, which lets a fact about the glass's extrinsic surroundings fix which simple disposition it has, even though an unpacked intrinsic duplicate of that very glass is, on anyone's view, disposed to break when struck without qualification. That looks to me like exactly the kind of extrinsically sensitive disposition-fixing that IDT was invoked to rule out in the first place, resurrected only when it happens to be convenient for handling masks.

I'd like you to argue for the claim that this is a genuine, damaging inconsistency in Lewis's overall position — that RCA, IDT, and the "getting specific" reply to masking cannot all be sustained together — rather than a tension that dissolves once the details are filled in carefully. Please also say which of the three commitments a Lewisian should be willing to jettison in order to restore consistency, and why that one rather than either of the others.
