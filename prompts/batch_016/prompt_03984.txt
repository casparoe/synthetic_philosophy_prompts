I've been following the debates in rationalist and effective-altruist circles about how to rank species by their likely degree of consciousness, where one recurring move is to invoke Integrated Information Theory as a substrate-independent yardstick, since it promises to assign a precise "phi" value to any physical system, biological or not. The trouble, as critics like Scott Aaronson have pointed out, is that certain very simple systems — a suitably wired grid of logic gates, or an expander graph — can be engineered to have a higher phi than an actual brain, despite doing nothing anyone would call thinking or feeling. Could you write me a joke that turns on this specific absurdity (some unglamorous circuit outscoring a real animal on a would-be consciousness-meter), rather than a generic "scientists ranking animals" joke? It should land for someone who already knows what phi and IIT actually refer to.
