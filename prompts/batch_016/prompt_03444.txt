I spent 35-odd years running a molecular genetics lab before retiring, and lately I've fallen down a philosophy of science rabbit hole (my wife calls it my second adolescence). Something's been bugging me: is the genetic code an actual code, or just a metaphor biologists got lazy and kept using? The "realist" side leans on how arbitrary the codon table is -- nothing chemically forces GGU to mean glycine rather then some other amino acid, so the mapping looks conventional, like a real symbol system, not a brute causal regularity. The skeptics say thats beside the point: once you take development seriously, DNA isn't privileged over the dozens of other causal factors in the cell and embryo, so calling DNA "the" carrier of information is something we're imposing after the fact, not discovering (I think this is what people call the "parity thesis"?). Could you write me a dialogue between defenders of each view, really pressing on each other's strongest points rather than just talking past each other?
