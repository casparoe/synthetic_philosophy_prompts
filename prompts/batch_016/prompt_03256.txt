Random question, but I think you might untangle this better than my agent or my partner did. Back in Feb I made an offhand joke on twitter about the prose style in a hugely popular fantasy series -- nothing mean, just a joke -- and it got quote-tweeted by an account with something like half a million followers. For about a week and a half I was "discourse": not really about the joke anymore, more like total strangers arguing with each other and using me as the occassion. Someone recut an old podcast clip of me to sound like I'd said the opposite of what I actually said, a couple of accounts I'd never heard of got genuinely furious on behalf of an author who almost certainly doesn't know I exist, and then my own book's Goodreads page picked up something like forty one-star reviews in two days, from accounts where you can tell just from the phrasing that nobody read past the blurb. My agent's advice was "don't read the replies," my partner thinks I'm being precious about a bunch of strangers who'll forget about it in a week, and both are probably true, but neither one touches the thing that's actually stuck with me since, which is that being disagreed with by an actual person feels completely different in kind from whatever that was. It didn't feel like a few thousand individuals each having a private opinion of me, it felt more like a single mood or weather system had assembled itself and was just raining on me -- there wasn't anybody "in there" I could've actually spoken to, even hypothetically.

Part of why I can't drop it is that I write literary fiction, and I'm drafting a novel right now where something similar happens to my main character around the midpoint. I keep getting stuck on how to render the crowd honestly, without either turning it into a mustache-twirling villain or shrugging it off as "well, that's just the internet." So really I've got one question wearing two hats: is there an actual difference in kind between being criticised by a person and being on the receiving end of a thing like that, or am I just dressing up a garden-variety pile-on in fancier language? And if the difference is real, does it change how a person ought to engage with it at all, or is "don't read the replies" simply correct and I'm overthinking this?
