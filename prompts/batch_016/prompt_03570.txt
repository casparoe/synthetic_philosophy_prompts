I'm writing a novel built around a manuscript that seems to comment on its own composition, and in one key scene two old friends from graduate school—a model theorist and her former student, now a committed Priest-style dialetheist—reopen an argument about the Liar paradox that they never settled. He thinks that "gap" is a dodge, since the Strengthened Liar ("this sentence is not true") shows that whatever you say about the original sentence's status can be folded back into a fresh paradoxical sentence—so, on his view, some contradictions must simply be true, provided the underlying logic is paraconsistent enough to block explosion. Please write their exchange as spoken dialogue rather than as a lecture, something like ten to fourteen turns, in which she has to answer the revenge problem he raises and he has to answer her worry about triviality, and let the disagreement stay genuinely open at the end.
