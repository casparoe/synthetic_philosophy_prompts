Coherence arguments for expected utility maximization rely heavily on the money-pump argument: an agent whose preferences cycle (she prefers A to B, B to C, and C to A) will accept a trade at every step of the cycle, paying a small fee each time, and so can be drained of an arbitrary amount of money purely as a consequence of having cyclical preferences, with no further assumptions needed about how she deliberates or chooses. This looks to me like a fully general, non-question-begging proof that transitivity is a precondition of rational agency, on pain of certain exploitation. I would like to present the argument this way in my graduate seminar on decision theory, but I keep encountering decision theorists who still treat the rational necessity of transitivity as an open question. What could they possibly still be disputing?
