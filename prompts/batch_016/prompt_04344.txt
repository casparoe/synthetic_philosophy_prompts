This term, my Philosophy of Religion seminar has spent several weeks on logical positivism, and we recently discussed A. J. Ayer's verification principle alongside the curious coda to his career: the cardiac arrest he suffered in 1988 and the near-death experience he described afterward in his essay "What I Saw When I Was Dead." For the creative-writing component of the course, I need to write a short story, fictionalizing Ayer himself, that treats this episode as a genuine philosophical problem rather than a simple conversion narrative -- dramatizing the specific bind facing a committed verificationist who undergoes a vivid, singular experience seemingly bearing on survival after death, precisely the sort of private, unrepeatable occurrence that his own criterion of meaningfulness would treat with suspicion. Please resist a tidy resolution in either direction (vindicated materialism or a confirmed afterlife); let the story close roughly where Ayer's own hedged, partially walked-back public remarks actually left the matter. Aim for 800-1,000 words, in whichever of first-person or close third-person best captures the interiority of the moment.
