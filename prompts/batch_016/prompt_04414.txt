As a science and ideas journalist, I'm working on a piece about self-trust: how much stock we should put, today, in opinions we're confident we'll only reach after further reflection or evidence. This has led me to Bas van Fraassen's Reflection Principle, which holds that a rational agent's current credence in a proposition, conditional on the supposition that their credence in it will equal some value r at a later time, must already equal r — a constraint van Fraassen originally defended via a diachronic Dutch book argument. Critics such as Talbott (1991) and Christensen (1991) have pressed cases — anticipated memory loss, anticipated lapses into irrationality — where honoring the principle seems to demand exactly the wrong response, prompting several commentators to favor restricted versions that exclude such cases. With both the original principle and these restrictions in mind, please describe the most important implications of Reflection for how much epistemic weight I should give, right now, to the opinion I fully expect to hold about a contested story once my reporting is complete.
