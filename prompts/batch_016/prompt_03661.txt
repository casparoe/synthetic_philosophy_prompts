For my school's philosophy club, I have been reading about the "special composition question": roughly, the question of when, if ever, some objects compose a further object (for instance, whether the front half of a trout and the back half of a turkey compose anything at all). Eli Hirsch argues that debates between the standard answers to this question, such as nihilism, universalism, and organicism, are "merely verbal," since each side simply means something different by "exists." Ted Sider disagrees, suggesting that one candidate meaning of "exists" might still be the uniquely "joint-carving" one, which would make the dispute substantive after all. I can follow each position on its own, but I do not have a firm grip on the underlying concept: what, in general, makes a dispute merely verbal rather than substantive, and how would one go about deciding which kind of dispute this is?
