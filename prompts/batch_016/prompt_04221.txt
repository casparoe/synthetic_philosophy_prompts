So I was at a dinner thing on Saturday (not really my beat — I mostly write about neuroscience — but I can't stop chewing on this one), and two friends of mine got into an argument that nobody at the table could settle.

Jess does pottery as a hobby. She told a story about sculpting a clay bust of her cat, loving how it turned out, photographing it, and then squashing it back into a ball because she needed the clay for a bowl the next day. Someone asked "so you destroyed it?" and she said yeah, obviously. Marcus, who apparently minored in philosophy, said no she didn't. Roughly how it went (I'm reconstructing from memory, so forgive any looseness):

1. Marcus: the bust just IS the clay, shaped a certain way — the clay never stopped existing, you only changed its shape, like melting an ice cube doesn't destroy the water, it just changes form. So technically, nothing was destroyed.

2. Jess: no, because the bust and the clay don't have the same survival conditions — the clay could survive being squashed, the bust obviously couldn't, that's literally what just happened to it. Two things with different properties can't be the very same thing, so there must've been two objects sitting on that table made of the same stuff, and only one of them got squashed out of existence.

3. Marcus: that's a cheat though. "Could survive squashing" isn't a property the bust has right now, sitting there before its squashed — it's a fact about what would happen later, in some hypothetical. You can't smuggle in a fact about the future and call it a "property" that splits one physical object into two.

4. Jess: but if you won't count facts like that as real properties, you can never distinguish any two coincident objects, ever, in any case — which just means you've assumed there's only one object here from the start. That's not an argument against her view, that's just restating his.

5. Marcus: fine, maybe that IS exactly right — there's only one object, "clay," and "bust" is just a temporary label for it in a certain shape, same way a "traffic jam" doesn't name some new object made of cars. Nothing was destroyed, only relabeled.

At that point Priya, who'd been quiet all night, goes "wait, so did Michelangelo ever actually make a statue, or did he just relabel a rock?" — and afterward Jess AND Marcus seperately told me this proved they were right.
