Had a long argument with my friend last night about the Knobe effect - the chairman/environment case where harming the environment as a side effect gets called "intentional" by something like 80% of people, but helping it as a side effect only gets called intentional by like 20%. We both agree the effect is real, we just disagree about why its happening. I was on team Uttich & Lombrozo: a norm violation just tells you more about what's going on in someone's head than norm-following behavior does, nothing inherently moral about it. My friend was team Sripada, arguing its really about whether the outcome matches the guy's "deep self" (his stable values/character), regardless of whether any norm got broken. We eventually realised the classic chairman case can't settle this between us, since the norm violation and the deep-self mismatch happen to line up perfectly there - harming the environment is both the norm violation AND the thing that matches how callous he supposedly is. Could you come up with a new example, pretty different from the chairman one, where the norm-violation account and the deep-self account would predict different calls on whether the outcome was intentional?
