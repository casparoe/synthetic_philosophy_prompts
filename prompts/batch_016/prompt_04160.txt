My sister and I are trying to settle our late father's estate without dragging the family through lawyers, and a process that started out perfectly amicable has curdled into something I don't quite know how to handle. My father's longtime accountant, who has been informally helping us work through the will, mentioned to me privately last week that whoever states a "final, non-negotiable" position first — and does so publicly enough that backing down would be embarrassing — tends to come out ahead in disputes like this. I didn't think much of it at the time.

The trouble is that my sister appears to have gotten identical advice from her husband, who prides himself on being a shrewd negotiator, and I now have good reason to believe she plans to announce her own "final" terms at a family dinner this Sunday. So I've seriously considered emailing my own position to the rest of the family tonight, before she gets the chance — even though, if I'm honest with myself, I haven't actually finished deciding what division of the lake house and my father's investment accounts I would even consider fair. When I described all this to a cousin of mine who does labor mediation for a living, she let out a laugh that wasn't entirely a joke and said, "you two are about to do the thing that turns an ordinary dispute into a ten-year feud," before she got pulled away and never finished explaining what she meant.

I've spent enough years reading rationalist and effective-altruism-adjacent blogs to have a nagging sense that there's a recognizable strategic pattern operating here — something about why committing first and hardest is supposed to hand you the advantage, and why that very same reasoning, adopted by both sides at once and in a hurry, might leave everyone worse off than if neither of us had bothered. But I can't reconstruct the actual argument clearly enough to know whether that instinct is even correct, or what it would imply I should do this weekend. Is there something real underneath this, or am I just dressing up garden-variety sibling friction in fancier language? And either way: should I go ahead and send my "final position" before she sends hers, hold off entirely, or handle the whole thing some other way?
