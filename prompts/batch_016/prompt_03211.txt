Ok so this is going to sound like a weird rabbit hole but I have zero academic background in any of this, I just spend too much time reading AI safety and game theory stuff on forums like LessWrong for fun. Theres this idea that keeps popping up called a "commitment race" - basically the worry that if both sides in a conflict know that whoever locks themselves into an irreversible position FIRST gets the upper hand (like the old Schelling idea about winning a game of chicken by ripping your own steering wheel out and chucking it out the window so the other guy literally has no choice but to swerve), then everyone's incentivized to slam down some extreme commitment as fast as possible, before they've even had time to think it through or figure out what the other side is actually going for. Taken to its logical end, the "smart" move becomes committing before you're even sure committing is the smart move, and if both sides do that at once you get two irreversible things smashing into each other, which is obviously worse than if neither had committed yet. Kind of reminds me of me and my brother fighting over the aux cord as kids - whoever grabbed it and physically wouldn't let go usually won, regardless of who actually had the better taste in music.

Anyway, instead of just reading peoples summaries of the debate I'd rather see two sharp people actually go at it. Could you write me a dialogue between two negotiators (human diplomats, rival AI systems, whatever setting lets you write the sharpest version of this) at the exact moment each one can tell the other is about to lock in some irreversible move, before either of them fully knows what the other one even wants. One of them argues the only sane response is to commit even harder and faster right now, before you end up the sucker who hesitated. The other thinks that whole line of reasoning is exactly how you turn a disagreement into a catastrophe, and is trying to find some way of not getting steamrolled without racing to throw away their own options first. Don't make it so one of them is obviously right - I want them really responding to each other's specific points with actual counterarguments, like two people who each see something true that the other is missing, not just restating their own side louder.
