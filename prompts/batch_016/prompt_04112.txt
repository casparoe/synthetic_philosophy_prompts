Ok this has been bugging me since it came up at a LW-adjacent meetup a few weeks back and nobody really had a satisfying answer. Everyone knows the standard move against ontological arguments — Anselm and Descartes supposedly smuggle existence in as if it were a perfection or predicate, Kant's "existence isn't a predicate" line, etc. But Gödel's modal version always struck me as different, and apparently it's not just an armchair thing anymore. Back in 2013-ish, Christoph Benzmüller and Bruno Woltzenlogel Paleo actually formalized the whole argument — all the axioms about "positive properties," the definition of "godlike," all of it — and ran it through automated theorem provers like Isabelle and Coq.

And it checked out. Valid, no hidden gaps, no sneaky step where the conclusion quietly sneaks into a premise. It even made headlines in Germany at the time. From what I can tell the only genuinely "existence-flavored" input anywhere in the system is that it's possible that a godlike being exists, and because the whole thing runs on S5 modal logic, that one modest-sounding possibility premise is enough to get you all the way to necessary existence. Someone in the discord tried to explain this to me twice and I think I mostly followed it.

So here's my actual question: given that Godel is maybe the most careful logician of the 20th century, and we now have independent, machine-checked confirmation that the inference chain is airtight, it seems weird that a bunch of otherwise pretty rigorous atheist friends of mine still just shrug and say "it's just word games," like the formalization changed nothing at all. Once a proof assistant has verified every step, isn't the hard part basically done? Shouldn't that push a good Bayesian's credence in theism up at least a notch? Since the logic itself has been mechanically checked and holds up, why doesn't this argument compell anyone who wasn't already a theist, and is "its just semantics" actually a real rebuttal at this point, or just a thought-terminating cliche people reach for?
