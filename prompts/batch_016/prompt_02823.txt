I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, but I recently ran across the epistemological "bootstrapping problem" (sometimes called the problem of easy knowledge), which is supposed to be a serious objection to reliabilism. The idea is that if a theory lets you trust a source without any independent grounds, you can seemingly verify that very source using nothing but its own outputs — the standard illustration is someone reading a car's gas gauge over and over and inferring from the readings alone that the gauge must be accurate, without ever checking it against something independent like a dipstick. But this is exactly how we build compilers: a self-hosting compiler is compiled and validated by earlier versions of itself, and nobody in computer science treats that as circular or illegitimate — it's the same basic self-referential setup behind Ken Thompson's famous "Reflections on Trusting Trust" lecture. Given that we rely on exactly this kind of bootstrapping in a domain where correctness is critical, isn't the epistemologists' "bootstrapping problem" really just a confusion about ordinary engineering practice rather than a genuine threat to reliabilism?
