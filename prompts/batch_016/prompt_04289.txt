For Ethics Bowl prep, I've been reading about the philosophical debate over hypocrisy and the standing to blame, in particular the idea that there's a "nonhypocrisy condition" on having the standing to call out someone else's wrongdoing — meaning a hypocrite can lose the right to blame someone even when the accusation itself is completely true, which is supposed to name a real phenomenon rather than just the tu quoque fallacy in disguise. Please describe the most important implications of this for a situation that comes up constantly at my school: a student who is clearly guilty of the very thing they're condemning (say, cheating or spreading rumors) calls someone else out for it, and everyone shuts them down with "you have no room to talk." Specifically, I would like to understand whether losing standing this way means onlookers should discount the accusation's content itself or only the accuser's right to voice it, whether it takes genuine hypocrisy — as opposed to any comparable wrongdoing on the accuser's part — to lose standing in the first place, and whether standing, once lost, can ever be earned back.
