For a column on the history and philosophy of science, I want to stage a fairly technical dispute rather than rehearse the standard, broad-strokes version of the scientific realism debate. Write a dialogue between a structural realist and an antirealist who champions Laudan's pessimistic meta-induction. Have them argue specifically over John Worrall's case study of the shift from Fresnel's elastic-ether theory of light to Maxwell's electromagnetic theory — a case in which the mathematical relations Fresnel derived for reflected and refracted light carried over into the new theory even though the vibrating ether they were meant to describe did not. The realist should argue that this episode shows how to save scientific realism by relocating belief in mathematical structure rather than underlying mechanism; the antirealist should engage with this specific case rather than simply reciting Laudan's list of abandoned theories, pressing on whatever they take to be the weakest point in the realist's account of what "preserved structure" really amounts to. Make sure each side directly answers the other's actual argument rather than talking past one another.
