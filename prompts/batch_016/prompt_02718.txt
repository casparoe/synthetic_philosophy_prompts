Write a dialogue between two researchers on a team building a self-modifying AI, arguing over whether to approve the system's next self-rewrite. One of them refuses to sign off unless the current system can actually prove its successor will be trustworthy, and keeps running headfirst into the Löbian obstacle every time. The other thinks that's a standard Löb's theorem guarantees can never be met, and wants to just go with something like parametric polymorphism, or drop full provability for something probabilistic instead. Get into the real technical weeds of why the naive proof-based approach breaks, rather then just hand-waving about Gödel -- and make sure they're actually responding to each other's specific moves, not just repeating their own side. neither one should be easy to refute.
