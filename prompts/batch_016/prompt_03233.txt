In my philosophy of language seminar this term, we've moved from Frege's original puzzle about identity statements to Kripke's extension of it in "A Puzzle about Belief," and I'd like to see the underlying disagreement played out directly. Write a dialogue between a Fregean, who holds that distinct senses explain why a true identity statement like "Hesperus is Phosphorus" can be informative even though "Hesperus is Hesperus" is not, and a Millian direct-reference theorist, who denies that a name contributes anything to the proposition expressed beyond its bearer. Have them work through Kripke's Paderewski case: Peter encounters the name "Paderewski" on two separate occasions, once attached to a concert pianist and once to a Polish statesman, and, never suspecting these are one and the same man, sincerely assents to both "Paderewski has musical talent" and "Paderewski has no musical talent." Each character should press the other on whether this case actually vindicates their own account of names, or whether, as Kripke suggests, it reveals a puzzle that survives no matter which theory of sense or reference one adopts. They are equally capable philosophers, so I want real engagement with each other's strongest points, not two parallel monologues.
