I spent my working life as an experimental physicist specializing in thin-film optics, so Fresnel's equations for reflectance and transmittance at an interface were part of my everyday toolkit long before I ever gave a moment's thought to their history. Since retiring, I have joined a small philosophy discussion group made up mostly of other retired academics, and we have lately been working through the literature on scientific realism. It was something of a shock to learn that Fresnel derived those very equations from a mechanical picture of light as a transverse vibration in an elastic-solid luminiferous ether, an ontology that was abandoned entirely once Maxwell's electromagnetic theory took hold, even though the mathematical content itself carried over essentially intact. Naturally, this episode keeps coming up in discussions of Larry Laudan's pessimistic meta-induction, as an example of both what a realist should fear — a once-central posit turning out not to exist — and what a realist might take comfort in, since something evidently did survive.

What I am struggling with is that two of the standard realist responses to Laudan reach for this very same episode and yet, as far as I can tell, diverge sharply on what they take it to show. John Worrall's epistemic structural realism, in his original 1989 paper, treats the case as evidence that only the mathematical structure of a theory, and not its ontology, is ever a reasonable object of realist commitment, whereas Stathis Psillos's "divide et impera," or selective, realism wants to say that some properly theoretical content, and not merely structure, was retained, on the grounds that only the "idle" parts of Fresnel's theory were ever really discarded. I have also come across the claim that an old objection due to the Cambridge mathematician Max Newman, originally aimed at Bertrand Russell, poses a serious problem for Worrall's position but not for Psillos's, and I cannot yet reconstruct for myself why that asymmetry should hold. Could you explain, carefully and without presuming that I have read Worrall or Psillos in the original, exactly where their two positions part ways on what survives the transition from Fresnel to Maxwell, and then why the Newman objection is thought to tell specifically against structural realism rather than against selective realism or scientific realism more generally?
