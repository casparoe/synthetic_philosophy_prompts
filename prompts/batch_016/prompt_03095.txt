Since I retired a few years back I've had a lot more time for philosophy than I ever did during my career (35 years as a physicist, mostly reactor stuff), and lately that's had me reading old essays by scientists thinking out loud about the big picture. Came across von Neumann's 1955 Fortune essay "Can We Survive Technology?" and got stuck on a bit of it. At one point, talking about things like automation and climate control, he says:

"Technology—like science—is neutral all through, providing only means of control applicable to any purpose, indifferent to all."

then further down in the same section, arguing against the idea that we could just ban the especially dangerous technologies one by one, he says:

"Technological power, technological efficiency as such, is an ambivalent achievement. Its danger is intrinsic."

Those two claims feel like they're pulling in opposite directions to me - if tech really is "neutral" and "indifferent to all" purposes, how can its danger also be "intrinsic" rather then just contingent on who's using it and for what? Is there a way to read this that's not just self-contradictory, or is he equivocating on something? Been chewing on it for days, curious what im missing.
