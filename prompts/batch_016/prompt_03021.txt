Ive been chewing on this for a while and it's bugging me. Everyone says we owe it to future generations not to wreck the climate, but then I read about the 'nonidentity problem' and it seems like it actually kills that whole argument. The specific people who'll be born in 100+ years only exist because of whatever policies we make now — a different policy means different couples have kids at different times, so literally different people end up getting born. So those future people could never say our choices harmed them, since the only alternative for them specifically was never existing, not existing in a nicer world. If thats really how the logic works, doesnt it mean philosophers have basically shown the whole 'harming future generations' thing is a confused argument, and we need some totally different reason to care about climate policy?
