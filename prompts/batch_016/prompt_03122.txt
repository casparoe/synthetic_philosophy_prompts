Last night a friend and I argued for hours about Gödel's ontological argument and never reached an agreement. I took the view that Sobel's modal-collapse objection is fatal: granting that necessary existence counts as a positive property, and that whatever is positive is necessarily positive, the essence clause then forces the conclusion that any actual truth turns out to be true in every possible world — an absurd result that no proponent of the proof should be willing to accept. My friend maintained that Anderson's emendation blocks the collapse without robbing the argument of its intended theological conclusion. Neither of us gave ground. Could you write a short dialogue between two equally sharp philosophers defending these two positions — one that actually pins down where in the derivation the collapse originates, rather than just trading slogans like "it proves too much"?
