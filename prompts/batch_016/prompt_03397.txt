I am a PhD student in philosophy, though my own research is in ethics rather than anything formal, so a small episode from my personal life has been nagging at me without my being able to place it theoretically.

My partner and I recently coordinated a surprise party for my sister. The plan depended on precise timing: I had to keep her away from her apartment until the decorations were ready, and my partner refused to start decorating until they were confident I had actually gotten her out of the building. We agreed to confirm each step by text, but messages sent from inside that building sometimes silently fail to go through. So we settled on a short back-and-forth before acting: I would text "leaving now," my partner would reply "got it, starting," I would reply "got your reply," and only then would each of us proceed.

Afterward it struck me that whoever sends the last message in such a sequence can never be entirely sure it arrived, so neither of us could, strictly speaking, ever be fully certain that the other knew what we knew; we could in principle have traded confirmations indefinitely without ever closing that gap. In practice we stopped after a few texts and it worked out, which feels perfectly reasonable, but I cannot work out whether stopping there was actually justified, or simply a convenient way of overlooking a problem that never really disappears. Is there a principled reason to treat some finite exchange as good enough, or were we just fortunate?
