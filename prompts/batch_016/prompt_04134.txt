At my high school's spring recital, I played a difficult piece from memory without a single mistake. Afterward, my piano teacher, who also runs the sound system for our concerts, told me something I hadn't known beforehand: she had set up the digital piano so that if I ever stopped playing or hit several wrong notes in a row, it would automatically take over and finish the piece flawlessly on its own, so the audience would never have realized I had failed. Since I actually got through the whole piece myself, that backup never switched on, and everything the audience heard that night was genuinely me playing. My older brother argues that because there was never any real chance the audience could have heard me fail, I don't deserve as much credit for pulling it off as I think I do. Something about his argument feels wrong to me, but I can't quite pin down why. Is he right, or is he missing something?
