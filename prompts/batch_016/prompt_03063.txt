Four years ago, worried about my tendency to panic-sell during market downturns, I arranged for my retirement account to be locked so that no buying, selling, or rebalancing can occur for a fixed period of ten years. Even so, whenever the market drops sharply I find myself wanting to check the account's current value, despite knowing that nothing I could learn from it would change what happens to the money before the lock expires. A friend insists that checking is harmless at worst, since more information can never leave a rational person worse off, and that refusing to look is really just a way of avoiding reality. I suspect there is something wrong with this argument, but I cannot quite articulate what. Is my friend right that checking carries no real downside, or is my reluctance actually justified?
