So I've been rereading old LessWrong threads on Roko's basilisk this week (I know, I know) and I think I've talked myself into an actual inconsistency, not just the usual "why would you be scared of a hypothetical torture AI" reaction. Brought it up at our local ACX meetup too and mostly got shrugs. To be clear I'm setting aside the whole saga of Eliezer deleting the original post and banning the topic for years — that's a separate question about infohazard policy, not about whether the underlying argument actually works.

Here's what bugs me: the standard dismissal you get is basically pure CDT reasoning — "once the AI exists it can't reach back and change the past by torturing people, so it has no incentive to follow through, so the threat is empty." Fine, except the entire reason the basilisk is even coherent as a hypothetical is that a lot of people in that scene explicitly reject CDT for exactly this kind of case — that's the whole point of TDT/UDT: you're supposed to one-box on Newcomb's problem and cooperate with a predicted copy of yourself in a prisoner's dilemma, because your decision is logically correlated with what the predictor simulates you doing, not just causally downstream of it. So it seems pretty convienient that the same crowd invokes that acausal correlation to justify one-boxing and cooperation, then quietly switches back to ordinary causal "no incentive to follow through" reasoning the second an acausal argument implies something scary instead of something nice like mutual cooperation. If your decision theory says the simulated prediction of you basically IS what your choosing between, shouldn't that logic cut both ways?

Am I missing some principled asymmetry between the cooperation case and the blackmail case, or is "people who take TDT/UDT seriously should logically be more worried about the basilisk, not less" actually the more consistent position, and everyone's just motivatedly avoiding that conclusion because it's unpleasant?
