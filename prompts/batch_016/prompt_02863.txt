I spent my career as an astronomer, and in retirement I have been working through classical Chinese philosophy, currently Gongsun Long and the School of Names. I keep returning to the white horse dialogue: Gongsun Long argues that since "horse" names a shape and "white" names a colour, and since a request for a horse can be filled by a black or yellow horse while a request for a white horse cannot, "white horse" and "horse" are not the same name -- so a white horse is not a horse. His opponent objects that wherever there is a white horse, one cannot say there is no horse.

This keeps recalling an episode from my own field: in 2006 the International Astronomical Union ruled that Pluto is a "dwarf planet," stating explicitly that planets and dwarf planets are two distinct classes -- that a dwarf planet is, by definition, not a planet -- overruling those who insisted Pluto is still a planet, merely a small one.

Reasoning strictly from the logical moves of his own argument, rather than its general moral about names and reality, how do you think Gongsun Long would have judged the IAU's ruling? Would he see it as vindicating his method, or would he detect a slide between senses of "is not" that his own logic would not have allowed?
