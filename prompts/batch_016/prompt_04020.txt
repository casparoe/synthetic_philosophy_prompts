Hey, for this week's short paper I want you to get into the weeds on higher-order theories of consciousness, past the usual HOT-vs-HOP stuff. Compare Rosenthal's original actualist HOT theory, where a state is conscious only if there's an actual, seperate higher-order thought targeting it, with Carruthers' later dispositionalist version (from Phenomenal Consciousness), where the state just needs to be disposed to cause such a thought. Carruthers motivates this partly with a 'cognitive overload' worry about the actualist view, but dispositionalism has its own headache: its not obvious why a merely-dispositional state should count as phenomenally conscious at all, and there's the added question of how each view handles misrepresentation, i.e. cases where the HOT misfires with no matching first-order state. So which view actually holds up better? Give me a real verdict, not a tradeoffs list.
