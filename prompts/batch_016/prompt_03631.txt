If Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory is consistent, the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem guarantees that it has a countable model — yet, since Cantor's theorem holds inside that model too, the model still proves that uncountable sets exist. The standard resolution is that uncountability is relative to a model: no bijection between such a set and the natural numbers exists inside the model, even though one exists outside it, from a richer external vantage point. What this relativity ultimately shows is disputed: Putnam took considerations along these lines to argue that even a complete theory of the world cannot pin down a single intended model of it, while others treat the whole episode as an artifact of first-order logic's limited expressive power rather than a substantive metaphysical lesson.
