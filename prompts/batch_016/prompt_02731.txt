Been down a rabbit hole lately on acausal trade -- started with some old LW posts on timeless decision theory, then I actually sat down and read (most of) Oesterheld's "Multiverse-wide Cooperation via Correlated Decision Making" paper, which was a genuine struggle in parts but also kind of rewired my brain a little. The core idea, that agents with zero causal contact -- different Everett branches, sims, aliens on the other side of an infinite universe, whatever -- can still "trade" via correlated/subjunctively dependent decisions (Hofstadter's superrationality, basically, scaled up to cosmic proportions) strikes me as either one of the most underrated ideas to come out of that whole scene or total nonsense wearing a decision-theory costume, and I genuinely go back and forth on which. Mentioned this to my study group and it turned into the debate that wouldn't die, except everyone just ended up restating their priors louder instead of actually engaging, which was unsatisfying.

So could you write the dialogue we never actually finished having? Two people: Priya, who thinks acausal trade is real and actually action-guiding (she's willing to bite the bullet that you should behave somewhat differently today because of your subjunctive relationship to distant similar agents, and she leans on the twin prisoner's dilemma as the clean intuition pump for why this isn't crazy), and Marcus, a skeptic who is NOT allowed to just wave the whole thing away as "obviously confused" -- he has to actually grant that subjunctive dependence is coherent and still push on what I think are the real soft spots: the individuation problem (how do you pick out a specific "trading partner" from the space of all possible minds instead of just projecting whoever you want onto the void), the worry that the framework is unfalsifiable enough to rationalize any conclusion you already wanted, and -- this is the one I really want to see argued out properly -- whether there's any principled way to distinguish acausal "trade" from acausal extortion, given that the whole Roko's basilisk mess came from someone running basically the same reasoning forward a few steps toward blackmail instead of cooperation. If the theory can't cleanly rule out the extortion case, is it really action-guiding, or just vibes with math?

I don't want either of them strawmanned, and I'd rather it not resolve neatly -- ideally each one lands at least one point the other has to visibly struggle with rather then just brushing off. Something like 800-1100 words, formatted as a straight back-and-forth with names (Priya:, Marcus:), maybe one line at the top to set the scene if you want but no need for heavy narration beyond that.
