A friend and I spent much of last night arguing about the philosophical upshot of Einstein's hole argument, and we ended up agreeing on a conclusion that's still nagging at me. Our reasoning was this: since one can always construct a hole diffeomorphism that leaves a solution of the Einstein field equations untouched outside some region but reshuffles the metric smoothly within it, and the resulting field configuration counts as just as good a solution as the original, general relativity must itself be a genuinely indeterministic theory, on a par with the way quantum mechanics is indeterministic on some interpretations: even a Laplace's demon—an observer with complete knowledge of the matter and metric fields on a full Cauchy surface preceding the hole—could never predict what geometry will obtain inside it. Is that really the correct moral to draw from the hole argument, or have we gone wrong somewhere?
