Since retiring from a career in condensed-matter physics, I have been reading through the philosophy of social science, and I think my background gives me a clean way to settle the long-standing dispute between methodological individualism and holism. In statistical mechanics, the pressure and temperature we measure in the laboratory are nothing but statistical summaries over the positions and momenta of enormous numbers of molecules: given the microdynamics and enough particles for the law of large numbers to take hold, every macroscopic regularity falls out, with nothing causally new added at the aggregate level. Social "wholes" such as institutions, classes, and cultures seem to me to work in exactly the same way — mere labels for statistical regularities among individuals — so that any holist explanation that treats such wholes as causally efficacious in their own right is, at best, a placeholder for an individualist reduction that has not yet been carried out. Am I missing some genuine disanalogy between the two cases, or does this essentially vindicate methodological individualism, in the same way that kinetic theory rendered holism about thermodynamic quantities unnecessary?
