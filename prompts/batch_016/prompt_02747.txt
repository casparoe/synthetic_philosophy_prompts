Okay so this has been rattling around in my head for like two weeks and I keep bringing it up with people but nobody actually settles it for me, so let me just lay it out. I do most of the hiring for the small business I run, and I've always leaned pretty hard on gut feeling in interviews. Theres this one guy from last month, great resume, said all the right things, but something about him just felt off to me, so I passed on him even though my business partner thought he was fine. A few days later she sent me a pile of studies showing that unstructured interview "gut reactions" are barely better than a coinflip at predicting who's actually good at a job, and that a lot of what people are picking up on is just unconscious stuff, like whether the candidate reminds them of someone from their past. Fine, I'm not going to pretend decades of research is wrong just because I don't like the conclusion. But here's the part that's messing with me: when I picture that interview again in my head, I don't feel one bit less sure that something was wrong with the guy. The feeling hasn't moved an inch even though I now believe, intellectually, that feelings like it are usually garbage.

My partner says if I really believe the research I should just drop the gut-feeling thing entirely and switch to a scorecard. My brother says that's overthinking it, and if the feeling is still that strong after seeing the data, I should trust it, because my brain is obviously tracking something real even if some study can't capture it. I don't have any background in this kind of stuff, I just can't shake the sense that both of them are missing something. Is it even possible to rationally believe "my gut is unreliable in general" and "my gut is right about this specific guy" at the same time, or does one of those have to give way? And is there any actual principled way to know when a broad finding like that should override a strong specific feeling, versus when the feeling should win out?
