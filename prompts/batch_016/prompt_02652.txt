Frankfurt cases are supposed to show that moral responsibility doesn't require alternative possibilities, without just smuggling in determinism to do the work. But there's a classic dilemma objection to these cases (Kane, Widerker, Ginet and others have all pushed versions of it): the 'prior sign' that tips off Black (the counterfactual intervener) is either causally sufficient for Jones's decision, in which case determinism is doing all the real work and the case just begs the question against libertarians, or it isn't sufficient, in which case Jones could've done something else (not given off that sign, e.g.), so PAP survives after all.

Can you construct a Frankfurt-style case that actually threads this needle, i.e. dodges both horns, rather then just being a fancier way of falling into one of them? Walk me through why it really escapes the dilemma and isn't secretly begging the question somewhere else.
