So I'm doing a PhD in HPS (history and philosophy of science, focused on biology) and this week's reading group was on the species problem, starting from Darwin himself. My advisor pointed out something I hadn't clocked before: Darwin was basically already a species skeptic. In Origin he flat-out says the term species is "arbitrarily given for the sake of convenience" and doesn't really differ in kind from "variety" -- which is kind of wild for a book literally called On the Origin of Species. Now I can't stop thinking about how he'd react to the state the concept is in today, which is honestly even messier than his own era's varieties-vs-species arguements over stuff like oak trees.

Could you give me your best reconstruction of what Darwin would say if someone briefed him on: (1) cryptic species -- cases where DNA sequencing reveals that populations which look and behave identically are actually reproductively isolated and have been diverging for ages, (2) ring species like the Ensatina salamander complex, which gets used in every textbook as proof that species boundaries are fuzzy, except apparently current genomic data has some herpetologists arguing Ensatina isn't even a clean ring species and is more like a "superspecies" made of several independent lineages, and (3) the fact that for bacteria, which barely fit the interbreeding-based species concept at all, taxonomists just settled on a pretty arbitrary numerical cutoff (~95-96% average nucleotide identity) for what counts as one species vs two -- partly because horizontal gene transfer messes with the whole tree-like picture of descent he assumed applied more or less universally.

I don't just want the "he'd probably be open-minded about it" answer -- I want you to actually reason from his specific commitments (gradualism, common descent, his resistance to essentialism, whatever else seems relevant) to something like a real position, and to flag anyplace where you think the modern data might have genuinely surprised or unsettled him rather than just confirming a hunch he already had.
