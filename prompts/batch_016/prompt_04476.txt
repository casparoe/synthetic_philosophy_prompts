Three weeks ago I went into cardiac arrest during a minor surgical procedure and was, by the anaesthetist's account, pulseless for a little over two minutes before the crash team got a rhythm back. I won't describe what I experienced, except to say it was vivid enough that I've spent the recovery reading philosophy of mind rather than the discharge paperwork.

That reading turned up something I hadn't known: A. J. Ayer went through much the same thing in 1988, choking on smoked salmon in hospital and going without a heartbeat for about four minutes. He wrote the episode up twice, first in the Sunday Telegraph and then more cautiously in The Spectator, describing a painful red light he took to be governing the universe and reporting that the experience had "slightly weakened" his conviction that death would be the end of him, though not his atheism. His own preferred explanation, faithful to his physicalism, was that his brain had probably kept working for a time after his heart stopped.

What I can't square is how this sits with the verification principle he built his career on, according to which claims about surviving bodily death aren't false but strictly meaningless, since no observation could ever confirm or refute them. Yet Ayer treated his own case as bearing on a real empirical question, and so, later, did science: the 2014 AWARE study hid target images in resuscitation wards specifically to test whether reported out-of-body perception was veridical (almost no one has ever identified one), and its 2023 sequel found bursts of near-normal, even gamma-band, EEG activity in some patients up to an hour into CPR -- closer to Ayer's own on-the-spot hypothesis than to anything supernatural.

Reasoning from Ayer's actual principles rather than his public reassurances, how do you think he would have assessed this research programme -- as finally giving empirical content to a question he'd long dismissed as nonsense, or as still missing the point of his own critique -- and how would he have resolved the tension with his own reported reaction?
