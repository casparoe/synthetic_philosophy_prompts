ok so we read epicurus's letter to menoeceus in my intro philosophy elective (the "death is nothing to us" part) and it turned into a whole argument at lunch afterward between two people in my class that i can't stop thinking about.

jake said epicurus is basically right: death can only be bad for you if theres a "you" around for it to be bad for. while you're alive it hasn't happened yet so it's not bad for you yet, and once it happens you don't exist anymore, so theres no you left for it to be bad for. so there's never actually a point where it's bad for you.

maya said that's a trick, because something can be bad for you without you being around to feel it — like if your gf cheats on you the entire relationship and you never find out, that's still bad for you, it doesn't stop being bad just because you never experienced it. death is bad the same way: its bad because of what it takes from you, not because of some experience of badness.

jake said fine, say you're right that harm doesn't need to be felt — there still has to be SOME time when it happens to you, and he doesn't see what time that could be. not while you're alive (nothing's wrong yet), not after (no you left for anything to happen to).

maya said it doesn't need one exact moment, it can just be true of your death overall that you got robbed of stuff. and she said this isn't the same as not existing before you were born, which nobody's bothered by — theres a real asymmetry, since we weigh future bad stuff way more than past bad stuff even when the past stuff was objectively worse (she brought up this hypothetical about waking up in a hospital not knowing if you already had a really painful surgery yesterday or are about to have a much less painful one tomorrow, and how most people would rather it be the one that already happened, even tho it was worse).
