Two fellow PhD students in my philosophy of mind working group got into a running argument after a seminar on the extended mind thesis, and I couldn't fully adjudicate it myself. The exchange, as best I can reconstruct it, went as follows.

(1) Owen defended the parity principle via Otto and Inga: since Otto's notebook plays exactly the functional role that Inga's biological memory plays — reliably consulted and acted on without further checking — there is no principled reason to deny that Otto's standing belief about the museum's address is partly constituted by the notebook.

(2) Reiko raised Adams and Aizawa's coupling-constitution objection: the notebook's being causally coupled to Otto's behavior doesn't make it part of his cognitive system. She added that notebook entries carry only derived, conventional content, whereas genuine cognition requires an independently motivated "mark of the cognitive" involving non-derived content, which the notebook lacks.

(3) Owen replied that the fallacy charge misfires here: the parity principle infers constitution from functional equivalence, not from mere causal contact. He added that whatever causal-informational story explains how neurons acquire "original" content might, on some naturalistic theories of content, equally apply to a reliably calibrated notebook, so the derived/non-derived line is not as sharp as Reiko assumes.

(4) Reiko shifted to the bloat worry: if reliable, trusted coupling plus functional equivalence suffices for extension, then GPS, Wikipedia, and a well-informed friend all become literal parts of one's mind whenever relied upon, which trivializes "cognitive."

(5) Owen answered that Clark and Chalmers' own "glue and trust" conditions — constant availability, easy accessibility, automatic endorsement without scrutiny, and prior conscious endorsement — already screen out most bloat cases, since casual googling or occasional advice-seeking typically fails at least one condition.

(6) Reiko's rejoinder was that this move isn't available to a consistent functionalist: functionalism is liberal about implementation by design, so adding dispositional conditions just to keep the class of extended-cognitive resources small looks motivated only by a wish to avoid bloat, not by the functionalist theory itself — so Owen must either accept the bloat or give up parity.
