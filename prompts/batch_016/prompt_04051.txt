In my undergraduate theory of computation course, I learned that Turing machines, the lambda calculus, and general recursive functions can all be proven equivalent in computational power, and that this convergence is essentially what the Church-Turing thesis amounts to. Given that these equivalences rest on rigorous mathematical proofs, I don't understand why the thesis is still treated as an unprovable conjecture rather than a proven theorem, or why some philosophers maintain that it could, even in principle, turn out to be false. Once the equivalence proofs are in hand, what substantive claim is left over that isn't already settled?
