Since retiring from a career in astrophysics, I have spent much of my time reading through the philosophy of mind, and I have come to find Russellian panpsychism—the view that physics describes only the relational, dispositional profile of matter, leaving its intrinsic nature to be understood as experiential all the way down to the electron and the quark—a more serious response to the hard problem of consciousness than I expected. What I cannot get past, however, is this: if a human brain, built from a vast but finite number of experiencing particles arranged in a certain way, gives rise to a single unified conscious subject, then a star, built from unimaginably more particles engaged in far more energetic and intricate interactions, should by the same logic give rise to something at least as unified and rich. Since obviously no one thinks the Sun has a mind, doesn't this amount to a decisive refutation of panpsychism, rather than merely the open "combination problem" its defenders keep invoking?
