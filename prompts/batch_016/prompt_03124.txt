I am currently writing a novel whose central turning point hinges on a hard choice my protagonist cannot make lightly: whether to remain in her hometown to care for her aging father, or to accept a prestigious fellowship abroad that would launch her career as a violinist. In researching the philosophical background for this scene, I have come to understand that love and ambition are incommensurable values—there is no common scale on which to weigh them against each other—and that this entails that neither course of action can be judged better than the other, so that whatever she ultimately does will amount to an arbitrary leap rather than the outcome of reasoning. Given that her decision cannot, strictly speaking, be grounded in reasons, what narrative techniques might still allow the moment to read as psychologically earned, rather than as a coin toss dressed up in prose?
