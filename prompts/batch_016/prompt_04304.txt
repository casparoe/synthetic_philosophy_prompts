I recently joined a weekly class working through Seder Kodashim, and our teacher spent a session on Maimonides' account, in the Guide for the Perplexed, of why God commanded animal sacrifice: essentially, it was a concession to wean the Israelites off the idolatrous worship they already knew, rather than an ideal mode of serving God in its own right. Since the whole rationale is to cure a need that idolatry itself created, it seems to follow that Maimonides must have held that the sacrificial order would have no place once idolatry is no longer a live temptation, so I take it he expected the rebuilt Temple of the messianic era to center on prayer alone. Given that, how should I understand our liturgy's continued petitions for the restoration of the sacrifices -- are these simply out of step with Maimonides' own considered view?
