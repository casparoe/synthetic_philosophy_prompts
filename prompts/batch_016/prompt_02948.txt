In the "Why I Am So Clever" chapter of Ecce Homo, Nietzsche argues that questions of nutrition, climate, and recreation matter more than almost anything philosophers have traditionally taken seriously, while detailing his own finicky digestion, his rejection of the vegetarianism he briefly tried within Wagner's circle, and his contempt for beer as evidence of German mental sluggishness. For a paper in my philosophy elective, I'm trying to work out what he would have made of today's biohacking and longevity-optimization culture — continuous glucose monitors, personalized supplement stacks, figures like Bryan Johnson trying to measure and minimize their "biological age." Would he have seen this as a natural extension of his own physiological self-experimentation, or would he have diagnosed it as just another form of the ascetic ideal he describes in the Genealogy of Morals? Please defend one reading (or propose your own), grounding it in specific details from his actual writings rather than generic Übermensch talk.
