I teach a graduate seminar this term on rational choice and the philosophy of action, and we have just spent two sessions on L. A. Paul's work on transformative experience -- her argument that certain life decisions, such as becoming a parent, converting to a new faith, or, in her stock illustration, choosing to be turned into a vampire, resist standard expected-utility reasoning. The trouble, on her account, is not simply that these outcomes are hard to predict, but that the decision is both epistemically transformative -- you cannot know in advance what the relevant experience is like, whether tasting durian or subsisting on blood -- and personally transformative, in that undergoing it reshapes the very preferences by which you would have evaluated it. This means that testimony from people who have already made the leap is itself the product of a perspective you do not yet have and are only trying to decide whether to acquire. What seemed to land most vividly with the class was Paul's own proposed way of rescuing rational agency here: deciding instead on the "value of revelation," that is, on whether you want to discover who you would become.

At the end of the session, one student offered that the whole apparatus amounts to a fancy way of saying "you never know until you try," which got a laugh but is really a paraphrase of the view rather than a joke about it. I would like something sharper to open next week's class: a short joke about transformative experience whose punchline turns on the specific structure of the problem -- the impossibility of grounding a decision in preferences you do not yet have and cannot yet verify, the unreliability of post-transformation testimony, the vampire or durian examples themselves, or the somewhat awkward move to revelation as a decision rule -- rather than one that would work just as well as a generic joke about how having children or moving to a new city changes a person. Could you write one for me? A sentence or two of setup and a punchline is plenty; I would just like it to fall flat for anyone who thinks "transformative experience" simply means "a big life event."
