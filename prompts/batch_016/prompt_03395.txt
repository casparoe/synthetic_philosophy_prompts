So I've been TAing an intro course on collective decision-making / social choice theory, and last week we finally got to the discursive dilemma (aka the doctrinal paradox) - you know, the classic setup with the three-judge panel deciding a contract case, where liability only holds if there was a valid contract AND the defendant breached it. Every judge is individually perfectly consistent, but if you aggregate by voting on the two premises seperately and then applying the rule (the "premise-based" procedure), you can get the exact opposite verdict from what you'd get by just voting on liability directly instead (the "conclusion-based" procedure). I keep bringing this up to people at parties expecting them to be as unsettled by it as I am, and mostly I just get blank stares.

Can you write me a joke about this? Not a generic "committees/juries are dumb" joke - I want the actual punchline to hinge on the premise-based vs. conclusion-based distinction, or on the fact that a group of totally rational, self-consistent individuals can end up collectively committed to a logically inconsistent set of judgments depending on how you count the votes. Basically, I want it to be the kind of joke that's extra funny if you actually know what the discursive dilemma is, not just generic nerd-humor with the terminology swapped in. Bonus points if it's short enough that I could actually use it as an icebreaker next time I bring this up at a party.
