Among Isaac Newton's unpublished theological manuscripts is a chronological scheme, developed over decades of studying Daniel and Revelation, in which he calculated that the 1,260-year corruption of the Church, dated from Charlemagne's coronation in AD 800, could not conclude before the year 2060. Newton was emphatic that this was not a prediction: he warned that "the folly of Interpreters has been, to foretell times and things by this Prophecy," insisting that prophecy's purpose was to confirm God's providence only after events had occurred, never to gratify curiosity about the future in advance. Yet his own prophetic timeline also held that the Jewish people's return to the land of Israel and the rebuilding of the Temple would be among the signs preceding the final events. Suppose Newton had lived to see the founding of the State of Israel in 1948, an event many later prophecy writers have treated as confirmation that the end times are already unfolding. Would Newton's own stated principles have required him to reject this kind of inference as exactly the "rash" date-setting he condemned in his contemporaries, or does his prophetic scheme itself commit him to reading 1948 as significant in a way his methodology forbids? Answer by reasoning through his specific interpretive commitments, not general impressions of his piety.
