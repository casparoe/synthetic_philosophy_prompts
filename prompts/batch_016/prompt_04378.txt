I'm taking a seminar this semester on Hume's political and economic essays, and we've just finished "Of the Balance of Trade," where he lays out what's now called the price-specie-flow mechanism: a trade surplus draws specie into a country, raising domestic prices and wages until its exports become less competitive and imports more attractive, so the surplus corrects itself (and symmetrically for a deficit). This mechanism seems to presuppose that trading nations each have their own currency, so that relative prices can adjust. It has no obvious analogue for members of a currency union like the eurozone, where a country running a persistent surplus (Germany, say) or deficit (Greece, say) cannot see its currency move against its trading partners, and the entire burden of adjustment falls instead on slow, politically costly changes to domestic wages and prices. Drawing specifically on what Hume argues in his essays on money, interest, and public credit, how do you think he would diagnose the eurozone's persistent internal imbalances, and would he consider monetary union itself to be a standing policy error by the lights of his own theory?
