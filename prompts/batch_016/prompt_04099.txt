At work, I'm responsible for forecasting how likely it is that our largest client will renew its contract, and that estimate feeds directly into our headcount and budget planning. Given how mixed the signals are this year, I want to report the likelihood as a range — realistically somewhere between 30 and 60 percent — rather than commit to a single figure that the evidence doesn't seem to support. My manager argues that this is an evasion: whatever staffing and spending decisions we ultimately make will only make sense relative to some particular number, so I'm implicitly relying on one anyway and should just state it rather than hide behind a range. I find this surprisingly hard to rebut, but it also feels too quick. Is she right that a range is really just a sharp number in disguise, or is there a genuine difference between the two?
