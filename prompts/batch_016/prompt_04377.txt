Joseph Kerman's Opera as Drama judges Mozart's Così fan tutte to be one of the composer's least dramatically coherent works, since Da Ponte's artificial, farcical plot seems unable to bear music that treats the lovers' feelings with real depth and seriousness. In On Opera, Bernard Williams starts from this same mismatch but reverses the verdict: for him, the gap between the music's emotional seriousness and the plot's blithe resolution is not a flaw but the source of the opera's unsettling truth about how social convention can override genuine feeling. Write a dialogue, set in the interval before the final scene, between a critic defending Kerman's verdict and a critic defending Williams's. Have them argue specifically about how to understand that scene, in which the two couples are reconciled within minutes of the opera's most painful revelations, each answering the other's strongest point rather than simply restating a general theory of opera — and let neither have an easy time of it.
