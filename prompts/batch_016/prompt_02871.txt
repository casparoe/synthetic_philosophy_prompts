So in my ancient phil seminar we just got through Books 1-2 of Plato's Laws and I was NOT expecting him to spend this much time praising drinking parties, especially since he trashes drunkenness pretty hard in the Republic. Here the Athenian Stranger argues that getting drunk is actually a great tool for civic education, because it's a cheap, low-stakes way to test whether someone has 'shame' (aidos) and can resist temptation -- basically the pleasure equivalent of how war tests courage against fear. But he's super strict about the conditions: there's gotta be a sober elder running the whole thing, zero wine before 18, only tasting/moderate amounts from 18 through your 30s, and you're not even supposed to get properly drunk until your pushing 40 (at which point he frames it as medicine against the crabbiness of old age). Given how specific and paternalistic that whole scheme is, what do you think Plato would actually say about the US drinking age being a flat 21 with no exceptions, and about frat/college party culture specifically? Would he count any of that as a real version of his 'testing' idea, or does the total lack of a sober authority figure plus the fact that it's mostly under-25s doing the drinking make it basically everything he warned against?
