Had a long, kind of heated argument with my friend last night about whether "you have a reason to do X" always has to connect back to something you actually care about (or would come to care about after really thinking it through), or whether some reasons apply to you no matter what you want. I was defending the first view, she had the second. Looked it up after and turns out I'd basically reinvented Bernard Williams's internalism (a reason only counts if theres a "sound deliberative route" from your existing desires/projects/loyalties - your "S" as he weirdly calls it - to the action), and she was channeling the McDowell/Korsgaard-style pushback against it.

Thing is, every explanation of this debate uses the same three examples (the gin that's secretly petrol, the guy who's awful to the people close to him and just doesn't care, Singer's kid drowning in the shallow pond), and honestly I can't tell anymore if internalism is obviously right or obviously lets monsters off the hook.

Can you invent a genuinely new example - not those three, not a thin reskin of one of them - that puts real pressure on Williams's view? And then actually give me a verdict: does internalism survive it, or does it have to bite a bullet somewhere?
