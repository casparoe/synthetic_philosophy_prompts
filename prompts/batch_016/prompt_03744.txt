Ok, weird rabbit hole I fell into: I've been reading about how committees and juries make group decisions and ran into this thing called the 'discursive dilemma.' The setup is that you can have judges who are each totally consistent on their own (their view on the final verdict always matches their view on the sub-issues), but if the group votes on each sub-issue seperately and then works out what that implies for the verdict, they get a different answer than if they'd all just voted on the verdict directly. Seems to me like the obvious fix is: don't bother voting on the sub-issues at all, just have everyone vote on the bottom-line verdict directly, since that's all that legally matters anyway. Wouldn't that make the whole 'dilemma' disappear for good? Feels too easy, so i'm guessing I'm missing something obvious.
