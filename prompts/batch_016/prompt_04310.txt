As a high school junior serving on my district's student advisory council, I'm drafting a statement about a proposed 'early warning system' that would use grades, attendance, and demographic data to flag students at risk of dropping out so counselors can prioritize outreach. While preparing, I came across the impossibility results in algorithmic fairness from Kleinberg, Mullainathan, and Raghavan, and separately from Chouldechova, which show that a risk score generally cannot be simultaneously calibrated across demographic groups and have equal false-positive and false-negative rates across those groups, unless the groups' base rates are equal or the prediction is perfect. Assuming our district's tool will fall short of at least one of these properties, what are the most important implications of this result for the decision our board should make, and for how the system should be designed or governed if it is approved?
