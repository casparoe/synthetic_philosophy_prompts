Here's roughly how the argument went:

2. Mira pushed back two ways. Nothing in the theorem, he said, promises anything once other people start adapting to the fact that you're looking.

4. Mira said sure, but that's exactly her complaint: once you carve out "must be free," "must not change anyone's incentives," "must be used correctly," there's barely any real decision left the theorem actually governs, so citing it to justify a concrete policy (always extend the canary) claims way more support from the math than the math gives you.
