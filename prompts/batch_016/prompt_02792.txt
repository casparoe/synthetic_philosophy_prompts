ok so I've been on an IIT kick again and theres two critiques of it that I feel like never get put next to each other, even though they're both clearly going for the jugular. First theres Aaronson's old "Unconscious Expander" post, where he builds a toy system (a Vandermonde matrix at first, then a sparser version using an expander graph wired up with plain XOR gates) whose phi is provably way bigger than any sane estimate of a human brain's phi, despite the thing obviously just doing glorified error-correction and nothing remotely mind-like. Then theres the separate "unfolding argument" (Doerig et al), which says that since any recurrent network can always be unfolded into a feedforward one with the exact same input-output behavior, causal-structure theories like IIT end up either false or just unfalsifiable, since the two nets look identical from the outside but IIT insists only the recurrent one is conscious.

Can you actually compare these two properly instead of just restating them - like what specific commitment of IIT is each one exploiting, are they secretly the same underlying objection wearing different clothes, or genuinely different attacks, and which one is actually the bigger problem for Tononi's theory? Give me a real verdict, don't just hedge and summarize both sides.
