Since I retired from the lab a few years ago (35 years of physical chemistry, mostly reaction kinetics) I've been filling the time with philosophy of physics, and I've gotten stuck on something about the arrow of time that I don't think gets enough air outside specialist circles. I wrote up my thinking as a page of notes for my old physics book club, and before I show my face there I'd like someone to tell me if I'm about to embarrass myself.

Here's the gist. Boltzmann's answer to Loschmidt's reversal objection and Zermelo's recurrence objection was never "entropy literally can't decrease," it was "a decrease is so fantastically improbable you'll never see one in practice, so relax." Fine. But apparently, prompted by an assistant of his (Schütz, if I'm remembering the name right), Boltzmann took this further in the 1890s and floated the idea that the whole universe just sits in equilibrium almost all the time, and we happen to exist during one of the rare local dips away from it, because creatures like us can only exist during such a dip. Cute bit of proto-anthropic reasoning. My worry is its a loaded gun Boltzmann left pointed at his own head. If "expect to find yourself in a fluctuation" is licensed only by "fluctuations that produce observers are what they are," the same logic says you should expect to be the cheapest, smallest, shortest-lived fluctuation capable of having any thought at all - a single freak brain with false memories of galaxies and lab notebooks - rather than a fluctuation big enough to make an entire galaxy with a consistent 13-billion-year history behind it. That's just the modern "Boltzmann brain" business in Victorian dress. So the very move that rescues statistical mechanics from Loschmidt and Zermelo, pushed one inch further, seems to undercut every memory and inference you have, including the ones that got you to the argument in the first place. And swapping in the newer move of just positing a low-entropy "Past Hypothesis" at the big bang as a brute boundary condition dodges Boltzmann's specific version, but doesn't obviously save you once a cosmological constant is back in the picture, since then there's an eternal future for exactly the same kind of fluctuation to happen again near the end of things instead of the beginning.

What I can't settle myself is whether this is a real problem or whether I'm just abusing "typicality" reasoning (a term I only learned last month) the way people abuse the gambler's fallacy - applying frequencies somewhere there's no principled measure to reason with in the first place, which would make the whole freak-brain worry a category error dressed up as a paradox. Could you go through it: first flag anywhere I've mangled the Loschmidt/Zermelo/Boltzmann/Schütz history, then tell me straight whether the "loaded gun" bit is a good argument, a well-worn one I've reinvented badly, or just confused, and finally - seperate question - whether this is even an interesting angle for a book club or old news dressed up. Don't be gentle on my account, I had forty years of journal referees for that.
