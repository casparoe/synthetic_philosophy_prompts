My neighbor Owen and his partner Farah have been arguing for weeks about his EV's dashboard "estimated miles of range remaining," and since neither of them will let it go I said I'd ask around.

(1) Owen: "I've watched this thing for two years. Whenever it says 40 miles left I can always drive close to 40 before it hits zero, and whenever it says 10 I get close to 10. It's never once misled me — that's plenty of evidence its accurate."
(2) Farah: "That's not a real check. The only reason you believe you 'got close to 40 miles' on those drives is the same range number ticking down as you go. You've never once run the battery to zero against some independent measure."
(3) Owen: "The odometer and my home charger's kWh readout back it up too — the miles-per-kWh implied by my trips is stable and matches the car's spec sheet. That's a totally different source of data, not the range estimate itself."
(4) Farah: "That only tells you total energy added, not whether the estimate is right at any given moment. A crude algorithm that just multiplies a constant by battery percentage, with zero correction for hills, weather, or driving style, would look exactly as 'stable' as an accurate one. Consistent isn't the same as correct."
(5) Owen: "That's an unfalsifiable standard though. By your logic nobody could ever trust a brand new instrument the first time, since any check either loops back through the instrument itself or through some other measure that needs its own validation, forever. We all trusted our first speedometer somehow."

Neither of them budged. Can you go through moves (1) through (5) and say who's got the better of the argument at each step — not just an overall winner — and flag anything you think they're both missing that a philosopher would bring up? Also, is there an actual non-circular check Owen could run here, or does Farah's standard really collapse into total skepticism about ever trusting an instrument, like Owen claims at the end?
