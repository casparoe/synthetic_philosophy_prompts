Coherence arguments—the Dutch-book and money-pump arguments for the axioms of expected-utility theory—are often cited in AI safety discussions as proof that any sufficiently capable agent must eventually act as an expected-utility maximizer, and therefore will be "goal-directed" in exactly the sense that makes a misaligned system dangerous. Since this is supposed to follow from mathematics rather than from any contingent fact about how today's systems are built, why do some AI safety researchers continue to treat the goal-directedness of advanced AI as an open empirical question rather than as something coherence arguments have already settled?
