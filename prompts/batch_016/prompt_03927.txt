I've recently been rereading the "Why I Am So Clever" section of Nietzsche's Ecce Homo for a small reading group, and what struck us wasn't any of the usual material about morality or the death of God, but how much space he devotes to highly specific opinions about nutrition, climate, and daily routine — going so far as to suggest that how one eats matters more for humanity's wellbeing than any question of theology ever has. There's something quite funny about the philosopher famous for demanding that we live beyond good and evil turning out to have such firm, fussy opinions about which regional cuisines supposedly dull the intellect, how large one's meals should be, and what a sedentary lifestyle does to a person's character.

I'd like you to write a short joke that gets some real mileage out of this contrast between the scale of Nietzsche's philosophical ambitions and the sheer specificity of his actual prescriptions about diet and daily life. For the joke to work, the punchline should turn on some genuine detail of what he argued in that text, rather than being a generic joke about picky eaters, or yet another riff on the Übermensch, eternal recurrence, or "God is dead" that has nothing to do with health or nutrition as such. Ideally, someone who has actually read that section of Ecce Homo should recognize the specific reference and find it funny, while the joke should still be at least somewhat legible to someone who only knows Nietzsche's reputation in the broadest strokes. A one- or two-line joke is fine, and so is a short setup-and-punchline structure — whichever serves the material better.
