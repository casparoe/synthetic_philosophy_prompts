I'm drafting a novel whose protagonist is, by design, unflinchingly courageous, yet a string of narrative accidents means she is never actually placed in a situation where that courage must be shown. A philosopher friend tells me this turns on the distinction, in the metaphysics literature, between 'finkish' dispositions and 'masked' dispositions -- two different kinds of counterexample to the simple conditional analysis of dispositions, roughly the idea that to be disposed to respond in a certain way to a stimulus is just to be such that one would so respond if the stimulus occurred. Could you explain, with some precision, what distinguishes a finkish disposition from a masked one, why neither is just a disguised case of lacking the disposition in the first place, and which -- if either -- best fits a trait like courage that I intend to be genuine even though it is never demonstrated on the page?
