Hey, I'm putting together fiction prompts for my death & dying seminar and want a sample to show students what I have in mind. Could you write me a short story (800ish words) set inside the waiting period of a DCD protocol -- vent's withdrawn, heart stops, and the team just waits a few minutes before they can declare death and go in for the organs. What's always gotten me is that this "circulatory" standard is so much thinner than the whole-brain one: the wait is calibrated to when the heart won't restart *on its own*, not to when it couldn't be restarted *at all*, so "irreversible" quietly becomes "permanent" right at the exact moment the stakes are highest. I don't want some maudlin unplug-grandpa story -- I want something that actually sits inside that window and makes the reader feel that sleight of hand happening in real time. Whoever's telling the story, make it someone you wouldn't expect (not the grieving spouse, please, thats too easy).
