This has been on my mind a lot since my grandma's dementia got worse a few months back. My aunt set her up with a notebook system to help her function semi-independently — appointments, who visited, whether shes eaten, little notes like "you already called mom, she's fine." She checks it constantly and trusts whatever's written there completely, no question asked. Then last week a phone scammer almost talked her into writing down a fake "amount she owes" as just another fact for later, which is honestly what got me spiraling on this.

I took one phil class years ago that covered the extended mind thesis (Clark and Chalmers, Otto and his notebook, i think?) and cant stop thinking about it watching this happen for real. Could you write a short story around a setup like this that actually sits in the hard parts instead of doing the neat "the notebook is her mind" version? I want it to wrestle with the coupling-constitution fallacy thing — that her relying on it automatically doesn't prove it's really part of her cognition instead of just being causally hooked up to it — and use the scammer angle to get at whether a corrupted entry is just an ordinary false belief or whether it exposes that the notebook's content was never really hers to begin with (something about derived vs non-derived content, might be misremembering the exact term). No need for a tidy ending, i'd rather it stay genuinely unresolved.
