Ok so this happened at my job a few weeks back and I still can't stop turning it over in my head. I'm a backend engineer at a mid-size startup, and while digging through old logs for an unrelated incident, I noticed a bug a teammate wrote something like 8 months ago that had been quietly leaking some user PII this whole time (nothing catastrophic, but still bad). I flagged it immediately, we disclosed to the affected users, patched it, wrote the whole postmortem, everything you're supposed to do. People were genuinely praising me for "doing the right thing even though nobody would've known," and my manager even brought it up in my perf review as an integrity thing.

Here's what's actually been bugging me though: our postmortem process is supposed to be blameless, but everybody quietly knows that if it ever came out later that you found something like this and sat on it, you'd be toast, and maybe the company too given the legal exposure. So I honestly cant tell anymore if I flagged the bug because I actually cared about the users who were affected, or because some part of my brain was just doing risk management for future-me. Both motives were probably active in my head at the same time, and theres no way for me to rerun the situation with only one of them present to see what I'd have actually done.

I don't have any background in philosophy or ethics or whatever, so maybe Im overthinking this, but I brought it up with a couple friends and got two totally opposite takes. One said the motive is irrelevant, a good outcome is a good outcome, full stop. The other said if I'd have stayed quiet in a world with truly zero chance of it ever surfacing, then what I did wasn't really integrity, just insurance that happened to look like integrity from the outside. So now I want a third opinion: is either of them right, and is there even a fact of the matter about what my "real" motive was, given that the two reasons for doing the same thing are this tangled together?
