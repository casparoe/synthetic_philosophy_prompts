Ok, this is something that's been bugging me for my phil of probability seminar. Bertrand's chord paradox and van Fraassen's cube factory case usually get taught as basically the same lesson about the principle of indifference: switch your parametrization, get a different uniform distribution, get a contradiction. But Jaynes thought he'd dissolved Bertrand's paradox by requiring the answer be invariant under the symmetries actually built into the physical randomizing procedure (translation, rotation, scale). Does that move even have an analogue for the cube factory case? Compare the two paradoxes for me and tell me whether their really the same problem in different clothes, or whether the cube factory is actually the harder case for the principle of indifference, precisely because it has nothing like Jaynes' symmetries to appeal to.
