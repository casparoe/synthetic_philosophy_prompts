ok so i was rereading turing's 1950 paper "computing machinery and intelligence" (the one that has the turing test in it) for a side project, and i totally forgot he's got this whole section near the end where he takes ESP dead serious. he flat out says the statistical evidence for telepathy is "overwhelming," and even works through a concrete example: a telepathic guy in the imitation game correctly names the suit of a hidden card 130 times out of 400, vs the machine getting only ~104 right by chance, so the interrogator can unfairly tell them apart. his fix is sticking both contestants in a "telepathy-proof room" — and he seriously worries the interrogator's psychokinetic powers could mess with the machine's random number generator lol.

from what i can tell, the "overwhelming" evidence turings talking about was mainly Samuel Soal's card-guessing experiments with a subject named Basil Shackleton, wich were basically the gold standard of parapsychology research at the time. except in 1978 a statistician named Betty Markwick found that Soal had hand-altered digits in his target sheets to fake the hits.

curious what you think turing would say if he learned both that his specific evidence was fabricated AND that another 70 years of parapsychology since has failed to produce anything that reliably replicates. would he just quietly drop objection 9 from the paper, or does getting fooled by "overwhelming statistics" here tell us something about how much weight we should put on his judgement in the rest of it?
