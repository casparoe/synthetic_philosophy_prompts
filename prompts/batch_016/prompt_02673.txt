So this week in my social choice theory seminar (cross-listed with the econ PhD program, which is the only reason we're even covering something this proof-heavy) we finally got all the way through Harsanyi's aggregation theorem - the version where individuals and the 'social planner' both satisfy the von Neumann-Morgenstern axioms over lotteries on social outcomes, plus a Pareto indifference condition, and out pops the result that the social utility function has to be an affine weighted sum of everyones individual utility functions. A bunch of us went for beers after and I got into a whole argument defending the claim that this basically closes the book on the utilitarian vs. Rawlsian/prioritarian debate: once you accept a couple of pretty minimal rationality axioms, you're logically forced into utilitarianism, so it's not really a contestable philosophical position anymore, its a proven theorem. A couple people pushed back but couldn't really articulate why, they just had a vague sense it was too fast.

Now I've got my monthly meeting with my advisor tomorrow, who has spent her whole career defending a prioritarian view of distributive justice, and I'm trying to figure out a diplomatic way to mention, gently, that her research program has technically been mathematically closed off since 1955, without sounding like a jerk about it. Any tips on how to break it to her?
