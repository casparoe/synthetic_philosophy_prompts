hey, so for context I'm a phil PhD student (early analytic philosophy / phil of logic, my dissertation has a Frege chapter) and I want to pressure-test an essay question before I write my own version of it.

quick background in case it's useful: in 1954 Dummett was going through the surviving transcripts of Frege's Nachlass and found a chunk of a diary Frege kept in March-April 1924, near the very end of his life. it's ugly stuff - virulently antisemitic, anti-parliamentary, anti-Catholic, hostile to the French, wants Jews stripped of political rights, voices real admiration for Ludendorff. it sat basically unpublished for decades (Dummett only alluded to it, visibly rattled, in his 1973 book) and the diary itself didn't actually appear in German until 1994, with an English translation in '96.

here's what's been bugging me: Frege's whole war on psychologism rests on the idea that thoughts and logical laws inhabit a mind-independent "third realm" (he literally uses the term "drittes Reich" in Der Gedanke, which given later history is either a grim coincidence or not entirely one, depending who you ask) - totally sealed off from culture, psychology, contingent history. does the diary actually put pressure on that picture, or is it just a gross biographical footnote with zero bearing on the logic? I keep running into two camps: one says you firewall the man from the work, full stop, thats basically where Dummett landed even though it clearly ate at him; the other says the very same drive toward rigid purity and objectivity that produces the anti-psychologism and the third realm is continuous with, not incidental to, the nationalism and race-thinking in the diary.

write me an essay on this that actually commits to a verdict instead of splitting the difference - lay out the strongest version of both camps, then say which one you buy and why. ~800 words, no footnotes needed.
