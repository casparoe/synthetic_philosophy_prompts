Kind of a random one, but it came out of an argument in my team's slack (don't ask — we were originally talking about AI timelines and someone brought up anthropic reasoning). I ended up spending way too much of my weekend reading about the Doomsday Argument, the idea that since your birth rank is presumably pretty "typical" among everyone who will ever be born, you should update towards humanity ending sooner then you'd otherwise think. I'm a backend dev with zero formal philosophy training, so a lot of this is very second-hand from blogs and wikipedia at 1am, but I got hooked on the anthropic-probability angle since its basically a sampling/Bayes problem, which is more my territory.

The part that really got me is that the doomsday logic depends entirely on this "self-sampling assumption" (reason like you're a random draw from your reference class of observers), and there's a rival "self-indication assumption" that's supposed to cancel the doomsday update out completely by making you favor theories that predict more observers exist in the first place. Except then SIA runs into this "presumptuous philosopher" thing, where two physicists have narrowed a theory of everything down to one predicting a trillion trillion observers and one predicting a trillion trillion trillion, and a strict SIA-follower apparently gets to "know" the second theory is right without even running the experiment — which feels like a genuinely bad bullet to bite. Meanwhile SSA has its own headache picking the reference class to begin with (all humans? all observers ever? Bostrom's own fix apparently swaps in "observer-moments" instead). I don't fully trust my own summary of any of this, but I think I've got the general shape of it right.

What I'd love is a dialogue between two people who end up arguing about this at the bar after some rationalist meetup — one who's fully bought into SSA and accepts the doomsday conclusion, one who's fully bought into SIA and thinks doomsday reasoning is just a mistake. I don't want either side to be a pushover: give the SSA person an actual good comeback to the presumptuous philosopher problem (or have them bite that bullet in a way that's genuinely defensible, not just hand-waved), and give the SIA person a real answer to the reference-class objection rather than dodging it. I want them responding to each other's specific arguments, not just restating their own view louder each time, and if they can't fully resolve things by the end, I'd at least like them to pin down exactly where the disagreement lives — priors over cosmological theories, what counts as a legitimate reference class, or whether this whole style of anthropic reasoning even makes sense to begin with.
