A friend and I recently had a long argument about whether my monthly donations to a global health charity actually do more good than harm. She pointed out that saving a life doesn't just add one person to the world: it changes how many children that person will eventually have, how local resources and economies are used, and countless further downstream events branching out indefinitely, none of which either of us has any way of tracing. Her conclusion was that, since the long-run balance of these effects is entirely opaque to us, I have no real grounds for believing the donation does more good than harm overall, only that it looks good within a time horizon we happen to be able to see. I wasn't satisfied with this, but I struggled to explain why our uncertainty about the long-term effects shouldn't simply cancel out my confidence in the short-term ones. Was my friend right that this kind of unpredictability undermines the case for donating, or is there a principled way to defend the choice despite it?
