I've been on a bit of an L.A. Paul kick lately (the Transformative Experience book, with her whole vampire thought experiment) and it collided in my head with an old LessWrong post about stream entry / "awakening" in meditation, where the author basically argues that once it happens there's no going back, and that people on the other side of it usually can't really explain what it was like to people who haven't done it. That struck me as a way more interesting instance of Paul's problem than the standard "should I have a kid" example, because a lot of contemplative traditions basically build the epistemic transformation into the pitch, and its not clear the "you" who'd supposedly benefit from the change is even continuous with the "you" who has to decide whether to spend ten thousand hours on a cushion to find out. None of the usual outs - trusting other people's testimony, going with your gut, or Paul's own move of just valuing "revelation" for its own sake - feel like they actually solve the decision problem instead of just relabeling it.

Could you write me a short story (a page or two, doesn't need to be longer) about a character facing a decision like this - something that's simultaneously epistemically transformative (they can't know what's on the other side until they're there) and personally transformative (the self who'd do the evaluating afterward won't be the same self doing the choosing now)? Doesn't have to literally be about meditation, and honestly I'd love it more if it wasn't - a sci-fi setup, an invented ritual, an AI or upload deciding whether to let its own values get rewritten, whatever lets you dramatize the problem cleanly. Mainly I don't want a tidy ending, no "and it turned out to be worth it" or "and they regretted it," since both quietly assume there's a stable narrator left at the end to deliver the verdict. If I finish it not even sure the character counts as the "same person" who made the choice, you've nailed it.
