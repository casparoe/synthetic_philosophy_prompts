Roughly it went like this:

(1) Kate said the argument basically finishes off moral realism: natural selection built our moral sense to promote survival and reproduction, not to detect some mind-independent moral reality, so if realism is true it'd be a wild coincidence that evolution happened to land us on the truth anyway — and beliefs that are that coincidental don't deserve our trust.

(2) Femi said that move proves too much: our number sense and basic logical instincts were shaped by selection for fitness too (nobody's ancestors got eaten for being bad at predicate logic), yet nobody thinks this makes "2+2=4" unjustified. (3) Kate said math and ethics aren't in the same boat: arithmetic truths are necessary, there's no possible world where 2+2=5, so any halfway reliable counting process was bound to land on the truth no matter what selection was "aiming" at. (4) Femi answered that this just pushes the problem back a step: plenty of realists think core moral truths are necessary too (that gratuitous cruelty is wrong in every possible world, say), and if that's right then Kate's own "no possible world where it's false" move saves ethics exactly the way it saves arithmetic — so the disanalogy only works if she's already assuming moral facts are contingent, which most serious moral realists deny anyway.
