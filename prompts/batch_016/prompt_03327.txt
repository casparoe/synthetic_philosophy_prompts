So this happened at work and I can't stop thinking about it. We just rolled out one of those employee monitoring tools (tracks screen activity, idle time, that sort of thing) and it turned into a whole debate at lunch between two of the backend engineers on my team, Raj and Mei.

Raj said its not actually a privacy problem because HR only ever sees aggregated weekly stats, not raw footage or individual keystrokes, and nobody is literally sitting there watching your screen - so nothing about you has really been "seen" by anyone. Mei said that's beside the point: the moment your activity is being captured and stored somewhere, your losing control over it, and that loss of control is the privacy violation, whether or not a human eyeball ever looks at it. Raj came back with: ok but by that logic basically everything is a privacy violation - your ISP logs your connection, the badge reader logs when you enter the building, the hallway camera records an empty hallway - none of that is "seen" by anyone either, so if mere storage-without-viewing counts as a violation, the word privacy stops meaning anything. Mei's comeback was that not all unviewed data collection is equivalent - this tool specifically tracks your ongoing work behavior for the purpose of evaluating you, which creates a power imbalance between you and the company, and that's a difference in kind from a badge log, not just a difference in how likely someone is to actually look at it.

They went a couple more rounds after that and never really landed anywhere. Can you go through it move by move and say who's got the better of it at each point (not just who wins overall), and point out anything important that neither of them seems to be noticing?
