I'm a philosophy and cognitive science double major, and in my seminar on philosophy of mind this term we just spent a week on William Ramsey's "job description challenge" and how it holds up against recent work in mechanistic interpretability. Ramsey's point, from Representation Reconsidered, is roughly that cognitive science reaches for the word "representation" too easily: a thermostat's bimetallic strip, a spark plug, and an edge-detecting neuron in V1 are all internal states that reliably respond to some external condition and causally feed forward into behavior concerning it, yet nobody wants to say a thermostat represents the temperature the way a belief represents the world. On his view, something only earns the title "representation" if it does further work that mere causal mediators don't -- it has to function as a stand-in that the system itself exploits, with independent conditions of getting things wrong, rather than just a state that reliably tracks its target and causes appropriate downstream effects.

Our professor then put this challenge to a case Ramsey never had to consider. In the Othello-GPT work, a transformer trained only to predict legal next-moves in the board game Othello turned out to internally track the whole current board state -- first shown with a complex nonlinear probe by Kenneth Li's team, then, more surprisingly, with a simple linear probe by Neel Nanda, using "mine/theirs" rather than absolute black/white directions in the model's activations. Crucially, editing those directions causally and predictably changes which move the model goes on to predict, tracking the edited board rather than the original one. Two people in my cohort, Mia and Theo, have been arguing about what this shows ever since class ended, in a long email thread I've been half-following: Mia thinks the intervention evidence is exactly what the job description challenge demands, so refusing to call this a representation now is just moving the goalposts; Theo thinks it's still only a fancier receptor -- the "correct" board state is fixed by the experimenters' labeling scheme rather than by anything the network itself is answerable to, and besides, Ramsey never denied that receptors could be causally efficacious, only that efficacy alone was enough for representation.
