A production outage at my company, caused by an engineer's rushed config change, has split my team into two camps. Half want the postmortem to name the engineer as the cause and note it in their next performance review. The other half call this unfair: the engineer only acted the way their experience, temperament, and judgment led them to act, and they never chose to have that particular experience, temperament, or judgment in the first place -- it was shaped by their upbringing and circumstances, in a chain stretching back indefinitely. Taken seriously, this seems to imply that nobody ever really originates their own choices, so no one, anywhere, truly deserves praise or blame for anything. I find this oddly hard to refute, even though it also seems too extreme to actually act on. Who has the better argument here, and what should it mean for how we run postmortems?
