My wife and I are in the middle of an IVF cycle and just got back genetic screening results on our four viable embryos. Beyond the standard test for chromosomal abnormalities, the clinic offers an add-on panel that scores each embryo's genetic risk for things like heart disease and certain cancers, and by those scores one embryo looks meaningfully healthier than the one our doctor would otherwise recommend transferring first. My wife believes that once this information exists, we're ethically obligated to transfer the lower-risk embryo, since choosing any other one would mean knowingly bringing a worse-off child into the world. Something about that argument doesn't sit right with me, though: whichever embryo we transfer becomes its own distinct person, so we wouldn't be making one child healthier or sicker by picking among them, we'd be deciding which of several different possible children gets to exist at all, and the ones we don't choose simply never live rather than living worse lives. Is my wife right that we're obligated to always transfer whichever embryo has the best odds, or is there a real problem with that reasoning that I'm just struggling to put into words?
