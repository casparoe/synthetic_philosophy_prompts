ok so i went down a rabbit hole of william lane craig debates a while back (dont judge me) and got kind of stuck on his favorite move on the problem of evil / divine sovereignty: Molinism, the idea that God has "middle knowledge" of every true counterfactual about what any possible free creature would do in any possible circumstance, logically prior to deciding which world to create. it's basically a theory of counterfactuals of freedom, which honestly feels like it should interest the counterfactual-reasoning crowd here even if you don't care about the theology at all.

the part i can't get past is the grounding objection (Adams, then Hasker) -- the claim that these counterfactuals can't be true because there's nothing to make them true. no Peter, no circumstance C, nothing in the world yet, for "if Peter were in C he'd freely deny Christ" to be grounded in, especially since by definition it has to hold independently of God's will AND prior to the creature ever existing. Flint and Freddoso have replies to this, Craig thinks the whole objection is overblown, but everything i've read just catalogs the moves on both sides without ever landing anywhere.

can you write an essay that actually lands somewhere? lay out the strongest version of the grounding objection, the best Molinist responses to it, and then tell me who wins and why. i want an actual verdict, not a "reasonable people disagree" cop-out -- a qualified verdict like "it survives if you reject truthmaker maximalism, otherwise not" totally counts, i just don't want you to duck the question.
