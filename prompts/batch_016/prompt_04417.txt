I spent most of my working life as an entomologist studying flash communication in fireflies, and the case that always fascinated me most was the "femme fatale" Photuris females who mimic the flash-reply codes of Photinus females for the sole purpose of luring Photinus males in close enough to eat them. Since retiring I've been getting into philosophy of mind, mostly because my grand-daughter is doing a cognitive science PhD and keeps forwarding me papers, and I recently fell down a rabbit hole on teleosemantics — the Millikan/Papineau/Neander idea that you can naturalize representational content by tying it to whatever a signal-detecting mechanism was selected by evolution to track, rather than treating "aboutness" as some unanalyzable primitive that a theory just has to take for granted.

Here's what's been bugging me for weeks now. On that story, a male Photinus's flash-response system gets to count as representing something (roughly "receptive female here") because it has the proper function of steering him toward mates, and that selected function is supposedly what fixes the content. But the whole Photuris con only survives, generation after generation, because it reliably trips that exact same machinery in exactly the way a real female would — the "misfire" isn't rare noise in the system, it's the routine, selected-for outcome that keeps an entire predatory lineage in business. That seems to sit pretty uneasily with the idea that content gets nailed down by whatever the mechanism was selected to track, because you could just as well say its function has quietly drifted, or that once a population has stable femme-fatale predation baked into it, there's no clean fact left about which signal source the machinery is even "supposed" to be locking onto anymore.

Could you talk me through the most important implications of teleosemantics for a case like this? I'm especially curious whether the theory actually delivers a determinate verdict on whether the male's flash response still has any content once the mimics are a permanent fixture of his environment, whether getting duped by a Photuris just counts as the content being false on that occasion, or whether teleosemantics gets pushed toward some more relativized, population-level answer that a plainer causal or informational theory of content wouldn't need. And selfishly, I'd love to know what it implies for how much weight I should have put, back when I was still publishing, on the standard ethological habit of saying a signal "represents" or "encodes" something — was that ever more than a convenient façon de parler? I never had formal philosophy training beyond one undergrad class forty-odd years ago, so please don't dumb it down on my account; I'd honestly rather wrestle with the real debate then get a watered-down version of it.
