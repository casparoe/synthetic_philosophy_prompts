So we just finished the social choice unit in my game theory class, where we covered Arrow's impossibility theorem one lecture and then the Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem the next, and I can't shake the feeling that GS is basically just Arrow's theorem wearing a different costume. Arrow says any rule that aggregates individual preference rankings into a single social ranking has to be a dictatorship unless it violates unrestricted domain, Pareto, or independence of irrelevant alternatives (IIA) - and GS says any rule that just picks a single winner has to be a dictatorship unless it's manipulable. Our professor even mentioned offhand that you can prove GS by basically converting the choice function into a full ranking and then invoking Arrow directly on it. So if the second theorem basically falls out of the first, why do people talk about Gibbard and Satterthwaite like they discovered something genuinely new twenty-odd years later, instead of it just being a quick corollary buried in a footnote of Arrow's original paper?

I brought this up with a friend from the class (she's a math major) and she insists I'm missing something about why "strategy-proof" and "independent of irrelevant alternatives" aren't actually the same idea just described in two different vocabularies, but neither of us could actually pin down what that something is. Would appreciate an actual explanation of whether these theorems are doing genuinely seperate conceptual work, or if it's mostly a historical accident that they get taught as two landmark results instead of one theorem plus a footnote.
