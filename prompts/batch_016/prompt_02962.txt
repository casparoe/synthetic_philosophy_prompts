My father passed away last year, and the will he left behind includes one clause about an old family property that's worded ambiguously enough that it can reasonably be read two different ways. Depending on which reading is correct, either my sister or I ends up with a significantly larger share. We've been going back and forth about it for months now, each of us convinced that our own reading is obviously the more natural one, and neither of us willing to budge.

What's been bothering me is something I've read about over the years, just out of personal curiosity: that when someone stands to gain from believing a particular thing, their confidence in that belief often isn't a very reliable sign that it's actually true, even when they're not consciously trying to deceive themselves. I know this applies to me as much as to my sister. I have a real financial stake in my interpretation being correct, so in the abstract I should expect that at least some of my certainty is coming from that stake rather than from the argument itself. But when I actually sit down and go through my reasoning about the clause line by line, it still feels completely solid, and I can't point to any actual flaw in it. What's strange is that if my sister made exactly the argument I'm making, but for a reading that favored her, I'd immediately suspect she was just seeing what she wanted to see. When it's my own argument, though, it doesn't feel any different from the inside. I mentioned all this to a couple of friends, and they mostly just said something like "go with your gut," which doesn't really address what's bothering me.

So I'd like a clearer way of thinking about this. Should I actually lower my confidence in my own interpretation simply because I have this abstract reason to distrust it, even though I can't find anything wrong with the reasoning behind it? Or would that be a strange, almost self-defeating thing to do, given that the very argument I'd be discounting still seems sound on its own terms every time I examine it? More generally, I'd like to understand how much weight a person is supposed to give their own convictions in situations like this, where they have good reason to think their judgment might be compromised but no specific objection to point to.
