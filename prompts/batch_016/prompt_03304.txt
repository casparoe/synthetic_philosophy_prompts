I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have long wondered why online discussion feels so weightless: enormous amounts of "public opinion" get produced while no actual person seems answerable for any of it. Reading on my own, I came across Kierkegaard's notion of "leveling" in his writing on the present age, where he argues that the press manufactures "the public" as an abstraction, a phantom held together only by idle talk and envy, letting individuals hide behind an anonymous collective without ever being personally responsible for what it produces.

Later I came across Habermas's account of the bourgeois public sphere, which treats much the same raw material — private individuals, newspapers, salons, coffee houses — as the historical birthplace of a genuinely rational public opinion capable of checking political power, even though he later concedes that this sphere becomes "refeudalized" once commercial mass media and public relations take it over.

So these two thinkers start from a strikingly similar picture of how a media-formed public comes into being, yet end up almost opposite on whether it could ever be a legitimate site of collective reasoning. Please write a dialogue between a committed Kierkegaardian and a committed Habermasian who work through this disagreement directly, each pressing the other on the weakest point in their own view rather than simply restating it.
