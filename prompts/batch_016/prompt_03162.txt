So I'm taking a seminar on existentialism this semester and we just finished the "bad faith" chapter in Being and Nothingness (the waiter, the woman on the date, all that), and something's been bugging me since. Isn't bad faith basically just Freud's idea of repression dressed up in fancier language? Like, one part of you hides some unwanted truth from another part of you, so the second part can go on acting like it doesn't know — that's the unconcious/ego split in a nutshell, and it seems to me that's exactly the structure Sartre is describing too. I brought this up in my study group and everyone insisted Sartre's whole point was to reject the Freudian unconscious, but nobody could actually explain how bad faith is different from repression if that's not what it is.

Assuming bad faith really is just repression under a different name, here's what I'm actually trying to figure out: if someone wanted to get out of bad faith and become more "authentic" in Sartre's sense, would that just mean going to therapy and doing the standard Freudian work of making the unconscious conscious? Or does Sartre think you'd need to do something else, even though the underlying mechanism he's describing is basically the same as what Freud already described?
