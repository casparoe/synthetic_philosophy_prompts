So I had this whole argument with my friend last night about, of all people, Gottlob Frege. Turns out he kept a diary in the last year of his life (1924) that's full of genuinely ugly stuff -- wants Jews stripped of political rights and preferably expelled, rants against the Weimar parliament, hero-worships Ludendorff -- and his own Nachlass editors quietly left it out when they published the rest of his unpublished writings. Apparently Dummett knew about it for decades (theres a letter to Russell from the 50s where he mentions it) before the full text finally got published in the 90s, and admitted reading it shook him.

My friend's take: total non-issue, you judge the Begriffsschrift and the sense/reference stuff on the arguments alone, dragging in his private politics is a genetic fallacy and basically just moralizing. My take: theres at least a serious case that his hardline anti-psychologism -- thoughts are objective, mind-independent, not up for grabs depending on who's thinking them -- isn't so obviously unconnected to a mind that also couldn't stand pluralism or democratic disagreement. We went back and forth for like an hour and got nowhere.

Can you write a dialogue between the two sides that actually earns its conclusion? Both people equally sharp, really responding to each other's best moves instead of talking past each other -- see if they can work out whether there's a real connection here or if I'm just reaching.
