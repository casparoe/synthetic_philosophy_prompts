I'm a retired condensed-matter physicist, and since retiring I've been reading my way through philosophy of mind — turns out the "what is matter, really" question that used to needle me on the job connects to it more than I expected. The bit that hooked me is the Russellian point that physics only ever gives you the structure and dispositions of things (how they're arranged, how they behave, what they'd do under such-and-such circumstances) and never actually says what any of it is like "from the inside," if it's like anything at all. Panpsychists use that gap to argue the intrinsic nature filling in the structure might just be experience, all the way down to whatever sits at the bottom of physics. My wife thinks I've completely lost it, going from solid-state theory to this, but I can at least follow that part of the argument.

Where I get stuck is the combination problem. I went back and read James's old dozen-men-dozen-words bit from the Principles of Psychology, which is a nice intuition pump, but then I started in on the more recent stuff — Goff on "phenomenal bonding," the "subject-summing" business — and I honestly can't tell if anyone's made real progress versus just giving the mystery a fancier name and moving on. There's a meta-worry I can't shake either: isnt the whole selling point of panpsychism that it avoids the hard problem's brute, unexplained jump from non-experiential stuff to experience? But then the combination problem just reinserts a structurally identical unexplained jump one level up, from a zillion micro-experiences to my one unified experience. If that's right, what did positing all that micro-experience actually buy anybody?

Could you write me a dialogue between two philosophers who both know this literature cold — one a committed panpsychist who thinks the combination problem is genuinely hard but tractable, and can say something substantive about why bonding/summing isn't just mystery-relabelling, and the other someone who presses exactly the "relocation, not solution" worry above and concludes panpsychism buys no real advantage over physicalism. I want real back-and-forth, each one responding to the other's specific last move rather than restating their own priors or caving early — neither should be a pushover, and I don't need it resolved by the end, just sharpened. A page or so is plenty.
