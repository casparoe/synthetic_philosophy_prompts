I am preparing a lecture on van Fraassen's Reflection Principle for my graduate seminar in formal epistemology, and I would like a second opinion before committing to a simplification. My plan is to tell students that Reflection is really just bookkeeping: since a Bayesian's current credence in a proposition is already a probability-weighted average of the credences she might hold in it later, requiring that her current credence conditional on a specific future credence equal that same value adds nothing beyond what conditionalization already guarantees. On this reading, van Fraassen's original Dutch Book argument in "Belief and the Will," and the subsequent literature on counterexamples involving forgetting and other non-evidential shifts of opinion, would just be fuss over a principle with no independent content. Is this an accurate way to present Reflection, or does it overlook something important about why the principle and its critics are usually taken more seriously than that?
