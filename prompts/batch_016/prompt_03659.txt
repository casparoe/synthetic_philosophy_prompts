Retired physicist here, got into philosophy of mind since retiring and it's basically eaten my brain. Brought up Goff's book on panpsychism at our retirees' reading group last week, and two of the members, Dave and Sheila, got into a proper argument about the 'combination problem' that nobody could settle.

Dave said panpsychism is sunk by it, full stop: if electrons each have some tiny flicker of experience, summing a trillion of them can't give you one unified subject having *this* experience, any more than a room full of people each thinking their own thoughts adds up to the room having a thought of its own. He said it's basically James's old point from 1890 and nobody's solved it, they've just renamed it a few times since.

Sheila said sure, but physicalism owes the exact same kind of story, and arguably a worse one, since physicalism has to get subjectivity out of stuff with zero experiential ingredients to start with, while panpsychism atleast starts with some experience already in the mix. So it's combination problem is a smaller ask than physicalism's problem of generating experience out of nothing.

Dave answered that this isn't so obvious, since ordinary cases of new properties emerging from parts (wetness from water molecules, say) are nothing like getting one subject out of many subjects. Subjecthood seems all-or-nothing, not something that scales up gradually, so maybe panpsychism's version of the jump is categorically worse, not smaller.

Sheila then brought up a view called panqualityism, where there's no subject at all at the micro level, just loose, unowned qualities, so there's no subject-summing to explain, since nobody's there to be summed in the first place.

Dave's comeback was that this doesn't rescue panpsychism, it just turns it into physicalism wearing a costume: you still need a story for how a subject shows up at the macro level out of ingredients that involved no subject anywhere, which just is the hard problem again.

Could you go through this move by move and say who actually gets the better of each exchange, and what, if anything, both of them are missing?
