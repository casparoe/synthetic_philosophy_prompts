For some time now, I've followed the recurring disputes over Integrated Information Theory that circulate on rationalist-adjacent blogs: Scott Aaronson's old "Unconscious Expander" posts, the 2023 open letter in which 124 consciousness researchers labeled IIT "pseudoscience," and the Cogitate Consortium's 2025 adversarial-collaboration paper in Nature. What I have not seen anyone do carefully is weigh these objections against one another, rather than simply cataloguing them together as though they were all equally serious. Some strike me as genuinely fatal to IIT's mathematical core; others read more like complaints about its philosophical commitments or its media coverage.

Please rank the following six challenges to IIT from most to least damaging to its core theoretical claims, where "most damaging" means hardest for a committed IIT proponent to absorb without either abandoning a central postulate or accepting a conclusion that most scientifically literate people would regard as a reductio:

- Expander-graph counterexample: Aaronson's argument that a simple grid or expander graph of XOR gates can be assigned arbitrarily high Φ despite doing nothing anyone would call cognition.
- Unfolding argument: Doerig, Schurger, Hess & Herzog's claim that a functionally identical "unfolded" feedforward network has zero Φ, putting causal-structure theories like IIT in a dilemma between falsity and unfalsifiability.
- Pseudoscience letter: the 2023 charge that IIT's commitment to panpsychism, together with the untestability of "the theory as a whole," disqualifies it as science.
- Exclusion postulate: the postulate that only a single, maximally irreducible complex can be conscious at a time, ruling out conscious subsystems or composites of separately conscious systems.
- Φ-intractability: the practical impossibility of ever computing exact Φ for a system resembling a real nervous system.
- Cogitate result: the adversarial collaboration's finding that posterior cortex failed to show the sustained synchronization that IIT's proponents had predicted.
