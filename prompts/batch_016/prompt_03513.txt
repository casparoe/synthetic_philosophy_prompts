I have published two novels of my own, and for the last three years I have also served as one of five readers on the jury for a small but well-regarded prize for debut fiction. Each of us reads the same shortlist of six novels independently beforehand; then, at a single deliberation dinner, we go around the table in a fixed order — alphabetically by surname, which puts me third — stating our ranking and a brief justification before any open discussion begins. Twice now I have noticed the same pattern: once the first one or two readers converge enthusiastically on the same novel, the remaining rankings fall into line behind it with startling speed, mine included. This year, out of curiosity, I compared my spoken ranking against the notes I had scribbled in the margins of each manuscript the week before, and discovered that my private notes were actually most admiring of a different book altogether, one that was dropped from contention within the first few minutes of discussion.

When I described this to a friend who teaches psychology, she called it "just groupthink" and left it there, but that label doesn't sit right with me: nobody at the table seemed lazy, cowed, or especially eager to avoid conflict; if anything, everyone appeared to be listening closely and updating in good faith. That is what unsettles me. It seems entirely possible that four serious, independent readers converging on the same novel really is meaningful evidence that I underrated it. But it seems equally possible that we would have crowned a different "unanimous" winner had we simply been seated in the opposite order, in which case the whole ritual of reading independently beforehand accomplishes rather less than we like to think. I have no training in decision theory, only in fiction, so I would like help thinking this through properly before the committee meets again next spring. What is actually happening to a group of well-intentioned readers in a situation like this? Should I be quietly embarrassed about the way my own ranking moved? And is there some concrete change to how we deliberate that would make the prize track the books' actual merits rather than the order in which people happen to speak?
