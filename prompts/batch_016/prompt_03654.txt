A few of us in my phil PhD program were going through the dogmatism-about-perception literature -- the view that just having an experience as of P immediately justifies believing P, no antecedent story about your senses being reliable required -- and then the bootstrapping / easy-knowledge objection that's supposed to be a real cost of holding that view. Two people in my cohort, Mira and Theo, ended up going a few rounds on whether the objection actually sinks the view, and I honestly can't tell who's winning which part, so I wanted an outside read.

(1) Mira opened with the standard gas-gauge case: if the needle reading 'F' immediately justifies believing the tank is full, you can drive around for a month, notice the reading always matches what you end up believing about the tank, and inductively conclude the gauge is reliable -- without ever once checking the tank in some independant way. She said that's obviously an illegitimate route to finding out an instrument is trustworthy, so any view that licenses every step of it can't be right. (2) Theo agreed it feels off but said the fishiness doesn't refute the principle, only shows the inference is redundant: if you're already entitled to trust each individual reading, as dogmatism grants, noticing the pattern afterward doesn't teach you anything new, any more than inferring 'P and P' from 'P' is illegitimate just because it's trivial. (3) Mira said 'redundant' is the wrong description -- you start out entitled only to claims about particular past readings and end up entitled to trust the gauge on brand-new, not-yet-observed occassions, which is a real upgrade from particular to general, not a restatement of what you already had. (4) Theo replied that this particular-to-general worry applies just as much to memory checking memory, or reasoning checking reasoning, so either it's fatal for every foundational source of justification and not some special defect of dogmatism, or Mira owes an account of why dogmatism is worse off here than everything else, instead of just insisting that it is.
