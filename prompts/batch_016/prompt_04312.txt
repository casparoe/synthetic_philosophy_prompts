I'm teaching a graduate seminar this term that spends a couple weeks on the School of Names, and right now we're stuck on Gongsun Long's White Horse Dialogue. Everyone already knows the famous opening move (horse names the shape, white names the color, so the two names pick out different things) and the standard point that 非 is ambiguous between "is not identical to" and "is not a member of" — that part's basically a party trick at this point, one of my students found it on the SEP page for the School of Names in about ten minutes flat. What's actually got us stuck is a passage later in the dialogue, after all the business about yellow and black horses and about "combining" horse and white into one compound name, that almost never comes up in the standard treatments.

Here's the bit, in the original plus my own rough translation (so blame me, not some poor sinologist, for any clunkiness): 白者不定所白,忘之而可也。白馬者,言白定所白也。定所白者,非白也。馬者,無去取于色,故黃、黑皆所以應。白馬者,有去取于色,黃、黑馬皆所以色去,故唯白馬獨可以應耳。無去者非有去也;故曰:白馬非馬。 "'White' on its own doesn't fix what particular thing is white — and that can just be left unsettled. But 'white horse' speaks of white as fixed onto some particular thing, and whatever's been fixed like that is no longer merely 'white,' unqualified. Now 'horse' involves no picking or excluding by color, so a yellow or black animal can equally answer to it. 'White horse' does involve that kind of exclusion — a yellow or black animal gets ruled out by its color — so only a white one answers to it. What excludes nothing isn't the same as what excludes something. Hence: white horse is not horse."

I ran this by a couple of colleagues down the hall in Asian Studies and got two different glosses back, which honestly just made things worse. So here's what I can't settle in my own head and would love your take on: what is "fixing" (定, ding) actually adding here that the earlier, much simpler point about yellow and black horses failing to satisfy "seek a white horse" hasn't already given you — is Gongsun Long smuggling in something like a rigid-designation idea, or a restricted-vs-unrestricted quantifier distinction, or is 定 just dressier vocabulary for the same exclusion point restated? And relatedly, even granting the whole arguement, does it actually establish non-identity of "white horse" and "horse," or does it just reconfirm, in fancier dress, that the two terms aren't coextensive — i.e., does this passage fall into exactly the identity-vs-membership equivocation everybody diagnoses for the dialogue as a whole, or does it somehow dodge that problem? Would love it if you could just walk through the logical bones of the argument with me, because I clearly haven't got them straight yet.
