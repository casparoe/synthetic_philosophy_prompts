I have no formal training in philosophy or economics, but I've spent a lot of time thinking about my own procrastination, and the popular explanation for it doesn't sit right with me. Weakness of will is now almost always explained through hyperbolic discounting: we supposedly act against our better judgment because we overvalue near-term rewards relative to how we'll weigh them later. But akrasia, in the classical philosophical sense, means doing something that you yourself judge, at the very moment of choice, to be the worse option. A hyperbolic discounter who grabs the near reward isn't violating their own judgment at that moment - given the discount function they're operating under, the near reward genuinely comes out ahead. It's only some earlier or later temporal 'self,' implicitly running a different discount function, who judges the choice a mistake. So what looks like one person failing to act on their own best judgment might really just be a handoff between a series of locally rational preference states. Please make the strongest case you can that hyperbolic discounting does not actually model classical akrasia, and that treating the two as equivalent is a conceptual mistake.
