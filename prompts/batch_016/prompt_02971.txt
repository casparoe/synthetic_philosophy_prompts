I've been reading about 19th-century constitutional interpretation for a piece I'm writing, and got pulled into a side-argument at a friend's Frederick Douglass reading group that's stuck with me. Context: through the 1840s Douglass was a Garrisonian, agreeing the Constitution was, per Garrison, "a covenant with death, and an agreement with hell," largely over the three-fifths and fugitive-slave clauses. In 1851 he reversed himself, and by 1852 was calling it "a GLORIOUS LIBERTY DOCUMENT" instead.

Two people got into it over whether that reversal was intellectually honest, and neither budged:

(1) Marcus: it's sound textualism -- "slave" appears nowhere in the text, the fugitive-slave clause can't cover slaves since a slave "cannot even make a contract" to owe service, and ambiguity should default to the preamble's "establish Justice" and "secure the Blessings of Liberty."
(2) Elena: that's anachronistic -- everyone at the time, including the Supreme Court in Prigg v. Pennsylvania, read that clause as covering slaves, and it's convenient that the reversal lines up with Gerrit Smith's money starting to bankroll Douglass's paper.
(3) Marcus: the funding point is a genetic fallacy -- who paid him doesn't bear on the argument's soundness -- and refusing to read unwritten intent into text is a legitimate interpretive stance, not a dodge.
(4) Elena: except Douglass doesn't apply that consistently -- he explains the missing word "slave" by saying the framers were "ashamed or afraid to say" it, itself a claim about their intentions, made exactly when convenient.
(5) Marcus: even so, the switch was vindicated in practice: it let abolitionists work through electoral politics instead of Garrison's dead-end "No Union with Slaveholders."
(6) Elena: that shows the new reading was useful, not true -- and a conversion that happens to be useful is exactly what should make us suspicious of it.

Nobody budged, and I wasnt about to referee my own material. Could you go through moves (1)-(6), say who's got the better of each one, flag what both are missing, and give your own verdict on whether Douglass's turn holds up as honest interpretation rather than motivated reasoning?
