I work in risk and compliance at a small prop trading firm, and about six months ago our quant team rolled out a new execution algo that uses reinforcement learning to continuously adjust its own position sizing and hedging based on live market feedback, plus it gets a fresh round of retraining every night using that day's data instead of running on the fixed rules our older systems used. Last Tuesday, during a weird low-liquidity window overnight, it worked its way through a sequence of trades nobody had ever seen it make before and lost the fund enough money to trigger a full formal post-mortem (I'd rather not put the exact number here, but it was a lot). Now leadership wants a "root cause" writeup with an actual name attached to it, and I got roped into drafting it, and honestly the more I dig through the logs the less sense that makes to me.

The quants who built the thing never coded that specific sequence of trades, they just set up the training process and the reward function months ago. The trader who switched it on that morning was just following the same checklist he'd followed for weeks. The risk officer signed off on the whole system back when it was stress-tested under totally different market conditions. Everyone seems to have done their actual job reasonably well by their own lights, but the disaster only exists because of what the algo taught itself somewhere in between all of that. I brought this up with my flatmate, who's doing a philosophy PhD, kind of expecting her to just tell me who's at fault, and instead she got this look on her face, said it reminded her of a debate from some paper she read years ago, then refused to say more and told me to "sit with it." So now I'm sitting with it and getting nowhere. My boss keeps insisting that "someone has to own this," but I'm honestly not sure that's even true anymore, or that it's the right way to be thinking about it. Is there something off about insisting that one person has to be held responsible in a case like this, and if so, where does that actually leave us?
