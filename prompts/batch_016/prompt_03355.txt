My younger brother ships out for basic training in a few weeks, so an argument that used to feel like pure philosophy-class abstraction suddenly hit close to home. At Sunday dinner it turned into a real back-and-forth between my uncle Dave (two tours, a while back) and my cousin Mia (finishing her poli-sci degree) about whether ordinary soldiers do anything wrong when they end up fighting in a war that turns out to be unjust.

(1) Dave opened by saying a soldier who sticks to the rules of engagement - no targeting civilians, no disproportionate force - hasn't done anything wrong, period, no matter which side started the war or why; that's exactly why the laws of war apply the same way to both sides, since you can't expect a 19-year-old private to correctly judge the justice of the whole war before he's allowed to fire back. (2) Mia said that can't be the whole story, because it implies a conscript on the aggressor's side, shooting at some guy defending his own city from invasion, is doing nothing more wrong than the defender is - and that's crazy, since one of them is helping carry out a war of aggression and the other's just trying not to loose his home; both following the same rulebook doesn't erase that difference. (3) Dave's comeback was that soldiers usually can't know, and aren't well positioned to figure out, whether their own side's cause is actually just, especially with propoganda and the fog of war in the mix - so it's unreasonable, maybe even dangerous, to hold individual soldiers to a standard they're structurally unable to meet. (4) Mia answered that not being able to know you're doing wrong doesn't mean you're not doing wrong - she compared it to a getaway driver who sincerely believes, because he's been lied to, that he's driving people to a legit job: we might not blame him personally, but the driving's still part of a robbery, and the same goes for the conscript, blameless or not.

Nobody at the table could agree on who actually landed the better point at each stage, and it's been bugging me since. Could you go through it move by move instead of just picking an overall winner?
