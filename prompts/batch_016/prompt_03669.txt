My eight-year-old daughter has just been referred to a pediatric endocrinologist, who is recommending growth hormone injections because her height falls below the first percentile for her age -- even though repeated testing has found no growth hormone deficiency or any other identifiable pathology. As far as anyone can tell, she is a healthy child at the short end of the normal range, and the hormone would not be correcting any deficient process so much as pushing an already-normal one further than it would otherwise go. When I asked the endocrinologist why this counts as 'treatment' rather than simply making a healthy child taller, I was told that medicine treats departures from species-typical functioning, and that her height falls outside it. That answer sent me into the bioethics literature on the therapy/enhancement distinction -- Norman Daniels' work in particular -- and I've come away unconvinced that 'restoring normal function' is doing the principled work it's meant to do here. Could you help me think through why this line is so hard to draw cleanly, using her case specifically, rather than simply restating the standard formula that therapy treats disease while enhancement goes beyond it?
