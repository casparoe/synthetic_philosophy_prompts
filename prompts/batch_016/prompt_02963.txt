I spent my career as a molecular biologist, and now that I'm retired I've fallen into reading philosophy of biology, and one thing I can't shake is wether the "genetic code" is a code in any real sense or just a metaphor we got attached to. Crick's own frozen accident idea implies the codon-to-amino-acid mapping is arbitrary, the way a real code (Morse, an alphabet) is arbitrary, but then you've got people like Sarkar arguing that "information" talk in biology is just ordinary chemical causation with fancier vocabulary, no reader or meaning required, while Maynard Smith went the other way and said genes are genuinely symbols with intentional content becuase natural selection put them there for a reason. Feels like a real philosophical fork, not something more biology is going to settle. Could you write me a short story (a page or two is fine) that dramatizes that tension -- where it actually matters to the plot whether what's happening in a cell, maybe a redesigned one like Romesberg's synthetic-base-pair organisms or Church's recoded E. coli, counts as reading a message versus just churning through chemistry? Not looking for a lecture dressed up as fiction, want real characters and stakes riding on the question.
