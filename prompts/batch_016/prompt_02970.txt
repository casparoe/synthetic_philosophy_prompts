I'm reporting on whether financial regulators should publish more granular bank stress-test results, and I want to use a result from decision theory to make the normative case for full disclosure. I. J. Good proved in 1967 that a rational Bayesian agent's expected utility from a decision can never be lowered by first consulting an additional piece of cost-free evidence — informally, information can't hurt. Since that's a mathematical certainty, doesn't full public disclosure of stress-test results have to be the policy that leaves everyone involved, banks and investors alike, at least as well off in expectation as any partial-disclosure alternative?
