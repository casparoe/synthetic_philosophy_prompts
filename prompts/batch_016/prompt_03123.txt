A friend and I spent much of a long train journey arguing about the cosmological argument, and by the time we got off neither of us had budged an inch. We'd both spent the past semester independently reading up on natural theology, but she came away persuaded that William Lane Craig's kalam argument is the strongest form it takes: the universe began to exist a finite time ago, whatever begins to exist has a cause, and — since a timeless, impersonal cause would have to produce its effect just as timelessly — that cause must be a free, personal agent who chose, at some moment, to bring the universe about. I kept arguing that this is a strategic mistake, and that the more Thomistic line associated with Edward Feser is on safer ground, since Aquinas's own argument doesn't need a beginning at all: it turns on the difference between a causal series ordered "per accidens" (like a line of fathers and sons, where earlier members needn't still exist for later ones to act) and one ordered "per se" (like a hand moving a stick moving a stone, where every member's causal power is simultaneously borrowed from the one before it). An infinite per accidens series may be unobjectionable, but an infinite per se series, Aquinas thought, is not — and that argument goes through whether or not the past turns out to be finite. She thinks I've just swapped one set of contestable premises (actual infinites, the beginning of time) for a worse one (Aristotelian act and potency), and that my version has a much harder time reaching anything personal at all; I think her version is needlessly hostage to whatever cosmologists announce next year.

Could you write a dialogue between the two of us that actually makes progress, rather than two monologues past each other? Give the kalam defender (K) and the Thomist (T) equally strong material, and have them respond to each other's actual points — for instance, T pressing K on whether "whatever begins to exist has a cause" is doing any independent work once you take away the act/potency framework that would normally motivate it, and K pressing T on whether the first member of an essentially ordered series has to have intellect and will, rather than just being an inert terminus. Real concessions and follow-ups on both sides would be more useful to me than a tidy verdict — I'd rather see exactly where the genuine pressure points are than watch one side win.
