So this actually happened at my building's tenant committee meeting, of all places. We've always picked stuff like where to hold the summer bbq by plain plurality vote -- everyone picks one option, most votes wins. But people kept complaining about "vote splitting," like two similar park options would split votes and let a widely disliked third option sneak through. So my neighbor Dave pushed for switching to ranked-choice (instant runoff) voting instead, arguing it basically solves strategic voting, since you can rank your genuine favorite #1 without worrying you're wasting your vote -- it just falls through to your next pick if your favorite gets eliminated.

Another neighbor, Priya, who took some game theory in grad school, said that's not really right -- she half remembered a theorem (Gibbard-Satterthwaite, she thought?) proving that literally any voting rule with 3+ options can be strategically gamed by somebody, so ranked-choice doesn't actually fix the underlying issue, it just moves it around.

Dave said he thought that theorem was about combining everyone's rankings into one fair social ranking, like Arrow's thing, not about whether an individual voter can lie about their own preferences -- so it might not even be the right theorem to cite here.

Priya said no, this one's specifically about individual manipulation, not aggregation, and it covers pretty much any non-dictatorial rule you could design, ranked-choice included.

Dave then said ok fine, but even if it's "gameable" in some contrived edge case, doesn't RCV still massively cut down how often anyone actually has an incentive to vote dishonestly, compared to plurality? isn't that a real improvement even if it's not a perfect fix?

Priya agreed it probably helps with some plurality-specific issues, but said Dave's original pitch -- that ranked-choice "solves" strategic voting -- was importantly overstated, and pointed out RCV can introduce its own weird failure modes, like a candidate straight up loosing because voters ranked them higher.

Now half the committee thinks Priya "won" and the other half thinks she was just being pedantic over a group bbq. can you go through this move by move and say who's got the better of it at each point, not just who wins overall? Also flag anything you think both of them are missing.
