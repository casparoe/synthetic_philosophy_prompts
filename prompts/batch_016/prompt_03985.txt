Okay so I need someone to adjudicate an argument I had with my friend Jake last night, because it's been bugging me all day. We're both in this environmental ethics seminar and we'd just finished the Parfit reading on the nonidentity problem — the 14-year-old girl case, and also the "Depletion vs Conservation" resource policy example — and afterward we went and got beers and somehow ended up in a two-hour arguement about whether the whole thing actually has any real-world teeth.

Jake's take was basically this: almost every long-term policy choice (how much we deplete resources, what energy mix we use, population policy, whatever) changes the timing and circumstances of conception for pretty much everyone downstream within a couple generations, so it changes WHO gets born, not just how well off they are. That means none of the actual people who end up existing in, say, 2150 can ever truthfully say "your choices made MY life worse than it would've been," because under any alternative choice they specifically wouldn't exist at all — and a life that's worth living, even a fairly mediocre one, presumably isn't worse than never existing. So his conclusion was: talk about "obligations to future generations" or being "unfair to our grandkids" is basically a category error, since there's no one who actually ends up wronged by the riskier/more depleting policy. If we want to justify conservation it has to be on totally different grounds, not because we owe it to anybody in particular.

I didn't have a great comeback in the moment and it's kind of rattled me since, because it feels like it proves way more than it should — like it would let us off the hook for almost any harm at all, as long as its implemented slowly enough to shift who gets born. But I can't pin down exactly where his reasoning breaks, if it does. Is Jake basically right that the nonidentity problem shows we can't be violating any real obligations to future people this way? Or is there a flaw in getting from "no specific person ends up worse off" to "no obligation was violated"?
