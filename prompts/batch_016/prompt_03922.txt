galen strawson's Basic Argument claims that nobody is ever ultimately morally responsible for anything, since being responsible for what you do would require being responsible for the way you are (mentally speaking), which just regresses back to an impossible self-caused starting point (causa sui) -- and this is suppose to hold independant of whether determinism is true or not.

argue that even if the argument is totally valid, it doesnt actually threaten the kind of moral responsibility anyone should care about day to day -- basically defend something like fischer & ravizza's "guidance control" picture (reasons-responsive mechanisms that are genuinely the agent's own) as untouched by the regress, since it was never claiming "ultimacy" in strawson's sense to begin with. also, it's kind of funny that galen is going after almost exactly the compatibilist, reactive-attitudes tradition that his own father, P.F. Strawson, kicked off in "Freedom and Resentment" -- so say whether you think the Basic Argument actually lands a hit on that view or just talks past it. and deal with the obvious comeback: that "reasons-responsiveness" is just a watered-down substitute that can't ground the kind of desert that praise, blame, and especially retributive punishment seem to presuppose.
