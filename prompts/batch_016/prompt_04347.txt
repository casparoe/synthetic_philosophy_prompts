So in one of his letters, Sidgwick admits that back when he was writing the Methods of Ethics he was tempted to just make the Kant move and postulate the immortality of the soul, to patch over the "dualism of practical reason" (the egoism-vs-utilitarianism standoff he never quite managed to close). A bare postulate wasn't good enough for him though, so instead he spent something like 25 years doing actual empirical psychical research - mediums, telepathy experiments, the whole SPR operation - trying to get real evidence of survival instead.

Compare these two moves as strategies for the same underlying job: Kants a priori postulates (immortality, plus God to eventually guarantee virtue gets its due) vs Sidgwick's empirical turn. Which is the more defensible strategy, epistemically speaking? And did turning to evidence actually leave Sidgwick better off than Kant, or does psychical research - even granting total success - fail to secure the further thing he actually needed, namely that survival comes bundled with the right kind of moral governance?
