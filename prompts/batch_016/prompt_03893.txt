In my decision theory seminar, we've been discussing money-pump arguments and their extension to time-inconsistent preferences like hyperbolic discounting: an agent may prefer a larger-later reward to a smaller-sooner one at a distance, yet reverse that ranking as the smaller-sooner reward draws near. This makes her exploitable, in principle, by anyone willing to charge her, again and again, to renew a commitment she keeps wanting to break. A live debate in the literature (Gustafsson's 'synchronic' money pump versus Levy's and Piper's insistence that a genuine pump is 'necessarily diachronic') turns on whether this vulnerability is a defect at a single moment, or a drama that can only unfold between different time-slices of one person. Write a short story of no more than 1,200 words dramatizing this predicament — a character money-pumped precisely because her present and future selves want different things — conveying the mechanics through plot rather than exposition, and ending on a note that leaves open whether we've witnessed one flawed agent or several selves in conflict.
