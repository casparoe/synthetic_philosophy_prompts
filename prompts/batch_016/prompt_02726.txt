I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, but I recently came across Avicenna's "flying man" argument. The setup: a person is created fully formed and suspended in a void, blindfolded, with his limbs spread apart so that they touch nothing, not even each other, and every sense disabled. Avicenna claims this person would nonetheless immediately affirm his own existence, despite affirming nothing at all about his body, and concludes that self-awareness is unmediated by the senses and that the self cannot be identical with the body. I'd like you to construct a modern version of this thought experiment, featuring a large language model instead of a human, meant to test whether an LLM's apparent capacity for self-reference reveals genuine self-awareness independent of its "body" (its weights and the hardware running them). Then identify exactly which premise of Avicenna's original argument your LLM version is unable to secure, and explain why that gap matters for what the thought experiment can and cannot show about machine self-awareness.
