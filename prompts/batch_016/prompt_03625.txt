I was a research chemist for 35 years before I retired, and now I've fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole, currently slogging through Davidson on radical interpretation. The bit that really got me is his claim that a creature can't have real beliefs unless its language-using, because belief requires the concept of error, and you only get that concept by "triangulating" with another interpreter on a shared world - so by his own lights even a smart dog doesn't actually believe the cat ran up that particular tree. Now, twenty-some years after Davidson died, outfits like Project CETI are using AI to crunch sperm whale codas, and back in 2024 they reported the clicks have a combinatorial, almost alphabet-like structure. So here's what I keep chewing on: would Davidson's own framework let two whales count as triangulating with each other and thereby having genuine beliefs, no humans required? And separately, could an AI thats just hoovering up statistical patterns in the acoustic data ever count as radically interpreting the whales in his sense, or is that necessarily a lesser, different thing?
