I've been a resident assistant in my dorm since the start of this semester, which means part of my job is writing up residents for violations like unregistered parties and noise complaints. Last week I wrote up a group of sophomores down the hall for a party that ran well past quiet hours. A few days later, one of them cornered me and pointed out, correctly, that during my own freshman year, before I had this job, I used to throw essentially identical parties in the same building and never once got written up for it myself. He's not disputing that his party broke the rules; his claim is that I, of all people, am not the one who gets to hold that against him, and that the write-up should be dropped on those grounds alone.

I mentioned this to some friends and it turned into a real argument. Half of them thought the sophomore had a genuine point: if I did the same thing myself, they said, I've forfeited any right to be the one wagging a finger at him, regardless of what hat I'm wearing when I do it. The other half thought this was a distraction: I'm not condemning him as a private citizen with a spotless record, I'm applying a policy I'm now formally responsible for enforcing, and what I did two years ago as a random freshman with no official role seems beside the point. To complicate things further, I'd genuinely admit, looking back, that my own parties were thoughtless toward the people trying to sleep next door, even though nothing ever came of it at the time.

This whole debate happened right after my ethics seminar covered whether someone can be blamed for actions they didn't fully recognize as wrong at the time, which isn't quite my situation, since I'd now readily admit that I knew better even then. I still can't decide whether my past behavior genuinely undercuts my footing to enforce the rule against him now, or whether that's irrelevant to the actual question of what should happen to his write-up. I'd be interested to hear how you'd think through it.
