A friend who works on AI safety and I spent an evening arguing about the Löbian obstacle and never resolved it. My view was that it marks a genuine structural limit: a theory as strong as Peano Arithmetic cannot prove "if I prove φ, then φ" for arbitrary φ without thereby proving its own consistency, which Gödel's second theorem forbids — so no formal reasoner can ever fully trust an unmodified copy of itself, only a successor it has first weakened. Her view was that the proposed fixes turn this into a solvable engineering problem: Model Polymorphism lets an agent trust a whole schema of successors indexed by proof strength rather than certifying one fixed successor, and Christiano's probabilistic reflection replaces the offending provability predicate with a self-referential probability one. I pushed back that both fixes are partial — Model Polymorphism only secures trust over a subset of an agent's goals and still forces stepwise self-modification, while probabilistic reflection has its own unresolved failure mode, a probabilistic version of the procrastination paradox. She said partial fixes aren't the same as an impossibility proof, and that I was moving the goalposts.
