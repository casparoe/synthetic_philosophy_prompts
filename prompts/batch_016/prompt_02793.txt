I've been reporting on this whole wave of "could an AI secretly be conscious" stories, and it sent me down a rabbit hole into illusionism -- the view, mostly associated with Keith Frankish, that phenomenal consciousness (the raw, ineffable "what it's like" quality of pain or seeing red) isn't actually a feature of our brains, just something our own introspection convinces us is there. Frankish's move is to say we have "quasi-phenomenal" properties instead -- real functional/representational states that introspection systematically dresses up as something mysterious (Dennett's old phrase "user illusion" keeps coming up in what I've been reading). Then I found Galen Strawson's New York Review of Books piece from a few years back where he goes after this whole camp -- he calls them "the Deniers" -- and his central charge is basically that denying experience is self-undermining: if it merely seems like you're in pain, isn't that seeming itself a conscious episode? So doesn't saying "consciousness is an illusion" just smuggle consciousness back in through the seeming?

I brought this up over dinner with a couple of philosopher friends and it turned into a genuine arguement that nobody actually won (we ended up just arguing about splitting the bill instead). So I'd love to see it played out properly: could you write me a dialogue -- real back-and-forth, not two people giving speeches at each other -- between a committed illusionist and a committed phenomenal realist, hashing out exactly this "seeming" objection? I want the realist to press it as hard as it legitimately goes, and the illusionist to give the actual sophisticated reply rather than a dodge, with each one genuinely responding to what the other just said rather than talking past them. If it fits naturally, have them end up trying to sort out whether they're disagreeing about something substantive going on in our heads, or whether the whole fight is really just downstream of the illusionist defining "phenomenal" differently than the realist does -- that's the bit that's been nagging at me since dinner and I still haven't figured it out.
