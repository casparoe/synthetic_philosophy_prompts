I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole with classical Chinese philosophy lately (it started from a footnote in a logic textbook about early, pre-formal takes on semantics, and now I can't stop reading about the "School of Names"). What's tripping me up is Gongsun Long and Hui Shi -- they're both filed under the same "school," and apparently ancient sources already frame the whole movement as revolving around two rival slogans: "separating hard and white" (Gongsun Longs thing) versus "unifying sameness and difference" (Hui Shi's thing). But as far as I can tell these aren't just two techniques, they're actually pulling in opposite directions philosophically. Gongsun Long's white horse dialogue insists that names have to be kept razor-precise: "horse" tracks shape, "white" tracks color, so "white horse" and "horse" turn out to be strictly different things, and he makes a similar move with a stone's hardness and whiteness, which he says are "separate" properties because you only ever grasp them through different senses (touch vs. sight), never both together. Hui Shi's ten theses head the opposite way entirely -- things like the sun at noon already being the sun on its way down, or setting off for the state of Yue and somehow arriving the day before you left, or the idea that heaven and earth are ultimately one single body so that all things deserve equal care -- all seemingly aimed at dissolving the very distinctions Gongsun Long is so insistent on.

Can you actually compare these two properly for me? Specifically: is there some deeper unifying project underneath both of them -- something like "naming and language don't carve up reality the way naive common sense assumes" -- that just cashes out into opposite-looking conclusions depending on which move you make first, or is grouping them together as one "school" basically a historical accident that papers over two incompatible philosophies of language? And if you had to defend one of the two as the philosophically stronger position, rather than just the more quotable paradox, who would you back, and why?
