I'm writing a longform piece on the entanglement of early modern astronomy with astrology, and the Kepler chapter keeps resisting the tidy narrative I'd like to give it. My editor's working assumption is that Kepler was essentially a hypocrite: a rigorous astronomer who cast horoscopes for the imperial court and, most famously, the general Wallenstein, purely because the pay was good, while privately regarding the whole enterprise as bunk. Having spent the last couple of weeks with *Tertius Interveniens* (1610) and *De Fundamentis Astrologiae Certioribus* (1601) in translation, alongside some of the secondary literature (Patrick Boner's book on the role of the soul in his cosmology, J.V. Field's older article on him as a "Lutheran astrologer"), I don't think that story survives contact with the primary texts. Kepler is genuinely scathing about large parts of the astrological tradition he inherited, in places sounding like a modern debunker, while insisting just as forcefully that he isn't throwing out astrology altogether, only purifying it into something compatible with geometry and harmonics. The trouble, for the piece, is that this "purification" doesn't track the boundary a present-day reader would expect.

So before I commit to a characterization, I want a sharper map of where he actually drew the line. Based specifically on what Kepler himself argues in his astrological writings — not on how any of this fares by the standards of modern science — please rank the following eight components of the astrological tradition he inherited, from the one he treats as most real and defensible to the one he rejects most emphatically as baseless:

- The system of the twelve astrological houses
- Planetary aspects (angular relationships such as conjunction, trine, square, opposition) as genuine, effective influences
- The claim that the configuration of the heavens at the moment of birth leaves a lasting imprint on an individual's temperament
- The classification of planets into "benefic" (e.g., Jupiter, Venus) and "malefic" (e.g., Saturn, Mars) types
- Astrometeorology: forecasting weather and terrestrial events from planetary aspects
- Planetary rulerships and dignities (each zodiac sign assigned a ruling planet, with exaltations and detriments)
- The notion that celestial configurations can compel human action, leaving no room for free will or providence
- The twelve-fold division of the zodiac into signs with distinct elemental or qualitative natures

Please give the result as a JSON object with two equal-length arrays: "ranking" (the eight items, abbreviated to a few words each, ordered from most to least endorsed) and "grounds" (for each item, in the same order, one sentence — not a paragraph — on the textual basis for its placement). Format it like this:
