Random one, but a while back I went down an internet rabbit hole reading about how Rousseau apparently despised theatre so much that he wrote this huge letter arguing his hometown Geneva should NOT build a playhouse, even though he basically admitted the plays and actors themselves could be perfectly virtuous and it still wouldn't matter. Weirdly specific argument, and I'd mostly forgotten about it until my friends Kayla and Marcus got into it after we all binged this true-crime drama about a charming serial killer.

(1) Kayla said stuff like that is basically a pressure valve -- you get to feel the dark curiosity and adrenaline without anyone actually getting hurt, and if anything it makes you sharper about spotting manipulators in real life.
(2) Marcus said the problem was never the violence, its that we sat there for six hours being fed feelings instead of doing anything, and that's exactly how you end up with people who mistake "I felt something watching TV" for actually being a good or engaged person.
(3) Kayla said that's a stretch, we feel plenty reading novels too and nobody thinks that turns you into a couch potato citizen.
(4) Marcus said it's not fiction in general, its specifically that actors are paid to counterfeit emotions they don't have as convincingly as possible, and a culture that hands its biggest rewards to whoever fakes it best is bad training, kind of like how you wouldn't want your politicians to just be great actors.
(5) Kayla shot back that literally every job involves performing feelings you don't have -- retail, waiting tables, just being polite at a party -- and nobody calls that corrosive to society, so Marcus's whole point proves way too much.

Nobody backed down and they made me referee, which I couldn't do. Using Rousseau's actual reasons (not just generic "is tv bad for you" opinions), can you go through this move by move, say who's got the better argument at each step, and point out anything important both of them are missing?
