(1) Carol said the ring is gone now, all that's really there is the gold, just wearing a new shape. her point was: if you'd shown grandma a puddle of melted gold and asked "is this your ring," she'd obviously say no — so somewhere between "ring" and "puddle" the ring stopped existing, even though not one atom of it was destroyed or removed.

(2) Ray said that's backwards. there's only ever been one thing sitting in that box for 50-something years: a chunk of gold. "ring" and "pendant" are just names for whatever shape that gold happens to be wearing at a given time, not two separate objects taking turns. nothing was created and nothing was destroyed, it just got reshaped, same as folding a piece of paper into a different shape and back.

nobody budged, and honestly, in the moment, all four things they said sounded at least a little right to me, which is exactly the problem. can you referee this properly, move by move? give me a score for each numbered statement, -2 (clearly wrong) to 2 (clearly right), as a json object like {"1": 0, "2": 0, "3": 0, "4": 0}, then a couple sentences justifying each score, then tell me if you think carol and ray are both missing something that would actually settle this.
