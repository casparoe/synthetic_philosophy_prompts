I've spent years following LessWrong and Overcoming Bias, and I've noticed that Aumann's agreement theorem gets treated in these circles as though it were a formal vindication of conciliationism about peer disagreement. Yet Yudkowsky's own "Against Modest Epistemology" insists that genuine Aumann agreement arises "incidentally," through each side's honest reasoning from a shared prior, and is not the same thing as the deliberate credence-splitting that the equal weight view (Elga) is usually taken to recommend.

Please compare conciliationism (specifically the equal weight view) with steadfastness as responses to peer disagreement, using Aumann's theorem and its dynamic generalization by Geanakoplos and Polemarchakis as your test case. The latter shows that iterated exchange of posteriors forces agreement after finitely many rounds, but not necessarily at the value that directly pooling all information would yield. So: which side, if either, can legitimately claim the theorem's support, and exactly where does the inference from the formal result to the informal epistemic norm break down?
