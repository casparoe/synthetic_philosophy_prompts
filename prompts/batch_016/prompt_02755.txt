I'm assembling a collection of short fiction in which each story is built around a genuine mathematical idea rather than a loose metaphor for one. For the next piece, I want to work with the Axiom of Choice—specifically the disquieting fact that it guarantees a well-ordering of the real numbers, yet nobody has ever exhibited one, and every serious attempt to construct one explicitly has ended in failure. Could you write a short story that makes a reader feel the vertigo of that situation—an object whose existence is secured by fiat yet recedes from every attempt to point to it or construct it? I'd like the mathematics handled precisely enough that a set theorist reading it wouldn't wince, even though the story's emotional weight shouldn't depend on the reader following the technical details.
