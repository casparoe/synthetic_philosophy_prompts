I spent my career as an observational astronomer, and now that I'm retired I've got a lot more time for reading philosophy, which has become a bit of an obsession (my wife says I've traded star charts for even stranger ones). A few months back a friend from my old department lent me some Max Tegmark, which got me hooked on his whole hierarchy of multiverses - distant regions of space in Level I, other post-inflation bubbles in Level II, Everett's branching worlds in Level III, and then the really wild one, Level IV, where every mathematical structure that could exist, does. Chasing that down a rabbit hole I landed on David Lewis's modal realism, and it seems to me like physics and philosophy have basically converged on the same idea from two directions: every possible universe is exactly as real and concrete as ours is, we just can't see the others because their causally cut off from us.

Here's what's bugging me: if a working cosmologist like Tegmark and a philosopher like Lewis end up in more or less the same place, doesn't that mean modal realism has essentially been vindicated by physics - or at least that it's really more of a physical hypothesis in fancy dress than some distinct, freestanding metaphysical claim? My old reading group is split on this and its honestly been keeping me up at night (in a good way, mostly). Am I conflating two things that just happen to rhyme, or is there actually less daylight between the multiverse and modal realism than most people assume?
