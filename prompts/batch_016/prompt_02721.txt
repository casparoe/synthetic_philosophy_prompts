I've been researching Einstein's "hole argument" for a feature on the philosophical foundations of general relativity. As I understand it, the argument establishes that the theory is fundamentally indeterministic: in any region of spacetime containing no matter, Einstein's field equations admit infinitely many distinct metric fields that agree exactly with one another everywhere outside that region yet differ within it. If that's correct, why do physicists still treat general relativity as a paradigm of classical determinism and use it to generate exact predictions—for instance, the arrival times of gravitational-wave signals or the trajectories of interplanetary probes—rather than treating such predictions as approximations that paper over a genuine gap in the theory's determinism?
