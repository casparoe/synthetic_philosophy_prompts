A friend of mine and I got into a long argument last night, after the reading group we're both part of finished discussing Leibniz's mill argument, about whether it has any real bite against contemporary functionalism or is simply an argument against materialism in general that happens to resemble it. My friend's view was that the mill argument, from section 17 of the Monadology, is aimed at any purely mechanical explanation of perception and has nothing special to say about multiple realizability, since Leibniz was writing centuries before anyone distinguished a system's functional organization from its physical substrate. I argued the opposite: that the mill argument, if anything, cuts deepest exactly against multiple realizability, since the whole point of that thesis is that it shouldn't matter whether the moving parts inside the mill are neurons, vacuum tubes, or silicon chips, so long as the causal organization among them is preserved. Neither of us could really settle it, and I'd like a more rigorous take than either of us managed.

Using only the metaphysical commitments Leibniz actually held, rather than a generic gloss on the mill passage, how do you think he would respond if confronted with twentieth- and twenty-first-century functionalism and the multiple realizability thesis? I'm especially interested in two things. First, would he treat something like Ned Block's China Brain thought experiment, in which the functional organization of a brain is implemented by an entire population communicating by radio instead of by neurons firing, as simply a scaled-up version of his own mill and therefore a vindication of it, or would he think functionalists have some reply available that the materialists of his own day did not? Second, and this is the part my friend and I really got stuck on, doesn't Leibniz's denial of genuine causal interaction between substances, in favor of a pre-established harmony among windowless monads, actually sit in tension with functionalism's core idea that mental states just are causal roles, so that he might end up rejecting the functionalist framework itself as incoherent rather than merely rejecting its conclusion? Please reconstruct the reasoning he would most plausibly give, in his own terms of perception, appetition, and simple substance, and flag clearly where you think the reconstruction is going beyond anything his texts can actually support.
