As a science journalist, I frequently encounter the charge — usually leveled by critics of evolutionary theory — that "survival of the fittest" is an empty tautology, since fitness is defined by survival and reproduction, the very outcome the theory is meant to explain. Philosophers of biology have tried to defuse this charge by replacing a frequentist reading of fitness (fitness as an organism's actual or average reproductive output) with Mills and Beatty's propensity interpretation, on which fitness is a probabilistic disposition to survive and reproduce that need not match the numbers any particular organism actually leaves behind. Write an essay evaluating whether this shift from frequencies to propensities genuinely dissolves the tautology charge, or whether the underlying circularity simply resurfaces a level up — for instance, in how the relevant reference class or environment gets fixed, or in the fact that propensities are themselves typically estimated from nothing but observed frequencies. Weigh the strongest considerations on both sides, and close with a definite verdict on whether the propensity interpretation truly earns its reputation as a solution to the tautology problem.
