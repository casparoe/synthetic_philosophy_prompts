Aristotle's defense of the law of non-contradiction in the Metaphysics is this odd "elenctic" argument: since the principle is too basic to prove directly, he instead corners anyone who denies it into admitting that for their words to mean anything at all, they have to signify one determinate thing - which he thinks already smuggles the law back in. Priest actually takes this exact argument on in his own work and doesn't think it survives against the dialetheist. Can you reconstruct Aristotle's real argument (not just "he liked logic, hated contradictions") and then predict how he'd actually respond if he had to sit down with Priest's stuff on the liar paradox and paraconsistent logic? Give me a real verdict - does the elenctic proof hold up against dialetheism, or would Aristotle need a whole new argument to deal with Priest?
