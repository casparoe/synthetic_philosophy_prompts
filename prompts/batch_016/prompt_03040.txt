I'm a PhD student working on Hegel's political philosophy. In the seminar I TA, students only ever encounter his defense of war reduced to the crude slogan that it's "good for the health of nations," or worse, treated as a proto-fascist celebration of violence for its own sake. I'd like a short story (a page or two) that instead dramatizes the actual mechanism behind his account of the state's relations to other states in the Philosophy of Right (roughly §321–340): that a state's individuality is only actual through a real, not merely formal, relation of possible negation to other states, and that a long enough peace lets the private, contractual habits proper to civil society harden until they mistake themselves for the whole of ethical life — so that war, or a credible threat of it, is what reasserts the state's ethical substance over that self-enclosed private interest. Don't have any character simply narrate this thesis in dialogue; let the plot and the characters' choices carry it, and feel free to write the story as an implicit rebuttal to Kant's proposal for a perpetual peace among states, which is the position Hegel has in his sights.
