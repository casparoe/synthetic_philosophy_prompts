I'm teaching a grad seminar on social epistemology this term, and we just spent two weeks on Fricker's *Epistemic Injustice*, so everyone's got the testimonial/hermeneutical injustice distinction down cold by now. To push things further before we move to the next unit, I had them read Kristie Dotson's 2011 Hypatia piece where she argues that Fricker's dyad doesn't actually exhaust the ways testimony gets shut down. The concept I keep chewing on is "testimonial smothering" - where a speaker truncates her own testimony because she reads her audience as unable or unwilling to properly take up what she'd otherwise say, so she restricts herself to whatever content she judges the audience "testimonially competent" to receive. It's clearly meant as a case of epistemic oppression, but it doesn't sit neatly in either of Fricker's boxes: there's no credibility deficit being handed down in the moment (the speaker's the one doing the restricting), and it isn't quite a hermeneutical gap either, since she isn't lacking the concepts to describe her experience, she's just choosing not to deploy them.

So here's what I'd like your take on. First, compare smothering and testimonial injustice properly - what's actually doing the causal/normative work in each case. Second, one of my students raised a worry in seminar that I couldn't fully answer: maybe smothering is just a second-order or anticipatory version of testimonial injustice, i.e. the speaker heading off a credibility deflation she predicts is coming anyway - in which case Dotson hasn't found a genuinely new phenomenon, just a psychologically interesting route into an old one. Do you think that objection succeeds? If you think smothering really is doing independent work that resists being cashed out in Fricker's terms, I'd like to hear specifically what resists translation, and what that implies about how complete (or not) Fricker's original taxonomy of epistemic injustice really was.
