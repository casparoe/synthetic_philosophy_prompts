Comparing notes turned into a fight about the epistemology of disagreement itself, and because I wanted to get it right, I wrote the exchange down more or less as it happened.

(1) Lena opened by insisting they were epistemic peers on this question — same three days of research, same long-run track record — so the only defensible response was for each to move substantially toward the other's number, rather than just restating their own. (2) Owen replied that "peer" can't mean equal accuracy averaged over years of unrelated forecasts; on this narrower sub-class of question — ballot measures with contested turnout models — his own track record is measurably better, which entitles him to weight his estimate more heavily than hers. (3) Lena shot back that this was exactly the move the equal-weight view rules out: Owen was using his own forecasting judgment — the very faculty in dispute — to certify itself as more reliable, which is just question-begging wearing the costume of humility. (4) Owen countered that, taken seriously, her principle makes the notion of a peer, or a novice, or an expert impossible to apply anywhere, since identifying anyone's comparative reliability always requires exercising some judgment; if that move is illegitimate, nobody could ever have evidence they're dealing with a peer in the first place. (5) Owen then pressed further: he and Lena now disagreed about a second thing — whether conciliating or standing firm is the right response to disagreement in general — and they were at least as much peers on that question as on the ballot measure. (6) Lena tried to hold the line by distinguishing the two disputes: she has independent arguments for the equal-weight view, she said, ones that don't just restate her 35 percent, so nothing commits her to treating the meta-level disagreement the same way as the object-level one. Owen's last word was that this "independence" move was steadfastness smuggled back in under a new label — she was now claiming the right to weigh her own philosophical judgment more heavily than his exactly where the two of them disagreed, which is the very move she'd rejected from him in step (2).
