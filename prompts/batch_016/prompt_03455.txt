A random rabbit hole from a side project has been living in my head rent-free, figured I'd dump it here. I've been trying to learn Idris in my spare time to get better at dependent types (day job is mostly Go and TypeScript, so this is a stretch), which led me down a wikipedia hole into the Curry-Howard correspondence, then into intuitionistic logic, then into this Oxford logician named Michael Dummett, who apparently wrote the paper that basically makes the philosophical case for intuitionism back in the 70s — something about meaning being grounded in "assertibility conditions" instead of truth conditions, though ngl I'm only following about 60% of it. Anyway, I went to read his wikipedia page expecting standard dry academic philospher stuff and instead found out this guy spent decades as an anti-racism campaigner, co-founded a group fighting racial discrimination back in 1964, fought the Commonwealth Immigrants Acts, and got knighted in 1999 partly "for services to racial justice." Then in 2001, in his mid-70s, he published an entire book called On Immigration and Refugees, arguing (roughly, secondhand, don't quote me) that there's basically a general right to immigrate and a presumption in favor of letting people — especially refugees — in unless the state has solid grounds to refuse, and that most of British immigration law is just racism wearing a technocratic disguise.

Dummett died in Dec 2011, so he completely missed the immigration fights that feel most "current" to me: the 2015 refugee crisis, Brexit killing free movement, and especially the 2018 Windrush scandal, where the UK ended up detaining and deporting people who'd lived there totally legally since the 50s and 60s, over paperwork gaps that trace back to the 1971 Immigration Act — a law his wife Ann had actively campaigned against decades earlier. The timing feels almost too on-the-nose, like the universe set this up as a final exam question for him specifically.

Could you try to reconstruct, as best you can from his actual arguments and values (not just an "old British philosopher, probably pro-immigration" guess), what Dummett likely would've said about the Windrush scandal, and maybe the Rwanda deportation scheme the UK tried more recently too? I'd love it grounded in his real reasoning — the stuff about state obligations, the Geneva Convention, and racism hiding behind bureaucratic-sounding rules — rather than just vibes.
