I've been reading Robert Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain thought experiment from Anarchy, State, and Utopia, and it seems to settle an argument I keep having with colleagues about wealth in the tech industry. Nozick's setup: start from any distribution of holdings you're willing to call just, then let a million fans each voluntarily hand a basketball star 25 cents to watch him play. The resulting distribution is far more unequal, but Nozick argues it is just all the same, since every step was a free transaction between consenting parties. That maps neatly onto a founder whose app goes viral: millions of users each voluntarily pay a small fee, and within a few years the founder is a billionaire. It seems to follow that as long as the market in which a fortune was built was reasonably competitive, the resulting wealth is automatically just, and taxing it back down toward some prior, more equal pattern would itself be the rights violation. Is that a fair generalization of Nozick's argument, or is there a gap in it I'm missing?
