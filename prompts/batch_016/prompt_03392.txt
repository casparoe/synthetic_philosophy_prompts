Grading term papers for my graduate seminar last month, I worked until nearly 1 a.m. The last paper offered a heterodox but carefully argued reading of Mill's harm principle; I wrote a full page of comments explaining exactly where I thought the argument broke down, and gave it a B-. A few days later a colleague mentioned research suggesting that instructors who grade late at night are, on average, measurably harsher on unconventional arguments than those who grade the same papers earlier in the day, apparently because fatigue erodes patience for unfamiliar reasoning. That was uncomfortably close to my own situation, so I went back and reread my comments on the paper several times. They still strike me as exactly right, point by point; I can't locate anything in them that looks like impatience or unfairness. Yet I can't shake the feeling that I ought to discount the grade, or redo it, simply because of when I assigned it. I recognize that taking the research seriously should lower my confidence in my own judgment here, but that judgment feels just as compelling as ever whenever I examine it directly. Is there something irrational about holding both of those thoughts at once, and, practically speaking, what should I do about the grade?
