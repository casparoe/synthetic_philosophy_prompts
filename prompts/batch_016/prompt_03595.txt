This semester, in my bioethics seminar, we moved from the standard material on whole-brain death into the more contested territory of organ donation after circulatory death, and one of the assigned case studies has left me doubting that the underlying framework is fully coherent. The case describes a procedure called normothermic regional perfusion: after a patient's family has withdrawn life-sustaining treatment and physicians have observed the required stand-off period with no pulse or blood pressure (satisfying the circulatory criterion for death under statutes modeled on the Uniform Determination of Death Act), surgeons clamp the vessels supplying the brain and then use a mechanical pump to restore oxygenated blood flow to the heart and abdominal organs, so that they remain viable for transplantation. In several of the cases we read about, the heart resumes beating on its own once this perfusion is restored.

What I cannot reconcile is how this is supposed to be compatible with the very death determination that authorized organ removal in the first place. With whole-brain death, the loss of function is understood to be total and settled: nothing is subsequently restarted. But here, physicians declare death specifically on the grounds that circulatory function has irreversibly ceased, and then, within minutes, mechanically cause circulation to resume in part of the body. It seems to me that there are only two consistent positions available, and the practice fits neither: either the cessation was not actually irreversible, in which case the declaration of death was premature, or it genuinely was irreversible, in which case restarting it should be exactly as impossible as restarting brain activity after brain death has been declared. Since organ procurement organizations and the relevant transplant societies apparently regard the practice as fully consistent with the dead donor rule, I raised this objection in section, but we moved on before getting to the bottom of it. I suspect there is some distinction I am failing to draw that dissolves the apparent contradiction, but I have not been able to reconstruct it on my own, and I would appreciate help thinking through where my reasoning goes wrong.
