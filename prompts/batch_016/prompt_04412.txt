So this happened at a conference dinner a few weeks back and its been bugging me since. The visiting plenary speaker spent his whole talk going off about "citation gatekeeping" -- senior people who only cite their friends and never cite grad students or junior scholars, and how everyone should call it out when they see it. Except this guy is kind of famous for exactly that himself, it's a running joke in the field (won't say who). Afterward two of my grad students, Nadia and Owen, got into it over dessert and I honestly couldn't tell who was winning.

1. Nadia: he had no business giving that talk. Not because the point about gatekeeping is wrong, but because being probably the worst offender in the room forfeits your standing to lecture everyone else about it.
2. Owen: that's basically tu quoque. If gatekeeping's unfair, it's unfair no matter who says so -- "you do it too" doesn't touch the substance.
3. Nadia: she's not touching the substance, that's her point -- there's a seperate question of whether he's the right person to press it. You can be dead right about a norm and still lack standing to enforce it.
4. Owen: but if hypocrisy disqualifies you from calling stuff out, nobody ever gets to criticize anything, since everyone's a hypocrite about something -- that just protects whoever's currently on top.
5. Nadia: fair, but that proves too much the other way too -- it'd mean hypocrisy *never* undermines standing. Picture the worst offender in the room standing up to shame one grad student over a single self-citation. Something's off there, beyond "well, self-citation is still bad." So there's presumably some line, maybe about how comparable, uncorrected, or unacknowledged the hypocrisy is.
6. Owen: sure, but the second you're grading it by degree like that, "standing" isn't doing independent work anymore -- you've just smuggled in a proportionality judgment and given it a fancier name.

I half-suspect there's an actual philosophical literature on standing to blame that would sort some of this out, but I don't know it well enough to referee. Could you go through the six moves and say who's got the better of it at each one -- not just declare an overall winner -- and flag anything you think both of them are missing? End with one line: Verdict: Nadia, Owen, or Draw.
