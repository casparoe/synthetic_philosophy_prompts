I have been reading up on formal epistemology for a piece on how forecasters — human and algorithmic alike — ought to update their beliefs on evidence that arrives wrapped in a conditional, and I cannot shake a puzzle from the probability-kinematics literature known as the Judy Benjamin problem.

A soldier is dropped with equal probability into one of four zones, formed by crossing two divisions — Red territory versus Blue territory, and, within each, a Headquarters area versus a Second Company area — so every zone starts out at 25 percent. Command radios her a single piece of information: they are not sure whether she strayed into Red territory at all, but if she did, there is a 3/4 chance she is in the Red Headquarters area rather than Red Second Company. Nothing at all is said about Blue, or about Red versus Blue generally.

The intuitive reaction is that this news is purely local to Red and should leave the 50/50 Red-versus-Blue split untouched. But the standard formal treatment of such evidence does not leave that split alone. It treats the conditional as a constraint on the posterior and selects whichever posterior is closest to the prior by relative entropy — the natural way of extending Jeffrey conditionalization to this sort of input — and the result nudges her credence that she is in Blue up to a little over 0.53, with Red dropping to a little under 0.47, purely as a side effect of resolving the 3:1 split inside Red. (The philosopher who devised the puzzle joked that Judy Benjamin seems to be indulging in wishful thinking about staying clear of the dangerous Headquarters.)

I can follow the optimization that produces those numbers, but I want the intuition underneath it: why should information that appears to say nothing whatsoever about Red versus Blue nonetheless shift that ratio? And once you see why it happens, would you say this "leak" is something a careful reasoner ought to simply accept, or does it expose a real problem with using minimum-relative-entropy updating as a general rule for learning conditional information? I am more interested in the underlying reasoning than in re-deriving the arithmetic.
