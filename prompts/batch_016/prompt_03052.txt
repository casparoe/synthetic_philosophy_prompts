I write a long-running series of "cozy" mysteries, and a question about the logic underlying the genre's conventions has been nagging at me since it came up at my writing group last week. Fair-play detective fiction rests on an old, largely unwritten contract with the reader, one that Ronald Knox tried to formalize in his 1929 list of "commandments" for the genre: the culprit must be someone the reader has already met early on, never a stranger produced out of nowhere in the final chapters. My own eleven novels have honored this rule without exception — in every book, the murderer has turned out to be a character introduced within the first three chapters — and my editor, delighted by how tidy this record is, now wants the jacket copy for book twelve to promise readers something considerably stronger: that any character who first appears after chapter three can be relied upon never to be the killer.

Here is what troubles me. It is true that whenever a character introduced later in one of my books turns out to be innocent — which describes nearly every late-arriving character I have ever written, since only one person per book is ever the murderer, down to a postman who appears once on page 220 and is never mentioned again — that fact seems to count as one more small piece of support for the very same rule, "the killer is always an early character," that my run of eleven correctly identified early culprits also supports. Yet it strikes me as absurd to say that the postman's innocence tells us anything at all about my habits as a novelist, and more absurd still to let it underwrite a guarantee printed on a book jacket. Something seems to have gone wrong in treating these two kinds of cases as equally good evidence for the same rule, but my writing group could not agree on what, and "it just feels wrong" is not an argument I can bring to my editor.

So: is there an actual flaw in reasoning from "every murderer so far has been an early character" to "therefore any late character can be promised innocent," or is my discomfort unfounded and the pattern really as solid as my editor believes? I would like a rigorous answer I can use to make my case one way or the other, not simply reassurance that my instinct is right.
