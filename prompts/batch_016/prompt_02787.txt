So a coworker linked Pascal's wager in our #random slack channel last week (we were arguing about EV calculations lol) and that sent me down a rabbit hole into the rest of the Pensées. Most of it's way over my head but I got stuck on this bit about justice and force that I can't stop thinking about. He's got this whole earlier riff about how justice flips depending which side of a river or mountain range your standing on, and then he writes this (Trotter translation, fragments 297-298):

"Veri juris. — We have it no more; if we had it, we should take conformity to the customs of a country as the rule of justice. It is here that, not finding justice, we have found force, etc.

Justice, might. — It is right that what is just should be obeyed; it is necessary that what is strongest should be obeyed. Justice without might is helpless; might without justice is tyrannical. Justice without might is gainsaid, because there are always offenders; might without justice is condemned. We must then combine justice and might and, for this end, make what is just strong, or what is strong just.

Justice is subject to dispute; might is easily recognised and is not disputed. So we cannot give might to justice, because might has gainsaid justice and has declared that it is she herself who is just. And thus, being unable to make what is just strong, we have made what is strong just."

What's tripping me up: the first sentence sounds like there's a real, independent "what is just" that force is failing to track, like force is just a stand-in for something realer. But by the end it reads like justice just collapses into "whatever the strong call just," full stop, no residue left over. Those feel like two different claims to me and I can't tell if Pascal thinks hes describing the same thing twice or if he's actually contradicting himself mid-fragment. Is there a consistent position buried in here, maybe connected to his whole corrupted-reason-after-the-Fall stuff elsewhere in the book, or is this basically just a fancier, more depressing version of plain old might-makes-right? Curious how you'd untangle it.
