kind of a weird one but I need a sanity check for a plot problem in the sci-fi novel I'm drafting. There's a teleporter device that malfunctions in a very specific way: it scans and transmits a person to the receiving pod like normal, but the destroy-the-original step fails, so the original is left on the sending end with fatal internal injuries from the scan (wrecked heart, a few days to live) while an exact psychological and physical duplicate is already up and walking around on the other end. I mentioned to two friends that this is basically Parfit's "branch-line case," and they got into it over whether the guy on the sending end should be comforted by any of this:

(1) Jess said no -- he's just an ordinary man dying of internal injuries who happens to have a very similar stranger living his life somewhere else, like finding out you have an identical twin on the day you die. Nothing comforting about that.

(2) Marcus said the twin comparison doesn't hold up, because the duplicate shares literally every memory and personality trait up to the scan, linked by the exact same chain of psychological connectedness that's supposed to make ordinary, non-malfunctioning teleportation fine. If you're not bothered by the normal version, he said, the twin framing can't be doing any real work, since an actual twin doesn't remember your last Tuesday.

(3) Jess said fine, granted the psychological connection is identical either way, but that was never the argument -- the argument is whether that connection is what matters, or whether identity is what matters and usually just tracks the connection. In the ordinary case there's no rival claimant, so you can still tell an identity story where original-you just is receiving-end-you. In the malfunction case there are two distinct people alive at once, and there's no principled way to say the identity goes to one of them rather than the other -- so on any of the usual theories, original-you doesn't survive as receiving-end-you. Pointing out the connection is "the same" doesn't settle whether the connection is the sort of thing that should matter to you.

(4) Marcus said okay, but here's what should bother an identity theorist: the only reason identity fails in the malfunction case instead of holding is that some leftover tissue takes a few extra days to give out back on the sending end. Nothing about the relationship between original-you-right-now and receiving-end-you changes one bit. So whether you get to count as surviving swings from "fine" to "basically dead" based on a fact that doesn't touch the person you're supposedly asking about. That should make you suspicious identity was ever tracking the thing you actually cared about.

Neither of them budged and I had to go deal with a plumber. Can you go through this move by move and say whos right at each step (not just who wins overall), and flag anything you think they're both missing? Then give your overall call on its own line as \answer{Jess} or \answer{Marcus}.
