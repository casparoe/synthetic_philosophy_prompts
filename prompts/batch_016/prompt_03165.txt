I've been reading on my own about something philosophers call a "transformative experience": a decision you can't really evaluate beforehand because you'd need to have already lived through it to know what it's like, and because living through it might change what you even want. This came up recently when a friend of ours, a corporate lawyer, told our group he was seriously weighing whether to leave his career, his relationship, and city life behind to join a monastic order that requires a lifetime vow of silence. Two others in the group, Nadia and Owen, spent a good part of the evening arguing over whether he could make that decision "rationally" at all, and I still haven't settled it in my own head.

Nadia said there's no rational way to make the call: he can't know what monastic life feels like from the inside until he's already living it, so weighing costs and benefits is a fiction, and he might as well flip a coin.

Owen answered that we make huge, irreversible decisions under exactly this kind of uncertainty all the time -- a first marriage, emigrating, having a child -- without calling them arbitrary, since he can lean on monks' memoirs and other converts' accounts of what it's like.

Nadia replied that testimony doesn't really solve it: it gives you facts about the daily routine, not the concept of what the inner experience is like, any more than a book about colour gives sight to someone born blind. And underneath that is a further problem: the self doing the evaluating a decade in would have different values than the self doing the deciding now, so it isn't even clear whose preferences should count.

Owen said that even granting all this, it still doesn't tell him what to do: "ordinary reasoning doesn't apply here" just restates the problem instead of solving it, and if the alternative on offer is "decide based on whether you want to find out who you'd become," that's not obviously different from following your gut with fancier words.

Could you go through their exchange move by move, say who has the better of it at each stage, and flag anything important that both of them seem to be missing?
