I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mathematics, and for the past semester I've been sitting in on my department's set theory reading group while drafting a dissertation chapter on realism about the continuum hypothesis (CH). We recently worked through Cohen's forcing construction and the Levy–Solovay theorem, which shows that no large cardinal axiom can settle CH, because CH's truth value can always be flipped by "small" forcing that leaves the relevant large cardinals intact. Going through this material has sharpened an ongoing disagreement I have with two people in the group. My advisor, working very much in Gödel's tradition, insists that CH has a perfectly determinate truth value and that its independence from ZFC just shows that ZFC is an incomplete description of "the" universe of sets; she's been following Woodin's Ultimate-L program with real hope that it, or something like it, will eventually settle the question once and for all. A postdoc in the group, by contrast, keeps pointing me to Hamkins's work on the set-theoretic multiverse, and argues that CH behaves less like a fact waiting to be discovered than like a "switch" that forcing can flip depending on which universe of sets you happen to be looking at — so that asking whether CH is "really" true, full stop, is a bit like asking whether it's "really" daytime without specifying a time zone.

I'd like to see these two positions actually argue with each other rather than just state their conclusions at me in office hours. Could you write a substantial dialogue — somewhere in the range of 15-25 exchanges — between a committed universist and a committed multiverse pluralist debating whether CH has a determinate truth value? I don't want either side to be a pushover: the universist should have to answer the charge that hunting for "new axioms" precisely calibrated to settle CH is uncomfortably close to deciding the answer in advance and dressing it up as discovery — a charge the pluralist can sharpen by pointing out that Woodin's own program has swung from suggesting, via Ω-logic, that the right new axioms should make CH false, to suggesting, more recently via Ultimate-L, that they should make it true. The pluralist, meanwhile, should have to answer for the fact that working set theorists plainly don't treat all models of ZFC as equally natural: they care a great deal about large cardinal hierarchies, canonical inner models, and forcing axioms like PFA, which sits uneasily with genuine indifference between universes. I'd like the dialogue to stay in contact with this kind of technical detail — forcing, Levy–Solovay, Freiling's symmetry argument, and the fate of Ultimate-L are all fair game — rather than drifting into generic realism-versus-anti-realism slogans, and I'd like each character to visibly respond to the specific point the other has just made (conceding minor points, drawing distinctions, etc.) rather than restating their opening position in new words.
