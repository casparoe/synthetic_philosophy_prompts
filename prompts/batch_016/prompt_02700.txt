Quick setup: a driver needs to take the second of two highway exits to get home (payoff 4), taking the first exit instead is bad (payoff 0), and missing both exits is just okay (payoff 1) -- but the two exits look identical and he can't remember whether he's already passed one, so whatever rule he uses has to be the same at both. If you work out the best strategy ahead of time, i.e. "continue with probability p" at each exit, maximizing p^2 + 4p(1-p) gives p = 2/3. She says this shows expected utility reasoning genuinely breaks down once you add self-locating uncertainty. I said it has to be some kind of double-counting mistake in that second calculation, because otherwise the theory would just be incoherent. We never settled it.
