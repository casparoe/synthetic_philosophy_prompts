Ok heres one for you -- the 'Death in Damascus' case Gibbard and Harper used against causal decision theory (the guy who bolts to Aleppo to dodge Death, not realizing Death predicted exactly that and is already there waiting). Compare how causal decision theory and evidential decision theory handle it. The wrinkle that gets my students every time is that CDT, once you require the chosen option to be ratifiable, leaves the guy with no stable pure strategy at all -- whichever city he commits to, the evidence immediately makes the other one look safer. Does evidential decision theory actually dodge that instability, or is it secretly stuck with the same problem? Tell me which theory comes out looking better here and why -- don't just say 'it depends.'
