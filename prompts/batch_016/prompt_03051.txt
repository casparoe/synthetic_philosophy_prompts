Two people in my physics reading group got into a lengthy dispute after we finished the chapter on Bell's theorem, prompted by a mention of the 2022 Nobel Prize for the entangled-photon experiments. Neither of us could settle who was right. The exchange went roughly as follows:

(1) Ana said Bell's theorem, together with those experiments, proves the world is nonlocal: measuring one entangled particle instantaneously affects the state of its distant partner, however far apart they are.

(2) Theo said that's too quick. Bell's theorem only rules out the conjunction of locality and realism. Ordinary quantum mechanics already denies realism -- it says particles don't have definite properties before they're measured -- so the correlations can be explained without any influence passing between the particles at all. Give up realism, not locality.

(3) Ana replied that denying realism doesn't actually save locality. Even orthodox quantum mechanics has the joint wavefunction collapse instantaneously the moment one particle is measured, changing what can correctly be said about its distant partner right away. You haven't removed the nonlocality, you've just relocated it from "hidden variables" to "collapse."

(4) Theo answered that the description changes instantly, sure, but nothing physical propagates and no message can be sent -- that's guaranteed by the no-communication theorem. Since failing to send a superluminal signal is the only thing relativity actually requires, there's no real conflict, whatever we call the formalism.

(5) Ana said that conflates two different claims: "you can't signal" is much weaker than "there's no nonlocal influence." Bell's own condition of local causality says that once everything in the overlapping past light cones of the two measurement events is fixed, the distant setting and outcome should become statistically irrelevant to one another -- and quantum correlations violate exactly that, even though they can't be exploited to signal. So the no-communication theorem shows the nonlocality is unusable, not that it's absent.

(6) Theo said fine, then here's a way to keep both locality and realism: deny that the particles' hidden properties are statistically independent of which measurement settings get chosen later on -- superdeterminism. If both trace back to correlated causes in their shared past, the correlations arise without anything influencing anything else at the time of measurement, or ever. Nobody has shown that's false.
