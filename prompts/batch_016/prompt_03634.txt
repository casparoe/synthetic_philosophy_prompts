I'm a backend engineer, and I tend to think about problems in terms of specs and edge cases, which is probably why something that has been nagging at me about parenting has taken on a strangely technical shape. My partner and I have a fifteen-month-old who is now mobile enough to get into everything, and I've been trying to childproof our apartment the way I'd approach hardening a system before a big launch: walk through every room, catalog every hazard, close every gap. The trouble is that each fix seems to create a new problem rather than simply eliminating an old one. We moved a side table away from the window because she had been using it to climb onto the sill, and within a week she had learned that the ottoman we put in its place works just as well. We put locks on the two cabinets we were sure were dangerous, only to discover she had started emptying a drawer we had never even considered, which turned out to hold something worse than anything we had locked away.

My partner's position is that we should stop treating this as something a sufficiently long list could ever finish, and instead just accept that we need to stay close and adjust in real time as new situations come up. When I've mentioned this to other parents, they mostly respond with more items to add to the list — outlet covers, corner guards, furniture anchors — which doesn't really address what's bothering me.

What I actually want to know is more basic than that: if we were simply diligent enough, and sat down and reasoned all the way through every object in the apartment and every way a curious toddler could possibly use it, shouldn't we, at least in principle, eventually reach a complete set of fixes that closes every gap — the same way you can, in theory, work through all the edge cases for a well-specified piece of software? Or is there something about this particular kind of problem — anticipating every downstream consequence of every change you make to a shared, open-ended environment — that makes a final, exhaustive list impossible in principle, no matter how careful or clever you are, so that my partner's "stop listing and stay adaptive" instinct isn't merely a practical fallback but is actually tracking something true? I would genuinely like to know who is closer to correct here, and why, rather than getting more parenting tips.
