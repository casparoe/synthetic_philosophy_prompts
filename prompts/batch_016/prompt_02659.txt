(1) Nora: you obviously go for the five. five deaths is a worse outcome than one death, and minimizing how bad things are is just what being moral requires here.

(2) Devon: worse for who though? nobody actually experiences "five deaths" as one big bad thing, each of the five just loses their own single life, exactly like the lone guy does. you can't add up separate peoples losses into some bigger loss and say it outweighs his.

(3) Nora: sure, but then you're saying his one life somehow outweighs all five of theirs put together, which is nuts. five has to be worse than one, thats not even a moral claim, its just counting.

(4) Devon: I'm not saying his life outweighs theirs. I'm saying "worse" doesn't obviously carry over between different people the way it does within one person's life. if your concern for all six of them is supposed to be equal, the fair way to choose is something like a coin flip so everyone's got a shot, instead of just handing it to whoever has more people on their side.

(5) Nora: ok, here's a way around that though. pair up one of the five with the lone guy — their claims cancel since they're identical — but that leaves four people in the group with nobody to cancel against. those four leftover claims are what tip it toward saving the five, and that's not the same move as just tallying up numbers.

(6) Devon: except why pair that particular one against him and not one of the other four? the pairing's arbitrary, and it looks like you've just smuggled the head count back in under a fancier name.
