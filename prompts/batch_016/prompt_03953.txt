Ok so I had this two hour arguement with my friend Priya last night after our neighborhood association meeting and I kind of need a tiebreaker because neither of us is budging.

Some background: I'm on a 5-person committee that hands out small quarterly grants for local projects, and the bylaws say a project only gets funded if it (a) stays under the $2,000 budget cap and (b) will benefit at least 50 households. Normally we vote on those two conditions seperately and then just apply the rule. Last night's proposal was for a community garden. Three of us said yes on the budget question, and a mostly-different three of us said yes on the household-benefit question, so technically both conditions passed 3-2, which under the rule means the garden gets funded. But Priya pointed out that if you look at who actually voted yes on BOTH conditions at the same time, it's literally just one guy, Marcus — everyone else on the committee was a yes on exactly one condition and a no on the other. So she made everyone just raise hands on "do you personally think we should fund this, yes or no, full stop," instead of going condition by condition, and only Marcus voted yes. 4-1 against, even though each individual condition had cleared 3-2 in favor.

Priya's position is that the straight up-or-down vote is obviously the honest result, and our usual method is basically a loophole for funding stuff that almost nobody in the room actually supports. My position is that voting condition by condition is more disciplined, since it forces people to defend their view on each specific criterion instead of just going with an overall gut feeling that could be hiding all kinds of personal bias (two of the no votes really don't like Marcus, for what it's worth). We went back and forth for so long that I stopped being sure either of us is being unreasonable, which is what's actually bugging me — both voting methods seem totally fair and defensible on their own, yet they flatly contradict each other on the same proposal with the same people. Is one of these actually the more legitimate way for a group to decide something, is this a known problem with group decision-making that has a name or some kind of fix, or do we basically just have to accept that any voting setup can contradict itself like this and picking one over the other is arbitrary?
