I've recently been reading Wittgenstein's On Certainty, where he argues that some propositions -- like Moore's 'here is a hand' or 'the earth has existed for many years' -- aren't held on the basis of evidence, but function as 'hinges': things we show we accept through action rather than assert as knowledge, and which have to stay fixed for ordinary doubt and inquiry to be possible at all. He compares this framework to a riverbed rather than a foundation, since over time a hinge can turn fluid and doubtable again, while a fluid belief can harden into a new hinge. I notice something similar in debugging, where I treat claims like 'the compiler correctly implements the spec' as effectively beyond question, and only revisit them once every other explanation has failed. Please describe the most important implications of Wittgenstein's hinge epistemology for this practice, including what it would actually take, on his view, for one of these normally-unquestioned debugging assumptions to legitimately become doubtable.
