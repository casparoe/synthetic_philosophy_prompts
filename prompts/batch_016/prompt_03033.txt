I'm putting together materials for a graduate seminar on practical reason and would like a dialogue to use as a discussion-starter on Bernard Williams's distinction between internal and external reasons. Write a dialogue between a Williams-style internalist and a McDowellian externalist who disagree about Henry James's Owen Wingrave — the young man whose family declares that family tradition gives him a reason to enlist, even though nothing in his actual motivations points that way. The internalist should hold that this claim can be true of Owen only if it connects, by a sound deliberative route, to his existing subjective motivational set, while the externalist should press McDowell's notion of "conversion" — coming to acquire a motivation that wasn't already latent in one's prior outlook — as a case the internalist's picture can't accommodate. Have each press the toughest objection they can find against the other's position rather than simply restating their own, and don't feel obliged to resolve the disagreement by the end.
