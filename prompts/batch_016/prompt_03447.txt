Ok so this one has been living in my head rent free for like a week. I do ML infra work (mostly RAG pipelines and evals) and "hallucination" is a word my team uses like 50 times a day, but I was grabbing drinks with an old friend from undergrad who's now ABD in philosophy, and she was telling me about this view called disjunctivism, and now I can't stop wondering if we're using the word wrong. From what I understood, disjunctivism says a veridical perception and a hallucination that feels 100% identical from the inside are NOT two versions of the same underlying mental ingredient, they're fundamentally different in kind, full stop, no common core. Apparently this is a response to the classic argument from illusion (bent stick in water, that kind of thing), which says that because illusions can be introspectively indistinguishable from the real deal, both must be built out of the same basic stuff (sense-data), which then implies even normal perception never touches the actual object directly.

Here's what's bugging me: when our model "hallucinates" a fact vs generates a true one, its running the exact same forward pass either way, there's no seperate hallucination-generating circuit, it's all just next-token prediction that happens to line up with reality or not. That sounds a LOT closer to the "common factor" story that disjunctivists are trying to reject, not their own picture. Though, we also have a RAG step where some answers are grounded in retrieved docs and others come from free generation off parametric memory, and part of me wonders if that split is secretly doing something like the disjunctivist's "good case / bad case" distinction, i.e. maybe grounded generation is structurally more like a direct relation to a source. Could you walk me through the most important implications of disjunctivism (just taking it as true for the sake of argument) for whether "hallucination" is actually a defensible concept to import into ML, or if we're quietly smuggling in a totally different, kind of opposed theory of mind every time we use it? Also curious whether the RAG vs free-generation split actually rescues the analogy at all, or if I'm just grasping at straws there.
