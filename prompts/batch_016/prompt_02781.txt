Over the weekend I had a long, rather heated conversation with a close friend, Owen, that I still haven't been able to shake off. A mutual friend of ours, Priya, has been staying at my apartment for the past couple of weeks while she puts some distance between herself and an ex who'd become frightening—turning up outside her workplace, messaging her at all hours, that sort of thing. One evening he texted me directly, asking point-blank whether she was staying with me. Rather than deny it outright, I wrote back, "Haven't seen her today," which was technically true—she'd been upstairs the whole time and I genuinely hadn't laid eyes on her—but was obviously meant to leave him thinking she wasn't staying there at all. He seems to have believed me, and as far as I know he hasn't bothered her since.

When I told Owen about this, he was oddly critical. He argued that what I did was no better than lying to the man's face—that once you're deliberately trying to plant a false belief in someone's head, it doesn't matter one bit whether the individual sentence you used happens to be accurate, and that I was just flattering myself by avoiding a direct falsehood. In his view I should either have lied cleanly or told the ex I simply wasn't going to discuss her whereabouts, and that the option I actually went with was the least honest of the three, not the most. I couldn't explain, in the moment, why the exact wording felt like it mattered to me, and by the end of the argument I wasn't fully sure it did. I'd genuinely like an outside read on this: is there a real moral distinction between lying outright, misleading someone with a statement that happens to be true, and simply refusing to answer—or was Owen right that intent is all that matters, and the careful wording was just self-deception on my part?
