Over Thanksgiving this year, two conversations happened a few hours apart that I still haven't managed to reconcile. In the afternoon, my cousin, who works in hospice care, gently raised the question of how aggressively to keep treating my grandmother's recurring infections. My grandmother has advanced Alzheimer's: she no longer recognizes anyone in the family, cannot form or communicate any preference about her own future, and requires round-the-clock custodial care. My uncle, who is normally the family's resident cost-benefit hardliner about everything from healthcare spending to charitable giving, was adamant that none of that was relevant here: she is a person, full stop, and it would be monstrous to weigh the burden on the rest of us against the marginal medical benefit to her. Nobody at the table disagreed.

That evening, the conversation turned to a news story about a university lab using macaques in invasive research on a degenerative brain disease. The same uncle was completely comfortable saying that, provided the expected benefit to future human patients was large enough, the suffering of some number of monkeys was simply a cost to be weighed against that benefit, no further questions asked. What has stuck with me is that nobody could point to any capacity my grandmother currently has, in her present state, that those macaques lack, and that would explain why she is treated as beyond any such calculation while they are treated as a line item in an expected-value sum.

I've read enough on LessWrong and the EA forum about which capacities are supposed to ground moral status, usually in arguments about animals or about digital minds, to suspect that "she still belongs to a kind that's normally rational" is doing a lot of unexamined work in cases like this, but I can't tell whether that move is legitimate or just a comfortable way of special-pleading for our own species. Is there an actual coherent way to hold onto both of my uncle's reactions at once, or does taking either one seriously commit you to revising the other?
