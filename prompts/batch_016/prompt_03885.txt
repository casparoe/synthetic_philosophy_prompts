Hey, quick one for a piece I'm working on about how philosophers' youthful obsessions sneak back into their mature work — I fell down a Rawls rabbit hole. Everyone knows "public reason" Rawls: religion is just one "comprehensive doctrine" among others, kept out of political debate except through the proviso (you can argue from faith as long as you eventually cash it out in public reasons too). Whats wild is that 20-something Rawls's senior thesis at Princeton was a full-on work of neo-orthodox theology (heavy Brunner/Barth), where he defines sin as basically a refusal to be in right relation with other people and God — rooted less in ordinary selfishness than in what he calls egotism, needing to feel superior to others — and builds a whole ethics of community out of it.

So compare the two Rawlses for me: is the mature "comprehensive doctrines + proviso" machinery basically a secularized descendant of that early relational picture of sin and community, just with the theology filed off, or is there a real rupture there? And does knowing about the thesis make the mature treatment of religion look more principled to you, or more like Rawls quietly working out his own lost faith rather than doing neutral political theory? Justify it either way — i don't have a strong take yet, just curious if it holds up.
