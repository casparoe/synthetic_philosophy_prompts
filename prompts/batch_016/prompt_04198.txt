Bit of background: I spent about thirty years as an experimental physicist working on rotating machinery (gyroscopes and the like), and now that I'm retired a friend from my old book club talked me into finally tackling some metaphysics. I've been slowly working through the endurantism vs perdurantism material — "objects are wholly present at each moment" vs "objects are just four-dimensional worms made of temporal parts stacked up through time" — and I thought I basically had the shape of the disagreement, until I ran into this rotating disc business, which apparently caused a real dust-up between David Lewis and a guy named Dean Zimmerman back in the late 90s (there's a Zimmerman paper, then a short reply from Lewis literally called something like "Zimmerman and the Spinning Sphere," and the idea apparently goes back even further, to Broad in the 1920s and, weirdly, to a jab Leibniz made at Descartes).

Here's the setup as best I understand it: take two discs that are perfectly homogeneous and featureless, identical in every intrinsic respect, except that one is rotating steadily around it's axis and the other is dead still. Because the disc has no marks or bumps anywhere, a "snapshot" of it at any single instant looks exactly the same either way — nothing about that instantaneous slice tells you which one is spinning. But the two discs obviously aren't physically the same: one has angular momentum and kinetic energy that the other lacks, and they'd respond quite differently the moment something interacted with them. That looks like a genuine problem if persistence (and maybe causation too) is supposed to be built entirely out of a sequence of instantaneous, purely qualitative time-slices, which is more or less what perdurantists, especially the Humean-minded ones among them, want to say.

So here's what I'm actually hoping you can do: walk me through how an endurantist and a perdurantist would each try to handle this case, then tell me straight whether one of them comes out ahead, or whether it's a wash. Does the endurantist really buy anything by insisting the disc is "wholly present" at every moment, or are they just relocating the same mystery into some unanalyzed notion of a persisting substance that "really" spins? And can the perdurantist keep both temporal parts and Humean supervenience here, or do they have to smuggle in something extra — brute cross-time identities for bits of matter, say, or treating instantaneous velocity as its own primitive quality? I'd like the actual mechanics of the disagreement, not the standard textbook-level summary of what each side says about caterpillars and statues.
