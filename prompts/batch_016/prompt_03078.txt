As a longtime reader of LessWrong and related rationalist writing, I keep returning to Eliezer Yudkowsky's "Logical Pinpointing" and its followup "Standard and Nonstandard Numbers," where he argues that second-order logic with full semantics can pin down the natural numbers uniquely up to isomorphism, escaping the nonstandard models that the Löwenheim–Skolem theorem guarantees for any first-order axiomatization. It strikes me that Skolem's paradox raises a structurally similar problem one level up: "full semantics" for the second-order quantifiers is only as determinate as our grip on the totality of "all subsets" of a domain, and that totality is itself only fixed relative to some background model of set theory, which is subject to the very same countable-model phenomenon. Leaving aside the standard resolution of the paradox itself (that countability turns out to be non-absolute, so no genuine contradiction arises), what do you take to be the most important implications of this regress for whether Yudkowsky's pinning-down move actually secures what it promises, and for mathematical Platonism more generally?
