okay so I had this whole argument with my friend Jordan last night about that passage in the Nicomachean Ethics, book 5 chapter 5, the reciprocity-in-exchange bit with the builder and the shoemaker, and neither of us backed down so now its stuck in my head. here's the bit we were fighting over (Ross translation):

"Now proportionate return is secured by cross-conjunction. Let A be a builder, B a shoemaker, C a house, D a shoe. The builder, then, must get from the shoemaker the latter's work, and must himself give him in return his own... This is why all things that are exchanged must be somehow comparable. It is for this end that money has been introduced, and it becomes in a sense an intermediate; for it measures all things... All goods must therefore be measured by some one thing, as we said before. Now this unit is in truth demand, which holds all things together (for if men did not need one another's goods at all, or did not need them equally, there would be either no exchange or not the same exchange); but money has become by convention a sort of representative of demand; and this is why it has the name 'money' (nomisma)—because it exists not by nature but by law (nomos) and it is in our power to change it and make it useless... Now in truth it is impossible that things differing so much should become commensurate, but with reference to demand they may become so sufficiently. There must, then, be a unit, and that fixed by agreement..."

(the greek word behind "demand" there is chreia, if that matters)

Jordan's take is that Aristotle basically stumbled onto subjective/marginal-utility value theory right here, 2000 years early — value just reduces to demand or need, and money is only a token standing in for it, full stop. I said that can't be the whole picture because a few lines later he turns around and says the unit is "fixed by agreement," which sounds like the opposite of tracking something as shifting as individual need. Jordan thinks I'm just squeezing too much out of one word and that Aristotle wasn't being that precise anyway. So who's actually got the better reading here, and what work is chreia really doing if it's not just "how much somebody happens to want something"?
