So this happened at a bonfire a couple weekends ago and it's kind of been bugging me since. My friend Dan and my friend Mia got into an argument about aliens (we'd just watched one of those space youtube videos earlier so it was primed to happen). (1) Dan's opening point was that we've been listening for alien radio signals for 60-something years now and heard absolutely nothing, and combined with this "great filter" idea from the video, that silence basically means intelligent life has to be crazy rare — otherwise the galaxy's old enough that it should already be crawling with signals or probes by now. (2) Mia said thats a stretch: the silence could just as easily mean aliens are out there but far away, stopped using radio ages ago, aren't interested in reaching out, whatever — so 60-ish years of us hearing nothing is basically weak evidence either way, not proof of rarity.

Then it kept going. (3) Dan switched tracks and said forget SETI entirely, here's a totally separate reason to think intelligence is rare: Earth is about 4.5 billion years old and only has maybe another billion years before the sun gets hot enough to boil the oceans off, so basically all of Earth's habitable lifetime has already been spent just getting to us. He said if evolving a brain like ours was "easy," you'd expect it to happen early on most planets that have life, with plenty of room to spare, so cutting it this close here means one or more of the steps along the way (life starting at all, complex cells, whatever) must be an incredibly rare fluke — and this argument doesn't even need SETI's silence, it stands on its own. (4) Mia's comeback was that this is just survivorship bias, like the WWII planes-that-came-back thing: we could only ever exist on the version of Earth where intelligence happened in time, so of course it looks "just in time" to us, no matter what the real underlying odds are — meaning his timing argument is worth basically nothing as evidence.

Dan couldn't explain exactly why (4) doesn't just completely wipe out (3), but he also had this nagging feeling it couldn't be that clean of a knockout, and honestly so did I. Could you go through this properly, move by move — (1), (2), (3), and (4) — and say who's actually right at each step, not just who wins overall? I'm especially hung up on whether Mia's survivorship-bias point in (4) really does fully cancel out Dan's timing argument in (3), or whether something real survives it, because the way she said it, it sounded like a total mic drop, but I can't quite shake the feeling it isn't as clean as that.
