I'm taking a seminar on early modern rationalism this term and we just spent a couple weeks on Leibniz's "best of all possible worlds" stuff, which i thought i had a decent handle on (infinite possible worlds sitting in God's understanding, compossibility constraints, God surveys them all and picks whichever has the best overall mix of variety and order). But then we got to section XIII of the Discourse on Metaphysics for this week and now I feel like I don't actually understand the gears turning underneath the slogan. Here's the passage (it's the Montgomery translation) that's got me stuck:

"As the individual concept of each person includes once for all everything which can ever happen to him, in it can be seen, a priori the evidences or the reasons for the reality of each event, and why one happened sooner than the other. But these events, however certain, are nevertheless contingent, being based on the free choice of God and of his creatures. It is true that their choices always have their reasons, but they incline to the choices under no compulsion of necessity. [...] But does it not seem that in this way the difference between contingent and necessary truths will be destroyed, that there will be no place for human liberty, and that an absolute fatality will rule as well over all our actions as over all the rest of the events of the world? To this I reply that a distinction must be made between that which is certain and that which is necessary. [...] In order to meet the objection completely, I say that the connection or sequence is of two kinds; the one, absolutely necessary, whose contrary implies contradiction, occurs in the eternal verities like the truths of geometry; the other is necessary only ex hypothesi, and so to speak by accident, and in itself it is contingent since the contrary is not implied. [...] Let us give an example. Since Julius Caesar will become perpetual Dictator and master of the Republic and will overthrow the liberty of Rome, this action is contained in his concept, for we have supposed that it is the nature of such a perfect concept of a subject to involve everything, in fact so that the predicate may be included in the subject ut possit inesse subjecto. We may say that it is not in virtue of this concept or idea that he is obliged to perform this action, since it pertains to him only because God knows everything. But it will be insisted in reply that his nature or form responds to this concept, and since God imposes upon him this personality, he is compelled henceforth to live up to it."

A little further down he says the resolution is that this kind of connection is only necessary "ex hypothesi" and not absolutely, because its ultimately grounded in God freely decreeing to always do "that which is, or which appears to be the best among several things equally possible" - not grounded in pure logic the way geometry is. I get what every sentence says individually but I don't get why this actually gets Leibniz out of the hole. If Caesar's complete concept already contained "crosses the Rubicon" as one of its predicates before any decree was even made (its just sitting there among the possibles, waiting to be surveyed for fitness), in what sense was NOT crossing the Rubicon ever really available to him? "Its necessary given the decree, not necessary in itself" kind of feels like relabeling rather then dissolving the problem - couldn't you say the same move about 2+2=4 being "necessary given what the numerals mean" and nobody would count that as making it contingent? Is Leibniz maybe quietly assuming that a non-crossing Caesar would just be a different individual in a different possible world, so freedom was never really about this Caesar having live alternatives, only about which complete Caesar (crossing or not) got selected into existence? Or is that reading unfair to him? Can someone walk me through what's actually doing the philosophical work in this passage and whether it succeeds at blocking the fatalism worry, given how much the best-of-all-possible-worlds story is being leaned on to make it go through?
