So I've been down a Sleeping Beauty rabbit hole all week after my rationalist reading group got into it over drinks, and I think I've finally found the argument that settles the whole halfer/thirder fight for good: the Dutch book one. As I understand it, if Beauty sticks to credence 1/2 every time she wakes up, a bookie can line up a sequence of bets across her Monday and Tuesday awakenings that guarantees her a loss no matter how the coin actually landed, whereas a thirder (credence 1/3) can't be exploited that way. And since avoiding sure losses is basically THE gold standard for rationality around here — Dutch book arguments are literally how we justify probabilism as a theory of credence in the first place — doesn't that mean thirding has just won, full stop? Not "the more popular position among philosophers" but provably correct, the same way having intransitive preferences is provably irrational rather then just a matter of taste.

What I don't get, then, is why there's still a whole philosophy literature with serious halfers in it (Lewis defended the position, and people are still actively publishing on both sides of this). Am I missing some reason the betting argument doesn't actually clinch things, or is this basically a case of some philosophers just refusing to update because they're attached to their position?
