Ok so this has been bugging me for a while and I don't think I've seen anyone address it head-on. I went down a rabbit hole a few weeks ago rereading Oesterheld's original "multiverse-wide superrationality" paper (from what's now the Center on Long-Term Risk, back before everyone started calling it "evidential cooperation in large worlds," aka ECL), and realized I can't actually articulate how its different from Hofstadter's much older superrationality idea from his Scientific American columns -- the one about cooperating in a prisoner's dilemma against your psychological twin because you should reason as if your choice determines both of your moves. Both seem to be doing the same basic move: drop the requirement that your decision causally affect the outcome and swap in a correlation/evidential one instead, so cooperating becomes "rational" even with zero communication or causal contact between the parties.

A friend from my meetup group insists ECL is basically just "Hofstadter's idea plus a cosmology lecture bolted on" and doesn't add anything decision-theoretically new. I'm not so sure, mainly because the classic superrationality thought experiments are always symmetric -- you and a copy of you, same game, same payoffs -- wheras ECL is explicitly meant to cover agents with totally different values, scattered across a possibly-infinite universe, who can never verify they're even reasoning alike. (I know Yudkowsky basically folded the same intuition into functional decision theory instead of using Hofstadter's word for it, but let's leave FDT out of this and just stick to the two.) Could you carefully compare the two -- what ECL keeps from superrationality, what it has to add or change to handle asymmetric values and merely-partial correlation, and whether it can be grounded entirely in bog-standard evidential decision theory rather than any of Hofstadter's more informal hand-waving about "renormalized rationality"? Then tell me which one you actually think is the more fundamental idea here -- is superrationality just a limiting case of ECL, or are the two answering different enough questions that it's kind of a type error to say one contains the other?
