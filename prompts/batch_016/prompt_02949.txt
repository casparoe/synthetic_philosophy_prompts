What strikes me is how much the two views converge on the very phenomenon they're meant to explain: DeRose's bank cases and Stanley's Hannah and Sarah cases share essentially the same structure — someone with little riding on the outcome counts as knowing the bank is open on Saturday, while someone facing serious consequences for being wrong does not — and both camps cite exactly this pattern as their central piece of evidence.
