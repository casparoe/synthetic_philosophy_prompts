I have no formal training in philosophy, but over the past year I've worked through several of Hannah Arendt's essays on truth and lying in politics on my own, and one distinction of hers has stuck with me. In 'Truth and Politics' and 'Lying in Politics,' she separates the old-fashioned political lie, which hides or denies some specific fact while leaving the rest of the shared world of facts intact, from what she calls modern image-making, where an entire artificial picture is substituted for reality and the people constructing it often end up believing it as much as the public does. Just as important to her argument: the deepest damage from sustained, organized lying isn't that people believe false things, but that they lose the capacity to tell fact from fiction at all, becoming willing to believe almost anything and, at the same time, nothing. Arendt died in 1975, well before algorithmically curated social media or generative AI that can produce a convincing fake photo, video, or article on demand. Reasoning strictly from those two ideas, rather than her general disapproval of dishonesty, what do you think she'd say about today's information environment? Would she treat it as continuous with the bureaucratic image-making she already diagnosed, or as a genuinely new danger? And would she see today's fact-checking efforts as aimed at the problem she actually identified, or beside the point?
