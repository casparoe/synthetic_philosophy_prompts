Write an essay evaluating whether Molinism's doctrine of middle knowledge can survive the grounding objection. On the Molinist picture, logically prior to any decision about creation, God knows the truth value of every counterfactual of creaturely freedom — what any possible free creature would do in any possible circumstance — and uses this knowledge to actualize a world with a particular history without thereby determining anyone's free choices. Critics such as Robert Adams and William Hasker object that these counterfactuals cannot have the truth values Molinism requires, since nothing available prior to the creature's actual choice could make them true. Weigh the strongest Molinist replies against the force of this objection, and defend a definite verdict on whether middle knowledge, as standardly conceived, is a coherent and theologically useful posit — don't settle for concluding merely that reasonable people disagree.
