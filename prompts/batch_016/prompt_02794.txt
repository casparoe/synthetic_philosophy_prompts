I've spent a good deal of the last decade reading LessWrong and the Alignment Forum, though I don't work in AI safety myself, and a disagreement that played out in the comments under a retrospective post on the shutdown problem a few weeks back has stuck with me in a way I haven't been able to resolve on my own. Two commenters, Naomi and Devin, went several rounds on it, and I'd like a second opinion on how the exchange actually shakes out, since I suspect one or both of them may be missing something a specialist would catch immediately.

(1) Naomi opened by arguing that the shutdown problem is basically solved in outline. She pointed to Soares, Fallenstein, Yudkowsky and Armstrong's utility-indifference construction, which builds an agent's utility function as a mix of a "normal" component and a "shutdown" component tuned so the agent is indifferent to whether the button gets pressed, and to Hadfield-Menell et al.'s off-switch game, where an agent that is uncertain about its own reward and treats a human's willingness to flip the switch as evidence will rationally preserve the human's ability to do so. Between the two, she said, we already have existence proofs that a rational agent need not resist correction, so treating this as a live theoretical problem rather than an implementation backlog is mostly institutional caution. (2) Devin answered that both results are narrower than Naomi was crediting them with. The corrigibility paper's own list of desiderata requires that an agent only build subagents and successors that preserve the same shutdown behavior, and the authors say outright that they don't yet have a construction meeting all their desiderata at once; an indifferent agent, he added, has no particular reason to spend even trivial effort protecting its own off-switch hardware or checking that the shutdown instructions it receives are genuine. As for the off-switch game, the deference result only holds given specific assumptions about the agent's humility relative to the human's rationality, and the guarantee can shrink or reverse once the agent's confidence in its own reward estimate outpaces what the human's judgment actually warrants. (3) Naomi conceded both specific gaps but said this just describes the normal texture of an unfinished research program: follow-up work on causal indifference and utility-preserving variants has been chipping away at exactly these failure modes, and "no fully general fix yet" is a long way from "structurally impossible." (4) Devin replied that the pattern is itself the evidence: every patch he could think of closes the specific loophole it was built for while leaving a neighboring one open, which is exactly what you'd expect if the target behavior cuts against the grain of expected-utility maximization rather than being an ordinary unsolved corner of it. Neither of them budged, and the thread just trailed off.

Could you go through this move by move, saying specifically where Naomi is right and where she's overclaiming in (1) and (3), and likewise for Devin in (2) and (4), rather than just declaring an overall winner? I'd also be curious whether there's a consideration neither of them raised that you think actually matters for who has the stronger position overall.
