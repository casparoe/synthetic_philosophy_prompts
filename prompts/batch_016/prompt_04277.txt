This term I am running a graduate seminar on metaontology, and we have just spent two sessions working through Eli Hirsch's quantifier-variance thesis alongside the realist backlash it provoked from Peter van Inwagen, Trenton Merricks, and especially Ted Sider. On paper, my students can dutifully summarize the positions: universalists such as David Lewis hold that any collection of things whatsoever, however scattered, composes some further object; van Inwagen and Merricks hold that only simples and living organisms exist, so that talk of "chairs" and "tables" is just a convenient way of describing simples arranged chair-wise or table-wise; and Hirsch's diagnosis of the whole dispute is that it is merely verbal, since every party agrees on the arrangement of every last particle and differs only in how liberally they are willing to use the word "exists." Sider resists this by arguing that quantifiers, like ordinary predicates, can carve reality at the joints more or less well, so that a dispute settled by no possible observation might still be perfectly substantive. What my students cannot yet do is inhabit these positions well enough to argue them against one another in real time, which is really the skill I am trying to cultivate.

So I would like your help building a model dialogue for class discussion. Set the scene as follows: a child has just snapped a dozen Lego bricks together into a small toy spaceship, and two metaphysicians watching her fall into an argument about what, if anything, has thereby come into existence. Write a dialogue between the Deflationist, who holds that the dispute over whether a "new object" now exists is merely a disagreement about how to use the word "exists" and not a disagreement about the world, and the Realist, who holds that there is a fact of the matter, discoverable in principle, about whether composition has actually occurred. Have the Deflationist open with the familiar charge that the two sides agree about every particle, every prediction, and every observable fact, and differ only terminologically; have the Realist resist by appealing to the idea that some candidate meanings of the quantifier carve nature at the joints better than others. I would especially like to see whether the Deflationist can turn that very move back on the Realist — since asserting that one meaning is more joint-carving than another itself seems to require quantifying over meanings, or joints, or facts of some kind, and it is not obvious why that quantifier should be exempt from the very variance under dispute — and whether the Realist has any principled way of blocking the resulting regress. Please make the two figures equally sharp, have them genuinely engage each other's strongest points rather than talk past one another, and let the exchange end wherever it honestly leads, whether that is a clarified crux, a stalemate, or something else entirely — I would rather have real philosophical friction than a manufactured resolution.
