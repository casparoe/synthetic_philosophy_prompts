A friend and I spent an evening arguing over a factual question with a determinate answer; even after walking each other through our reasoning, neither of us found a flaw in the other's case, yet we still disagreed. That deadlock became a second argument: I thought intellectual honesty required both of us to move toward the middle, while my friend thought one of us could be entitled to hold firm regardless.

Write a dialogue between a conciliationist and a steadfaster working through a disagreement of exactly this shape. Once each has made their strongest first-order case, have them notice that they are themselves epistemic peers who disagree about conciliationism versus steadfastness itself. Have each apply their own theory reflexively to this meta-disagreement, pressing the other on whether their view, turned on itself, is self-undermining or just conveniently self-exempting. Neither character should concede easily, and neither view should simply win by the end.
